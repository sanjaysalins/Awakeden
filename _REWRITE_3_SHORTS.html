Rahab and the red cord

OLD (failed panel)

# jericho - spoken narration (KJV quotes) for the doctrine panel Jericho had the strongest walls in the world. And God told His army to march around them... in silence. Six days they circled. Thirteen laps in all. Not one arrow. Just feet on sand... and one scarlet cord, hanging from one window. Inside those walls lived Rahab. A woman with a past... who hid God's spies, and believed. Their answer: “Bind this line of scarlet thread in the window.” On the seventh day the trumpets tore the sky. “The people shouted with a great shout... the wall fell down flat.” Every stone came down. One window stood. Scripture says it plainly: “By faith the harlot Rahab perished not.” Not spared for being good. Spared for believing. Now read Jesus' family tree: “Salmon begat Booz of Rachab.” The woman behind the scarlet cord became His family. There is a scarlet line running from her window to a cross. His blood. Bind everything to it.

NEW (v3)

One red cord kept a woman alive when a city fell. Her name was Rahab, and people knew her for her sin. But she hid two of God's spies, because she believed in Him. They promised her: "thou shalt bind this line of scarlet thread in the window". That cord was the sign of the promise. Then God's army marched around her city, and on the seventh day "the people shouted with a great shout, that the wall fell down flat". The city fell. But Rahab and her family were saved, because she trusted what they said. Hebrews gives the reason: "By faith the harlot Rahab perished not with them that believed not". It was her faith, not her past. Matthew even names her in the family line of Jesus: "And Salmon begat Booz of Rachab". Christians see a picture in that cord: the blood of Jesus, which covers all who trust Him. Rahab could not hold up a wall. She only hung a cord in her window, and trusted the promise. Hang your hope on Jesus the same way.

What changed: Scarlet cord = sign of the promise, now said out loud. Hebrews line explained. Spelling lesson (Rachab) cut. Landing built from the cord.

Still weak: Longer than the old cut (180 words vs 153). Typology line "Christians see a picture" still a little teachy.

Aaron and the two goats

OLD (failed panel)

# two_goats - spoken narration (KJV quotes) for the doctrine panel Once a year I walked behind a curtain to meet God and I was never sure I would walk back out. The law gave me two goats for one sin. I killed the first and carried its blood inside the veil. The second I did not kill I laid my hands on its head, confessed every sin of the people over it, and drove it into the desert to be lost. One to pay. One to carry it away. For years I asked why it took two. I know now. No single creature could hold both halves. Long after me a Man named Jesus did what my two goats only pictured He was the price paid, and the One the guilt was laid on: and the LORD hath laid on him the iniquity of us all. He carried it away Himself once and sat down. Finished. The curtain I trembled behind tore in two the hour He died. I was only the sign. He is the substance. Don't come to me, or a goat, or an altar. Come to Jesus the way in is torn wide open. Walk in.

NEW (v3)

One goat died. One goat was sent away alive. Why two? I am Aaron, the first high priest, and once a year I handled both. The law gave me two goats, and together they were one sin offering for the people. The first I killed, and I carried its blood inside the veil, the curtain that shut people out of God's presence. The second I did not kill. I laid both hands on its head, and confessed over it all the sins of the people. Then it was led into the wilderness, to a land where no one lived. Two goats pictured one rescue. One showed sin paid for by death. One showed sin carried far away. Long after me, Jesus did what my goats only pictured. Isaiah wrote of Him: "the LORD hath laid on him the iniquity of us all". He died for our sins, and He bore them away. When He died, "the veil of the temple was rent in twain from the top to the bottom". The curtain is open. For all who trust Him, sin has gone where that goat went, to a land where no one lives. Walk in.

What changed: Goats now "pictured" the rescue; only Jesus really paid. Veil explained before it is used. Landing uses the goat that went away.

Still weak: Longest (195 words). Two goats + torn veil is still two ideas; I kept one thread: sin carried away.

Moses and the brass snake

OLD (failed panel)

# bronze_serpent - spoken narration (KJV quotes) for the doctrine panel I am Moses. My people were dying of snakebite — and God told me to forge a snake of bronze and lift it on a pole. The serpents were no accident — we had spoken against God, and the venom was the judgment our sin had earned. I begged Him to take the snakes away. He would not. Instead He told me to forge the image of the very thing killing us, and lift it high. The bitten had only to look — and live. I speak now from the far side of my life, by the light that came after — a night I never saw, when one they called Teacher answered a seeker: And as Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, even so must the Son of man be lifted up: My bronze was only a shadow. They lifted Jesus on a Roman pole, made a curse for us, bearing our judgment in our place. So hear me, you who are bitten — that is every one of us. The cure was never in you; it hangs in plain sight, and costs you nothing but a look. Lift your eyes to Jesus, lifted up for you. Look, and live.

NEW (v3)

God answered snakebite with a snake on a pole. I am Moses, and I made it. My people were dying of snakebites, because they had spoken against God. They came to me: "We have sinned, for we have spoken against the LORD, and against thee". I prayed. Instead of taking the snakes away, God told me to make a serpent of brass, and set it on a pole. Whoever was bitten looked at it, and lived. Long after me, Jesus told a man named Nicodemus: "And as Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, even so must the Son of man be lifted up: That whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have eternal life." My brass serpent was only a picture. On the cross, Jesus took on Himself the sin that bites us all, and Paul says He was "made a curse for us". Lifted up where all can see, He is the cure. Lift your eyes to Him, and live.

What changed: Opens on the strange answer. Adds the missing link: Jesus took the sin-bite on the cross. Flat line "to look is to believe" cut.

Still weak: Landing "Lift your eyes to Him, and live" is close to a stock line, but it uses the pole image.
