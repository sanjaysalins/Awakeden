## 0. Answer to the owner's challenge

You asked: can AI find Bible insights nobody has seen before?
Honest answer: no. AI cannot give new revelation, and we must never pretend it can.
What AI can do is read all 66 books at once and find links that are **already in the text**: the same words in two books, a verse the New Testament quotes, a small detail most people read past.
So the bar is **"old in the text, new to the viewer."** Never new doctrine.
Our first hunt (Bethlehem) scored about 6 out of 10 for newness. Real, but small.
It was small for one reason: we picked a famous topic first, then looked for a fresh angle after the film was made.
This plan flips that. **We hunt the link first, and only make the film if the link is strong.** That is how we push 6 toward 7 or 8.

## 2. The insight engine

### 2a. The six kinds of link we allow

| Kind of link | Example | Honesty label | What the film says out loud |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. NT says "fulfilled" or quotes it of Jesus | Psalm 16:10 in Acts 2 | **PROPHECY** | "The Bible itself says this was about Him." |
| B. NT itself names the pattern or states the link | The veil, Hebrews 10:20 | **TYPE** | "The New Testament makes this link." |
| C. Same words in two books (word-chain) | Genesis 3:7 and Luke 24:31 | **ECHO** | "The Bible does not say these are linked. But look at the words." |
| D. Shape or order echo (structure) | Leper and Jesus swap places, Mark 1 | **ECHO** | Same as C. |
| E. Overlooked detail in the passage itself | "would have passed by them", Mark 6:48 | **DETAIL** (no cross-book claim) | "It is right there in the verse." |
| F. Culture or language fact the text grounds | Fringes on the garment, Numbers 15:38 | **ECHO** + "language note" | "Here is what that word meant." Keep rare. |

Rule: the strongest label in a piece sets the headline. An ECHO can never be the title claim of a Driver 1 "prophecy" episode. It can be the surprise inside it.

### 2b. The hunt (all $0, copy of `prophecy/01_bethlehem/fresh_hunt/`)

| Step | What happens | Tool |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Loom first | Pull verbatim word matches across the whole KJV for the passage. | `/concordance-loom` |
| 2. Kill list | Write the 4-6 angles "told a million times". Seats may not use them. | `BRIEF.md` template |
| 3. Blind hunt | 6 seats (Fable + Sonnet + claude / codex / gemini / grok CLIs). Each works alone. Each gives angles + exact quotes + label + "what it does NOT prove". | `run_seats.py` |
| 4. Machine check | Every quote checked. Any MISMATCH is fixed or the angle is dropped. | `kjv_check.py` |
| 5. Neutral cards | Angles re-written as plain cards. No authors. Random order. | `CARDS.md` |
| 6. Blind judges | 6 fresh seats score every card. | `run_judges.py` |
| 7. Bank it | Winner goes in the Insight Bank with its scores. Losers are kept too. | `_roadmap/INSIGHT_BANK.md` (new, simple table) |

### 2c. The scores and the bar

| Score | Meaning | Pass bar (median of 6 judges) |
|---|---|---|
| FRESH | How few churchgoers have been shown this | **7.0 or more** |
| TRUE | Really in the text, label honest | 8.0 or more |
| SOUND | Doctrine and overclaim | All PASS. One factual FLAG = fix or drop. |
| CLEAR | Newcomer follows it in 60 s | 7.0 or more |
| JESUS | Lands on Him without forcing | 8.0 or more |

- FRESH 6.0 to 6.9 = "classic, told well". Allowed, but at most **1 in 4** episodes.
- FRESH under 6 = stays in the bank. No film.
- Freshness never excuses a stretch. TRUE and SOUND win every tie.

### 2d. What pushes newness above 6

| Lever | Why it helps |
|---|---|
| Hunt before the topic is locked | Bethlehem was hunted after the film was paid for. Only a small fold-in was possible. |
| Two-hop chains (OT → OT → NT) | One-hop links are the famous ones. The second hop is where "I never saw that" lives. |
| Start from the loom, not from memory | The loom finds matches no seat thought to recall. |
| Kill list on every brief | Stops seats handing back the Sunday-school answer. |
| Show both verses on screen, side by side | The viewer sees the link with their own eyes. That is the "hyperlink" feel. |
| Ask real people | 5-10 seed viewers: "Had you seen this before? Yes / No." AI judges only guess at this. |

## 3. Driver 2 needs its own "so what"

A Jesus-event episode must end with a take-away that is **earned by the event**, not stuck on.

| Test | Pass means | Fail looks like |
|---|---|---|
| S1. From the text | The take-away uses a word or act from the passage's own KJV. | "So trust God more." |
| S2. About Him first | It says what **Jesus is like or did**, then what that means for you. Grace before duty. | A to-do list. |
| S3. One true sentence | A newcomer can repeat it. No church words. | "Walk in your calling." |
| S4. Life-size | It touches one real thing: fear, shame, failure, being unseen, purpose, loss. | Abstract ideas. |

How to build it:
1. Write the "so what" as **one line** during the hunt, before any narration. Judges score it as GROWTH 0-10 (bar: 7.0).
2. Use the "earned landing word" pattern: the closing line turns on one concrete word from the passage (owner liked this before).
3. No questions that push ("Will you...?"). State what He did. Let it sit.
4. Run the 5 guardrail questions. "What is the main take-away" must come back the same from every seat. If seats give different take-aways, the film is not clear yet.
