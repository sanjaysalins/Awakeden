# Independent review — codex (OK, 194s)

The final fixes still leave scriptural overclaims in two starters and misleading assurances on the owner page.

Reviewed only the [roadmap](F:/slk/PycharmProjects/JesusInTheBible/_roadmap/TWO_DRIVERS_ROADMAP.md), [owner page](F:/slk/PycharmProjects/JesusInTheBible/_roadmap/_TWO_DRIVERS.html), and local KJV passages. Tool implementation and ledger figures remain unverified.

1. **HIGH — T3’s revised anchor does not establish its headline.**  
   §10 changes the anchor to Isaiah 9:1, but retains “The first land to fall into the dark was the first to see the light” (§5, T3; page line 61). Isaiah contrasts affliction “at the first” with more grievous affliction “afterward”; it does **not** rank this territory as the first land ever afflicted or first illuminated. Matthew 4:14–16 establishes fulfillment in the named region, not that historical superlative. “God goes first to the place that was hurt first” then turns an unproved chronology into a general rule about God. The new anchor does not fix the inference.

2. **HIGH — E9 packages selected requests as an exhaustive textual fact.**  
   “Three people ask Jesus” (§5, E9) is inaccurate: the requesters include demons and a population. “Three requests” on page line 57 also needs qualification: Mark records appeals in 5:7, 5:10, 5:12, 5:17 and 5:18. Three *selected request groups* can structure a sermon; that is different from a DETAIL count. “The only one who hears ‘no’ is the man who loves Him” adds an unstated motive. His desire to accompany Jesus is explicit; this comparative claim about love is not.

   “A ‘no’ from Him can be your purpose” also drifts toward a familiar motivational slogan. Here Jesus gives an **explicit command to testify to His mercy**. The passage does not identify ordinary disappointment or unanswered prayer as a commission from Jesus.

3. **MEDIUM — T12 needs its contextual evidence shown.**  
   Its final **NT-STATED** attribution of Jesus’ glory is supportable; I find no demonstrated doctrinal error in that claim. But Isaiah 6:1 → John 12:41 skips the connecting evidence: John 12:37–40 identifies Jesus as the subject and quotes Isaiah 6:10 immediately before “his glory.” Include that chain in the FACTS check. Also, page line 39 still defines THE THREAD as “Jesus in prophecy,” despite §10 expressly distinguishing this starter from prediction.

4. **HIGH — Some latest “fixes” still mishandle context.**

   - **E7, §10:** Job 9:11 is called “Stronger than Exodus 33 alone,” but its full sentence says God passes **unseen and unperceived**. That does not directly substantiate the proposed explanation, “‘Pass by’ is what God does when He shows His glory.” A possible echo requires argument, not merely matching words.
   - **E12, §10:** Hebrews 7:11 distinguishes priestly orders. It does not prohibit an Aaron/Jesus narrative echo; Hebrews 9:6–14 itself compares Levitical ministry with Christ. Holding the episode is reasonable, but the stated disqualification is exegetically insufficient.

5. **HIGH — The application tests still admit promises the passages do not make.**  
   E6 concludes, “He comes to find you at each [step].” John 9 records Jesus finding this man after his expulsion; it does not promise a repeated encounter at every stage of everyone’s faith. E5 compresses Isaiah’s experience into “Both fall apart. Both get a job,” omitting the intervening removal of guilt and purging of sin in Isaiah 6:6–7. That omission makes holiness, cleansing and commissioning sound like reassurance for feeling inadequate. These are still active candidates after §10.

6. **HIGH — The owner page overstates clearance and promises an unauthorised pace increase.**  
   Page line 64 says, **“None are wrong doctrine; they need fixing.”** The plan’s narrower statement is “None judged UNSOUND” (§10). A panel’s recorded assessment does not establish doctrinal correctness, especially while the claims remain unresolved.

   Page line 72 promises **“1 a week for 4 weeks, then 2.”** §10 instead says, **“Raise pace only from measured numbers.”** Four weeks is not the release condition. The page also presents “The first 3 films” without the roadmap’s clear warning that each candidate must still qualify.

7. **MEDIUM — The replacement freshness gate has no executable procedure.**  
   §10 makes seed viewers “the true gate,” but never inserts that gate into the workflow before paid production. Who recruits them, which audience qualifies, what they see, and what happens on failure remain unspecified. The existing 60% threshold means three unfamiliar people out of five can pass a familiar sermon illustration. That does not establish the stated measure, “How few churchgoers have been shown this.”

8. **MEDIUM — Daily capacity and cost claims remain unproved after the pace fix.**  
   Each hunt requires six hunters plus six fresh judges (§2b): **12 seat runs per day**, before writing and film panels. Yet §10 records Grok timing out twice, with no rule for retries, replacement seats or an incomplete panel. That can stall the pipeline or quietly weaken its gates.

   “$0” reasonably describes no additional metered charge, but subscription quota and operator time still constrain throughput. The $8–14 production estimate cites two completed examples and one only “through clips”; it does not establish a repeatable allowance for rejected assets and rerenders. Human recruitment and review time also need inclusion in the pilot measurement.
