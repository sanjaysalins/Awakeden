# Independent review — codex (OK, 275s)

The main weakness is that cautious labels coexist with stronger, unsupported conclusions. Round 1 softened some wording without fixing the underlying claims.

1. **E2 still turns an echo into substitution.** “He took your place outside so you could come in” claims more than Mark establishes. Mark 1:43–45 explicitly connects Jesus’ restricted movement to the healed man’s publicity despite Jesus’ instruction. “Free to go out and tell everyone” obscures that disobedience. Hebrews 13:12 concerns Jesus’ suffering, not the cause of His location in Mark. Section 9’s geography edit does not resolve this, and calling E2 one of the “lowest risk” starters is unjustified.

2. **Several Encounter landings promise more than their passages support.**
   - E6: “He comes to find you **at each one**.” John 9:35 records Jesus finding the man after his expulsion; it does not establish repeated visits at every stage.
   - E8: “**Your** worst day … He had already prayed.” Luke 22:32 addresses Peter specifically. Applying Christ’s intercession to viewers needs its own warrant and audience scope; Hebrews 7:25 specifies those coming to God through Him.
   - E12: “The blessing never ends **on the page**.” A narrative ending during a blessing does not prove an eternally continuing gesture. Hebrews supports continuing intercession.
   
   The missing safeguard is an explicit distinction between what happened, what it reveals about Jesus, and what may legitimately be promised to the viewer.

3. **T9 contains a factual error and an interpretation presented as settled fact.** “Silent all night” contradicts Jesus’ answers during His interrogation in John 18:20–23. “The ‘coming’ part is still ahead” also requires qualification: Daniel 7:13 describes movement **toward the Ancient of days**. Its relationship to enthronement and future return cannot be settled by quote matching. These belong in the FACTS review before approving the angle.

4. **T7 still merges an unnamed psalm figure with Ahithophel.** “Betrayed by a table friend … The friend hangs himself” presents one continuous identification, although the label acknowledges “the psalm does not name the friend.” Second Samuel identifies Ahithophel as David’s counsellor; the supplied anchors do not establish that he is Psalm 41’s table companion. Likewise, Jesus’ omission of “in whom I trusted” is observable, but any explanation of *why* He omitted it remains interpretation. Speaking “ECHO” once does not neutralize stronger narration.

5. **Some memorable applications introduce unsupported general rules.** T3’s “God goes first to the place that was hurt first” turns a geographical observation into a promise about God’s priorities. E1’s “With Him, love comes before work” moves from the Father’s declaration about His unique Son to an unspecified viewer without establishing that bridge. E5’s “Both fall apart. Both get a job” skips Isaiah 6:6–7’s cleansing between confession and commission—a substantial omission in an episode claiming to explain how grace leads to purpose.

6. **The drivers are distinguishable in intention, but their admission rules remain blurred.** Driver 1 promises verses that “lock together” as something “the Bible promised,” yet T12 is an identification of Isaiah’s vision, explicitly “not a prediction.” Driver 2’s E7 is substantially an OT-to-NT argument about Jesus’ identity—the Thread’s stated territory. Also, Q2 lists only PROPHECY, TYPE and ECHO, while section 2 introduces DETAIL. Overlap is workable, but the plan needs a consistent classification rule and a Thread promise broad enough to cover its actual episodes.

7. **The freshness machinery risks rewarding stretches before validating demand.** “Seats may not use” familiar angles and “the second hop is where ‘I never saw that’ lives” pressure the hunt toward increasingly remote connections. Every hunt requires six generators plus six judges, before the later writing and film panels. Meanwhile, real viewers appear as a suggested lever, without a clear decision point. Shared AI approval cannot establish either unfamiliarity or doctrinal soundness. The pilot should test viewer understanding and interest before this becomes a daily operation.

8. **Section 9’s pilot is necessary, but its success criteria are missing.** “Measure real time and cost” does not specify what results justify two films weekly. Section 4’s “4–5 small decisions” omits or compresses insight selection, words, separate paid approvals, voice review, clips review, finished-film review and pack approval. The advertised “1 hunt a day” also differs from the weekly table, which schedules hunts on four days. Neither subscription capacity nor the owner’s review burden has been demonstrated. There is no basis yet for “Then 3 films a week is possible.”

9. **The calendar assumes away its own rejection rate.** Section 6 expects roughly 30–40% of the 24 angles to fail, leaving around 14–17 passing episodes against a 24-film schedule. “The bank keeps spares” does not establish that those spares exist. Weeks 3–4 also allocate two Thread slots while mentioning T6, T8 **and** T5. Replacement hunts and the six-passed-insight buffer need to precede a dependable release calendar.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Resolve E2’s substitution claim and audit every landing for unsupported promises to viewers.
2. Correct T9 and separate explicit biblical statements from inferred links, especially in T7.
3. Make cadence conditional on a measured pilot, viewer validation, and a stocked replacement bank.
