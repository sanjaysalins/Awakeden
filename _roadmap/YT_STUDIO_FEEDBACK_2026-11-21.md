Winning patterns:
1. **The Great Exchange** — a guilty / broken person walks free or is taken in; Jesus steps in.
2. **Raw human failure** — sleeping, betrayal, denial, conflict.
3. **Jesus stops for one person** in a high-stakes moment.

Comments: viewers come for **relief**, not facts (grief, storms, shame, feeling unworthy).
Viewers ask: "Why did Judas do it?"

## 2. What Studio says is weak

1. Academic / verse-first hooks and titles (e.g. leading with "Zechariah 12:10").
2. Channel mix: kids' animation + Hindi worship songs + prophecy shorts split the audience.
3. Endings too predictable (KJV quote -> explanation -> warm landing, every time).

Our notes: our real first lines are already mostly human; the "Seed of the Woman (Prophecy)"
example is our internal label, not a live title. Point 3 matches our own "landing fatigue" finding.

## 3. Hook formula (first 3 seconds)

**Flip** (surprising, breaks an assumption) -> **Old Testament link** (an intended mirror) ->
**Personal landing** tied to the viewer's real weight (shame, fear, failure). Open on the weight,
turn on the verse. Vary the ending type: a question / a quiet image / the story's own last line.
