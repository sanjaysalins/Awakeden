W3
REPLACE: "In the King James Bible, outside is "without." The sin offering, an animal killed for the people's sin, had its blood carried into the sanctuary, God's holy tent. Its body went out, burned "without the camp." Then it says of Jesus: "Wherefore Jesus also, that he might sanctify the people with his own blood, suffered without the gate.""
WITH: "In Israel's law, the bodies of animals offered for sin were burned outside the camp. Hebrews says that is why Jesus went outside the city gate, to make the people holy: "Wherefore Jesus also, that he might sanctify the people with his own blood, suffered without the gate.""
REPLACE: "Reproach is shame. Bearing it is carrying it, as Simon carried the wood. Simon was forced out. You are asked. Turn on your own road, and carry his shame after him, freely this time. Only the shame."
WITH: "That is not a heavy load — it means standing outside the walls with the Jesus who was rejected, because out there, His blood has already made sinners clean. Simon was forced to carry that cross down the road, yet at the end, Jesus was the One crucified on it."
TITLE: Forced to Carry a Cross He Never Chose — Jesus Died on It
NOTE:
W1: Sentence 5 repeats the throw-overboard event after sentence 1 already had the fish swallow him; Matthew 12:38 includes Pharisees with the scribes ("we would see a sign", not just "Jesus gave them only my name"); KJV shifts from "great fish" to "whale's belly," which may puzzle second-language listeners.
W2: "Men screaming for their lives" is not in Matthew 8:23-27; "in the stern" and "doesn't care" come from Mark 4:38 rather than Matthew 8; the quote in paragraph 3 puts narrator text ("Then he arose...") inside Jesus's spoken quotation marks; "when the water reaches your knees, you don't trust the Christ in the boat" still assumes the viewer's faith and echoes the knees detail.
W3: "Walking the other way," "Same road," and "Simon turns" infer movement beyond "passed by, coming out of the country" (Mark 15:21); "atoning" in the final sentence is hard vocabulary for English learners.
