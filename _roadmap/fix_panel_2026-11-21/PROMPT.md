## The channel
A YouTube Shorts channel: "Find Jesus in the whole Bible, one story at a time." 40-90 second narrated Bible shorts, King James Version (KJV) quotes, watched worldwide (many viewers speak English as a second language). Every short lands on Jesus, with grace, never fear or pressure.

## What the channel's own data says works (YouTube Studio, last 28 days)
Best shorts keep 83-94% of the video watched. They share 3 shapes:
1. THE EXCHANGE - a guilty or broken person goes free / is taken in; Jesus steps in (Barabbas 94%, the thief on the cross 85%).
2. FAILURE MET BY GRACE - raw human weakness (friends who slept when Jesus needed them, 83%).
3. JESUS STOPS FOR ONE - He had every reason to hurry and stopped for one overlooked person (blind Bartimaeus 92%).
Viewers come for RELIEF, not facts: comments open up about grief, fear, shame, failure, feeling unworthy.
What loses viewers: a first line that sounds like a classroom or trivia ("this prophet wrote this 700 years early"), leading with a book/verse number, and endings that all sound the same.
The first 3 seconds should: name a human weight -> flip an assumption -> (later) show the old link -> land on Jesus for the viewer.

## THE 11 SHORTS
## S1
TITLE NOW: Why One Red Cord Survived the Fall of Jericho
SPOKEN WORDS (as recorded):
When Jericho fell, one family walked out alive. Her name was Rahab. She lived in Jericho's wall. The Bible calls her a prostitute. Two spies came from God's people, Israel. She hid them, because she believed in their God. They told her: hang a red cord in your window, and your family will be safe. Israel marched. The people shouted. And "the wall fell down flat". Rahab and her family were spared. The cord was the sign. Trusting the promise saved her. A book of the Bible, Hebrews, says it in two words: "By faith". She is named in the family line of Jesus, God's Son, who "taketh away the sin of the world". Rahab could not stop a wall. She tied one cord to a promise. Tie your trust to Jesus, and bring everyone you love inside.

## S2
TITLE NOW: A Warning at Jesus' Birth Came True at the Cross
SPOKEN WORDS (as recorded):
An old man in the temple held a baby and told the mother one dark sentence.
Yea, a sword shall pierce through thy own soul also.
Simeon told Mary. Some thirty years later, the sword lands.
Now there stood by the cross of Jesus his mother.
Close enough to watch her son die. Jesus looks down, sees her wound, and turns to bind it.
When Jesus therefore saw his mother, and the disciple standing by, whom he loved, he saith unto his mother,
Woman, behold thy son!
Then saith he to the disciple,
Behold thy mother!
And from that hour that disciple took her unto his own home.
He gave her the disciple he loved. A son. A home.
While judgment fell on him, he cared for someone else. That is who is dying there.
Your wound is not invisible to him. He sees it from the cross.
Stand where Mary stood. The Son who saw her will behold you, and bind what the sword tore.

## S3
TITLE NOW: Why Was Jesus Born in Bethlehem?
SPOKEN WORDS (as recorded):
The Bible experts knew where the King would be born. Did they go?
About seven hundred years before, the prophet Micah named a small town: Bethlehem. God promised a King from there:
ruler in Israel.
It goes on:
from everlasting.
Bethlehem would be His birthplace, not His beginning. There, God's promise came true: Jesus, the King, was born. Wise men came asking for this King. King Herod asked the experts.
In Bethlehem of Judaea.
Bethlehem was close by.
The Bible never says the experts went. The wise men went, and found the child.
Jesus grew up in Galilee. People argued: can the King come from Galilee? The promise said Bethlehem. He was born there.
But Jesus came much farther. He came down from heaven. He died for our sins. On the third day, He rose from the dead.
Before anyone went to Him, He came to us.

## S4
TITLE NOW: Jesus Stopped Reading Mid-Sentence
SPOKEN WORDS (as recorded):
Jesus stopped reading in the middle of a sentence.
In Nazareth, His home town, He read God's promise of someone sent with good news for the poor, and a time of God's welcome.
The prophet Isaiah's sentence goes on:
and the day of vengeance of our God.
He left the day of vengeance unread, and sat down. (pause)
This day is this scripture fulfilled.
It was about Him. They would want miracles, and say:
Physician, heal thyself.
He reminded them God once sent help to foreigners, not to their own. Furious, they took Him to a cliff to throw Him down. He walked right through them. (pause)
Later, at the cross, the same dare:
He saved others; let him save himself.
This time, He stayed. He died, and rose again. (pause)
He stopped reading at the welcome. It is still open, for you, today.

## S5
TITLE NOW: Ten Were Healed. Only One Came Back.
SPOKEN WORDS (as recorded):
Jesus told ten men with leprosy to go — before a single one of them was healed. They were cleansed on the road. Only one of them came back.
They'd stood at a distance, as their disease required, and cried out, "Jesus, Master, have mercy on us." He told them, "Go shew yourselves unto the priests." They obeyed before they could see any difference. "And it came to pass, that, as they went, they were cleansed."
Nine kept going. One turned back, "and with a loud voice glorified God, and fell down on his face at his feet, giving him thanks: and he was a Samaritan."
Jesus asked it out loud, naming exactly what nine men had skipped: "Were there not ten cleansed? but where are the nine? There are not found that returned to give glory to God, save this stranger."
To the one still on his face, he said, "Arise, go thy way: thy faith hath made thee whole."
Nine had exactly what they'd asked for, and kept walking. One turned back to him. Luke calls it thanks. Jesus calls it faith. That question didn't end with the nine. Go back and find him — at his feet, not on the road.

## S6
TITLE NOW: Jesus Died Praying a Child's Bedtime Prayer
SPOKEN WORDS (as recorded):
A dying man's last words. And they were a child's prayer.
Jewish tradition made these words an evening prayer. Words a child could learn for the dark.
Into thine hand I commit my spirit: thou hast redeemed me, O LORD God of truth.
On the cross, the three hours of darkness have passed. With his last breath, he prays those evening words.
Father, into thy hands I commend my spirit.
He begins with a word the Psalm does not have. Father. Not judge. Not stranger. Father.
The Psalm he prayed says, thou hast redeemed me. On that cross, he was buying that redemption.
He dies like a child falling asleep in his father's arms.
On the third day, morning came.
Because he rose, death loses its last word over everyone who is his. You can close your eyes in those same hands. Not because death is small. Because the hands are strong.
Commend your spirit to the Father, tonight and every night. He keeps the morning.

## S7
TITLE NOW: God Hung Up a Snake. Looking at It Saved Them.
SPOKEN WORDS (as recorded):
You'd expect the cure to be hung up looking hopeful - a light, a flag. God hung up a snake.
Not a comforting symbol. The precise shape of their death, lifted up where the whole camp could see it.
"And Moses made a serpent of brass, and put it upon a pole, and it came to pass, that if a serpent had bitten any man, when he beheld the serpent of brass, he lived." Call it too grim for a cure if you like. But God never once dressed it up as something else. It stayed exactly what it was.
We keep reaching for a gentler version of what Christ did - something less costly. He didn't become a general sacrifice. He became your curse.
God hung up a snake so the camp could live. He hung up Christ, His own Son, carrying your exact curse, so you could too.

## S8
TITLE NOW: What Thief Wraps the Face-Cloth? The Empty Tomb
SPOKEN WORDS (as recorded):
Someone took the body. That was Mary's first thought at the empty tomb of Jesus.
The first day of the week, while it was still dark, Mary Magdalene found the stone rolled away — and ran to Peter and John:
They have taken away the Lord out of the sepulchre, and we know not where they have laid him.
They ran. The body was gone. Only the linen — and the cloth that had covered his head:
And the napkin, that was about his head, not lying with the linen clothes, but wrapped together in a place by itself.
What thief wraps the face-cloth and lays it aside?
Then John stepped inside. No angel. No risen Lord. Only the linen, lying where the body should have been. The gospel says only this:
and he saw, and believed.
Believed what? That his Lord was risen. He did not yet know the scripture — that Christ must rise from the dead. The linen was enough.
Thomas refused to believe until he saw. Eight days later the risen Jesus showed him his hands — Thomas saw, and believed. Then Jesus said:
blessed are they that have not seen, and yet have believed.
You were not at the tomb. You never saw the linen. Jesus was speaking about you. Believe what John believed at the linen — the Lord is risen. Already, he calls that faith blessed.

## S9
TITLE NOW: The Man Who Carried Jesus' Cross Was Walking the Other Way
SPOKEN WORDS (as recorded):
The man who would carry the cross of Jesus was walking the other way.
Mark says the soldiers "led him out to crucify him. And they compel one Simon a Cyrenian, who passed by, coming out of the country." Jesus, out of the city. Simon, in from the country. Same road. They put the cross on Simon's back. Nobody asked him. Luke says, "on him they laid the cross, that he might bear it after Jesus." Simon turns, and walks out too.
Now he is through the gate. In the King James Bible, outside is "without." The sin offering, an animal killed for the people's sin, had its blood carried into the sanctuary, God's holy tent. Its body went out, burned "without the camp." Then it says of Jesus: "Wherefore Jesus also, that he might sanctify the people with his own blood, suffered without the gate." Out there, where Simon is walking.
"Let us go forth therefore unto him without the camp, bearing his reproach." Reproach is shame. Bearing it is carrying it, as Simon carried the wood. Simon was forced out. You are asked. Turn on your own road, and carry his shame after him, freely this time. Only the shame. He went out through that gate first, and the atoning, the dying, the finishing, that was his, with his own blood.

## S10
TITLE NOW: Jesus Slept Through a Storm. Then He Asked One Question.
SPOKEN WORDS (as recorded):
Waves breaking over the rail. Water in the boat past your knees. Men screaming for their lives. And in the stern — He is asleep.
He is not asleep because He doesn't care. He is asleep because He has nothing to fear from the very thing that is killing them.
They shake Him awake, raging that He could possibly sleep through this. He opens His eyes: "Why are ye fearful, O ye of little faith? Then he arose, and rebuked the winds and the sea; and there was a great calm." You'll say — easy for Him, He's God. Exactly. The One who could sleep through it is the One who silenced it with a word.
You have admired this scene your whole life. But when the water reaches your knees, you don't trust the Christ in the boat. You wake Him, furious, certain He doesn't care.
He doesn't despise little faith. He grows it by showing you Himself. Come to the Christ who slept unafraid. He is asking you — why are you afraid?

## S11
TITLE NOW: The Second Fire
SPOKEN WORDS (as recorded):
Is there one night you can't stop thinking about? The night Jesus was arrested, His friend Peter stood by a fire. People asked if he was one of His followers.
"I am not."
Three times, he said no. Jesus died. He rose from the dead.
Peter went back to fishing. All night, nothing.
At sunrise, Jesus stands on the shore. He tells them where to throw the net. It fills with fish. A friend cries, "It is the Lord." Peter jumps into the sea to reach Him.
On the beach: "a fire of coals." In the King James Bible, these words appear only twice. Here, and the night Peter said no.
"Come and dine."
Come and eat. Jesus hands them bread and fish. After the meal, He asks Peter three times:
"Lovest thou me?"
Do you love me? The third time, Peter is sad.
"Lord, thou knowest all things; thou knowest that I love thee."
You know everything. You know I love you.
"Feed my sheep."
Take care of my people. Three times, no, that night. Three times, yes, at the second fire.
And before Jesus asked Peter to feed anyone, Jesus fed Peter.
