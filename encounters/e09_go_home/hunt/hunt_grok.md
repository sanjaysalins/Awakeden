He worshipped and adjured Jesus
HOOK: Why does he worship Jesus and adjure him?
VERSES:
Mark 5:2 "And when he was come out of the ship, immediately there met him out of the tombs a man with an unclean spirit,"
Mark 5:6 "But when he saw Jesus afar off, he ran and worshipped him,"
Mark 5:7 "And cried with a loud voice, and said, What have I to do with thee, Jesus, thou Son of the most high God? I adjure thee by God, that thou torment me not."
Mark 5:8 "For he said unto him, Come out of the man, thou unclean spirit."
Mark 3:11 "And unclean spirits, when they saw him, fell down before him, and cried, saying, Thou art the Son of God."
LABEL: DETAIL on Mark 5:6-8. ECHO on Mark 3:11 (same shape; Mark never says the two meetings are one).
WHAT IT DOES NOT PROVE: It does not prove the man already had clean, settled faith. The worship and the loud cry are one sequence, and that cry says "What have I to do with thee" and "torment me not." Mark 3:11 does not prove this man is those earlier spirits.
FACTS:
- The man who met Jesus was a man with an unclean spirit, come out of the tombs (Mark 5:2).
- He saw Jesus afar off, ran, and worshipped him (Mark 5:6).
- The same cry calls him "Jesus, thou Son of the most high God," says "What have I to do with thee," and adjures him by God not to torment him (Mark 5:7).
- Mark 5:8 begins with "For": Jesus had already said, "Come out of the man, thou unclean spirit."
- Elsewhere in Mark, unclean spirits who saw him fell down and called him the Son of God (Mark 3:11). Mark does not say that verse is this same day.
SO WHAT: Jesus came out of the ship to the man who ran and worshipped him while that voice still said, "What have I to do with thee," so if you carry shame, you are not past him — Romans 5:8 says Christ died for us while we were yet sinners.
WHY MOST CHURCHGOERS HAVE NOT HEARD IT: They file this under "demons know who Jesus is" and never stop on "worshipped" sitting in the same cry as "What have I to do with thee." Freshness: 7
HOW IT LANDS ON JESUS: The first words spoken to him in this country call him Jesus, Son of the most high God, and he is already the one who has said, "Come out."
YOUR CONFIDENCE: 8

Jesus gave them leave
HOOK: Why must the devils ask Jesus for leave?
VERSES:
Mark 5:10 "And he besought him much that he would not send them away out of the country."
Mark 5:12 "And all the devils besought him, saying, Send us into the swine, that we may enter into them."
Mark 5:13 "And forthwith Jesus gave them leave."
LABEL: DETAIL
WHAT IT DOES NOT PROVE: It does not prove Jesus took orders from the devils, or made a pact with them. It does not say what happened to the spirits after the swine were choked. It does not say he granted their first plea in the words they chose.
FACTS:
- Before the swine are mentioned, he besought Jesus much not to send them out of the country (Mark 5:10).
- All the devils then besought him, "Send us into the swine" (Mark 5:12).
- The leave Mark records is that plea: "Jesus gave them leave," and the unclean spirits went out of the man and entered the swine (Mark 5:13).
- The herd, about two thousand, ran into the sea and were choked (Mark 5:13). The verse says the herd was choked, not that the spirits were.
SO WHAT: Jesus gave them leave when all the devils had to beseech him, so the man they held was not the one who had to be stronger; if you feel you have failed, Colossians 2:10 says you are complete in him, the head of all principality and power.
WHY MOST CHURCHGOERS HAVE NOT HEARD IT: The line they remember is "Come out." The later line, that Legion must ask and Jesus gives leave, is passed over. Freshness: 6
HOW IT LANDS ON JESUS: A legion does not steer this meeting. They beseech, and Jesus is the one who gives leave.
YOUR CONFIDENCE: 9

Go home to thy friends
HOOK: What does Jesus call the man at the end?
VERSES:
Mark 5:3 "Who had his dwelling among the tombs; and no man could bind him, no, not with chains:"
Mark 5:9 "And he asked him, What is thy name? And he answered, saying, My name is Legion: for we are many."
Mark 5:19 "Howbeit Jesus suffered him not, but saith unto him, Go home to thy friends, and tell them how great things the Lord hath done for thee, and hath had compassion on thee."
LABEL: DETAIL
WHAT IT DOES NOT PROVE: It does not prove every believer's task is to tell a personal story. It does not show the friends' reply, and it does not say Jesus gave the man a new personal name. No verse here records one.
FACTS:
- His dwelling had been among the tombs (Mark 5:3).
- The only name asked and answered in the scene is "Legion," explained "for we are many" (Mark 5:9).
- Jesus' words to him are "Go home to thy friends," then tell them the Lord's great things, "and hath had compassion on thee" (Mark 5:19).
- Mark 5:1-20 never speaks a personal name for the man.
SO WHAT: Jesus sent the man whose dwelling was among the tombs home to his friends and said the Lord had had compassion on him, so he spoke of him as a man among his own people; if you feel unseen, 1 Peter 5:7 says he careth for you.
WHY MOST CHURCHGOERS HAVE NOT HEARD IT: "Go home and tell" is taught as a duty, so "home" and "thy friends," said to a man who dwelt among the tombs, never get a hearing. Freshness: 7
HOW IT LANDS ON JESUS: Jesus does not send him on as Legion. He speaks home, friends, and the Lord's compassion over the man from the tombs.
YOUR CONFIDENCE: 8

Jesus suffered him not
HOOK: Why does Jesus say no to this man?
VERSES:
Mark 5:17 "And they began to pray him to depart out of their coasts."
Mark 5:18 "And when he was come into the ship, he that had been possessed with the devil prayed him that he might be with him."
Mark 5:19 "Howbeit Jesus suffered him not, but saith unto him, Go home to thy friends, and tell them how great things the Lord hath done for thee, and hath had compassion on thee."
Mark 3:14 "And he ordained twelve, that they should be with him, and that he might send them forth to preach,"
LABEL: DETAIL on Mark 5:18-19. ECHO on Mark 3:14 (same words "with him"; Mark never says the man was asking to be one of the twelve).
WHAT IT DOES NOT PROVE: It does not prove Jesus liked him less than the twelve, or that Jesus refused him care. The same answer that says "suffered him not" also says the Lord "hath had compassion on thee." The echo does not prove an apostleship request.
FACTS:
- The people prayed Jesus to depart out of their coasts (Mark 5:17).
- When Jesus was come into the ship, the man who had been possessed prayed that he might be with him (Mark 5:18).
- Jesus suffered him not (Mark 5:19).
- In that same reply Jesus tells him the Lord has done great things for him and has had compassion on him (Mark 5:19).
- Mark had used "be with him" for the twelve he ordained (Mark 3:14). He does not say this prayer is that same calling.
SO WHAT: Jesus suffered him not when he prayed to be with him, and told him the Lord had had compassion on him, so this no came with compassion; if you feel left out, Hebrews 13:5 says, "I will never leave thee, nor forsake thee."
WHY MOST CHURCHGOERS HAVE NOT HEARD IT: The man's "let me be with you" is hurried past so the talk can land on telling your story. Freshness: 8
HOW IT LANDS ON JESUS: Jesus can refuse the boat and still be the Lord who, in that same reply, has had compassion on him.
YOUR CONFIDENCE: 9

Hath had compassion on thee
HOOK: What does Jesus say this was about?
VERSES:
Mark 5:19 "Howbeit Jesus suffered him not, but saith unto him, Go home to thy friends, and tell them how great things the Lord hath done for thee, and hath had compassion on thee."
Mark 5:20 "And he departed, and began to publish in Decapolis how great things Jesus had done for him: and all men did marvel."
LABEL: DETAIL
WHAT IT DOES NOT PROVE: It does not, by itself, lay out the whole doctrine of the Trinity, and it does not say this healing was forecast by a named Old Testament verse. It does not say all men believed. Mark says they marvelled.
FACTS:
- Jesus tells the man to report "how great things the Lord hath done for thee, and hath had compassion on thee" (Mark 5:19).
- Jesus' own summary words here are "great things" and "compassion," not a retelling of the herd (Mark 5:19).
- The man published in Decapolis "how great things Jesus had done for him" (Mark 5:20).
- The hearers' recorded response is "all men did marvel" (Mark 5:20).
SO WHAT: Jesus said the Lord had had compassion on the man, and the man published how great things Jesus had done for him, so the compassion in this story wears Jesus' name; if you feel unseen, Psalm 145:8 says the LORD is full of compassion, and Psalm 145:9 says he is good to all.
WHY MOST CHURCHGOERS HAVE NOT HEARD IT: They leave with Legion and the sea, and miss that Jesus' own word for this is compassion, then Mark puts "Jesus" where Jesus had said "the Lord." Freshness: 8
HOW IT LANDS ON JESUS: What Jesus calls the Lord's great things and compassion, the man publishes as what Jesus did.
YOUR CONFIDENCE: 8

MY TOP PICK = Hath had compassion on thee
Jesus' own sentence names compassion and the Lord, and Mark says the man told that Jesus did it.
