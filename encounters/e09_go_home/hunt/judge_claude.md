**MY TOP 3**
1. **G**: Jesus said yes to the devils and the town but no to the healed man. That hook is fresh and true, and the Mark 3:14 echo deepens it. "A no with a purpose" is life-size. Fix the count first.
2. **C**: Jesus says "the Lord", the man says "Jesus", and Luke repeats the pair. It is a high-Christology detail in the text itself, and almost nobody has seen it.
3. **H**: He went from "dwelling among the tombs" to "Go home to thy friends". It is clean, true, says belonging and not just relief, and newcomers can follow it.

**REJECT: B.** It is a stretch dressed as fresh. The graves and swine in Isaiah 65:4 describe the rebellious people Paul sets against the Gentiles who "found" God (Romans 10:20-21). Then the SO WHAT quietly upgrades the ECHO into a claim about Jesus.

**MISSING (optional):** The same verb, "pray him", appears twice in a row. The town "began to pray him to depart" (5:17). The healed man "prayed him that he might be with him" (5:18). Two prayers side by side ask for opposite things: "go away" and "let me stay with you". This is a DETAIL. It could tighten G or stand alone.
