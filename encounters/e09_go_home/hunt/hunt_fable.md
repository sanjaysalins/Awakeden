# Fresh angle hunt - THE MAN AMONG THE TOMBS (Mark 5:1-20) - blind seat

Note for the checker: every Mark 5:1-20 quote below is an exact substring of the KJV pack.
Quotes from OUTSIDE the pack (Mark 4, Mark 5:21, 5:43, Mark 7, Luke 8, Isaiah 65, Psalm 68, Romans,
Ephesians, 2 Corinthians, Luke 19) are from memory and must be machine-checked against the KJV file.

---

## ANGLE 1

1. TITLE: He Said "The Lord." The Man Said "Jesus."

2. HOOK QUESTION: Jesus told him to say one name. Why did he say another?

3. THE VERSES
Mark 5:19 "tell them how great things the Lord hath done for thee"
Mark 5:20 "began to publish in Decapolis how great things Jesus had done for him"
Luke 8:39 "shew how great things God hath done unto thee"
Luke 8:39 "published throughout the whole city how great things Jesus had done unto him"
Mark 5:7 "Jesus, thou Son of the most high God"

4. LABEL
Mark 5:19 to Mark 5:20 - DETAIL
Luke 8:39 beside Mark 5:19-20 - DETAIL (same event, second Gospel, same swap: "God" then "Jesus")

5. WHAT IT DOES *NOT* PROVE
It does not prove the man had a full, worked-out belief about who Jesus is.
It does not say he disobeyed. The text only reports what he was told and what he then said.

6. FACTS
Jesus is the speaker of "the Lord hath done for thee" (Mark 5:19).
The narrator, not Jesus, reports that the man published what "Jesus had done" (Mark 5:20).
The same phrase "how great things" stands in both lines, one verse apart (Mark 5:19, 5:20).
Luke records the same pair, with "God" in Jesus's mouth and "Jesus" in the report (Luke 8:39).
Jesus adds a second thing to tell: "and hath had compassion on thee" (Mark 5:19).
Before this, the only voice in the scene to say who Jesus is was the unclean spirit (Mark 5:7).
The result: "all men did marvel" (Mark 5:20).

7. SO WHAT
Jesus called it what "the Lord hath done" and the man called it what "Jesus had done" - so the one who "had compassion on thee" in your shame is the Lord himself.
Verse for all believers: Romans 10:13 "For whosoever shall call upon the name of the Lord shall be saved."

8. WHY MOST CHURCHGOERS HAVE NOT HEARD IT
Sermons stop at the pigs or at "go and tell"; few people read verse 19 and verse 20 side by side.
Fresh: 8

9. HOW IT LANDS ON JESUS
The Bible sets "the Lord" and "Jesus" one line apart for the same act, so the viewer sees who Jesus is.

10. CONFIDENCE: 9

## ANGLE 2

1. TITLE: The Only One Jesus Told No

2. HOOK QUESTION: Why did Jesus say yes to devils and no to the healed man?

3. THE VERSES
Mark 5:12 "Send us into the swine, that we may enter into them"
Mark 5:13 "And forthwith Jesus gave them leave"
Mark 5:17 "And they began to pray him to depart out of their coasts"
Mark 5:18 "prayed him that he might be with him"
Mark 5:19 "Howbeit Jesus suffered him not"
Mark 5:19 "Go home to thy friends"

4. LABEL
All links - DETAIL (inside the passage)
2 Corinthians 12:8-9 as a side note only - ECHO (a believer asks, is not given it, and is given something else)

5. WHAT IT DOES *NOT* PROVE
It does not prove Jesus favours devils or that a "no" means he does not care.
It does not give a rule for why any one prayer today is not granted. The text gives no reason for the no; it only gives the job that came with it.

6. FACTS
Three groups ask Jesus for something in this scene: the devils (Mark 5:10, 5:12), the people (Mark 5:17), the healed man (Mark 5:18).
The devils get what they ask: "Jesus gave them leave" (Mark 5:13).
The people ask him to go, and the next verse has him "come into the ship" (Mark 5:17-18).
The healed man is the only one refused: "suffered him not" (Mark 5:19).
The same verse that refuses him gives him a task and a place: "Go home to thy friends, and tell them" (Mark 5:19).
What he asked for was closeness: "that he might be with him" (Mark 5:18).
He obeyed and "all men did marvel" (Mark 5:20).

7. SO WHAT
Jesus "suffered him not" and in the same breath said "Go home" - so when he does not give you what you asked, he has not turned you away; he has a purpose for you.
Verse for all believers: Romans 8:28 "And we know that all things work together for good to them that love God, to them who are the called according to his purpose."

8. WHY MOST CHURCHGOERS HAVE NOT HEARD IT
People remember the pigs, not the count of who asked and who got a yes.
Fresh: 6 (some preachers know "the three requests"; most viewers do not)

9. HOW IT LANDS ON JESUS
The one man who wanted only to be with Jesus is the one Jesus trusts with his message.

10. CONFIDENCE: 9

## ANGLE 3

1. TITLE: From the Tombs to "Go Home"

2. HOOK QUESTION: He lived with the dead. Who still had a home for him?

3. THE VERSES
Mark 5:3 "Who had his dwelling among the tombs"
Mark 5:5 "And always, night and day, he was in the mountains, and in the tombs"
Mark 5:19 "Go home to thy friends"
Mark 5:15 "sitting, and clothed, and in his right mind"
Psalm 68:6 "God setteth the solitary in families: he bringeth out those which are bound with chains"

4. LABEL
Mark 5:3 to Mark 5:19 - DETAIL (his "dwelling" at the start, "home" at the end)
Psalm 68:6 - ECHO (same shape and the same word "chains"; the Bible does not state the link)

5. WHAT IT DOES *NOT* PROVE
It does not tell us who his friends were, how long he had been away, or how they received him.
Psalm 68:6 is not called a prophecy of this man anywhere in the Bible.

6. FACTS
His dwelling was "among the tombs" (Mark 5:3).
He had been "often bound with fetters and chains" (Mark 5:4).
He was alone in the text: no one is with him in the mountains and tombs (Mark 5:5).
Jesus's words show he had a home and friends to go to (Mark 5:19).
He wanted to leave by ship with Jesus instead (Mark 5:18).
Jesus sent him back to his own people, and he then spoke across the region: "in Decapolis" (Mark 5:20).
The first word of the sending is not "tell" but "Go home" (Mark 5:19).

7. SO WHAT
Jesus found a man whose "dwelling" was "among the tombs" and said "Go home to thy friends" - so he does not only stop your pain, he gives you back a place where you belong.
Verse for all believers: Ephesians 2:19 "Now therefore ye are no more strangers and foreigners, but fellowcitizens with the saints, and of the household of God"

8. WHY MOST CHURCHGOERS HAVE NOT HEARD IT
"Go home and tell" is preached for the word "tell"; the word "home" is skipped.
Fresh: 7

9. HOW IT LANDS ON JESUS
Jesus is the one who takes a man out from among the dead and sends him home alive.

10. CONFIDENCE: 9 for the DETAIL, 6 for using Psalm 68:6 on screen (keep it as an echo only)

## ANGLE 4

1. TITLE: He Crossed a Storm for One Man

2. HOOK QUESTION: Why did Jesus sail through a storm, then sail straight back?

3. THE VERSES
Mark 4:35 "Let us pass over unto the other side"
Mark 5:1 "And they came over unto the other side of the sea"
Mark 5:2 "immediately there met him out of the tombs a man with an unclean spirit"
Mark 5:18 "And when he was come into the ship"
Mark 5:21 "And when Jesus was passed over again by ship unto the other side"
Isaiah 65:1 "I am found of them that sought me not"
Isaiah 65:4 "Which remain among the graves, and lodge in the monuments, which eat swine's flesh"
Romans 10:20 "I was found of them that sought me not"

4. LABEL
Mark 4:35 to Mark 5:21 (the round trip) - DETAIL
Isaiah 65:1 with Romans 10:20 - NT-STATED (Paul applies Isaiah's line to people who did not seek God; he does not name this scene)
Isaiah 65:4 with Mark 5 (graves + swine) - ECHO (the Bible does not state the link)

5. WHAT IT DOES *NOT* PROVE
The text never says Jesus crossed "only" for this man or states his reason for the trip. We may say only: in Mark's record, this man is the one thing that happens on that shore.
Isaiah 65 is not a forecast of the Gadarene man, and Romans 10:20 is not about him by name.

6. FACTS
The trip was Jesus's idea: he said "Let us pass over" (Mark 4:35).
A great storm came on the way over (Mark 4:37).
The man met him "immediately" when he left the ship (Mark 5:2).
Mark records no other healing, teaching or meeting on that side before Jesus is back in the ship (Mark 5:2-18).
The local people asked him to leave (Mark 5:17).
He crossed back again (Mark 5:21).
The man did not send for Jesus; nobody in the passage asks Jesus to come (Mark 5:1-2).
The man saw him "afar off" and ran to him (Mark 5:6).
The scene has both tombs and swine (Mark 5:3, 5:11), the same pair as Isaiah 65:4.

7. SO WHAT
Jesus "came over unto the other side of the sea" to a man nobody could reach - so if you feel unseen, you are not too far for him to come to.
Verse for all believers: Luke 19:10 "For the Son of man is come to seek and to save that which was lost."

8. WHY MOST CHURCHGOERS HAVE NOT HEARD IT
The storm (chapter 4) and the tombs (chapter 5) are taught as two separate stories, so the round trip is never seen.
Fresh: 7 for the round trip; 9 for the Isaiah 65 graves-and-swine line

9. HOW IT LANDS ON JESUS
Jesus is the one who makes the journey first, before the lost man can ask.

10. CONFIDENCE: 8 (must keep the wording "in Mark's record", never "the only reason")

## ANGLE 5

1. TITLE: The One Man Told to Tell

2. HOOK QUESTION: Jesus told others to keep quiet. Why not this man?

3. THE VERSES
Mark 5:19 "Go home to thy friends, and tell them"
Mark 5:43 "And he charged them straitly that no man should know it"
Mark 5:17 "And they began to pray him to depart out of their coasts"
Mark 5:20 "And he departed, and began to publish in Decapolis"
Mark 7:31 "through the midst of the coasts of Decapolis"
Mark 7:32 "And they bring unto him one that was deaf"

4. LABEL
Mark 5:19 beside Mark 5:43 (tell / do not tell, same chapter) - DETAIL
Mark 5:17 to Mark 5:20 (Jesus leaves, the man stays as his voice) - DETAIL
Mark 5:20 to Mark 7:31-32 (Decapolis again, now people bring someone to him) - ECHO (Mark does not say the man's telling caused the welcome)

5. WHAT IT DOES *NOT* PROVE
The text does not give Jesus's reason for "tell" here and "tell no man" there. Do not state a reason as fact.
It does not prove the later welcome in Decapolis was the result of this man's work.

6. FACTS
In the same chapter Jesus tells one family that no man should know (Mark 5:43) and tells this man to tell (Mark 5:19).
The people asked Jesus to go (Mark 5:17), and he went (Mark 5:18, 5:21).
The man stayed in the region that sent Jesus away (Mark 5:19-20).
He was told "thy friends"; he went wider, "in Decapolis" (Mark 5:19, 5:20).
The message he was given has two parts: what was done, and "compassion" (Mark 5:19).
Jesus is later in Decapolis again (Mark 7:31), and people there "bring unto him" a man and "beseech him" (Mark 7:32).
The man was sent the same day he was freed; no training is recorded (Mark 5:15-20).

7. SO WHAT
Jesus let them send him away, but he left behind a man to "tell them" of his "compassion" - so your worst years are not wasted; he can make them the story someone else needs.
Verse for all believers: 2 Corinthians 1:4 "Who comforteth us in all our tribulation, that we may be able to comfort them which are in any trouble"

8. WHY MOST CHURCHGOERS HAVE NOT HEARD IT
"Go and tell" is used as a general rule (kill list); few notice it is the opposite of what Jesus says in the same chapter, or that he comes back to Decapolis.
Fresh: 7

9. HOW IT LANDS ON JESUS
Jesus does not give up on the people who asked him to leave; he leaves them a living witness of his compassion.

10. CONFIDENCE: 8 for the DETAIL links, 5 for the Mark 7 echo (use as a quiet closing picture at most)

MY TOP PICK = He Said "The Lord." The Man Said "Jesus."
Why: it is two lines of plain KJV, one verse apart, backed by Luke 8:39, nothing to explain, and the whole surprise IS who Jesus is. Best closing act to pair with it: Angle 3's "Go home".
