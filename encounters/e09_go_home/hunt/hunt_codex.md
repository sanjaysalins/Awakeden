**1. TITLE: The Lord's Compassion Has A Name**

**HOOK QUESTION:** Why does Jesus say "the Lord," but the man say "Jesus"?

**THE VERSES**

Mark 5:19 "Howbeit Jesus suffered him not, but saith unto him, Go home to thy friends, and tell them how great things the Lord hath done for thee, and hath had compassion on thee."  
Mark 5:20 "And he departed, and began to publish in Decapolis how great things Jesus had done for him: and all men did marvel."  
Hebrews 4:14 "Seeing then that we have a great high priest, that is passed into the heavens, Jesus the Son of God, let us hold fast our profession."  
Hebrews 4:16 "Let us therefore come boldly unto the throne of grace, that we may obtain mercy, and find grace to help in time of need."

**LABEL:** DETAIL — the change from "the Lord" to "Jesus" within Mark 5:19–20.

**WHAT IT DOES NOT PROVE:** It does not mean every occurrence of "Lord" throughout the Bible refers to Jesus, or explain his relationship with the Father by itself.

**FACTS**

Jesus tells the man to report what "the Lord" has done, including compassion — Mark 5:19.  
The man reports what "Jesus" has done, repeating the wording "how great things" — Mark 5:19–20.  
Hebrews names Jesus before inviting believers to approach for mercy — Hebrews 4:14,16.

**SO WHAT:** Jesus has compassion on this man, and through him you can seek mercy after failure — Hebrews 4:14,16.

**WHY MOST CHURCHGOERS HAVE NOT HEARD IT:** My guess: the familiar lesson about telling your story draws attention away from the changed name; freshness **8/10**.

**HOW IT LANDS ON JESUS:** The man names Jesus as the one who has done the Lord's compassionate work.

**CONFIDENCE:** **9/10.**


**2. TITLE: A Home Beyond The Tombs**

**HOOK QUESTION:** Why does Jesus say "home" to someone dwelling among tombs?

**THE VERSES**

Mark 5:3 "Who had his dwelling among the tombs; and no man could bind him, no, not with chains:"  
Mark 5:19 "Howbeit Jesus suffered him not, but saith unto him, Go home to thy friends, and tell them how great things the Lord hath done for thee, and hath had compassion on thee."  
Hebrews 2:11 "For both he that sanctifieth and they who are sanctified are all of one: for which cause he is not ashamed to call them brethren,"

**LABEL:** DETAIL — "his dwelling" among the tombs beside Jesus' words "home" and "thy friends."

**WHAT IT DOES NOT PROVE:** We are not told who his friends were, why he lived apart, or how they received him. This does not promise every believer a restored former household.

**FACTS**

Mark identifies the tombs as the man's dwelling — Mark 5:3.  
Jesus directs him toward home and people he calls "thy friends" — Mark 5:19.  
Hebrews says Jesus is not ashamed to call those he sanctifies his brethren — Hebrews 2:11.

**SO WHAT:** Jesus sends this man home to his friends, and he is not ashamed to call you family if you belong to him — Hebrews 2:11.

**WHY MOST CHURCHGOERS HAVE NOT HEARD IT:** My guess: attention to the man's visible recovery can hide Jesus' words about his relationships; freshness **7/10**.

**HOW IT LANDS ON JESUS:** Jesus addresses the man as someone with a home and people to return to.

**CONFIDENCE:** **9/10.**


**3. TITLE: Jesus Refuses Someone He Has Helped**

**HOOK QUESTION:** Does Jesus' refusal mean his compassion has ended?

**THE VERSES**

Mark 5:18 "And when he was come into the ship, he that had been possessed with the devil prayed him that he might be with him."  
Mark 5:19 "Howbeit Jesus suffered him not, but saith unto him, Go home to thy friends, and tell them how great things the Lord hath done for thee, and hath had compassion on thee."  
Romans 8:35 "Who shall separate us from the love of Christ? shall tribulation, or distress, or persecution, or famine, or nakedness, or peril, or sword?"  
Romans 8:39 "Nor height, nor depth, nor any other creature, shall be able to separate us from the love of God, which is in Christ Jesus our Lord."

**LABEL:** DETAIL — the refusal and the instruction to speak of compassion occur in the same reply.

**WHAT IT DOES NOT PROVE:** It does not explain every unanswered prayer or promise that every refusal comes with an immediately visible assignment.

**FACTS**

The man's request is that he might be with Jesus — Mark 5:18.  
Jesus does not permit that request and instead directs him home — Mark 5:19.  
In that same answer, Jesus tells him to report the compassion he has received — Mark 5:19.  
Paul assures believers that suffering and created things cannot separate them from God's love in Christ — Romans 8:35,39.

**SO WHAT:** Jesus refuses this request while speaking of compassion, and if you belong to him, even loss cannot separate you from his love — Romans 8:35,39.

**WHY MOST CHURCHGOERS HAVE NOT HEARD IT:** My guess: the sending usually receives more attention than the refused request immediately before it; freshness **7/10**.

**HOW IT LANDS ON JESUS:** Jesus' compassion remains part of his answer even when he refuses the journey the man requests.

**CONFIDENCE:** **9/10.**


**4. TITLE: From Crying To Speaking Of Jesus**

**HOOK QUESTION:** What becomes of the voice heard among the tombs?

**THE VERSES**

Mark 5:5 "And always, night and day, he was in the mountains, and in the tombs, crying, and cutting himself with stones."  
Mark 5:19 "Howbeit Jesus suffered him not, but saith unto him, Go home to thy friends, and tell them how great things the Lord hath done for thee, and hath had compassion on thee."  
Mark 5:20 "And he departed, and began to publish in Decapolis how great things Jesus had done for him: and all men did marvel."  
2 Corinthians 1:4 "Who comforteth us in all our tribulation, that we may be able to comfort them which are in any trouble, by the comfort wherewith we ourselves are comforted of God."  
2 Corinthians 1:5 "For as the sufferings of Christ abound in us, so our consolation also aboundeth by Christ."

**LABEL:** DETAIL — Mark describes the man's crying and later gives the subject of his public speech.

**WHAT IT DOES NOT PROVE:** Mark does not say the man never cried again. This does not mean suffering exists to produce a story or that hurting people must speak publicly.

**FACTS**

Mark first describes the man crying night and day — Mark 5:5.  
Jesus gives him something specific to tell: the Lord's deeds and compassion — Mark 5:19.  
The man subsequently publishes what Jesus has done — Mark 5:20.  
Paul describes comfort received from God and consolation through Christ — 2 Corinthians 1:4–5.

**SO WHAT:** Jesus gives this crying man a message about compassion, and through him you can receive comfort in your loss — 2 Corinthians 1:4–5.

**WHY MOST CHURCHGOERS HAVE NOT HEARD IT:** My guess: the familiar visual changes receive more attention than the change in what the man voices; freshness **6/10**.

**HOW IT LANDS ON JESUS:** Jesus becomes the subject of the man's public words because of what he has done for him.

**CONFIDENCE:** **9/10** for the narrative observation; the passage does not describe his later emotional life.


**5. TITLE: Jesus Meets A Man From The Tombs**

**HOOK QUESTION:** Where else does Scripture put graves and swine together?

**THE VERSES**

Mark 5:2 "And when he was come out of the ship, immediately there met him out of the tombs a man with an unclean spirit,"  
Mark 5:11 "Now there was there nigh unto the mountains a great herd of swine feeding."  
Isaiah 65:4 "Which remain among the graves, and lodge in the monuments, which eat swine's flesh, and broth of abominable things is in their vessels;"  
Ephesians 2:13 "But now in Christ Jesus ye who sometimes were far off are made nigh by the blood of Christ."

**LABELS:**  
DETAIL — Jesus' arrival brings an immediate encounter with a man from the tombs; swine are nearby in the same scene.  
ECHO — Isaiah 65:4 also brings graves and swine together. Neither passage states that Mark's encounter fulfills Isaiah.

**WHAT IT DOES NOT PROVE:** Isaiah is not explicitly predicting this individual. Mark does not say anyone ate the swine or identify Isaiah as his source.

**FACTS**

The man meets Jesus immediately after Jesus comes out of the ship — Mark 5:2.  
The man comes out of the tombs — Mark 5:2.  
A herd of swine feeds near the mountains — Mark 5:11.  
Isaiah describes people remaining among graves and eating swine's flesh — Isaiah 65:4.  
Ephesians tells believers formerly far off that they have been brought near through Christ's blood — Ephesians 2:13.

**SO WHAT:** Jesus meets this man out of the tombs, and through him you can come near to God when you feel unseen — Ephesians 2:13.

**WHY MOST CHURCHGOERS HAVE NOT HEARD IT:** My guess: Isaiah's graves-and-swine passage is seldom placed beside this encounter; freshness **8/10**.

**HOW IT LANDS ON JESUS:** The stated event is Jesus meeting this man; Isaiah supplies a possible scriptural resonance within that encounter.

**CONFIDENCE:** **10/10** for the scene's details; **7/10** for an intentional Isaiah echo.
