## Script A (all Mark)

1. **Who for:** Curious newcomers who like a puzzle; Bible readers who never saw the three requests side by side.
2. **Take-away:** Jesus told him to tell what "the Lord" did; he told what "Jesus" did.
3. **Global:** Mostly yes. Short words, "asked" not "besought". But "suffered him not" needs its gloss (it has one) and "carrying compassion" is vague for a second-language listener.
4. **Accurate:** Yes. Every quote is exact Mark: "that he might be with him" (5:18), "Jesus suffered him not" (5:19), the Jesus line (5:19, cut cleanly before "and hath had compassion"), "how great things Jesus had done for him" (5:20). "In his right mind" = 5:15. Small soft spots: "The man said, Jesus" - it is Mark's report of what he published, not his quoted speech. "Asked" is weaker than the text ("besought", "pray him") but not wrong. "Two got a yes" counts the town's request as a yes - fair, He left.
5. **Best version?** No. Fix the last line: "carrying compassion" is abstract and not what the text says he carried. Say: "He sent him home to tell. And what he told was Jesus."

## Script B (all Mark)

1. **Who for:** Scrollers - the hook "Jesus said yes to devils" stops a thumb. Same Bible-reader bonus as A.
2. **Take-away:** Same as A (the Lord -> Jesus), but the last line blurs it.
3. **Global:** Yes. "Begged" is plain and strong. "He would not let him" is a clear gloss.
4. **Accurate:** Quotes all exact Mark. "Begged" fits "besought" (5:12) and is fair for "began to pray him" (5:17; Luke 8:37 says "besought"). One slip: "He asked to stay with Jesus" - Jesus was leaving, so the man asked to go WITH Him, not stay. Last line's "the Lord had compassion on him" is a fair paraphrase of 5:19.
5. **Best version?** No. The last line only repeats lines 4-6 and drops the Lord/Jesus pay-off it just made. Fix: end on the name - "He was sent to tell what the Lord had done. He told them about Jesus."

## Script C (all Luke)

1. **Who for:** Same scrollers as B, plus total newcomers - "God" vs "Jesus" needs no Bible knowledge to feel.
2. **Take-away:** Jesus said tell what God did; the man told what Jesus did. Clearest of the three.
3. **Global:** Best pay-off line for a global ear ("tell what God did. He told what Jesus did."). Cost: more old words - "suffered them", "thine own house", "shew". "Suffered" is glossed at once; "shew" sounds like "show" when spoken, so fine.
4. **Accurate:** Quotes exact Luke: "And he suffered them." (8:32), "that he might be with him" (8:38), the Jesus line (8:39), "how great things Jesus had done unto him" (8:39). "Told the whole city" = "published throughout the whole city". "Got back in the boat" = 8:37. No Mark/Luke mixing. Two problems:
   - Line 5: KJV is "but Jesus sent him away" (lower-case, mid-verse). A capital "But" inside the quote marks is not verbatim.
   - Line 9: "His no did not mean go away" contradicts the verse just quoted ("Jesus sent him away") and "It meant..." states a meaning the text does not give.
5. **Best version?** Not yet - close. Fix line 9 (see below).

## The 2 line edits for C

**Edit 1 - line 9 (biggest win).**
- Now: `His no did not mean go away. It meant go home, and tell. And what he told was Jesus.`
- Change to: `He was sent away. But not with nothing. He was sent home to tell. And what he told was Jesus.`
- Why: stops fighting "sent him away", drops the claimed meaning, keeps the good last five words.

**Edit 2 - line 5 (verbatim fix).**
- Now: `(pause) "But Jesus sent him away."`
- Change to: `(pause) But "Jesus sent him away."`
- Why: "But" moves outside the quote marks, so the quoted words are exact KJV.
