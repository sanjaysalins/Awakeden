# Watch-and-tell answer (Sonnet seat)

## 1. Who / What / Why (me)
- WHO: Jesus, a man freed from devils, a town, the devils.
- WHAT: Jesus let the devils go into pigs. The town told Him to leave. The healed man wanted to go with Him. Jesus said no, and sent him home to tell people.
- WHY: The man's job was to tell his own people what God did for him. Staying close was not the plan.

Could N say all three? **No.** N gets a rough WHO and WHAT. WHY is blurry.
- N does not know who "devils" are, why they "begged", or why pigs. The man is "set free" from what?
- The town leaving, the boat, and "the Lord" vs "Jesus" are unexplained.

## 2. Lines that need explaining
- "Jesus said yes to devils." Why would He? N may think Jesus is on the devils' side. The line is a hook, but N has no setup to read it as a twist.
- "The devils begged to go into the pigs." N has no idea what this means: spirits inside animals?
- "The town begged Him to leave. He got in the boat." Why would a town send a helper away? No reason is given (they lost their pigs, they were afraid). N cannot guess.
- "that he might be with him." / "Jesus suffered him not." R knows "suffered" means "allowed". N may hear "suffered" as pain, and "suffered him not" as nonsense. A hard line.
- "thy friends" and "hath done": archaic. Fine to hear once, but they stack up.
- "Jesus said, 'the Lord.' The man said, 'Jesus.' It's right there in the text." This is the key twist, but N cannot follow it. Who is "the Lord"? Is it a different person? N may hear two different people. R may get it (Jesus pointed to God, the man named Jesus), but N will not. "It's right there in the text" also sounds like an argument with someone N cannot see.
- Last line: "how the Lord had compassion on him" is new wording that does not appear before. Compassion was never set up.

## 3. First 3 seconds
Question opened: "Why did Jesus say yes to devils, and no to the man He freed?" That is a real, strong hook. Did the end answer it? Only half. The answer is "He sent him to tell, not stay." But the "no" has no emotional or logical reason given beyond the verse. N is left with a puzzle that resolves into a rule, not an insight. **Score 5/10** (hook strong, payoff thin for N, decent for R).

## 4. Main take-away
Being sent to tell people what Jesus did for you can matter more than staying near Him.

## 5. Who is it for?
Bible readers who half know this story (R). A newcomer will be lost. Not really built for a global ESL audience yet.

## 6. Scores
- N follows it: **3/10**
- R learns something new: **6/10** (the Lord/Jesus swap is a real, neat observation, if R catches it)

## 7. Lands on Jesus without feeling forced?
**6/10.** Jesus is the centre throughout, so it is not forced. But it ends on a text-study point, not a feeling. The "compassion" line is warm but arrives late and from nowhere. It is not preachy.

## 8. The ONE change
Fix the confusing "Lord / Jesus" line, and give N the missing setup first.
- Old: "Jesus said, 'the Lord.' The man said, 'Jesus.' It's right there in the text."
- New: "Jesus said, 'Tell them what God did for you.' The man told them what JESUS did for him. He knew who Jesus was."

(Also worth a one-line setup early: "A man was held by evil spirits. Jesus set him free." That alone would help N a lot.)

## 9. OVERALL
**5/10.** Strong hook and a smart insight for Bible readers, but too much is assumed for a newcomer or ESL listener, and the key twist line is unclear.
