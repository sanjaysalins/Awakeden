# Watch-and-tell: Sonnet seat

## 1. Who / What / Why (me)
- WHO: Jesus, a man He healed, devils, a town.
- WHAT: Jesus let devils go into pigs. The town told Him to leave. The healed man begged to come along. Jesus said no, go home and tell.
- WHY: Jesus's "no" was a job, not a rejection. The man told about Jesus.
- Could N say all three? WHO: mostly. WHAT: partly. WHY: only the last two lines. Answer: NO, not fully (yes on "a man was told to go home and tell").

## 2. Lines that need explaining
- "Jesus said yes to devils." N does not know what devils are here, or why a yes to them is shocking. No setup.
- "The devils begged to go into the pigs." N does not know why pigs, or that the devils lived in the man. Never said the man was possessed.
- "And he suffered them." Archaic KJV. "Suffered" sounds like pain. Means "allowed". The next line "He let them" helps, but it comes after.
- "The town begged Him to leave." Why? (The pigs died.) Not said. N sees no cause.
- "He got back in the boat." What boat? Where?
- "'that he might be with him.'" "Might be with him" is stiff English.
- "shew how great things God hath done unto thee." Old English (shew, hath, thee). N may not parse it.
- "Return to thine own house." Old words.
- "He told what Jesus did. It's right there in the text." R gets "text". N: what text? Sounds like homework, not a story.
- "His no did not mean go away." Good idea, but "no" is the hook word and the payoff is a bit abstract for N.

## 3. First 3 seconds
Question opened: "Why would Jesus say yes to devils and no to a good man?" Strong, odd, makes you stay. Did the end answer it? Yes, in the last two lines: the no meant "go home and tell." Score 7 (answered, but the answer comes late and fast).

## 4. Main take-away
When Jesus says no to what you want, it may be because He has a different job for you: go home and tell what He did.

## 5. Who is it for
Bible readers and curious believers who have met a "no" from God. For N, it is mostly confusing.

## 6. Scores
- N follows: 4. (Missing: the man was full of devils, pigs died, why the town feared.)
- R learns something new: 7. (Seeing that God vs Jesus in "tell what God did / he told what Jesus did" is a quiet proof of Jesus being God is fresh. But it is said fast.)

## 7. Lands on Jesus without preaching
8. The last line "what he told was Jesus" is earned by the text. Not preachy. Slightly clipped because "It's right there in the text" sounds like an argument.

## 8. ONE change
Add one plain line after the hook so N knows the setup.
Replace: "The devils begged to go into the pigs."
With: "The devils lived inside a man. They begged to go into a herd of pigs."
(Also swap "And he suffered them." to keep the KJV word once but follow right away with "He let them." It already does. Fine.)

## 9. OVERALL
6 / 10. Strong hook and a fresh landing. A newcomer is lost in the middle because the man's story is never set up.
