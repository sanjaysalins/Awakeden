1. **WHO:** Jesus, demons (devils), a healed man, and a frightened town. 
**WHAT:** Jesus granted the requests of the demons and the town, but denied the healed man's request to stay with Him, sending him home to share his story instead. 
**WHY:** It reveals that a "no" from Jesus is often a redirection to a greater purpose, and equates what Jesus did with what God did.
**Could N say all three?** No. (N might grasp the "Who" and "What," but the "Why" relies on theological nuances and abrupt context that would likely lose them).

2. **For N:** `"Return to thine own house, and shew..."` N (as an ESL speaker) would likely be confused by the archaic words "thine" and "shew". 
**For R:** `"Jesus said yes to devils."` R might initially balk at this phrasing and need the immediate context of the pigs to not reject the video outright as heretical, though the script does resolve this quickly.

3. **Question opened:** Why would Jesus agree to the demands of demons but reject a man He just saved?
**Did the end answer it?** Yes, by explaining the "no" was actually a specific mission to go home and witness.
**Score:** 9/10.

4. **The main take-away:** Jesus's "no" is not a rejection but a purposeful redirection, and telling others what Jesus did is the exact same as telling them what God did.

5. **Who is this video for?** Regular Bible readers (R) who know the story of the Gadarene demoniac but have likely glossed over the contrast of the three "begs" and the subtle text shift between "God" and "Jesus."

6. **N follows it:** 4/10. (The lack of backstory, rapid jumps from devils to pigs to boats, and King James English make it a tough watch for a newcomer).
**R learns something new:** 8/10. (The framing of the three requests and the specific God/Jesus wordplay is a fresh, compelling angle on a familiar text).

7. **Does it land on Jesus without feeling forced or preachy?** 9/10. It feels like an organic, "aha!" literary discovery ("It's right there in the text") rather than a forced sermon application.

8. **The ONE change that would most improve it:** 
*Quote the line:* `[jesus] "Return to thine own house, and shew how great things God hath done unto thee."`
*New line:* `[jesus] "Return to your own house, and show what great things God has done for you."` (Removing the archaic English keeps the exact same meaning but prevents N from stumbling over the vocabulary).

9. **OVERALL:** 8/10. Brilliant hook and excellent textual insight, but slightly held back by using an older Bible translation in a fast-paced, modern vertical video format.
