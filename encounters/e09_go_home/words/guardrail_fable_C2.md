## The 5 questions

**1. Who is it for?**
A newcomer who has never read this story, and a Bible reader who has never noticed the God / Jesus swap in verse 39. Both get something.

**2. Main take-away**
Jesus told the man to tell what God had done. The man told what Jesus had done. The "no" was really a sending: go home and tell.

**3. Global audience?**
Mostly yes. Short sentences, plain words, "begged" three times gives an easy shape to follow.
Weak spots: "Same deeds. Two names." is a little cryptic for a second-language viewer, and the pigs are never finished (a newcomer does not learn what happened to them or why the people wanted Jesus gone).

**5. Best version possible?**
Close. The hook is strong and honest, and the last line pays off the "no". Three things hold it back:
- "The town" is not what Luke says (must fix).
- The hook's first half ("yes to devils") opens a gap that is never closed. The film pays off the "no" only. One short clause on the pigs would help.
- "Two names" is the key beat and it is the least clear line for a second-language viewer.

## MUST-FIX (1)

1. **Line 4, "The town begged Him to leave."** Luke 8:37 says the whole multitude of the country round about.
   Fix: "The people of that country begged Him to leave."

## SHOULD-FIX (not blocking)

- Line 2: "ruled" -> "had". Fix: "This man had devils for a long time." (matches v27 exactly, simpler word)
- Line 3: finish the pigs. Fix: "Jesus let them. The pigs ran into the lake and drowned." (v33) — only if the runtime allows.
- Line 9: "Two names." -> plainer. Fix: "Same deeds. He said God. The man said Jesus."
- Optional: v37 gives the reason the people asked ("taken with great fear"). It is in the text, so it may be said: "They were afraid."
