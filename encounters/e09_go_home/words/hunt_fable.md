# The man who wanted to stay - one blind draft

Spine: the only "no" in the story went to the healed man, and the "no" sent him home with words to tell. The Lord / Jesus lines are the payoff of that one spine.

## (a) Script

[narrator] Three asked Jesus for something. Two got a yes. The no went to the man He had just healed.

[narrator] The devils asked to go into the pigs. Jesus let them.

[narrator] The town asked Him to leave. He got in the boat.

[narrator] Then the man, now in his right mind, asked "that he might be with him."

[narrator] "Jesus suffered him not." He did not let him. (pause)

[jesus] "Go home to thy friends, and tell them how great things the Lord hath done for thee."

[narrator] So he went. And he told "how great things Jesus had done for him."

[narrator] Jesus said, the Lord. The man said, Jesus. It's right there in the text.

[narrator] Jesus did not take him along. He sent him home, carrying compassion.

Voice note: the text gives the healed man no quoted words after he is healed, so there is no [man] line. I did not invent one.

## (d) Four lines

- Who it is for: anyone, anywhere, with no Bible knowledge, plus Bible readers who only remember the pigs and "Legion".
- Main take-away: Jesus said no to the one man who asked to stay with Him, and sent him home to tell what the Lord had done. The man told what Jesus had done.
- The curiosity gap: why would Jesus say yes to devils and to a town that wanted Him gone, but no to the man He had just healed?
- Why a global newcomer gets it: short plain sentences, three simple asks in a row, one old word ("suffered") said again in plain English, and the two key lines set side by side so the viewer sees the swap for themselves.
