# Watch-and-tell answer (Sonnet, newcomer / ESL lens)

## 1. Who / What / Why
- WHO: Jesus. A man who had devils in him. A town.
- WHAT: Jesus freed the man. The devils went into pigs. The town told Jesus to leave. The man asked to go with Jesus. Jesus said no, go home and tell. The man told the whole city about Jesus.
- WHY: Jesus did not send the man away to punish him. He sent him to tell others.
- Could N say all three? WHO yes. WHAT mostly yes. WHY partly. Answer: **no, not fully** (the "why" is shaky).

## 2. Lines that need explaining
- "Jesus said yes to devils. And no to the man He set free." N does not know why a good Jesus says yes to devils. It is a riddle, and it is not solved until later.
- "Devils" and "tombs": N may not know what a tomb is, or what "ruled" means here.
- "The devils begged to go into the pigs. Jesus let them." No reason is given. N is confused: why pigs? Why did Jesus allow it?
- "The town begged Him to leave." Why? The pigs are never shown to be lost, so the town's fear makes no sense.
- "Now in his right mind" - an idiom. N may not get it.
- "that he might be with him" - who is "him"? Unclear.
- "shew how great things God hath done unto thee" - old English (shew, hath, thee, thine).
- "Same deeds. Two names. It's right there in the text." Hard. "Two names" means God and Jesus are the same. N has no frame for this. R gets it; N does not.

## 3. First 3 seconds
Question opened: "Why would Jesus say yes to devils and no to a freed man?" The end half-answers it: the "no" was really a "go and tell". The "yes to devils" is never explained. Score: **6/10**.

## 4. Take-away
When Jesus tells you to tell what God did, telling what Jesus did is the same thing, because Jesus is God.

## 5. Who is it for
A Bible reader who knows the story and wants a fresh angle. Less so for a newcomer.

## 6. Scores
- N follows it: **5/10**
- R learns something new: **7/10**

## 7. Lands on Jesus without feeling forced
**8/10.** The ending is quiet and comes from the text. The "God = Jesus" claim is a bit heavy for N, but it does not preach.

## 8. The ONE change
Quote: "Jesus said: tell what God did. He told what Jesus did. Same deeds. Two names. It's right there in the text."
New line: "Jesus said: tell what God did. The man told what Jesus did. To him, Jesus and God were one."
Why: "Two names" and "in the text" are vague for N. Plain words make the point land.

## 9. OVERALL
**6/10.** A strong hook and a clever ending, but it moves too fast in the middle. N loses the thread at the pigs and the town, and the "same deeds, two names" turn is too compressed.
