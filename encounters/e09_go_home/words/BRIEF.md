# Write ONE narration from scratch - "The man who wanted to stay" (Mark 5:1-20, Luke 8:38-39). You are ONE blind writer; you have seen no other draft.

Do not use tools, do not read files, do not ask questions. Output your answer text only. The KJV text is at the bottom.

## The series
"The Encounter": one moment with Jesus, a detail most people read past, what it shows about Him, and one true thing it means for you. Every episode lands on Jesus. Everything must come from the Bible text.

## Who it is for
Worldwide, many with English as a second language, many with no Bible knowledge. Also Bible readers who think they know this story (the wild man, "Legion", the pigs).

## Length (hard)
UNDER 60 SECONDS. 105-125 spoken words total, including quotes. Vertical short.

## The two details to build on (both checked against the KJV below)
1. **The asks.** In this story the devils ask Jesus for something and get a yes (5:12-13). The people of the town ask Him to leave and get a yes - He gets in the boat (5:17-18). The healed man asks to stay with Him, "that he might be with him", and gets the only no: "Jesus suffered him not" - He sends him home instead (5:18-19).
   Careful: the devils also plead in 5:7 and 5:10. Do NOT say "only three requests". Say who asked, not a count, or say "three asked Him".
2. **The words.** Jesus tells him: "tell them how great things the Lord hath done for thee" (5:19). Mark reports he told "how great things Jesus had done for him" (5:20). Luke says it even more plainly: "shew how great things God hath done unto thee" / "how great things Jesus had done unto him" (Luke 8:39).
   Careful: the text puts the two lines side by side. It does not say what the man understood, and it does not explain it. Do not say "he knew Jesus was God" or "Mark is proving". Let the viewer see the two lines. You may say plainly: "It's right there in the text."

## Pick ONE spine and hold it from first line to last
Your choice, as long as it stays true: the "no" that became a mission; the man who was told "the Lord" and said "Jesus"; or both, with one leading. One spine only. Do not list facts. Do not retell the whole story - a newcomer needs only enough to follow.

## Hard rules
1. First 3 seconds: plain words that open a curiosity gap a stranger must close by watching.
2. KJV quotes verbatim and short. Never let an old word ("suffered", "shew", "publish") carry the meaning alone - say it in plain English too.
3. Honest: no invented feelings, motives, numbers, places or details beyond the text. Do not say why Jesus said no beyond what 5:19 says. Do not say the man "loved" Jesus or was "sad" - the text does not say it.
4. Do not centre the told-a-million-times parts: "Legion = many demons", the pigs drowning, chains could not hold him, the town was afraid, "go and tell" as a generic sermon. A few words of setup are fine.
5. The ending lands on Jesus, earned by your spine, using a word from the passage. Grace, not pressure. No stock closer ("come to Jesus", "just ask Him", "He's waiting", "will you...?"). No fear. No gain/loss. You may close with one line for the viewer, but say what Jesus did first, and do not promise the viewer anything the text does not.
6. Voices: one line per voice: [narrator], [jesus], [man]. Quotes spoken by a character get that character's voice. Mark one (pause).
7. No wording about AI or how the film is made. No hashtags. Write for the ear.

## Output (only this)
(a) the script; (b) spoken word count; (c) KJV fragments with verse refs; (d) 4 lines: who it is for, main take-away, the curiosity gap, why a global newcomer gets it.
