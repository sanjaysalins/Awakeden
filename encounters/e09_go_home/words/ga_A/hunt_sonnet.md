# Watch-and-tell answer (seat: Sonnet)

## 1. Who / What / Why
- WHO: Jesus, a man who was sick in his mind (devils in him), and a town.
- WHAT: Jesus healed the man. The man begged to go with Jesus. Jesus said no. He sent him home to tell his people what God did.
- WHY: Jesus' "no" was a job, not a rejection. The man became the first messenger in his own town.
- Could N say all three? NO. N gets "Jesus said no, go home and tell." N does not get who the devils are, why pigs, why the town sent Jesus away, or why "no" is good news.

## 2. Lines that need explaining
- "Three asked Jesus for something. Two got a yes." Only the devils and the town are named; the "two yes" is not clear. Devils' ask = yes; town's ask = yes (He left); man = no. N has to count in their head, fast, in a second language.
- "The devils asked to go into the pigs. Jesus let them." N: what devils? why pigs? what happened to the pigs? Nothing shown or said about the healing itself.
- "The town asked Him to leave." N: why? (The pigs died. Never said.)
- "now in his right mind" - idiom. N may not know the man was mad or sick.
- "Jesus suffered him not." Archaic KJV. The next line explains it, which helps. R fine. N is lost for a second.
- "that he might be with him" - fine, quoted.
- "Jesus said, the Lord. The man said, Jesus. It's right there in the text." This is the weakest line. N cannot parse it. Who said what? R may get it (Mark says "the Lord", the man tells "Jesus"), but it is a text-nerd point, not a story point.
- "carrying compassion" - abstract, odd phrase. N: carrying what? Compassion for whom?

## 3. First 3 seconds
Opened: "Who asked, who said no, and why was the no for the healed man?" Good hook, a clear puzzle: the man got the no.
Did the end answer it? Partly. "He sent him home" answers what, but not why the no was better. "Carrying compassion" is vague. Score: 5/10.

## 4. Main take-away
When Jesus says no to what you want, he may be giving you a bigger job: go home and tell people what he did for you.

## 5. Who is it for
People who feel Jesus did not answer their prayer. Also new believers who want to "serve" far away but have family at home. Bible readers like a small fresh detail.

## 6. Scores
- N follows it: 4/10
- R learns something new: 6/10 (the "Lord" vs "Jesus" detail and the three-asks frame are fresh; the frame is clever)

## 7. Lands on Jesus without feeling forced
6/10. It is about Jesus the whole time, and no preaching. But the last line is a vague moral and the "Lord/Jesus" line makes Jesus' identity a text-trick, not a feeling. The point that the man called Jesus "Lord" is strong but is said too fast and sideways.

## 8. The ONE change
Replace the weak last two lines:
- Old: "Jesus said, the Lord. The man said, Jesus. It's right there in the text." / "Jesus did not take him along. He sent him home, carrying compassion."
- New: "Jesus told him: tell them what the Lord did. The man told them what JESUS did. To him, they were the same." / "Jesus said no to the boat. He said yes to the mission. Go home. Start there."
(Also add one short line early: "The man had been cruel and broken. Jesus made him well." so N knows what was healed.)

## 9. OVERALL
5/10. Strong opening puzzle and a real, fresh Bible detail. Hurt by missing backstory (what the man suffered, why the town was afraid), an archaic quote, and a muddy second-to-last line that N cannot follow. Fix the middle and the ending and it is a 7.
