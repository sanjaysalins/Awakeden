## (a) Script

[narrator] Three asked Jesus for something. Two got a yes. One got a no. (pause)
[narrator] Evil spirits begged to enter pigs. Yes. The townspeople begged Him to leave. Yes. He stepped into the boat.
[narrator] Then the man He had just healed begged to come too. "That he might be with him."
[narrator] Jesus did not let him. He said:
[jesus] "Go home. Tell them how great things the Lord hath done for thee, and hath had compassion on thee."
[narrator] Tell them what the Lord did for you. He felt for you.
[narrator] So he went, and told it everywhere. Mark writes his words:
[man] "How great things Jesus had done for him."
[narrator] Jesus said, "the Lord." The man said, "Jesus." (pause) It's right there in the text.

## (d) Four lines

- Who it is for: newcomers and second-language English viewers, plus Bible readers who think they know the pig story.
- Main take-away: Jesus said no to the man's wish, and gave him a message instead. The man said the name: Jesus.
- Curiosity gap: "Three asked Jesus. Two got a yes. One got a no." Who got the no, and why does it matter?
- Why a global newcomer gets it: a simple yes, yes, no pattern, then two short lines side by side ("the Lord" and "Jesus"). Each hard KJV word is followed at once by plain English.
