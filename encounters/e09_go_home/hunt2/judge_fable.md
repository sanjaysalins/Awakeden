## MY TOP 3

1. ANGLE E - The only card whose whole point is inside the passage, in one verse, word for word. A newcomer can say it back in one sentence, and it lands on who Jesus is without any help. Weakness: the 1 Samuel anchor adds little, and the SO WHAT is a bit abstract.
2. ANGLE A - The deepest link on the page. Isaiah 65:1-4 and Luke 8 really do change each other, and Paul himself quotes the Isaiah line. It speaks to being unseen. Weakness: three passages in 60 seconds, and the rebels-versus-victim gap must be said out loud.
3. ANGLE F - Fresh, and the house-to-house frame (8:27 to 8:39) is real text. The take-away about belonging is life-size. Weakness: the chains do not come off the way the title says; it works only if the script keeps to "no house, then a house".

## THE ONE I REJECT

ANGLE G. The shared words are a common idiom, not a link. It sets a grieving mother beside a cry that comes from devils, and then hangs "no condemnation" on that cry. It is the most likely card to teach something the text does not say. (ANGLE I is the next weakest: thin link, common take-away.)

## MISSING ANGLE (optional)

DETAIL: the question in the boat is answered on the shore. The disciples ask "What manner of man is this!" (Luke 8:25) and nobody answers; three verses later the first voice on land says "Jesus, thou Son of God most high" (Luke 8:28) - the same title the angel gave before his birth, "shall be called the Son of the Highest" (Luke 1:32). The friends ask; the enemy answers.
