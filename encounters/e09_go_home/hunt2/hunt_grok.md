**1. TITLE:** He Broke The Bands And Was Still Driven

**2. HOOK:** If he broke the chains, why was he still driven?

**3. ANCHOR-1:** Luke 8:29 "he brake the bands, and was driven of the devil into the wilderness."
**ANCHOR-2:** Isaiah 61:1 "to proclaim liberty to the captives, and the opening of the prison to them that are bound"

**3b. WHY-VERSE:** Luke 4:21 "This day is this scripture fulfilled in your ears."
Luke 4:18 is the reading Jesus had just given: "to preach deliverance to the captives... to set at liberty them that are bruised." That verse joins Isaiah 61 to Jesus' mission. It does not name the man in the tombs.

**4. LABEL:** NT-STATED

**5. WHAT IT DOES NOT PROVE:** It does not prove Luke 8 is the day Isaiah 61 was fulfilled. Jesus said "this day" in Nazareth. It does not prove broken iron is liberty, or that every bound person is this man.

**6. FACTS:**
- The man had had devils a long time (Luke 8:27).
- Oftentimes the spirit caught him (Luke 8:29).
- He was kept bound with chains and fetters (Luke 8:29).
- He broke the bands and was still driven into the wilderness (Luke 8:29).
- Jesus had commanded the unclean spirit to come out of the man (Luke 8:29).
- Afterward the man was clothed and in his right mind (Luke 8:35).
- In Luke's order, the Nazareth reading (Luke 4) comes before this lake crossing (Luke 8).

**7. SO WHAT:** He brake the bands and was still driven of the devil, and then Jesus commanded the unclean spirit out and the man was in his right mind: Jesus came to set at liberty them that are bruised, so the failure of your own strength is where his liberty meets you (Luke 4:18).

**8. WHY MOST CHURCHGOERS HAVE NOT HEARD IT:** Sermons use the chains to say he was too strong to hold, and pass over the next words, that he broke them and was still driven. Freshness: 7

**9. HOW IT LANDS ON JESUS:** Jesus had already taken Isaiah's captive-liberty words as his own errand, and in this scene his command, not the broken bands, is what left the man in his right mind.

**10. CONFIDENCE:** 7

**1. TITLE:** He Said God. The Man Said Jesus.

**2. HOOK:** Why did the man say Jesus when Jesus said God?

**3. ANCHOR-1:** Luke 8:39 "how great things God hath done unto thee" / "how great things Jesus had done unto him."
**ANCHOR-2:** John 14:10 "the Father that dwelleth in me, he doeth the works."

**3b. WHY-VERSE:** No verse says John 14:10 is why this man spoke, or why Luke set the two clauses together. The join is ECHO. The film may show both clauses. It may not say the man was teaching a doctrine.

**4. LABEL:** ECHO

**5. WHAT IT DOES NOT PROVE:** It does not prove the man explained the Trinity, or that Luke hid a code in the verse. It does not prove every act told about Jesus is understood by the speaker.

**6. FACTS:**
- Jesus told him to shew what God had done (Luke 8:39).
- Luke says the man published what Jesus had done (Luke 8:39).
- The man had asked to stay with Jesus (Luke 8:38).
- Jesus sent him away to his own house (Luke 8:38-39).
- He published it throughout the whole city (Luke 8:39).
- The same verse does not say he disobeyed.

**7. SO WHAT:** Jesus said shew how great things God hath done unto thee, and the man published how great things Jesus had done unto him: the Father that dwelleth in him doeth the works, and you were called to shew forth his praises when you have felt unseen (John 14:10; 1 Peter 2:9).

**8. WHY MOST CHURCHGOERS HAVE NOT HEARD IT:** The usual telling stops on "what God hath done" and does not read the matching clause about Jesus. Freshness: 7

**9. HOW IT LANDS ON JESUS:** The act Jesus called God's, the man published as Jesus' act, and Jesus' own words elsewhere say the Father, dwelling in him, does the works.

**10. CONFIDENCE:** 8 on the two clauses in Luke 8:39. 6 on John as their meaning. Overall: 7

**1. TITLE:** They Begged Not To Go Into The Deep

**2. HOOK:** What is the deep the devils begged to escape?

**3. ANCHOR-1:** Luke 8:31 "command them to go out into the deep."
**ANCHOR-2:** Revelation 20:3 "cast him into the bottomless pit"

**3b. WHY-VERSE:** None. No verse says this plea is the pit of Revelation 20. Same Greek word, *abyssos*, is a standard lexicon fact: KJV has "the deep" here and "bottomless pit" in Revelation. That is word identity, not a stated fulfillment.

**4. LABEL:** ECHO

**5. WHAT IT DOES NOT PROVE:** It does not prove Jesus sent these devils into the pit. He suffered them to enter the swine (Luke 8:32). It does not prove they are the dragon, or that Romans 10:7 ("descend into the deep," meaning bringing Christ up from the dead) is the same plea.

**6. FACTS:**
- "Legion" is explained in the text as many devils in one man (Luke 8:30).
- Those devils besought him not to command them into the deep (Luke 8:31).
- They also besought him to let them enter the swine (Luke 8:32).
- He suffered the swine request (Luke 8:32).
- Luke never says he sent them into the deep.
- The herd ran into the lake and were choked (Luke 8:33).

**7. SO WHAT:** The devils besought Jesus not to command them into the deep, and the man was found at his feet in his right mind: Jesus gives his own eternal life, and they shall never perish, neither shall any man pluck them out of his hand — that is his answer to your fear (John 10:28).

**8. WHY MOST CHURCHGOERS HAVE NOT HEARD IT:** Tellings follow Mark, where the plea is about being sent out of the country, so Luke's "deep" is rarely read. Freshness: 8

**9. HOW IT LANDS ON JESUS:** Unclean spirits beg Jesus about where they may be commanded to go, and the man they had held sits at Jesus' feet.

**10. CONFIDENCE:** 7

**1. TITLE:** Graves And Swine Named Together

**2. HOOK:** Why do tombs and pigs show up together?

**3. ANCHOR-1:** Luke 8:27 "but in the tombs" and Luke 8:32 "an herd of many swine"
**ANCHOR-2:** Isaiah 65:4 "remain among the graves" and "eat swine's flesh"

**3b. WHY-VERSE:** None. Romans 10:20-21 quotes Isaiah 65:1-2 about Gentiles and about Israel. It does not quote Isaiah 65:4, and it does not mention this man. Label stays ECHO.

**4. LABEL:** ECHO

**5. WHAT IT DOES NOT PROVE:** It does not prove Isaiah predicted Gadara, that this man ate swine, or that the herd's death fulfilled Isaiah. Isaiah 65:4 charges a people who stay among graves. Luke gives a different cause for this man.

**6. FACTS:**
- The man wore no clothes and did not live in a house, but in the tombs (Luke 8:27).
- He had had devils a long time (Luke 8:27).
- He was driven of the devil into the wilderness (Luke 8:29).
- Luke does not say he chose the tombs.
- A herd of many swine was feeding on the mountain (Luke 8:32).
- Luke does not say the man ate them.
- Jesus sent him to his own house (Luke 8:39).

**7. SO WHAT:** Jesus met the man who ware no clothes and abode in the tombs, and they found him clothed and in his right mind: him that cometh to Jesus he will in no wise cast out, and that is his word over your shame (John 6:37).

**8. WHY MOST CHURCHGOERS HAVE NOT HEARD IT:** Isaiah 65:4 is almost never set beside this story, because Romans quotes the verses just above it and not this one. Freshness: 8

**9. HOW IT LANDS ON JESUS:** Jesus went to the tombs, where a man lived among graves near a herd of swine, and that man was found at Jesus' feet, clothed and in his right mind.

**10. CONFIDENCE:** 6

**1. TITLE:** Driven Of The Devil Into The Wilderness

**2. HOOK:** Who else was in the wilderness with the devil?

**3. ANCHOR-1:** Luke 8:29 "driven of the devil into the wilderness."
**ANCHOR-2:** Matthew 4:1 "led up of the Spirit into the wilderness to be tempted of the devil."

**3b. WHY-VERSE:** None. No verse says the two wilderness scenes explain each other. Matthew 4 is a different event, not a retelling of Luke 8.

**4. LABEL:** ECHO

**5. WHAT IT DOES NOT PROVE:** It does not prove the man was tempted as Jesus was, that possession and temptation are the same, or that Jesus was driven by the devil. Matthew says the Spirit led Jesus. Luke says the devil drove this man.

**6. FACTS:**
- "Oftentimes" the man was caught, bound, and driven into the wilderness (Luke 8:29).
- This was his state before they found him healed (Luke 8:29, 8:35-36).
- Jesus commanded the unclean spirit to come out (Luke 8:29).
- They found him sitting at the feet of Jesus, clothed, and in his right mind (Luke 8:35).
- Matthew 4:1 is Jesus' own temptation at the start of his public work, not this crossing.

**7. SO WHAT:** The man was driven of the devil into the wilderness, and afterward he sat at the feet of Jesus in his right mind: Jesus hath suffered being tempted, and he is able to succour them that are tempted, so he meets you in your fear (Hebrews 2:18).

**8. WHY MOST CHURCHGOERS HAVE NOT HEARD IT:** The wilderness line is read as background color for how wild the man was, not beside Jesus' own wilderness. Freshness: 6

**9. HOW IT LANDS ON JESUS:** The man the devil drove into the wilderness is found at the feet of Jesus, the one who was led into the wilderness to be tempted and who succours the tempted.

**10. CONFIDENCE:** 7
