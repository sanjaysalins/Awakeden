# Fresh angle hunt - Luke 8:22-39 (the storm and the man from the tombs)

Blind seat. All quotes checked against data/kjv_full. Labels are given per link, honestly.
Four of my five angles rest on an ECHO for the Luke link. Only Angle 1 has a stronger label behind its headline.

---

## ANGLE 1

1. TITLE: Found by a Man Who Never Asked

2. HOOK QUESTION: Why did Jesus cross a lake for a man who told Him to go away?

3. ANCHOR-1: Luke 8:27 "there met him out of the city a certain man, which had devils long time, and ware no clothes, neither abode in any house, but in the tombs."
   ANCHOR-2: Isaiah 65:1 "I am sought of them that asked not for me; I am found of them that sought me not: I said, Behold me, behold me, unto a nation that was not called by my name."
   Extra (same chapter, the surprise): Isaiah 65:4 "Which remain among the graves, and lodge in the monuments, which eat swine's flesh"

3b. WHY-VERSE: Romans 10:20 "But Esaias is very bold, and saith, I was found of them that sought me not; I was made manifest unto them that asked not after me."

4. LABEL:
   - Isaiah 65:1 -> Romans 10:20 = PROPHECY (Paul quotes Isaiah and applies it to people who were not looking for God).
   - Luke 8 -> Isaiah 65:1 = ECHO (Luke does not quote Isaiah. The shape matches: a man who did not seek, and was found).
   - Luke 8 -> Isaiah 65:4 (graves + swine) = ECHO (same two pictures, tombs and swine, six verses apart in one chapter).
   Headline must rest on the PROPHECY link (God said He would be found by people who never asked), with this scene shown as a picture of it, not as "the fulfilment".

5. WHAT IT DOES *NOT* PROVE:
   - It does not prove Isaiah foretold THIS man or this shore. Luke never says so.
   - Luke never calls the man a Gentile. Do not state it as fact. (Swine in the district and "over against Galilee" are all the text gives.)
   - Isaiah 65:2-4 (graves, swine) describes a rebellious people; Romans 10:21 says "But to Israel he saith" of verse 2. So we may not say "Isaiah 65:4 is about the Gadarenes". It is a picture match only.

6. FACTS:
   - Jesus chose the trip. He said "Let us go over unto the other side of the lake." (Luke 8:22)
   - The man lived in the tombs, not in a house. (Luke 8:27)
   - The man's first words to Jesus were not a request for help: "What have I to do with thee ... I beseech thee, torment me not." (Luke 8:28)
   - Luke says those words came after Jesus had commanded the spirit to come out. (Luke 8:29)
   - A herd of swine was feeding there. (Luke 8:32)
   - Luke records one person helped on that shore. (Luke 8:27-36)
   - Then Jesus "went up into the ship, and returned back again." (Luke 8:37)
   - At the end the same man "besought him that he might be with him." (Luke 8:38)
   - Nobody in the passage asks Jesus to come to that shore. (Luke 8:22-27 - none is recorded)

7. SO WHAT: Jesus crossed the lake and "met" a man who never asked for Him - He finds people before they look for Him, so being unseen by everyone else does not mean you are unseen by Him.
   Verse for all believers: Romans 10:20 (above); also Ephesians 2:13 "But now in Christ Jesus ye who sometimes were far off are made nigh by the blood of Christ."

8. WHY NOT HEARD: Sermons stay on the demons and the pigs; almost no one reads Isaiah 65 next to this shore. FRESH: 8

9. LANDS ON JESUS: God said "I am found of them that sought me not", and here is Jesus, stepping out of a boat to a man in the tombs who did not seek Him.

10. CONFIDENCE: 8 (the PROPHECY link is solid; the Luke link is honest ECHO and must be worded as a picture, not a fulfilment)

## ANGLE 2

1. TITLE: The Deep the Devils Feared

2. HOOK QUESTION: What is "the deep" that even the devils begged not to enter?

3. ANCHOR-1: Luke 8:31 "And they besought him that he would not command them to go out into the deep."
   ANCHOR-2: Romans 10:7 "Or, Who shall descend into the deep? (that is, to bring up Christ again from the dead.)"

3b. WHY-VERSE: Romans 10:7 itself - "(that is, to bring up Christ again from the dead.)" It says the deep is where Christ was brought up FROM. No verse links it to Luke 8, so the link is ECHO.
   Supporting: Revelation 20:3 "And cast him into the bottomless pit, and shut him up, and set a seal upon him"

4. LABEL: ECHO. (Plain lexicon fact: "the deep" in Luke 8:31 and Romans 10:7, and "the bottomless pit" in Revelation 20:1-3, are the same Greek word, abyssos. The Bible does not tie the three verses together.)
   Because it is an ECHO it cannot be the headline claim on its own. The headline must stay on the scene: the devils beg, Jesus decides.

5. WHAT IT DOES *NOT* PROVE:
   - It does not prove what Jesus did or where He was between the cross and the resurrection. Romans 10:7 only says He was brought up "from the dead".
   - It does not prove the swine or the lake were "the deep". The devils asked NOT to go to the deep; the herd went into the lake. Two different things.
   - No timetable of the end from Revelation 20.

6. FACTS:
   - The devils, not the man, do the begging here: "they besought him". (Luke 8:31)
   - They beg twice: not the deep (Luke 8:31), then the swine (Luke 8:32).
   - Jesus only "suffered them". They could not go without His leave. (Luke 8:32)
   - Three groups "besought" Jesus in this scene: the devils (8:31-32), the whole multitude (8:37), the healed man (8:38).
   - The herd went into "the lake", not "the deep". (Luke 8:33)
   - In Revelation the devil is shut into the bottomless pit by an angel with a key. (Revelation 20:1-3)

7. SO WHAT: The devils begged Jesus not to send them to "the deep", yet Jesus Himself went down among the dead and was brought up again - so the fear of death does not have to hold you.
   Verse for all believers: Hebrews 2:14-15 "that through death he might destroy him that had the power of death, that is, the devil; And deliver them who through fear of death were all their lifetime subject to bondage."

8. WHY NOT HEARD: Verse 31 is one line between "Legion" and the pigs, and the English hides that it is the same word as "bottomless pit". FRESH: 7

9. LANDS ON JESUS: The place the devils feared most is the place Jesus entered by dying, and God raised Him out of it.

10. CONFIDENCE: 6 (the word fact is sound; the link is only an ECHO, and it is easy to overclaim)

## ANGLE 3

1. TITLE: One Verse, Two Calms

2. HOOK QUESTION: Why does Luke put a wild sea and a wild man side by side?

3. ANCHOR-1: Luke 8:24 "Then he arose, and rebuked the wind and the raging of the water: and they ceased, and there was a calm."
   ANCHOR-1b: Luke 8:35 "sitting at the feet of Jesus, clothed, and in his right mind"
   ANCHOR-2: Psalms 65:7 "Which stilleth the noise of the seas, the noise of their waves, and the tumult of the people."
   Extra: Psalms 89:9 "Thou rulest the raging of the sea: when the waves thereof arise, thou stillest them."

3b. WHY-VERSE: No verse says Luke 8 fulfils Psalm 65. The meaning verse is Psalm 65:7 itself - it puts the sea and the people in ONE line as things God stills. Label stays ECHO.

4. LABEL: ECHO (Psalm 65:7 and 89:9 are praise about what God does, not a forecast. Shared word: "raging". Shared shape: sea first, then people.)
   This is NOT the kill-list Psalm 107 link, and the point is not "He has power over storms". The point is the pairing: sea, then a person.

5. WHAT IT DOES *NOT* PROVE:
   - It does not prove the Psalm predicted this day, or that Luke had Psalm 65 in mind.
   - "The tumult of the people" in the Psalm is about peoples/nations, not one possessed man. We may only say the two calms sit in the same order.
   - It does not promise that every trouble in a viewer's mind will be stilled at once.

6. FACTS:
   - The two events are back to back: calm on the lake (8:24), then "they arrived" (8:26), then the man (8:27).
   - The water is called "raging" (8:24). The man broke chains and was "driven" (8:29).
   - After Jesus speaks, the lake is "a calm" (8:24) and the man is "sitting ... in his right mind" (8:35).
   - Both calms make people afraid: the disciples "being afraid wondered" (8:25); the townspeople "were afraid" (8:35).
   - The disciples ask "What manner of man is this!" (8:25). The next person to speak to Jesus answers it: "Jesus, thou Son of God most high" (8:28).
   - The Psalms say it is the LORD who does this: "O LORD God of hosts, who is a strong LORD like unto thee?" (Psalms 89:8), then verse 9.

7. SO WHAT: Jesus made "a calm" on the lake and then left a man "in his right mind" - He stills what is raging outside you and what is raging inside you, so fear is not the end of your story.
   Verse for all believers: Philippians 4:7 "And the peace of God, which passeth all understanding, shall keep your hearts and minds through Christ Jesus."

8. WHY NOT HEARD: The storm and the man are almost always preached as two separate sermons on two separate Sundays. FRESH: 6

9. LANDS ON JESUS: The Psalm says God stills the seas and the tumult of the people, and in one trip Jesus does both.

10. CONFIDENCE: 7

## ANGLE 4

1. TITLE: Naked, Afraid, Then Clothed

2. HOOK QUESTION: Who gave the naked man his clothes?

3. ANCHOR-1: Luke 8:27 "which had devils long time, and ware no clothes, neither abode in any house, but in the tombs."
   ANCHOR-1b: Luke 8:35 "sitting at the feet of Jesus, clothed, and in his right mind"
   ANCHOR-2: Genesis 3:10 "I heard thy voice in the garden, and I was afraid, because I was naked; and I hid myself."
   ANCHOR-2b: Genesis 3:21 "Unto Adam also and to his wife did the LORD God make coats of skins, and clothed them."

3b. WHY-VERSE: No verse links Luke 8 to Genesis 3. Label ECHO. The verse that gives the meaning for a believer is Galatians 3:27 "For as many of you as have been baptized into Christ have put on Christ."

4. LABEL: ECHO (same shape: naked, afraid, among death - then God clothes. Same word "clothed" in Genesis 3:21 and Luke 8:35.)
   Inside it, a DETAIL: Luke tells us he wore no clothes (8:27) and then that he was "clothed" (8:35), and never says where the clothes came from.

5. WHAT IT DOES *NOT* PROVE:
   - It does not prove the man is "a new Adam" or that Luke meant Genesis 3.
   - The text does not say who gave the clothes. We must not say "Jesus gave him His own cloak" or "the disciples did". Say only: he met Jesus naked and was found clothed.
   - It does not say the man's state was caused by his own sin. Luke does not say that.

6. FACTS:
   - Before: no clothes, no house, lived in the tombs. (Luke 8:27)
   - After: sitting, clothed, in his right mind, at the feet of Jesus. (Luke 8:35)
   - Luke does not say where the clothes came from. (Luke 8:27-35)
   - In Genesis the man hid because he was naked and afraid. (Genesis 3:10)
   - In Genesis it is the LORD God who makes the coats and clothes them. (Genesis 3:21)
   - In Luke 8 the man is told to go back to a house: "Return to thine own house". (Luke 8:39) So all three losses in verse 27 are reversed: clothes, mind, house.

7. SO WHAT: The man met Jesus with no clothes and was found "clothed" at His feet - Jesus covers what shame has stripped, so you do not have to hide.
   Verse for all believers: Galatians 3:27 (above); also Isaiah 61:10 "he hath clothed me with the garments of salvation".

8. WHY NOT HEARD: "Clothed" is read as a small proof that he was healed, not as the first thing God did for the first ashamed people. FRESH: 6

9. LANDS ON JESUS: In the garden the LORD God clothed the ashamed, and on this shore a naked man is found clothed at the feet of Jesus.

10. CONFIDENCE: 6 (true to both texts, but it is an ECHO and the film must not fill the gap about the clothes)

## ANGLE 5

1. TITLE: A Host Drowned, A Captive Freed

2. HOOK QUESTION: Where else in the Bible does an enemy army drown in the sea?

3. ANCHOR-1: Luke 8:33 "the herd ran violently down a steep place into the lake, and were choked."
   ANCHOR-1b: Luke 8:39 "shew how great things God hath done unto thee."
   ANCHOR-2: Exodus 14:28 "And the waters returned, and covered the chariots, and the horsemen, and all the host of Pharaoh that came into the sea after them; there remained not so much as one of them."
   ANCHOR-2b: Exodus 14:31 "And Israel saw that great work which the LORD did upon the Egyptians: and the people feared the LORD"

3b. WHY-VERSE: No verse links Luke 8 to the Red Sea. Label ECHO. The verse that gives the meaning for a believer is Colossians 1:13 "Who hath delivered us from the power of darkness, and hath translated us into the kingdom of his dear Son:"

4. LABEL: ECHO (same shape, four steps in the same order: wind and water obey God; a crossing; the captor's host goes down into the water; the people who see it are afraid and the great work is told.)

5. WHAT IT DOES *NOT* PROVE:
   - It does not prove Luke meant a second Exodus, or that the swine "are" Pharaoh's army.
   - "Legion" is a Roman army word (plain lexicon fact), but the text makes no point about Rome. Do not make one.
   - The devils are not said to be destroyed in the lake. Luke says the herd "were choked". Nothing more.

6. FACTS:
   - Wind and water obey: "he commandeth even the winds and water, and they obey him" (Luke 8:25); "the LORD caused the sea to go back by a strong east wind" (Exodus 14:21).
   - The name "Legion" is given "because many devils were entered into him". (Luke 8:30)
   - The man had been "kept bound with chains and in fetters". (Luke 8:29)
   - The herd went into the lake and were choked. (Luke 8:33) Pharaoh's host was covered by the sea. (Exodus 14:28)
   - Fear follows both: "they were taken with great fear" (Luke 8:37); "the people feared the LORD" (Exodus 14:31).
   - Jesus says "how great things God hath done"; the man tells "how great things Jesus had done". Luke lets both stand. (Luke 8:39) Israel saw "that great work which the LORD did". (Exodus 14:31)
   - Difference to keep honest: Israel then believed (Exodus 14:31); the Gadarenes asked Jesus to leave (Luke 8:37).

7. SO WHAT: Jesus let the "Legion" go down into the lake and sent the man home free - He does not make peace with what holds you, He sets you free from it and gives you somewhere to go.
   Verse for all believers: Colossians 1:13 (above).

8. WHY NOT HEARD: The pigs are usually treated as a money problem or an odd detail, not set beside the Red Sea. FRESH: 7

9. LANDS ON JESUS: The "great work which the LORD did" at the sea is the kind of work the freed man says "Jesus had done" for him.

10. CONFIDENCE: 5 (the pattern is real but nothing in the Bible states it; safest as a short picture inside another angle, not as a film on its own)

## Honest note on what I left out
- Psalm 44:23 "Awake, why sleepest thou, O Lord? arise" next to "awoke him ... Then he arose" (Luke 8:24): a true word match, but too close to the kill-list "He slept / He is God" sermon.
- Isaiah 61:1 (liberty to the bound): Jesus claims it in Luke 4, but that is the same book, and it is the kill-list "He frees the bound" point.

MY TOP PICK = Found by a Man Who Never Asked - it is the only angle with a link the New Testament itself states (Isaiah 65:1 -> Romans 10:20), the tombs-and-swine match in Isaiah 65:4 is a real surprise, and the scene shows it plainly: nobody sent for Jesus, and He came anyway.
