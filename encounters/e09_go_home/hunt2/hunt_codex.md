Two angles clear the gate. I would stop at two.

**1. The Name Changes in One Verse**

**HOOK QUESTION:** Why does one verse call this both God’s work and Jesus’ work?

**ANCHOR-1 — Luke 8:39:** “Return to thine own house, and shew how great things God hath done unto thee. And he went his way, and published throughout the whole city how great things Jesus had done unto him.”

**ANCHOR-2 — John 14:10:** “the Father that dwelleth in me, he doeth the works.”

**WHY-VERSE — John 14:10:** “the Father that dwelleth in me, he doeth the works.” Jesus explicitly explains the connection between his works and the Father’s work.

**LABEL:** NT-STATED.

**WHAT IT DOES NOT PROVE:** The wording does not make Jesus and the Father the same person. John explains the relationship between their works; he does not explicitly cite this encounter.

**FACTS:**

- Jesus tells the man to describe what God has done — Luke 8:39.
- The narrator describes the man reporting what Jesus has done — Luke 8:39.
- Both descriptions occur within the same verse — Luke 8:39.
- Jesus says the Father dwelling in him does the works — John 14:10.
- Jesus can save all who come to God through him — Hebrews 7:25.

**SO WHAT:** Jesus did God’s work for the man, so feeling unseen need not keep you from coming to God through him.

Viewer warrant — **Hebrews 7:25:** “he is able also to save them to the uttermost that come unto God by him”.

**WHY MOST CHURCHGOERS HAVE NOT HEARD IT:** My guess: teaching often concentrates on the instruction to tell, passing over the change from “God” to “Jesus.” **Freshness: 8/10.**

**HOW IT LANDS ON JESUS:** Jesus explains that the Father is working in what he does.

**CONFIDENCE:** 9/10.

**2. The Report Calls It Healing**

**HOOK QUESTION:** Why does Luke say this man was “healed”?

**ANCHOR-1 — Luke 8:36:** “They also which saw it told them by what means he that was possessed of the devils was healed.”

**ANCHOR-2 — Acts 10:38:** “How God anointed Jesus of Nazareth with the Holy Ghost and with power: who went about doing good, and healing all that were oppressed of the devil; for God was with him.”

**WHY-VERSE — Acts 10:38:** “who went about doing good, and healing all that were oppressed of the devil”. Acts explicitly places Jesus’ help for people oppressed by the devil within his work of healing and doing good.

**LABEL:** NT-STATED.

**WHAT IT DOES NOT PROVE:** This supplies no modern medical diagnosis and establishes no rule that mental illness means possession. It also does not declare the man morally faultless.

**FACTS:**

- The man is described as “clothed, and in his right mind” — Luke 8:35.
- The account calls what happened to him healing — Luke 8:36.
- Acts describes people affected by the devil as “oppressed” and Jesus’ healing as “doing good” — Acts 10:38.
- Believers are invited to approach for mercy and help when they need it — Hebrews 4:16.

**SO WHAT:** Jesus healed the suffering man, so your shame need not silence your request for mercy and help.

Viewer warrant — **Hebrews 4:16:** “Let us therefore come boldly unto the throne of grace, that we may obtain mercy, and find grace to help in time of need.”

**WHY MOST CHURCHGOERS HAVE NOT HEARD IT:** My guess: the dramatic departure of the devils receives more attention than Luke’s quiet description of the man as healed. **Freshness: 7/10.**

**HOW IT LANDS ON JESUS:** Jesus’ action is explicitly described as doing good to someone suffering oppression.

**CONFIDENCE:** 9/10.

MY TOP PICK = The Name Changes in One Verse  
The surprise is visible in one KJV verse, and Jesus himself supplies its meaning in John.
