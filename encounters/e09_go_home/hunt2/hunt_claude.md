# Luke 8:22-39: five fresh angles

## 1. Two storms, one voice

**HOOK:** Which God hushes the sea and wild people too?

**ANCHOR-1:** Luke 8:24 "rebuked the wind and the raging of the water: and they ceased, and there was a calm"

**ANCHOR-2:** Psalm 65:7 "Which stilleth the noise of the seas, the noise of their waves, and the tumult of the people."

**WHY-VERSE:** Psalm 65:7 itself puts both kinds of calm in one sentence, done by God. No NT verse ties it to Luke 8, so this is ECHO only.

**LABEL:** ECHO. Inside Luke 8 there is also a DETAIL: the calm sea is followed straight away by a calm man.

**DOES NOT PROVE:**
- That Luke built the chapter on Psalm 65.
- That Psalm 65 foretold this day.
- Psalm 65:7 speaks of "the people" (crowds and nations), not of one man.

**FACTS:**
- The storm comes first, then they land in the Gadarenes' country (8:23-26).
- The man was wild: he broke chains and was driven into the wilderness (8:29).
- Afterwards he is "sitting... in his right mind" (8:35).
- The disciples ask "What manner of man is this!" (8:25).
- Next, the man calls him "Son of God most high" (8:28).

**SO WHAT:** Jesus hushed the "raging" water and then a raging man. The calm he gives reaches inside you too, not just the weather around you.
- Backing verse: Philippians 4:7 "the peace of God, which passeth all understanding, shall keep your hearts and minds through Christ Jesus." (Fear)

**WHY UNHEARD:** Sermons split the storm and the tombs into two stories. Psalm 107 takes all the cross-ref attention. **Fresh: 7/10**

**LANDS ON JESUS:** What the Psalm says only God does, Jesus does twice in one day.

**CONFIDENCE:** 8/10

## 2. The deep the demons feared

**HOOK:** Why did the demons beg not to be sent "into the deep"?

**ANCHOR-1:** Luke 8:31 "they besought him that he would not command them to go out into the deep"

**ANCHOR-2:** Romans 10:7 "Who shall descend into the deep? (that is, to bring up Christ again from the dead.)"

**WHY-VERSE:** Romans 10:7. Its own bracket explains "the deep" as the place of the dead, from which Christ was brought up. Nothing ties it to Luke 8. "Deep" is the same Greek word (abyssos) in both, a plain lexicon fact.

**LABEL:** ECHO (same word).

**DOES NOT PROVE:**
- Any detailed teaching about where Christ went between the cross and the rising.
- That Luke had Romans in mind.
- Keep it to "same word, same place of dread".

**FACTS:**
- It is the devils, not the man, who beg (8:30-31).
- They ask for the swine instead, and he allows it (8:32).

**SO WHAT:** The devils begged to stay out of "the deep". Jesus went down among the dead himself and was brought back up, so death does not get the last word over you.
- Backing verse: Hebrews 2:14-15 "deliver them who through fear of death were all their lifetime subject to bondage." (Fear, loss)

**WHY UNHEARD:** Readers skip 8:31 to get to the pigs. **Fresh: 8/10**

**LANDS ON JESUS:** The place the dark powers dread is the place Jesus came back from.

**CONFIDENCE:** 6/10

## 3. "God did it." "Jesus did it."

**HOOK:** Jesus said "tell what God did". What did the man tell?

**ANCHOR-1:** Luke 8:39 "shew how great things God hath done unto thee... published throughout the whole city how great things Jesus had done unto him"

**ANCHOR-2:** 1 Samuel 12:24 "consider how great things he hath done for you."

**WHY-VERSE:** No verse links the two books. 1 Samuel 12:24 shows that "how great things" is a phrase about the LORD's acts.

**LABEL:**
- ECHO for the phrase across the two books.
- DETAIL for the swap from "God" to "Jesus" inside verse 39.

**DOES NOT PROVE:**
- That the man meant to say Jesus is God.
- That Jesus approved the wording. Jesus had already left (8:37).
- The swap is in Luke's own telling. Say only that.

**FACTS:**
- The man asked to go with Jesus (8:38).
- Jesus sent him home instead (8:38-39).
- He told the "whole city", the same city he once lived outside, in the tombs (8:27, 39).

**SO WHAT:** Jesus sent the man back to the very city that knew him as the man from the tombs. What Jesus does for you can be told as God's own work, even where you were once ashamed.
- Backing verse: 1 Peter 2:9 "that ye should shew forth the praises of him who hath called you out of darkness into his marvellous light." (Shame, purpose)

**WHY UNHEARD:** People read past one changed word. **Fresh: 6/10**

**LANDS ON JESUS:** The man's report puts Jesus' name where Jesus had said "God".

**CONFIDENCE:** 8/10

## 4. From the tombs to the student's seat

**HOOK:** Why does the Bible say he sat "at the feet"?

**ANCHOR-1:** Luke 8:35 "sitting at the feet of Jesus, clothed, and in his right mind"

**ANCHOR-2:** Acts 22:3 "brought up in this city at the feet of Gamaliel, and taught"

**WHY-VERSE:** Acts 22:3. There, "at the feet of" goes with being "taught", so it is a learner's place. No verse says this about Luke 8.

**LABEL:** ECHO.

**DOES NOT PROVE:**
- That the man was formally made a disciple.
- What Jesus taught him. Neither is stated.

**FACTS:**
- He lived "in the tombs" (8:27).
- Afterwards he is found sitting at Jesus' feet (8:35).
- He wanted to "be with him" (8:38).

**SO WHAT:** The man everyone chained, Jesus sat at his feet like a student. No one is too much of a mess to learn from him.
- Backing verse: Matthew 11:29 "learn of me; for I am meek and lowly in heart." (Shame, being unseen)

**WHY UNHEARD:** "At the feet" gets read as gratitude, not as the posture of a learner. **Fresh: 6/10**

**LANDS ON JESUS:** Jesus is the teacher who takes the outcast as his student.

**CONFIDENCE:** 7/10

## 5. Graves and swine: Isaiah's picture

**HOOK:** Did Isaiah describe a scene of graves and pigs centuries earlier?

**ANCHOR-1:** Luke 8:27, 32 "abode in any house, but in the tombs... an herd of many swine"

**ANCHOR-2:** Isaiah 65:4 "Which remain among the graves, and lodge in the monuments, which eat swine's flesh"

**WHY-VERSE:**
- Romans 10:20 "I was found of them that sought me not." This applies Isaiah 65:1 to people who never looked for God.
- No verse ties Isaiah 65:4 to Luke 8, so that link is ECHO.

**LABEL:**
- ECHO: Luke 8 and Isaiah 65:4.
- NT-STATED: Isaiah 65:1 and Romans 10:20.

**DOES NOT PROVE:**
- That Isaiah foretold this man.
- In Isaiah 65:2-4 the graves and swine describe "a rebellious people" (Romans 10:21 says Israel). Isaiah is not describing outsiders. Do not blur this.

**FACTS:**
- The man did not come looking for Jesus. He "met him" when Jesus landed (8:27).
- Jesus chose to go "over unto the other side" (8:22).

**SO WHAT:** Jesus crossed a storm to a man living among graves who never went looking for him. Being far off does not mean you are unseen.
- Backing verse: Ephesians 2:13 "ye who sometimes were far off are made nigh by the blood of Christ." (Being unseen)

**WHY UNHEARD:** Isaiah 65:4 is rarely read aloud. **Fresh: 9/10**

**LANDS ON JESUS:** Jesus is found by the man who "sought me not".

**CONFIDENCE:** 5/10. The context is easy to overclaim.

---

**MY TOP PICK = Two storms, one voice.** It is clean, the Psalm says it plainly, it is easy for newcomers, and it joins the two halves of the passage that sermons usually split.
