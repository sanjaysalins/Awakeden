# hunt_sonnet - Luke 8:22-39 - 5 fresh angles (blind seat: Sonnet)

All quotes checked against data/kjv_full.

---
## 1. TITLE: Chains, No House, Then "Thine Own House"
2. HOOK: The man had no house and no clothes. Where does the Bible say God fixes that?
3. ANCHOR-1: Luke 8:29 "he was kept bound with chains and in fetters" (with Luke 8:27 "neither abode in any house, but in the tombs" and Luke 8:39 "Return to thine own house")
   ANCHOR-2: Psalm 68:6 "God setteth the solitary in families: he bringeth out those which are bound with chains"
3b. WHY-VERSE: John 5:19 "for what things soever he doeth, these also doeth the Son likewise." (It says the Son does what the Father does. It does not name Psalm 68.)
4. LABEL: ECHO (same two acts: chains off, a home given). The why-verse is a general principle, not a statement about this link.
5. DOES NOT PROVE: That Psalm 68:6 is a forecast of this man, or that Luke was quoting it. Only that Jesus does the very work the Psalm says God does.
6. FACTS:
   - The man lived in the tombs, not in a house (Luke 8:27)
   - He had been bound with chains and fetters and broke the bands (Luke 8:29)
   - After the healing he was sitting clothed and in his right mind (Luke 8:35)
   - Jesus told him to go to his own house (Luke 8:39)
   - Psalm 68:5-6 describes God as doing both: chains off, the lonely set in families
7. SO WHAT: Jesus first took off the chains and sent the man to a house; so if you feel shut out and alone, he is the one the Psalm says puts the lonely in a family. Believer verse: Ephesians 2:19 "ye are no more strangers and foreigners, but fellowcitizens with the saints, and of the household of God".
8. WHY UNHEARD: Sermons focus on the demons and the pigs; the "house" word at the start and end is read past, and nobody holds it next to Psalm 68. FRESH: 7/10.
9. LANDS ON JESUS: Jesus does what Psalm 68 says only God does, and he does it for one lonely man.
10. CONFIDENCE: 7/10 (words match; the film must say "same work", not "fulfilled").

## 2. TITLE: They Woke Him. He Arose.
2. HOOK: The old prayer begged God to wake up. What happened when someone did?
3. ANCHOR-1: Luke 8:24 "they came to him, and awoke him" and "Then he arose, and rebuked the wind"
   ANCHOR-2: Psalm 44:23 "Awake, why sleepest thou, O Lord? arise, cast us not off for ever."
3b. WHY-VERSE: None says it. Label ECHO. (Nothing says the disciples had Psalm 44 in mind.)
4. LABEL: ECHO (same two verbs, awake and arise, in the same order).
5. DOES NOT PROVE: That the disciples were praying Psalm 44, or that the Psalm predicted this boat. Do not say Jesus "was God asleep"; the text says only that he fell asleep (Luke 8:23).
6. FACTS:
   - Jesus fell asleep as they sailed (Luke 8:23)
   - The disciples came and awoke him, saying "Master, master, we perish" (Luke 8:24)
   - He arose and rebuked the wind and the water; there was a calm (Luke 8:24)
   - Psalm 44:23 is a cry to the Lord to awake and arise
7. SO WHAT: When they woke him, Jesus got up and the sea went calm; so when God feels asleep and you feel unseen, calling is not wasted. Believer verse: Romans 10:13 "whosoever shall call upon the name of the Lord shall be saved."
8. WHY UNHEARD: Preachers use Jonah or "he slept" for his humanity; the Psalm's awake/arise words are rarely laid beside the boat. FRESH: 6/10. (Close to the kill-list "he slept" and "do not fear" lines, so the film must lead with the Psalm cry, not the storm.)
9. LANDS ON JESUS: The cry of the old Psalm, "arise", is answered in a boat by a man who arises when called.
10. CONFIDENCE: 7/10.

## 3. TITLE: Where Demons Begged Not To Go
2. HOOK: The spirits had one fear. Who did they ask to spare them?
3. ANCHOR-1: Luke 8:31 "they besought him that he would not command them to go out into the deep"
   ANCHOR-2: Revelation 20:3 "And cast him into the bottomless pit, and shut him up, and set a seal upon him" (Revelation 20:1 "having the key of the bottomless pit")
3b. WHY-VERSE: Revelation 1:18 "have the keys of hell and of death" (said by the one who "liveth, and was dead"). It says Christ holds keys of death and hell. It does not say the Gadarene spirits were headed to the pit.
4. LABEL: ECHO for the "deep = bottomless pit" tie (standard lexicon fact: the same Greek word, abyssos, is behind both KJV words). The claim "Christ holds the keys" is NT-STATED.
5. DOES NOT PROVE: Where those particular spirits ended up, or the timing of the end. No details of the pit beyond the verses.
6. FACTS:
   - The spirits asked Jesus not to command them into the deep (Luke 8:31)
   - Their request treats Jesus as the one who gives that command (Luke 8:31)
   - Revelation 20:1-3 shows the bottomless pit shut with a key and a seal
   - Revelation 1:18: the risen Christ has the keys of hell and of death
7. SO WHAT: The spirits asked Jesus for time, so the one who holds the keys is the one who answers to fear; if shame or fear of evil follows you, you are asking the right person. Believer verse: Romans 8:38-39 "neither death, nor life, nor angels, nor principalities, nor powers ... shall be able to separate us from the love of God, which is in Christ Jesus our Lord."
8. WHY UNHEARD: "The deep" reads like the lake; the link to the same word in Revelation is almost never made. FRESH: 7/10.
9. LANDS ON JESUS: The spirits begged him because he alone is the one who may give or hold the command.
10. CONFIDENCE: 6/10 (the word tie is sound; keep "keys" as the claim and avoid pit detail).

## 4. TITLE: Sent Away, Yet Already "With Him"
2. HOOK: The man begged to stay with Jesus. Why was "no" a gift?
3. ANCHOR-1: Luke 8:38 "besought him that he might be with him: but Jesus sent him away" (with Luke 8:35 "sitting at the feet of Jesus")
   ANCHOR-2: Mark 3:14 "he ordained twelve, that they should be with him, and that he might send them forth to preach" (plus Acts 4:13 "they had been with Jesus")
3b. WHY-VERSE: Mark 3:14 says the call to be with him and the sending are one calling for the twelve. It does not say the same of this man.
4. LABEL: ECHO (same pairing: be with him, then be sent).
5. DOES NOT PROVE: That the man became an apostle. Does not say Jesus' no was a punishment or a reward; the text gives no feeling.
6. FACTS:
   - The man was found sitting at the feet of Jesus (Luke 8:35)
   - He begged to be with Jesus; Jesus sent him away (Luke 8:38)
   - Jesus told him where to go and what to say (Luke 8:39)
   - He published throughout the city what Jesus had done (Luke 8:39)
   - The twelve were chosen to be with him and be sent (Mark 3:14)
7. SO WHAT: Jesus sat the man down at his own feet first, then sent him with a message; you do not have to earn a place near him before he gives you words. Believer verse: Acts 4:13 shows ordinary people marked by "they had been with Jesus".
8. WHY UNHEARD: The "go home and tell" angle is well known; the match to Mark 3:14's be-with-then-sent shape is not. FRESH: 5/10 (close to the kill-list "tell your story" line).
9. LANDS ON JESUS: He gives the man what the twelve were given, near him first and then a mission.
10. CONFIDENCE: 5/10.

## 5. TITLE: Found By Him Who Was Not Sought
2. HOOK: Nobody on that shore asked for Jesus. So why did he cross a storm?
3. ANCHOR-1: Luke 8:22 "Let us go over unto the other side of the lake" and Luke 8:28 "What have I to do with thee, Jesus"
   ANCHOR-2: Isaiah 65:1 "I am found of them that sought me not"
3b. WHY-VERSE: Romans 10:20 "I was found of them that sought me not; I was made manifest unto them that asked not after me." (Paul applies it to those who did not seek; it is not about this scene.)
4. LABEL: ECHO for the link to this scene. (Romans 10:20 is NT-STATED only for its own audience.)
5. DOES NOT PROVE: That the Gadarenes were Gentiles (Luke does not say), or that Isaiah 65 is about this man. Isaiah 65:2-4 rebukes Israel, so do not borrow that part.
6. FACTS:
   - Jesus chose the crossing (Luke 8:22)
   - The storm came during it (Luke 8:23)
   - The first person who met him on landing was the man from the tombs (Luke 8:27)
   - The man's first words were "torment me not", not a request for help (Luke 8:28)
   - Jesus then returned back across (Luke 8:37)
7. SO WHAT: Jesus went to the man who asked for nothing and drove straight through a storm to do it; if you never asked and feel unseen, he found you first.
8. WHY UNHEARD: The Isaiah 65:1 / Romans 10:20 "found by those who sought not" is usually taught on Gentiles, not on this crossing. FRESH: 6/10. (Near kill-list "one man is worth more than pigs", so lead with "who sought whom".)
9. LANDS ON JESUS: He is the one who seeks and finds.
10. CONFIDENCE: 4/10 (shape is true to the text but the link is loose; the weakest of the five).

---
MY TOP PICK = Chains, No House, Then "Thine Own House" - three plain words in the text (chains, house, house) match Psalm 68:6 exactly, it touches loneliness, and it is told in simple words a newcomer can repeat; just keep the label ECHO.
