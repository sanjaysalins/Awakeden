Rule: surprising about the TEXT, never about the TRUTH. No invented doctrine. An ECHO is never called a prophecy.

Labels used on the cards: PROPHECY (OT looks forward, NT says fulfilled) / NT-STATED (the NT makes the link; the

passage itself, usually read past).

- COMMON?: is this angle already in typical sermons or commentaries? yes / no / partly. Be honest; say where you have seen it.
  The KILL LIST below is the common-sermon list: an angle that matches or re-words an item on it is COMMON yes.

# Angles on Luke 8:22-39 (the storm and the man from the tombs)
Order is random. Every quote is checked word for word against the KJV. Each card names where the link comes from and what it does NOT prove.

## ANGLE A - He Crossed the Lake for a Man Who Never Asked
HOOK: Who asked Jesus to come to the tombs?
ANCHOR-1: Luke 8:22 "Let us go over unto the other side of the lake"
ANCHOR-2: Isaiah 65:1 "I am found of them that sought me not"
WHY-VERSE: Romans 10:20 "Esaias is very bold, and saith, I was found of them that sought me not"
LABEL: ECHO (Paul states the step from Isaiah 65:1 to Romans 10:20; nothing states the step from Luke 8 to Isaiah 65:1)
INSIGHT: Jesus picks the trip, the first person he meets has not asked for him, and three verses on in the same chapter Isaiah pictures people who "remain among the graves" (Isaiah 65:4 "Which remain among the graves, and lodge in the monuments, which eat swine's flesh") - tombs and swine, as in Luke 8:27 and 8:32.
DOES NOT PROVE: That Isaiah foretold this man or this shore. Isaiah 65:2-4 describes a rebellious people (Romans 10:21 says Israel), so the graves and swine are a picture match only. Luke never calls the man a Gentile, and no request for Jesus is recorded in 8:22-27 (the text is silent, not proof that none was made).
SO WHAT: Jesus crossed a storm to a man who had not asked for him, so feeling unseen does not mean he has not seen you. Believer verse: Luke 19:10 "For the Son of man is come to seek and to save that which was lost."

## ANGLE B - One Trip, Two Calms
HOOK: Why does the calm lake come right before the calm man?
ANCHOR-1: Luke 8:24 "rebuked the wind and the raging of the water: and they ceased, and there was a calm"
ANCHOR-2: Psalm 89:9 "Thou rulest the raging of the sea: when the waves thereof arise, thou stillest them"
WHY-VERSE: Psalm 65:7 "Which stilleth the noise of the seas, the noise of their waves, and the tumult of the people"
LABEL: ECHO (shared words "the raging of the"; Psalm 65:7 puts the stilled sea and the stilled people in one sentence, but no verse ties it to Luke 8)
INSIGHT: The point is not the storm alone; it is the order. A calm sea (Luke 8:24), then a man found "in his right mind" (Luke 8:35) - the two stillings Psalm 65:7 puts in one line, done by one visit.
DOES NOT PROVE: That the Psalms predicted this day or that Luke had them in mind. "The people" in Psalm 65:7 means peoples in general, not one man. It does not say the man's trouble was "like a storm"; that comparison is ours.
SO WHAT: Jesus quieted the water and then the man, so what rages inside you is not too big for the one who stilled the sea. Believer verse: Philippians 4:7 "And the peace of God, which passeth all understanding, shall keep your hearts and minds through Christ Jesus."

## ANGLE C - The Deep the Devils Feared
HOOK: What is "the deep" that the devils begged not to enter?
ANCHOR-1: Luke 8:31 "they besought him that he would not command them to go out into the deep"
ANCHOR-2: Romans 10:7 "Who shall descend into the deep? (that is, to bring up Christ again from the dead.)"
WHY-VERSE: Romans 10:7 "that is, to bring up Christ again from the dead"
LABEL: ECHO (same Greek word, abyssos, behind "the deep" here and in Romans 10:7 and "the bottomless pit" in Revelation 20:3; a plain lexicon fact. No verse ties Luke 8:31 to either)
INSIGHT: It is the devils, not the man, who beg; and the one thing they dread is a place Paul describes as where Christ was "brought up" from, and Revelation 20:3 "cast him into the bottomless pit, and shut him up, and set a seal upon him".
DOES NOT PROVE: Where Jesus was between the cross and the rising, or where these devils ended up. Luke says Jesus "suffered" them to enter the swine (8:32) and the herd ran into "the lake" (8:33), not "the deep". Keep it to: same word, same place of dread.
SO WHAT: The devils begged not to be sent into the deep, but Jesus himself went through death and was brought up again, so death does not rule the ones he saves. Believer verse: Hebrews 2:14-15 "that through death he might destroy him that had the power of death, that is, the devil; And deliver them who through fear of death were all their lifetime subject to bondage."

## ANGLE D - Naked and Afraid, Then Clothed
HOOK: Who gave the naked man his clothes?
ANCHOR-1: Luke 8:35 "sitting at the feet of Jesus, clothed, and in his right mind"
ANCHOR-2: Genesis 3:21 "did the LORD God make coats of skins, and clothed them"
WHY-VERSE: Genesis 3:10 "I was afraid, because I was naked; and I hid myself"
LABEL: ECHO (shared word "clothed" after nakedness; no verse states the link)
INSIGHT: Luke says the man "ware no clothes" (8:27) and then that he was "clothed" (8:35), and never says who gave the clothes. In Genesis the first man, who said he hid because he was naked and afraid, was clothed by the LORD God himself.
DOES NOT PROVE: That Luke meant Genesis 3, that the man is a "new Adam", or that Jesus gave the clothes (Luke does not say). It does not say the man's state came from his own sin.
SO WHAT: A man who had no clothes was found clothed at the feet of Jesus, so you do not have to hide what left you exposed. Believer verse: Galatians 3:27 "For as many of you as have been baptized into Christ have put on Christ."

## ANGLE E - Jesus Said "God." The Man Said "Jesus."
HOOK: Why did the man say "Jesus" when Jesus said "God"?
ANCHOR-1: Luke 8:39 "shew how great things God hath done unto thee. And he went his way, and published throughout the whole city how great things Jesus had done unto him"
ANCHOR-2: 1 Samuel 12:24 "consider how great things he hath done for you"
WHY-VERSE: John 14:10 "the Father that dwelleth in me, he doeth the works"
LABEL: ECHO (the shared phrase "how great things" is about the LORD's acts in 1 Samuel 12:24; John 14:10 says the Father does the works in Jesus, but never mentions this scene)
INSIGHT: In one verse the same deed carries two names: Jesus says "God hath done", and Luke says the man told what "Jesus had done".
DOES NOT PROVE: That the man understood or taught that Jesus is God, or that Jesus approved the swap (Luke does not say what Jesus thought of it). Luke only sets both clauses side by side. It does not say the man disobeyed.
SO WHAT: Luke gives the same great deed to God and to Jesus, so when Jesus acts for you, God is acting for you. Believer verse: Hebrews 7:25 "he is able also to save them to the uttermost that come unto God by him"

## ANGLE F - Chains Off, a House to Go To
HOOK: He had chains and no house. What does Psalm 68 say God does for people like that?
ANCHOR-1: Luke 8:29 "he was kept bound with chains and in fetters"
ANCHOR-2: Psalm 68:6 "God setteth the solitary in families: he bringeth out those which are bound with chains"
WHY-VERSE: Psalm 68:6 "God setteth the solitary in families"
LABEL: ECHO (shared picture of "bound with chains" and a home; no verse ties Psalm 68:6 to Luke 8)
INSIGHT: Luke starts the man "neither abode in any house, but in the tombs" (8:27) and ends with "Return to thine own house" (8:39); Psalm 68:6 names the same two moves, chains off and a place in a family, as the work of God.
DOES NOT PROVE: That Psalm 68:6 foretold this man. Luke says "thine own house", so he had a home to go back to; "solitary" is the Psalm's word, not Luke's. Luke says the man himself broke the bands (8:29), so do not say Jesus took the chains off.
SO WHAT: Jesus freed the man from what drove him and sent him to a house, doing the work the Psalm gives to God, so if you feel shut out he is the one who finds you a place. Believer verse: Ephesians 2:19 "Now therefore ye are no more strangers and foreigners, but fellowcitizens with the saints, and of the household of God"

## ANGLE G - The Words Said to the Man of God
HOOK: Who else cried "What have I to do with thee" to a man of God?
ANCHOR-1: Luke 8:28 "What have I to do with thee, Jesus, thou Son of God most high? I beseech thee, torment me not"
ANCHOR-2: 1 Kings 17:18 "What have I to do with thee, O thou man of God? art thou come unto me to call my sin to remembrance, and to slay my son?"
WHY-VERSE: 1 Kings 17:24 "Now by this I know that thou art a man of God"
LABEL: ECHO (same words of fear before a holy man; no verse states the link)
INSIGHT: The widow of Zarephath cried these words to Elijah when her son lay with "no breath left in him" (1 Kings 17:17-18), afraid his coming had called her sin to mind; he gave her son back alive. Jesus himself named a widow of that city earlier in Luke (Luke 4:26 "Unto none of them was Elias sent, save unto Sarepta, a city of Sidon, unto a woman that was a widow").
DOES NOT PROVE: That Luke 8:28 quotes 1 Kings 17, or that the devils feared for their sins as the widow did. The same words appear elsewhere with other meanings (2 Samuel 16:10). It is the cry of someone afraid of the holy one, not a prophecy.
SO WHAT: Both cries expected harm, and both ended in life given back; Jesus came to the man who cried "torment me not" and left him clothed and in his right mind. Believer verse: Romans 8:1 "There is therefore now no condemnation to them which are in Christ Jesus"

## ANGLE H - From the Tombs to the Student's Seat
HOOK: Why does Luke say the man sat "at the feet" of Jesus?
ANCHOR-1: Luke 8:35 "found the man, out of whom the devils were departed, sitting at the feet of Jesus"
ANCHOR-2: Acts 22:3 "brought up in this city at the feet of Gamaliel, and taught"
WHY-VERSE: Acts 22:3 "at the feet of Gamaliel, and taught"
LABEL: ECHO (shared words "at the feet of"; Acts 22:3 shows this is where a pupil sits, but no verse says the man became a pupil)
INSIGHT: In Acts 22:3 "at the feet of" goes with being "taught"; Luke 10:39 uses the same place for listening: Mary "also sat at Jesus' feet, and heard his word". The man who had lived among the tombs is found in the learner's seat.
DOES NOT PROVE: That he was made a disciple, or what Jesus taught him. Luke says only that he sat there and then asked to be "with him" (8:38). Luke 10:39 is a second Luke scene, not a second book.
SO WHAT: Jesus let the man from the tombs sit at his feet, so there is room beside him for the one who feels out of place. Believer verse: Matthew 11:28 "Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest."

## ANGLE I - The Unclean Spirit Who Must Leave
HOOK: Where else does the Bible say the unclean spirit will have to go?
ANCHOR-1: Luke 8:29 "he had commanded the unclean spirit to come out of the man"
ANCHOR-2: Zechariah 13:2 "I will cause the prophets and the unclean spirit to pass out of the land"
WHY-VERSE: Zechariah 13:1 "there shall be a fountain opened to the house of David and to the inhabitants of Jerusalem for sin and for uncleanness"
LABEL: ECHO (shared words "the unclean spirit"; no verse states the link)
INSIGHT: Zechariah promises, "in that day", a fountain for uncleanness and the unclean spirit passing out; in Luke 8 an unclean spirit is told to come out by Jesus' command.
DOES NOT PROVE: That Zechariah 13 foretold this scene. In Zechariah the setting is Jerusalem and the house of David (13:1); Luke puts this shore "over against Galilee" (8:26) and says only that the devils went into the swine (8:33). It does not say the spirits are gone for good.
SO WHAT: The unclean spirit left at the command of Jesus, so what holds you does not get the last word. Believer verse: 1 John 3:8 "For this purpose the Son of God was manifested, that he might destroy the works of the devil."
