## TITLE
Jesus Stopped Reading Mid-Sentence

## DESCRIPTION
He left part of Isaiah's sentence unread. In the Bible, Jesus reads Isaiah 61 in Nazareth (Luke 4:16-30).

In the synagogue of His home town, Jesus read from Isaiah, closed the book and sat down (Luke 4:16-20). Luke 4:19 ends at "the acceptable year of the Lord." The sentence in Isaiah 61:2 goes on. The Bible does not say why Jesus stopped there.

"To proclaim the acceptable year of the LORD, and the day of vengeance of our God;" (Isaiah 61:2, KJV)

Then He said: "This day is this scripture fulfilled in your ears." (Luke 4:21, KJV)

Jesus knew they would want miracles. He said they would tell Him: "Physician, heal thyself" (Luke 4:23). He reminded them that God once sent Elijah to a foreign widow (Luke 4:25-26), and that Naaman, a foreign leper, was healed in the days of Elisha (Luke 4:27). The crowd became very angry and led Him to the edge of a cliff. Jesus passed through the middle of them and went on His way (Luke 4:28-30).

Later, at the cross, the rulers said: "He saved others; let him save himself" (Luke 23:35). The words are different, but the challenge is the same: save yourself. This time Jesus stayed. He died and rose again:

"For I delivered unto you first of all that which I also received, how that Christ died for our sins according to the scriptures; And that he was buried, and that he rose again the third day according to the scriptures" (1 Corinthians 15:3-4, KJV)

Read it yourself: Isaiah 61:1-2; Luke 4:16-30; Luke 23:35; 1 Corinthians 15:3-4.

He stopped reading at the welcome. It is still open, for you, today.
