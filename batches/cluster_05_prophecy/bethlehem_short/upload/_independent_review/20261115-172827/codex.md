# Independent review — codex (OK, 208s)

The kit is not ready to copy straight into upload forms.

- **Instagram — hashtag limit exceeded.** The block beginning `#Bethlehem #BibleProphecy` contains **eight hashtags**. Instagram’s announced caption limit is five. The embedded review’s “Instagram has 8 hashtags; fine for the platform” is outdated. This is a publishing defect, not an optional trim. [Current guidance and announcement references](https://socialhelper.app/blog/instagram-hashtag-limit).

- **YouTube Short, YouTube long-form, Facebook — inaccurate footer.** “Scripture quoted from the King James Version (KJV), public domain” makes an unqualified rights claim. UK Crown rights apply to the KJV. The embedded reviewer explicitly flagged this, yet all three upload blocks retain it. [Cambridge’s rights notice](https://assets.cambridge.org/97805211/63347/copyright/9780521163347_copyright_info.pdf).

- **Kit-wide — contradictory verification claims.** “Gates: ALL PASS ✅” and “pre-checked, red-teamed and panel-reviewed” conflict with the included `VERDICT: REVISE` and its unresolved footer finding. “UK-G7 … plain ASCII” is also false for every caption containing `✝`. These assurances give the uploader unjustified confidence.

- **YouTube long-form — wrong asset framing.** “YouTube (Long-form 16:9)” has no corresponding asset identified. The supplied `bethlehem_final.mp4` is **1080 × 1920, 59.5 seconds**: a vertical Short. Presenting this section as immediately usable leaves the uploader without the advertised horizontal version. A different title and description do not establish a different video format.

- **TikTok / Instagram — the distinctive hook arrives late.** Both begin “Why was Jesus born in Bethlehem?”, then explain the prophecy before reaching “The experts knew. Who went to see the King?” That creates two competing questions and delays the film’s distinctive tension. This is an editorial weakness, not demonstrated clickbait: the [locked narration](/F:/slk/PycharmProjects/JesusInTheBible/batches/cluster_05_prophecy/bethlehem_short/narration.md) supplies the wise-men payoff and preserves the distinction between biblical silence and the experts refusing to go.

- **YouTube Short tags — redundant search targeting.** “king herod and the wise men” and “the wise men and herod” duplicate the same search intent. “who was micah in the bible” is marginal: the narration briefly identifies Micah but does not develop a Micah explainer. This is search dilution rather than evidence of wholesale tag-stuffing.

- **Cross-platform title option 4 — forgettable packaging.** “Bethlehem: A Promise Before Christmas” hides the specific prophecy and the experts/wise-men tension behind a generic seasonal phrase. It gives viewers little concrete reason to choose this particular video.

I found no altered words in the quoted [Micah 5:2](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Micah+5%3A2&version=KJV%3BNIV) or [John 6:38](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John+6%3A38&version=KJV%3BNKJV). No explicit fear, gain-loss, or works-based salvation framing appears in the supplied metadata.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Reduce Instagram’s hashtag block to five and correct its platform verification.
2. Remove the unqualified “public domain” claim from both YouTube descriptions and Facebook.
3. Resolve the short/long-form asset mismatch and replace “ALL PASS” with an accurate verification status.
