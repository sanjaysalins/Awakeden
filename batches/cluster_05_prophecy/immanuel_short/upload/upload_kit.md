Why is Jesus called Immanuel? The famous Christmas promise began with a frightened king who said no to God.

About 700 years before Jesus, King Ahaz of Judah, from King David's family, was afraid of war. Two armies were coming. God said "fear not" and offered him any sign he wanted. Ahaz refused. In that same war he begged the king of Assyria: "I am thy servant and thy son: come up, and save me" (2 Kings 16:7), and paid him with silver and gold from God's own house (2 Kings 16:8).

God gave the sign anyway:
"Therefore the Lord himself shall give you a sign; Behold, a virgin shall conceive, and bear a son, and shall call his name Immanuel." (Isaiah 7:14)

Seven hundred years later it came true. Mary, a virgin, was with child by the Holy Spirit. An angel told Joseph, another son of David, to fear not, and said the child's name would be JESUS, "for he shall save his people from their sins" (Matthew 1:21). Joseph obeyed. Matthew says the name Emmanuel means "God with us" (Matthew 1:23).
