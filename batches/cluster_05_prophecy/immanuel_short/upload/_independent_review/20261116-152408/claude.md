# Adversarial review: Upload Kit for "Why Is Jesus Called Immanuel?"

## Blockers

1. **The kit fails its own gate and is not ready to upload.** The header says "Gates: FAILURES ❌" and UK-G7 failed on three platforms: YouTube Short, YouTube Long and Facebook. In all three, "Isaiah" does not appear before the "more" fold (157 characters). The intro line still says the kit is "pre-checked, red-teamed and panel-reviewed". That is not true while a gate is red. The house rule says no upload until the gate passes.
   - **Fix:** put the verse before the fold. For example, YouTube Short line 1: *"Isaiah 7:14: a frightened king told God no. God answered anyway."* Do the same on Facebook. For YouTube Long, use *"Why is Jesus called Immanuel? Isaiah 7:14 began with a frightened king…"*

2. **The red team's REVISE was never applied.**
   - **TikTok:** "Isaiah 7:14 was God's answer. **Watch to the end.**" The red team said to cut this templated line, and it is still there.
   - **Instagram:** "What was it, and when did it come true? **Watch to the end.**" Same problem: the red team said to keep the question and drop the last sentence.
   - **YouTube Long:** "Two armies were coming." The red team asked for "(Isaiah 7:1)". The film never says this, so the citation is needed.

3. **The format does not match.** The header says "Format: short" and points to one video, `immanuel_final.mp4`. But the kit includes a full "YouTube (Long-form 16:9)" block. Its 68-character title and its description cover the cross, the resurrection (1 Cor 15:3-4) and Matt 28:20, which a 60-second short may not show. If no 16:9 cut exists, delete that block. Otherwise someone may paste it onto the 9:16 short, and the metadata would promise content the film doesn't deliver.

## KJV and accuracy

All the quoted verses match the KJV word for word:
- Isaiah 7:14
- Matt 1:21, "for he shall save his people from their sins"
- Matt 28:20, "I am with you alway"
- 2 Kings 16:7, "I am thy servant and thy son: come up, and save me"

The 2 Kings 16:7 quote stops in the middle of the verse; it goes on "…out of the hand of the king of Syria". That is acceptable, but a "…" would be more honest.

Small fixes:
- "About 700 years before Jesus" is later followed by **"Seven hundred years later it came true"**, with no "About". Ahaz is about 735 BC and the birth about 5 BC, so it is roughly 730 years. Write "About 700 years later" on YouTube Short, YouTube Long and Facebook.
- YouTube Long has "with child by the **Holy Spirit**". The kit is KJV-branded and Matt 1:18 says "Holy Ghost". It isn't in quote marks, so this is not a fail, but it is inconsistent.
- "God said 'fear not'": in Isaiah 7:4 the Lord speaks through Isaiah, so this is fine.
- "offered him any sign" is a fair paraphrase of 7:11 and is not in quote marks. Good.

## Platform fit and clarity

- **YouTube Short:** "One king begged a king to save him. This child saves." The line is cryptic, and "a king… a king" is confusing for second-language viewers. The YouTube Long version is clearer: "One son of David begged a foreign king…" Use a simple form like that.
- **TikTok:** the caption never names Jesus or Immanuel; Jesus appears only in the brand CTA line. Add one line such as *"Matthew says it came true in Jesus: 'God with us.'"* That pays off the curiosity gap and gives a real CTA to Jesus.
- **Instagram:** 9 hashtags is acceptable. #ChristianShorts is filler.
- **Tags:** "christmas bible story" is borderline, because the film centres on Ahaz. "2 kings 16" and "matthew 1 23" get almost no searches but are honest. There is no tag-stuffing.
- **Titles:** they are honest. "Why Is Jesus Called Immanuel?" is answered by Matt 1:23. "The King Who Told God No" is fair, since Isaiah 7:13 rebukes Ahaz for wearying God.

## Doctrine and brand

The kit points to Christ. It has no fear, gain-or-loss or works framing. The copy never calls Joseph the father, and it presents Ahaz and Joseph as a contrast, not a fulfilment. The footer, the handle link and the KJV notice are intact.
