# Independent review — Sign of Jonah study-page copy

I checked every quoted string against the repo's KJV cache (`C:\Users\sanjay\PycharmProjects\JesusInTheBible\data\kjv_cache.json` — Jonah 1, Jonah 2:1-10, Matthew 12:38-41, Isaiah 53:6, Mark 4:39 all present) and against KJV for the verses not cached (John 10:18, Acts 2:24, Matthew 28:6, Psalm 42:7).

## KJV verbatim

**One real violation, as presented.** The "A psalm sung inside a grave" card reads:

> [Jonah 2:3] "All thy billows and thy waves passed over me. **Compare Psalm 42:7: all thy waves and thy billows are gone over me.**"

The editorial sentence "Compare Psalm 42:7: …" sits **inside the quotation marks attributed to Jonah 2:3**. Those are not Jonah 2:3's words. Both fragments are individually verbatim KJV (cache confirms both), but the citation block as rendered puts non-Jonah words inside a Jonah quote. In this project verbatim-inside-quotes is a hard rule; this must be split into two separately-attributed quotes.

Everything else inside quotes checks out verbatim, including the fragments: "Take me up, and cast me forth into the sea; so shall the sea be calm unto you" / "For I know that for my sake this great tempest is upon you" (Jonah 1:12, split at the KJV colon — fine), "No man taketh it from me, but I lay it down of myself" (John 10:18), "The LORD hath laid on him the iniquity of us all" (Isaiah 53:6, mid-verse start dropping "and" — fine), Jonah 1:17, Matthew 12:40, Jonah 2:2, Acts 2:24, Jonah 2:10, Matthew 28:6, Matthew 12:39, Mark 4:39, and Matthew 12:41 ("They repented…" starts mid-clause after KJV's "because" — acceptable, contiguous).

Minor clarity note on Acts 2:24: the quote starts at "Having loosed…", which drops the subject ("Whom God hath raised up"). A zero-Bible-knowledge reader can't tell from the quote **who** loosed the pains of death; the surrounding line ("Death was not able to hold him") mostly covers it, but starting one clause earlier would be cleaner.

## Doctrine / overclaim

- The five-row typology is exactly what Matthew 12:40 itself authorizes — no invented parallel. "Goes down guilty / goes down innocent" is sound and is the strongest row.
- "He knew exactly how merciful God is and hated it" — grounded in Jonah 4:1-2 ("I knew that thou art a gracious God… therefore I fled"). "Hated it" is strong but defensible from "very angry." Fine.
- **Precision problem:** "Jesus refuses every sign except one, a resurrection." Read cold, this says Jesus never did miracles — but the Gospels are full of signs. What he refused was a *sign on demand to that generation of sign-seekers* (Matt 12:39 says "no sign… **but** the sign of the prophet Jonas" *to this generation*). The card's first sentence sets the on-demand context, but the flat sentence still overreaches. Say "refuses their demand — one sign only" or "gives that generation only one sign."
- "**AD 33**" stated as flat fact; the crucifixion year is genuinely contested (AD 30 vs 33). "About 780 BC" is properly hedged; AD 33 isn't. Say "about AD 33" or "AD 30–33."
- "Jonah chapter 2 is a song" — it's a prayer in psalm form ("Then Jonah prayed," Jonah 2:1). The same sentence says "prayed," which self-corrects, but "song" is the load-bearing word of the card title. Borderline; acceptable.
- "Another Man went into the grave with a psalm on his lips" — Psalm 31:5 / Psalm 22:1 were spoken **on the cross**, before death. "Went into the grave with a psalm on his lips" is fair poetic compression; passes.

## Clarity, grace, thread

- First-hearing clarity is good; "demanded paperwork" is the one colloquialism, and it lands.
- Conviction is grace-anchored: "the deep you chose is not deeper than the mercy that follows you into it" — no fear/gain-loss framing anywhere. The application turns Jonah's *flight from mercy* into the invitation, which is this piece's own move, not a bolt-on.
- One thread (down into the deep → up alive) runs from the first row to the last sentence. No thread-swap.

## Scores

- **HOOK: 8.** The page opens on the Jonah 1:12 / John 10:18 contrast — "Offers himself to the sea" vs "No man taketh it from me." Text-specific, no template open, genuinely arresting for a study page.
- **LANDING: 8.** "Come up out of the deep, and live" is built from this piece's own down/up/deep imagery and could not close a different piece. "Stop running, and turn" alone would be stock; the final sentence rescues it.
