## Independent adversarial review

### KJV verbatim — hard fail on one block

**[Jonah 2:3] citation is contaminated.** Quoted as one string:

> "All thy billows and thy waves passed over me. Compare Psalm 42:7: all thy waves and thy billows are gone over me."

Problems:
1. Editorial prose (`Compare Psalm 42:7:`) sits **inside** quotation marks tagged as Jonah 2:3.
2. Two distinct KJV passages are fused under one verse tag.
3. Word order differs between the two texts — Jonah 2:3 has *"billows…waves"*; Psalm 42:7 has *"waves…billows"* — so this is not one contiguous KJV substring.

That violates verbatim-inside-quotes for this project.

**Other quotes:** Partial elisions are mostly acceptable (Jonah 1:12 split at the KJV colon; Isaiah 53:6 mid-verse; Acts 2:24, Matthew 12:40/41, Mark 4:39, etc.). One nit: Acts 2:24 drops *"Whom God hath raised up,"* so a zero-Bible listener cannot tell from the quote alone **who** loosed death — the surrounding line helps, but the fragment is thin.

### Doctrinal soundness — mostly orthodox, two overclaims

**1. "Jesus refuses every sign except one, a resurrection."** (meat card *Why 'sign' is a loaded word*)

Too absolute. Matthew 12:39 limits what sign would be given **to that evil, sign-demanding generation** — not a universal refusal of miracles. Read cold, this sounds like Jesus did no signs at all, which the Gospels contradict. The next sentences ("proof without surrender," tomb empty) recover the intent, but the flat sentence overreaches. Safer: *He refuses their on-demand proof — that generation gets one sign: the sign of Jonah (death, burial, rising).*

Repeated in Today: *"Jesus answered that demand once, for everyone: one sign."* Same overclaim, now universalized.

**2. "A thousand years of Scripture later, another Man went into the grave with a psalm on his lips."** (*A psalm sung inside a grave*)

Imprecise. Psalm 22 (and/or Psalm 31:5) was spoken **on the cross before death**, not while entering the tomb. The fish→grave typology is Jesus-authorized (Matt 12:40); compressing cross-psalm into *"went into the grave with a psalm on his lips"* blurs historical level with typological echo. Evocative, not heresy — but it is an overclaim a careful reader will catch.

**3. Pattern row 1 — "Offers himself to the sea to save the men" // John 10:18**

Typologically licensed, but it flattens Jonah (guilty, fleeing, calming a storm he caused) toward Christ-like voluntary offering without the guardrail your locked narration carries: *"Jonah is the SIGN, never the substitute."* Row 2 corrects guilty/innocent, but row 1 still risks a listener hearing parallel substitution where Scripture gives parallel **sign**.

**4. "Two sleepers, two storms"**

Narratively true (Jonah 1:5; Mark 4:38–39). Orthodox punch. On a **Sign of Jonah** page it is a side motif — not the sign Jesus names in Matt 12:39–40 — so it dilutes the spine without being false.

**5. "because he knew exactly how merciful God is and hated it"**

Grounded in Jonah 4:2. Strong language, defensible. Not invented doctrine.

**6. "AD 33"** stated as flat fact while Jonah gets *"about 780 BC."* Crucifixion year is contested (AD 30 vs 33). Asymmetric precision.

### Clarity on first hearing

- Pattern rows: clear **if** the listener can track parallel columns; cold audio with no visual table is harder.
- *"demanded paperwork"* — colloquial, lands.
- *"belly of hell"* (Jonah 2:2) — a zero-Bible listener may hear damnation, not Sheol/death-realm; surrounding context does not define it.
- *"A man singing Scripture from inside his own tomb"* — Jonah is in a **fish** (Jonah 1:17); fish-as-tomb is typology, not narrative fact. Clever, but a first-time listener may not know which layer is which.
- *"Awakeden means by seeing Jesus…"* — brand voice; carries no doctrine.

No outright self-contradiction. Main fog: type stated as fact, and the overloaded Jonah 2 / Psalm card.
