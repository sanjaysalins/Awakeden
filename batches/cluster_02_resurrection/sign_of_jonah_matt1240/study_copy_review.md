## The pattern (OT to NT rows)

- **Jonah, about 780 BC**: Offers himself to the sea to save the men on the ship. [Jonah 1:12] "Take me up, and cast me forth into the sea; so shall the sea be calm unto you."
- **Jesus, AD 33**: Lays his life down. No one takes it from him. [John 10:18] "No man taketh it from me, but I lay it down of myself."

- **Jonah**: Goes down guilty. The storm was his own. [Jonah 1:12] "For I know that for my sake this great tempest is upon you."
- **Jesus**: Goes down innocent. The storm was ours. [Isaiah 53:6] "The LORD hath laid on him the iniquity of us all."

- **Jonah**: Three days and three nights in the belly of the fish. [Jonah 1:17] "And Jonah was in the belly of the fish three days and three nights."
- **Jesus**: Three days and three nights in the heart of the earth. [Matthew 12:40] "So shall the Son of man be three days and three nights in the heart of the earth."

- **Jonah**: Cries from the belly of hell, and is heard. [Jonah 2:2] "Out of the belly of hell cried I, and thou heardest my voice."
- **Jesus**: Death was not able to hold him. [Acts 2:24] "Having loosed the pains of death: because it was not possible that he should be holden of it."

- **Jonah**: The deep gives him back to the dry land, alive. [Jonah 2:10] "And it vomited out Jonah upon the dry land."
- **Jesus**: The tomb gives him back. Empty. [Matthew 28:6] "He is not here: for he is risen, as he said."

## Pattern note
This is what Awakeden means by seeing Jesus in the whole Bible: the Old Testament draws the outline, the New Testament fills it in.

## Meat cards

### Why 'sign' is a loaded word
The scribes wanted a miracle performed on demand: proof without surrender. Jesus refuses every sign except one, a resurrection. He stakes the whole claim on it. If the tomb is not empty, you can walk away. That is not how frauds talk.
[Matthew 12:39] "An evil and adulterous generation seeketh after a sign; and there shall no sign be given to it, but the sign of the prophet Jonas."

### A psalm sung inside a grave
Jonah chapter 2 is a song, prayed from inside the fish in the language of the Psalms. A man singing Scripture from inside his own tomb, certain he will be heard. A thousand years of Scripture later, another Man went into the grave with a psalm on his lips.
[Jonah 2:3] "All thy billows and thy waves passed over me. Compare Psalm 42:7: all thy waves and thy billows are gone over me."

### Two sleepers, two storms
Jonah slept in the hold while the storm raged (Jonah 1:5). Jesus slept in the stern while the storm raged (Mark 4:38). Both were shaken awake by terrified men afraid to perish. The sea calmed when Jonah was thrown in. The sea calmed when Jesus simply spoke. Same scene, utterly different man.
[Mark 4:39] "Peace, be still. And the wind ceased, and there was a great calm."

### A greater than Jonas is here
Jonah preached one reluctant sermon and a whole violent city repented in sackcloth. Jesus' point cuts: pagan Nineveh repented at the lesser prophet, while the experts stood in front of the greater One and demanded paperwork.
[Matthew 12:41] "They repented at the preaching of Jonas; and, behold, a greater than Jonas is here."

## Today (application)
Q: Show me a sign and I'll believe.
That is still the deal we offer God. Prove yourself first, fix this diagnosis, this marriage, this bank balance, and then I'll listen. Jesus answered that demand once, for everyone: one sign. A grave that could not keep him.
And Jonah is in the story for the ones who are running. He did not drift from God; he bought a ticket in the opposite direction, because he knew exactly how merciful God is and hated it. If you have gone down that far on purpose, the fish is the point: the deep you chose is not deeper than the mercy that follows you into it.
Stop running, and turn. The One who went down into your dark and rose again is Jesus. Come up out of the deep, and live.
