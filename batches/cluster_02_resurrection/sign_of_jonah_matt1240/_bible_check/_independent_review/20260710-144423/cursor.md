## Independent adversarial review — `sign_of_jonah_matt1240` fact sheet v2

### CITATION TRUTH (critical)

**1. Matt 12:40 is misapplied as a tomb-visual citation (most serious error).**

Line 34: *"The 'heart of the earth' darkness (Matt 12:40): the sealed tomb interior is DARK — no lamps or candles… no window light."*

Matt 12:40 only states the **duration parallel** (“three days and three nights in the whale’s belly… in the heart of the earth”). It says nothing about a tomb chamber, darkness, lamps, windows, or a visible body. Mapping metaphor → sealed-rock interior is **typological interpretation**, not a KJV-stated visual fact. This is licence/constraint at best, not **[SPECIFIED]**. The same card also smuggles in *“A closed-tomb still may show the wound body at rest”* — no cited verse; wounds-at-rest is not in Matt 12:40.

**2. “Merchant ship” is not in Jonah 1:3.**

Line 11: *“Jonah fled on a **merchant** ship…”* / *“a working **merchant** vessel.”*

KJV: *“he found **a ship** going to Tarshish: so he paid the fare thereof.”* Fare payment implies commercial travel but **“merchant” is not stated**. Under BC-G2 over-reach rules, this is inference in the wrong bucket.

**3. Lot material is invented under [SPECIFIED].**

Line 13: *“small plain **stones/pebbles** as lots.”*

Jonah 1:7: *“they **cast lots**, and the lot fell upon Jonah.”* Method and material are **unspecified**. Pebbles vs dice vs other lots is tradition/render guidance, not biblical specification.

**4. Fish appearance details are not biblical.**

Line 19 image line: *“one **vast dark** fish, **no cartoon teeth/eyes**.”*

Jonah 1:17 only says *“a **great fish**.”* Size, color, and teeth are artistic constraints, not text.

**5. Stone position “OUTSIDE” is inferred, not quoted.**

Lines 33–35: *“stone seals or stands at the entrance **OUTSIDE** — never inside the chamber”* / *“stone moved aside **OUTSIDE**.”*

Matthew 27:60: *“rolled a great stone **to the door** of the sepulchre.”* Mark 16:4: *“the stone was **rolled away**.”* Neither verse says “outside.” The inference is reasonable archaeology, but labeling it **[SPECIFIED]** overstates the citation (compare empty_tomb v2, which ties “outside” to John 20:1 *“stone taken away from the sepulchre”* — still absent here).

**6. “Rich man’s” tomb lacks the supporting citation.**

Line 30: *“A **rich man’s** NEW rock-cut tomb…”*

The quoted strings are Matthew 27:59–60 and John 19:41. **“Rich man”** is Matthew **27:57** (*“a rich man of Arimathaea, named Joseph”*), not in the quote block. Claim is true; citation set is incomplete/misleading.

**7. Stooping verses prove action, not doorway architecture.**

Line 31: *“The entrance is LOW… never a tall standing-height doorway.”*

John 20:5 / Luke 24:12 say Peter **stooped** and looked/beheld. They do not describe opening height. Stooping could be posture at a low opening *or* at a normal opening to peer in. “Low, small opening” is strong inference — acceptable as **[CONSTRAINED]**, shaky as **[SPECIFIED]**.

**8. Face-covered burial leans on post-resurrection evidence.**

Line 32: *“FACE COVERED by the napkin.”*

John 19:40 and Matthew 27:59 say wrapped/wound in linen; neither explicitly says the **face** is covered at burial. John **20:7** (*“the napkin, that was about his head”*) is an **empty-tomb** observation proving prior head-wrapping — valid retro-inference, but the burial-time face claim is not directly quoted from burial verses. Tighten wording or cite 20:7 explicitly as retrospective proof.

**9. Jonah 2:5 weeds — scene-boundary issue.**

Line 21: weeds on head in *“sinking scenes.”*

Jonah 2:5 is **prayer recollection** of drowning (*“the weeds were wrapped about my head”*), not a description of life inside the fish belly. Correct for `jonah_sinking_down`; would be wrong if applied to `jonah_in_the_deep` (sheet doesn’t, but the card doesn’t fence the beat).

**10. “Raging sea in every ship scene BEFORE Jonah goes over” — editorial, not textual.**

Line 12 image directive. Jonah 1:4 supports a mighty tempest; it does not require storm in **every** ship frame or define a storyboard sequence. Minor overreach in the image clause.
