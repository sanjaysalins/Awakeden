## Version X

- **Newcomer:** About Jonah and Jesus. Jonah is swallowed by a fish; later Jesus says this connects to his own death and rising. It matters because Jesus offers mercy and hope. **May not follow:** “I went down guilty — He went down innocent, bearing what was ours; He gave Himself.”
- **Bible reader:** The Jonah–Jesus connection and the contrast between Jonah’s guilt and Jesus’s innocence come through. But Jonah was in the fish for three days and three nights, not “buried alive”; “long after I was dust” is poetic, not stated in these passages. Matthew gives Jesus’s sign as Jonah, then says the Son of Man will be in the heart of the earth. “Bearing what was ours” goes beyond these passages.
- **Curiosity:** Yes; the fish and the mystery of the connection open it, and Jesus’s sign and resurrection pay it off. **8/10**
- **Ending:** **Yes** — it ends with an invitation to live, grounded in Jesus’s descent and rising.

## Version Y

- **Newcomer:** About Jonah and Jesus. Jonah is swallowed by a fish; later Jesus connects Jonah’s story to his own death and rising. It matters because Jesus offers mercy and hope. **May not follow:** “I went down guilty — the storm was mine.”
- **Bible reader:** It makes the storm Jonah’s responsibility, which fits Jonah 1, where he identifies himself as the cause. But “the storm was mine” is less explicit than X’s “the storm came for my sake.” The same issues apply: “buried alive” is poetic, “long after I was dust” is not in these passages, and “bearing what was ours” goes beyond them.
- **Curiosity:** Yes; the fish opens the question of what Jonah has to do with Jesus, and the sign and resurrection answer it. **8/10**
- **Ending:** **Yes** — the final invitation lands on Jesus and feels complete.

## Pick: Y

**One cut-only fix:** Delete “the storm was mine.” It repeats the earlier storm-and-lot line and slightly slows the move from Jonah’s guilt to Jesus’s innocence.
