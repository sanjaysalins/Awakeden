## 1. WHO IS THIS FOR (one named viewer; who stops, who finishes, who shares; who is NOT served)

A newcomer like **Mina**, watching on her phone, may stop at “ONE SIGN ONLY. WHICH ONE?” but not know what kind of sign is meant. She might finish because the images show a storm, a fish, a tomb, and a man rising, and “He rose” is easy to understand (line 10). She may share it if she understands that it is inviting her to trust Jesus (lines 11–14).

It does not serve someone who needs names and events explained before being asked to accept the conclusion. “Nineveh” and “scribes” are never explained (lines 3, 5).

## 2. MAIN TAKEAWAY (one sentence a viewer could say back; which line carries it; did the film make it clear?)

“Jesus went into death and rose, so his mercy can reach people who feel they have gone too far” (lines 8–14). The film makes the invitation clear, but not why Jonah’s story is being used to explain Jesus.

## 3. GLOBAL AUDIENCE (who / what / why in my own words; where I got lost or misled, quote the line; words that fail in translation)

I think a man called Jonah runs from God, ends up in the sea, and is swallowed by a fish. Jesus later uses Jonah’s name to answer people asking for a sign. The film says Jesus died and rose, and offers this as a reason to stop running and turn to him.

I guessed that “the lot fell on me” means people chose Jonah by chance, but I do not know what “the lot” is (line 4). “Nineveh, the city I hated” tells me Jonah hated it, but not why he was sent there or what it means for the city to be “spared” (line 3). “Scribes demanded a sign” leaves me unsure who the scribes are, what they wanted, and why Jonah’s name answers them (line 5). “The heart of the earth” sounds like a poetic phrase for a grave, but the film does not explain it (line 6). I also do not know who “ours” means in “bearing what was ours” (line 8), or what I am being asked to do by “turn” (line 12).
