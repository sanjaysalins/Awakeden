## 1. WHO IS THIS FOR
**Named viewer:** Grace, 40s, a churchgoer who knows "Jonah and the whale" from Sunday school and scrolls Shorts around Easter.
- **Who stops:** church people, because of the card "ONE SIGN ONLY. WHICH ONE?"
- **Who finishes:** believers. The turn at line 6 to Jesus' own voice holds them.
- **Who shares:** youth leaders, and a parent sending it to a child who has drifted (line 11).
- **Who is not served:** a total newcomer, who is never told that "Son of man" (line 6) means Jesus. Also not served: a careful Bible reader who notices that Nineveh, the point of line 2, never comes back.

## 2. MAIN TAKEAWAY
"Jonah's three days in the fish point to Jesus' three days in the grave. He rose, so no one is too far down for mercy." Lines 6, 9 and 11 carry it. It is clear. But it is the most familiar reading of Jonah there is, the one preached a thousand times. The only rarely-taught thread is the "went down" motif in line 7 (Jonah 1:3, 1:5, 2:6), and it is used for one line and then dropped.

## 3. GLOBAL AUDIENCE
**In my own words:** A prophet ran from God. He was thrown into the sea and swallowed by a fish. Jesus later said that was His sign. Jesus died and rose. Turn back.

**Where I got lost or misled:**
- **Line 6:** "Son of man" is never decoded. A newcomer may think it means Jonah's son.
- **Line 6 against line 1:** the film says "whale" and "great fish" with no card. JONAS = JONAH is decoded, but whale/fish is not.
- **Line 4:** "the lot fell on me" is an idiom. Casting lots is never explained or shown clearly.
- **Phrases that fail in translation:** "heart of the earth" (line 6), "long after I was dust" (line 5), Nineveh called "her" (line 3).
- **Order:** the pictures open on the throw, then the fish, then the storm. That reversed order confuses people.

## 4. BIBLICALLY ACCURATE
- **L1:** "told the sailors to throw me" is RIGHT (1:12). "swallowed me" is RIGHT (1:17). "buried me alive in the dark" is OVERCLAIMED but defensible as poetry ("out of the belly of hell," 2:2).
- **L2:** "ran from God because His mercy was too wide" is RIGHT in substance (4:2: "Therefore I fled... for I knew that thou art a gracious God, and merciful"). Two cautions: "too wide" is the film's own reading, and 4:2 is hindsight, spoken after Nineveh was spared.
- **L3:** "the city I hated" is UNSUPPORTED. No verse says Jonah hated Nineveh. "I'd sooner drown than see her spared" is also UNSUPPORTED. In 1:12 he gives his reason as "that the sea may be calm... for my sake." The nearest support is 4:1, "displeased... very angry," and that comes after the sparing.
- **L4:** RIGHT (1:7 "the lot fell upon Jonah"; 1:12 "for my sake").
- **L5:** "scribes demanded a sign" is RIGHT, with a nit: it was "scribes and... Pharisees" (12:38). "gave them only my name" is OVERCLAIMED. He gave "the sign of the prophet Jonas" (12:39), then explained it in 12:40-41.
- **L6:** RIGHT, word for word Matthew 12:40.
- **L7:** RIGHT (1:12).
- **L8:** "innocent, bearing what was ours" is UNSUPPORTED by the supplied text. It is sound theology elsewhere, but not in Matthew 12. "He gave Himself" is muddy, because Jonah also offered himself: "Take me up, and cast me forth" (1:12). So it is not a clean contrast.
- **L9:** The Jonah half is RIGHT ("brought up my life from corruption," 2:6; "vomited out Jonah," 2:10). The Jesus half goes beyond Matthew 12:40, which says three days, not rising.
- **L10:** UNSUPPORTED by the supplied text. It is true from the wider Gospels.
- **L11-14:** These are application, not claims, and fair. They echo 2:2 and 2:10.
- **Card:** "ONE SIGN ONLY" is RIGHT (12:39, "no sign... but the sign of the prophet Jonas").
- **Picture:** "lowering" Jonah softens "cast him forth" (1:15).

**Church group?** Yes, after line 3 is fixed. As it stands, a sharp reader will flag "hated."

## 5. BEST VERSION POSSIBLE
The best explainer would pay off line 2. "Mercy too wide" sets up Nineveh, then the film cuts Matthew 12:41: "they repented at the preaching of Jonas... a greater than Jonas is here." Jonah ran from mercy for his enemies, and that same mercy reaches you. That is the real arc, and it is in the text.

A second, rarely-taught line is the sailors' prayer, "lay not upon us innocent blood" (1:14). They threw a guilty man. With Jesus, that blood really was innocent. This would also put line 8 on a cited verse.

**ONE highest-value change:** add 12:41 (Nineveh repented) so the "mercy too wide" setup lands.
**Score: 6/10**

## CURIOSITY GAP
- The card and line 5 open "which sign?", and line 6 pays it off. Good.
- Line 2 opens "why was mercy too wide?" and it is never paid.
- **Score: 6/10.** Worth the wait for believers. It feels flat for anyone who already knows Matthew 12.

## THREE SMALLEST FIXES
- **L3:** "He sent me to Nineveh, that great city. I ran the other way, to Tarshish." (1:2-3)
- **L5:** "Jesus gave them one sign only: the sign of Jonah."
- **L8:** "The sailors begged, 'Lay not upon us innocent blood.' I was guilty. He was the innocent one, and He went down for us."
