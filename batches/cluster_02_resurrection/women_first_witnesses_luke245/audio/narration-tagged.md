[serious] The first report that Jesus was risen was dismissed as nonsense — by His own apostles.

<speaker name="scripture">And their words seemed to them as idle tales, and they believed them not.</speaker>

[matter-of-fact] The report came from women — and in that world, a woman's word carried almost no weight in court. No one inventing a resurrection would open it there. The Gospel writer Luke gains nothing by recording that — unless it is simply what happened.

[reflective] And Luke named these women long before — Mary Magdalene, and Joanna — following Jesus through Galilee, supporting Him from their own means. They watched Him die. They saw where He was buried. At dawn, they were back.

[quietly] So the angels pointed them to His own promise:

<speaker name="scripture">Why seek ye the living among the dead? He is not here, but is risen: remember how he spake unto you when he was yet in Galilee, Saying, The Son of man must be delivered into the hands of sinful men, and be crucified, and the third day rise again.</speaker>

[reflective] They had followed Him closely enough to hear that promise made. The promise had been kept — and they were the first to know. The idle tale was true, and it went first to the witnesses the world would not hear.

[emphasized] He still sends His word to the overlooked. You need no standing in any court to receive it. Stop seeking the living among the dead — the risen Christ is seeking you.
