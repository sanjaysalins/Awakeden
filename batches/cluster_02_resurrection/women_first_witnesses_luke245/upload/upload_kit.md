When the empty tomb was first reported, the news was not believed. Luke records that the apostles heard it as "idle tales" - nonsense. And the reason cuts deeper than doubt: the report came from women, and in the first-century world a woman's testimony carried almost no weight in a court of law. No one fabricating a resurrection story would ever have chosen those witnesses to open it.

Luke had named these women long before - Mary Magdalene, and Joanna - who followed Jesus from Galilee and supported His ministry from their own means. They watched Him die. They saw where He was buried. At dawn, they were the ones who came back. And so the angels pointed them not to a new idea, but to a promise they had been close enough to hear Him make:

"Why seek ye the living among the dead? He is not here, but is risen: remember how he spake unto you when he was yet in Galilee, Saying, The Son of man must be delivered into the hands of sinful men, and be crucified, and the third day rise again." (Luke 24:5-7)
