## 1. WHO IS THIS FOR
**Named viewer:** Grace, 40s, in a church small group, who has heard the "women as first witnesses" point once or twice.
- **Who stops:** Line 1. "Dismissed as nonsense, by His own apostles" is a strong hook.
- **Who finishes:** church people who enjoy apologetics.
- **Who shares:** a youth leader, probably near Easter.
- **Who it does not serve:** a total newcomer. The film never says who the women are, who "the apostles" or "the eleven" are, or what "Son of man" means (line 11).

## 2. MAIN TAKEAWAY
"The women were close enough to hear His promise, and the news went to them first." Line 12 carries it, and it is the freshest line in the film. But lines 3–4 pull toward a different idea, the "nobody would make this up" argument. So the film has two takeaways, and the clearer one (12) gets only 4 seconds.

## 3. GLOBAL AUDIENCE
- **Who / what / why in my words:** some women find Jesus's tomb empty. Angels remind them of His promise. The men don't believe them, but they were right.
- **Where I got lost:**
  - Line 3, "in court": which court? Why does a court matter here?
  - Line 8, "So": so, because of what?
- **Words that fail in translation:** "idle tales" (2), "spake" (11) and "Son of man" (11). The KJV wording is fine to keep, but the narrator never explains these words.

## 4. BIBLICALLY ACCURATE
- **L1** "first report… dismissed as nonsense, by His own apostles": **RIGHT** (24:9–11, "idle tales… believed them not"). It is first within Luke's account.
- **L2**: **RIGHT**, word for word 24:11.
- **L3** "a woman's word carried almost no weight in court": **UNSUPPORTED** by any supplied verse. It is an outside history claim, and "almost no weight" is **OVERCLAIMED**: women's testimony was limited in some settings, not worthless. It is also the line most likely to be challenged in comments.
- **L4** "No one inventing a resurrection would open it there": an apologetic **INFERENCE**, not text. Fine only if L3 is softened.
- **L5** "They watched Him die": **RIGHT**. 23:49 says they "stood afar off, beholding these things," and the far-off cross picture matches.
- **L6**: **RIGHT** (23:55, "beheld the sepulchre, and how his body was laid").
- **L7** "At dawn, they were back": **RIGHT** (24:1, "very early in the morning").
- **L8** "So the angels pointed them": **OVERCLAIMED** on two points.
  - Luke 24:4 says "two men… in shining garments." Calling them angels is a fair reading, but it is not the word in these verses.
  - "So" suggests a cause that the text doesn't give. The text says they spoke "as they were much perplexed… afraid" (24:4–5).
- **L9–11**: **RIGHT**, word for word 24:5–7.
- **L12** "followed Him closely enough to hear that promise made": **RIGHT**. Three verses back it up:
  - 24:6, "spake unto you… in Galilee"
  - 24:8, "they remembered his words"
  - 8:2–3 and 23:55, "came with him from Galilee"
- **L13** "it went first to the witnesses the world would not hear": **OVERCLAIMED**. In the text, the ones who would not hear are the apostles (24:11), not "the world." The film misses its own irony.
- **L14** "He still sends His word to the overlooked": this is application, which is fine. But in Luke the word comes through the two men, not from Jesus in person.
- **L15**: application backed by 19:10 ("seek and to save"). **RIGHT** as application. The flip from "seek ye" to "seeking you" is good.
- **Pictures:**
  - The risen Jesus at sunrise next to the women imports John 20 or Matthew 28. Jesus does not appear to the women in Luke 24:1–11.
  - "Three women" undercounts. The text names three plus "other women" (24:10) and "certain others" (24:1).

**Would I send it to my church group?** Yes, after L3 and L13 are fixed.

## 5. BEST VERSION POSSIBLE
The best explainer would **name them**. Luke names them: "Mary Magdalene, and Joanna, and Mary the mother of James" (24:10). Luke 8:2–3 says Mary had "seven devils" cast out, and Joanna was the wife of "Herod's steward." That shows the overlooked from the text itself (a healed outcast and a woman from the king's household), so the film doesn't need an outside court claim.

**ONE highest-value change:** replace L3–4 with the named women from 8:2–3 and 24:10.

**Score:** 6.5/10.

## CURIOSITY GAP
- **Opens:** line 1 (why would His own men call it nonsense?).
- **Pays off:** lines 12–13.
- **Score:** 6/10.

It is worth the wait, but lines 3–4 answer a different question (is it invented?) before the real payoff (they were the ones who listened).

## THREE SMALLEST FIXES
1. **Line 3:** "The report came from women: Mary Magdalene, once possessed by seven devils, and Joanna, from Herod's own household."
2. **Line 8:** "Then two men in shining garments pointed them to His own promise:"
3. **Line 13:** "The idle tale was true, and it went first to the witnesses even His own apostles would not hear."
