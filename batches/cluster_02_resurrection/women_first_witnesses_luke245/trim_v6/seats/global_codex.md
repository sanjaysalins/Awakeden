## Version X

**Newcomer**
- About: Jesus and the people who reported that he had risen.
- What happens: The apostles dismiss the women’s report, but angels remind them Jesus had predicted his death and return to life.
- Why it matters: The risen Jesus may be seeking people who feel overlooked.
- Likely to lose them: The long KJV quotation, especially “delivered into the hands of sinful men.”

**Bible reader**
- Gains: The apostles’ disbelief makes the women’s first report more striking.
- Luke accuracy: “The promise had been kept” is an interpretation; in Luke, the women remember Jesus’ words and report them, but the apostles do not believe. “They were the first to know” is also a little too broad: the women had heard the promise before, and Luke says they found the tomb empty.

**Curiosity:** Yes—the dismissal opens a question in the first seconds; the angels’ reminder and the apostles’ response pay it off. **7/10.**

**Ending:** Yes. It ends on the risen Christ, though “is seeking you” is a devotional application rather than Luke’s wording.

## Version Y

**Newcomer**
- About: Jesus and the people who reported that he had risen.
- What happens: The apostles dismiss the women’s report, but the women had heard Jesus predict his death and return to life.
- Why it matters: The risen Jesus may be seeking people who feel overlooked.
- Likely to lose them: The same long KJV quotation, especially “delivered into the hands of sinful men.”

**Bible reader**
- Gains: It emphasizes that the women were followers who had heard Jesus’ teaching.
- Luke accuracy: “They had followed Him closely enough to hear that promise made” is not quite what Luke says. Luke 8 names women who accompanied Jesus; Luke 24 says the women remembered his words, but doesn’t say when or where they heard this particular promise.

**Curiosity:** Yes—the opening dismissal raises the question; the promise and the women’s report answer it. **7/10.**

**Ending:** Yes. It ends on Jesus, with a clear devotional close.

## Pick: X

**One cut-only fix:** Delete “and they were the first to know.” It overstates what Luke says and the women’s discovery of the empty tomb already carries the point.
