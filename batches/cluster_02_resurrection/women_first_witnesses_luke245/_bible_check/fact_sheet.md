## The women — who they are
- **[SPECIFIED]** They are a GROUP of named women, not one: "It was Mary Magdalene, and Joanna, and Mary the mother of James, and other women that were with them, which told these things unto the apostles" (Luke 24:10). Image: several women together — never a lone figure in the report/dawn beats.
- **[SPECIFIED — for any beat depicting the cross]** They watched the crucifixion FROM AFAR: "the women that followed him from Galilee, stood afar off, beholding these things" (Luke 23:49). Image (Luke-scoped): women beholding the cross at a DISTANCE — never crowded at the foot of the cross (that is John's Mary, not this Luke beat).
- **[SPECIFIED — for any beat depicting the burial]** They saw the sepulchre and HOW the body was laid: "beheld the sepulchre, and how his body was laid" (Luke 23:55). Image: women looking on as the body is laid in the tomb — Luke does NOT say they watched the stone rolled shut / the tomb sealed (that is Matthew/Mark), so no closing-stone beat is required.
- **[CONTEXT — narrative, un-renderable, drives NO image]** Their identity and support are Luke facts but nothing a still can match or contradict: Mary Magdalene "out of whom went seven devils" (Luke 8:2); the women "ministered unto him of their substance" (Luke 8:3, their OWN means); they "followed him from Galilee" (Luke 23:49) / "came with him from Galilee" (Luke 23:55). Cite each verse only for its own claim — Luke 8:2 is identity/healing, Luke 8:3 alone carries "of their substance." These inform WHO the women are; they are not visual laws.
- **[CONSTRAINED]** Dress (secondary/cultural, not stated by the cited verses): 1st-century Judean — simple wool tunics, a woman's mantle that may be drawn over the head, sandals, muted natural dyes. Not required by Scripture; must not contradict the period.

## Dawn at the tomb
- **[SPECIFIED]** First day of the week, VERY EARLY, bringing SPICES: "Now upon the first day of the week, very early in the morning, they came unto the sepulchre, bringing the spices which they had prepared, and certain others with them" (Luke 24:1). Image: women bringing spices toward a rock-cut tomb. ("very early in the morning" is specified; the exact light level is not — see constrained.)
- **[SPECIFIED]** The stone was found ROLLED AWAY: "And they found the stone rolled away from the sepulchre" (Luke 24:2). Image: the stone is AWAY from the opening (not blocking it). Luke does not state the stone's size or the opening's height (see constrained).
- **[SPECIFIED]** The body was NOT there: "they entered in, and found not the body of the Lord Jesus" (Luke 24:3). Image: an empty hewn resting place; no corpse.
- **[CONSTRAINED]** Light level (secondary): Luke 24:1 "very early" permits either grey pre-dawn OR first light and does NOT require full daylight; the sunrise reading is Mark 16:2 ("at the rising of the sun"), not cited here. Free to paint pre-dawn or breaking dawn — just not bright midday.
- **[SPECIFIED — Luke-grounded typology]** The tomb is HEWN IN STONE and NEW (never before used): "laid it in a sepulchre that was hewn in stone, wherein never man before was laid" (Luke 23:53). Image: a rock-cut tomb — never a freestanding masonry building, dome, or coffin.
- **[CONSTRAINED]** 1st-century Judean rock-cut tomb detail (secondary, archaeology — beyond Luke 23:53's "hewn in stone"): small crawl-height opening, disc/boulder rolling stone in a groove, low hewn chamber with a waist-low bench/arcosolium, tool-marked walls. A GARDEN setting is permitted (John 19:41, non-contradiction) but is Johannine, not Luke-required. No church/dome forms, no columns, no burning lamps inside. Spices may be shown in period jars/pouches (form is licence, not specified).
- **[CONSTRAINED — Luke fence]** NO Roman guard/soldiers at the women's dawn tomb: the watch and the sealed stone are Matthew-only (Matt 27:65-66; 28:4), absent from Luke's account — do not put guards in this Luke dawn beat (parallels the foot-of-cross and closing-stone fences above).

## The two men / angels
- **[SPECIFIED]** TWO men in SHINING garments stood by them: "behold, two men stood by them in shining garments" (Luke 24:4); later named "a vision of angels" (Luke 24:23). Image (Luke-scoped): TWO radiant figures in bright/shining garments — two, not one; STANDING (Luke says "stood by them"). Note the synoptic difference: Matthew's angel sits ON the stone (Matt 28:2), Mark's young man sits INSIDE on the right (Mark 16:5) — Luke's two STAND, so keep them standing.
- **[SPECIFIED]** The women were AFRAID and BOWED their faces to the earth: "as they were afraid, and bowed down their faces to the earth" (Luke 24:5). Image: women bowed low, faces toward the ground before the shining figures.

## The angels' words (the prophecy remembered)
- **[SPECIFIED — text/content]** The words are Luke 24:5b-7 verbatim: "Why seek ye the living among the dead? He is not here, but is risen: remember how he spake unto you when he was yet in Galilee, Saying, The Son of man must be delivered into the hands of sinful men, and be crucified, and the third day rise again." This is spoken content, not a rendered object. **Never animate/render legible text** (no scroll, no letters). The women's response is that "they remembered his words" (Luke 24:8) — a remembering/recognition beat, not a written one.

## The apostles' unbelief
- **[SPECIFIED]** The women reported to the eleven AND all the rest, and the report was DISMISSED as untrue: "returned from the sepulchre, and told all these things unto the eleven, and to all the rest" (Luke 24:9); "their words seemed to them as idle tales, and they believed them not" (Luke 24:11). Image: women reporting to the gathered disciples, whose response is DISBELIEF. Luke specifies the unbelief, NOT the exact gesture (a "turning away" pose is licence, not required) nor the location — the setting (indoor room, courtyard, etc.) is licence; keep it 1st-century, never a modern court.

## A woman's word carried no legal weight (the narration's apologetic frame)
- **[CONSTRAINED — secondary/cultural, drives NO required visual]** In 1st-century Jewish and Greco-Roman practice a woman's testimony carried little formal legal weight. This is a HISTORICAL inference used by the narration (why no forger would open a resurrection with women) — it is NOT stated in Luke 24 and guards no image. If a "court"/testimony idea is shown at all, keep it 1st-century — NEVER an anachronistic modern courtroom, gavel, or dock.

## The risen Christ (landing)
- **[CONSTRAINED]** The risen Christ is alive — no gore, no crown of thorns, no death pallor. Luke-scoped: IF wounds are shown they are in the HANDS and FEET — "Behold my hands and my feet... he shewed them his hands and his feet" (Luke 24:39-40) — NOT the pierced side (that is John 20:20/27). "Alive and whole" means living and bodily, NOT unscarred — the hand/foot marks may remain. Dress: 1st-century Judean, simple tunic, sandals (period, not mandated).
- **[FREE — thematic, not a Luke-24 visual fact]** The narration's landing theme is the living Christ SEEKING the overlooked ("the Son of man is come to seek and to save that which was lost," Luke 19:10). This is a Christ-lens graft chosen by the narration (panel-defended at the narration stage), NOT a fact this pericope depicts — so it does not bind the still. The passage's OWN climax that a landing image may lean on is "He is not here, but is risen" (Luke 24:6): the empty tomb / the risen Christ foretold and kept.
