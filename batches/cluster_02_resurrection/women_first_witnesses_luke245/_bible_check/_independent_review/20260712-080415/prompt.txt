## The women — who they are
- **[SPECIFIED]** They are a GROUP of named women, not one: "It was Mary Magdalene, and Joanna, and Mary the mother of James, and other women that were with them, which told these things unto the apostles" (Luke 24:10). Image: several women together — never a lone figure in the report/dawn beats.
- **[SPECIFIED]** They ministered to Him of their OWN MEANS: "Mary called Magdalene, out of whom went seven devils" (Luke 8:2); "Joanna the wife of Chuza Herod's steward, and Susanna, and many others, which ministered unto him of their substance" (Luke 8:3). Image: women as long-standing supporters of Jesus.
- **[SPECIFIED]** They FOLLOWED HIM FROM GALILEE: "the women that followed him from Galilee" (Luke 23:49); "the women also, which came with him from Galilee" (Luke 23:55). (This "from Galilee" element is carried by 23:49/55 — NOT by Luke 8:2-3.)
- **[SPECIFIED]** They watched the crucifixion FROM AFAR: "the women that followed him from Galilee, stood afar off, beholding these things" (Luke 23:49). Image (Luke-scoped): women beholding the cross at a DISTANCE — never crowded at the foot of the cross (that is John's Mary, not this Luke beat).
- **[SPECIFIED]** They saw the sepulchre and HOW the body was laid: "beheld the sepulchre, and how his body was laid" (Luke 23:55). Image: women looking on as the body is laid in the tomb — Luke does NOT say they watched the stone rolled shut / the tomb sealed (that is Matthew/Mark), so do not require a closing-stone beat here.
- **[CONSTRAINED]** Dress (secondary/cultural, not stated by the cited verses): 1st-century Judean — simple wool tunics, a woman's mantle that may be drawn over the head, sandals, muted natural dyes. Not required by Scripture; must not contradict the period.

## Dawn at the tomb
- **[SPECIFIED]** First day of the week, VERY EARLY, bringing SPICES: "Now upon the first day of the week, very early in the morning, they came unto the sepulchre, bringing the spices which they had prepared" (Luke 24:1). Image: women bringing spices toward a rock-cut tomb. ("very early in the morning" is specified; the exact light level is not — see constrained.)
- **[SPECIFIED]** The stone was found ROLLED AWAY: "And they found the stone rolled away from the sepulchre" (Luke 24:2). Image: the stone is AWAY from the opening (not blocking it). Luke does not state the stone's size or the opening's height (see constrained).
- **[SPECIFIED]** The body was NOT there: "they entered in, and found not the body of the Lord Jesus" (Luke 24:3). Image: an empty hewn resting place; no corpse.
- **[CONSTRAINED]** Light level (secondary): Luke 24:1 "very early" permits either grey pre-dawn OR first light and does NOT require full daylight; the sunrise reading is Mark 16:2 ("at the rising of the sun"), not cited here. Free to paint pre-dawn or breaking dawn — just not bright midday.
- **[CONSTRAINED]** 1st-century Judean rock-cut tomb typology (secondary, archaeology — Luke requires only a sepulchre entered by the women with the stone rolled away): small crawl-height opening, disc/boulder rolling stone in a groove, low hewn chamber with a waist-low bench/arcosolium, tool-marked walls. ## The two men / angels
- **[SPECIFIED]** TWO men in SHINING garments stood by them: "behold, two men stood by them in shining garments" (Luke 24:4); later named "a vision of angels" (Luke 24:23). Image (Luke-scoped): TWO radiant figures in bright/shining garments — two, not one; STANDING (Luke says "stood by them"). Note the synoptic difference: Matthew's angel sits ON the stone (Matt 28:2), Mark's young man sits INSIDE on the right (Mark 16:5) — Luke's two STAND, so keep them standing.
- **[SPECIFIED]** The women were AFRAID and BOWED their faces to the earth: "as they were afraid, and bowed down their faces to the earth" (Luke 24:5). Image: women bowed low, faces toward the ground before the shining figures.
