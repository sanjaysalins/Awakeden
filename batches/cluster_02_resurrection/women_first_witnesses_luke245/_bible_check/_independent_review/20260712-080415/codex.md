# Independent review — codex (OK, 201s)

**Findings**

- **[SPECIFIED] “They ministered to Him of their OWN MEANS”** overstates the cited support. Luke 8:2-3 supports Mary Magdalene, Joanna, Susanna, and “many others” ministering, but it does **not** identify **Mary the mother of James** or Luke 24:10’s “other women” as doing so. Do not apply this to the whole Luke 24 witness group as specified.

- **“Image: women as long-standing supporters of Jesus”** is not a directly visual biblical fact. It is an inference from Luke 8’s earlier placement and “ministered unto him of their substance,” not something every woman in Luke 24 is explicitly said to be.

- **[SPECIFIED] “Image: women bringing spices toward a rock-cut tomb”** cites Luke 24:1, but Luke 24:1 does not say rock-cut. The rock/hewn detail comes from Luke 23:53, not this citation.

- **[SPECIFIED] “Image: an empty hewn resting place”** cites Luke 24:3, but Luke 24:3 only says they entered and did not find the body. “Hewn” again needs Luke 23:53.

- **[CONSTRAINED] “Luke requires only a sepulchre entered by the women with the stone rolled away”** is false. Luke also explicitly says the body was laid “in a sepulchre that was hewn in stone, wherein never man before was laid” (Luke 23:53). “Hewn in stone” and “never man before” belong in **specified**, not archaeology-only constraint.

- **Missing specified fact:** Luke 23:53 says Jesus was **wrapped in linen**. Since the sheet includes a burial-looking-on beat, it should guard against showing an unwrapped body, coffin, or other burial form.

- **Missing specified fact:** Luke 23:53 says the tomb was one “wherein never man before was laid.” The sheet should prohibit prior bodies, bones, ossuaries, or a visibly already-used tomb if the interior is shown.

- **[CONSTRAINED] “small crawl-height opening, disc/boulder rolling stone in a groove, low hewn chamber with a waist-low bench/arcosolium, tool-marked walls”** is too prescriptive for this bucket. Some of this may be plausible archaeology, but Luke does not require most of it. Treat as optional historical guidance, not a checkable biblical constraint.

- **Missing specified reaction:** Luke 24:4 says the women were “much perplexed thereabout” before the two men appear. The sheet includes fear and bowing from Luke 24:5, but omits this explicit empty-tomb reaction.

- **[SPECIFIED] “women reporting to a group of men (the apostles and others)”** overstates Luke 24:9. The eleven are male, but “all the rest” is not specified as all male. Require “the eleven and others,” not a male-only recipient group.

- **“Luke states no location — the setting … is licence”** is too loose. Luke 24:9 says they “returned from the sepulchre” before telling the eleven and the rest. Exact location is licence, but the report should not be shown as happening at the tomb.

- **“react with disbelief / turning away”**: disbelief is specified by Luke 24:11; “turning away” is not. It can be an artistic shorthand, but not a specified fact.

- **“A woman’s word carried no legal weight … why no forger would open a resurrection with women”** is not a Luke 24 fact. The sheet admits it is historical, but “used by the narration” makes a modern apologetic inference sound narratival. Keep it outside biblical-fact checks.

- **[CONSTRAINED] “IF wounds are shown they are in the HANDS and FEET”** overclaims the wording of Luke 24:39-40. Luke says Jesus showed his hands and feet; it does not explicitly say wounds, scars, or marks. The side wound exclusion is valid for Luke-scoping, but hand/foot “wounds” should be labeled as inference.

- **[FREE] “seeking the overlooked” from Luke 19:10** is not what the cited verse says; it says “lost.” Since this is marked thematic/free it does not break a visual fact, but it should not be presented as the meaning of the citation.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Move Luke 23:53’s hewn-stone, unused tomb, and linen-wrapped burial details into SPECIFIED with correct citations.
2. Narrow overbroad SPECIFIED claims about the women ministering/supporting Jesus and about the report audience being male.
3. Separate real Luke-specified facts from archaeology, apologetic framing, and inferred wound/thematic details.
