# Independent review — codex (OK, 60s)

**Findings**

1. **Wrong bucket / citation overreach:** “**[SPECIFIED] They followed Jesus from GALILEE and ministered to Him of their OWN MEANS**” cites Luke 8:2-3. Luke 8:2-3 supports that named women ministered “of their substance,” but it does **not** say there that they followed Him “from Galilee.” That comes from Luke 23:49/55. Also, “**1st-century Judean dress with mantles drawn over the head**” is not stated in Luke 8:2-3 and should not sit inside a specified fact.

2. **Invented visual detail in specified bucket:** “**Image: women observing the burial / the tomb being closed**” overreaches Luke 23:55. The verse says they “beheld the sepulchre, and how his body was laid.” It does **not** say they saw the tomb being closed or the stone moved into place.

3. **Invented tomb details attached to specified claims:** “**a great stone moved aside from a LOW opening — never inside the chamber, never small**” goes beyond Luke 24:2. The verse says the stone was rolled away from the sepulchre. It does not specify “great,” “low opening,” or the stone’s size. “Great stone” is Matthew 27:60, not the cited Luke text.

4. **Constrained archaeology is too specific and possibly misleading:** “**disc/boulder rolling stone in a groove, low hewn chamber with a waist-low bench/arcosolium, tool-marked walls, garden setting**” is presented as constrained. Luke only requires a sepulchre/tomb, entered by the women, with a stone rolled away. A garden setting is Johannine (“in the garden a new sepulchre,” John 19:41), not Luke. These details should be softened or moved toward licence unless externally justified as secondary visual plausibility.

5. **Citation misapplication / synoptic mix-up:** “**standing (not sitting on the stone — that is Matthew/Mark)**” is inaccurate. Matthew has one angel sitting on the stone (Matt. 28:2). Mark has a young man sitting **inside** on the right side (Mark 16:5), not sitting on the stone. The Luke rule “standing” is fine, but the parenthetical misstates Mark.

6. **Luke-scoped dawn claim overstates the cited KJV:** “**first light / breaking dawn — NOT the pitch-dark of John 20:1**” is stronger than Luke 24:1 KJV, which says “very early in the morning.” It does not explicitly require visible dawn light. Also John says “when it was yet dark,” not necessarily “pitch-dark.” Better: specified = first day, very early morning; dawn lighting = constrained/free.

7. **The apologetic frame is asserted as narrative meaning, not biblical fact:** “**This is the narration’s rhetorical point (why no forger would open a resurrection with women)**” is not stated by Luke 24. It may be an apologetic inference, but it should not be described as “the narration’s rhetorical point” in a biblical fact sheet.

8. **Risen Christ section mixes Luke 24 with unrelated thematic claim:** “**The landing image = the living Christ seeking the overlooked (‘the Son of man is come to seek and to save that which was lost,’ Luke 19:10).**” Luke 19:10 is true Scripture, but it does not specifically state that the resurrection appearance/landing image is “seeking the overlooked.” That is theological interpretation, not a visual fact guarded by the cited verse.

9. **Potential missing specified guardrail:** Luke 24:4 says the women were “**much perplexed thereabout**” before the two men appeared. If depicting the empty tomb moment, this is an explicit emotional/action detail that could be guarded.

10. **Potential missing specified guardrail:** Luke 24:8 says “**they remembered his words**.” The sheet includes the angelic command to remember, but not the specified result. If a sequence includes the prophecy-remembered beat, this should be guarded.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Remove invented visual details from specified facts, especially head mantles, tomb closure, low opening, great stone, and dawn light.
2. Separate Luke’s explicit claims from harmonized, historical, or apologetic inferences.
3. Correct the synoptic parenthetical about Matthew/Mark and soften the tomb archaeology constraints.
