# Independent review — codex (OK, 32s)

Hook score: 7/10. It is arresting, but “If you were inventing…” is a familiar apologetics setup and “never” overstates the case.

Landing score: 5/10. “Seek Him” is a stock closer. This exact ending could close many resurrection pieces, so it does not fully land from this narration’s own image.

Findings:

- “in a world that counted a woman's word as nearly worthless in court” is too broad. It compresses a complicated historical/legal claim into a sweeping line. “Court” also risks making the point sound like modern legal admissibility rather than social credibility.
- “No forger keeps that verse” is overclaimed. A forger might keep an embarrassing detail for realism. Better: it is unlikely, not impossible.
- “Luke was writing down what happened” is doctrinally acceptable, but rhetorically it jumps from probability to certainty. The narration should not make the apologetic argument carry more weight than it can.
- KJV issue: “[scripture] and their words seemed…” should be “And their words seemed…” if presented as verbatim KJV Luke 24:11.
- “Remember — you can only remember what you stayed close enough to hear” is clever but not airtight. Luke 24:6 says the angels told them to remember Jesus’ Galilee words; it does not prove every woman named heard that exact saying firsthand.
- “The ones love kept closest” is evocative, but slightly sentimental and under-specified. The text shows faithful presence; “love kept closest” is an inference.
- “Your standing in the world's court was never the question” shifts from women-as-witnesses/apologetic credibility to personal insecurity/status. That is a thread-swap near the CTA.
- “Stop seeking the living among the dead” risks applying the angel’s rebuke as a direct moral command without explaining what “the dead” means for the listener. On first hearing, it may sound poetic rather than clear.
- The CTA leans imperative-heavy: “Stop… Seek Him.” It is not works-salvation, but it lacks the grace-anchored invitation already available in the text: Christ is risen before they understand, believe, or persuade anyone.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Replace the overclaims: “never,” “nearly worthless in court,” and “No forger keeps that verse.”
2. Repair the thread-swap in the CTA so it lands on faithful witnesses, remembered words, and the risen Christ.
3. Correct the KJV capitalization: “And their words seemed…”
