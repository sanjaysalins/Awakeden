### Doctrinal soundness — real problems

**1. The script misreads what Luke’s angel is actually saying.**

After `They never left. And the angels knew it:`, the piece quotes `remember how he spake unto you when he was yet in Galilee` and then claims:

> `Remember — you can only remember what you stayed close enough to hear.`

In Luke 24:6–8, *remember* points to **Jesus’s prior resurrection prophecy in Galilee** (24:7), not to the women’s long-arc physical faithfulness. Luke 24:8: `And they remembered his words` — recall of **what Jesus said**, not “proximity to Jesus over time.”

The metadata thread admits the stretch: *“the angel's ‘remember... Galilee’ certifies them as long-arc eyewitnesses.”* That certification is **imported**, not in the text. Orthodox claim, but **exegetically forced** for freshness.

**2. `And the angels knew it` assigns intent Luke does not give.**

The angels announce resurrection and cite Galilee teaching. Nothing in Luke 24:5–6 shows the angels “knew” the women “never left.” That line romanticizes the text beyond what it says.

**3. `No forger keeps that verse. Luke was writing down what happened.`**

Still an apologetic conclusion stated as certainty. Defensible in genre (“Resurrection on Trial”), but it leaps from narrative detail to historiographical verdict in one sentence. A hostile listener hears **proof-by-rhetoric**, not proof-by-text.

**4. `The first witnesses at the empty tomb of Jesus were women`**

Luke-local this holds. Stated absolutely with no “Luke records” guardrail, it collides with John’s tomb race (Peter/“that other disciple” in John 20:3–8). Not heresy, but **overbroad** for a piece whose header cites Luke but whose narrator speaks in universals.

### Clarity on first hearing — fails for a zero-Bible listener

**1. `They never left` is ambiguous and nearly self-contradicting.**

Immediately before: `following Jesus from Galilee` (they left Galilee), `They watched Him die`, `they were back` (implying they had gone away and returned). A cold listener cannot tell whether “never left” means never left **Jesus**, never left **Jerusalem**, never left **the tomb**, or never left **the story**. The script never names the object. Clever compression, not clear speech.

**2. The `remember` turn is a logic trick on first hearing.**

Listener hears: angels say remember → narrator redefines remember as “stayed close enough to hear” → conclusion “Heaven gave the first news… to witnesses the world would not hear.” Step 2 does not follow from the quoted verse without Bible background. **Self-contradiction risk:** if “remember” just means “recall what you heard in Galilee,” then apostles who also heard those words should be equally certified — yet the piece’s whole point is that **women** were sidelined. The script never resolves that tension.

**3. `He has not changed` is a hard pivot.**

No antecedent in the prior beats except vague “He.” Zero-Bible-knowledge listener does not know what “changed” refers to — God’s values? resurrection? cultural contempt for women? The line reads like a imported stock phrase (Hebrews 13:8 echo), not a earned turn from the women-witness thread.
