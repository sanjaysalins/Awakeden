# Independent review — gemini (OK, 64s)

As an independent, adversarial reviewer, here is my evaluation of the artifact.

LENS EVALUATION:
- **Doctrinal soundness**: Generally sound, but the phrase "paying His way from their own money" is overly transactional. It reduces the biblical concept of "ministered unto him of their substance" (Luke 8:3) to something resembling a modern campaign expense, losing the relational care and service implied by the text.
- **KJV verbatim**: PASS. Both Luke 24:11 ("And their words seemed to them as idle tales, and they believed them not.") and Luke 24:5-6 ("Why seek ye the living among the dead? He is not here, but is risen: remember how he spake unto you when he was yet in Galilee") are exact KJV matches.
- **Clarity on first hearing**: There is a significant referent issue. The line "Luke gains nothing by that verse" appears two sentences *after* the Scripture quote, following a sentence about "inventing a resurrection." An audio listener will be confused about which "verse" is being referenced (the quoted verse about the apostles, or the unquoted fact that women were the witnesses). Additionally, the transition into the next paragraph ("But Luke named these women long before") uses a false contrast. The "But" implies opposition to the previous point, when in fact it is building on their historical credibility.
- **Grace-anchored conviction**: PASS. The landing ("You need no standing in any court to receive His word") perfectly anchors the conviction in grace, avoiding any works-based, fear-based, or gain/loss framing.
- **Freshness = faithful depth**: PASS. Opening with the apostles' unbelief ("dismissed as nonsense — by His own apostles") is a fresh, textually faithful angle that avoids standard Easter clichés.
- **One thread spine**: The script struggles slightly at the seam between the apologetic argument ("weight in court", "inventing a resurrection") and the devotional narrative ("never abandoned Him"). The pivot feels a bit disjointed, though the CTA successfully manages to tie the "court" imagery back in at the end.
- **SCORE THE HOOK**: 8/10. "The first report that Jesus was risen was dismissed as nonsense — by His own apostles." It is a strong, narrative-driven opening that subverts expectations and avoids template phrasing.
- **SCORE THE LANDING**: 9/10. "Stop seeking the living among the dead — the risen Christ is already seeking you." This is an exceptional closing. It takes the specific vocabulary of the angel's question and flips it into a powerful, grace-anchored CTA that echoes Luke 19:10. It is entirely bespoke to this script and could not end a different piece.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Fix the muddied referent in "Luke gains nothing by that verse" (the audio listener will lose track of what verse is meant) and remove the false contrast of "But" in "But Luke named these women long before".
2. Change "paying His way from their own money" to something less transactional that better reflects the relational care of Luke 8:3 (e.g., "supporting Him from their own means").
3. Smooth the pivot between the apologetic "court/inventing" argument and the devotional "never abandoned Him" narrative so they flow as a single cohesive thought.
