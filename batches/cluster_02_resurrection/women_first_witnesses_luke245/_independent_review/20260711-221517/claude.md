## Findings

**KJV verbatim — CLEAN.**
- Quote 1 matches Luke 24:11 KJV word-for-word, including the restored verse-initial "And."
- Quote 2 matches Luke 24:5b–6 exactly (verified against `data/kjv_cache.json`). One honest caveat: KJV 24:6 ends at "Galilee**,**" — the sentence runs on into v7. The quote stops mid-sentence, and the *content* of the prophecy ("the Son of man must… rise again") is never quoted, only paraphrased ("He had told them He would rise"). Disclosed in the header, and the paraphrase is accurate to 24:7 — but quoting through v7 would make the "remember" beat land on Scripture instead of narrator summary.

**Doctrine — SOUND.** Every narrative claim checks out against the cited texts: Mary Magdalene and Joanna are named in both Luke 8:2–3 and 24:10; "paying His way from their own money" is a fair rendering of "ministered unto him of their substance"; 23:49/23:55/24:1 support watched-die / saw-buried / back-at-dawn. Crucially, "they had stayed close enough to hear it" is grounded in the angel's own words ("remember how he spake unto **you**") — the fresh insight is textual, not invented. The landing reversal rests on Luke 19:10. No contrarian reading anywhere.

**Grace-anchored — YES.** No fear, no gain/loss. "You need no standing in any court to receive His word" and "already seeking you" put the initiative on Christ. Correct.

**Specific weaknesses (cite-level):**
1. *"Luke gains nothing by that verse"* / *"But Luke named these women long before"* — a zero-Bible listener doesn't know who Luke is. He arrives unintroduced as an argument-carrying character. One word fixes it ("the writer, Luke" or "the Gospel writer").
2. *"a woman's word carried almost no weight in court"* — extra-biblical historical claim stated as flat fact. It's the standard, defensible apologetic (and "almost" softens it), but it is the only load-bearing claim in the piece not checkable against the text itself.
3. *"He still trusts the overlooked."* — the one sentence that drifts past the text. God *entrusting the first news* to overlooked witnesses is in the passage; "still trusts" as a standing general claim is narrator inference, and "trusts" is theologically loose. "He still sends His word to the overlooked" would be exact.
4. The final beat carries **two** landing ideas fused: (a) overlooked-witnesses/no-standing and (b) seek-reversal. Both are earned, but stacking them in four sentences slightly blurs which one the listener walks away with. The seek-reversal is the stronger close; (a) could compress to one clause.

**HOOK: 8/10.** "The first report that Jesus was risen was dismissed as nonsense — by His own apostles" is concrete, ironic, non-template, and lands inside 5 seconds. Docked from higher only because "dismissed as nonsense" tells before the KJV shows — the verse then says the same thing, a small redundancy.

**LANDING: 8/10.** "Stop seeking the living among the dead — the risen Christ is already seeking you" is built from THIS piece's quoted verb, and the He-seeks-first reversal (19:10) could not close a different piece as-is. Not stock. Docked for the double-landing crowding noted in #4.

**Thread spine — HOLDS.** Dismissed report → the never-left witnesses → the promise they were present to hear → the overlooked receive first → He seeks you. One spine; the court frame serves it as a single beat rather than swapping threads.

VERDICT: PASS
TOP FIXES:

2. Tighten "He still trusts the overlooked" to a text-exact verb (e.g. "He still sends His first word to the overlooked") and compress the final beat so the seek-reversal stands alone as the single closing idea.
3. Consider extending quote 2 through Luke 24:7 so the prophecy's content is Scripture's words, not narrator paraphrase (the KJV sentence currently ends mid-thought at "Galilee,").
