# Independent review — codex (OK, 36s)

HOOK SCORE: 8/10  
LANDING SCORE: 6/10

**Findings**

- **Doctrinal imprecision:** “He still trusts the overlooked” is weak theologically. Scripture shows Christ entrusting testimony to these women, but “trusts the overlooked” risks making the point about social status as a spiritual category rather than God’s sovereign grace and truthfulness.

- **Historical overclaim:** “a woman’s word carried almost no weight in court” is broadly defensible as cultural framing, but stated too flatly. “Almost no weight” is sweeping and could invite pushback, especially because Luke’s point is not strictly courtroom admissibility but apostolic unbelief and unexpected witnesses.

- **Possible apologetics thread-swap:** “No one inventing a resurrection would open it there. Luke gains nothing by that verse” shifts from the women’s faithfulness to an apologetic-for-authenticity argument. It fits the series title, but it briefly competes with the stronger spine: the witnesses who stayed.

- **Clarity issue:** “They never abandoned Him” overstates the text. Luke shows they remained near the cross and burial, but “never abandoned” sounds absolute and could conflict with the broader Gospel picture of disciples failing, fleeing, or misunderstanding.

- **KJV check:** The scripture wording appears verbatim KJV:
  - “And their words seemed to them as idle tales, and they believed them not.”
  - “Why seek ye the living among the dead? He is not here, but is risen: remember how he spake unto you when he was yet in Galilee”
  No altered words found.

- **Landing problem:** “Stop seeking the living among the dead — the risen Christ is already seeking you” uses the text’s verb well, but the final sentence could still close many resurrection/devotional pieces. It is better than “Come to Jesus,” but not yet specific enough to this piece’s women-witness spine.

- **Grace-anchored conviction:** Mostly sound. “You need no standing in any court to receive His word” is strong and grace-shaped. But “Stop seeking” needs more concrete connection to the listener, or it can sound like a generic religious command.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Tighten the landing so it resolves the overlooked-witnesses thread, not just a general “seek” CTA.
2. Soften or specify the courtroom claim about women’s testimony.
3. Replace “He still trusts the overlooked” with a more biblically precise claim about Christ entrusting His word.
