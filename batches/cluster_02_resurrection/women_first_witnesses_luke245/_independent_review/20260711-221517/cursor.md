### Doctrinal soundness
**Mostly sound; two soft overclaims.**

- **Orthodox core:** women first at the tomb, apostles’ dismissal (24:11), long-arc naming (8:2–3 → 23:49/55 → 24:10), angelic *remember* tied to Galilee teaching — all standard, not contrarian.
- **Overclaim —** `They never abandoned Him.` Luke 23:56 has the women resting on the Sabbath per commandment before returning at dawn. *Never abandoned* compresses that into a loyalty slogan a hostile listener can challenge. Not heresy, but it trades precision for punch.
- **Overclaim —** `a woman's word carried almost no weight in court.` Stated as courtroom fact for a zero-context listener. It’s a common cultural-reading move in evangelical exposition, but it’s asserted, not shown from Luke. Fine for Awakeden; risky under “biblically faithful, no invented doctrine” if taken as legal history rather than narrative embarrassment.

`No one inventing a resurrection would open it there` / `Luke gains nothing by that verse` — criterion-of-embarrassment apologetics, orthodox, not clickbait. Acceptable for this series charter.

### Clarity on first hearing
**Several lines assume insider knowledge or ask the ear to do unpaid work.**

1. **`Luke gains nothing by that verse`** — *Luke* is never identified (gospel writer, historian). A stranger hears a proper name with no anchor.
2. **`He had told them He would rise`** — The resurrection promise lives in Luke 24:7, which is **never spoken** in the piece. The angel only says *remember how he spake unto you when he was yet in Galilee*. The listener must supply 24:7 from nowhere, or trust the narrator’s gloss.
3. **`The first news that He is risen went to witnesses the world would not hear`** — *would not hear* is a double meaning (would not listen vs. could not be heard). Clever on paper; muddy in a 60s ear.
4. **Unclosed setup:** Hook opens on `dismissed as nonsense — by His own apostles` / `idle tales`. The piece never closes that loop (apostles wrong, women vindicated). The arc walks away from its sharpest tension.

Not a stock “Come to Jesus” cap-at-5 closer — v3 clearly improved on bare `Seek Him.` — but not an 8+ “only this piece could end this way” landing.
