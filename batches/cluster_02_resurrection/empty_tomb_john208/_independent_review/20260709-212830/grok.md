# Independent Adversarial Review — Narration

**Artifact:** The Empty Tomb — John 20:8 (with 20:2, 20:7, 20:29)

---

## Hook score: **6 / 10**

> “The first person at the empty tomb of Jesus thought someone had taken his body.”

Not a template open (“Did you know / Imagine / What if”). That helps.

But it is soft. A stranger gets a mild setup, not a hard stop. No tension spike, no concrete image, no question that forces a second of attention. It reads like episode intro copy, not a scroll-stop.

Under 8 → cannot PASS on hook alone.

---

## Landing score: **7 / 10**

> “Believe him without seeing him. Already, he calls that faith blessed.”

Better than stock (“come to Jesus,” “turn to Him”). It uses this piece’s verb from 20:29.

Still not bones-deep unique:
- “Believe him without seeing him” could close many faith-without-sight pieces.
- The piece’s best image — the napkin / linen — is spent one line earlier (“But the linen still speaks…”), then the close drifts into a more transferable imperative.

Close to good. Not an 8+.

Under 8 → cannot PASS on landing either.

---

## Doctrinal soundness

**Mostly sound.** Empty tomb, ordered grave-clothes, John believes before seeing the risen Lord, Thomas contrast, blessing on unseen faith — standard evangelical reading of John 20. No contrarian/clickbait doctrine.

**Soft overclaim (not heresy, still a problem for a locked piece):**

> “But the linen still speaks: the body was not stolen — Jesus is risen.”

The text shows linen + napkin “wrapped together in a place by itself.” It does **not** say “therefore not stolen.” That is apologetic inference. Fine as a *question* earlier (“What thief wraps the face-cloth…?”). Stated as settled proof in the CTA is stronger than the verse.

**Minor narrative tightening issue:**

> “He did not yet know the scripture — that Christ must rise from the dead.”

John 20:9 is plural: *“they knew not the scripture.”* The piece makes it singular “He” (John only). Small, but you are in a KJV-precision project; don’t blur the text you just quoted.

## Clarity on first hearing

Followable for a cold listener: Mary thinks body stolen → men run → only cloths → John believes → Thomas contrast → you.

**Name density is fine.** Setup before the napkin quote is good (“the cloth that had covered his head”).

**One slight logic rub:**  
> “What thief wraps the face-cloth and lays it aside?”  
then later asserts “the body was not stolen” as fact. First-time ear hears a rhetorical question become a verdict without being told this is *inference*, not a quoted claim. Not confusing enough to fail, but it over-sells.

---

## Grace-anchored conviction

**PASS.** No fear, no gain/loss, no works pressure.

> “Believe him without seeing him. Already, he calls that faith blessed.”

Invitation + Christ’s own blessing. Spirit convicts; script invites. Good.

---

## Freshness = faithful depth

**Solid middle.** Best line is the text-rooted one:

> “What thief wraps the face-cloth and lays it aside?”

and

> “John believed before he ever saw the risen Lord.”

Surprise is in the *text* (napkin placement; belief before appearance), not in a novel claim. Orthodox landing. No invented doctrine for freshness.

---

## Thread spine (hook → middle → CTA)

**Mostly one spine:** empty-tomb mistaken theft → ordered linen → John believes without seeing Jesus → Thomas / “blessed… not seen” → you believe without seeing.

**Mild dual-load at the end:**
1. Forensic: “linen… body was not stolen”
2. Faith-without-sight: “Believe him without seeing him… blessed”

They *can* join (John’s belief from signs, not from seeing Jesus), but the close leads with the theft-apologetic, then pivots to the beatitude. Slight thread-wobble, not a full swap.

---

## Concrete findings (priority order)

1. **Hook too soft (6).** Opening line does not earn a stop-scroll.
2. **Landing under bar (7).** Final imperative is still partly portable; napkin image should *own* the last beat, not only the penultimate sentence.
3. **Apologetic overclaim.** “the body was not stolen” is inference, not text; keep the question energy or hedge the claim.
4. **“He did not yet know the scripture”** should not singularize John 20:9’s “they.”
5. No KJV corruption in quoted scripture. No grace-violation. Doctrine otherwise sound.

---

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Raise the hook above 8: open on the napkin oddity or “he saw, and believed” before any risen appearance — not a soft “first person thought the body was taken” setup.
2. Rebuild the last 1–2 sentences so the close is un-stealable from another piece: land on *this* linen/napkin + “blessed… have not seen,” not the portable “Believe him without seeing him.”
3. Soften or reframe “the body was not stolen” so the napkin remains text-faithful evidence/question, not a declared proof; and keep John 20:9 plural (“they knew not”) if you paraphrase that verse.
