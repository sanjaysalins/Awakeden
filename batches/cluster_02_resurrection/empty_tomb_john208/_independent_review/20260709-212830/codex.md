# Independent review — codex (OK, 94s)

KJV checked against [Bible Gateway John 20 KJV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John+20&version=KJV). I treated the `[scripture]` and `[jesus]` lines as quoted text. No altered KJV words found.

HOOK SCORE: 6/10. “The first person at the empty tomb of Jesus thought someone had taken his body” has a decent tension, but it rests on an over-absolute “first person” claim. John names Mary first, but “we know not” complicates making her solitary or universally first.

LANDING SCORE: 6/10. “Believe him without seeing him. Already, he calls that faith blessed” uses the John 20:29 idea, but it is still a broad faith CTA. It could close many belief-without-sight pieces and does not land through the piece’s strongest concrete image: the linen/napkin.

Findings:

- “The first person at the empty tomb of Jesus” is too absolute. The text can say Mary Magdalene came early in John’s account; it should not overclaim beyond the passage, especially when Mary says “we.”
- “What thief wraps the face-cloth and lays it aside?” leans on a popular apologetic inference more than the text itself. John says the napkin was “wrapped together in a place by itself”; the narration turns that into a thief-behavior argument.
- “John believed before he ever saw the risen Lord” is too certain. John 20:8 says “he saw, and believed,” but 20:9 immediately says they did not yet know the scripture. This should be handled as textual wonder, not a settled psychological claim.
- “Peter and John” / “John stepped inside” assumes the beloved disciple is John. That is traditional and acceptable in many evangelical contexts, but the narration presents it as the verse’s explicit wording.
- “You have only heard” is clear, but thin. It risks making the listener’s position sound like weaker evidence, when John’s Gospel itself presents written testimony as sufficient for faith.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Remove or soften the overclaims: “first person,” “John believed,” and the thief inference.
2. Make the linen/napkin detail surprising without pretending it proves more than John says.
3. Rewrite the CTA so it closes through the linen/tomb image, not a generic believe-without-seeing line.
