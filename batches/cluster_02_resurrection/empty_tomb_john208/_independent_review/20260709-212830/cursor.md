### Doctrinal soundness — orthodox, one exegetical overreach

No contrarian reading, no invented doctrine, no Christ-denying move. Resurrection landing is evangelical-standard.

**The problem is confidence, not heresy.** After the exact quote *"and he saw, and believed,"* the script asserts:

> *"John believed before he ever saw the risen Lord."*

John 20:8 does **not** name the object of belief. John 20:9 (same visit, same breath in the gospel) immediately says *"they knew not the scripture, that he must rise again from the dead."* The piece acknowledges that with *"He did not yet know the scripture"* but **never resolves the tension** — it states resurrection-faith as settled fact. That is a mainstream harmonization, not what the verse itself says. Sold as straight reportage (*"Of that moment, the gospel says only this"* → then commentary that goes beyond *only this*), it overreaches.

**Secondary:** Peter enters first, sees the same linen and napkin (John 20:6–7), and is not said to believe (20:8). The script never accounts for that. Not heresy — but it is a selective reading presented as the simple gospel sequence.

**Theft frame is imported, not quoted.** Mary’s KJV line is *"They have taken away the Lord"* — agent unspecified. The hook now correctly says *"someone"* (better than an earlier *"thieves"* draft), but the middle still asks *"What **thief** wraps the face-cloth…?"* and the landing verdict is *"the body was not **stolen**."* John never uses *thief* or *stolen* in the cited verses. The apologetic is ancient and sound; pinning it on Mary’s stated thought is still an inference upgraded to narrative spine.

### Clarity on first hearing — two logic gaps

**1. Believed *what*?**

A zero-Bible listener hears:

- *"and he saw, and believed"*
- *"He did not yet know the scripture — that Christ must rise from the dead"*
- *"John believed before he ever saw the risen Lord"*

That sequence **assumes** the listener already knows resurrection is what John believed. On first hearing it reads like a contradiction: he believed the risen Lord before he knew resurrection scripture? The script never names the intermediate step (what the ordered grave clothes *showed*) before jumping to *"Jesus is risen."*

**2. Hook protagonist ≠ payoff protagonist.**

Hook: *"The **first person** at the empty tomb… thought someone had taken his body."*  
Payoff: **John’s** pre-resurrection-appearance belief (20:8) and Thomas (20:29).

Mary is named in line 2, but the hook’s *"first person"* never pays off on Mary — the piece pivots to John without saying *why we leave Mary’s mistake for John’s moment*. A stranger can follow the story; they may not follow **why this is the John 20:8 piece** and not a Mary piece.

**Minor friction:** Mary’s quote says *"**we** know not where they have laid him"* while the narration has only Mary at the tomb (20:1). Who is *we*? Small, but real for the stated audience.

**Thomas arrives cold:** *"Thomas refused to believe until he saw"* — no *"one of the twelve,"* no *"the other disciples told him."* Bible-literate listeners bridge it; strangers get a name-drop.

### Freshness = faithful depth — mixed

**Strong:** Pinning to John 20:8’s sparse *"he saw, and believed"* (not the full Easter montage), the ordered-napkin detail, and the 20:29 bridge to modern hearers who *"were not at the tomb"* — all text-grounded.

**Weak:** *"What thief wraps the face-cloth and lays it aside?"* is standard empty-tomb apologetics. Once 20:7 is read, that beat is familiar sermon territory, not a surprising textual depth. The freshest move is John-before-Thomas + 20:29; the linen argument is the well-worn half.

### One thread spine — mostly PASS, one wobble

Arc holds: missing body → ordered linens → John’s belief-without-seeing-the-Lord → Thomas → blessed faith for hearers who only *"have heard."*

No full thread-swap. The wobble: the hook’s *"first person / someone took the body"* frame is Mary’s; the theological center is John’s. The thread survives, but the **opening image and closing claim are not the same character’s journey**.

### Hook score: **6/10**

*"The first person at the empty tomb of Jesus thought someone had taken his body."*

Not a template opener (no *Did you know / Imagine / What if* cap). Concrete and biblical. But it is **declarative commentary**, not a scroll-stopping paradox or unresolved tension in the first ~5 seconds — it *informs* more than it *arrests*. It also front-loads a mistaken assumption that the piece does not return to; the payoff belongs to John, not *"the first person."*

### Landing score: **7/10**

Earned elements: *"You were not at the tomb. You have only heard"* bridges cleanly to 20:29; *"the linen still speaks"* is piece-specific; *"Already, he calls that faith blessed"* grows from Jesus’ quoted beatitude.

Docked because:

- *"Believe him without seeing him"* is **portable** — it could close any Thomas / 20:29 / Hebrews-11 faith-without-sight piece without the linen setup.
- The exact final sentence — *"Already, he calls that faith blessed"* — could end a different 20:29 episode with no napkin preamble.
- *"Jesus is risen"* in the penultimate block is true and orthodox, but as a narrator verdict it outruns what the cited verses explicitly say (same overreach as the John 20:8 claim).

Both scores under 8 → **REVISE** per rubric.
