Here is the adversarial review of the narration artifact based on the provided lenses.

**SCORE THE HOOK: 6/10**
- *Citation:* `[narrator] The empty tomb of Jesus was first mistaken for a grave robbery.`
- *Critique:* It avoids being a template ("Did you know..."), which saves it from a 5, but it is dry. It reads like the opening of a History Channel documentary, not a viral 60-second short. It lacks visceral punch. Framing it around the panic of a "crime scene" would be much more scroll-stopping.

**SCORE THE LANDING: 6/10**
- *Citation:* `Believe him without seeing him — and the blessing is yours: already, he calls that faith blessed.`
- *Critique:* It avoids being a stock closer (it successfully uses the specific seeing/believing theme built up in the script). However, it directly violates the "NO gain/loss / self-interest framing" constraint. "And the blessing is yours" turns the gospel invitation into a transactional exchange, dangling a reward to manufacture conviction. 

**Doctrinal Soundness: PASS**
- *Citation:* `What thief stops to set a grave in order?` and `He did not yet know the scripture.`
- *Critique:* Solid and orthodox. The apologetic deduction regarding the folded napkin is standard and faithful. Noting that John believed prior to knowing the scripture aligns perfectly with John 20:9.

**KJV Verbatim: PASS**
- *Critique:* All Scripture blocks (John 20:2, 20:7, 20:8, and 20:29) are perfectly matched character-for-character to the KJV text. No altered words found.

**Clarity on First Hearing: PASS**
- *Critique:* The narrative arc is very clear. A listener with zero Bible knowledge can easily follow the progression from the missing body, to the strange evidence of the folded cloth, to John's immediate belief, to Jesus's later words. 

**Grace-Anchored Conviction: FAIL**
- *Citation:* `and the blessing is yours`
- *Critique:* As noted in the landing score, this relies on self-interest. The script must invite the viewer to trust the risen Christ because He is true, not to extract a blessing for themselves.

**Freshness & One Thread Spine: PASS**
- *Critique:* The piece maintains a single, cohesive thread: from the physical evidence (the napkin) -> to John believing without seeing Christ -> to Jesus extending that same principle to us today. It is a faithful, deep, and cohesive use of the text.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Rewrite the final CTA to eliminate the transactional gain/loss framing ("and the blessing is yours"). Root the invitation in the grace and reality of the risen Christ rather than a reward.
2. Punch up the opening hook to be more arresting and viral. Move away from the dry, historical tone of "first mistaken for a grave robbery."
3. Smooth the transition into the Thomas scene (`About a week later, to a disciple who demanded proof...`). It's slightly abrupt and breaks the immediate tension established at the tomb.
