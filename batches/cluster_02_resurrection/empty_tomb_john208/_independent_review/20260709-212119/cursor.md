### Doctrinal soundness — mostly orthodox, one real overreach

**1. The centerpiece claim outruns John 20:8.**

The line *"John believed before he ever saw the risen Christ"* names the object of belief as resurrection faith. John 20:8 only says *"and he saw, and believed"* — it does not say *what* he believed. John 20:9 immediately qualifies the same tomb visit: *"For as yet they knew not the scripture, that he must rise again from the dead"* (plural *they*). The piece nods at that with *"He did not yet know the scripture"* but never resolves the tension; it jumps straight to resurrection certainty. That is a standard evangelical harmonization presented as narrative fact, not as what the verse itself states.

**2. Peter is erased from the evidence-to-belief beat.**

In the passage, Peter enters first and sees the same linen and napkin (20:6–7). Only then does John enter and believe (20:8). The script never asks why identical evidence did not produce the same response in Peter. Not heresy, but a selective reading of the pericope sold as straightforward reportage.

**3. Anti-theft logic is inference, upgraded to verdict.**

*"What thief stops to set a grave in order?"* and *"the body was not stolen — Jesus is risen"* are fair apologetic inferences from 20:7, but John never says them. The script moves from grave-cloth observation → theft refutation → *"Jesus is risen"* as narrator verdict in one step. Doctrinally fine for evangelical resurrection faith; exegetically it skips what the text leaves implicit.

No contrarian or clickbait reading. No invented doctrine. But the John 20:8 claim is stated more confidently than the text allows.

### Clarity on first hearing — two friction points

**1. Belief without a named object.**

A zero-Bible listener hears: *"and he saw, and believed"* → *"He did not yet know the scripture"* → *"John believed before he ever saw the risen Christ."* Believed **what**, if he did not yet know resurrection scripture? The script assumes the listener already knows the answer. That is a logic gap on first hearing, not a clever conceit that pays off in the piece.

**2. Identity and "we" go unexplained.**

*"Then John stepped inside"* — which John? The text’s *"other disciple, whom Jesus loved"* never surfaces. Mary’s quote uses *"we know not where they have laid him"* while the narration has only Mary running (20:1). A stranger may wonder who *"we"* is. Minor, but real clarity cost in a piece aimed at hearers with no Bible background.

The revised hook — *"The empty tomb of Jesus was first mistaken for a grave robbery"* — fixes the earlier draft’s subject–verb slip. That line is now clear.
