# Independent review — codex (OK, 50s)

KJV check: the scripture excerpts match John 20:2, 20:7, 20:8, and John 20:29 KJV as checked against Bible Gateway’s KJV text.

Findings:

- Hook score: 6/10. “The empty tomb of Jesus was first mistaken for a grave robbery” is clear and relevant, but it is more explanatory than arresting. It states the setup without a sharp image or unresolved tension.
- Landing score: 6/10. “Believe him without seeing him — and the blessing is yours” is built from the piece’s seeing/believing thread, but it still lands as a fairly stock faith CTA. “the blessing is yours” also leans toward benefit framing.
- Grace concern: “and the blessing is yours” risks making faith sound like a transaction: believe correctly and receive the prize. John 20:29 does call such believers blessed, but the narration should avoid sounding like gain-loss motivation.
- Doctrinal/interpretive overstatement: “What thief stops to set a grave in order?” is rhetorically strong, but it slightly overclaims the text. John 20:7 says the napkin was “wrapped together in a place by itself,” not that the whole grave was “set…in order.”
- Clarity issue: “No angel had spoken to him” may confuse a zero-knowledge listener because angels appear shortly after in the same chapter. It is true for John at that moment, but the line needs tighter anchoring.
- Thomas framing is a little harsh: “a disciple who demanded proof” is accurate enough from John 20:25, but it flattens Jesus’ gracious response to Thomas and makes the contrast with John feel moralized.
- Thread spine is mostly coherent: mistaken robbery -> ordered linen -> John believes -> Jesus blesses unseen belief. The final line does follow the spine, but it could land more specifically on the linen/empty-grave image rather than a generalized command to believe.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Remove or qualify the overclaim “set a grave in order.”
2. Rework “the blessing is yours” so conviction is invitation, not reward-framing.
3. Sharpen the landing around the linen/empty tomb image so it feels less stock.
