## Hook (6)

Exact open:

> *“The empty tomb of Jesus was first mistaken for a grave robbery.”*

Problems:

- **Informative, not arresting.** A cold historical claim. No tension in the first beat beyond “someone got the story wrong.”
- **Payoff delayed.** The real scroll-stop (“What thief stops to set a grave in order?”) sits mid-piece. The stranger has already scrolled past dry setup: *“The first day of the week, when it was yet dark…”*
- **Not template** (good — not “Did you know / Imagine / What if”), so not capped at 5 — still only a **6**: clear, orthodox, mild curiosity, weak viral grip.

---

## Landing (7)

Exact close:

> *“Believe him without seeing him — and the blessing is yours: already, he calls that faith blessed.”*

Strengths:

- Built from **John 20:29** (“blessed are they that have not seen, and yet have believed”).
- Not stock “Come to Jesus / turn to Him / look to Jesus.”
- Grace-shaped invitation, not threat.

Weaknesses (keep it under 8):

- **Transfer test fails half the sentence.** *“Believe him without seeing him — and the blessing is yours”* could end almost any Thomas / 2 Cor 5:7 / Hebrews 11 piece. The distinctive spine of **this** short is the **napkin / ordered tomb / John believed before he saw Christ**. That image is dropped before the last clause.
- **“the blessing is yours”** is soft and portable; only *“already, he calls that faith blessed”* re-anchors 20:29.
- Earlier promise *“the linen still speaks”* never becomes the closer. The piece sets a unique object, then lands on a general faith-beatitude.

---

## Doctrinal soundness

**Mostly sound.** Resurrection, empty tomb, John 20:8 faith, 20:29 beatitude — mainstream evangelical, not contrarian.

### Overclaim (not heresy, but sloppy persuasion)

> *“But the linen still speaks: the body was not stolen — Jesus is risen.”*

- The napkin detail (20:7) **supports** “not a rushed theft” as a traditional reading; it does **not** by itself entail “Jesus is risen.”
- You stack *not stolen* → *risen* as if the linen closes both. That’s a **logic stretch on first hearing**, and it over-sells an apologetic inference as if it were the text’s own explicit proof chain.
- Doctrine of the resurrection is true; **this argument from the napkin is over-weighted**.

### Acceptable but worn inference

> *“What thief stops to set a grave in order?”*

Classic Easter apologetic, not invented doctrine, not clickbait-contrarian. **Faithful depth?** Mild. This is Sunday-school familiar, not a fresh cut into the text.

### “John believed before he ever saw the risen Christ”

Orthodox and text-supported (20:8 with 20:9’s “as yet they knew not the scripture, that he must rise”). Keep.

## Clarity on first hearing

### Ambiguous absolute

> *“He did not yet know the scripture.”*

- Sounds like John was biblically illiterate.
- Text (20:9) means they did not yet know **the scripture that he must rise again from the dead**.
- Zero-Bible listener hears the wrong claim. **Must specify.**

### Mild leap

> *“the linen still speaks: the body was not stolen — Jesus is risen.”*

- “Not stolen” is already an inference; “risen” is a further leap. First-hearing logic feels slick rather than clean.

### Otherwise clear

- Robbery → linen → John believes without seeing Jesus → 20:29 → you → same path is followable.
- Naming “John” for “the other disciple” is standard; fine for a short.

## One thread spine

**Mostly one spine, with a soft split:**

1. Robbery / ordered linen (apologetic evidence)  
2. John believes without seeing Christ  
3. You believe without seeing → blessed (20:29)

Bridge works: empty tomb evidence → John saw (linen) and believed without seeing *Jesus* → same for the hearer.

**Problem:** middle sells **napkin/theft**, landing sells **unseen faith**. The spine holds, but the **closing image does not match the middle’s unique object**. Feels like a clean hinge to Thomas rather than one continuous image through the CTA.

## Concrete findings (ranked)

1. **Hook underpowered (6).** Opens as encyclopedia: *“was first mistaken for a grave robbery”* — not a 5-second stop.
2. **Landing under 8 (7).** *“Believe him without seeing him — and the blessing is yours”* is portable; does not lock the napkin / John-before-sighting spine into the final sentence.
3. **Clarity error:** *“He did not yet know the scripture”* misleads; needs 20:9’s content (*that he must rise*).
4. **Evidentiary overclaim:** *“the linen still speaks: the body was not stolen — Jesus is risen”* over-proves from 20:7.
5. **Freshness soft:** thief/napkin is familiar; the piece underuses the sharper 20:8–9 moment it already names.
6. **KJV quotes:** clean.  
7. **Grace / doctrine core:** sound; no contrarian reading.

## Bottom line

Not a FAIL — no invented doctrine, no stock “come to Jesus,” KJV quotes exact, grace intact.  
Not a PASS — **hook 6, landing 7**, plus the 20:9 clarity slip and the linen→risen overclaim. By your scoring rule this is **REVISE**.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Rebuild the open so the first ~5 seconds carry the piece’s unique tension (ordered napkin / “what thief…?” or John believing before seeing Christ)—not a dry “mistaken for robbery” summary.
2. Rewrite the final sentence so it cannot end another piece: fuse the linen/John-saw-and-believed image with the 20:29 blessing in one non-portable close.
3. Fix “He did not yet know the scripture” to the 20:9 sense (they did not yet know that he must rise), and soften “linen still speaks… body was not stolen — Jesus is risen” so the napkin is witness/weight, not a full proof chain.
