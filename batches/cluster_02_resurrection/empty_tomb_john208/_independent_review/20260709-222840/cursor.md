### KJV verbatim
All four quoted scripture lines match KJV exactly:

---

### Doctrinal soundness
**Over-asserted reading of John 20:8.** The line *"John believed before he ever saw the risen Lord"* treats a debated moment as settled fact. John 20:9 immediately qualifies it: *"they knew not the scripture, that he must rise again from the dead."* The text says John *"believed"* — it does not say he believed *in the resurrection*. The piece builds its whole arc on an inference the passage does not spell out.

**The 20:9 fix makes the tension worse, not better.** *"He did not yet know the scripture — that Christ must rise from the dead. John believed before he ever saw the risen Lord"* stacks two claims a careful listener will hear as contradictory: he did not yet grasp resurrection scripture, yet he believed (resurrection implied) before seeing Jesus. That is not heresy, but it is **freshness built on a contested certainty** — exactly what the charter forbids.

**Apologetic overreach on the napkin.** *"What thief wraps the face-cloth and lays it aside?"* imports modern empty-tomb apologetics. KJV 20:7 only reports the napkin *"wrapped together in a place by itself"* — it does not argue thief psychology. Clever, not textual.

Otherwise orthodox: resurrection, faith-without-seeing, Thomas contrast, no invented doctrine.

---

### Clarity on first hearing
**The John/listener parallel breaks on one hearing.** *"You have only heard — like John at the linen, before he ever saw"* collapses two different experiences:
- John **was** at the tomb.
- John **saw** the linen.

The listener has neither. Equating *"only heard"* with *"like John at the linen"* will lose a zero-Bible listener mid-CTA.

**Unnamed object of belief.** After *"and he saw, and believed"*, the script never states what John believed. A stranger hears *"John believed before he ever saw the risen Lord"* and reasonably asks: believed **what**? Theft? Mary's report? Resurrection? The middle assumes Bible literacy it does not earn.

**Unexplained bridge word.** *"He did not yet know the scripture"* — which scripture? A first-time listener has no anchor for *"the scripture"* here (Psalm 16? Isaiah? Jonah?). The line gestures at 20:9 without making it followable.

**Thomas pivot is abrupt.** *"When Thomas heard, he refused to believe until he saw. Eight days later..."* — heard **what**, from **whom**? Eight days after **what**? Timeline is fixed (good), but the narrative handoff is still thin for a cold listener.

### Grace-anchored conviction
Clean on this axis. *"Believe him without seeing him"* and *"Already, he calls that faith blessed"* invite; they do not threaten loss, promise gain, or lean on works. No fear framing.

### Freshness = faithful depth
**Strengths:** Theft misdirection (*"Someone took the body"*), linen/napkin detail, John 20:8 as anchor rather than angel/risen-Lord spectacle — genuinely text-surprising and orthodox in aim.

**Weakness:** The thief-rhetoric question and the forced 20:8→20:29 equivalence push past what the text actually argues.

---

### One thread spine (hook → middle → CTA)
Mostly one spine: **misread tomb → linen evidence → faith without seeing risen Christ → blessedness of unseen faith.**

**Strain point:** The strongest image is **ordered linen in an empty tomb**; the conviction beat pivots to **Thomas eight days later**. Related theme, different scene. The close returns to linen only in the weakened phrase *"like John at the linen"* — not as the driving image.

Peter is named (*"Peter and John"*) then vanishes. Minor, but signals a compressed middle.

---

### Hook score: **7/10**
*"Someone took the body"* — strong crime-scene open; not a template hook.

Deflated by *"at the empty tomb of Jesus"* in the same breath: for anyone with even faint Christian exposure, the mystery collapses into familiar Easter territory within ~5 seconds. Good tension, not a scroll-stopper for strangers.

---

### Landing score: **6/10**
*"Believe him without seeing him. Already, he calls that faith blessed"* — biblical, but **portable**. That exact pair could close any John 20:29 short, a generic resurrection devotional, or a Thomas piece. It is not linen-locked.

*"like John at the linen"* tries to tie back, but the heard/saw logic error undercuts it. The landing's verbs are **faith/blessed**, not **linen, napkin, empty ledge** — the piece's own strongest image.

Not a stock *"come to Jesus"* closer, so not capped at 5 — but still under the 8 bar.

---

### Summary judgment
KJV-clean, orthodox in direction, grace-safe. Fails on **first-hearing logic** (John parallel, unnamed belief-object), **textual overclaim** (20:8 certainty, thief question), and **landing portability** (generic unseen-faith close). Hook and landing both under 8 → **REVISE**.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Name what John believed in plain speech (or qualify it: he saw the linen and believed *something real had happened* — then let 20:29 carry unseen faith, without asserting full resurrection faith at 20:8).
2. Repair the closing parallel: John *saw* the linen but not the risen Lord; the listener has not seen either — drop *"You have only heard — like John at the linen"* and land through the **ordered linen / empty ledge** image with a CTA verb drawn from *that* scene.
3. Soften or replace *"What thief wraps the face-cloth and lays it aside?"* with an observation faithful to 20:7 (*wrapped apart, body gone, cloths left*) — observe the text, don't prosecute a thief.
