## Doctrinal soundness

Orthodox spine: empty tomb → linen not like theft → John believes without seeing the risen Lord → Thomas contrast → blessing on unseen faith. No contrarian “they stole the body and kept it that way” twist sold as history.

**Soft pressure points (not FAIL-level heresy, but real):**

1. **What did John believe?**  
   *“and he saw, and believed… John believed before he ever saw the risen Lord.”*  
   Mainstream evangelical reading: resurrection faith from the empty tomb/linen. That is defensible. The script never states *what* he believed in plain words. A hostile reader can still hear “he believed Mary’s report that the body was stolen” — which would undercut the whole piece. First-hearing ambiguity is a real problem even if the intended doctrine is sound.

2. **“What thief wraps the face-cloth and lays it aside?”**  
   Apologetic inference, not a verse claim. Fine as rhetoric if it stays “this doesn’t look like theft.” It is not invented doctrine, but it is sold as near-proof. Acceptable; not a FAIL.

3. **“No angel spoke. No risen Lord stood there”**  
   True for Peter/John in John’s narrative. Not a gospel contradiction in this pericope. OK.

---

## Clarity on first hearing (weak)

Several first-hear snags:

1. **Self-adjacent paradox, not resolved in ear-time**  
   *“He did not yet know the scripture — that Christ must rise from the dead. John believed before he ever saw the risen Lord.”*  
   Zero-Bible listener: believed *what*, if he didn’t know he must rise? The text’s “for as yet…” needs one plain beat (“he trusted Jesus was alive / risen”) or it sounds like a logic trick.

2. **False parallel in the close**  
   *“You have only heard — like John at the linen, before he ever saw.”*  
   Listener: only **heard**.  
   John: **saw** the linen, then believed, without seeing the **risen** Lord.  
   The “like” equates hearing with standing at the linen. It is not the same. Thomas / “not seen” is the clean parallel; this line muddies it.

3. **“Peter and John”**  
   Fine for regulars; first-hearers never get that “the other disciple” is John. Minor.

No fear, gain/loss, or works ladder.  
*“Believe him without seeing him. Already, he calls that faith blessed.”* — invite + blessing, not threat. Passes this lens.

## Freshness / thread spine

**Freshness:** The napkin + “what thief…?” is faithful depth from the text. Good.

**Thread spine — partial break:**

| Beat | Image / claim |
|------|----------------|
| Hook | Stolen body |
| Proof | Wrapped napkin / not theft |
| Pivot | John saw linen and believed |
| Contrast | Thomas refuses until he sees |
| CTA | Believe without seeing / blessed |

The hook sells **theft vs orderly linen**. The landing sells **unseen faith (20:29)**. Linen is the distinctive text-image; it is **not** in the close. The note claims a “linen-locked close”; the artifact does not deliver one. That is thread drift: evidence-at-the-tomb → generic believe-without-seeing.

Not full thread-swap, but the spine frays at the CTA.

## Bottom line

Doctrine: basically sound, not clickbait.  
KJV quotes: clean.  
Grace: fine.  
Fails the bar on **first-hearing clarity** (believe *what*; broken “like John” parallel), **spine** (linen proof abandoned at CTA), and **scores** (hook 7, landing 6). Not a FAIL (no invented doctrine, no quote corruption), but not PASS.

VERDICT: REVISE  
TOP FIXES:  
1. Make the close linen-locked and piece-unique — end on the napkin / empty place / “he saw, and believed,” then only *then* fold in “blessed… have not seen”; kill the portable “Believe him without seeing him” stock shape.  
2. State in one plain clause what John believed (Jesus is risen / alive), then the 20:9 “not yet know the scripture” beat, so zero-Bible ears don’t hear a contradiction.  
3. Fix the false parallel: do not say the hearer is “like John at the linen” if the hearer only has testimony; align listener with Jesus’ blessing on those who have not seen the risen Lord (Thomas contrast), not with John’s eyes on the grave-clothes.
