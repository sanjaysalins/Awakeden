# Independent review — claude (OK, 95s)

All four KJV quotes verified verbatim against `data/kjv_cache.json`. Findings below.

**KJV verbatim — clean.** John 20:2b ("They have taken away the Lord out of the sepulchre, and we know not where they have laid him"), 20:7 in full, 20:8b ("and he saw, and believed"), and 20:29b ("blessed are they that have not seen, and yet have believed") all match the cached KJV exactly, including commas. The 20:9 material ("He did not yet know the scripture — that Christ must rise from the dead") is correctly paraphrased *outside* quote marks.

**Doctrine — sound.** The reading of 20:8 (John believed at the graveclothes, before seeing the risen Lord) is the mainstream evangelical reading, not a contrarian one, and the piece honestly carries the 20:9 qualifier rather than hiding it. "What thief wraps the face-cloth and lays it aside?" is framed as a question, not an invented claim. "No angel spoke. No risen Lord stood there" is accurate *within John's account* of the Peter/John visit (angels appear to Mary afterward, 20:12). Thomas "eight days later" matches 20:26.

**Weak spot 1 — the landing analogy bends slightly.** "You have only heard — like John at the linen, before he ever saw." John did *see* — physical evidence, the linen; the listener has seen nothing. The qualifier "before he ever saw" (i.e., the risen Lord) rescues it on the page, but on first hearing "only heard — like John" can register as false, since the whole middle of the piece is John *looking* at the linen. One clause would fix it (e.g., "John saw only linen. You have not seen even that.").

**Weak spot 2 — the Thomas compression drops the contrast.** In 20:29 the blessing lands *against* "because thou hast seen me, thou hast believed." The narration jumps straight from "showed him his hands" to the blessing, omitting both Thomas's confession and the seen/believed contrast that gives the quoted half its force. Nothing false is asserted, but the blessing arrives with half its setup missing.

**Minor:** "Someone took the body. That was the first thought" — whose thought is only clarified by the 20:2 quote two lines later; attributing it ("Mary's first thought") would remove a beat of confusion.

**HOOK: 8/10.** "Someone took the body." is a specific, non-template misdirection open — it states the wrong explanation as fact and the piece spends its length dismantling it. A stranger would plausibly stop.

**LANDING: 8/10.** "Believe him without seeing him. Already, he calls that faith blessed." is built from this piece's own verse-pair (seen/believed → blessed) and could not close a different piece without its verse. Not a stock closer. It would be a 9 if the John-analogy clause before it were tightened (fix 1).

**Grace-anchored — yes.** No fear, no gain/loss; the CTA is an invitation framed by Christ's own blessing. **One thread** (seeing vs. believing) runs unbroken hook → linen → John → Thomas → listener.

VERDICT: PASS
TOP FIXES:
1. Tighten the landing clause "You have only heard — like John at the linen, before he ever saw" — John *did* see the linen; make the contrast explicit (he saw linen only; you have seen nothing) so the analogy can't misfire on first hearing.
2. Restore half a line of the 20:29 contrast before the Jesus quote (Thomas saw and believed) so "have not seen, and yet have believed" lands against its intended foil.
3. Attribute the opening thought ("Mary's first thought") so the hook's owner is clear before the 20:2 quote arrives.
