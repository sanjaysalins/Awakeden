# Independent review — codex (OK, 30s)

Hook score: 7/10. “Someone took the body” is concrete and text-rooted, but the second sentence immediately explains it, weakening the stop-scroll tension.

Landing score: 8/10. “like John at the linen” ties the CTA to the piece’s own image. Stronger than stock, though “Believe him without seeing him” is close to generic.

Findings:

- KJV appears verbatim in the scripture/Jesus lines. No altered words found in John 20:2b, 20:7, 20:8b, or 20:29b.
- Possible overclaim: “John believed before he ever saw the risen Lord.” John 20:8 says he “saw, and believed,” but John 20:9 immediately says they did not yet know the scripture. The narration should avoid implying John had fully formed resurrection faith if the text may mean his belief was still dawning.
- Clarity issue: “You have only heard — like John at the linen, before he ever saw.” John did not “only hear”; he saw the linen evidence. The listener may stumble over the comparison.
- Doctrinal/conviction risk: “Already, he calls that faith blessed” is faithful to John 20:29, but the final appeal slightly turns faith into admiring John’s evidence-process rather than resting directly on Christ’s risen word.
- Thread mostly holds: body missing -> linen ordered -> John believes -> Thomas blessing -> listener invited. No major thread-swap.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Qualify “John believed before he ever saw the risen Lord” so it does not overstate John’s understanding before John 20:9.
2. Fix “You have only heard — like John at the linen” because John saw physical evidence; the analogy is not clean.
3. Preserve the linen image in the CTA while anchoring belief more directly in Christ’s word, not just the pattern of John’s response.
