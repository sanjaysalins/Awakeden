# Independent review — gemini (OK, 48s)

Here is the independent, adversarial review of the narration artifact.

**Doctrinal soundness**: 
The script asserts "John believed before he ever saw the risen Lord" while acknowledging "He did not yet know the scripture". This is a faithful, orthodox reading of John 20:8-9, correctly distinguishing between John believing the physical evidence of the resurrection and understanding its theological necessity from Scripture. No invented doctrine.

**KJV verbatim**: 
- "They have taken away the Lord..." (John 20:2) — Exact.
- "And the napkin, that was about his head..." (John 20:7) — Exact.
- "and he saw, and believed." (John 20:8) — Exact.
- "blessed are they that have not seen..." (John 20:29) — Exact.

**Clarity on first hearing**: 
- "What thief wraps the face-cloth and lays it aside?" is a brilliant, clear distillation of the apologetic argument regarding the grave clothes.
- However, the phrasing in the CTA is slightly dangerous on first hearing: "You have only heard — like John at the linen, before he ever saw." The entire point of the preceding paragraph was that John *did* see the linen ("and he saw, and believed"). Saying "before he ever saw" without an object right after mentioning the linen could cause a split-second of cognitive dissonance for the listener. It needs to be "before he ever saw the Lord" or "before he ever saw Him".

**Grace-anchored conviction**: 
The CTA ("Already, he calls that faith blessed") is a beautiful, grace-filled invitation. There is no fear, works, or gain-loss framing used here.

**Freshness = faithful depth**: 
The juxtaposition of John (who believed by seeing the linen but not the Lord) and Thomas (who refused to believe until he saw the Lord) is an exceptional, fresh insight drawn directly from the text. 

**One thread spine**: 
The thread of "seeing vs. believing" is perfectly sustained from the physical evidence (the linen) to the theological conclusion (blessed are those who have not seen).

**SCORE THE HOOK**: 8/10. 
"Someone took the body. That was the first thought at the empty tomb of Jesus." It's an active, *in media res* opening that avoids template traps. (Minor note: "empty tomb of Jesus" is slightly clunky; "at the empty tomb" would be punchier since Jesus is implied and named shortly after).

**SCORE THE LANDING**: 9/10. 
"Believe him without seeing him. Already, he calls that faith blessed." This is the opposite of a stock closer. It is inextricably linked to the Thomas quote and the John narrative, and it lands with profound weight.

VERDICT: PASS
TOP FIXES:
1. Clarify the CTA line: change "like John at the linen, before he ever saw" to "like John at the linen, before he ever saw the Lord" to avoid confusion, since John *did* see the linen.
2. Consider trimming the hook slightly for punchiness: "That was the first thought at the empty tomb." instead of "at the empty tomb of Jesus."
3. Change "Believe him without seeing him" to "Believe in Him without seeing Him" for standard evangelical phrasing, though the current version works as a direct echo of the Thomas quote.
