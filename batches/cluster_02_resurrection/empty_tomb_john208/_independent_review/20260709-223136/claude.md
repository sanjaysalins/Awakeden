## Doctrine — sound, with two soft spots
1. **"Believed what? That his Lord was risen."** — This is the mainstream evangelical reading of John 20:8 (Carson, Westcott), and the piece honestly carries the v9 tension ("He did not yet know the scripture"). Not contrarian, not invented. But it IS the piece's strongest interpretive claim, stated flatly as fact. It survives because v29's "seen **me**" contrast backs it. Acceptable.
2. **"Peter and John"** — the text says "Simon Peter, and… the other disciple, whom Jesus loved." Naming him John is near-universal tradition, fine for a 60s short, but it is an identification the text never makes.
3. **"Jesus was speaking about you."** — application of a general beatitude to the listener; standard homiletics, and John 20:31 ("that ye might believe") licenses it. OK.
4. Sequence fidelity is good: stone → Mary runs → Peter sees the napkin (v7) → **then** John steps in (v8) → Thomas eight days later. No harmonization errors within John; "No angel spoke" is true of THIS visit (angels appear to Mary in v12, after).

## Accuracy nits
- **"not the empty ledge"** — the ledge/shelf is extra-textual (John says only "the place where the body of Jesus had lain" for the napkin). Archaeologically fair, doctrinally harmless, but it's the one invented physical detail in a piece whose whole engine is "only what the text says." Swap for the text's own furniture ("not the place where they laid him") and the piece is airtight.
- **"showed him his hands"** — text is "behold my hands… thrust it into my side." Hands-only is a fair reduction, not an error.

## Clarity on first hearing
Follows cleanly with zero Bible knowledge: body gone → face-cloth folded → John believes from linen alone → Thomas demands sight → Jesus blesses sight-less faith → you. The v8/v9 paradox ("believed… did not yet know the scripture") is the hardest beat, and the v2 fix ("The linen was enough") lands it in one hearing. One residual wobble: "John believed before he ever saw the risen Lord" vs. the beatitude "have not **seen**" — John DID see evidence. The script defuses this ("You have seen nothing — not the linen…"), so the listener ends up *below* John, not equal to him. Coherent.

## Grace-anchored conviction
No fear, no gain/loss, no pressure. "Already, he calls that faith blessed" is pure grace — the blessing precedes the listener's response. Clean.

## Thread spine
One spine, no swaps: linen-as-evidence → belief without sight → the beatitude → you. The rhetorical hinge "What thief wraps the face-cloth and lays it aside?" is the same thread, not a detour.

## Scores
- **HOOK: 8/10.** "Someone took the body." is a genuine cold-open misdirection in Mary's own voice — no template, and it's the actual first thought of the actual first witness. It would stop a stranger. Not a 9 because "empty tomb of Jesus" in sentence two tips the subject slightly early.
- **LANDING: 8/10.** "Take him at his word: he is risen. Already, he calls that faith blessed." — built from THIS piece's verb (believe without seeing) and THIS piece's verse (20:29). The final sentence could not close a different piece; "Already" does new work (the blessing is pre-spoken, waiting). "Take him at his word" alone would be semi-stock, but it's load-bearing here: his *word* is all the listener has, which is the whole point.
