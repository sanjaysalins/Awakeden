# Independent review — codex (OK, 29s)

Hook score: 7/10. “Someone took the body” is clean and immediate, but it is not yet visually or emotionally distinct enough to cross 8.

Landing score: 6/10. “Take him at his word: he is risen” could close many resurrection pieces, so it reads partly stock.

Findings:

- Doctrinal / exegetical risk: “Believed what? That his Lord was risen.” This is a debated reading of John 20:8, especially because the next sentence says, “He did not yet know the scripture — that Christ must rise from the dead.” You need to make the claim less overconfident or explain the tension better.
- Clarity issue: “The linen was enough” sounds like the linen itself generated faith. Better to anchor the faith in the apostolic witness/sign rather than make the object feel almost sacramental.
- Possible overreach: “Jesus was speaking about you.” John 20:29 certainly includes later believers, but this phrasing sounds too direct and individualized. “That blessing reaches people like you” would be safer.
- KJV quotations: I do not see altered words in the quoted KJV lines.
- Thread strength: The piece begins with Mary’s mistaken explanation, moves to John’s belief from the linen, then shifts to Thomas and the viewer. That is coherent, but the “Mary theft theory” hook fades quickly; the final CTA lands more on Thomas than on the opening image.
- Grace-anchored conviction: Mostly good. “You have seen nothing” is stark but not manipulative. No works or gain-loss framing.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Soften or clarify “Believed what? That his Lord was risen” because John 20:8-9 is interpretively contested.
2. Make the final CTA arise from the linen/tomb image, not a generic “Take him at his word.”
3. Keep one spine: either theft-to-linen-to-faith, or unseen-belief through Thomas; right now the close shifts threads.
