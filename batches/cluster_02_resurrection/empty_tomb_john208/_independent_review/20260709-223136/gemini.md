# Independent review — gemini (OK, 59s)

Here is the independent, adversarial review of the artifact.

**Doctrinal soundness:**
Sound and biblically faithful. The script correctly identifies that John believed the resurrection based on the evidence of the linen before encountering the risen Christ (John 20:8-9), and accurately contrasts this with Thomas's demand for physical sight. 

**KJV verbatim:**
Flawless. All four scripture insertions (John 20:2b, 20:7, 20:8b, 20:29b) are exact KJV matches with no altered words.

**Clarity on first hearing:**
There is a dangling character that will confuse a first-time listener. The script says Mary "ran to tell his disciples, Peter and John" and "They ran to see." But Peter immediately vanishes from the narrative ("Then John stepped inside... And John's gospel says..."). Introducing Peter wastes cognitive load and audio time when the entire focus of the scene is exclusively on John's reaction. 

**Grace-anchored conviction:**
Excellent. "Take him at his word: he is risen. Already, he calls that faith blessed." This is pure invitation and grace. There is no manipulation, fear, or works-based framing here.

**Freshness = faithful depth:**
Highlighting the folded face-cloth ("What thief wraps the face-cloth and lays it aside?") as the specific catalyst that sparked John's belief *before* he ever saw Jesus is a fantastic, fresh angle that grounds the resurrection in textual evidence rather than just emotion.

**One thread spine:**
**FAIL.** The script commits a major thread swap. 
- The Hook introduces a "grave robbery / stolen body" thread: *"Someone took the body."*
- The Middle uses the folded napkin to solve the robbery mystery, but then immediately pivots to a completely new "sight vs. faith" thread featuring Thomas. 
- The Landing is entirely about the "sight vs. faith" thread: *"You have seen nothing... he calls that faith blessed."*
The hook writes a check that the landing does not cash. You bait the listener with a true-crime theft mystery, but end with an epistemological lesson on faith without sight. 

**SCORE THE HOOK: 7**
"Someone took the body." It is punchy and avoids template opens, which is good. However, it scores lower because it sets up a false promise. It introduces a premise that is abandoned halfway through the script. 

**SCORE THE LANDING: 9**
"You were not at the tomb. You have seen nothing — not the linen, not the empty ledge, not him... Already, he calls that faith blessed." This is a phenomenal, bone-deep landing that perfectly applies Jesus's words to the modern viewer. It is not a stock closer. But it belongs to a script whose hook is about *sight*, not *theft*.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Fix the thread-swap by aligning the hook with the landing. If the CTA is about believing without seeing, the hook must be about sight/evidence (e.g., "What does it take to believe a resurrection? Mary needed a body. Thomas needed scars."). Drop the "stolen body" framing.
2. Cut the dangling character. Change "ran to tell his disciples, Peter and John" to just focus on John, since Peter has no payoff in this 60-second script.
3. Smooth the transition to Thomas. The jump from John's belief at the tomb directly to "When Thomas heard, he refused to believe" feels like a jarring collision of two different scenes. Add a micro-transition to contrast John's evidence-based faith with Thomas's doubt.
