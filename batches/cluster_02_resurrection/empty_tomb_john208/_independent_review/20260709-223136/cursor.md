### Doctrinal soundness
**Over-asserted reading of John 20:8.** After the quote *"and he saw, and believed"*, the script answers its own question: *"Believed what? That his Lord was risen."* John 20:8 does not name the object of belief. 20:9 immediately says *"they knew not the scripture, that he must rise again from the dead"* — which many commentators read as tension with a settled resurrection-faith claim at v.8, not confirmation of it.

This is not heresy; it is a **standard evangelical inference presented as settled fact**. For an adversarial read, that is freshness riding a contested moment — the charter’s weak spot.

**Apologetic import, not text.** *"What thief wraps the face-cloth and lays it aside?"* is modern empty-tomb argumentation. KJV 20:7 only reports arrangement: napkin *"wrapped together in a place by itself."* The gospel does not prosecute thief psychology. Clever, not textual.

Otherwise orthodox: resurrection affirmed, Thomas contrast is fair, no contrarian readings, no invented doctrine.

### Clarity on first hearing
**Improvement:** *"Believed what? That his Lord was risen"* names the object — better than the prior draft.

**Still thin for a cold listener:**
- *"When Thomas heard, he refused to believe until he saw"* — heard **what**, from **whom**? The resurrection report is assumed, not stated.
- *"Eight days later"* — after **what**? The first post-resurrection appearance to the other disciples (John 20:19–25) is never named.
- *"He did not yet know the scripture"* — **which** scripture? A zero-Bible listener has no anchor. 20:9 is gestured at, not made followable.
- *"empty ledge"* — not KJV language; burial-shelf jargon that a stranger may not picture. The piece’s own quoted evidence is **linen** and **napkin**, not “ledge.”

**CTA logic gap.** Jesus’s quoted line is about those who *"have not seen, and yet have believed"* — it does not say *"he is risen."* The close supplies that object in narrator voice: *"Take him at his word: he is risen."* A first-time listener must bridge: blessed unseen faith → faith **in the resurrection** — without the quote ever saying so.

**John/listener parallel — mostly repaired.** *"You have seen nothing — not the linen, not the empty ledge, not him"* correctly distinguishes the listener from John (who **saw** the linen). Good fix. The parallel now works: John believed before seeing the **risen Lord**; you believe without seeing **anything** — both under Jesus’s beatitude. That holds on reflection; the Thomas detour still costs seconds a cold listener may not have.
