### Doctrinal soundness

Mostly orthodox. Specific frictions:

1. **`"Believed what? That his Lord was risen."`**  
   The gospel does not supply an object for “believed.” Filling it with “risen” is the common evangelical reading (linen as evidence of resurrection, not theft), not a fringe contrarian claim — but it is still **interpretive fill**, not text. State it as inference from the linen, not as what “John’s gospel says only this” already made explicit. The next sentence almost does that; the “Believed what?” line over-certifies.

2. **`"He did not yet know the scripture — that Christ must rise from the dead."`**  
   John 20:9 is plural: **“they knew not the scripture.”** Singular “He” misstates the verse. Minor, but this piece is built on close reading of 20:8–9; do not mis-number the subject.

3. **`"Jesus was speaking about you."`**  
   20:29 blesses a class (“they that have not seen”), not a direct address to the listener. Application is sound; **direct identification is overstated**. “This is about people like you” / “that blessing reaches you” is faithful; “about you” is rhetorical overclaim.

4. **Napkin / thief line** (`"What thief wraps the face-cloth and lays it aside?"`) — standard apologetic use of 20:7, not invented doctrine. Fine.

No gain/loss gospel, no contested “secret meaning,” no clickbait. Doctrine is in the safe zone with the two overclaims above.

---

### Clarity on first hearing (zero Bible knowledge)

**Real problem here.**

Sequence:

- `and he saw, and believed.`  
- `Believed what? That his Lord was risen.`  
- `He did not yet know the scripture — that Christ must rise from the dead.`  
- `The linen was enough: John believed before he ever saw the risen Lord.`

A first-time listener can hear: *He believed Christ rose… but he didn’t know Christ must rise.* The distinction (experiential belief from the tomb vs. knowing the OT said it *must* happen) is real and classical, but **too fine for one pass in ~60s**. The dash phrase packs “scripture” + “must” without teaching either. That is a logic-gap risk, not a small polish item.

Other clarity notes:

- **`"Only the linen — and the cloth that had covered his head"`** then a full quote only about the napkin: slightly front-loaded; followable if attention holds.
- **Thomas block lands late.** “When Thomas heard…” is correct timeline, but the listener just learned linen→John’s faith; then a new character, “eight days,” hands, then the beatific saying. Necessary for the CTA spine, but dense — the napkin apologetic + John + Thomas is three beats fighting for one spine.
- **`"the empty ledge"`** is not in the text (invented visual). Harmless as narration color, but it is not evidence the listener “missed.”

### Freshness = faithful depth

Strongest asset: **the napkin ordered “in a place by itself”** and **John believes without seeing the risen Lord**, then **20:29** lands on the hearer. Surprise is in the text (folded cloth + “saw, and believed”), claim is orthodox, landing is Christ. Not a gimmick reading. Good.

---

### One thread spine

Intended spine: *empty tomb / linen evidence → believe without seeing Him → that faith is blessed (you).*

It mostly holds. Weak joint:

- Hook and middle open on **theft / napkin as anti-theft evidence**.  
- CTA pivots to **unseen faith (Thomas contrast + 20:29)**.

Those are joinable (linen is what lets John believe *without* seeing Jesus), but the script never **names that hinge in one clear line**. The thief question can feel like a mini-apologetics detour before the Thomas moral. Not full thread-swap; it is a **spine that needs one explicit suture**.

### HOOK score: **7 / 10**

`"Someone took the body. That was Mary's first thought at the empty tomb of Jesus."`

- Not a template open (not Did you know / Imagine / What if) → not capped at 5.  
- First sentence has motion and a false lead.  
- Second sentence **explains and church-signals** (“empty tomb of Jesus”) — softens the stop-scroll for a stranger who is not already in that content stream.  
- Solid cold open, not elite. **Under 8 → forces REVISE.**

---

### LANDING score: **7 / 10**

`"Take him at his word: he is risen. Already, he calls that faith blessed."`

- Second half is **piece-owned** (20:29 “blessed” + unseen faith). Good.  
- First half (`"Take him at his word: he is risen"`) could close many other Easter shorts with almost no change.  
- Stock-closer family avoided (“come to Jesus,” “turn to Him”) — so not capped at 5.  
- Combined closer still feels half-portable. **Under 8 → forces REVISE.**

Linen-specific close is set up earlier (`"The linen was enough"`) but **not paid off in the final sentence**. The note claims “linen-locked close”; the actual last lines lock on **word / blessed**, not linen. That is a self-brief miss.

### Bottom line

KJV quotes are clean. Grace is clean. Core claim is orthodox. The piece is **not** FAIL-level false teaching.

It is **REVISE** because:

1. Hook and landing both score **7** (rule: either under 8 → REVISE).  
2. First-hearing contradiction risk on “believed risen” vs “did not know … must rise.”  
3. Landing does not cash the linen image it spent so hard to earn.  
4. Small text slips: “He” vs “they” (20:9); “speaking about you” overclaim.

VERDICT: REVISE  
TOP FIXES:  
1. Re-score the close so the final sentence is locked to THIS piece’s image/verb (linen / “not seen” / “blessed”) — kill or subordinate portable “Take him at his word: he is risen” as the emotional end-line.  
2. Untangle 20:8–9 for first hearing: one plain beat that John trusted the resurrection from the empty linen *without yet grasping the written “must,”* and use KJV’s plural “they” if you name that ignorance.  
3. Sharpen the hook’s first ~5s (keep Mary’s theft lead; drop or delay the soft “empty tomb of Jesus” sermon tag) and add one explicit hinge line that joins napkin-evidence → believe-without-seeing → 20:29 so Thomas is contrast, not a new sermon.
