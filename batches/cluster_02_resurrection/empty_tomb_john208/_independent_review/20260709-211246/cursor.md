## Independent adversarial review — *The Empty Tomb* (John 20:8)

### Doctrinal soundness
**Mostly sound, but two compressions overclaim what the text says.**

1. **“No appearance. No angel.”** — True for Peter/John’s first tomb visit (vv. 5–8). It is **not** true for John 20 as a whole: Mary meets two angels (v. 12) and then Jesus (v. 14). A listener who knows the chapter may hear this as a factual error; a zero-Bible listener is fine, but the line is **over-broad** for a chapter titled John 20.

2. **“Thieves snatch. They do not stop to wrap grave-linen neatly. Robbers leave wreckage — this grave was left in order.”** — This is **apologetic inference**, not John’s claim. The text reports the napkin *“wrapped together in a place by itself”* and linen *lying*; it never says thieves were ruled out or that order proves resurrection. Orthodox as sermon logic, but the script states it as narrator-fact. That edges toward **“explaining the theology”** rather than showing the text.

3. **“John believed before he ever saw the risen Christ.”** — Defensible (v. 8 precedes v. 19). But v. 9 immediately adds: *“as yet they knew not the scripture, that he must rise again from the dead.”* The script never acknowledges that tension. You risk implying full resurrection faith where John may mean “believed the signs at the tomb” without yet grasping the resurrection from Scripture.

4. **Thomas → modern reader bridge** — Applying *“blessed are they that have not seen, and yet have believed”* to hearers who weren’t at the tomb is standard, orthodox exposition. No invented doctrine there.

### Clarity on first hearing
**Generally followable**, one soft spot:

- **“Eight days later, to a disciple who demanded proof”** — Never names Thomas. A stranger can follow (“another disciple, later, about believing without seeing”), but the jump is **compressed**. *“You have only heard. That is who he meant”* assumes the listener has already connected Thomas’s scene to themselves — that link works, but only on a second beat, not instantly.

- **“Only the linen”** then the napkin verse — minor friction. The quoted detail is specifically the **napkin**, not all linen. Not fatal, but slightly sloppy on first pass.

### Grace-anchored conviction
**PASS.** No gain/loss, no fear lever, no works framing. *“he calls that faith blessed”* invites; it does not coerce. Spirit convicts; script points.

### Freshness = faithful depth
**Mixed.**

- **Fresh spine:** John believing **at the empty tomb**, before any resurrection appearance — that is a faithful, text-pinned turn (v. 8 → v. 19 gap).
- **Stale ballast:** The orderly-grave / anti-robbery napkin argument is **heavily trodden Easter-sermon territory** in evangelical media. It eats runway without surprising a Bible-adjacent audience. Freshness lives in the John-believed-first move; the thief/robber lines feel catalog.

---

### One thread spine (hook → middle → CTA)
**PASS.** Single thread: empty tomb → orderly grave detail → John believes without seeing Jesus → Thomas beatitude → *you* are the “have not seen” hearer. No thread swap.

### Hook score: **7 / 10**
*“The tomb of Jesus stood open — and nothing was stolen.”*

- **Not** a template open (no “Did you know / Imagine…” cap).
- Creates immediate tension by reversing the theft assumption Mary voices in the very next beat.
- Stops short of an 8+ because it is **narrative setup**, not a visceral scroll-stop image or ache in the first ~5 seconds. Strong, not irresistible.

---

### Landing score: **6 / 10**
*“Jesus does not ask you to see — he asks you to believe. Already, he calls that faith blessed.”*

- Uses **see / believe / blessed** from this piece’s own vocabulary — better than a stock *“come to Jesus.”*
- But **could this exact closing pair end a different piece?** Yes — any “faith without sight” short (Thomas, 1 Peter 1:8, post-resurrection doubt texts) could land here. The parallel slogan *“does not ask you to see — asks you to believe”* is portable.
- Landing never names **what** is to be believed (a risen Christ, an empty tomb, resurrection). The middle says *“risen Christ”*; the close drops it. For a Resurrection-cluster piece, the CTA stays abstract where the thread was concrete.
- Under 8 → triggers **REVISE** per your rubric.

---

### Additional flags
- **Conviction beat is thin.** *“You were not at the tomb. You have only heard.”* is identification, not a piercing turn. Constitution wants holy tension, not just audience mapping.
- **Christ-landing is implicit.** Jesus is named, but the final invitation is generic *believe*, not *believe Him risen* / *believe what John saw*. Weak for a “whole Bible through Jesus” resurrection piece.

---

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Rebuild the landing from this piece’s **specific tomb evidence** (linen/napkin → John *saw and believed* → *your* faith in a risen Christ without having stood there) — drop the portable see/believe slogan so the final sentence could not close a different “faith without sight” script.
2. Tighten or cut the **anti-thief apologetic** (*“Thieves snatch… grave was left in order”*); let the KJV napkin line do the work, or soften to “John noticed” rather than narrator-proof for robbery.
3. Fix **“No appearance. No angel.”** to scope to John’s moment only (*“No vision in that tomb. No angel spoke to him there.”*) and optionally nod to v. 9 so “believed” isn’t oversold as full resurrection understanding before Scripture clicked.
