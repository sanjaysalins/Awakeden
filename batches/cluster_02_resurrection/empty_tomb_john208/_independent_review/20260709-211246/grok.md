I'll review this narration adversarially against the lens criteria—checking KJV wording and doctrine carefully.## Independent review — Empty Tomb (John 20)

### KJV verbatim
Quoted lines match KJV for 20:2b, 20:7, 20:8b, 20:29b. No altered words inside quotes.

Narrator paraphrase is fine (not quoted). Minor imprecision later: **“wrap grave-linen neatly”** over-reads **“wrapped together in a place by itself”** (20:7). KJV is *wrapped / set apart*, not “neatly.” Soft stretch, not a quote fail.

---

### Doctrinal soundness
Orthodox spine: empty tomb → linen evidence → John believes without a Christophany → 20:29 blessing on unseen faith. Not clickbait or contrarian.

**Overclaim:** opening **“nothing was stolen”** is asserted as fact. The passage gives Mary’s opposite claim (**“They have taken away the Lord…”**) and ordered linen as *evidence*. Classic apologetic, not heresy — but the script states the conclusion before the text earns it.

**Apologetic tidy-up of 20:9:** **“he saw, and believed”** is used without 20:9 (*“For as yet they knew not the scripture…”*). Mainstream evangelical reading (John believed from the signs) is defensible; the piece still flattens a real interpretive knot.

**Gospel object thin:** “risen Christ” appears once in passing. For an unchurched ear, the claim never lands as plain speech: *Jesus rose; the body was not taken.*

---

### Clarity on first hearing
**Hook paradox:** **“The tomb of Jesus stood open — and nothing was stolen.”** Then the body is gone and Mary says it was taken. Zero-Bible listener: if the body is gone, something *was* taken — unless “stolen” means thieves vs resurrection. That logic is not taught until the napkin beat. Clever, not clear in the first 5 seconds.

**Bare “believe”:** **“he asks you to believe”** — believe *what*? That thieves didn’t do it? That Jesus rose? Trust him as Lord? Object is implied, not said.

**Speaker of 20:2:** Mary runs → scripture line with no “she said.” On first hearing it can float as voice-of-God caption rather than Mary’s wrong theory — which undercuts the hook’s contrast.

---

### Grace-anchored conviction
No fear, gain/loss, or works pressure. Invitation stays soft. Passes this lens.

---

### Freshness / thread spine
**Freshness:** napkin ≠ stolen body is real-text depth, not the fake “Jewish folded napkin = I’ll return” myth. Good restraint. For church ears it is well-worn Easter apologetics, not a surprising angle on the text.

**Thread:** half A = ordered grave / not stolen; half B = believe without seeing / Thomas beatitude. Linked (John’s faith from signs), but the **landing drops the napkin/order image entirely**. Closing only uses the “not seen → believe” half. Mild spine drift at the CTA.

### Other nits

- **“That is who he meant”** — pastoral application of 20:29 is standard, a bit absolute.  
- **“John never forgot”** — narrative color; fine.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Re-earn the open and the close from one spine: lead with the napkin/order (or Mary’s “taken away”) as a question, not a settled “nothing was stolen,” and end on that same image/verb so the CTA cannot close a different piece.
2. Name the gospel object in plain speech for first-time ears: the body was not stolen — Jesus is risen; “believe” must mean faith in the risen Lord, not a vague faith-without-sight slogan.
3. Tighten the napkin line to the KJV (“wrapped together… by itself”), not “neatly,” and make Mary’s 20:2 clearly *her* wrong theory so the contrast lands on first hearing.
