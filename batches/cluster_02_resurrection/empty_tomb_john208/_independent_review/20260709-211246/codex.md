# Independent review — codex (OK, 36s)

Findings:

- Hook score: 7/10. “The tomb of Jesus stood open — and nothing was stolen” is clean and visual, but it depends on a conclusion the piece must prove. It is not a template open, but it is more apologetic thesis than visceral hook.
- Landing score: 6/10. “Jesus does not ask you to see — he asks you to believe. Already, he calls that faith blessed” uses this passage, but the final sentence is still close to a stock “believe” closer. It does not strongly return to the piece’s own image: the ordered grave-clothes / empty tomb / heard-not-seen faith.
- Doctrinal risk: “That is who he meant” overstates John 20:29. The blessing certainly includes later believers who have not seen, but saying Jesus “meant” the listener specifically narrows the text too aggressively.
- Textual/interpretive risk: “John believed before he ever saw the risen Christ” is plausible, but John 20:9 immediately says, “For as yet they knew not the scripture, that he must rise again from the dead.” The narration should not flatten that tension. It risks making John’s faith sound fuller than the passage itself states.
- KJV accuracy: the Scripture lines appear KJV-exact: “They have taken away the Lord out of the sepulchre…,” “And the napkin…,” “and he saw, and believed,” and “blessed are they that have not seen, and yet have believed.”
- Clarity issue: “Thieves snatch. They do not stop to wrap grave-linen neatly” is clear, but a bit too airtight. The text says the napkin was “wrapped together in a place by itself”; it does not explicitly say all grave-linen was neatly arranged. “This grave was left in order” is a faithful inference, but should feel like an observation, not a courtroom proof.
- Grace/conviction: mostly sound. “Jesus does not ask you to see — he asks you to believe” is acceptable, but could sound like bare demand unless anchored more clearly in Christ’s resurrection and blessing.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Qualify “That is who he meant” so the application does not overclaim the verse.
2. Handle John 20:9’s tension so “John believed” does not imply fully formed resurrection understanding.
3. Make the final CTA land through the ordered grave-clothes / heard-without-seeing image instead of a generic believe closer.
