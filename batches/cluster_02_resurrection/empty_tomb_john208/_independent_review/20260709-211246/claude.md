## Findings

**KJV verbatim — clean.** All four quotes checked exact against `data/kjv_cache.json`:
- John 20:2b "They have taken away the Lord out of the sepulchre, and we know not where they have laid him." ✓
- John 20:7 full verse ✓ (including both commas around "that was about his head")
- John 20:8b "and he saw, and believed." ✓ — and the narrator honestly paraphrases the elided "Then went in also that other disciple" as "Then John stepped inside," so the fragment isn't misleading.
- John 20:29b "blessed are they that have not seen, and yet have believed." ✓ (correctly tagged `[jesus]`)

**Doctrine — sound, with one soft spot.** The tidy-graveclothes apologetic ("Thieves snatch. They do not stop to wrap grave-linen neatly") is a legitimate, mainstream inference from 20:7, not invented doctrine. But note: "John believed before he ever saw the risen Christ" is stated with full confidence while John 20:9 ("For as yet they knew not the scripture...") sits right after v.8 and is silently skipped. The traditional evangelical reading (v.8 = genuine resurrection faith from evidence) supports the line, so this is not a FAIL — but the piece leans its whole climax on a verse whose very next verse qualifies it, and never acknowledges that. A hostile commenter will quote v.9 back at you.

**One overclaim.** "That is who he meant" (re: 20:29) asserts specific intent — that Jesus meant *you, the listener*. The blessing certainly *includes* every later believer (and John 20:31 supports addressing the reader), but "meant" is a shade stronger than the text states. "That is who he blessed" would claim exactly what the verse says.

**Clarity nit — "napkin."** A zero-Bible listener hears "napkin" and pictures a dinner table. The following narrator line ("grave-linen") partially rescues it, but there's a two-second confusion window on first hearing. One narrator word before the quote ("the face-cloth — the napkin —" or "Only the linen — and the cloth from his head:") would close it.

**Hook tension is intentional but slightly self-colliding.** "nothing was stolen" (line 1) is immediately followed by Mary crying "They have taken away the Lord" (line 3). That's the designed paradox and it resolves at "Thieves snatch," but the resolution arrives ~20 seconds in. It works; just flagging that the hook asserts the conclusion before the evidence.

**Thread spine — solid.** One spine throughout: open tomb → orderly linen → saw-and-believed → blessed-without-seeing → you. No thread-swapping; the landing verb pair (see/believe) is the same pair the whole piece turns on.

**Grace-anchored — yes.** No fear, no gain/loss, no pressure. "Already, he calls that faith blessed" is pure grace framing.

## Scores

**HOOK: 8/10.** "The tomb of Jesus stood open — and nothing was stolen." Not a template open, genuine paradox, five words of setup before the twist. Docked from higher because "the tomb of Jesus stood open" alone is a familiar image; the payload is all in the back half of the sentence.

**LANDING: 8/10.** "Jesus does not ask you to see — he asks you to believe. Already, he calls that faith blessed." Built from this piece's own see/believe axis and 20:29's own verb; it could not close a Prodigal Son or Psalm 22 piece. Not stock. Docked from higher because "he asks you to believe" edges toward generic; "Already, he calls that faith blessed" is the line doing the real earned work.

VERDICT: PASS
TOP FIXES:
1. Soften "That is who he meant" to "That is who he blessed" — claims exactly what John 20:29 says instead of asserting intent beyond it.
2. Gloss "napkin" for the zero-Bible listener: "Only the linen — and the cloth from his head:" before the 20:7 quote.
3. Consider one clause acknowledging John 20:9's frame (e.g. "Before any Scripture made sense to him — he saw, and believed") so the climax can't be undercut by the verse that follows it.
