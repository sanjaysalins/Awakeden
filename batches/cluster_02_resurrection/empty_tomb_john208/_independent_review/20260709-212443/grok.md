## Doctrinal soundness

**1. Hook overclaims Mary’s thought (precision fail).**  
> *“The first person at the empty tomb of Jesus thought **thieves** had taken his body.”*

John 20:2 has *“They have taken away the Lord”* — removal, not theft. Mary later thinks the gardener moved him (20:15). “Thieves” is Matthew’s guard-story / apologetic frame, not her first words. Not heresy, but the cold open invents a motive the text does not give her.

**2. “John believed… risen Christ” is mainstream, not contrarian.**  
> *“John believed before he ever saw the risen Christ.”*

Reading 20:8 as resurrection-faith from the grave-clothes (before any appearance) is the ordinary evangelical reading and fits John’s aim (20:31). Not clickbait novelty. Acceptable.

**3. Mild compression, not false teaching.**  
Peter enters first (20:6); the script jumps to John. Fine for 60s if you don’t imply John was alone in discovering the linen.

## Clarity on first hearing

**1. Cryptic beat (real problem).**  
> *“He did not yet know the scripture.”*

Zero Bible knowledge hears: *which* scripture? *Why* does that matter? KJV 20:9 supplies the missing object: *“that he must rise again from the dead.”* Dropping that leaves a logic hole on first listen.

**2. Hook vs quote mismatch.**  
Listener hears “thieves,” then Mary’s line is only “They have taken…” Small dissonance; trains the ear on an apologetic frame the quote never says.

**3. Names.**  
“Peter and John” / “Thomas” are thin for a total outsider, but the story line still tracks. Not blocking.

**4. No self-contradiction** in the main claim: napkin orderly → not stolen → risen → faith without sight of the risen Lord.
