# Independent review — claude (OK, 102s)

**KJV verbatim check — clean.** All four quotes verified exact against KJV:
- John 20:2b "They have taken away the Lord out of the sepulchre, and we know not where they have laid him." ✓
- John 20:7 full verse ✓ (word-for-word, including "wrapped together in a place by itself")
- John 20:8b "and he saw, and believed." ✓
- John 20:29b "blessed are they that have not seen, and yet have believed." ✓

Attribution frames stay in narrator voice; "He did not yet know the scripture" correctly tracks John 20:9; "No angel had spoken to him" is accurate for John's sequence (angels appear to Mary in 20:12, after this). "About a week later" fairly renders "after eight days" (20:26).

**Finding 1 — grounding over-claim in the hook (the real problem).** "The first person at the empty tomb of Jesus thought thieves had taken his body." John never says Mary thought *thieves*. Her words are "They have taken away the Lord" (20:2) — agent unspecified — and her actual working theory surfaces in 20:15, where she asks the presumed *gardener* "if thou have borne him hence," i.e. lawful removal, not theft. The stolen-body theory belongs to the guards' rumor in Matthew 28:13, not to Mary. The narration states her inner thought as fact, and then builds the spine on it ("What thief wraps the face-cloth…", "the body was not stolen"). The apologetic itself is sound and ancient (the undisturbed grave-clothes argument), but pinning "thieves" on Mary is exactly the recurring witness-over-claim defect this corpus keeps catching. One word fixes it: "thought **someone** had taken his body" — the thief *question* can still be posed by the narrator over the napkin, where it's an inference, not an assertion.

**Finding 2 — "The gospel says only this."** Rhetorically nice, but literally false: 20:8 also says "Then went in also that other disciple, which came first to the sepulchre." A zero-Bible listener takes "only this" at face value. Soften to "Of that moment, the gospel says only this" or "The gospel gives him three words."

**Finding 3 — clarity: "He did not yet know the scripture."** A listener with zero Bible knowledge doesn't know which scripture or why it matters. Half a clause fixes it: "He did not yet know the scripture — that Christ must rise from the dead" (paraphrasing 20:9 in narrator voice, not quoted).

**Doctrine:** sound throughout. Grace-anchored — no fear, no gain-loss; the landing invites ("Believe him without seeing him") and pronounces the blessing Jesus already spoke. No contrarian reading; "John believed before he ever saw the risen Christ" is the mainstream evangelical reading of 20:8.

**Thread spine:** one clean line — stolen-body assumption → the wrapped napkin refutes it → John believes without seeing the risen Christ → Thomas contrast → you, who have only heard, believe without seeing. No thread-swap; the Thomas beat serves the spine rather than replacing it.

**HOOK: 8/10.** Non-template, specific, genuinely surprising (the first witness's first thought was theft, not resurrection). Would stop a scroll. Its only flaw is the accuracy issue in Finding 1 — which is why it must be fixed, not because it's weak.

**LANDING: 8/10.** "Believe him without seeing him. Already, he calls that faith blessed." Built from this piece's own verse and verb; "Already, he calls that faith blessed" could not close a different piece — it's the 20:29 blessing turned present-tense onto the listener. Not stock.

Both scores clear 8, but the hook states as fact something the text points away from (20:15), and the whole spine leans on it — that's a grounding error under this project's own bar, so it goes back for the one-word repair.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Hook: "thought thieves had taken his body" → "thought someone had taken his body" — John 20:2/20:15 never attribute a theft theory to Mary (she suspects the gardener moved him); keep the thief inference where it belongs, in the narrator's napkin question.
2. "The gospel says only this" → "Of that moment, the gospel says only this" (20:8 says more; don't let a literal listener catch the narration in an overstatement).
3. Gloss the scripture reference for first-hearing clarity: "He did not yet know the scripture — that Christ must rise from the dead" (John 20:9, narrator paraphrase, unquoted).
