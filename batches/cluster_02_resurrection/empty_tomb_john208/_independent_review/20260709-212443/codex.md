# Independent review — codex (OK, 33s)

Findings:

- **Hook score: 7/10.** “The first person at the empty tomb of Jesus thought thieves had taken his body” is text-grounded and mildly surprising, but it states the premise plainly rather than creating immediate tension. Under 8 means revise.

- **Landing score: 7/10.** “Believe him without seeing him. Already, he calls that faith blessed” is tied to John 20:29, but it is still close to a stock closer. “Believe him without seeing him” could end many Thomas/resurrection pieces. The stronger unique image is “the linen still speaks,” but the final sentence does not fully land through that image.

- **KJV accuracy: no altered words found** in the Scripture lines:
  - “They have taken away the Lord out of the sepulchre…”
  - “And the napkin, that was about his head…”
  - “and he saw, and believed.”
  - “blessed are they that have not seen, and yet have believed.”

- **Potential overclaim:** “What thief wraps the face-cloth and lays it aside?” The folded/placed napkin supports order rather than grave robbery, but this rhetorical question can overstate the apologetic force. John reports the detail; he does not explicitly argue, “therefore no thief.” Keep it as evidence, not proof by cleverness.

- **Doctrinal concern:** “But the linen still speaks: the body was not stolen — Jesus is risen.” The resurrection is true, but the narration risks making the linen cloth carry more evidential weight than the passage itself assigns. John’s belief is important, but the fuller resurrection witness in John 20 includes Mary seeing Christ and Jesus appearing to the disciples.

- **Clarity issue for new listeners:** “He did not yet know the scripture” may confuse a zero-Bible listener. They may hear it as “John did not know the Bible at all,” when John 20:9 means they did not yet understand the Scripture that Christ must rise from the dead.

- **Thread spine mostly holds:** hook → missing body → ordered graveclothes → John believes → Thomas/blessed faith → listener invited to believe. The Thomas move works, but it slightly shifts from “empty tomb evidence” to “faith without sight.” That shift is biblical, but the bridge could be tighter.

- **Grace-anchored conviction:** Mostly sound. “Believe him without seeing him” is direct but not fear/gain-loss/works framed. No manipulation detected.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Make the final CTA land through the piece’s own image: the linen/face-cloth, not a generic “believe him.”
2. Soften the “What thief…” line so it does not overclaim beyond John’s text.
3. Clarify “He did not yet know the scripture” as “did not yet understand the Scripture that Christ must rise.”
