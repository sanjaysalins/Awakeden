# Independent review — gemini (OK, 43s)

Here are the findings from an independent, adversarial review of the narration:

- **Doctrinal soundness:** PASS. The script faithfully tracks the biblical sequence without inventing doctrine. It accurately captures Mary's initial assumption of theft, the physical state of the grave clothes, John's immediate belief (John 20:8), and Jesus's later words to Thomas. 
- **KJV verbatim:** PASS. All quotes ("They have taken away...", "And the napkin...", "and he saw, and believed.", "blessed are they...") are exact, unadulterated KJV matches.
- **Clarity on first hearing:** PASS. The progression from the empty tomb to the evidence of the cloth, to John's reaction, to Thomas, and finally to the listener is easy to follow.
- **Grace-anchored conviction:** PASS. The lines "Believe him without seeing him. Already, he calls that faith blessed" offer a pure, grace-based invitation. There is no manipulation, fear-mongering, or works-based framing.
- **Freshness = faithful depth:** PASS. Contrasting John (who believed by seeing only the folded cloth) with Thomas (who demanded to see the physical Christ) is a sharp, faithful insight that elevates the script beyond a standard Easter summary.
- **One thread spine:** REVISE. The script suffers from a mid-point thread swap. It opens with a "grave robbery" thread ("thought thieves had taken his body"), resolves that mystery immediately ("What thief wraps the face-cloth..."), and then pivots to a completely different "believing without seeing" thread for the second half. The hook and the CTA belong to two slightly different thematic spines.
- **SCORE THE HOOK:** 6. "The first person at the empty tomb of Jesus thought thieves had taken his body." While not a banned template, it reads like a dry Bible commentary summary. It lacks visceral grip and doesn't immediately compel a scroller to stop. 
- **SCORE THE LANDING:** 8. "But the linen still speaks: the body was not stolen — Jesus is risen. Believe him without seeing him. Already, he calls that faith blessed." This is a strong, earned landing that successfully uses the specific imagery of the script to make a direct, non-stock appeal to the listener.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Rewrite the hook to score an 8+ by giving it more visceral punch and aligning it with the true spine of the script ("believing without seeing" rather than just "thieves").
2. Bridge the two halves of the script. Explicitly connect the physical evidence (the folded face-cloth proving no thief was there) to John's leap of faith, making the transition from "theft" to "belief" seamless.
3. Tighten the narrator lead-in: "The first day of the week, when it was yet dark, Mary Magdalene saw the stone taken away — and ran to tell Peter and John:" is slightly clunky and wordy for spoken audio pacing.
