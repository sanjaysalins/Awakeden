# Independent review — codex (OK, 34s)

Hook Score: 8/10. “The resurrection was first reported as a grave robbery” is concrete, text-rooted, and has tension without sounding gimmicky.

Landing Score: 7/10. “Already, he calls that faith blessed” is connected to John 20:29, but “Believe him” is a stock CTA and weakens the landing before the final sentence.

Findings:

- KJV verbatim appears correct in the quoted scripture lines. I do not see altered wording inside quotation marks.
- Doctrinal concern: “That is who he blessed” over-applies John 20:29. Jesus blesses “they that have not seen, and yet have believed,” not everyone who merely “has only heard.” The line should preserve the condition of faith, not collapse hearing and believing.
- Clarity issue: “The resurrection was first reported as a grave robbery” is punchy, but technically Mary reported the missing body, not the resurrection. This is probably defensible as a hook, but it risks sounding like the resurrection itself was misunderstood rather than the empty tomb.
- Possible overstatement: “Thieves snatch — they do not stop to set a grave in order.” This is a good observation, but it is stated as absolute psychology. The text implies order, but does not prove what all thieves would or would not do.
- Grace framing is mostly sound, but “Believe him” lands as bare imperative. It would be stronger if the invitation emerged more directly from the text’s own movement: hearing, seeing the signs, and receiving Jesus’ blessing.
- Thread is coherent: grave robbery report -> ordered linens -> John believes before seeing -> blessing on unseen faith. No major thread-swap.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Fix “That is who he blessed” so it clearly means those who hear and believe, not merely those who were absent.
2. Replace the stock CTA “Believe him” with a closer built from the linen/order/blessed-without-seeing image.
3. Soften “Thieves snatch” from absolute claim to text-based inference.
