## Findings

**KJV verbatim — CLEAN.** All four quotes exact. Fragment quoting of 20:8 ("and he saw, and believed") is honest — the narrator line "Then John stepped inside" fairly replaces the elided "Then went in also that other disciple…".

**Doctrine — sound, with one precision wobble in the hook.**
- Hook line: *"The resurrection was first reported as a grave robbery."* Mary's actual words (quoted next) are *"They have taken away… we know not where they have laid him"* — a report of **removal by unknown hands**, not explicitly *robbery* (she later guesses the gardener moved him, 20:15; the explicit theft story is the guards' bribed lie in Matthew 28:13). The narrator's own "Thieves snatch" bridge makes the robbery inference explicit, so it holds together — but strictly, the hook slightly over-states what Mary said. Defensible, not a doctrinal error; worth one word of softening ("reported as a stolen body" would be airtight).
- *"He did not yet know the scripture"* — directly grounded in John 20:9. *"No angel had spoken to him; nothing had appeared"* — true within John's account at that moment. *"John believed before he ever saw the risen Christ"* — the standard evangelical reading of 20:8; not contrarian.
- The 20:29 link is legitimate: the blessing genuinely is for those who believe without seeing. No sleight — though note John *did* see the grave-clothes evidence, which the listener hasn't; the piece wisely routes the listener's claim to blessing through 20:29, not through John's experience. Sound.
- Conviction is grace-anchored: *"Already, he calls that faith blessed"* — pure invitation, zero fear/gain-loss framing. Good.

**Clarity on first hearing — mostly strong, two small snags.**
- *"nothing had appeared"* is slightly odd phrasing aloud (appeared to whom? what kind of thing?). "no vision had come" or "he had seen no risen Lord" would land cleaner by ear.
- *"Eight days later"* — the anchor is ambiguous (eight days after the tomb morning, or after something else?). KJV says "after eight days" from the first appearance; close enough that no listener is misled, but "About a week later" would be both safe and clearer.

**Thread spine — intact.** Robbery-report → linen ordered, not snatched → John believes unseen → Thomas contrast → "you have only heard; that is who he blessed." One spine, no swap. The linen is the through-image and the landing reuses it (*"The linen still speaks"*). Well built.

**HOOK: 8/10.** Not a template open; a genuine pattern-interrupt that is (nearly) true of the text itself. Docked from higher only for the robbery/removal precision wobble.

**LANDING: 8/10.** *"Believe him. Already, he calls that faith blessed."* is built from this piece's own verb (believed, 20:8/20:29) and its own blessing; the final sentence could not close a different piece. "Believe him" alone skirts stock, but the "Already… blessed" turn does new work.
