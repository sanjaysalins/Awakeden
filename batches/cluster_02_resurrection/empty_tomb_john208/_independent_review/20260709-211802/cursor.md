### Doctrinal soundness
Orthodox resurrection faith; no contrarian reading. Two problems:

**1. The John 20:8 claim is stated more confidently than the text allows.**

The line *"John believed before he ever saw the risen Christ"* treats the object of *"believed"* as settled: resurrection faith. John 20:8 does not name what he believed. John 20:9 immediately follows: *"For as yet they knew not the scripture, that he must rise again from the dead"* — plural *they*, same tomb visit. The piece never resolves that tension. A hostile reader can say you imported a standard evangelical harmonization and presented it as narrative fact.

**2. Peter is silently erased — and that weakens the centerpiece.**

John 20:6–7: Peter enters first and sees the same linen and napkin arrangement. Only after that does John enter and *"believed."* The narration never asks why identical evidence produced belief in one apostle and not the other. That omission is not heresy, but it is a selective reading of the pericope presented as straightforward reportage.

**3. Anti-theft logic is apologetics, not John’s words.**

*"Thieves snatch — they do not stop to set a grave in order"* and *"The linen still speaks: the body was not stolen — Jesus is risen"* are fair inference from 20:7, but John never states them. The script upgrades grave-cloth observation to a settled theft refutation, then to *"Jesus is risen"* as narrator verdict. Doctrinally fine; exegetically it skips a step the text itself leaves implicit.

### Clarity on first hearing
**Hook logic slip (concrete):**

*"The resurrection was first reported as a grave robbery."*

On first hearing, that sounds like the resurrection itself was reported as robbery. What the text actually gives is: an empty tomb was reported, and Mary/Peter/John feared theft. The resurrection was not reported at all yet — only absence of the body. A zero-Bible listener can stumble on that subject–verb mismatch before the piece corrects course.

**Identity gap:**

*"Then John stepped inside."* Which John? The text’s *"other disciple, whom Jesus loved"* is never surfaced. In a 60s short that may be acceptable, but it is a clarity cost for strangers.

**Timeline compression:**

*"No angel had spoken to him; nothing had appeared"* is true for John’s tomb visit, but without a time marker a new listener may think no resurrection appearances ever happened in the chapter. Minor, but the line assumes chronology the listener does not yet have.

**Thomas beat:**

*"to a disciple who demanded proof"* softens John 20:25’s specific demand (wounds/nails/side). Not wrong, but vaguer than the passage — *proof* is narrator gloss, not KJV language.
