# Independent review — gemini (OK, 60s)

I have reviewed this artifact with an adversarial, skeptical lens. Here are the findings based on the required criteria:

- **Doctrinal soundness:** PASSES. The script asserts, "John believed before he ever saw the risen Christ," anchored to John 20:8 ("and he saw, and believed"). This is an orthodox, biblically faithful reading of the empty tomb narrative. No invented doctrine or contrarian readings are present.
- **KJV verbatim:** PASSES. "They have taken away the Lord..." (John 20:2), "And the napkin..." (John 20:7), "and he saw, and believed" (John 20:8), and "blessed are they that have not seen..." (John 20:29) are all perfectly exact KJV matches. 
- **Clarity on first hearing:** SLIGHT ISSUE. The narrator's setup line, "The first day of the week, when it was yet dark, Mary Magdalene saw..." bleeds archaic KJV phrasing directly into the modern narrator's voice. For a listener with zero Bible knowledge, this causes stylistic whiplash before the actual quote even starts. Additionally, the phrase "to a disciple who demanded proof" introduces an unnamed new character (Thomas) right at the climax, which slightly distracts from the core narrative. 
- **Grace-anchored conviction:** PASSES. The lines "That is who he blessed" and "Already, he calls that faith blessed" are pure grace. There is no manufactured fear, works-framing, or self-interest leverage used. 
- **Freshness = faithful depth:** PASSES. Drawing a straight line from the specific detail of the folded face-cloth ("not lying with the linen clothes") to John's sightless faith, and then extending that exact paradigm to the viewer, is a highly effective and surprisingly deep textual thread.
- **One thread spine:** PASSES. The logic chain is unbroken: a suspected grave robbery -> evidence of the folded linen (not a robbery) -> John believes without seeing -> Jesus blesses those who believe without seeing -> the listener is invited into that exact blessing. 

- **SCORE THE HOOK: 9.** "The resurrection was first reported as a grave robbery." This is an exceptional, punchy hook. It relies on zero template crutches (no "Did you know") and instantly reframes the most famous event in history as a true-crime scene. It forces the listener to stop scrolling.
- **SCORE THE LANDING: 8.** "The linen still speaks: the body was not stolen — Jesus is risen." This is a fantastic thematic payoff. However, the final staccato sequence ("Believe him. Already, he calls that faith blessed.") gets a bit choppy, and the phrase "Believe him" flirts dangerously with being a generic stock closer. It survives only because the preceding imagery and the subsequent blessing are so deeply anchored to this specific script. 

VERDICT: PASS
