**FINDINGS & ANALYSIS**

**Doctrinal Soundness:** 
Pass. The script is biblically faithful. It accurately captures the sequence of John 20: John's belief triggered by the empirical evidence of the folded face-cloth *before* he understood the prophetic scriptures (John 20:9 is rightly summarized as "He did not yet know the scripture"), contrasted with Thomas who demanded to see Jesus' body directly. No contrarian theology is invented here.

**KJV Verbatim:**
Pass. 
- "They have taken away the Lord..." (John 20:2b) - Exact.
- "And the napkin, that was about his head..." (John 20:7) - Exact.
- "and he saw, and believed." (John 20:8b) - Exact.
- "blessed are they that have not seen..." (John 20:29b) - Exact.

**Clarity on first hearing:**
The logic and transitions are tight, but the narrator’s voice is compromised in the second line. 
*Line:* `[narrator] The first day of the week, when it was yet dark, Mary Magdalene saw the stone taken away...`
You have bled archaic KJV syntax ("when it was yet dark") into the modern narrator's block. The narrator must speak in crisp, contemporary English to provide a stark audio contrast to the KJV quotes. Reading KJV phrasing as the narrator muddles the audio distinction. 

**Grace-anchored conviction:**
Pass. The conviction is purely grace-anchored: "Believe him without seeing him. Already, he calls that faith blessed." No fear-mongering or works-based pressure.

**Freshness & Thread Spine:**
Pass. The thread is excellent and unbroken. Hook (stolen body) -> Evidence (folded linen disproves theft) -> Climax (John believes by seeing the linen, not Jesus) -> Contrast (Thomas demands to see Jesus) -> CTA (We cannot see either, but we receive the blessing of faith). It never loses the thread.

**SCORE THE HOOK: 7**
*Line:* `[narrator] Someone took the body — that was the first thought at the empty tomb of Jesus.`
It avoids the 5-point template cap, but it reads like a dry museum audio tour. It lacks visceral punch. It states a fact rather than creating immediate narrative tension or mystery for a scroller. A 60-second short needs to grab the viewer by the collar immediately (e.g., "The first eyewitness to the empty tomb didn't think 'resurrection'. She thought 'crime scene.'"). Because this is under an 8, it triggers a REVISE.

**SCORE THE LANDING: 9**
*Line:* `You were not at the tomb. You have only heard. But the linen still speaks: the body was not stolen — Jesus is risen. Believe him without seeing him. Already, he calls that faith blessed.`
Outstanding. It is highly specific to this exact script's thread (the speaking linen, seeing vs. not seeing) and seamlessly applies Christ's words to Thomas directly to the modern viewer. It is not a stock closer.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Rewrite the hook to be punchier and more urgent (score 7). Create immediate tension rather than just stating "that was the first thought".
2. Modernize the narrator's phrasing in line 2 ("when it was yet dark"); keep KJV archaic syntax strictly inside the `[scripture]` tags.
3. Consider changing "Believe him without seeing him" to "Believe in him without seeing him" for slightly more natural modern conversational flow in the CTA.
