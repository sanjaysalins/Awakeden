## Doctrine / facts
- No invented doctrine. The face-cloth apologetic ("What thief wraps the face-cloth and lays it aside?") is a legitimate inference straight from v7, not a contested reading.
- "He did not yet know the scripture — that Christ must rise from the dead" — faithful paraphrase of John 20:9, kept in narrator voice, not quote marks. Correct handling.
- "About a week later" for John 20:26's "after eight days" — fine as narrator paraphrase.
- **Attribution note:** the text says Mary ran to "Simon Peter, and to the other disciple, whom Jesus loved" — the narration names him **John** ("Peter and John", "Then John stepped inside"). Near-universal traditional identification, acceptable for this format, but be aware the gospel itself never names him.
- **Logical wrinkle a sharp listener may catch:** the quoted proof-text is literally "he **saw**, and believed," yet the piece uses John as the pattern for believing *without* seeing. The script defends itself precisely ("John believed before he ever saw the **risen Lord**") — but the distinction (saw the linen, not the Lord) is carried by one word and could slip past on first hearing. One clarifying beat ("He saw no angel, no risen body — only the linen") would close the gap fully.
- **Slight inferential leap:** "the linen still speaks: the body was not stolen — **Jesus is risen**." Strictly, the folded cloth disproves theft; it doesn't by itself prove resurrection. Forgivable in 60 seconds because the whole account carries the claim, but the dash smuggles the biggest step in the argument.

## Grace / conviction
Clean. No fear, no gain-loss, no works framing. "Already, he calls that faith blessed" is invitation built on Jesus's own pre-spoken beatitude — grace-anchored.

## Thread spine
One spine, no swaps: stolen-body theory → linen evidence → John believes before seeing the risen Lord → Thomas contrast → "you have only heard" → believe without seeing. Hook and CTA are the same thread.

## Scores
- **HOOK: 8/10.** "Someone took the body" is a non-template, in-text misdirection (it's Mary's own first theory) that earns its resolution. Docked from higher because the trailing clause "— that was the first thought at the empty tomb of Jesus" flattens the punch slightly; opening on the bare claim and revealing the tomb one beat later would be stronger.
- **LANDING: 8/10.** "Believe him without seeing him. Already, he calls that faith blessed." Built from THIS piece's verb pair (seen/believed) and its own quoted verse; "Already" does new work (the blessing was pronounced in advance, over the listener). It could not cleanly end a different piece — "the linen still speaks" two sentences earlier anchors it here. Not a 9+ because "believe him" sits adjacent to stock-closer territory even though the seeing-frame rescues it.

VERDICT: PASS
TOP FIXES:
1. Add one clarifying beat before the Thomas turn — "He saw no angel, no risen Lord — only the linen" — so first-hearing listeners don't trip on "he saw, and believed" being used to commend faith *without* sight.
2. Soften the inferential dash in the CTA line: let the linen disprove the theft and let the *risen Lord's own words* (already quoted) carry "Jesus is risen," rather than asserting both from the cloth alone.
