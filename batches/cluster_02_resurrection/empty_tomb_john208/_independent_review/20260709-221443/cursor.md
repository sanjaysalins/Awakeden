### Doctrinal soundness — orthodox, but overclaims what the text says

No contrarian reading, no Christ-denial, no invented theology. Resurrection landing is standard evangelical.

**Problem 1 — belief-object overreach.** After the exact quote *"and he saw, and believed,"* the script asserts:

> *"John believed before he ever saw the risen Lord."*

John 20:8 does **not** name what he believed. John 20:9 (same visit) says *"they knew not the scripture, that he must rise again from the dead."* The piece half-acknowledges that (*"He did not yet know the scripture"*) but **never resolves the tension** — it states resurrection-faith as settled fact. That is a mainstream harmonization, not what the cited verse says. Worse, it follows *"Of that moment, John's gospel says only this"* — then immediately goes beyond *only this*.

**Problem 2 — theft frame imported.** Mary’s KJV line is *"They have taken away the Lord"* — no thief named. The middle asks *"What **thief** wraps the face-cloth…?"* and the landing verdict is *"the body was not **stolen**."* Ancient apologetic, sound in sermon — but John 20:2/7 never use *thief* or *stolen*. The script upgrades inference to narrative spine.

**Problem 3 — selective witness.** Peter enters first, sees the same linen and napkin (John 20:6–7), and is not said to believe. The piece never accounts for that. Not heresy — but a selective reading sold as the straight sequence.

**Problem 4 — "John" as explicit identity.** *"Then John stepped inside"* treats the beloved disciple as named in these verses. John 20:1–8 never names him; that is tradition read as on-screen fact.

---

### Clarity on first hearing — three gaps

**1. Believed *what*?** A zero-Bible listener hears *"and he saw, and believed"* → *"He did not yet know the scripture — that Christ must rise from the dead"* → *"John believed before he ever saw the risen Lord."* That reads like a contradiction unless the listener already knows resurrection is the object. The script never names what the ordered grave clothes **showed** before jumping to *"Jesus is risen."*

**2. "You have only heard" vs. what just happened.** The viewer **just heard** the tomb narrated in the last ~45 seconds. *"You were not at the tomb. You have only heard"* is the right **theological** bridge to John 20:29, but on first hearing it sounds self-undermining: *You literally just told me — what do you mean I only heard?* Needs one clarifying beat (historical witness vs. your position now).

**3. Mary's "we."** Her quote says *"**we** know not where they have laid him"* while the narration has only Mary at the tomb (John 20:1). Who is *we*? Small, but real for the stated zero-knowledge audience.

**Thomas arrives cold:** *"Thomas refused to believe until he saw"* — no *"one of the twelve,"* no *"when the others told him."* Bible-literate listeners bridge it; strangers get a name-drop.

### Freshness = faithful depth — mixed

**Strong:** Pinning to John 20:8’s sparse *"he saw, and believed"* (not a full Easter montage), the ordered-napkin detail, and the 20:29 bridge to hearers who *"were not at the tomb."*

**Weak:** *"What thief wraps the face-cloth and lays it aside?"* is standard empty-tomb apologetics — once 20:7 is read, that beat is familiar sermon territory, not surprising textual depth.

### One thread spine — mostly PASS, one wobble

Arc holds: missing body → ordered linens → John’s belief-without-seeing-the-Lord → Thomas → blessed faith for those who only *"have heard."*

No full thread-swap. The wobble: the hook’s theft frame is **Mary’s** report; the theological center is **John’s** moment. Mary vanishes after line 2. The thread survives, but the opening image and closing claim are not the same character’s journey — and the piece is titled *John 20:8* without the hook signaling that.
