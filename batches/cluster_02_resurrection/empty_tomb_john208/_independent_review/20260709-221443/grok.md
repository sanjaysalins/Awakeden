## Doctrinal soundness — mostly sound, one overclaim

**Orthodox claim overall.** Empty tomb → ordered grave-clothes → resurrection faith without seeing the risen body → 20:29 blessing on those who believe without seeing. That is mainstream evangelical reading, not contrarian clickbait.

**Problem: belief-object overclaim.**

After *"and he saw, and believed,"* the script asserts:

> *"John believed before he ever saw the risen Lord."*

John 20:8 does **not** name the object of belief. John 20:9 immediately qualifies the same visit (paraphrased next line as *"He did not yet know the scripture — that Christ must rise from the dead"*). The piece half-nods at 20:9, then states resurrection certainty as flat reportage. Standard harmonization is defensible in a sermon; as **narrative fact** it overreaches the verse.

**Problem: theft frame is inference upgraded to spine.**

Mary’s KJV is *"They have taken away the Lord"* — agent unspecified. The hook softens to *"Someone took the body"* (better than “thieves”), but the middle still asks:

> *"What **thief** wraps the face-cloth and lays it aside?"*

and the landing verdict is:

> *"the body was not **stolen**"*

John never says *thief* or *stolen* in the cited verses. The ordered-napkin apologetic is ancient and sound; pinning it as the piece’s hard proof is still inference, not text.

Not heresy. Not FAIL. Still a grounding soft spot.

---

## Clarity on first hearing — fails the stranger test in two places

**1. Belief contradiction on the ear.**

Sequence a zero-Bible listener hears:

1. *"and he saw, and believed."*
2. *"He did not yet know the scripture — that Christ must rise from the dead."*
3. *"John believed before he ever saw the risen Lord."*

Believed **what**, if he did not yet know that Christ must rise? The script assumes the listener already knows the answer. That is a logic gap, not a clever conceit that pays off.

**2. Thomas timeline is wrong.**

> *"About a week later, Thomas refused to believe until he saw. Jesus told him:"*

Thomas refuses when the others report (resurrection-day evening, John 20:24–25). Jesus appears **after eight days** (20:26) and speaks 20:29. The script collapses refusal and appearance into one false beat: “about a week later, Thomas refused… Jesus told him.” A careful listener who later reads John will feel misled.

**3. Seen / not-seen only resolves late.**

John *did* see (the linen). The needed distinction is *saw the grave-clothes, not the risen Lord*. The phrase *"before he ever saw the risen Lord"* is the right fix — but it arrives after the confusing 20:8/20:9 pair, and the landing still has to carry too much.

**Surprising about the text?** Partially. The napkin line (*"wrapped together in a place by itself"*) and the sparse *"he saw, and believed"* are real text-hooks. *"What thief wraps the face-cloth"* is classic empty-tomb apologetics — faithful, not fresh.

**Orthodox landing?** Yes. No novelty-of-truth problem.

## One thread spine — mostly holds

Spine: *body gone / theft suspicion → ordered napkin → John believes without seeing the risen Lord → Thomas needs sight → blessed are those who believe without seeing → you.*

That is one arc. Thomas is contrast, not a thread swap. Good.

Weak join: the hook centers Mary’s first thought; the piece’s real spine is John’s tomb-moment. The pivot works, but a stranger may not know why we left Mary so fast until the linen line lands.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Rebuild hook and close around this piece’s own non-portable image (ordered napkin + “he saw, and believed” / “have not seen, and yet have believed”) so neither open nor final sentence could serve a different episode.
2. Fix the first-hearing belief gap after “and he saw, and believed”: name what the linen showed (body gone, grave not rifled) before asserting resurrection faith; keep 20:9 as wonder, not a near-contradiction.
3. Correct Thomas’s timeline (refused when told; Jesus speaks ~eight days later) and keep “thief/stolen” as narrator inference from the napkin, not Mary’s stated thought.
