## 1. WHO IS THIS FOR (one named viewer; who stops, who finishes, who shares; who is NOT served)
Named viewer: Grace, 34, a churchgoer who knows Easter but never noticed the folded head-cloth.
- **Stops:** scrollers, on line 1 ("Someone took the body") - it sounds like a crime story.
- **Finishes:** Bible readers; the detective question in line 9 carries them to line 13.
- **Shares:** believers, for lines 19-20.
- **NOT served:** the total newcomer. The film never says Jesus was killed and buried, never says why hands matter (line 16), and Thomas arrives with no introduction (line 15).

## 2. MAIN TAKEAWAY (one sentence a viewer could say back; which line carries it; did the film make it clear?)
"I was not there, but Jesus blessed people like me who believe he rose without seeing." Lines 18-20 carry it. Clear for a believer. Less clear for others, because line 21 sends them back to "the linen" while line 18 praises not seeing. The film argues from evidence (line 9), then praises faith without evidence (line 18), and never joins the two.

## 3. GLOBAL AUDIENCE (who / what / why in my own words; where I got lost or misled, quote the line; words that fail in translation)
- **Who:** Mary, two friends of Jesus, later Thomas.
- **What:** the grave is empty, the head-cloth is neatly set apart, John believes; Thomas needs to see.
- **Why:** so I believe too.
- **Lost at line 2:** "Mary's first thought" - which Mary? Many will think his mother. The name only comes in line 3.
- **Lost at line 15:** "Thomas refused to believe until he saw" - who is he, and believe what? It is a jump to a second story.
- **Lost at line 21:** "at the linen" is not natural English and will not translate.
- **Words that fail:** "napkin" (line 8) means a table napkin in modern English, and only SEPULCHRE gets a decode card. "Risen" (lines 13, 21) is hard for ESL and is never once said as "alive again". "Wrapped together" (line 8) is unclear, though line 9 half-rescues it.

## 4. BIBLICALLY ACCURATE (claim by claim: RIGHT / OVERCLAIMED / UNSUPPORTED / WRONG, quote the line; say only what you can support)
- Line 4, John 20:2 - RIGHT, verbatim.
- Line 8, John 20:7 - RIGHT, verbatim.
- Line 11, John 20:8 - RIGHT, verbatim.
- Line 18, John 20:29 - RIGHT, verbatim.
- Lines 1-2, "Mary's first thought" - RIGHT as inference from 20:2.
- Line 3, "Peter and John" - mildly OVERCLAIMED. The text says "the other disciple, whom Jesus loved". John is tradition, not the verse.
- Line 9, "What thief wraps the face-cloth" - fair INFERENCE (an old argument), but the text draws no such conclusion.
- Line 10, "Then John stepped inside" - RIGHT (20:8), but Peter went in first (20:6) and has vanished.
- Lines 12-13, "Believed what? That his Lord was risen" - OVERCLAIMED. The verse does not say what he believed. Verse 9 follows at once, and some old readers (Augustine) take it that he believed Mary's report. It is the majority reading, stated here as fact.
- Line 14 - RIGHT in sense. KJV has "they knew not ... he must rise again", so this is a paraphrase from plural to singular.
- Lines 15-16 - RIGHT (20:25-27, "after eight days"; 20:29 "because thou hast seen me, thou hast believed").
- Lines 19-20, "Jesus was speaking about you" - fair INFERENCE (20:29-31), slightly OVERCLAIMED. The blessing is on those who believe, not on every viewer.

## 5. BEST VERSION POSSIBLE (what would the best explainer in the world do differently; the ONE highest-value change; score 0-10 against that bar)
The best explainer would run one line of thought: John believed from a cloth, Thomas needed the wounds, you have their witness (20:31). He would also say "alive again" once.

**ONE change:** rebuild line 15 so Thomas is introduced and tied to the same question. It fixes the newcomer's who/what and joins the two halves.

**Score: 6/10.**

## CURIOSITY GAP (opens at line 1, pays off at line 13; score 6/10; worth the wait?)
Line 9 sharpens the gap well. The payoff is told, not shown. Line 12 opens a second gap and line 13 closes it one second later. After line 14 no new gap is open, so lines 15-18 coast. Worth the wait for believers; mild for others.
