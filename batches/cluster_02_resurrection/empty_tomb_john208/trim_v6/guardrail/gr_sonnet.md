## 1. WHO IS THIS FOR
A churchgoing viewer, say a 30-year-old who half-remembers "doubting Thomas" and scrolls Shorts at lunch. She stops at line 1 ("Someone took the body"), finishes for the napkin puzzle (9, 12), and shares line 19-20 ("Jesus was speaking about you"). Not served: a total newcomer (Peter, John, Thomas, "the Lord" are never introduced) and a Bible reader who wants a careful reading of John 20:8.

## 2. MAIN TAKEAWAY
"You can believe in the risen Jesus without having seen him." Carried by lines 18-20. The film makes this clear, but line 21 muddies it: it says to believe what John believed, and John saw the linen (20:8). That sets John, who saw, against "they that have not seen" (20:29).

## 3. GLOBAL AUDIENCE
In my words: Mary thought someone stole Jesus' body; two men ran and found only cloths; one believed; Thomas doubted until he saw; Jesus blessed those who believe without seeing. Where I got lost: line 13 ("That his Lord was risen") and line 14 ("did not yet know the scripture") seem to contradict each other. Words that fail in translation: "napkin" (line 8, modern English means a dinner napkin), "sepulchre" (decoded on card, good), "face-cloth" (line 9) vs "napkin" (line 8), "Christ" (line 14, never explained), "Lord."

## 4. BIBLICALLY ACCURATE
- Line 1-2: RIGHT as Mary's thought (20:2, "They have taken away the Lord"). Minor: v.1 says only that she saw the stone gone, not an empty tomb.
- Line 3: "stone rolled away" is mild OVERCLAIM; John 20:1 says "taken away." "Peter and John": UNSUPPORTED by this text. It says "the other disciple, whom Jesus loved" (20:2). John is the tradition, not the verse.
- Line 4: RIGHT, verbatim (20:2).
- Line 5: "They ran." RIGHT (20:4).
- Line 6: "The body was gone." RIGHT by implication; v.5-7 show only cloths.
- Line 7-8: RIGHT (20:5-7).
- Line 9: "What thief wraps the face-cloth and lays it aside?" INFERENCE, not text. John never makes this argument, and "wrapped together" (20:7) is not clearly "folded." Fine as a question, but it is the film's own reasoning.
- Line 10: "Then John stepped inside." Partly RIGHT, but it skips that Peter went in first (20:6) and John followed (20:8).
- Line 11: RIGHT (20:8).
- Line 12-13: "Believed what? That his Lord was risen." OVERCLAIMED. The verse never says what he believed, and 20:9 follows at once: "as yet they knew not the scripture, that he must rise." Many read it as dawning belief, others as believing Mary's report. The film states a contested reading as fact.
- Line 14: RIGHT (20:9). "He" for "they" narrows it.
- Line 15: RIGHT (20:25), though Thomas also demanded to touch.
- Line 16: "Eight days later" RIGHT (20:26). "Thomas saw, and believed": RIGHT in effect (20:29); the text never says he touched.
- Line 18: RIGHT, verbatim (20:29).
- Line 19-20: "Jesus was speaking about you." INFERENCE, sound (they that have not seen = later believers), but label it as application.
- Line 21: "Believe what John believed at the linen." OVERCLAIMED, as line 13.

## 5. BEST VERSION POSSIBLE
The best explainer would anchor the ending on a verse that is actually in the text: Thomas's "My Lord and my God" (20:28), and drop the John-believed-resurrection claim. It would also introduce the cast in one breath. The ONE highest-value change: replace lines 13 and 21 so the film does not claim to know what John believed. Score: 7/10.

## CURIOSITY GAP
Opens at line 9 and again at line 12; "pays off" at line 13, but with the weakest claim in the film. Score 6/10. Worth the wait for feeling; not worth it for the text.

## THREE SMALLEST FIXES
1. Line 13: "John does not say. But Jesus will soon say it for him." (Keep line 14 as is.)
2. Line 21: "Say what Thomas said: My Lord and my God."
3. Line 3 (and its caption): "ran to Peter and the disciple Jesus loved" in place of "Peter and John"; also change "napkin" to "head-cloth" in line 8 and caption.
