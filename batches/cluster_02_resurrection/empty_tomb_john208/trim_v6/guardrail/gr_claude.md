## 1. WHO IS THIS FOR
**Named viewer:** Priya, 34. She grew up near a church, never reads the Bible, and scrolls Shorts at night.
- **Who stops:** anyone who hears line 1 "Someone took the body". It sounds like a crime story.
- **Who finishes:** church people. Line 9 "What thief wraps the face-cloth…" pays off something they half-know.
- **Who shares:** small-group leaders in Easter week.
- **Not served:** a sceptic who wants the "stolen body" theory answered properly. Line 9 asks the question but does no real work on it.

## 2. MAIN TAKEAWAY
"John believed from the folded cloth before he saw Jesus, and Jesus blesses people like me who believe without seeing."
- It is carried by lines 18–21.
- It is clear, but rushed. The real hinge is that John believed *without seeing the risen Jesus*, and line 21 says it in about 4 seconds. Line 21 should state it outright.

## 3. GLOBAL AUDIENCE
**Who / what / why:** A woman finds Jesus' tomb open. Two friends run there and find the grave cloths left tidy. One of them believes. Later, Thomas believes because he sees Jesus. Jesus says people who believe without seeing are blessed. That means you.

**Where a newcomer gets lost:**
- Line 3, "Peter and John": who are they? Nothing says they are Jesus' followers.
- Line 7 "linen" and line 8 "napkin" fail in translation. To a second-language viewer a napkin is something for dinner. The 16s caption ("cloth from his head") saves it, but the spoken KJV line works against it.
- Line 13 and line 14 seem to contradict each other: he believed, yet he didn't know. Nothing explains that this is the point.
- The SEPULCHRE = TOMB card works well.

## 4. BIBLICALLY ACCURATE
- **L1–2** "Someone took the body… Mary's first thought": **RIGHT** as a paraphrase of v2. "First" is a small inference.
- **L3** "found the stone rolled away": **OVERCLAIMED, minor.** John 20:1 says "taken away"; "rolled away" is Mark 16:4. The picture of the tomb at sunrise also clashes with "when it was yet dark" (v1).
- **L3/L10** "John": **UNSUPPORTED by this text.** v2 says "the other disciple, whom Jesus loved". Calling him John is church tradition. Say so, or call him "the disciple Jesus loved".
- **L4, L8, L11, L18** (the KJV quotes): **RIGHT.**
- **L5** "They ran": **RIGHT** (v4).
- **L6–7** "The body was gone. Only the linen…": **RIGHT** (v5–7).
- **Order of events:** v6 has *Peter* go in first and see the napkin. The film skips this, so viewers will assume John saw it first. That is not wrong, but it misleads.
- **L9** "What thief wraps the face-cloth…": **INFERENCE, fair** from v7. Mary said "they", not "thief", but the question is a reasonable one.
- **L12–13** "Believed what? That his Lord was risen": **OVERCLAIMED.** v8 does not say *what* he believed. v9 ("as yet they knew not the scripture") is why some readers, Augustine among them, take it as believing Mary's report. Most read it as belief in the resurrection, but that is an inference presented as text.
- **L14** "He did not yet know the scripture": **SLIGHTLY OFF.** v9 says "*they* knew not". The film narrows it to one man.
- **L15** "refused to believe until he saw": **UNDERSTATED.** v25 asks to *touch*: "put my finger… thrust my hand".
- **L16** "Eight days later… showed him his hands… Thomas saw, and believed": **RIGHT** (v26–27, v29). The film rightly does not claim he touched. But it leaves out v28, "My Lord and my God", the climax of the passage. Any Bible reader will notice.
- **L19–20** "Jesus was speaking about you": **INFERENCE, sound** from v29.
- **L21**: **INFERENCE.** It rests on the overclaim in L13.

**Would I send it to my church group?** Yes, after fixing L13 and adding v28.

## 5. BEST VERSION POSSIBLE
The best explainer would make the twist visible: **the disciple Jesus loved is the first person in John who believes *without seeing Jesus*. He is the model for the blessing in v29.** Show John at the cloth and Thomas at the hands side by side, then turn to camera.

**ONE change:** make L21 say it plainly: "John believed before he saw Jesus. That's you."

**Score: 7/10.**

## CURIOSITY GAP
- Opens at L1. It sharpens at L9 and L12.
- It pays off at L13, and again at L20–21.
- **Score: 7/10.** It is worth the wait, but the L13 payoff is stated more firmly than the text allows.

## THREE SMALLEST FIXES
- **L13:** "John doesn't say. But he saw no robber had done this, and he believed."
- **L16:** "Eight days later Jesus showed him his hands. Thomas said: 'My Lord and my God.'"
- **L21:** "The disciple Jesus loved believed before he saw Jesus. So can you. The Lord is risen."
