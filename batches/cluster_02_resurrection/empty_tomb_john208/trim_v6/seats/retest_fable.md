# Retest seat: Fable (blind, worked alone)

## NEWCOMER
- **Who:** Mary, then Peter and John, then Thomas. All followers of Jesus, though the film never says so.
- **What:** Jesus' tomb is empty. The grave cloths are left behind, the face-cloth neatly wrapped. John sees and believes. Thomas believes only after seeing Jesus' hands.
- **Why:** A thief would not stop to fold a cloth, so Jesus rose. You did not see it, and Jesus blesses those who believe without seeing.
- **Line they would not follow:** "He did not yet know the scripture — that Christ must rise from the dead."
  - It comes straight after "Believed what? That his Lord was risen", so it sounds like a contradiction: he believed it, but did not know it?
  - "The scripture" and "Christ" are both unexplained. A newcomer may not know Christ = Jesus.
- **Smaller trips:**
  - "sepulchre" and "we know not where they have laid him" are hard for ESL. The picture must carry it.
  - "napkin" means a table napkin today. The gloss before it ("the cloth that had covered his head") saves it.
  - "Thomas refused to believe" has no introduction. Who is Thomas? Believe what? One word would fix it ("Thomas, another follower, ...").

## BIBLE READER
- **What they get:**
  - The napkin detail used as an argument ("What thief wraps the face-cloth?"). [INFERENCE] on John 20:7. Old and sound, though not rare.
  - The "saw, and believed" echo: John in 20:8, Thomas in 20:29, then "have not seen, and yet have believed". [TEXT] A nice frame, and the best thing in the piece.
- **Errors against John 20:** none that I can find. Both KJV quotes (20:2, 20:7) and Jesus' line (20:29) are word-true.
- **Simplifications, not errors:**
  - "John" — the text says "the other disciple, whom Jesus loved" (20:2). Naming him John is tradition.
  - Peter went in first (20:6). The film skips him. Fine.
  - "Mary's first thought at the empty tomb" — 20:1 only says she saw the stone taken away. Fine.
  - "He did not yet know the scripture" — 20:9 says "they". Fine.
- **One claim stated harder than the text:** "Believed what? That his Lord was risen." [INFERENCE] 20:8 does not say what he believed. This is the majority reading, but a minority (Augustine) reads it as believing Mary's report that the body was gone, because of 20:9. A careful reader may notice. Not an error.
- **Slight overreach:** "Jesus was speaking about you." The blessing is on those who "have believed". For a viewer who does not yet believe it is an invitation, not a description. The last line ("Believe what John believed") repairs this.
- **Missing:** Thomas' "My Lord and my God" (20:28). A Bible reader will feel the gap, but it is a fair cut at 64 seconds.

## CURIOSITY — 7/10
- **Opens:** yes. "Someone took the body" is a mystery in the first 2 seconds.
- **Sharpens:** "What thief wraps the face-cloth and lays it aside?" is the real hook, but it comes about a third of the way in.
- **Pays off:** yes. "That his Lord was risen", then again in the last line.
- **Why not higher:** the payoff lands mid-film. The Thomas part is then a second, smaller story rather than a rising one.

## ENDING — yes
- "Believe what John believed — the Lord is risen" lands on Jesus, closes the loop back to the tomb, and is plain English.
- "You were not at the tomb. Jesus was speaking about you." is a strong turn to the viewer just before it.
- It is an invitation, with no pressure.

## OVERALL — 7.5/10
- **The ONE weakest line:** "He did not yet know the scripture — that Christ must rise from the dead."
- It blurs the win the line before it just made, and confuses the newcomer.
- **Fix idea:** make the contrast plain, e.g. "No one had explained it to him yet. He saw the cloths — and believed." Or cut it.

*Simulated viewers are not real viewers. Scores are roughly ±1.*
