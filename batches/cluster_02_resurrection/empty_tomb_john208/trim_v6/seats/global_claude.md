# Seat answer: Version X vs Version Y

## VERSION X

**NEWCOMER**
- **Who:** Mary, Peter, John, Thomas, and Jesus.
- **What:** The tomb is empty. The head cloth is folded and set aside. John believes, and later Thomas sees Jesus.
- **Why it matters:** You can believe without seeing, and Jesus means you.
- **Line they would not follow:** "He did not yet know the scripture — that Christ must rise from the dead." What scripture? Who is "Christ"? "Napkin" may also suggest a table napkin, but the line just before it says "cloth that had covered his head", which saves it.

**BIBLE READER**
- **What they get:** The folded cloth as evidence against a thief (20:7). John believes before he knows the scripture (20:8-9). The ending ties back to 20:29. That is a nice arc.
- **True to John 20?**
  - "John": the text says "the other disciple, whom Jesus loved". Calling him John is tradition, not the text, but readers accept it.
  - 20:9 says "**they** knew not" and "**he** must rise again". The narration says "He did not yet know" and "Christ must rise". This is a small slip in a line that sounds like a quote.
  - "Believed what? That his Lord was risen." This is a reasonable reading [INFERENCE], but some read it as believing Mary's report.

**CURIOSITY: 8/10.** "Someone took the body" opens a question at once. "What thief wraps the face-cloth?" answers it.

**ENDING: yes.** "Jesus was speaking about you" ties Jesus' words to the viewer. It is short and feels finished.

## VERSION Y

**NEWCOMER**
- **Who, what, why:** The same as X.
- **Line they would not follow:** "ran to Peter and John: They ran." The colon leads into nothing. Mary's words are gone, so the opening "Someone took the body" loses its Bible backing. The ending ("Believe what John believed at the linen") also asks them to remember a detail from 40 seconds earlier.

**BIBLE READER**
- **What they get:** "The gospel says only this" is an honest signal, which is good.
- **True to John 20?**
  - It has the same 20:9 slip as X.
  - The last line turns the debated "believed what?" into an order: "Believe what John believed... the Lord is risen." That states more than the text does.

**CURIOSITY: 6/10.** The setup is weaker because Mary's quote is cut.

**ENDING: half.** It does land on "the Lord is risen", but it feels tacked on and preachy. The real ending is already "Jesus was speaking about you", so Y ends twice.

## PICK: X

**My one cut:** delete the whole line "He did not yet know the scripture — that Christ must rise from the dead."
- It is the line newcomers would trip on.
- It removes the misquote of 20:9 ("he" for "they", "Christ" for "he").
- "That his Lord was risen. The linen was enough." still flows and keeps the point.
- It saves about 4 seconds.
