# Retest seat: Sonnet

**NEWCOMER**
- Who: Mary, Peter, John, Thomas, and Jesus (who died and is alive again).
- What: A tomb was found empty. Mary thought a thief took the body. John went in, saw the folded face-cloth, and believed Jesus was alive. Thomas doubted until he saw Jesus' hands.
- Why: To say Jesus is risen, and that you can believe without seeing.
- Lines they would not follow:
  - "Believed what? That his Lord was risen." It jumps. The cloth clue ("what thief...") never says outright that the neat cloth shows no thief.
  - "He did not yet know the scripture." What scripture?
  - "Sepulchre", "napkin", "linen clothes" are old words. Also "Believe what John believed" is a little circular.

**BIBLE READER**
- Gets: the napkin detail (20:7), the "what thief" question, John's belief (20:8), and Thomas leading to 20:29 with "you" as the target. The ordering is faithful.
- Errors:
  - None serious.
  - Possible nit: "ran to Peter and John" compresses 20:2 correctly.
  - "Eight days later" is correct (20:26).
  - The line "That his Lord was risen" is an inference. John 20:9 says they did not yet know the scripture, which reads as a tension. That is a simplification, not an error.
  - The 20:8 "believed" is arguably less than full understanding. The script handles this partly.

**CURIOSITY:** 7/10. "Someone took the body" opens it right away. The cloth question pays it off. "Believed what?" opens a second small gap that is answered quickly.

**ENDING:** Yes. "The Lord is risen" lands on Jesus and sounds like a close. It is a bit of a statement rather than a punch, but it works.

**OVERALL:** 7/10.

**Weakest line:** "He did not yet know the scripture — that Christ must rise from the dead." It stalls the pace and puzzles a newcomer. It also sits oddly next to "believed".
