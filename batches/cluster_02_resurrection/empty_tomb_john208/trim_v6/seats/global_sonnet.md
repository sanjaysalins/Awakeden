# Seat: global_sonnet

## VERSION X
- NEWCOMER: Who: Jesus' friend Mary, and his followers John and Thomas. What: Jesus' tomb is empty, only the cloths are left, John believes, Thomas doubts then believes. Why it matters: Jesus is alive, and people who believe without seeing are blessed (that means you). Would not follow: "He did not yet know the scripture" (what scripture?), "sepulchre", "napkin", "linen clothes". "Believed what? That his Lord was risen" then "did not yet know" feels contradictory.
- BIBLE READER: Gets the Mary quote (20:2), the napkin detail (20:7), the "saw, and believed" (20:8), the scripture note (20:9), Thomas and the beatitude (20:27-29). All true to John 20. Small stretches: "John stepped inside" skips that Peter went in first (20:6); "Thomas saw, and believed" is a tidy paraphrase (he is not told to have touched; 20:29 says "hast seen"). "The linen was enough" is inference, not text. No real error.
- CURIOSITY: 7/10. "Someone took the body" opens it. "What thief wraps the face-cloth?" is a good hook and is paid off by John believing. Small leak: "Believed what?" answers itself at once.
- ENDING: Yes. "You were not at the tomb. Jesus was speaking about you." Lands on Jesus' own words turned to the viewer, short, feels final.

## VERSION Y
- NEWCOMER: Same story. Extra problem: "ran to Peter and John: They ran." has a colon with nothing after it. It reads broken. "The gospel says only this:" is a clear signpost. Last line "Believe what John believed at the linen" is hard (what linen? what did he believe?).
- BIBLE READER: Loses the Mary quote (20:2), the grief and the "we know not" of the story. Otherwise the same and true. Final line "Believe what John believed" is faithful to 20:8 but a reader will say it is a command the text does not make; it is the preacher's line, not Jesus' line.
- CURIOSITY: 6/10. Same hook, but the broken colon in the first seconds hurts the open, and the extra closing instruction dilutes the payoff.
- ENDING: Partly. It ends on "the Lord is risen", which is about Jesus, but after the strong line it feels like a tacked-on sermon. The story already ended on "about you". Weaker landing.

## PICK: X

## ONE cut-only fix (to X)
Delete: "He did not yet know the scripture — that Christ must rise from the dead."
Why: for a newcomer it contradicts "believed" and adds an unexplained "scripture". "The linen was enough" still works without it.
