# Seat: global_fable (blind, worked alone)

## VERSION X

**NEWCOMER**
- Who: Mary, then John, then Thomas, at the empty tomb of Jesus.
- What: The body is gone. The head cloth is wrapped up neatly. John sees it and believes Jesus is alive. Thomas believes only after he sees Jesus.
- Why: Jesus blesses people who believe without seeing. That means the viewer.
- Would not follow: "They have taken away the Lord out of the sepulchre, and we know not where they have laid him." "Sepulchre" is never explained, and "we know not" / "laid him" are old English. It arrives about 8 seconds in, the worst place to lose someone. "Napkin" is fine, because the narrator explains it first.

**BIBLE READER**
- Gets: the wrapped napkin as a clue against theft (20:7), the link of "saw, and believed" (20:8) to Thomas (20:29), and the honest note that they did not yet know the scripture (20:9).
- Not true to John 20:
  - "Believed what? That his Lord was risen." is stated as fact. The text says only "he saw, and believed" (20:8). It is a fair reading, but an inference.
  - "John" is the common reading. The text says "the other disciple, whom Jesus loved" (20:2).
  - "He did not yet know": the text says "they knew not" (20:9). Small.
  - "The linen was enough" is inference.
  - "Thomas refused to believe until he saw": he also asked to touch (20:25). A simplification, not an error.
  - "My Lord and my God" (20:28) is missing. A reader will feel that.

**CURIOSITY: 7/10.** "Someone took the body" opens at once, and Mary's own words back it up. Payoff comes at "What thief wraps the face-cloth?" and "risen". The old-English quote slows the open.

**ENDING: yes.** "Jesus was speaking about you." Jesus is the subject, the viewer is named, and it is short and final. It does not say what to believe, so a newcomer may be left a little open.

## VERSION Y

**NEWCOMER**
- Who: same people. Mary, John, Thomas, Jesus.
- What: same story, with less old English at the start.
- Why: same, plus a plain call at the end: believe that the Lord is risen.
- Would not follow:
  - "ran to Peter and John: They ran." The colon promises words from Mary and none come. It sounds like a mistake, and "They" is unclear for a second.
  - "Believe what John believed at the linen". "At the linen" is an odd phrase for a second-language ear.

**BIBLE READER**
- Gets: the same as X. "The gospel says only this:" is a real gain. It tells the viewer honestly that the text is brief, just before the narrator's own reading.
- Not true to John 20: the same inferences as X ("That his Lord was risen", "John", "the linen was enough"). The extra last line repeats the inference as a command ("what John believed").
- Loss: Mary's own words (20:2) are gone, so the hook "Someone took the body" is now only the narrator's claim. It is still true to 20:2.

**CURIOSITY: 7/10.** The open is faster and cleaner for a newcomer. The payoff is the same, and the last line says it out loud. It loses a point for the broken colon join and for dropping Mary's proof of the hook.

**ENDING: yes.** The last words are "the Lord is risen". It lands on Jesus and tells the newcomer exactly what to believe. It is a second ending after "Jesus was speaking about you", so it is a little long, and "at the linen" trips.

## PICK: Y

Why: the newcomer does not hit "sepulchre" at second 8, "The gospel says only this" is more honest, and the last words name the risen Lord.

**The ONE cut-only fix:** delete "at the linen" from the last line.
- Result: "Believe what John believed — the Lord is risen."

Not a word change, but needed: the colon after "Peter and John" must become a full stop, or it reads as a dropped quote.

Simulated viewers, not real ones. My confidence in Y over X is about 60%, give or take 15. The two are close.
