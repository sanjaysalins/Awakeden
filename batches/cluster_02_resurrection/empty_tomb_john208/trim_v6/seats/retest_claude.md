**One seat, blind. John 20:1-10 and 20:24-29 checked against the KJV.**

## NEWCOMER (no Bible, English is a second language)
- **Who:** Mary, Peter, John, Thomas, Jesus. But no one says who Peter, John or Thomas are.
- **What:** The body is gone, the cloths are left neat, so someone believes Jesus came back to life.
- **Why:** A robber would not fold a cloth, so maybe he rose. That part is clear.
- **Lost on:**
  - "the napkin, that was about his head… wrapped together in a place by itself." It is long, and "napkin" and "linen" are odd words.
  - "He did not yet know the scripture, that Christ must rise." Which scripture? Who is "Christ"?
  - "Eight days later," after what? Thomas comes in with no setup. Why did he doubt? Who did he not believe?

## BIBLE READER
- **They get:** the face-cloth question ("what thief folds it?"). It's a good hook, but it's a well-known sermon point, not a fresh one.
- **Errors (not just simpler wording):**
  1. **"John."** The text never names him. It says "the other disciple, whom Jesus loved" (20:2, 8). It's a safe tradition, but the film says it as fact.
  2. **"Believed what? That his Lord was risen."** The film states this as certain. Verse 9 ("as yet they knew not the scripture") makes some readers think he only believed Mary's report. Call it a reading, not a fact.
  3. **"Believe what John believed."** Jesus blesses people who have *not seen* (20:29), but 20:8 says John "saw, and believed." This works only if you say he saw cloths, not Jesus. The film never says that.
- **Missing:** Thomas saying "My Lord and my God" (20:28), the peak of the chapter. A Bible reader will feel it's gone.

## CURIOSITY: **6/10**
- "Someone took the body" opens a question at once. Strong.
- The folded cloth pays it off by about the middle.
- Then the jump to Thomas is a second story, and the first question goes slack.

## ENDING: **Partly**
- The real end is Jesus' line, "blessed are they that have not seen." That lands well, and it points at the viewer.
- But the narrator speaks after him, so the last voice is not Jesus.
- "Believe what John believed" sends you back to John, not to Jesus.
- Better: end on Jesus' line, or on "My Lord and my God."

## OVERALL: **6/10**

**Weakest line:** "He did not yet know the scripture — that Christ must rise from the dead." A newcomer can't follow it, it slows the climb, and it raises "which scripture?" with no answer.

**My pick:** Cut that line. Use the time to set up Thomas in one short line ("Thomas wasn't there. He said: unless I see…"). Then end on Jesus' own words.
