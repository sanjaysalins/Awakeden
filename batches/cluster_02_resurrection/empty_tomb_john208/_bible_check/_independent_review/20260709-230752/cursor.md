## Independent adversarial review — `empty_tomb_john208` fact sheet

Overall: the core Johannine spine (garden tomb, stoop-and-look, great stone moved, linen + separate napkin, Mary pre-dawn, Peter/John run sequence) is mostly well anchored. The problems are **specified-bucket inflation** (archaeology and dimensions dressed as Scripture), **one misapplied citation**, and **coverage gaps** that matter for this piece’s stills.

---

### CITATION TRUTH — over-reach and misapplication

**1. Mark 16:5 is misapplied for bench geometry**

> *"The interior burial place is a rock SHELF/BENCH with a head-end and a foot-end… angels later sat 'the one at the head, and the other at the feet' (John 20:12); 'sitting on the right side' (Mark 16:5)."*

- **John 20:12** supports head/foot placement of the angel pair.
- **Mark 16:5** does not: *"a young man sitting on the right side"* — one figure, one side, no head/foot bench layout. Citing it for shelf/bench shape is a **wrong citation**. For a John 20 piece, leaning on a synoptic angel beat that **contradicts John’s two-angel head/feet tableau** is especially risky.

**2. “Low entrance” is supported; dimensions and shape are not**

Citations (John 20:5, 20:11, Luke 24:12) prove **stooping to look in**, not:

- *"small crawl-height square opening (~1 m)"*
- *"NEVER a tall standing-height doorway"*

Stooping ≠ crawl-height; Peter *"went into the sepulchre"* (John 20:6). A ~1 m crawl opening is **archaeological guesswork**, not KJV text.

**3. Stone shape, track, and resting position are over-specified**

Matthew 27:60 / Mark 16:3–4 / John 20:1 support a **great stone rolled to/away from the door**. They do **not** support:

- *"massive disc/round stone"*
- *"rolled aside in its channel"*
- *"RESTING OUTSIDE beside the opening"*

“Rolled” implies rollability; **disc, groove/track, and exact resting place** belong in **constrained** (or free), not under **[SPECIFIED] Image:**.

**4. Bench/arcosolium language exceeds the verses**

John 20:5 proves linen visible when looking in; John 20:12 proves **head and feet** as locus of the body. None of the cited verses say:

- *"rock SHELF/BENCH"*
- *"waist-low hewn bench/arcosolium"*
- *"never a sunken pit, trough, coffin or sarcophagus"*

*"Where the body of Jesus had lain"* (20:12) is the biblical claim; **kokh/bench typology is archaeology** — should not be **[SPECIFIED]**.

**5. Grave-clothes image directives go beyond the text**

John 19:39–40 / 20:5 support plural linen, spices at burial, and linen **lying**. They do **not** require:

- *"layered strips/folds with residual bulk from the spices"*
- *"visibly EMPTY (no head, no limbs, no body silhouette)"*

Empty/collapsed is a fair **inference** for resurrection art, but not a verbatim specified visual mandate. The **100 pound** weight (19:39) is biblical at burial; it is **not** stated as visible on the abandoned linens in 20:5–7.

**6. Risen Christ fact under-cites and over-claims**

> *"The risen Jesus later SHOWS the wounds in his hands (John 20:20, 20:27); he is alive, whole, serene."*

- **John 20:20**: *"he shewed unto them his hands **and his side**"*
- **John 20:27**: hands **and side** (Thomas)

Citing these verses for **hands only** is a **citation omission**. The side wound is explicit in both verses.

*"Whole, serene"* is **not KJV** — temperament/state beyond “risen/alive” is artistic licence, not **[SPECIFIED]**.

**7. Mary beat — mostly good; one inference**

John 20:1–2 solidly supports early, dark, stone moved, runs to Peter and the beloved disciple.

*"The tomb is DARK — no light glowing from inside"* is **not stated** for Mary’s beat (20:1–2 doesn’t say she looked inside). Pre-dawn **outside** darkness is specified; interior lighting is inference.

### COVERAGE — missing guards this piece needs

1. **Peter/John tomb visit (John 20:3–10): no angel, no risen Lord visible** — parallel to the Mary guard; critical for `john_believes_inside`, `linen_left_lying`, `napkin_wrapped_apart`.
2. **John 20:5 sequence: John stoops and looks but does not enter first** — easy to paint him inside before Peter.
3. **Side wound** on risen Christ (20:20, 20:27) — hands-only guard is incomplete.
4. **"First day of the week"** (20:1) — checkable time fact, absent.
5. **Thomas scene timing** — 20:27 is *"after eight days"* (20:26); if `jesus_shows_thomas` / `risen_christ_wounds` depicts 20:27, the sheet should guard **eight days later, Thomas present, doors shut** vs the first-day tomb scenes.
6. **No linen/napkin on disciple arrival** — body already gone (20:2 report, 20:6–7 observation); worth a specified negative against showing a corpse.
