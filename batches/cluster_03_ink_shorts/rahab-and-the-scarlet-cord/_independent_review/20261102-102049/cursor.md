## Independent adversarial review — Rahab / scarlet cord narration

### Doctrinal soundness
- **Mostly sound, one soft spot.** The thread (Rahab, sign-cord, Jericho fall, faith in Hebrews, Rachab in Matthew’s genealogy, typological read) is standard evangelical and lands on Christ by faith, not works. **“It was her faith, not her past”** fits Hebrews 11 without works-righteousness.
- **Typology is hedged but still the heaviest claim.** **“Christians see a picture in that cord: the blood of Jesus, which covers all who trust Him”** is not *in* Joshua; it’s a long-used sermon link (scarlet → blood). **“Christians see a picture”** keeps it honest, but adversarially this is the least *text-surprising* move in the piece—many listeners will feel they’ve heard this exact bridge before.
- **Cause vs faith tension.** You say she hid spies **“because she believed in Him”**, then later **“because she trusted what they said.”** Hebrews anchors **faith**; the spy **promise** is the historical mechanism. A careful listener can hear “saved for taking men at their word” *before* **“By faith the harlot Rahab…”** reframes it—not heresy, but the middle briefly undercuts the doctrinal spine you want Hebrews to carry.

### Clarity on first hearing
- **Hebrews line is a first-hear trap.** **“perished not with them that believed not”** is grammatically correct KJV but a **double negative + “believed not”** stack. A listener with zero Bible background often parses this as mush (“believed not” sounds like *she* didn’t believe) unless they already know Jericho’s faith vs unbelief. You don’t paraphrase after it—you only add **“It was her faith, not her past,”** which helps *morally* but not *syntactically*.
- **Hook omits place and stakes.** **“One red cord kept a woman alive when a city fell”** is vivid but **which city, why a cord, who she is** arrive late. Follow-up **“people knew her for her sin”** is euphemistic until **“the harlot Rahab”** lands—some will not connect *sin* → *harlot* on one pass.
- **March-around is compressed.** **“God’s army marched around her city, and on the seventh day…”** skips trumpets/shouts setup; still followable, but Jericho’s weirdness (why march?) is unexplained—acceptable for 60s, not confusing logic, just thin context.

### Grace-anchored conviction
- **Pass.** **“not her past”**, faith-not-merit framing, no fear sells, no “or you’ll die like Jericho” pressure, no gain/loss manipulation. Conviction is invitation-shaped (**“Hang your hope…”**), not coercion.

### Freshness = faithful depth
- **Mixed.** Stronger fresh beats: **Matthew / Rachab in Jesus’ line**, **“Rahab could not hold up a wall”** (concrete incapacity vs God’s deliverance). Weaker: **scarlet cord = blood of Jesus**—orthodox but **high-frequency** in this story’s genre; depth here is mostly *packaging*, not a overlooked textual detail (e.g., sign in the window, oath to spies, Gentile in the lineage).

### One thread spine (hook → middle → CTA)
- **Pass.** Scarlet cord → promise/sign → fall/salvation → faith → genealogy → typology → **hang/trust parallel**. No hard thread swap; Jesus enters via Matthew and the typology sentence, not a random pivot.
