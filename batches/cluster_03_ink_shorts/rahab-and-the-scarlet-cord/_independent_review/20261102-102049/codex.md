# Independent review — codex (OK, 68s)

**Hook: 8/10.** “One red cord kept a woman alive when a city fell” creates immediate visual tension and curiosity. But it assigns the saving power to the cord; the narration later has to correct that impression.

**Landing: 7/10.** “Hang your hope on Jesus the same way” grows from this piece’s cord imagery, so it is not a stock closer. But “the same way” carries an inadequately explained comparison between displaying a rescue signal and trusting Christ.

1. **The blood connection carries more weight than the text establishes.** “Christians see a picture in that cord: the blood of Jesus” identifies this as interpretation, which matters. Nevertheless, neither Joshua nor the cited New Testament passages identifies the scarlet cord as Christ’s blood. The doctrine of Christ’s saving blood is orthodox; this particular correspondence is an illustrative reading, not an explicit biblical teaching. Making it the bridge to the CTA conflicts with your requirement to avoid contested readings as the piece’s foundation. [Relevant passages](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Joshua+2%3B+6:16-25%3B+Matthew+1:5%3B+James+2:25%3B+Hebrews+11:31&version=KJV)

2. **“She only hung a cord” distorts the account.** She also sheltered the spies, helped them escape, and was instructed to gather her household. Hebrews specifically connects her faith with receiving the spies. These actions did not purchase salvation, but erasing them makes faith sound passive and contradicts your own earlier account of her hiding them. [Joshua 2; Hebrews 11:31](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Joshua+2%3BHebrews+11%3A31&version=KJV)

3. **The object of trust becomes blurred.** “Because she believed in Him” becomes “because she trusted what they said,” then becomes trusting Jesus “the same way.” Those connections need explanation. Otherwise, a newcomer can hear that confidence in a promise or performance of a symbolic act saves.

4. **The spine takes a genealogical detour.** “Matthew even names her in the family line of Jesus” introduces belonging and an overturned reputation, then immediately abandons that development for blood symbolism. It adds another destination without strengthening the cord-and-promise argument.

5. **First-hearing clarity is uneven.** “God’s spies” and “God’s army” leave Israel unidentified; Jericho is never named. “Booz of Rachab” supplies two unfamiliar names without explaining that Rachab is Rahab.

**KJV check:** All four quoted excerpts preserve the exact KJV words. No altered word found. [KJV comparison](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Joshua+2%3B+6:16-25%3B+Matthew+1:5%3B+James+2:25%3B+Hebrews+11:31&version=KJV)

**Grace framing:** There is no explicit threat or prosperity appeal. The concern is the repeated cord-action emphasis, which can obscure the mercy and faith the narration intends to convey.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Ground the Christ connection in explicit biblical teaching rather than making scarlet-cord symbolism carry it.
2. Remove “only” and distinguish the rescue sign from the saving source.
3. Clarify the object of faith and build the closing invitation from that clarified connection.
