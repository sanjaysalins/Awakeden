**Doctrinal Soundness:** 
Sound and orthodox. The typological connection between Rahab's scarlet cord and the blood of Christ is a historic, faithful evangelical reading. The emphasis on faith over her past is biblically accurate.

**KJV Verbatim:** 
All four quotes check out perfectly against the KJV text (Joshua 2:18, Joshua 6:20, Hebrews 11:31, Matthew 1:5). No altered words. 

**Clarity on First Hearing:** 
The narrative is easy to follow initially, but the back half turns into a rapid-fire list of theological facts. Hitting a listener with a Hebrews quote, then a Matthew genealogy quote ("Salmon begat Booz of Rachab"), and then an explicit theological explanation ("Christians see a picture...") derails the story's momentum. The Matthew quote in particular introduces confusing, archaic names that add friction for a non-biblically literate listener.

Strong. "Rahab could not hold up a wall. She only hung a cord... and trusted" is a beautiful, grace-anchored framing of faith versus works/strength.

The insight that she couldn't hold up the wall is fresh. However, the phrase "Christians see a picture in that cord" is incredibly didactic and textbook. It *tells* the listener the metaphor rather than letting the narrative reveal it organically.

**One Thread Spine:** 
The thread of the "cord" and "hanging/trusting" is present, but it gets temporarily lost in the middle when the script detours into her genealogical status in Matthew.

"One red cord kept a woman alive when a city fell." It is intriguing and avoids template traps, but it feels a bit passive and detached. It lacks the visceral, immediate danger of the impending slaughter to truly demand a stranger's attention.

**SCORE THE LANDING: 8** 
"Hang your hope on Jesus the same way." This is an earned, non-stock closer. The verb "hang" perfectly mirrors the physical act of hanging the cord in the window. It lands well.
