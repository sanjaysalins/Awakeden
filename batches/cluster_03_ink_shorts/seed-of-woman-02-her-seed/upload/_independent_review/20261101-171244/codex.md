# Independent review — codex (OK, 117s)

The main problem is that the kit presents an interpretive connection as explicit biblical wording. I reviewed the supplied metadata; whether the video delivers these promises remains unverified.

1. **YouTube Short/Long and TikTok — “made of a woman, the woman promised in the garden.”** This conflates the woman with her seed. Genesis 3:15 promises conflict and victory involving her seed; it does not promise “the woman.” Galatians 4:4 does not explicitly identify its woman with the woman in Genesis. A Christ-pointing connection is appropriate, but this wording misstates what the texts actually say. [KJV passages](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Genesis+3%3A15%2CGalatians+4%3A4&version=KJV)

2. **YouTube/Facebook titles — “Was Jesus God's Plan B? Genesis Says No”; YouTube descriptions — “He was the plan, named first, kept word for word.”** Genesis 3:15 supports the promised-seed theme, but the promise occurs after the fall. By itself, that chronology does not establish a plan predating the fall. Also, Jesus is not explicitly named there, and “kept word for word” implies a verbal correspondence the caption never demonstrates. The hook needs supporting argument in the video; the metadata currently overstates its cited evidence. [Genesis 3:15, KJV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Genesis+3%3A15%2CGalatians+4%3A4&version=KJV)

3. **Alternative title — “Why Paul Wrote 'Made of a Woman'”; YouTube descriptions — “Paul could have written son of David or son of Abraham.”** This promises an explanation of Paul’s wording and then speculates about alternatives he supposedly rejected. Unless the video establishes that reasoning from context, it attributes an intention it has not demonstrated.

4. **YouTube Long — “Long-form 16:9” and tag “bible shorts.”** The kit declares **“Format: short”** and supplies one video path. It provides no evidence of a separate long-form version, while copying the Short metadata wholesale. The long-form block is therefore not upload-ready, and its “bible shorts” keyword conflicts with its stated format.

5. **TikTok/Instagram opening — “Plan B? Genesis already named this seed.”** “This seed” has no clear antecedent for a new viewer, and neither opening identifies Jesus immediately. The curiosity depends on prior context. **Instagram — “#PlanB”** also introduces an ambiguous keyword with a prominent unrelated meaning. The hashtag counts themselves are not the problem.

6. **Verification — “pre-checked, red-teamed and panel-reviewed” versus “Gates: FAILURES ❌” and “UK-G7 lint.”** The kit claims readiness while recording unresolved failures. Its internal 157-character requirement is explicitly unmet in both YouTube descriptions. That is a failed house check, not evidence of a platform upload prohibition. “No clickbait/overclaim tokens” also cannot substantiate doctrinal accuracy.

The full Galatians 4:4 quotations in YouTube and Facebook match KJV wording; no altered word was found. [KJV text](https://biblehub.com/kjv/galatians/4-4.htm) “Find Jesus in the whole Bible” supplies the requested Jesus-directed CTA, and the displayed footer links are intact. No explicit fear, works, or gain-loss framing appears.
