# Independent review — codex (OK, 116s)

**This kit needs revision.** It retains a known false title, overextends its strongest theological claim, and presents failed checks as upload-ready verification.

1. **Definite factual error — title option 3: “Before God Spoke to Adam, He Spoke to the Snake.”** God addresses Adam and Eve in Genesis 3:9–13 before addressing the serpent in verse 14. The embedded auditor identifies this error, yet the false title remains available for selection. Remove it. [Genesis 3:9–17, KJV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Genesis+3%3A9-17&version=KJV)

2. **Ambiguous hook — YouTube Short, YouTube Long and Facebook: “God Promised Rescue Before He Gave the Sentence.”** Which sentence? The serpent’s judgment begins in verse 14, before the promise in verse 15. The defensible claim concerns the subsequent sentences on **Adam and Eve**. The descriptions supply that qualification; the titles omit it. This is an avoidable overclaim in the main click-driving text. [Genesis 3:14–17, KJV](https://biblia.com/bible/kjv/genesis/3/14-17)

3. **Doctrinal overextension — both YouTube descriptions: “Rescue was spoken before ruin” and “Grace was spoken before your sentence.”** The first blurs the distinction between announcing Adam and Eve’s consequences and the ruin already evident in their sin, shame and hiding. The second turns a narrative sequence into a personal assertion about every viewer’s judgment. Neither follows directly from this passage. Keep the application grounded in Christ’s promised victory without inventing a universal chronology.

4. **Weak, indirect conversion CTA — TikTok, Facebook and Instagram: “Come meet the Voice that spoke first.”** This repeats the disputed “first” motif and never explicitly names Jesus in the invitation. “Find Jesus in the whole Bible” supplies brand positioning, but the actual invitation remains cryptic. On TikTok and Instagram especially, the caption never explains how the promise points to Jesus; that connection is mostly left to a hashtag and the footer.

5. **Format mismatch — “YouTube (Long-form 16:9)” uses identical short metadata and the tag “bible shorts.”** The artifact declares **“Format: short”** and identifies only one video. No separate long-form version is established. The long-form block therefore lacks a demonstrated basis, and “bible shorts” is mismatched within that block.

6. **False readiness assurance — “Copy each block straight into the platform’s upload form” and “pre-checked, red-teamed and panel-reviewed.”** These assurances conflict with **“Gates: FAILURES ❌,”** the unresolved lint failures, and the retained false title. “UK-G3 doctrine — no clickbait/overclaim tokens” also substitutes a token check for evaluating meaning. The 157-character requirement is an internal gate here; the artifact does not establish it as a platform rule.

7. **Repetitive short-platform hooks — TikTok: “He spoke … Grace was spoken first … Voice that spoke first”; Instagram repeats the same closing phrases.** The opening premise creates curiosity, but the repetition consumes valuable caption space without developing the Christ connection. “Genesis 3: Grace Came First” is also a generic alternative that loses the specific serpent-and-promise hook.

The quoted Genesis 3:15 wording is exact KJV; I found no altered word. Hashtag quantities are restrained, and the tags are relevant apart from the long-form mismatch. The displayed footer URLs are intact. Actual video delivery, working links and sibling-title collisions cannot be verified from this text alone.
