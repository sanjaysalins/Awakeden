# Independent review — grok (OK, 168s)

Hook: 8. Landing: 6. The first sentence can stop a scroll. The last sentence does not land in the bones. Under 8 on the landing means REVISE.

The quoted words match the KJV. "We have sinned, for we have spoken against the LORD, and against thee" is Numbers 21:7. The Nicodemus speech is John 3:14-15 word for word. "made a curse for us" is a consecutive slice of Galatians 3:13 ("being made a curse for us"). No altered word inside the quotation marks.

The spine is one thread: a serpent lifted up, then the Son of man lifted up, then a call to look and live. The close invites. It does not threaten, bargain, or tell anyone to earn life. The trouble is inside that thread.

"I prayed. Instead of taking the snakes away, God told me to make a serpent of brass, and set it on a pole." The word "Instead" has nothing to push against. The quote stops before the people's plea in Numbers 21:7: "pray unto the LORD, that he take away the serpents from us." A first-time listener never hears that plea, so the best surprise in the piece arrives unearned. The same sentence also misreports God's order. Numbers 21:8 has the LORD say "Make thee a fiery serpent, and set it upon a pole." Verse 9 says Moses "made a serpent of brass." The narration puts the brass into God's mouth.

"Whoever was bitten looked at it, and lived." On a first hearing, every bitten person looked, and looking was automatic. Numbers 21:8-9 says the bitten person who looked lived. The look is the whole point. This line deletes it. The John quote brings the condition back ("whosoever believeth"), and the last line asks for it again. The middle and the close disagree.

"On the cross, Jesus took on Himself the sin that bites us all" and "He is the cure." In this story the snakes bite. The bite is the judgment. The brass serpent has the shape of the thing that is killing them. John 3:14-15 ties the lifting to believing, and the result is "should not perish, but have eternal life." "The sin that bites us all" is a new metaphor. It turns the serpent into sin, and "cure" turns the cross into bite medicine. The sound line is already here: Paul says He was "made a curse for us."

"Lifted up where all can see" adds a claim the text does not make. John ties the lifting to "whosoever believeth." "Lift your eyes to Him, and live" walks back to eyesight and to "live," after the quote has already said "eternal life." That last sentence could close a different "look to Him" piece. It reuses this story's look/live picture, which keeps it above a pure stock cap, and it still misses this piece's own verbs.

"I am Moses, and I made it" is a costume. A listener with no Bible background then has to follow that speaker into Nicodemus, "Son of man," and "Paul," with no handle on any of them. "made a curse for us," left unexplained, sounds like a shock phrase on a first pass.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Put the people's plea to take the serpents away back in front of "Instead," and keep God's order as a fiery serpent on a pole, with the brass as what Moses made.
2. Rewrite "Whoever was bitten looked at it, and lived" so life follows the look, and close on believe / not perish / eternal life, still using the lifted pole.
3. Cut "the sin that bites us all" and "He is the cure." Let "made a curse for us" and the lifted-up Son of man carry the meaning.
