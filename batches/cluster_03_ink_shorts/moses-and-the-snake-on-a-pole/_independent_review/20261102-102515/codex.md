# Independent review — codex (OK, 59s)

**Hook: 8/10.** “God answered snakebite with a snake on a pole” creates immediate, concrete curiosity. The surprise comes from the biblical event itself.

**Landing: 5/10.** “Lift your eyes to Him, and live” echoes the looking imagery, but this exact sentence could close numerous other devotionals. Under your portability test, it triggers the stock-closer cap.

Concrete findings:

1. **The closing metaphor needs a plain explanation of faith.** “Lifted up where all can see, He is the cure. Lift your eyes to Him, and live” leaves a newcomer with visual attention as the apparent response. The quotation says “believeth,” but the narration never explains that looking to Christ means trusting Him. “Where all can see” also adds an unnecessary universal visibility claim.

2. **“My brass serpent was only a picture” overstates the contrast.** It foreshadowed Christ, but it also served as God’s appointed means of deliverance in an actual historical event. “Only” diminishes what the narration has just described. Jesus establishes the serpent-to-cross connection explicitly, so freshness requires no speculative symbolism. [Numbers 21:8–9; John 3:14–15](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Numbers+21%3A8-9%3BJohn+3%3A14-15&version=KJV)

3. **The account blurs command, execution, and inference.** “God told me to make a serpent of brass” attributes the material to God’s recorded instruction; the text records God commanding a fiery serpent and Moses making it in brass. “Instead of taking the snakes away” reasonably describes the narrative’s emphasis, but their subsequent removal or continued presence is not explicitly reported. These are precision issues, not invented doctrines. [Numbers 21:7–9](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Numbers+21%3A5-9&version=KJV)

4. **The Moses voice creates avoidable first-hearing friction.** “I am Moses” supplies a name without explaining his role. Later, “Long after me, Jesus told…” and “Paul says” turn that historical character into a retrospective Christian commentator without signalling the dramatic convention. This is a narration problem, not evidence of doctrinal error.

5. **Grace is present, but the final emphasis is survival.** “Jesus took on Himself the sin that bites us all” and “made a curse for us” locate salvation in Christ’s work. There is no explicit works demand or manipulative threat. However, “the cure” followed by “and live” makes personal rescue the dominant appeal. Clarify Christ’s gracious provision and the invitation to trust Him; do not remove the passage’s biblical death-and-life language.

6. **KJV check: no altered words in any quotation.** The confession and “made a curse for us” are exact excerpts; John 3:14–15 is exact. The confession stops before the verse’s semicolon, which is excerpt punctuation, not an altered word. [Numbers 21:7](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Numbers+21%3A7&version=KJV), [John 3:14–15](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John+3%3A14-15&version=KJV), [Galatians 3:13](https://biblia.com/bible/kjv/galatians/3/13-14)

The central thread remains intact: the lifted serpent leads to the lifted Christ and an invitation to look. The needed revision is in precision and landing, rather than replacing that spine.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Explain looking as trusting Christ, and make the final invitation specific enough to escape the stock-closer cap.
2. Remove “only” and distinguish God’s recorded command from Moses’s execution and narrative inference.
3. Resolve the Moses narrator’s unexplained retrospective voice and identify his role for newcomers.
