# Independent review — claude (OK, 40s)

**Review of the Moses and bronze serpent narration**

**KJV check**
- Numbers 21:7, "We have sinned, for we have spoken against the LORD, and against thee": exact.
- John 3:14-15: exact.
- Galatians 3:13, "made a curse for us": a true fragment of "being made a curse for us". It is exact, but the fragment is cut so short it loses its grammar.
- No altered words. One small attribution issue: "God told me to make a serpent of brass." In Numbers 21:8 God says "fiery serpent". The brass is Moses' own choice in v.9. This is minor, but it is a text-faithfulness slip in a piece built on the text.

**Doctrine**
- Sound and Christ-centred. The John 3:14 typology and the 2 Cor 5:21 and Gal 3:13 substitution are orthodox. Nothing is invented.
- "Instead of taking the snakes away" is accurate. The people asked for that in v.7, and God didn't do it.
- No fear framing. "Dying because they had spoken against God" is straight from the text.

**Clarity on first hearing**
- "Lifted up" is never tied to crucifixion until "On the cross". A newcomer holds "lifted up" twice before the payoff arrives.
- "Son of man" and "Nicodemus" are unexplained. The first is jargon, and the second is just a name.
- "Made a curse for us" is archaic and unglossed. It is the key doctrinal line, and a non-native listener will not parse it. It breaks the global-audience rule.
- "The sin that bites us all" mixes the snakebite picture with the sin picture. It works, but only just.
- **Voice break:** Moses speaks in the first person, then says "Jesus took on Himself…" and "Paul says…". That is a different voice from the "I made it" frame. A Moses who knows Paul is awkward. "Long after me" covers Nicodemus but not the Paul line. G9 multi-voice: the Jesus quote and the people's confession are not obviously routed to their own voices.

**Spine**
- Snake, pole, look, live: one clean thread from hook to CTA. This is the strength of the piece.

**HOOK: 8/10.** "God answered snakebite with a snake on a pole." The paradox is concrete and odd, and it opens the gap. It is not a template open. It loses points because it is a flat statement with no stake for the viewer.

**LANDING: 7/10.** "Lift your eyes to Him, and live."
- It is partly earned, because it echoes "looked at it, and lived".
- It is also the "look to Jesus" stock family. "Lift your eyes to Him" could end almost any gospel piece.
- It doesn't use the piece's most distinctive image: the cure looked like the thing that bit them. Nothing in the last line carries that.
- The line before it, "He is the cure", is flat.

**Grace and works:** The landing invites rather than demands. "Live" carries a mild self-benefit note, but it is the text's own verb and is acceptable.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Rebuild the landing from the piece's unique irony: the cure looked like the thing that bit them. Say plainly that Jesus took the shape of our sin on the pole, then end on the looking. For example: "The cure looked like the poison. He hung there as the thing that bit us. Look at Him, and live." The aim is a last line that could not end any other piece. Aim for 8 or higher.
2. Fix the voice. Keep Moses in his own lane, or move the Jesus and Paul content to the narrator. Route the Jesus quote and the people's confession to their own voices (G9). Gloss "lifted up" as "put up on a cross" before the payoff, and replace or explain "made a curse for us" with plain words (e.g. "took the curse for us").
3. Fix the Numbers 21 detail and the clarity gaps. Say God told Moses to make a "fiery serpent" (or have Moses say he chose brass), and give "Nicodemus" and "Son of man" one short gloss. Strengthen the hook with a stake, such as "…and the cure was the thing that bit them".
