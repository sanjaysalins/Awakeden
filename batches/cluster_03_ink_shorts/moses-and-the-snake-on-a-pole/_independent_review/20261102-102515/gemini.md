# Independent review — gemini (OK, 64s)

Here is the independent review of the narration artifact:

**Doctrinal soundness:** Sound. The typology connecting the bronze serpent to Christ on the cross is deeply orthodox, drawing directly on Jesus's own words in John 3. 
**KJV verbatim:** Passes. The quotes ("We have sinned, for we have spoken against the LORD, and against thee", "And as Moses lifted up...", "made a curse for us") are accurate to the KJV text.
**Clarity on first hearing:** The narrative logic completely collapses in the final third. You establish a first-person eyewitness conceit ("I am Moses, and I made it"), but then Moses suddenly quotes the Apostle Paul ("and Paul says He was 'made a curse for us'"). This shatters the immersion. How is Moses citing a New Testament epistle? It turns your historical narrator into a modern Sunday School teacher and creates a jarring, confusing break in the voice.
**Grace-anchored conviction:** Clean. No fear or works-based framing. The solution relies entirely on Christ being "lifted up" and the listener simply lifting their eyes. 
**Freshness = faithful depth:** The first-person Moses perspective is a strong, fresh entry point into a familiar typology, but the execution gets lazy when it abandons that perspective to drop an academic citation to Paul.
**One thread spine:** The spine is solid. It tracks "snakebite/venom -> lifted up -> look and live" consistently from the hook to the CTA.

**SCORE THE HOOK: 8.** "God answered snakebite with a snake on a pole. I am Moses, and I made it." It immediately presents a paradox, establishes the character, and completely avoids template opens. 
**SCORE THE LANDING: 8.** "Lifted up where all can see, He is the cure. Lift your eyes to Him, and live." It earns its landing by perfectly mapping the physical act of the Israelites (looking at the pole to survive) to the spiritual act of the believer. It is not a stock closer.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Cut the anachronistic reference to Paul ("and Paul says..."). It shatters the first-person Moses conceit. Let Moses describe the reality of the cross in his own prophetic/eternal voice without citing an epistle written 1,500 years later.
2. Smooth the metaphor "sin that bites us all" — it comes across slightly clunky. Consider something like "the venom of our sin" to keep the imagery tighter.
3. Fix the punctuation on the first KJV quote: the period should be inside the quotation marks (`against thee."`).
