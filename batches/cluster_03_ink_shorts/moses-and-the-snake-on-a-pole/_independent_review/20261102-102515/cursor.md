## Independent adversarial review — Moses / bronze serpent narration

### Doctrinal soundness
- **Mostly orthodox typology** (Numbers 21 → John 3:14–15 → cross / curse). No contrarian readings detected.
- **“Jesus took on Himself the sin that bites us all”** — substitution is in line with the John/Nicodemus frame, but **“sin that bites us all”** is your metaphor, not the text’s. A careful listener may ask whether the wilderness snakes stand for sin or only for judgment; you never name that link in Scripture (Jesus points to **lifted up**, not “bite = sin”). Minor overreach, not heresy.
- **“My brass serpent was only a picture”** — typologically fine for a 60s short, but **“only”** can sound like you’re downplaying that God **really** healed lookers in the wilderness (Numbers 21:9). Safer: “picture **of what was coming**” without “only.”
- **First-person Moses** saying **“Paul says He was…”** is a **voice/anachronism** risk: Moses-as-eyewitness suddenly channels Pauline atonement language. Doctrinally OK for the audience; dramatically easy to misread as sloppy POV.

### KJV verbatim (quotation marks)
- **Numbers confession** — `"We have sinned, for we have spoken against the LORD, and against thee"` matches the opening of Numbers 21:7 KJV for the words quoted ( excerpt; acceptable if your house rules allow partial quotes).
- **John 3:14–15 block** — matches project KJV cache for the combined 14–15 wording (including **“Son of man”**, **“believeth”**, **“perish”**, **“eternal life”**).
- **FAIL — Galatians 3:13:** Paul says He was **"made a curse for us"**.  
  KJV: **“being made a curse for us”** (Galatians 3:13). You dropped **“being”** and wrapped a **trimmed, non-contiguous** fragment in quotes. That violates **exact KJV inside quotation marks**.

### Clarity (first hearing, zero Bible knowledge)
- **Hook vs body order:** **“God answered snakebite with a snake on a pole”** sounds like the **first** response to bites. The next lines clarify snakes came from rebellion and the pole-serpent came **after** confession and prayer. On one pass, **“answered”** can blur **judgment (snakes)** vs **remedy (pole)**.
- **“against thee”** in the quote — **“thee” = Moses** is clear only if they already heard **“I am Moses”**; still archaic on a global audience (your own charter prefers plain load-bearing words outside short KJV snippets).
- **“I am Moses, and I made it”** — stops the scroll for some; others may think **skit/roleplay** and bounce before John 3 lands.
- Thread from **look → lifted up → live** is followable once John’s quote runs; the **Paul/curse** line is a **second NT lane** in one breath — dense for ~60s.

### Grace-anchored conviction
- **No obvious fear/gain-loss/worksd** pitch. **“believeth… eternal life”** is Jesus’ own gospel wording in the quote.
- **“Lift your eyes to Him, and live”** invites by echoing **look and live** — conviction anchored in the story, not manufactured pressure. OK.

### Freshness = faithful depth
- **Paradox hook + Moses POV** is fresher than template opens; the **typology itself** is well-trodden — freshness lives in **“snakebite answered with snake on pole”** and **Moses made it**, not in a novel doctrine.
- **Faithful** as far as it goes; it doesn’t surface an overlooked textual detail (e.g. **beheld** the serpent, brass vs worship later in 2 Kings) — safe but not deep.

### One thread spine (hook → middle → CTA)
- Spine is **lifted up / look / live**, consistent from wilderness → Nicodemus → cross → closing.
- Small wobble: **“sin that bites”** and **“made a curse”** introduce **bite + curse** sub-metaphors beside **lift/look** — still related, but the middle **thickens** faster than the hook promises.
