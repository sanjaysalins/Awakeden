## Independent adversarial review — upload kit (He Was Asleep in the Storm)

### HOOKY BUT HONEST / bait-and-switch

- **YouTube (Short) + YouTube (Long-form) description:** *“The disciples wake Him, certain He does not care.”* Anchor is **Matthew 8:23–27**. In Matthew, they say *“Lord, save us: we perish”* (8:25) — no “don’t care” line. That accusation is **Mark 4:38** territory (*“Master, carest thou not…”*). Packaging a **Matthew** reference while importing Mark’s emotional charge is synoptic bleed; a Matthew-only reader is misled about what *this* passage records. The in-engine red-team note admits this and still calls it “acceptable” — that’s a soften, not a fix.

- **TikTok description:** *“The storm was killing them”* — nobody dies in the pericope; KJV has threatened perishing, not accomplished killing. Same hyperbole in **Instagram (Reel)** opening line. Reads as sensationalized beyond the text (and beyond **Facebook**, which stays closer to *“we perish”*).

- **Alt title 2:** *“The Storm Was Killing Them. He Was Asleep.”* — same overstatement if used anywhere public-facing.

- **YouTube (Short) description vs actual narration** (`_storm_spoken.txt`): Upload CTA is *“When the water reaches your knees, come to the Christ who slept unafraid.”* The locked script is sharper: *“when the water reaches your knees, you don't trust the Christ in the boat”* and closes with *“He is asking you — why are you afraid?”* Metadata sells a gentler “come to Him unafraid” arc; the video **prosecutes** “you don’t trust” / “furious, certain He doesn't care.” Not a lie, but a **tone bait-and-switch** for scrollers expecting comfort-copy and getting indictment audio.

- **YouTube description:** *“It is easy to say, easy for Him, He is God. Exactly.”* Matches the video’s turn; Christology is mainstream evangelical, not overclaim — **PASS** on doctrine, but it **is** interpretive gloss sold as punchy fact (*“Exactly”*).

### KJV verbatim

- **Quoted spans labeled KJV:** *“Why are ye fearful, O ye of little faith?”* — substring of Matthew 8:26 KJV; **OK** as a partial quote.

- **YouTube (Short/Long) description — immediately after the 8:26 citation, unquoted:** *“Then **He rose**, and rebuked the winds and the sea, and there was a great calm.”* KJV Matthew 8:26 has ***“Then he arose”***, not *“rose”*, and uses a semicolon before *“and there was a great calm.”* This sits **outside** quotation marks but mimics scripture line-for-line with an altered verb — exactly the kind of drift UK-G2 automation can miss (gates only police long **quoted** spans). Flag for human KJV-strict lens.

- **YouTube description — 8:27 quote:** *“What manner of man is this, that even the winds and the sea obey him!”* — matches project KJV cache for 8:27 speech. **PASS.**

- **Facebook (Reel/Video) description:** *“Lord, save us: we perish”* — matches Matthew 8:25 KJV wording. **PASS** (prose framing, not falsely labeled as a full-verse block).

- **Misleading citation habit:** *(Matthew 8:26, KJV)* attached only to the **question** clause, while the rebuke/calm half of 8:26 appears in non-KJV prose (*“Then He rose…”*). Not a word-change inside the quotes, but it **splits one verse** across “official KJV” and paraphrase — easy for reposters to treat the whole sentence as Scripture.
