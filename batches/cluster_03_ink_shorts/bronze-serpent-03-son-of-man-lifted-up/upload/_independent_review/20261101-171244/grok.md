# Independent review — grok (OK, 208s)

The kit stamps **ALL PASS** while its own red-team still says **REVISE**, and the unfixed line is a real misstatement of John 3.

**False gate.** The header says `Gates: ALL PASS` and tells the uploader to copy each block straight in. The in-kit auditor already rejected this YouTube sentence and asked for John 12:32-33: `John uses the same word of lifting again at the cross`. That sentence is still in both YouTube descriptions. No platform copy cites John 12 anywhere.

**Jesus is given a line he did not say.** YouTube opens: `By night, Jesus tells a Pharisee something strange: the lifting that shames Him is what John calls His glory.` TikTok and Instagram open the same way: `By night, Jesus tells a Pharisee: the lifting that shames Him is His glory.` In John 3:14-15 Jesus tells Nicodemus that the Son of man must be lifted up as Moses lifted the serpent, so that whoever believes has eternal life. He does not mention shame, and he does not call that lifting his glory. `Something strange` hangs the hook on that false summary.

**The "one word" claim overreaches.** The chosen title `One Word Joins Jesus' Shame and His Glory` sells a word the copy never names. YouTube then says `shame and glory, one lifting`. John never says "shame" here. Shame is the cross (Hebrews 12:2). Glory in John is a different word, in a later chapter (John 12:23, with the same "lifted up" verb at John 12:32-33). Facebook goes further and is linguistically wrong: `The lifting that shamed Him is the word John uses for His glory.` John does not use "lifted up" as his word for glory.

**Alt title 2 is worse.** `Jesus Told a Pharisee the Cross Was His Glory` puts "cross" and "glory" in Jesus's mouth on the night visit. John 3 does not say that. Do not let anyone swap to it.

**KJV quote itself is exact.** `And as Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, even so must the Son of man be lifted up: That whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have eternal life.` (John 3:14-15) matches the KJV, including `eternal life` (verse 16 is the one that says `everlasting`). Prose says `bronze serpent`. The KJV says brass. That is outside the quote, but it fights the `KJV-strict` stamp.

**Doctrine elsewhere is fine.** `Believe on Christ, lifted up for you, and live.` is faith in Christ, not works, fear, or gain. The verse's `should not perish` is the text, not an added threat. Footer and `https://awakeden.com` / `https://youtube.com/@awakeden` are intact.
