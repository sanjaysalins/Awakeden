The main problem is that the copy presents a theological interpretation as something Jesus explicitly told Nicodemus. The “ALL PASS” certification also contradicts the kit’s own evidence.

1. **TikTok / Instagram / title option 2 — overstated attribution.** “Jesus tells a Pharisee: the lifting that shames Him is His glory” and “Jesus Told a Pharisee the Cross Was His Glory” attribute an explicit glory statement to the Nicodemus conversation. John 3:14–15 speaks of lifting up, believing and eternal life. The glory connection requires explanation from elsewhere in John. This is defensible interpretation presented as reported speech. [Compare John 3:14–15 and John 12:23–33.](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John+3%3A14-15%3BJohn+12%3A23-33&version=KJV)

2. **Facebook — misleading lexical claim.** “The lifting that shamed Him is the word John uses for His glory” suggests that “lifted up” and “glorified” translate the same Greek word. They do not: John 3:14 uses *hypsoō*; John 12:23 uses *doxazō*. The relationship needs explaining as a theological connection, rather than asserting lexical identity. This also affects the chosen title’s “One Word” promise. [John 3:14 Greek text](https://biblehub.com/text/john/3-14.htm), [John 12:23 Greek text](https://biblehub.com/text/john/12-23.htm).

[Supporting passage](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John+12%3A23-33&version=KJV).
