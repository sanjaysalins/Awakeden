# Independent review — codex (OK, 60s)

**Hook: 8/10. Landing: 5/10.**

1. **The ending gives the listener conflicting directions.** “The curtain is open” establishes an entrance into God’s presence. But the next sentence moves to “a land where no one lives,” immediately followed by “Walk in.” On first hearing, the nearest destination is the wilderness where sin went. The listener must mentally return to the curtain to understand the invitation.

2. **The spine changes before the CTA.** “Why two?” promises an explanation of the two goats. “One showed sin paid for by death. One showed sin carried far away” answers it. Then the torn curtain introduces a second governing image, and the final wilderness sentence switches back again. These themes are biblically connected, but the narration does not make their connection clear enough for its closing command.

3. **“Sin has gone where that goat went” pushes the picture into an unsupported geographical claim.** Leviticus describes the goat bearing Israel’s iniquities into an uninhabited land. It does not establish that forgiven sins literally occupy that location. The problem is the categorical “has gone where”: distinguish the enacted picture from Christ’s accomplishment. [Leviticus 16:21–22](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Leviticus+16%3A21-22&version=KJV).

4. **“Walk in” has insufficient specificity and emotional resolution.** It could close another piece about a doorway, sanctuary, or invitation, so the stock-closer cap applies. It also leaves a Bible novice to infer what entering means. Drawing near to God through Christ is biblically grounded; the narration needs to make that destination explicit. [Hebrews 10:19–22](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Hebrews+10%3A19-22&version=KJV).

5. **The ancient setting needs one concrete anchor.** “The law gave me two goats” leaves an unfamiliar listener wondering whose law and which people. “High priest” names Aaron’s office without explaining his role. Identifying Israel and its annual Day of Atonement would make the ritual easier to follow.

**KJV verbatim:** Both quoted excerpts are exact, contiguous KJV wording. No altered words in either the [Isaiah quotation](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Isaiah+53%3A6&version=KJV) or the [Matthew quotation](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Matthew+27%3A51&version=KJV).

**Doctrinal and grace assessment:** The central death-and-removal interpretation is orthodox; “my goats only pictured” distinguishes symbol from fulfillment. “For all who trust Him” anchors the application in faith. There is no evident fear, bargain, or works pressure. The main doctrinal concern is the overextended location metaphor, not the underlying gospel claim.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Resolve the wilderness/entrance collision so the listener knows where the invitation leads.
2. Keep the two-goat thread governing the conclusion; explicitly connect any curtain imagery to it.
3. Replace the literal-sounding destination claim with a clear statement of what the goat pictured.
