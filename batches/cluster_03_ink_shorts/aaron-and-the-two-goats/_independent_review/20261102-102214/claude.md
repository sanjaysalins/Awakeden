# Independent review — claude (OK, 33s)

**Doctrine and KJV check**
- Both quotes are exact KJV. Isaiah 53:6 and Matthew 27:51 match word for word.
- The narration never says where the "bore them away" claim comes from. Lev 16:21-22 supports the live goat carrying sins away. John 1:29 and Heb 9:26 are not cited in the script. A few evangelical readers see the scapegoat (Azazel) differently. The script is not wrong, but it states the typology as settled and cites no verse.
- "Together they were one sin offering" matches Lev 16:5 ("two kids of the goats for a sin offering"). That is accurate.
- Aaron says "Isaiah wrote of Him" and "Long after me". That is an anachronism, but it is a clear device and it is fine.
- No fear, gain/loss or works framing. Grace tone is acceptable. Jesus is named late, and the "trust Him" line is the only grace word.

**Clarity**
- **Image clash in the last three sentences.** "The curtain is open." Then "sin has gone where that goat went, to a land where no one lives." Then "Walk in." The last line follows the wilderness image, so a newcomer hears "walk into the land where no one lives." The intended meaning is to walk in through the open curtain. That image was set up two sentences earlier and then dropped. This is the main flaw, and it fails the first-hearing test.
- "Veil" is explained once, which is good. "Sin offering" and "high priest" are glossed lightly. The phrase "inside the veil" comes before the glossing makes sense.
- "Handled both" is vague. "Confessed over it all the sins" is slightly archaic word order, but understandable.

**Thread spine**
The two-goats thread holds from hook to close. The veil and the wilderness are two sub-images competing in the ending.

**Hook: 7/10.** "One goat died. One goat was sent away alive. Why two?" is concrete, short and a real curiosity gap. It is not a template open. It is a little riddle-like, and nothing in it is visual or emotionally urgent.

**Landing: 6/10.** "Walk in." comes from the veil image and not from a stock closer. But the sentence before it ends on the wilderness, so the verb has no clear destination. "Walk in" could also end any piece about the torn veil or Heb 10:19-20. The script's own KJV never uses the verb "walk in". The last beat should return to the live goat, since that is this piece's own image.

Both scores are under 8.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Fix the ending's image clash. Either end on the open curtain, with the wilderness line placed before it ("sin gone where that goat went... and the curtain is open. Walk in."), or make the final line pay off the live goat. Don't let "Walk in" sit directly after "a land where no one lives".
2. Make the landing specific to this piece. Use the goat or the sent-away image in the last sentence, for example "The goat never came back. Neither will your sin." Do this only if it can be said in a grace-anchored way and not as a pressure line. Avoid generic "Walk in".
3. Support the claim "He bore them away" with Scripture, for example John 1:29 or Heb 9:26, in KJV. This guards against contested scapegoat readings and ties the typology to the Bible and not to the author's gloss. Gloss "sin offering" and "high priest" earlier, so a newcomer has them before the veil line.
