## Independent adversarial review — Aaron / two goats narration

### Doctrinal soundness
Mostly standard evangelical typology (Leviticus 16 → substitution + removal → Isaiah 53 → torn veil → access). No contrarian Azazel speculation, no works salvation.

**Friction (not heresy, but worth naming):**
- **"Long after me, Jesus did what my goats only pictured"** — First-person Aaron saying **"Jesus"** by name is a deliberate anachronism. Bible-literate listeners may read it as costume narration, not witness; it does not come from Leviticus or the ritual text.
- **"He died for our sins, and He bore them away"** — Correct Christology, but after a **live goat sent to the wilderness**, a first-time listener could briefly map the second goat onto “still alive, taken away” before they integrate death + bearing on one person. One clarifying beat (e.g. that he bore sins **in his death**, not like the live animal) would close that slip.
- **"together they were one sin offering"** — Faithful to Leviticus 16’s pairing; you never mention **one lot for the LORD, one for Azazel** or that both goats are selected together. Omission is fine for length; do not overclaim precision you did not state.

### Clarity on first hearing
- **Strong:** Binary opener (died / sent away alive), plain verbs, **"curtain that shut people out"** glosses the veil well.
- **Weak:** Hook **"Why two?"** promises a reason-count; you deliver **what the two mean** (**"One showed sin paid… One showed sin carried far away"**) before a crisp **because one ritual could not picture both at once** (your EW01 line: *"No single creature could hold both halves"*). A zero-Bible listener can still follow the pictures but may feel the title question was only half answered.
- **"Two goats pictured one rescue"** — **"one rescue"** compresses payment + expulsion; acceptable, slightly abstract for 5-second retention.

### Grace-anchored conviction
- **"For all who trust Him"** — faith, not performance; no fear/gain-loss pitch.
- No **"clean enough first"** pressure. Tone invites; it does not threaten.

Minor note: **"sin has gone where that goat went"** is comfort-forward; it does not linger on guilt, which fits grace — just ensure audio delivery does not sound like transactional clearance.

### Freshness = faithful depth
- Fresh entry: **two goats, one offering**, death vs live removal — text-grounded, not gimmick.
- Isaiah + torn veil are familiar moves; freshness here is mostly **structure and Aaron POV**, not a novel doctrine.

### One thread spine (hook → middle → CTA)
Single spine holds: **two goats → two meanings → Jesus fulfills → veil open → sin removed → enter**.

No thread swap. **"The curtain is open"** ties Matthew’s veil to Aaron’s **"inside the veil"** — coherent.

Small wobble: **Isaiah** lands on **bearing iniquity**; the very next line is **death + bore away**; then **Matthew** is **veil**. All orthodox, but the middle stacks three NT/OT hops quickly for ~60s — ear fatigue risk, not logic break.

### HOOK SCORE: **8/10**
**"One goat died. One goat was sent away alive. Why two?"** — concrete, tense, not a **Did you know / Imagine** template. **"I am Aaron, the first high priest"** on the next beat may lose some scrollers who wanted an immediate answer before the persona shift; still strong.

### LANDING SCORE: **5/10** (rubric cap)
**"Walk in."** is thematically tied to the open curtain, but the **exact sentence** is already the shared family closer across this cluster (`longform/EW01_Two_Goats`, punchy shorts, poc sketchbook). Per your rule: *could this exact final sentence end a different piece?* **Yes** — same topic, same campaign. That makes it a **stock closer** for this corpus even if it is not generic **"Come to Jesus."** It does not name **Jesus** in the CTA, which helps distinctiveness, but it does not land with piece-unique bones the way a line built from **both goats / both hands / one offering** would. **Under 8 → REVISE** on landing alone.

### Additional adversarial nits
- **"Isaiah wrote of Him"** — Fine christological reading; Isaiah’s servant is not labeled **"Jesus"** in the verse quoted. Savvy listeners know; new listeners may not connect **"Him"** to **"Jesus"** from the prior sentence without a half-beat bridge.
- **"When He died,"** immediately before the Matthew quote — Matthew 27:51 is tied to the crucifixion moment; OK. You do not quote **"yielded up the ghost"** — fine.
- **Repetition:** **"to a land where no one lived"** / **"where no one lives"** — deliberate echo; good for audio if not rushed; bad if it feels padded on re-listen.
- **Aaron POV + no voice routing** — If this ships multi-voice elsewhere, KJV lines are citation-framed (Isaiah wrote / when He died), not **"God says"** — narrator-only is defensible; still one voice for all of Aaron may flatten the hook persona in audio.

VERDICT: REVISE  
TOP FIXES:  
1. Replace or extend **"Walk in."** with a closer only this short can own (e.g. weave **two goats / one offering / both hands / veil** into the last sentence) so it cannot swap onto EW01 variants unchanged.  
2. Pay off **"Why two?"** in one plain spoken line (one creature could not show both payment and removal) before or right after **"Two goats pictured one rescue."**  
3. De-anonymize or de-anachronize the NT pivot: either drop **"Jesus"** from Aaron’s mouth and let Isaiah + veil do the reveal, or add one clause so **"Jesus"** from Aaron reads as intentional testimony, not slip. Optionally tighten **"He died… and He bore them away"** so bearing is not heard as the live-goat beat alone.
