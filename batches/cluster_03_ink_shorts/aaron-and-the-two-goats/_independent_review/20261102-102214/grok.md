# Independent review — grok (OK, 180s)

This piece needs a revise. The two-goat teaching is sound, and both Bible quotes are exact. The close is foggy, and the last line is a stock closer.

**Hook: 8.** "One goat died. One goat was sent away alive. Why two?" is a concrete puzzle in the first breath. It clears the template-open cap. A stranger still has no personal stake in those five seconds, but the picture is strong enough to stop a scroll.

**Landing: 4.** Under 8, so the verdict is revise.

"Walk in." fails the own-image test. That exact sentence could end a piece about any door, gate, or house. Stock-closer cap is 5. It also points at the wrong place. The sentence before it is "to a land where no one lives." On a first hearing, "Walk in" sends the listener into the empty land where sin was sent. The open curtain was two sentences earlier. The inner room and the wilderness get swapped in the ear.

**KJV.** No altered word.
- "the LORD hath laid on him the iniquity of us all" matches Isaiah 53:6.
- "the veil of the temple was rent in twain from the top to the bottom" matches Matthew 27:51 and Mark 15:38.

**What holds.** "Together they were one sin offering" matches Leviticus 16:5. Blood inside the veil matches 16:15. Both hands, confession, and the wilderness match 16:21-22. "Jesus did what my goats only pictured" keeps the goats as pictures. "He died for our sins, and He bore them away" is the normal evangelical reading. "For all who trust Him" stays on faith. The tone invites. There is no threat, no bargain, and no works ladder.

**What to fix.**

"The curtain that shut people out of God's presence" says too much. The veil shut people out of the Most Holy Place, the inner room. Israel still came to God at the altar. A newcomer can leave thinking no one knew God until the cloth tore.

The spine splits. The hook asks why two goats. The middle answers: "One showed sin paid for by death. One showed sin carried far away." The close then changes jobs. "The curtain is open" and "Walk in" answer a second question, about access. The last three sentences toggle between the open curtain and the empty land. One thread has to own the last line.

"Sin has gone where that goat went, to a land where no one lives" drops the word "pictured" and sounds like a place on a map. A listener with no Bible background can think the sin is sitting in a desert, and that "Walk in" sends them there.

Two sharp details are quoted and then left unused: "once a year," and "from the top to the bottom." The text's own surprise is that the priest did this every year, and the tear started at the top. The landing spends that on a two-word exit.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Rebuild the last three sentences so "Walk in" no longer follows "a land where no one lives," and replace "Walk in" with a last line only this two-goat piece could end on.
2. Keep one spine from "Why two?" to the last word: either land on death-plus-carried-away, or open on the shut curtain if the close is entering.
3. Narrow "shut people out of God's presence" to the inner room behind the veil, and keep the wilderness a picture of sins gone, not a place the listener goes.
