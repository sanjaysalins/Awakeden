# Independent review — codex (OK, 117s)

The main problem is the kit’s central contrast: it makes Christ’s wound sound survivable in itself, obscuring his actual death and resurrection. The “ALL PASS” stamp is not justified.

1. **Major — YouTube Shorts/long-form and Facebook: “A bruised heel is survivable. A crushed head is not.”** Applied to Christ, this suggests he survived a lesser injury. Christ actually died; his victory involves resurrection, not merely surviving the cross. “A bruised heel that carried a cross” compounds that omission. Scripture explicitly connects his victory over the devil with his death. [Hebrews 2:14, KJV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Hebrews%202%3A14&version=KJV)

2. **Major — Title options: “One Gets a Bruise. The Other Gets a Crushed Skull” and “The Promise Where Only One Side Survives.”** The first turns an interpretive image into a graphic anatomical claim. The second repeats the misleading survival contrast and can imply Satan’s literal extinction. Neither is a careful statement of Genesis 3:15. The quoted KJV uses *bruise* for both wounds; the different locations support interpretation, but do not make “crushed skull” verbatim Scripture. **The actual verse quotations in both YouTube descriptions and Facebook are exact KJV.** [Genesis 3:15, KJV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Genesis%203%3A15&version=KJV)

3. **Major — Both YouTube descriptions: “You keep hoping for a tie you can live with.”** This invents a spiritual motive for the viewer and shifts into accusation. Nothing preceding it establishes that the viewer wants compromise with Satan. It undermines the requested grace-centred tone.

4. **Platform failure — Instagram: eight hashtags, from “#Bible” through “#Faith.”** Instagram announced a rollout limiting post/Reel captions to five hashtags. This block exceeds that announced limit, contradicting “UK-G5 … hashtag counts + link rules OK.” Reduce it to five relevant tags. [Platform announcement reported by Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-implements-new-limits-on-hashtag-use/808309/)

5. **Format/SEO mismatch — YouTube “Long-form 16:9”: “bible shorts.”** The kit declares “Format: short” and supplies one video path, yet presents a ready-to-copy long-form package without identifying a corresponding version. The “bible shorts” tag is mismatched to that section. The remaining keywords are relevant; broad tag-stuffing is not evident.

6. **Hook weakness — TikTok and Instagram: “Genesis 3:15 is not a tie.”** “Tie” introduces a contest before explaining the two wounds, so newcomers must decode the premise. Facebook’s identical title is particularly dependent on prior Bible knowledge. The chosen YouTube title is more concrete because it adds “Look at the Two Wounds”; it is not inherently dishonest. Whether the video delivers that examination remains unverified from this text.

7. **Verification credibility — “UK-G7 lint — plain ASCII” and “pre-checked, red-teamed and panel-reviewed.”** The copy contains “✝”, directly contradicting the ASCII claim. The supplied material also cannot establish sibling-title uniqueness, panel review, or video-to-metadata fidelity. These blanket assurances conceal unresolved checks.

The “Find Jesus in the whole Bible” CTA is present throughout. The displayed footer URLs are intact where included, although their destinations have not been verified.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Remove the survival contrast that obscures Christ’s actual death and resurrection.
2. Withdraw the “crushed skull” and “only one side survives” titles, and remove the accusation about viewers wanting a tie.
3. Reduce Instagram hashtags to five, resolve the long-form mismatch, and correct the unsupported “ALL PASS” claims.
