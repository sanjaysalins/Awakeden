### KJV verbatim vs. surrounding copy (honesty / bait-and-switch)

- **YouTube (Short) — DESCRIPTION**, line immediately above the quote: *“One side gets a bruise. The other gets a crushed skull.”* Then the KJV reads *“it shall **bruise** thy head, and thou shalt **bruise** his heel.”* Same verb both times in KJV. Metadata sells a **bruise vs. crush/skull** contrast the quoted verse does not state. That is not an altered quote, but it **is** a bait-and-switch for anyone who reads the description before watching: the “sharp” hook is modern gloss, not KJV diction.
- Same pattern repeats: *“A crushed head is not”* (YouTube), *“a crushed head”* (TikTok, Instagram), *“crushed head”* (Facebook caption). All platforms push **“crush/crushed”** while the only full verse uses **“bruise.”**
- **YouTube TAGS**: `crush the serpent` — common search phrase, but **not** Genesis 3:15 KJV language (the verse says *bruise*, not crush). SEO honesty flag: tag promises vocabulary the anchor verse does not use.
- **Alt title 2**: *“One Gets a Bruise. The Other Gets a Crushed Skull”* — same exaggeration relative to KJV; fine as unused alt but shows the kit’s default rhetorical move.

### Hooky but honest / doctrine

- **YouTube (Short) — DESCRIPTION** opener: *“You have heard that Genesis 3:15 means Jesus beats the devil.”* Colloquial typology, not a verse quote — acceptable if the short actually unpacks that; slightly **flattening** but not clearly false.
- **YouTube — DESCRIPTION**: *“A bruised heel is survivable.”* In-engine red-team already flagged this as gloss. For upload copy it still risks **understating the cross** (real death, then resurrection). Metadata presents “survivable” as plain fact beside a gospel claim — edgy for evangelical precision, even if typology on the *heel wound* is defensible.
- **YouTube — DESCRIPTION**: *“You keep hoping for a tie you can live with.”* Second-person motive assignment — not fear/gain-loss in the stock sense, but **accusatory** and unsupported in metadata (listener may not be “hoping for a tie”). Grace note (*“Christ already delivered the blow”*) is Christ-pointing but **declarative**, not an invitation.
- **Alt title 3**: *“The Promise Where Only One Side Survives”* — if ever used, **overclaims** vs. the text (both sides are wounded; asymmetry is outcome, not “only one survives” in a naive read). Not chosen, but weak alt in the kit.
