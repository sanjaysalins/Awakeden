# Independent review — claude (OK, 117s)

**Findings**

1. **Hook/body mismatch on the claim "You have heard that Genesis 3:15 means Jesus beats the devil. Look closer."** (YouTube description, first line). It frames the common reading as wrong, then the body only restates that reading ("Christ already delivered the blow"). The "look closer" tease overpromises a fresh insight. The only new point is that a heel bruise is survivable and a crushed head is not. That is honest, but it is a mild bait.

2. **Title "Is Not a Tie" attacks a straw man.** Almost nobody reads Gen 3:15 as a tie. The title and the "You have heard…" line invent an opposing view. Moderate clickbait, though not doctrinally false.

3. **"Look at the Two Wounds" is vague.** "Wounds" is a loose word for the heel and head. A global or ESL viewer may not get it. This breaks the project's simple-words rule.

4. **"A bruised heel that carried a cross"** (YouTube and long-form). This is a mixed metaphor. Heels do not carry crosses, and the Cross is a crushing, not a "bruise". It also glosses Gen 3:15 as if the heel-bruise equals the Crucifixion without saying so. Orthodox, but loose.

5. **"A crushed head that stays crushed."** This is a doctrinal overreach as stated. The text says "it shall bruise thy head", and KJV "bruise" is the same verb in both clauses. The video's "crushed skull" and "crushed head" is an interpretive gloss, and the description presents it as the text. Title option 2 ("Crushed Skull") and the first YouTube line repeat it. A reasonable reading, but the "bruise vs. crush" contrast leans on the English, while the Hebrew *shuph* is the same verb in both halves. That is a real accuracy risk for the "Look at the Two Wounds" premise. The red-team called this "a gloss, not text" and passed it too lightly.

6. **"The outcome was fixed before the fight began."** This is a sound claim, but "fixed" could read as fatalism or predestinarian framing. It is not a fear or works framing, so it is fine.

7. **"You keep hoping for a tie you can live with."** This is an accusatory, mildly guilt-framing line aimed at the viewer. It conflicts with the grace-anchored, no-manufactured-pressure rule. It also does not land on a grace invitation. The CTA is only "Subscribe to Awakeden", not an invitation to Jesus.

8. **CTA-to-Jesus is weak.** Every platform ends with "Find Jesus in the whole Bible… Follow Awakeden." That is a channel CTA, not a call to the viewer to trust or come to Christ. Brand rule G4 is passed on a technicality.

9. **The long-form block is a duplicate of the short.** The title, description and tags are identical, including "bible shorts" in the tags. That tag is wrong for a 16:9 long-form. Long-form framing is not differentiated.

10. **KJV quote.** Gen 3:15 is exact: "And I will put enmity between thee and the woman, and between thy seed and her seed; it shall bruise thy head, and thou shalt bruise his heel." Verified verbatim. The Facebook version drops the opening "(Genesis 3:15, KJV)" parenthetical but the verse is intact.

11. **Hashtags.** The YouTube short has 5, and the long-form has 3. Counts are sane, but the YouTube short should have #Shorts. Instagram has 8, including a templated #Faith. The tags are mostly real, but "crush the serpent" and "bruise his heel" are phrase-level tags with little search volume. "Protoevangelium" is fine but jargon.

12. **TikTok first line.** "Genesis 3:15 is not a tie." is a weak cold open for a general audience. A viewer who does not know the verse gets no stake. The Instagram opener has the same problem.

13. **Footer.** Facebook and YouTube carry a "Follow: YouTube: @awakeden" line on a YouTube description, which is redundant. The AI-disclosure line the house packaging rule asks for is absent from every platform.

14. **Title option 4, "Heel vs Head: The Fight Was Decided First"**, is the more honest and searchable title. The chosen one is more bait-leaning.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Remove or soften the "bruise vs. crush" contrast and "crushed head that stays crushed", because the KJV and the Hebrew use the same verb for both. Reframe it as "the heel is wounded, the head is a fatal blow" and mark it as the video's reading. Drop the straw-man "You have heard… Look closer" opener and the "tie" title, or use option 4.
2. Replace the accusing "You keep hoping for a tie you can live with" with a grace invitation, and give each platform a real CTA to Jesus ("Trust Him / Come to Him"), not just "Follow Awakeden".
3. Differentiate the long-form (remove "bible shorts", add #Shorts to the short), add the honest AI disclosure, and rewrite the TikTok and Instagram first lines so they have a stake for a newcomer.
