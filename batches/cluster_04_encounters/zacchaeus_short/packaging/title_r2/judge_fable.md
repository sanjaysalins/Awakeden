# Blind judge — seat: Fable

| pair | tap-newcomer | tap-Bible reader | honesty | one-line reason |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| P1 "Why Did Jesus Pick Him?" + "ME? / YES. YOU." | 9 | 6 | 8 | Five tiny words, a real question, and the thumb words match the two faces exactly (startled man, smiling Jesus). "Pick" says grace, not earning. |
| P2 "The Man Nobody Wanted Jesus to Visit" + "COME DOWN. / TODAY." | 7 | 8 | 9 | Best honest hook for people who know the story (the grumbling crowd), but the title is long and the thumb words mean little to a newcomer. |
| P3 "He Climbed a Tree to See Jesus" + "BUT WHO / FOUND WHO?" | 6 | 7 | 9 | Pays off the film's last line perfectly, but the title is a plain fact with no gap, and "who found who" is a hard read for second-language viewers. |
| P4 "Zacchaeus Climbed a Tree to See Jesus. Jesus Called Him by Name" + "HE CLIMBED A TREE. / JESUS FOUND HIM." | 3 | 4 | 9 | Tells the whole story before the tap. Too long (gets cut off), hard name first, and the thumb repeats the title. No gap left. |

## YOUR PICK

**P1** — "Why Did Jesus Pick Him?" + "ME? / YES. YOU."

Why: it is the only pair where title and thumbnail do two different jobs. The title asks the question. The thumbnail makes it personal ("you"). A newcomer understands every word in under a second. The viewer also reads "YES. YOU." as said to them, which is the film's real point.

Runner-up: P2 (stronger for Bible readers, weaker on a fast scroll).

## One small word fix

None needed in the title. Keep "Pick" — it is the simplest word and it signals that Jesus chose first.

(If any fix is made at all: do not change "Pick" to "Choose" or "Call"; both are weaker or less clear for second-language viewers.)

## The one biggest risk

**The "why" must be answered out loud, in plain words.** The title promises a reason. The film's answer is in KJV wording ("to seek and to save that which was lost"). A newcomer may not hear that as the answer to "why him?". If the plain line "Zacchaeus came to look. Jesus came to find him" does not land clearly as the answer, the title feels like bait.

Smaller risk, same pair: "YES. YOU." puts words in Jesus's mouth that are not in the Bible text. It reads as a poster line, not a quote, but a strict Bible reader may object. Do not put quote marks around it.
