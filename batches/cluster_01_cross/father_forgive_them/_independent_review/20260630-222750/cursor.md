If the channel rule is “every word inside quotation marks = exact KJV,” either drop the inner quotes or quote the full KJV clause.

### Doctrinal soundness

**What holds:**

- *"'They know not what they do' does not excuse the sin; it intercedes for the sinner"* — sound; avoids the ignorance-as-innocence trap.
- *"while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us"* — correct Pauline atonement anchor (v1’s main error is fixed).
- *"still lives to make intercession for sinners"* — orthodox (Hebrews 7:25 / Romans 8:34 territory), appropriately framed as ongoing ministry, not a swap for the cross.
- Lands on Christ at the cross; no contrarian reading; grace tone, not fear/works.

**Problems:**

1. **"them" still over-specified.** Luke’s **"them"** is whoever is implicated in the crucifixion scene (parting raiment, casting lots, crucifying) — not textually limited to *"the soldiers beneath him."* Same narrowing problem as v1, softened not removed.

2. **Landing over-extends Luke’s prayer.** *"the forgiveness he asked of the Father is yours to receive"* — grammatically, he asked the Father to forgive **them**, not the listener. Homiletical application is normal, but on first hearing it can sound like Jesus’ specific prayer for his executioners was directly **for you**, without a bridge. That revives the v1 **them → us** problem at the **close**, not just the middle.

3. **Intercession vs. offer still slightly blurred.** Earlier the script correctly has Jesus **asking the Father**. The landing treats that prayer as something **yours to receive** — which collapses *intercession* (Son → Father) into *offer to sinner* without one line on how the Father answers through the cross. A zero-Bible listener may not know **who** forgives or **when** the prayer “lands.”

4. **"This is the gospel:"** labels a Romans 5:8 death-summary while the piece’s fresh spine is **intercession while being wronged**. Both are true; stacked without attribution, a listener may think Luke’s moment **is** “Christ died for us” rather than “Christ prayed for mercy mid-crucifixion.” Not heresy — imprecise gospel framing.

### Clarity on first hearing

1. **Chronology slip:** *"While they are still crucifying him"* — Luke places the saying **after** *"there they crucified him"* (23:33), in the same breath as parting raiment (23:34). On a plain hearing, *"still crucifying"* suggests nails still going in **during** the prayer. The text has him **on** the cross, being wronged, while garments are divided — not an ongoing nailing scene.

2. **"And we were not just watching that crowd"** — **"watching"** is a metaphor for moral distance, but a listener with zero Bible background may hear it as “we were there too,” then wonder how. The next line (*"our sin put him there"*) helps, but the jump still feels like a logic leap without one concrete link (human sin crucified Christ / representative guilt).

3. **Self-contradiction risk:** *"The hands being nailed down are the hands still reaching for you"* — poetically intentional, but on first pass **nailed** and **reaching** fight each other. The text’s actual force is **intercession from fixed hands**, not motion toward the listener. Sentiment over text.

### Grace-anchored conviction

Mostly clean: *"Come to the cross"* invites; no fear, gain-loss, or works merit.

**Residual concern:** *"yours to receive, by faith in him"* — orthodox, and it fixes v1’s “already asking” automatic-forgiveness vibe. But if **faith-conditioning** meant “don’t end on a doctrinal gate,” this still tags the invitation with a condition at the last beat. Not works-framing, but less purely invitational than the channel ideal.

### Thread spine / landing / hook

- **Hook (first ~5s):** Strong — *"Nails through his hands… not a curse"* grips quickly. ✅
- **Thread:** Mostly one spine — unexpected mercy-prayer amid crucifixion → ignorance ≠ excuse → our guilt → ongoing intercession → atonement → invitation. ✅ with minor wobble when Rom 5:8 death-summary briefly outruns the intercession thread.
- **Landing does NEW work?** **Weak.** *"Come to the cross"* + *"by faith in him"* is familiar Shorts DNA. The freshest move — intercession **in the same breath** as garment-lots — is under-used at the close. The landing doesn’t cash the “he prays **before** they understand” insight; it pivots to generic receive-at-cross language.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Stop narrowing Luke’s **"them"** to soldiers only — use crucifixion-scene language Luke actually supports (*those who crucified him / parted his raiment*), and bridge **them → us** before *"the forgiveness… is yours."*
2. Fix chronology: replace *"While they are still crucifying him"* with language that matches Luke 23:33–34 (on the cross, garments divided in the same breath as the prayer).
3. Rebuild the landing around Luke’s intercession insight (mercy pleaded **while** being wronged, hands fixed on the cross) — not *"hands still reaching for you"* + generic *"Come to the cross"*; keep Rom 5:8 death and faith reception, but don’t let them erase the text’s distinctive beat.
