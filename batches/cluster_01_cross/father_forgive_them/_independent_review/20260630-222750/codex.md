# Independent review — codex (OK, 23s)

Findings:

- **Still over-narrows “them” to soldiers.** The line “Jesus prays for the soldiers beneath him” resolves the referent too tightly. Luke’s immediate scene includes soldiers dividing garments, but the wider “them” is not explicitly limited to soldiers. This repeats the prior “nail-drivers” problem in softer form.

- **The “them -> us” jump is improved but still abrupt.** “And we were not just watching that crowd — our sin put him there” is orthodox, but it moves from historical intercession for “them” to universal human guilt without showing the bridge. A zero-Bible listener may not understand why “our sin” is now in view.

- **KJV quotation appears accurate.** “Then said Jesus, Father, forgive them; for they know not what they do. And they parted his raiment, and cast lots.” matches KJV Luke 23:34. “while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us” also matches the KJV wording from Romans 5:8, though it is unreferenced.

- **Possible KJV-verbatim issue inside quotation marks:** “They know not what they do” is an exact word sequence, but the KJV sentence has “for they know not what they do,” with lowercase “they” in context. If your rule is strict presentation, avoid quotation marks or quote the full phrase.

- **Grace-anchored landing is mostly sound, but “hands still reaching for you” risks sentimental imagery over textual depth.** The text’s force is Christ interceding while being sinned against. “Reaching for you” is not doctrinally false, but it is less text-grounded than “still lives to make intercession for sinners,” which is stronger.

- **The landing partly repeats a familiar CTA.** “Come to the cross” and “yours to receive, by faith in him” are sound, but the final beat does not do much new work with Luke 23:34 specifically. The stronger fresh landing is the intercession angle: the crucified Christ does not wait for sinners to understand before He pleads mercy.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Stop limiting “them” to “the soldiers”; say “those beneath him” or “the sinners around him” unless you explicitly frame soldiers as the immediate visible example.
2. Add one clear bridge from their sin to ours so “our sin put him there” does not feel like a sudden logic jump.
3. Make the landing lean harder on Christ’s intercession from the text, not the generic “Come to the cross” close.
