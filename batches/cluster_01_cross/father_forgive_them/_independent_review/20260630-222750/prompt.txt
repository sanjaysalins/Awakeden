Nails through his hands. Soldiers at his feet, gambling for his clothes. And from the cross comes a word no one expected — not a curse.

It is a prayer. While they are still crucifying him, Jesus prays for the soldiers beneath him.

Father, forgive them; for they know not what they do.

Luke records the moment exactly.

Then said Jesus, Father, forgive them; for they know not what they do. And they parted his raiment, and cast lots.

He asks the Father to forgive them in the same breath they divide his clothing. "They know not what they do" does not excuse the sin; it intercedes for the sinner.

And we were not just watching that crowd — our sin put him there, and he gave himself willingly. The same Lord who prayed for his executioners still lives to make intercession for sinners.

This is the gospel: while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us. The hands being nailed down are the hands still reaching for you. Come to the cross — and the forgiveness he asked of the Father is yours to receive, by faith in him.
