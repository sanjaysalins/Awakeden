### Doctrinal soundness & biblical grounding

**1. Ungrounded leap: “he was already praying for us”**

> *“And before we ever turned to him, he was already praying for us.”*

Luke 23:34’s **“them”** is the crucifixion crowd/soldiers in that moment — not stated as a direct prayer for every later hearer. The move from *them* → *us* is standard homiletical application, but here it is asserted with no bridge: no Romans 5:8 citation, no “representatives of humanity,” no Acts 2 / corporate guilt link. On your grounding standard, this line **over-claims what Luke says** and asks the listener to supply the theology.

**2. Gospel-heart misstatement: “while we were yet sinners, Christ interceded”**

> *“This is the heart of the gospel: while we were yet sinners, Christ interceded.”*

This clearly echoes Romans 5:8 KJV — *“while we were yet sinners, Christ **died for us**.”* Swapping **died for us** → **interceded** is not a harmless synonym swap:

- Luke 23:34 **is** intercession (a prayer to the Father).
- Paul’s “heart of the gospel” in Romans 5:8 is **atoning death**, not intercessory speech.

So the script **imports Romans’ phrase but changes Paul’s claim**, then labels that altered claim “the heart of the gospel.” That blurs death and prayer and weakens the atonement anchor at the landing. Intercession is true of Christ (Rom 8:34; Heb 7:25), but it is **not** what Romans 5:8 says, and it is **not** a safe substitute for “died for us” when you invoke that line.

**3. Over-specified actors: “the very men driving the nails”**

> *“Jesus prays for the very men driving the nails.”*

Luke ties the saying to those who **part his raiment and cast lots** in the same verse — not explicitly to nail-drivers only. Crucifixion involved multiple soldiers/roles; the text does not isolate “the men with the hammer.” Plausible dramatization, but **not what Luke says**; safer: “those crucifying him” / “the soldiers beneath him.”

**4. Mild imprecision: “forgiveness offered” vs “pleads for the Father to forgive”**

Earlier the script correctly says Jesus **pleads to the Father** (*“He pleads for the Father to forgive them”*). Later: *“forgiveness offered in the very act of being wronged”* and *“receive the forgiveness he was already asking for.”* Orthodox reception is through the Father’s answer to the Son’s work, received by faith — not Jesus unilaterally “offering” absolution on the spot. The landing (*“Come to the cross, and receive…”*) saves this somewhat, but the middle still flattens **intercession** into **already-available offer**, which can confuse a zero-Bible listener about who forgives and how.

**5. What holds up**

- *“They know not what they do” does not excuse the sin; it intercedes for the sinner* — sound; avoids the “ignorance = innocence” trap.
- *“our sin held him there”* — orthodox substitution/culpability framing (Isa 53; Acts 2:23 territory).
- Grace tone: invitation (*“Come to the cross, and receive…”*), not fear/works — acceptable.
- Lands on Christ at the cross — yes.

**6. Not flagged (KJV-channel scope)**

Luke 23:34’s textual witness is disputed in critical editions; irrelevant for a KJV-only channel that treats the verse as canonical.

---

### Summary

KJV quotes pass. Core evangelical direction is fine. The real problems are **biblical grounding** (Luke’s *them* → *us* without support) and **doctrinal precision** (Romans 5:8 echo with *interceded* replacing *died for us* at the gospel landing).

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Ground “praying for us” — bridge Luke’s “them” to the listener via Romans 5:8 / corporate sin (or soften to “the kind of prayer he still makes for sinners” with Heb 7:25), not a direct Luke-to-me jump.
2. Fix the gospel landing: keep Romans 5:8’s **“Christ died for us”** (or quote it KJV) and treat Luke 23:34 as intercession *at* the cross, not as a swap for atoning death.
3. Replace “the very men driving the nails” with language Luke actually supports (crucifiers / soldiers beneath him, raiment/lots in the same breath).
