Nails through his hands. Soldiers at his feet, gambling for his clothes. And from the cross comes a word no one expected — not a curse.

It is a prayer. While they are still hurting him, Jesus prays for the very men driving the nails.

Father, forgive them; for they know not what they do.

Luke tells us exactly when he said it.

Then said Jesus, Father, forgive them; for they know not what they do. And they parted his raiment, and cast lots.

He pleads for the Father to forgive them in the same breath they are dividing his clothing — forgiveness offered in the very act of being wronged. "They know not what they do" does not excuse the sin; it intercedes for the sinner.

We were not just watching that crowd — our sin held him there. And before we ever turned to him, he was already praying for us.

This is the heart of the gospel: while we were yet sinners, Christ interceded. The hands being nailed down are the hands still reaching for you. Come to the cross, and receive the forgiveness he was already asking for.
