## Independent adversarial review — Luke 23:34 narration (v4)

### Doctrinal soundness
- **Mostly sound and mainstream evangelical.** Prayer at the cross, ignorance language as intercession (not excuse), substitution (“the sin that put him there was ours too”), ongoing intercession (Heb 7:25 echo), willing self-giving — all orthodox if you already accept the text.
- **Narrow “them.”** You tie the prayer to “the very people putting him to death” and soldiers gambling. Luke’s “them” is debated (soldiers, leaders, crowd). You don’t overclaim, but you also don’t flag that the prayer may reach wider than the dice-players. Not heresy — just a compressed read a hostile listener could call too neat.
- **Landing theology is safe:** “mercy … held out … receive it by faith” avoids universalism and avoids works — invitation, not bargain.

### KJV verbatim (quoted material only)
- **`[jesus] Father, forgive them; for they know not what they do.`** — Matches KJV Luke 23:34 (incl. semicolon). **PASS.**
- **`[scripture] And they parted his raiment, and cast lots.`** — Matches KJV (same pericope). **PASS.**
- **Narrator: `"They know not what they do"`** — Exact KJV substring. **PASS.**
- **Not in quotes:** `while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us` — Rom 5:8 wording without attribution. **Not a KJV violation** per your rule (quotes only), but see clarity below.

### Clarity on first hearing (zero Bible background)
- **Strong open:** nails, soldiers, clothes, “not a curse” — concrete, followable.
- **Weaker jump at:** `And the sin that put him there was ours too.` A stranger just heard about Roman soldiers; “ours” assumes substitution without one plain beat (“not only their nails — the rebellion that required this cross”).
- **Compressed proof logic:** `"They know not what they do" does not excuse the sin; it intercedes for the sinner.` Correct for church ears; first-time listeners may hear “does not excuse” and “intercedes” as tension without a half-second “he’s asking the Father for them, not saying they’re innocent.”
- **Timeline mash:** `the Lord who prayed for his executioners still lives to make intercession for sinners` blends **that moment** with **now** (Heb 7:25). Faithful; a cold listener may not know two eras are being stitched.

### Grace-anchored conviction
- **No fear/gain-loss/works sell.** Conviction is observational + invitation (`come, and receive it by faith`). **PASS.**
- **`This is the gospel:`** — Slightly pulpit-label; not fear-based, but it tells rather than shows after a strong visual hook.

### Freshness = faithful depth
- **Fresh anchor is textual:** gambling for raiment + unexpected prayer — not clickbait contrarianism. **PASS.**
- **`word no one expected`** — Mild drama; not a doctrine problem, slightly unearned for skeptics (some victims did curse). Hook still works visually.

### One thread (hook → middle → CTA)
- **Single spine:** cruelty at the cross → prayer for killers → Luke/raiment → ignorance as intercession → our sin → living intercessor → gospel mercy offered. **No thread swap.** **PASS.**

### Hook score (1–10): **8**
- Opens with **image + tension** (`Nails through his hands` / gambling / `not a curse`), not a template “Did you know / Imagine / What if.”
- Would stop many scrollers in ~5s; not a 9–10 only because **“no one expected”** is assertive without a ear-hook sound bite from Jesus in the first line.

### Landing score (1–10): **5** (stock-closer cap)
- **Strong, piece-owned middle:** `pleading mercy before we knew we needed it` — tied to the cross prayer; hard to paste onto a random topic.
- **Final sentence fails the swap test:** `That mercy is held out to you now: come, and receive it by faith.` — Could close **many** mercy/grace shorts unchanged; “come … receive … by faith” is generic CTA family (same cap logic as “come to Jesus” / “turn to Him”). Per your rule: **under 8 → REVISE.**
