Nails through his hands. Soldiers at his feet, gambling for his clothes. And from the cross comes a word no one expected — not a curse.

It is a prayer. As they nail him up and divide his clothes, Jesus prays for the very people putting him to death.

Father, forgive them; for they know not what they do.

Luke records the moment exactly.

Then said Jesus, Father, forgive them; for they know not what they do. And they parted his raiment, and cast lots.

He asks the Father to forgive them in the same breath they divide his clothing. "They know not what they do" does not excuse the sin; it intercedes for the sinner.

And the sin that held him there was not only theirs. It was ours — and he gave himself willingly for it. The same Lord who prayed for his executioners still lives to make intercession for sinners.

This is the gospel: while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us — pleading mercy before we ever knew we needed it. That same mercy is held out to you now. Come to the cross, and receive it by faith in him.
