## Findings (by lens)

### Doctrinal soundness
No invented doctrine or contrarian readings detected. The script correctly presents Luke 23:34 as **intercession**, not absolution or excuse, and explicitly blocks the common misread:

> **"They know not what they do" does not excuse the sin; it intercedes for the sinner.**

That is orthodox and faithful to the text’s function (Jesus asking the Father, not unilaterally declaring forgiveness).

The **them → us** bridge is standard substitutionary framing, not a swap of who Luke’s “them” refers to in the prayer itself:

> **And the sin that held him there was not only theirs. It was ours — and he gave himself willingly for it.**

Pairing “held him there” with **“he gave himself willingly”** avoids the error of making sin overpower the Son against his will.

**Hebrews intercession line** — minor precision, not heresy:

> **The same Lord who prayed for his executioners still lives to make intercession for sinners.**

Hebrews 7:25 (KJV) has ongoing intercession **for those who come unto God by him** (“for them”), not generically for all sinners irrespective of faith. In context, your landing (**“receive it by faith in him”**) keeps this evangelical, but the middle line slightly over-broadens Hebrews on its own. Not a blocking doctrinal error for a 60s short; worth knowing.

Luke 23:34a as canonical: accepted per brief; not flagged.

“Pleading mercy” grammatically rides the em-dash off Rom 5:8, but the **pleading** image comes from Luke’s prayer **for them** (executioners). A careful first-time listener may briefly wonder *who* was being pleaded for before the faith landing clarifies reception. Thread holds; wording is slightly ambiguous for ~1 beat.
