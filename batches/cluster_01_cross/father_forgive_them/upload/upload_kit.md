The first recorded words of Jesus on the cross are not a cry of pain and not a curse on his executioners. They are a prayer for them.

"Then said Jesus, Father, forgive them; for they know not what they do. And they parted his raiment, and cast lots" (Luke 23:34, KJV).

Notice Luke's timing: the prayer rises while the soldiers divide his clothes and gamble beneath him. Jesus intercedes for the very men driving the nails, in the very moment of the driving.

"They know not what they do" does not excuse the sin; it intercedes for the sinner. There is a difference, and the difference is the gospel. The sin that put him on that cross was ours too, and he gave himself willingly. The Lord who prayed for his executioners did not stop when he rose: he ever lives to make intercession for those who come to God by him.

In this piece: the first Word from the cross in Luke's own sequence, what the prayer does and does not say, and the mercy that was pleading for you before you knew you needed it.
