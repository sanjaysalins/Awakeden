# Independent review — codex (OK, 28s)

Hook score: 7/10. “The first time the Bible says finished, nothing was broken yet” is strong and text-rooted, but it needs Bible-literacy to feel the Genesis/John connection immediately.

Landing score: 6/10. “Enter his rest” is connected to the piece, but the final sentence “He finished the work. Enter his rest” is still close to a stock evangelical closer and imports Hebrews language without naming or developing it.

Findings:

- KJV verbatim: no altered wording found in the three quoted Scripture lines. Genesis 2:1, John 17:4, and John 19:30 appear exact KJV.

- Doctrinal risk: “Two finished works. One Workman. He finished creation, and rested.” This is orthodox if grounded in Christ as Creator, but the narration has not established that from the quoted texts. Genesis 2:1 says “the heavens and the earth were finished,” not explicitly “the Son finished creation.” Without John 1/Colossians 1 support, “One Workman” may feel like a clever doctrinal leap.

- Clarity issue: “So when sin wrecked that world, the Son came down to work again.” A zero-knowledge listener may not know who “the Son” is, what “came down” means, or why creation/rest connects to crucifixion/tomb. The theological compression is high.

- Thread strain: “He finished redemption, and rested in the tomb through the sabbath.” The creation/rest rhyme is good, but “rested in the tomb” risks sounding like the tomb itself is the redemptive rest, when the saving work is His death, and resurrection vindicates it. This needs more precision.

- Grace framing is mostly strong: “You were never asked to finish it. You are asked to rest in it.” That avoids works framing well. But “You are asked” could be softened into gospel invitation; as written, it still sounds like a requirement.

- Best line: “He bowed his head so you could lift yours.” It is vivid and built from John 19:30. The closing should probably land on that image instead of moving to the more generic “Enter his rest.”

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Ground “One Workman” more clearly so the creation-to-cross claim does not feel like an unsupported leap.
2. Clarify “the Son came down to work again” for listeners with no Bible background.
3. Rebuild the final CTA from “bowed his head / lift yours” instead of ending on the more generic “Enter his rest.”
