### Doctrinal soundness — flags

**1. “He finished creation” compresses two different subjects into one.**

Gen 2:1 is read in the **scripture** voice with no speaker named. The next narrator line says **“God rested on the seventh day.”** Eight lines later: **“He finished creation, and rested.”** By then **“the Son came down”** is the last named actor, so **“He”** reads as Jesus finishing Genesis creation. That is Trinitarian inference, not what Gen 2:1 says on its face. A careful listener can hear **modalist collapse** or **Son-only creation** — neither is intended, but the line causes it.

**2. “rested in the tomb through the sabbath” mixes categories.**

God’s seventh-day rest (Gen 2) is not the same thing as a dead body in a tomb. Typology (Hebrews 4) can bridge that, but the script never signals typology. On first hearing, **“rested in the tomb”** sounds like death = divine rest. That is soft category error, not heresy — still a real problem for clarity and doctrine.

**3. John 17:4 is the weak middle pillar.**

Jesus says **“I have finished the work which thou gavest me to do”** the night *before* the cross. Redemption is not finished until **“It is finished.”** The band-aid **“Then he carried it to the end”** is one short line; it does not resolve the contradiction for anyone listening linearly. You build a three-beat **finished → finished → finished** arc; the middle beat undercuts the arc’s logic.

**4. Hook claim is narrow but defensible.**

**“The first time the Bible says finished”** — in KJV canonical order, Gen 2:1 is the first occurrence of “finished.” Fine for KJV shorts. Just note: Hebrew *kalah* and Greek *tetelestai* are not the same word; the rhyme is English-concordance depth, not original-language word study. Acceptable for this format; do not oversell as linguistic proof.

### Clarity on first hearing — flags

**1. “nothing was broken yet”** — broken *what*? Sin is not named until the next beat (**“sin wrecked that world”**). A zero-Bible listener hears a riddle in line 1.

**2. “Two finished works. One Workman.”** — Which two? Creation and redemption are named only *after* this punch line. The structure is clever; the order is backwards for a 60s short.

**3. “Enter his rest.”** — Rest from what? Rest how? No prior line defines **“his rest”** as Sabbath, completed work, or Christ. The thread is there for Bible-literate listeners; it is abstract for the stated zero-knowledge audience.

**4. “He bowed his head so you could lift yours.”** — Strong image, tied to John 19:30. But it is *not* the closing line; the abstract **“Enter his rest”** follows and dilutes it.

### LANDING SCORE: **6/10**

**“Salvation is not a job half done. He finished the work. Enter his rest.”** — The final sentence **“Enter his rest.”** could close any Hebrews-4 / Sabbath-rest piece. It is thread-adjacent, not thread-locked to **“It is finished”** or **“he bowed his head.”** The stronger, piece-specific image (**“He bowed his head so you could lift yours”**) is penultimate, not the closer. Under the “could this exact final sentence end a different piece?” test: yes.
