# Independent review — claude (OK, 51s)

**KJV verbatim check — all four quotes verified exact:**

- Gen 2:1 "Thus the heavens and the earth were finished, and all the host of them." — exact.
- John 1:3a "All things were made by him" — exact, clean clause boundary (cut at the semicolon).
- John 17:4 "I have glorified thee on the earth: I have finished the work which thou gavest me to do." — exact.
- John 19:30 "When Jesus therefore had received the vinegar, he said, It is finished: and he bowed his head, and gave up the ghost." — exact, full verse.

**Factual/doctrinal probes (I tried to break these):**

- "The first time the Bible says finished" — checked: Genesis 2:1 is indeed the first KJV occurrence of "finished." The hook's factual claim holds.
- Attributing John 1:3 ("him" = the Word) to Jesus is orthodox via John 1:14 — fine.
- "Hours before the cross, he prayed" — John 17 is the same night; "hours" is accurate (the earlier "before" ambiguity was fixed per the note).
- "He finished redemption, and rested in the tomb through the sabbath" — this is the one soft spot. That his body lay in the tomb over the Sabbath is narrative fact; "rested" is an interpretive verb Scripture never applies to it. It's a classic, widely-preached parallel and I don't judge it invented doctrine — but it is an inference presented with the same confidence as the quoted verses. Advisory, not blocking.
- Conviction is grace-anchored: "You were never asked to finish it. You are asked to rest in it" — no fear, no gain/loss, pure Hebrews-4 invitation. Sound.

**Thread:** one spine, no swap — *finished → rest* runs hook (creation finished, God rested) → middle (redemption finished) → CTA (enter his rest). The two-finishes symmetry is genuinely fresh about the TEXT while the claim stays orthodox.

**Clarity on first hearing:** clean. One wobble: "Two finished works. One Workman." — "Workman" is archaic and slightly odd aloud; a zero-Bible listener parses it, but "the same hands" (already set up by "The hands that finished creation") would land smoother.

**HOOK — 8/10.** "The first time the Bible says finished, nothing was broken yet." Non-template, specific, opens a real curiosity gap, and it's factually true. Stops the scroll.

**LANDING — 8/10.** "He bowed his head so you could lift yours. The work is finished. Enter his rest." Built from this piece's own images (bowed head from the quoted verse itself; rest from the Genesis/tomb spine). "Enter his rest" is Hebrews-4 language, not the stock "come to Jesus" family, and it could not end a piece that hadn't built the rest thread. Earned.

VERDICT: PASS
TOP FIXES:
1. Soften "rested in the tomb through the sabbath" toward the narrative fact (e.g. "and his body lay at rest in the tomb through the sabbath") so the inference isn't voiced with quoted-verse confidence.
2. Replace "One Workman" with "the same hands" (or similar) — smoother on first hearing and it reuses the line already planted two beats earlier.
3. None blocking — optionally tighten "the Son came down to mend it" ("mend" undersells redemption; "buy it back" or "make it new" is stronger and still plain).
