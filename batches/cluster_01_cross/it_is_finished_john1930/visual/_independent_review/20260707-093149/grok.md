# Independent review — grok (OK, 119s)

**Problems identified:**

**1. Non-biblical material injected into the "biblical" fact sheet and panel questions.**  
Panel question 3 states: "does biblical hyssop (a small-leaved herb, likely Syrian marjoram) make our branch inaccurate?"  
No KJV verse in the artifact (or anywhere in Scripture) describes hyssop's leaf size, growth habit, or species. This violates the project's own rule ("everything else ... must be Scripture-driven") and the review criteria ("NO INVENTED FACTS", "HISTORY STAYS SECONDARY"). The fact sheet is using extra-biblical botany to potentially judge the image.

**2. S3 is placed in SPECIFIED with a citation that does not explicitly state the visual fact at the depicted moment.**  
S3: "Jesus wore a crown of thorns."  
> John 19:2 (KJV): "And the soldiers platted a crown of thorns, and put it on his head..."

The cited verse describes the crowning before Pilate. The artifact's own depiction section concerns the cross at the vinegar offer (John 19:29-30). No verse quoted or referenced states that the crown remained on his head during the crucifixion or at the moment the sponge was put to his mouth. This is inference, not "Bible explicitly states this visual fact." It belongs in CONSTRAINED.

**3. The "Depiction being judged" section asserts multiple concrete visual details that are neither classified in any bucket nor supported by the cited Scripture.**  
- "(near hand nailed through the palm)" — The Bible never says "palm." John 20:25 (not cited) refers only to "the print of the nails" in "his hands." This is a non-trivial, contested extra-biblical claim about the action of crucifixion. No fact guards it.
- "Dark storm sky" — C3 only supports "there was a darkness over all the earth" (Luke 23:44). "Storm" (weather implication) is not in the text.
- "pale porous sea-sponge" — The verses say only "spunge." "Sea" and "porous" are not stated.
- "pressing the sponge gently to Christ's lips" — John 19:29 says "put it to his mouth." "Gently" and "lips" (vs. mouth) are softening not present in the text.

These appear in the description of what the image actually shows, yet they sit outside the SPECIFIED/CONSTRAINED/FREE structure.

**4. The vessel is quoted but not guarded.**  
John 19:29 (in S1): "Now there was set a vessel full of vinegar: and they filled a spunge with vinegar..."  
The depiction description mentions only the soldier reaching up the branch with the already-soaked sponge. No fact requires the vessel to be visible or present in the scene. A painting could easily omit the source vessel entirely with no SPECIFIED fact to catch it.

**5. C1 and C2 analysis contains interpretive over-reach presented as grounding.**  
C1 claims: "the nearest accounts say 'one of them' — i.e. one of the bystanders standing there who were mocking; Luke separately has soldiers offering vinegar during the earlier mocking."  
The Matt 27:47-48 and Mark 15:36 citations support "one of them," but the text does not explicitly label that person a non-soldier "bystander" or definitively separate Luke's mention as "earlier mocking" distinct from the sponge event. This is harmonization, not a direct statement from the quoted verses. The sheet correctly puts the "who" question in CONSTRAINED, but the surrounding gloss weakens the rigor.

**6. S2 placement and scope.**  
S2 ("Jesus received the vinegar, then said, It is finished") is listed under SPECIFIED. The verse is accurate, but this is the *subsequent* action and words, not a visual fact of the sponge-offering moment captured in the still ("pressing the sponge... to Christ's lips"). The slug is `vinegar_sponge_reed`. The fact conflates the offering action with its immediate aftermath.

**7. Coverage gaps on checkable details the image could get wrong.**  
- No fact addresses the vessel "set" with vinegar (quoted but not required to appear).
- No fact addresses how the sponge is attached ("put it upon hyssop" / "bearing a... sponge") or reachability (the well-known tension between a short hyssop plant and a crucified man's mouth is acknowledged in C2 but not turned into a concrete visual constraint).
- No fact notes that the Synoptic accounts tie the sponge offer to the "Elias" cry and mocking words ("Let alone; let us see whether Elias will come"), while John does not. Since the shorts quote John, the absence of mocking attitude is defensible, but it is not explicitly guarded.
- Nail placement through the palm (see #3) and "storm" sky are left unclassified.

**8. Minor citation looseness in S1.**  
S1: "A sponge filled with vinegar was put upon hyssop and put to His mouth."  
The verse says "they filled a spunge with vinegar, and put it upon hyssop, and put it to his mouth." The claim is close but slightly smooths the sequence ("filled a spunge... and put it") and drops the vessel that precedes the filling. Acceptable for summary, but under "CITATION TRUTH" it is not verbatim support.

The core citations for the sponge + vinegar + hyssop action (John) and the darkness are solid. The Christ-lens is not obviously distorted. However, the artifact fails its own standards on bucket honesty, invented/non-scriptural details in the review questions, and uncovered specifics in the very depiction it is supposed to police.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Remove the entire non-scriptural botanical claim ("small-leaved herb, likely Syrian marjoram") and any similar extra-biblical plant identification from the panel questions and criteria; judge hyssop solely by the words "hyssop" in the cited John 19:29 text.
2. Move S3 to CONSTRAINED (or add an explicit note) because John 19:2 describes the earlier crowning, not the state of the head at the cross during the vinegar offer; the text never explicitly states the crown remained on at that moment.
3. Add a new CONSTRAINED fact requiring or noting the visible "vessel full of vinegar" that "was set" (John 19:29) and classify or explicitly mark as free the depiction's current unbucketed claims (nail "through the palm", "Dark storm sky", "sea-sponge", "gently", "lips"); remove or qualify those details in the depiction description to match the quoted text exactly.
