## Real problems

**1. The image contradicts the one thing S1 actually specifies: "upon hyssop."**
S1 pins *hyssop* as a SPECIFIED visual fact, but the depiction (and Question 3) renders it as a **"large laurel-like leafy branch."** Biblical hyssop (Ex 12:22; 1 Kings 4:33) is a small aromatic herb (Syrian marjoram / *Origanum*), not a broad laurel bough. So the rendered plant fails the very fact the sheet marks as mandatory-match. This is currently soft-pedalled as a "question for the panel" — it should be a flagged **constraint the image is violating**, not an open question.

**2. Missing Christ-lens / typology — the whole reason John writes "hyssop."**
John deliberately swaps the Synoptics' *reed* for **hyssop**, the Passover plant used to strike the lamb's blood on the doorposts (Ex 12:22) — Christ the Passover Lamb, dying as the lambs are slain. The sheet never notes this. Because the image renders the plant as an unrecognizable laurel branch, that intended Passover typology is destroyed on screen and nothing in the fact sheet guards it. For a "whole Bible through Jesus" project this is a substantive coverage gap, and it compounds problem #1.

**3. "Dark storm sky" imports weather the text does not state.**
C3 cites the darkness (Luke 23:44), but Luke 23:45 attributes it to the sun's light failing, and Matt 27:51 gives an earthquake — **not** a thunderstorm. The depiction's "Dark **storm** sky" adds meteorological weather Scripture never mentions. Minor, but it's licence dressed near a specified fact; the constraint should read "darkness (failed sunlight), not a storm."

**4. S3 cites the purple robe without guarding against it appearing at the cross.**
S3 quotes John 19:2 including "they put on him a purple robe." By crucifixion the robe was removed and his own garments restored, then parted (Matt 27:31; John 19:23-24). Nothing in the sheet flags "NO purple robe at the cross." A painter could wrongly clothe Christ in purple and no fact would catch it. Add a constrained fact.

## Minor
- Depiction "presses the sponge **gently**" softens the Synoptic framing, where the offer carries a taunt ("let us see whether Elias will come," Mark 15:36). John doesn't frame it as mockery, so this is defensible — but worth noting the gesture is not clearly tender in the text.
- "hand nailed through the palm" — palm vs wrist is fair licence; hands *are* specified (John 20:25). No change needed.

The citations are sound and the buckets are mostly honest, but the SPECIFIED "hyssop" is actively contradicted by the rendered laurel branch, and the Passover-lamb typology that motivates John's word choice is absent. That's enough to send back.
