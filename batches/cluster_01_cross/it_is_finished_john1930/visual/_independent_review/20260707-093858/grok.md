# Independent review — grok (OK, 98s)

**Citation truth failures (core criterion):**

- **C1 exact phrase:** "*(No species/leaf-size is claimed as biblical — only "a bunch" and "small," which the verses state.)*"  
  1 Kings 4:33 does not state "small." It says "the hyssop that springeth out of the wall." Exodus 12:22 states "a bunch of hyssop" but for the Passover blood ritual, not the crucifixion. John 19:29 (the actual event) says only "put it upon hyssop" — no "bunch," no "small." This is a direct false claim about what the cited verses state.

- **S4 wording vs. citation:** Listed under SPECIFIED. Depiction text: "The sky is in **deep unnatural darkness (the sun blotted out)**."  
  Luke 23:44-45 says "there was a darkness over all the earth" and "the sun was darkened." "Unnatural" and "blotted out" are added interpretation not present in the verse. The fact is also taken from the Synoptics while the scene is defined by John 19:28-30 (which never mentions darkness).

- **C3 phrasing:** "Christ still bore the crown of thorns" presented as a CONSTRAINED fact.  
  John 19:2 only records it was placed on his head earlier. No verse states it remained on at the moment of the vinegar. "Still bore" is an assumption, not what the text states.

**Bucket honesty failures:**

- C1 puts "Hyssop is a SMALL plant, taken as a BUNCH (not a large branch/bough)" in CONSTRAINED and the depiction hard-codes "**small handheld bunch of hyssop**."  
  The Bible never states the size or "bunch" form for this event. "Bunch" is imported from Exodus for typology. This is licence/interpretation presented as a constraint the image must honour. The Christ-lens note then doubles down: "the plant must stay identifiable as a small hyssop bunch for the typology to read." Typology preference is being used to dictate visual specifics instead of explicit statement.

- S4 (darkness) is placed in SPECIFIED for a John-defined still. Parallel-gospel details that are not recorded in the governing text do not belong in the "Bible explicitly states" bucket for this scene.

- Depiction description asserts details with no bucket assignment at all:  
  "a man in a **plain rough tunic** (unidentified, no armour) **stands below and reaches up**"  
  "**Barren 1st-century Judea**."  
  None of these are stated or constrained by any cited verse. "Plain rough tunic," "stands below and reaches up," "barren," and "1st-century" are artistic inventions presented as part of the scene being judged.

**Invented or over-specified facts:**

- The project rule requires "everything else (objects, actions, plant/material, who does the action, setting, period) must be Scripture-driven." The fact sheet itself introduces "plain rough tunic," "reaches up," "Barren 1st-century Judea," and "deep unnatural darkness (the sun blotted out)" without scriptural warrant and without placing them in FREE.

- "small handheld bunch" is treated as the required form. John 19:29 does not describe the physical delivery method or size. The Synoptics say "reed." Choosing hyssop (per John) is defensible; mandating "small handheld bunch" is not stated in the text.

**Coverage gaps (checkable biblical details left unguarded):**

- John 19:29 explicitly: "Now there was set a vessel full of vinegar." No fact addresses whether a vessel must or may appear, or whether the sponge is shown being filled from it. A painter could omit it entirely.

- The action is only "put it to his mouth" and "put it upon hyssop." The mechanics ("stands below and reaches up") are not stated. No fact guards against showing a ladder, platform, or different posture.

- The two other crucified men are part of the immediate setting in all four Gospels. No fact addresses whether they must be absent or may be visible in the frame.

- "hands nailed" is asserted in the depiction description. While John 20:25 later refers to nail prints in the hands, the vinegar scene itself (John 19:28-30) does not describe the nailing. Later text is being used to specify an earlier visual.
