## CONSTRAINED facts (not stated exactly; the depiction must not contradict)

**C1 — Hyssop is a SMALL plant, taken as a BUNCH (not a large branch/bough).**
> Exodus 12:22 (KJV): "And ye shall take a bunch of hyssop, and dip it in the blood that is in the bason..."
> 1 Kings 4:33 (KJV): "...from the cedar tree that is in Lebanon even unto the hyssop that springeth out of the wall..." (hyssop = the small plant, opposite the great cedar). *(No species/leaf-size is claimed as biblical — only "a bunch" and "small," which the verses state.)*

**C2 — The one who offered it is UNIDENTIFIED (not specified as a Roman soldier at this moment).**
> John 19:29 (KJV): "...they filled a spunge..." (plural, unnamed).
> Matthew 27:47-48 (KJV): "Some of them that stood there... one of them ran, and took a spunge..." (a bystander). *(Luke 23:36's soldiers offering vinegar is the EARLIER mocking, a different moment — not cited as warrant here.)* The image shows a plain unidentified man, which does not contradict any account.

**C3 — Christ still bore the crown of thorns; He wore His OWN garments (no purple robe at the cross).**

> Matthew 27:31 (KJV): "...they took the robe off him, and put his own raiment on him, and led him away to crucify him." (the purple robe was removed before the cross — the image must NOT show it).

**C4 — Setting: crucifixion; John names hyssop where the Synoptics name a reed.**
> Matthew 27:48 / Mark 15:36 (KJV): "...put it on a reed..." vs John 19:29 "...upon hyssop." These shorts quote John, so hyssop is used (a small bunch, per C1), not a bare cane.

## Christ-lens note
John deliberately writes **hyssop** (not the Synoptics' reed): hyssop is the Passover plant that applied the lamb's blood to the doorposts (Exodus 12:22) and the plant of purification (Psalm 51:7). At the true Passover, the Lamb of God receives the vinegar on hyssop — so the plant must stay identifiable as a small hyssop bunch for the typology to read.
