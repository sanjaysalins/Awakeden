# Independent review — claude (OK, 76s)

I checked every citation against the KJV and the harmonized passion narrative. Most of the sheet is solid, but one CONSTRAINED fact is affirmatively wrong and creates a coverage hole.

## Findings

**F1 — C3 is contradicted by Scripture: at the cross Christ was NOT in "His own garments."** C3 asserts as a constrained fact: *"He wore His OWN garments (no purple robe at the cross)"* and cites **Matthew 27:31** ("put his own raiment on him, and led him away to crucify him"). But Matthew 27:31 describes the walk **to** Golgotha, not the state **on** the cross. **John 19:23-24** — the very chapter this still quotes — states that at the crucifixion *"the soldiers... took his garments, and made four parts... and also his coat... They parted my raiment among them, and for my vesture they did cast lots."* The garments were stripped and divided **before** he hung there. So the depicted state should be **stripped / loincloth**, not clothed in his own raiment. C3 points the painter in the wrong direction and mis-anchors the citation. This is the one real over-reach.

**F2 — Coverage gap flows directly from F1.** Nothing on the sheet guards against the still showing a fully-clothed Christ, and C3 actively invites it. The checkable, easily-gotten-wrong fact (John 19:23-24: garments parted; Christ stripped to a loincloth) is **missing from the SPECIFIED/CONSTRAINED buckets**. Add it.

**F3 — S2 and S3 are textual/narrative anchors, not visual facts, yet sit in the "image MUST match" SPECIFIED bucket.** A still cannot depict *"He said, I thirst"* or *"It is finished."* These are fine as context that names the beat, but labeling spoken-word facts as things the image "MUST match" is a mild bucket-category slip. Mark them as beat-context, not depictable-visual musts.

**F4 — C2's "not... a Roman soldier" is a slightly stronger claim than the text warrants (but the bucket verdict is OK).** In John's own narrative the nearest antecedent of *"they filled a spunge"* (John 19:29) is *"the soldiers"* (John 19:23). Matthew/Mark identify a bystander who heard "Elias." Scripture is genuinely ambiguous and does **not** exclude a soldier. A plain unidentified man doesn't contradict anything, so "constrained" is the right bucket — but drop/soften the parenthetical implying Scripture rules out a soldier. It doesn't.

**Verified clean:** S1 (John 19:29), S4 (Luke 23:44-45 — combined verbatim), C1 (Exodus 12:22 / 1 Kings 4:33 — "bunch"/small correctly not over-claimed), C4 (reed-vs-hyssop harmonization is honest and correct), and the Christ-lens/typology note (Ex 12:22 + Ps 51:7) is sound. The FREE bucket is disciplined. Darkness at the ninth hour is within the biblical window and defensible.
