## SPECIFIED facts (Bible explicitly states → the image MUST match)

**S1 — A sponge filled with vinegar was put upon hyssop and put to His mouth.**
> John 19:29 (KJV): "Now there was set a vessel full of vinegar: and they filled a spunge with vinegar, and put it upon hyssop, and put it to his mouth."

**S2 — He said, "I thirst" (the beat's hinge; guards the `i_thirst` short).**
> John 19:28 (KJV): "After this, Jesus knowing that all things were now accomplished, that the scripture might be fulfilled, saith, I thirst."

**S3 — Jesus received the vinegar, then said, "It is finished" (guards the `it_is_finished` short).**
> John 19:30 (KJV): "When Jesus therefore had received the vinegar, he said, It is finished: and he bowed his head, and gave up the ghost."

**S4 — Darkness was over all the earth; the sun was darkened.**
> Luke 23:44-45 (KJV): "And it was about the sixth hour, and there was a darkness over all the earth until the ninth hour. And the sun was darkened, and the veil of the temple was rent in the midst."

## CONSTRAINED facts (not stated exactly; the depiction must not contradict)

**C1 — Hyssop is a SMALL plant, taken as a BUNCH (not a large branch/bough).**
> Exodus 12:22 (KJV): "And ye shall take a bunch of hyssop, and dip it in the blood that is in the bason..."
> 1 Kings 4:33 (KJV): "...from the cedar tree that is in Lebanon even unto the hyssop that springeth out of the wall..." (hyssop = the small plant, opposite the great cedar). *(No species/leaf-size is claimed as biblical — only "a bunch" and "small," which the verses state.)*

**C2 — The one who offered it is UNIDENTIFIED, and a Roman soldier is a defensible reading.**
> John 19:29 (KJV): "...they filled a spunge..." (plural, unnamed) — and the nearest antecedent in the same chapter is the soldiers: John 19:23 (KJV): "Then the soldiers, when they had crucified Jesus, took his garments..."
> Matthew 27:47-48 (KJV): "Some of them that stood there... one of them ran, and took a spunge..." Scripture is genuinely ambiguous; a soldier is NOT excluded. The image shows a Roman soldier at the foot of the cross, which does not contradict any account.

**C3 — At the cross Christ was STRIPPED to a loincloth (His garments had been parted); He still bore the crown of thorns.**
> John 19:23-24 (KJV): "Then the soldiers, when they had crucified Jesus, took his garments, and made four parts... and also his coat... They parted my raiment among them, and for my vesture they did cast lots." (Christ hangs stripped — the image must NOT clothe Him in robe/raiment; a loincloth only.)
> John 19:2 (KJV): "And the soldiers platted a crown of thorns, and put it on his head..." (crowned earlier; removal never stated, so the crown may remain).

**C4 — Setting: crucifixion; John names hyssop where the Synoptics name a reed.**
> Matthew 27:48 / Mark 15:36 (KJV): "...put it on a reed..." vs John 19:29 "...upon hyssop." These shorts quote John, so hyssop is used (a small bunch, per C1), not a bare cane.

## Christ-lens note
John deliberately writes **hyssop** (not the Synoptics' reed): hyssop is the Passover plant that applied the lamb's blood to the doorposts (Exodus 12:22) and the plant of purification (Psalm 51:7). At the true Passover, the Lamb of God receives the vinegar on hyssop — so the plant must stay identifiable as a small hyssop bunch for the typology to read.
