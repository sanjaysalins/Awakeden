Of the seven sayings from the cross (John 19:28), the shortest is two words: I thirst.

It sounds almost small beside the others. It is not. A thousand years before Golgotha, Psalm 22 painted the dying body behind that cry: "My strength is dried up like a potsherd; and my tongue cleaveth to my jaws; and thou hast brought me into the dust of death." Dried clay. A tongue stuck fast. The dust of death.

And then John's eyewitness line: "After this, Jesus knowing that all things were now accomplished, that the scripture might be fulfilled, saith, I thirst" (John 19:28, KJV).

Notice what John notices: Jesus speaks the cry KNOWING all things were now accomplished. Even his thirst walks inside the Scriptures.

And the deepest turn: this is the same voice that stood up at a feast and offered living water to anyone who would come. The Giver of living water hung there dry, in your place, so that your thirst could be answered forever.
