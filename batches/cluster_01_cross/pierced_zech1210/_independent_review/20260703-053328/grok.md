## Independent adversarial review — *Whom They Pierced — Zechariah 12:10*

### KJV verbatim — FAIL on one quoted line

**Scripture block (Zechariah excerpt):**
> `and they shall look upon me whom they have pierced, and they shall mourn for him, as one mourneth for his only son`

KJV Zechariah 12:10 has:
> `...as one **that** mourneth for his only son`

The word **"that"** is dropped. Under your rule — every word inside quotation marks must be exact KJV — this is not verbatim. Not a capitalization quibble; it is a missing word.

**John block:**
> `And again another scripture saith, They shall look on him whom they have pierced.`

This matches KJV John 19:37. No issue there.

---

### Doctrinal soundness — mostly sound, one compression risk

The me/him reading is standard evangelical Messianic use of Zechariah 12:10. John 19:37 support is fair.

**Risky line:**
> `The LORD who speaks is the Son who bleeds.`

Zechariah’s speaker is **the LORD** (“me”). Christian reading: the pierced one is God incarnate. But **“the LORD who speaks = the Son”** can sound like a flat identity swap (Father = Son) to anyone who knows basic Trinity language. Safer claim: the **pierced One** is the LORD in the flesh / the Son — not that “LORD” and “Son” are interchangeable labels in every sense.

**Also compressed:**
> `A Roman soldier raised his spear on a Friday afternoon, and finished a sentence God had started over five hundred years earlier.`

Zechariah 12:10 is often read as **future** national mourning (also echoed in Revelation 1:7), with the cross as fulfillment of “pierced,” not necessarily that the soldier “finished” the whole promise in one thrust. John quotes the **piercing**; he does not quote the **mourning**. The script treats cross + mourning + grace-pouring as one fused moment. Defensible for a short, but it **silently skips** the eschatological half of the oracle. A hostile listener could call that over-tidy.

**Interpretive stretch:**
> `He gives the tears he asks for.`

Zechariah gives **“the spirit of grace and of supplications.”** “Gives the tears” is preacherly paraphrase, not text. Not heresy, but it drifts from **faithful depth** into **poetic license** without signaling that it is summary.

---

### Clarity on first hearing — mixed

**Strong:**
- Spear + Friday + John = concrete.
- `Look upon ME. Mourn for HIM.` sets up a real puzzle, then answers it.

**Weak for zero Bible knowledge:**
- No book name on the first scripture read. A new listener hears `In Zechariah twelve` in POINT, but the **first** quoted lines float without “this is the Old Testament prophet Zechariah.”
- `The spirit of grace and of supplications` lands without a one-beat plain-English gloss (e.g. mercy that moves you to pray/repent). Paired with `Not wrath` it helps, but the phrase itself is still churchy on first pass.
- `let that look lead you home` — **home** where? Forgiveness? Heaven? Return to God? Too vague for a listener with no schema.

---

### Grace-anchored conviction — PASS

No fear selling, no gain/loss, no works ladder.

> `God did not send a stranger to bleed for you. He came himself.`

Personal, but invitation-shaped, not transactional.

> `Not wrath. The spirit of grace and of supplications.`

Grace-forward. Good tone for this project.

---

### Freshness = faithful depth — moderate

**Fresh:**
- Hook’s “finished a sentence God had started” is vivid and text-rooted.
- Me/him hinge is the right kind of surprise — from the **text**, not contrarian novelty.

**Less fresh:**
- “God came Himself” / incarnation-not-substitute is familiar sermon territory.
- Landing’s “look at the pierced One” repeats the proof beat more than it **extends** it.

---

### One thread spine — PASS

Hook (spear completes Zechariah) → LORD owns the wound → me/him + John → grace not stranger → look at the pierced One. No thread swap.

---

### Landing — does NEW work? — borderline REVISE

> `So do what the verse says. Look at him. Look at the pierced One, and let that look lead you home.`

It **does** obey the text’s imperative (“look”), so it is not a generic “will you trust Him?” cliché.

But **“lead you home”** is the tired part: emotional, underspecified, and it does not spell out what the **look** changes (mourning, repentance, recognition of the pierced LORD). The landing **restates** the command without a fresh consequence tied to “mourn” or “spirit of supplications.”

---

### Hook — grips in ~5 seconds? — PASS

> `A Roman soldier raised his spear on a Friday afternoon, and finished a sentence God had started over five hundred years earlier.`

Concrete image, time stamp, mystery, doctrine-adjacent without jargon. Strong cold open.

TOP FIXES:

2. Tighten `The LORD who speaks is the Son who bleeds` so the pierced **Messiah/LORD incarnate** is clear without sounding like Father=Son's interchangeable wording; optionally acknowledge Zechariah’s mourning is not fully “finished” at the spear (John cites piercing; mourning is the Spirit-wrought response).
3. Rewrite the landing so “look” does new work — tie look → mourn/repent/recognize the pierced LORD (from the verse), and replace vague `lead you home` with one concrete, grace-anchored outcome.
