# Independent review — codex (OK, 39s)

Concrete findings:

- **Doctrinal overcompression:** “**The same person. The LORD who speaks is the Son who bleeds.**” This is orthodox in intent, but “same person” is too blunt for Trinitarian precision. It risks collapsing the Father/Son distinction. Better: John reveals the pierced one as the divine Son, not merely “the same person” in an unqualified way.

- **Possible historical overclaim:** “**A Roman soldier raised his spear... and finished a sentence God had started over five hundred years earlier.**” Strong hook, but “finished a sentence” is a clever conceit that could imply the prophecy was incomplete until the soldier acted. The fulfillment framing is clear enough, but the wording is a little cute.

- **KJV check:** “**and they shall look upon me whom they have pierced...**” is KJV wording for that excerpt of Zechariah 12:10. “**And again another scripture saith, They shall look on him whom they pierced.**” is exact KJV John 19:37. No altered quoted KJV words found.

- **Context omission risk:** The narration says, “**And the promise begins with what he pours out. Not wrath. The spirit of grace and of supplications.**” This is faithful to Zechariah 12:10, but the scripture excerpt omits the opening words: “I will pour...” That makes the later claim depend on a part of the verse the listener has not heard.

- **Grace-anchored conviction is mostly strong:** “**He gives the tears he asks for**” is a good grace-centered landing from the text. No works/gain-loss/self-interest framing detected.

- **CTA is slightly generic:** “**Look at him... let that look lead you home**” fits the verse, but “lead you home” is familiar devotional language and does not do much new work beyond “look to Jesus.” The better landing is already present earlier: “He gives the tears he asks for.” That should probably carry the close.

- **Thread spine holds:** pierced Christ, Zechariah prophecy, John’s witness, grace poured out, look to Him. No major thread-swapping.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Replace or qualify “The same person” to avoid Trinitarian sloppiness.
2. Include the “I will pour...” portion of Zechariah 12:10 if you build the conviction on it.
3. Strengthen the landing around “He gives the tears he asks for” instead of ending with generic “lead you home.”
