# Independent review — claude (OK, 103s)

Independent adversarial review — read against KJV (Zech 12:10, John 19:37), doctrine, clarity, and the shorts charter.

## KJV verbatim — CLEAN
- "and they shall look upon me whom they have pierced, and they shall mourn for him, as one mourneth for his only son" — exact KJV Zech 12:10, mid-verse excerpt, no altered words. ✅
- "And again another scripture saith, They shall look on him whom they pierced." — exact KJV John 19:37. ✅
- "the spirit of grace and of supplications" in narrator voice also matches KJV word-for-word. ✅

## Doctrine — SOUND
- The me/him reading ("Look upon ME. Mourn for HIM. The same person. The LORD who speaks is the Son who bleeds.") is the standard evangelical reading of Zech 12:10, confirmed by John 19:37's application to the cross. Not contrarian, not invented. ✅
- "God did not send a stranger to bleed for you. He came himself." — orthodox (the pierced one is Yahweh the Son), and the piece has already identified the speaker as the Son, so no patripassian confusion. ✅
- "He gives the tears he asks for." — grace precedes mourning; matches the verse order (pour → look → mourn). Genuinely fresh AND faithful. Best line in the piece. ✅
- Grace-anchored: "Not wrath. The spirit of grace…" — no fear, no gain/loss framing. ✅

## Real problems (all clarity, none doctrinal)

1. **HOOK — "finished a sentence" is ambiguous at a crucifixion.** A first-time listener can hear judicial *sentence* (a death sentence the soldier carries out) instead of a spoken sentence God began. Both half-work, which is worse than one working — the listener spends the POINT beat resolving the pun. Say "finished a sentence God had *begun speaking*" or similar to force the right meaning.

2. **PROOF — "And again another scripture saith" dangles when quoted cold.** In John 19 this follows the bones prophecy (v36), so "again another" refers back. Ripped out, a zero-Bible listener hears "again? another than what?" The frame "He wrote:" doesn't resolve it. Either start the quote at "They shall look on him…" (still verbatim, attribution stays in narrator voice) or add a half-line of setup ("a second prophecy fulfilled in one thrust:").

3. **LANDING — "let that look lead you home" is stock.** "Look at him / do what the verse says" is strong landing work (the verse's own imperative — good). But "lead you home" is an unearned phrase; nothing in the piece set up "home." The verse's own payoff is *mourning that turns to grace* — land there ("and let the mourning He gives you become the grace He promised," or tie the look to the pierced side that proves He came himself).

Minor: "supplications" is heavy KJV vocabulary for a cold listener — "He gives the tears he asks for" glosses it well enough, so acceptable.

VERDICT: PASS
TOP FIXES:
1. Disambiguate the hook's "finished a sentence" — force the spoken-word meaning so the judicial-sentence misread can't happen.
2. Fix the cold "And again another scripture saith" quote — start at "They shall look on him…" or add a half-line of narrator setup.
3. Replace the generic "lead you home" close with a landing that pays off the piece's own thread (grace-given mourning / God came himself).
