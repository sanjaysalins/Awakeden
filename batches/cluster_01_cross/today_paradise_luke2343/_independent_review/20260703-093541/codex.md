# Independent review — codex (OK, 33s)

Findings:

- **Hook score: 7/10.** “Two crosses, one on each side of Jesus. Same nails. Same hill. Same afternoon to die.” is clear and visual, but not especially arresting. Under 8 means revise.
- **Landing score: 7/10.** “Remember me. And hear his answer. With me. Today. Paradise.” is built from the passage’s own language, so it is not stock. But “Ask what the thief asked” is generic application, and the final sentence mostly restates the quoted promise rather than landing with a fresh, embodied turn. Under 8 means revise.
- **KJV verbatim:** No altered KJV words found in the scripture lines. “If thou be Christ, save thyself and us”; “Dost not thou fear God…”; “Lord, remember me…”; and “Verily I say unto thee, To day…” match KJV partials.
- **Doctrinal risk:** “Every heart hangs beside one of those two men” is rhetorically strong but theologically imprecise. Listeners may hear a binary based on attitude rather than grace: be like the good thief, not the bad thief. It needs a clearer anchor that salvation rests on Christ’s mercy, not merely choosing the better posture.
- **Potential overstatement:** “One wanted Jesus himself” goes slightly beyond the text. The thief asks, “remember me when thou comest into thy kingdom.” That is faith in Jesus’ kingship and mercy, but the script psychologizes it into “wanted Jesus himself.”
- **Grace framing is mostly good:** “No merit. Just trust.” is orthodox and concise. But “Fix this. Rescue, without repentance” risks sounding like the mocker’s problem was asking for rescue instead of asking correctly. The sharper contrast in the text is unbelieving mockery versus repentant faith.
- **Clarity:** “He asked for a memory. He got company” is clever, but “memory” may confuse first-hearing listeners. The thief is not asking Jesus to think fondly of him; he is asking to be remembered in the kingdom.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Make the hook more arresting without using a template.
2. Replace “wanted Jesus himself” with a claim closer to the text: trusted Jesus as innocent King.
3. Clarify the landing so it convicts by grace, not by “be the better thief” moral contrast.
