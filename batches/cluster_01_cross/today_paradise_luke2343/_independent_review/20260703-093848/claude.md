**KJV verbatim — CLEAN.** All four quoted lines are exact KJV:

- 23:40b–41 "Dost not thou fear God, seeing thou art in the same condemnation? And we indeed justly; for we receive the due reward of our deeds: but this man hath done nothing amiss." ✓ — the join across the verse break is contiguous in KJV, correctly tagged.

**Doctrine — sound, one inference to be aware of.** "No merit left to earn. Just repentance, and trust in the innocent King." is textbook evangelical and the piece earns both halves from the verse itself (confession in 23:41, trust in 23:42). "Opposite eternities" (hook) asserts the mocker's damnation — Scripture never states his fate explicitly. It's the near-universal traditional reading of Luke's narrative and I would not fail it, but it is the one line resting on inference rather than text. "Both spoke to him" stays true within Luke and doesn't collide with Matthew 27:44. No invented or contrarian doctrine.

**Grace-anchored — yes.** No fear leverage, no gain/loss. "We are all one of those two men" is conviction by mirror, not pressure, and the CTA is built on the thief's own prayer.

**Thread spine — tight.** "Same nails. Same hill. Same distance" (hook) → "same afternoon, at his side" (middle) → "Same hill. Same distance… Today. Paradise." (landing). One thread, and the landing deliberately reprises the hook's triplet. No swapping.

**Clarity nits (first hearing):**
1. "He asked for someday, **at the edge of a kingdom**" — this phrase is fuzzy heard once. The verse's idea is a *future* kingdom; "at the edge of" sounds spatial and makes a zero-Bible listener stall. "He asked for someday, in a kingdom still to come" (or just "He asked for someday") is cleaner.
2. "**One demanded proof.**" — the mocker's line is "save thyself and us": he demanded *rescue*, with proof only implicit in "If thou be Christ." The narrator already called it "a taunt, not a prayer," so the recap slightly re-characterizes it. "One demanded rescue. One asked for mercy." matches the quoted verse more exactly.

**HOOK: 8/10.** "Two criminals died beside Jesus that afternoon. Same nails. Same hill. Same distance. Opposite eternities." — not a template open; the triplet has rhythm and "Opposite eternities" plants the payoff (the split) inside the first five seconds. It stops the scroll. What keeps it from 9–10: "Two criminals beside Jesus" is still a familiar image, and the punch word arrives fourth, not first.

**LANDING: 9/10.** "Ask what the thief asked. Remember me. And hear his answer. With me. Today. Paradise." — built entirely from this piece's own verse and verbs; this exact sentence could not close any other short. Not a stock closer.

Both scores clear 8, doctrine is clean both ways I can check it, and the KJV is verbatim. The remaining items are polish, not blockers.
