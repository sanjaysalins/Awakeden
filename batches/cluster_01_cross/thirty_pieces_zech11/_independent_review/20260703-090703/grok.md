**Doctrinal soundness**

Sound and orthodox. Slave-price tie (Ex 21:32), Zechariah typology → Judas fulfilment, sovereignty without puppetry (*"Nobody was trying to fulfil prophecy. Every coin still found its mark"*), ransom/redemption landing. No contrarian readings, no fear/gain-loss framing.

One pressure point: *"The law's price for a slave"* → *"He was weighed like a slave to buy you back"* applies slave-valuation language to Christ. Bold but standard typology if the listener already heard Judas sold **Jesus**. A totally cold listener might briefly hear diminishment before *"buy you back"* rescues it — minor, not a fail.

---

**Clarity on first hearing**

**Real logic gap.** After Judas’s quote, the narrator says:

> *"Cast down. In the temple. Blood money. It bought the potter's field."*

Judas cast coins and **departed**. Matthew 27:6–7 says the **chief priests** took the blood money and bought the field. On first hearing, with zero Bible knowledge, *"It"* has no clear agent — the coins on the floor did not walk themselves to a real-estate closing. The Zechariah half is clear (cast → potter → house of the LORD). The Judas half jumps a step. That breaks the five-beat echo for listeners who do not already know the story.

**Pronoun ambiguity:** *"Then he said where they must go."* — **He** is the LORD (per Zech 11:13), but the prior line is scripture in the LORD’s voice, then a narrator line with an unnamed *he*. One replay to pin the speaker.

*"The LORD took it personally"* is vague between “God owned the insult” and “God accepted the payment.” The next quote fixes it for attentive listeners; casual listeners may not link them.

Hook line *"Each one lands on a mark written five hundred years early"* is poetic but followable. ~550 years rounded to five hundred is fine.

---

**Grace-anchored conviction**

Good. *"Stop counting your coins"* rejects self-payment. *"Receive it, and go free"* invites; no threats, no heaven/hell ledger, no works bargain. Conviction comes from the weighed ransom image, not manufactured pressure.

---

**Freshness = faithful depth**

Strong. The five-point coin ledger (Thirty / Weighed / Cast / House of the LORD / Potter), slave-price law, and *"a goodly price that I was prised at of them"* are text-native surprises, not clickbait. The sovereignty line is the freshest doctrinal beat and it is earned.

---

**One thread spine**

Single spine throughout: silver coins → weighed price → cast → temple/potter → ransom → invitation. Hook, middle, and CTA all run on the same ledger. No thread swap.

**HOOK: 8/10**

*"Thirty silver coins hit the temple floor. Each one lands on a mark written five hundred years early."* — Concrete, kinetic, non-template. A stranger likely pauses. Not a 9–10 only because *"mark written five hundred years early"* asks for one beat of interpretation.

---

**LANDING: 6/10**

Setup is earned and piece-specific: *"The ransom is weighed out in blood. Stop counting your coins."* — weighed/price/coins language from the whole script.

But the **exact final sentence** is *"Receive it, and go free."* Per your rule: that line could close a Passover piece, a Romans 6 piece, a prodigal return, or any freedom-from-bondage short. Portable closer → cap at 5; the coin setup bumps it to 6, not 8. Under 8 triggers REVISE.

Also: *"Receive it"* never names Christ in the landing; antecedent is *"ransom… in blood"* — workable for insiders, thin for the zero-knowledge listener this lens targets.

VERDICT: REVISE

TOP FIXES:
1. Close the Judas→field gap in one plain narrator beat (priests took the blood money and bought the potter’s field) so *"It bought the potter's field"* is followable on first hearing.
2. Replace or extend the final sentence so the invitation is unmistakably from THIS ledger — e.g. tie *receive/go free* explicitly to the weighed thirty-shekel ransom/blood-price image; the exact last line must not work on a different episode.
3. Name the LORD in *"Then he said where they must go"* (or fold in *"Cast it unto the potter"*) so the Zechariah half does not stall on an ambiguous pronoun.
