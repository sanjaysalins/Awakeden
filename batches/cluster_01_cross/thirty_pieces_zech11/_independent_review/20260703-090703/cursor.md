### Doctrinal soundness

**Sound overall.** Slave-price echo (30 shekels), typological fulfilment via Matthew, sovereignty without puppetry (*"Nobody was trying to fulfil prophecy. Every coin still found its mark"* ≈ Acts 2:23), ransom/redemption landing — all standard evangelical, not contrarian.

**One compression worth flagging:** *"Blood money. It bought the potter's field."* Matthew’s chain is two-step: Judas casts silver in the temple → priests take it → they buy the potter’s field (Matt 27:3–7). Here it reads as if **Judas’s cast coins directly purchased the field**. That is not what the text says. A listener with zero Bible knowledge gets the wrong actor. Not heresy, but **grounding slip** on a piece whose whole point is precise fulfilment.

*"He was weighed like a slave to buy you back"* is bold but orthodox (substitution/redemption), not “God was a slave” sloppiness.

---

### Clarity on first hearing

**Strong:** The five-beat drum (*Thirty / Weighed / Cast down / temple / potter*) is followable and memorable.

**Weak spots:**

1. **Potter’s field causality** (above) — the money path is garbled.
2. **"house of the LORD" → "the temple"** — the script treats these as the same echo (they are), but **never bridges them for a stranger**. A zero-knowledge listener may not hear the parallel the writer intends.
3. **"Then he said where they must go"** — pronoun *he* after Zechariah/shepherd setup; context implies the LORD (next quote is his command), but on audio-only first pass *he* is slightly muddy.
4. **"Each one lands on a mark written five hundred years early"** — vivid hook, but *mark* stays abstract until *"Every coin still found its mark"* late. Works on repeat; first pass it’s a tease without payoff for ~40 seconds.

**Pass.** No fear sells, no heaven/hell ledger, no “don’t lose out.” *"Stop counting your coins"* reads as anti-works/self-payment, not greed moralism. Judas’s *"Guilt broke him"* is narrative, not applied to the listener as endpoint — though juxtaposing Judas’s guilt-death with *"go free"* without a half-beat distinction is a missed grace beat (Judas is the counter-example the script never names).

*"Rewind five centuries"* is TV-native but not a charter template cap.

*"Thirty silver coins hit the temple floor. Each one lands on a mark written five hundred years early."*

Concrete, auditory, scroll-stopping. Not *Did you know / Imagine / What if*. Minor ding: *mark* is clever before it’s explained.

### LANDING — **7/10**

*"The ransom is weighed out in blood. Stop counting your coins. Receive it, and go free."*

First two sentences are **earned from this piece’s ledger** (weighed, coins, price, ransom). The invitation exists — improvement over pure declaration.

But the **terminal sentence** *"Receive it, and go free"* could close any redemption short (Passover, Isaiah 53, generic atonement). Charter rule: if the exact final sentence is portable, cap at 5 **for that sentence**; as a **closing beat** the couplet *Stop counting… / Receive it…* is stronger but still not bone-specific like tying **potter’s field for the dead** to **blood ransom for the living** would be. Under 8 → REVISE.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. **Fix potter’s-field causality:** *"Blood money. It bought the potter's field"* collapses Matthew’s priest step — name the chain (cast in temple → priests → field) or soften (*"that silver would buy…"*) so a first-time listener doesn’t mislearn the fulfilment.
2. **Land harder from this piece’s own nouns:** keep the weighed/coins invitation, but close on an image only this thread owns (silver → field for the dead / blood weighed for the living — or explicit temple ↔ house-of-the-LORD echo), so the final line isn’t portable to every atonement piece.
3. **Log or bridge partial KJV pulls:** Matt 27:5a is noted; add Zech 11:12 mid-verse fragment to the partial-verse log, and/or one narrator bridge for *house of the LORD* = *the temple* so the five-beat echo lands for listeners with zero Bible background.
