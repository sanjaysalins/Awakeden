# Independent review — codex (OK, 24s)

**Findings**

Hook score: 7/10. “Thirty silver coins hit the temple floor” is concrete and visual, but “Each one lands on a mark written five hundred years early” is abstract and slightly unclear on first hearing. Also “early” should be “earlier.”

Landing score: 7/10. “Stop counting your coins” grows from the coin image, so it is not fully stock. But “Receive it, and go free” is generic enough to end many ransom-themed pieces, and “The ransom is weighed out in blood” risks turning the precise silver/field image into a broader slogan.

KJV issue: “A goodly price that I was prised at of them.” should preserve KJV casing if presented verbatim from Zechariah 11:13: “a goodly price…” after the colon. Also, the artifact says KJV is “quoted,” but the scripture lines are not in quotation marks, which makes the review standard harder to apply.

Clarity issue: “Each one lands on a mark written five hundred years early” is confusing grammar and timing. “Early” means before schedule, not “earlier.”

Doctrinal/clarity risk: “The law’s price for a slave” needs care. Exodus 21:32 gives thirty shekels as compensation for a servant killed by an ox; saying simply “price for a slave” is broadly recognizable but compressed enough to sound like a market-price claim.

Grace framing: “Stop counting your coins” is vivid, but it could imply the listener is trying to pay God off. That may work, but the script has not established that image in the listener’s conscience before commanding it.

Thread spine: mostly coherent: silver -> Zechariah -> Judas -> cross -> ransom. The weakest swap is from prophecy fulfillment to personal application; “to buy you back” appears suddenly and needs one more bridge.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Fix the hook wording: “five hundred years early” is confusing and should be “earlier” or recast.
2. Tighten KJV handling, especially exact casing/text presentation for Zechariah 11:13.
3. Strengthen the final move from fulfilled prophecy to personal ransom so “Stop counting your coins” feels earned.
