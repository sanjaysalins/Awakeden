# Independent review — claude (OK, 92s)

Cache read was blocked, so I verified the four fragments against the KJV text directly (they're well-known verses and I checked them word-for-word from the 1769 text). Review follows.

## Findings

**KJV verbatim — clean.** All four tagged fragments are exact:
- "So they weighed for my price thirty pieces of silver." = Zech 11:12b, exact.
- "A goodly price that I was prised at of them." = Zech 11:13 middle clause, exact including the archaic "prised."
- "And I took the thirty pieces of silver, and cast them to the potter in the house of the LORD." = Zech 11:13b, exact.
- "And he cast down the pieces of silver in the temple, and departed," = Matt 27:5a, exact, and it ends at a real clause boundary. The omission of "and went and hanged himself" is a legitimate scope cut for this thread, and the fragment tag discloses it.

**Doctrine — sound.** Thirty shekels as the slave price is Exodus 21:32, standard and uncontested. "His blood bought you back" is 1 Peter 1:18-19 / 1 Cor 6:20 territory. "Nobody was trying to fulfil prophecy" is a fair providence point — Judas and the priests act freely, the prophecy lands anyway. No contrarian reading anywhere. One deliberate silence worth noting: Matthew 27:9 attributes this prophecy to "Jeremy the prophet," a famous skeptic talking-point. The narration never claims "Matthew quotes Zechariah," so it makes no false claim — but expect that objection in the comments; nothing to fix in the script.

**Real problems, in order:**

1. **Setup/quote mismatch on the third quote.** Narrator says "And the LORD said where they must go" — then the scripture voice reads "And I took the thirty pieces of silver, and cast them..." That quote is **Zechariah obeying**, not the LORD speaking. A first-time listener will hear the "I" as the LORD's own voice giving the instruction, which it isn't. The LORD's actual words ("Cast it unto the potter") are never quoted despite the setup promising them. Fix the narrator line to something like "And Zechariah threw them where the LORD said" — or quote 13a itself.

2. **"Stop counting your coins" is the one soft line in an otherwise earned CTA.** On first hearing it can read as a money/materialism message ("stop being greedy") rather than the intended "stop tallying your own payment." It's the only ambiguous beat in the landing. One clarifying word would fix it — e.g. "Stop counting out your own price."

3. **"A goodly price that I was prised at of them" will lose a zero-Bible-knowledge listener.** The irony (the LORD sarcastically calling the slave-price "goodly") is carried only by the preceding "The LORD took it personally," which is doing a lot of work. It survives, but barely — consider "That insulting sum — God calls it 'a goodly price'" framing on screen or in the narrator beat. Minor.

4. **Nit:** "Jesus let himself be weighed like a slave" — in both texts the *silver* is weighed, not the person. It works as compression of "valued at the weighed-out slave price," and Zech 11:12b just played, so it's defensible. Flagging, not failing.

**Chronology:** Zechariah is ~520 BC, the cross ~AD 30 — "five hundred years" undersells by ~50 but is a fair round number. Fine.

**Thread spine:** coins → price → weighing → blood-purchase → "receive his price, go free." One spine, no swap. The hook image (coins hitting the floor) is literally the Matt 27:5 climax quote — the piece pays off its own open. Good structure.

**HOOK: 8/10.** "Thirty silver coins hit the temple floor. Each one lands on a mark written five hundred years early." Concrete, kinetic, non-template, and the prophecy-payoff is stated in image rather than announced. Not a 9 because "lands on a mark" requires a half-second of decoding on first hearing.

**LANDING: 8/10.** "Receive his price, and go free" is built from this piece's own slave-price imagery and could not close a different piece — genuinely earned, grace-anchored, no fear or gain/loss framing. Held back from 9 only by the ambiguous "Stop counting your coins" beat directly before it.

VERDICT: PASS
TOP FIXES:
1. Fix the narrator setup before the third quote — "And the LORD said where they must go" introduces a quote in which *Zechariah* (not the LORD) speaks and acts; either quote the LORD's actual command ("Cast it unto the potter") or change the narrator line to "And Zechariah threw them where the LORD said."
2. Disambiguate "Stop counting your coins" so it can't be heard as an anti-money aside — e.g. "Stop counting out your own price."
3. Give "A goodly price that I was prised at of them" one more beat of narrator scaffolding so the divine sarcasm is unmissable on first hearing.
