## Independent adversarial review — *Thirty Pieces of Silver* (Zechariah 11:12–13)

### KJV verbatim
All three quoted spans are **exact KJV fragments** (verified against `data/kjv_cache.json`):

- `"So they weighed for my price thirty pieces of silver."` — Zechariah 11:12 ✓  
- `"And I took the thirty pieces of silver, and cast them to the potter in the house of the LORD."` — Zechariah 11:13 ✓  
- `"And he cast down the pieces of silver in the temple, and departed."` — Matthew 27:5 ✓  

No altered words inside quotation marks.

**Non-quote problem:** the narrator echo **"Cast down"** (line 10) is **Matthew language applied to Zechariah**. KJV Zechariah 11:13 says *"cast them to the potter"* — not *cast down*. Zechariah’s acted parable and Judas’s temple act are being merged for rhythm, but a listener who just heard the Zechariah quote will think *he* cast coins down. That is not what the text says.

---

### Clarity on first hearing
**Hook → rewind without a signpost:**  
*"Thirty silver coins hit the temple floor. Each one lands on a mark written five hundred years early."*  

That is Judas’s fulfillment scene (temple floor). The very next beat is *"Zechariah heard the LORD name his own price."* A zero-Bible listener has no cue that we rewound 500 years. They will likely assume one continuous event: Zechariah, temple floor, coins falling. Chronology is clever on paper; on first listen it is a **logic jump**.

**"Then God traced where the coins would fly"** — *fly* is not in either passage. Zechariah casts **to the potter**; Judas casts **down in the temple**. The verb blurs two distinct actions.

**"Zechariah heard the LORD name his own price"** — imprecise. In 11:12 **they** weigh thirty pieces as *my price*; in 11:13 the LORD speaks **after** the amount is set (*"Cast it unto the potter… a goodly price that I was prised at of them"*). The LORD comments on and directs the coins; he does not name the number in 11:12–13 the way this line implies.

---

### Doctrinal soundness
Core claim is orthodox: exact fulfillment, human freedom + divine sovereignty, substitutionary payment. *"Nobody was trying to fulfil prophecy. Betrayer, priests, all moving freely. Every coin still landed where God said"* is a faithful reading of Matthew 27:9–10 and avoids puppet-theology if heard as coin-destination, not Judas’s sin.

**Soft flags, not heresy:**
- *"A slave's price for God"* — Exodus 21:32 background is standard; acceptable.
- *"If God steered even the blood money"* — *steered* is doing a lot of work; saved only by the next line’s *"moving freely."* A hostile ear could still hear divine authorship of betrayal, not just of fulfillment.

No contrarian clickbait readings detected.

---

### Grace-anchored conviction
No fear-selling, no works ladder, no *"what you gain if you…"* pressure. Good.

**Mild self-interest tint:** *"It was the plan to buy you back"* and *"His blood paid your price"* center **your** benefit before any invitation to respond. That is atonement language (1 Cor 6:20), not works — but the piece **announces** payment without **inviting** reception. Compared to locked cluster siblings, conviction stays informational, not grace-extended.

---

### Freshness = faithful depth
Genuine textual surprise: weighted → cast → potter → blood money → field for the dead. The *"Thirty. Weighed. Cast down…"* staccato echo is earned from the text, not a gimmick. Fresh and orthodox.

### One thread spine
Single spine holds: **silver weighed → cast → potter/temple → sovereign fulfillment → blood pays price**. No thread swap. Hook’s coins, middle’s prophecy/fulfillment, and landing’s silver/blood/field/dead all match.

### Gospel Five-Beat / CTA charter gap
Binding structure (`data/structures.json`) requires Landing to include **a direct invitation to Jesus and a response call**. Locked cluster pieces do this:

- *Father, forgive them*: *"come, and receive it by faith"*
- *Whom They Pierced*: *"Look, and live"* (from the piece’s own verb)

This artifact ends declarative: *"The silver bought a field for the dead. His blood paid your price."* Strong image; **no invitation, no question, no response to Christ.** For a CTA-to-Jesus short engine, that is a structural miss, not a stylistic choice.

### Hook score: **7 / 10**
Sensory, specific, non-template — coins on stone is scroll-stopping. Capped because the hook opens at **fulfillment** then silently rewinds to Zechariah with no bridge; first-time listeners will mis-locate the scene.

*"The silver bought a field for the dead"* is tightly earned from this piece’s silver/field/dead thread. But *"His blood paid your price"* is portable substitution language — it could close other atonement shorts — and there is **no invitational verb** built from this text (weigh / cast / potter / blood / field). Declarative payoff, not a CTA landing. Under the project’s own landing standard, this does not reach 8.
