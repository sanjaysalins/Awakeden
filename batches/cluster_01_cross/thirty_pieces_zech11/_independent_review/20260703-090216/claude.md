# Independent review — claude (OK, 90s)

**Verified the KJV quotes first** (Zech 11:12, 11:13, Matt 27:5 — checked word-by-word):

- "So they weighed for my price thirty pieces of silver." — exact ✓
- "And I took the thirty pieces of silver, and cast them to the potter in the house of the LORD." — exact ✓
- "And he cast down the pieces of silver in the temple, and departed." — words exact, but the verse is **cut mid-sentence** (KJV continues "…and went and hanged himself"). Same elision pattern the panel caught on Acts 8:35. The trim is defensible for 60s, but it must be a logged, deliberate choice — as printed it reads like a complete verse.

**Doctrine** — sound. Slave-price (Ex 21:32), sovereignty-without-puppetry ("Nobody was trying to fulfil prophecy… Every coin still landed" = Acts 2:23), redemption-purchase landing (1 Pet 1:18-19). No contrarian reading, no fear/gain framing. "A slave's price for God" is bold but orthodox (deity of Christ). The two-step fulfilment (Judas casts down in the temple → priests' money reaches the potter) is handled honestly, not forced.

**One real grounding mismatch:** the narrator claims *"Zechariah heard the LORD name his own price"* — but the quote that follows says **"they weighed** for my price." The line that actually proves the claim is the half of Zech 11:13 the piece *doesn't* quote: "a goodly price that I was prised at of them" — God's own sardonic claiming of the sum. On first hearing, claim and quote don't match. Quote the LORD's line (it's also the freshest phrase in the passage) or soften the narrator sentence.

**Clarity** — good. The five-point echo (Thirty / Weighed / Cast down / House of the LORD / Potter) is followable with zero Bible knowledge.

**Thread spine** — one spine (the price/coins) hook → middle → landing. ✓ No swap.

**HOOK: 8/10.** "Thirty silver coins hit the temple floor. Each one lands on a mark written five hundred years early." Concrete, visual, no template open. Stops the scroll.

**LANDING: 7/10.** "The silver bought a field for the dead. His blood paid your price." The couplet is earned by this piece's own noun ("price" ×4). But two problems: (1) the final sentence alone — "His blood paid your price" — could close several other atonement pieces (Passover, Isaiah 53); it's the *couplet* that's specific, not the closer. (2) There is **no invitation at all** — the charter model is CTA-to-Jesus; this is pure declaration. The Spirit convicts, the script invites — this script never invites. Under 8 = REVISE by the stated rule.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Landing: keep the earned "silver bought a field for the dead / His blood paid your price" contrast, but add ONE invitation line built from this piece's own ledger image (weighed / price / paid) — right now there is no CTA-to-Jesus, and the bare final sentence is portable to other pieces.
2. Fix the claim/quote mismatch: "the LORD name his own price" sits over "so THEY weighed…" — quote the LORD's actual line "a goodly price that I was prised at of them" (Zech 11:13a) or reframe the narrator sentence.
3. Log the Matt 27:5 elision ("…and departed[, and went and hanged himself]") as deliberate, or mark the quote as partial — mid-verse cuts presented as whole verses are the exact defect class the panel caught before.
