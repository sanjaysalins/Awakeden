# Independent review — codex (OK, 135s)

Hook score: 7/10. Strong image, but “written five hundred years early” is a grammar error and “lands on a mark” is a little clever before the listener knows the text.

Landing score: 6/10. “The silver bought a field for the dead. His blood paid your price.” uses the piece’s imagery, but it is not really a CTA, and “His blood paid your price” could close many ransom/cross pieces.

KJV spot-check sources used: [Zechariah 11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zechariah_11), [Matthew 27:5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_27%3A5), [Matthew 27:6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_27%3A6), [Matthew 27:7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_27%3A7).

Findings:

- “Zechariah heard the LORD name his own price” is too compressed and risks speaker confusion. In Zechariah 11, the shepherd asks for wages, they weigh the price, and the LORD commands where it goes. Do not make the LORD “name” the price unless you explain the typology.
- “Thirty. Weighed. Cast down. In the house of the LORD. To the potter.” conflates Zechariah and Matthew. Zechariah has “cast them to the potter”; “cast down” belongs to Judas in Matthew 27:5.
- The KJV words appear exact in the excerpts checked, but “And he cast down the pieces of silver in the temple, and departed.” silently truncates Matthew 27:5 before “and went and hanged himself.” If marked as scripture, signal it is an excerpt.
- “If God steered even the blood money” is doctrinally risky. It can sound like God directly drove Judas’s evil. Say sovereignly fulfilled or overruled, not “steered,” unless you add a guardrail.
- “A slave’s price for God” is orthodox shorthand, but unclear for a zero-knowledge listener. It needs the Exodus background or it sounds like a raw slogan.
- “It buys the potter’s field” arrives before the audience knows why that matters. “Field for the dead” comes too late to carry the first-hearing logic.
- “Nobody was trying to fulfil prophecy” is probably right narratively, but “Nobody” is an absolute claim. The cleaner point is that Judas and the priests did not mean to honor God, yet their actions fulfilled what was written.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Guard the sovereignty language so God fulfills prophecy without being framed as the moral driver of blood money.
2. Separate Zechariah’s “cast ... to the potter” from Matthew’s “cast down” and mark truncated scripture excerpts clearly.
3. Rebuild the ending into an actual CTA from the piece’s own image, while clarifying “slave’s price” and “potter’s field” for first-hearing listeners.
