### Findings (concrete)

**1. Instagram — absolute falsehood (hook honesty)**  
Phrase: `Everyone stops reading Psalm 22 at the forsaken cry.`  
That is not a curiosity hook; it is a false universal. YouTube softens it to `Most people…`; Instagram escalates to **Everyone**. For “hooky but honest,” this is the clearest miss. Soft clickbait the banned-token list will never catch.

**2. Instagram — citation overclaim (doctrine / text honesty)**  
Phrase: `Hebrews names the singer: the risen Jesus`  
Hebrews does **not** “name the singer” of Psalm 22. It applies the saying to Christ (Heb 2:11–12). “Names the singer” sounds like historical authorship ID. In-engine red-team waved this as “minor, non-blocking.” It is not minor for an upload kit that claims citation honesty. Fix to something like: `Hebrews puts those words on the risen Jesus` / `Hebrews says the speaker is the risen Jesus`.

**3. TikTok — looser hook than the video**  
Phrase: `Most people stop reading Psalm 22 at the cross.`  
Video + YT copy pin the stop-point as the **forsaken cry**, then the keep-reading turn. “At the cross” is vaguer and easier to hear as a historical claim about the psalm text itself. Same beat works with the tighter line the short already uses.

**4. TikTok / Instagram — “us brethren” without the careful quote fence**  
TikTok: `not ashamed to call us brethren`  
Instagram: same pattern.  
KJV is **them** (`he is not ashamed to call them brethren`). Narration was careful: quote = `them`; application = us/you **outside** the quote. Short captions drop the quote marks and rewrite the pronoun. Not a UK-G2 fail (no quoted span), but paste-risk for anyone who treats the caption as the verse. Prefer: keep the fragment exact, or clearly mark application (`calls us family — He is "not ashamed to call them brethren"`).

**5. YouTube Long — mild SEO off-thread**  
Tags include `adoption`.  
The piece never teaches adoption; it teaches **brethren / family** via Heb 2:11. Related systematic category ≠ content keyword. That is mild tag-stuffing, not catastrophic.

**6. YouTube Long — template hype**  
Phrase: `Hebrews adds the word that changes everything`  
House tone is grace + text, not trailer-trailer. The actual work is “brethren / family,” not “the word that changes everything.” Trim the superlative.

**7. Backup title (not chosen) — keep it out of rotation**  
Option 3: `Hebrews Puts This Psalm in the Mouth of the Risen Jesus`  
Hebrews cites **the line / verse** (Ps 22:22 via 2:12), not “this psalm” as a whole. If anyone swaps this in later, it overclaims.

**8. Facebook title — slight over-compress (borderline)**  
`The Psalm of the Cross Ends in a Family Song`  
Acceptable as series branding, but “Psalm of the Cross” is traditional shorthand, not a verse title. Fine if the description immediately grounds Ps 22:22 + Heb 2 (it does). Not a fail; watch siblings so every Ps 22 short doesn’t all become “Psalm of the Cross…”

**9. Platform / product mismatch (operational)**  
Header: **Format: short**. Kit still ships a full **YouTube (Long-form 16:9)** pack. House engine always emits five slots — fine as a template — but this piece only has the vertical short. Pasting the long pack onto a 9:16 file is a workflow trap, not a doctrine fail. Mark long as “only if a 16:9 exists” or leave it clearly secondary.

**10. Status line is too self-congratulatory**  
`Gates: ALL PASS` + red-team PASS + “panel-reviewed” in the paste blurb. The Instagram absolute + “names the singer” prove the automated/self red-team is soft. Do not treat that banner as evidence.
