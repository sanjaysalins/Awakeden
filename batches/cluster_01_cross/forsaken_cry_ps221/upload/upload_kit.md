At the ninth hour, from the darkness over Golgotha (Psalm 22:1, Matthew 27:46), Jesus cried out with a loud voice. And the words he chose were not new. They were a thousand years old.

"My God, my God, why hast thou forsaken me? why art thou so far from helping me, and from the words of my roaring?" (Psalm 22:1, KJV).

David's psalm opens in the voice of a forsaken man, and Jesus made that voice his own, quoting the very first line as his own cry.

But hear what the cry is not. It is not lost faith. Even forsaken, he still says MY God, twice. The possessive survives the dark. This is the sinless Son standing in our place, bearing the forsaking that our sin deserved, so that the door home would stand open from the inside of the darkness itself.
