### HOOKY BUT HONEST / overclaim

**YouTube Short — TITLE:** *“The Psalm That Starts With One Dying Man and Ends With Every Nation”*  
Psalm 22 does **not** end at 22:27; vv. 28–31 follow (your sibling *he_hath_done_this* correctly anchors v31 as the psalm’s last line). “Ends with every nation” compresses the arc for hook value and matches the **spoken** script, so it is not a bait-and-switch against the video — but it **overstates psalm structure** for anyone who knows the text. Safer honest hook: *“…and closes with the nations turning”* or name v27 explicitly.

**YouTube Short / Long / Facebook / TikTok — DESCRIPTION (shared opener):** *“Psalm 22 opens with one forsaken man dying alone, and ends with every nation on earth”*  
Same structural stretch, repeated on **four platforms** — reads templated, not tailored.

**TikTok — DESCRIPTION:** *“nation after nation **has turned to the LORD**”*  
This **upgrades** the video’s careful wording (*“people in nation after nation have turned to the LORD”*). It shifts from *people among nations* to *nations as units converted*, edging toward universalist/completed-history tone. Your own red-team caught this; it is **still unfixed** in the shipped kit.

**Instagram — DESCRIPTION:** *“the gospel **has been crossing nations ever since**”*  
 Softer than TikTok, but still present-perfect sweep language; paired with the title’s “every nation,” the stack sounds more fulfilled than the narration’s inaugurated “have turned.”

**Title option 4 (unused):** *“The Song That Swept the Whole Earth”*  
If ever swapped in, it would overclaim relative to the locked narration’s “people in nation after nation have turned.”
