## Findings

**1. "Thirty-three years later, the sword lands." — narrative over-claim (the recurring corpus failure class).** Scripture never gives Jesus's age at death. Luke 3:23 says "about thirty years of age" at ministry start; "thirty-three" is tradition-derived arithmetic presented as a bare fact. This is exactly the class the 2026-06-29 corpus re-audit fixed (Noah cubits, Ps22 "first"). One word of hedge or a non-numeric phrase ("Some thirty years later" / "Decades later") keeps the punch without the unfounded precision.

**2. "He sees her wound. And he dresses it first." — "first" is ambiguous and clashes with this very cluster.** The intended sense is "before his own wound," but a listener — especially one watching this cluster's sibling shorts — can hear it as sequence-of-the-sayings, and traditionally "Father, forgive them" and "To day shalt thou be with me in paradise" both come *before* this word. The cluster itself contains `father_forgive_them` and `today_paradise_luke2343`, so the pieces will sit side by side and contradict each other. Fix: "he dresses hers before his own" or simply drop "first."

**3. "He gave her John." — extra-textual naming.** John 19:26-27 says only "the disciple... whom he loved"; the name John is traditional attribution (near-universal, evangelical-mainstream, low severity). Acceptable, but the piece could stay inside the text at zero cost: "He gave her the disciple he loved. A home. A son." Flagging per the both-ways discipline; your call.

**4. Minor, not blocking:** "He sees it from the cross" (present tense) is devotional rhetoric, defensible via Hebrews 4:15-style sympathy; and "nails in his hands" is grounded by John 20:25. No action needed.

## What works

Doctrine is otherwise sound: the Simeon-sword → cross connection is a standard faithful reading, the substitution line ("While judgment fell on him, he was binding someone else's wound") is orthodox, and "bind" echoes Psalm 147:3. Conviction is grace-anchored — no fear, no gain-loss. One spine: sword → sword lands → he binds her wound → he binds yours. Clarity on first hearing is good; "Simeon said it to Mary" resolves the cold open.

**HOOK: 8/10.** "An old man in the temple held a baby and told the mother one dark sentence." — concrete, ominous, non-template. A stranger plausibly stays for the sentence.

**LANDING: 8/10.** "Stand where Mary stood. Let him behold you, and bind what the sword tore." — built from this piece's own verbs (behold, sword, bind); it could not close a different piece. Not stock.

Scores clear the bar, but findings 1 and 2 are real grounding defects of the exact class this project has committed to catching, so the verdict is REVISE — both are one-line surgical fixes.
