# Independent review — codex (OK, 33s)

Hook Score: 7/10. “An old man in the temple held a baby…” is concrete and intriguing, but “one dark sentence” is slightly vague and melodramatic.

Landing Score: 7/10. “Stand where Mary stood” ties back to the scene, and “bind what the sword tore” uses the piece’s own image. But “Let him behold you” is theologically awkward because John 19 is Jesus beholding Mary in a unique historical moment, not a direct promise that the viewer can occupy Mary’s role.

Findings:

- Doctrinal overreach: “He sees her wound. And he dresses it first.” The text shows Jesus providing for Mary, not explicitly “dressing” her wound. “First” also risks implying Mary’s emotional wound was Jesus’ priority before atonement, which is not stated in John 19.

- Possible theological imprecision: “While judgment fell on him” is orthodox if referring to substitutionary judgment, but in this scene the narration pivots from Mary’s suffering to atonement language without explaining it. A zero-knowledge listener may not understand whose judgment, from whom, or why.

- KJV issue: “Yea, a sword shall pierce through thy own soul also.” The KJV text is a parenthetical clause ending with “also,” not “also.” as a full sentence. Words are exact, but the punctuation has been normalized. If strict verbatim display is required, flag this.

- KJV issue: “Now there stood by the cross of Jesus his mother.” This is a clause-boundary partial from John 19:25, but the KJV continues with a comma and additional people. The note explains this, but the narration itself could make it sound like Mary alone stood there.

- Clarity issue: “Thirty-three years later, the sword lands.” The age/timeline is traditional, not explicit in the cited text. “The sword lands” is vivid, but a new listener may not know whether the “sword” is literal, symbolic, Roman violence, or Mary’s grief.

- Grace concern: “Stand where Mary stood. Let him behold you” may unintentionally turn the invitation into imitation of Mary’s physical nearness and unique role, rather than receiving Christ’s grace. It is not works-heavy, but it is a little mystical and imprecise.

- Thread is mostly coherent: prophecy -> cross -> Jesus seeing Mary -> Jesus seeing wounds. The main weakness is the final application: it shifts from Jesus caring for Mary through John to Jesus binding the viewer’s wounds generally. That landing is plausible devotionally, but not tightly built from the actual command “Behold thy son / mother.”

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Remove or qualify the overclaim “he dresses it first”; keep the focus on Jesus providing for Mary without implying priority over atonement.
2. Tighten the final CTA so it flows from Jesus’ provision in the text, not from the viewer becoming Mary.
3. Make scripture display/punctuation strictly KJV or clearly mark partials without sentence-final punctuation changes.
