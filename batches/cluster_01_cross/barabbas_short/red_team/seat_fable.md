# Seat: Fable (hostile independent review) — Barabbas short (40 s)

Read only: FILM_AND_COPY.md, KJV.txt, frames_0-19s.jpg, frames_20-39s.jpg, thumb_16x9.jpg, thumb_9x16.jpg.

## 1. Who / take-away / curiosity gap — HOOK + PAYOFF: 7/10

- **Who:** Christian short-form viewers (Passion / Easter), plus "hidden meaning in a Bible name" fans. Works for a church-adjacent newcomer too.
- **Take-away:** a guilty man named "son of the father" walked free; the true Son of the Father took his place; through that death we get the name. One clean idea, one swap.
- **Gap:** "the name means son of the father" → "so who went free?" → Bar-ABBA / Abba → "Jesus died to give it to us." The payoff lands and is earned from the piece's own words (name → Abba → adoption). Payoff alone is 9/10.
- **Why not higher:** the cold open is a *definition* (a text card + a prisoner's feet for 3 s). For anyone who has never heard "Barabbas", the first 2 seconds carry no stake; the question ("look who went free") lives only in the title, not in the first spoken line. Hook alone is ~6/10.

## 2. Global audience

- **Who / what / why:** yes. A newcomer can say: a robber (Barabbas), a governor (Pilate), Jesus; the governor offered to free one; the crowd picked the robber; the leaders were jealous. Good.
- **Lines I had to re-read:**
  - "Barabbas, or Jesus **which** is called Christ?" — archaic "which" for a person; short enough to survive, and the picture (Pilate pointing at the two men) carries it.
  - "The true Son of the Father **did not**." — the verb is left out ("did not go free"). ESL listeners get a half-beat of "did not what?"; the picture of Jesus small behind the gate with soldiers rescues it. Acceptable but not free.
  - "Jesus died to give **it** to us." — the whole gospel rests on a pronoun. "That name" in the sentence before makes it work; still, a heavy load for "it".
- **Bar-ABBA / Abba link:** audible and explicit — "Listen: Bar-ABBA. Jesus called God 'Abba.' It means Father." The caption spells the stress. One gap: "Bar = son" is never said; the viewer infers it from "means son of the father" + "Abba means Father". Fine for most; a tiny step for the slowest listener.
- Not explained, and acceptable: why Jesus is on trial at all; the feast-release custom ("offered to free one man" is enough).

## 3. Biblical accuracy (checked against KJV.txt)

- **"Barabbas means son of the father"** — safe. Aramaic bar (son) + abba (father); standard lexicon gloss. Note the KJV itself never gives the meaning, and the film doesn't claim it does. The *irony* (the robber carried the title the true Son owned) is a reading, not a doctrine, and the film presents it as irony. OK. Small honesty note: a scholar could say it was a plain patronymic ("son of [a man called] Abba"); the film's "the father" leans on the definite article. Defensible, not an overclaim.
- **"Robber"** — John 18:40 "Now Barabbas was a robber." Exact. (Other Gospels add insurrection/murder; "robber" understates him but is not wrong.)
- **"He knew the leaders were jealous of Jesus"** — Matt 27:18 "For he knew that for envy they had delivered him." "They" = the chief priests and elders (v.20 confirms who did the persuading). "Leaders" and "jealous" are fair plain-word renderings. Fair.
- **Dialogue:** 27:17 partial — verbatim. 27:21 "Barabbas." — verbatim. 27:23 "Why, what evil hath he done?" — verbatim. 27:22 "Let him be crucified." — verbatim.
  - Nit on order/tag: in KJV "Let him be crucified" (v.22) comes BEFORE Pilate's "Why, what evil…" (v.23); the cry that follows his question is v.23b "they cried out the more, saying, Let him be crucified." The film's order is therefore supported, but the on-screen tag should arguably read **MATTHEW 27:23**, not 27:22. Cosmetic.
  - The film cuts Pilate's "What shall I do then with Jesus…?" so his "Why, what evil hath he done?" answers "Barabbas." It still reads naturally. OK.
- **"Jesus called God 'Abba.' It means Father."** — Mark 14:36 "Abba, Father". Exact, and Mark glosses it himself.
- **"So the robber went free. The true Son of the Father did not."** — Matt 27:26. Exact.
- **"Jesus died to give it to us"** — sound. Gal 4:4-7: sent "to redeem… that we might receive the adoption of sons… crying, Abba, Father"; Rom 8:15 "Spirit of adoption, whereby we cry, Abba, Father." The death is the means of the sonship. No overclaim.
  - Observation, not an error: there is **no response line** (receive / believe / call Him Abba). "Us" is left universal. The gift is stated, not offered. Doctrinally it is still true (the gift is for us); but a hostile listener could hear "automatic". If the house wants a CTA-to-Jesus, this is a soft landing without one.
- **Good omission:** "His blood be on us, and on our children" (v.25) is NOT quoted. Right call.

## 4. Fairness / harm / reverence

- **Blame:** the film names "the leaders" (jealous) and "the crowd" — exactly what Matthew names. No "the Jews", no v.25. Low antisemitism risk on the words.
- **Pictures:** the crowd (frames 14-15, 16-19) is a bared-teeth, fist-shaking mob with manga speed lines — sensational in *style*, but generic bearded men in robes, no ethnic caricature features that I can see at this size. Watch it at full size once.
- **Jesus' aura:** blue-and-orange **flame** shape. It is consistent, so it reads as a "holy" code after a second — but it is literally fire-shaped. In Gethsemane (frames 20-23) He kneels inside flames. A newcomer may think "why is He burning?" and it reads like an anime power-up. Not irreverent, but a style risk; it would be safer as a soft light than tongues of fire.
- **Cross beam:** a full cross carried, robed, no wounds — traditional and reverent. Fine.
- **The giant hand from the sky (frames 30, 37-39):** a medieval "hand of God" motif. Some viewers (Reformed / Orthodox) object to any image of the Father. Precedent exists; flag, don't block. But see §5 — it is also the LAST image, so the piece does not close on Christ.

## 5. Pictures vs words

Matches well at: feet/"robber in prison" (3-4); Pilate pointing at the two men/"Barabbas, or Jesus…" (10-13); Jesus calm inside the mob/"Why, what evil…"/"Let him be crucified" (16-19); Gethsemane/"Abba" with Mark 14:36 tag (22-26); Barabbas out through the arch, Jesus small behind with soldiers/"the robber went free" (28-29); Jesus carrying the cross/"Jesus died to give it to us" (33-36). The two-men-side-by-side panel is the right image for the Bar-ABBA beat.

**Defects found:**
1. **Garbled foreign marks — frames 14 and 15 (crowd close-up with Barabbas in chains).** Japanese-looking manga sound-effect glyphs (katakana-like "ギ!!", "フ", "ッ") are drawn in the sky above the mob. Random Japanese text on a KJV Bible short is an obvious glitch and will get "why is there Japanese?" comments. Must go.
2. **Four crosses — frames 32-36.** Three crosses already stand on the hill while Jesus is still carrying His. Matthew 27:38 has the two thieves crucified WITH Him, so at most two should be up (or none). Bible readers will catch it.
3. **The "jailbreak" burst — frame 27.** Barabbas smashes out of a door with sparks and shackle fragments flying. He was *released* (27:26), he did not break out. A newcomer could read it as an escape. Also the caption on that frame is still "Jesus called God Abba…" — a 1 s word/picture mismatch.
4. **Caption arrives before the cut — frame 5.** "Pilate, the governor, offered…" sits over the chained-feet shot for 1 s before Pilate appears. Minor.
5. **Last image is a hand, not Christ — frames 37-39.** The landing hold ends on the glowing hand over a sunlit landscape, with no caption. The film's own last words are about Jesus dying; the eye ends on an abstract hand.
6. **Slideshow risk:** frames 6-9 (Pilate before the elders), 16-19 (Jesus in the mob), 33-36 (cross walk) and 37-39 (hand) each look near-identical at 1 fps. Pilate's arm does shift across 6-9, so there is *some* motion; I cannot judge the rest from stills — check real playback for dead holds.
- **Second Jesus / ghost figures:** none wrong. The small glowing Jesus behind the arch (28-29) and in the bottom panel of 20-21 is intentional and reads correctly.
- **Anatomy / costume:** nothing disqualifying. Pilate in red cape and tunic, soldiers with red crests, elders in robes — fine. Frame 6-9 shows Pilate addressing the *elders*, whereas Matthew has him address the multitude — tolerable compression.
- **Thumbnails:** both clear, match the film, question-mark title opens the gap, no false promise. The 16:9 crops Barabbas at the shoulders under the dark band — fine.

## 6. Public text

- **Title** — honest, opens a gap, not clickbait. The colon construction is slightly clunky; keep.
- **Description** — accurate. "Quoted word for word from the KJV: Matthew 27:17, 21, 22, 23" — true of the words used (they are partials, and the description says the full v.17 itself). "Robber" / "Abba" sources correct. Good.
- **No line about how the film was made** — nothing false, nothing claimed. But also no honest AI/animation disclosure; if the house standard wants one short line, it is missing.
- **Hashtags** — fine. #Easter is seasonally off for a November post; Passion content is evergreen so it is not wrong, just weak. Consider #GoodFriday or #Gospel instead.
- **Pinned comment** — fine, genuine question, not manipulative.
- **Tag nit (on-screen):** "THE CROWD / MATTHEW 27:22" after Pilate's 27:23 question — 27:23 is the more exact source (see §3).

## 7. Worst problem, first change, verdict

- **ONE worst problem:** the Japanese-looking manga sound-effect glyphs in the sky over the mob (frames 14-15). It is the one thing every viewer, Bible-reader or not, will see as a mistake, and it undercuts the "word for word from the KJV" trust the description builds.
- **ONE change first:** re-render or clean-plate those two crowd frames with no sky text; while in there, fix the hill to two crosses (or none) in frames 32-36.
- Runner-up fixes, in order: swap the frame-27 "break-out" for a quiet walk-out (he was released, not escaped); end the hold on Jesus/the cross rather than the hand; fix the 27:22 → 27:23 tag.

**VERDICT: REVISE.** Words and doctrine are sound and the payoff is earned; the picture track has one visible glitch (foreign glyphs), one Bible-reader catch (four crosses), and one misleading beat (jailbreak). All fixable without touching the narration.
