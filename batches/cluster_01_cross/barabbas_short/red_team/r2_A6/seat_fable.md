# Seat: Fable — hostile blind review, "Barabbas" 41.6 s short (r2_A6)

Read only: FILM_AND_COPY.md, KJV.txt, frames_0-20s.jpg, frames_21-41s.jpg, thumb_16x9.jpg, thumb_9x16.jpg.
I saw stills only (one per second). I did not hear the sound or see motion.

## 1. Who / take-away / curiosity gap

- **Who for:** Shorts viewers who half-know the Easter story, plus curious newcomers. Works best for people who already know the name Barabbas.
- **Take-away:** The man named "son of the father" went free. The real Son of the Father took his place, so we can call God Father.
- **Curiosity gap:** "His name means son of the father — so what?" It opens fast (0 s).
- **Pay-off:** Yes, at 28.9 s ("The true Son of the Father did not") and 33.5 s.
- **Weak spot:** The hook gives the name meaning away in second 0. "Listen: Bar-ABBA" at 20.9 s repeats it, so the middle "reveal" is not new. The real surprise is the swap, and that lands.
- **Score: hook 7/10, pay-off 8/10.**

## 2. Global audience

- **Who/what/why:** A newcomer can say: a prisoner called Barabbas was freed, Jesus was killed in his place, and the name is the irony. Pass, with gaps.
- **Not explained:** "Christ", "crucified", who "the leaders" are, why a governor frees a prisoner at all.
- **Lines I re-read:**
  - "Barabbas only had the name." (33.5 s) Short, but the meaning is compressed. A second-language viewer may not get "he had the title but was not the real thing".
  - "Why, what evil hath he done?" (17 s) "hath" is archaic, but it is short and the picture carries it.
  - "Jesus which is called Christ" — "which" for a person reads as a mistake to learners. It is KJV, so keep, but it costs clarity.
- **Bar-ABBA / Abba link:** Mostly clear. The film never says "Bar means son". The viewer must work that out from the hook plus "Abba means Father". One extra word would fix it ("Bar = son. Abba = father.").
- **"too" in the last line:** small ambiguity. "Father too" = like Jesus does (intended), but could be heard as "like Barabbas".

## 3. Biblical accuracy (checked against KJV.txt)

1. **"In Aramaic, Barabbas means son of the father"** — Not in the KJV text itself; it is name scholarship. It is the standard, mainstream reading (bar + abba). Safe to state. Small honesty note: some read it as "son of Abba" (a man's name). "means" is acceptable; no change needed.
2. **"He was a robber in prison"** — "robber" is word for word John 18:40. "In prison" is not in the supplied text (Matthew says "notable prisoner"), but is a fair inference. OK. Note: "robber" is the mildest label; the pack does not include Mark 15:7 / Luke 23:19 (insurrection, murder). Not wrong, just soft.
3. **"Pilate, the governor, offered to free one man"** — fair to Matt 27:15, 17.
4. **"Pilate knew the leaders were jealous of Jesus"** — Matt 27:18: "he knew that for envy they had delivered him." "They" is not named in v18; v20 names chief priests and elders. "The leaders" is a fair reading. "jealous" for "envy" is fine. OK.
5. **Quotes:**
   - "Barabbas, or Jesus which is called Christ?" — verbatim partial of 27:17. Tag right.
   - "Barabbas." — verbatim 27:21. Tag right. But in the KJV this answers the SECOND question ("Whether of the twain", v21), not the v17 question shown. A splice of two moments. Tags are honest, so minor.
   - "Why, what evil hath he done?" — verbatim 27:23. Tag right.
   - "Let him be crucified." — verbatim 27:23 (and 27:22). Tag right.
6. **"Jesus called God Abba. It means Father."** — Mark 14:36. Right verse. Sound.
7. **"So the robber went free. The true Son of the Father did not."** — Matt 27:26. Sound.
8. **"Jesus died so we could call God Father too"** — Fair to Gal 4:5-6 ("to redeem ... that we might receive the adoption of sons ... crying, Abba, Father"). Sound doctrine. Two small notes: (a) Galatians says "redeem" and "sent forth his Son", not "died" — the death is implied, not stated in those two verses; "follows Galatians 4:5-6" in the description is the honest wording, good. (b) "we" is open; adoption in the text is for those redeemed/who receive it. Not an error, slight breadth.
9. **Overclaims in PICTURES (not words):**
   - 24-28 s: Barabbas and Jesus stand face to face and look at each other. No such meeting is in the text.
   - 32 s: Barabbas looks back with a tear. Invented feeling/repentance. Not in the text.
   - 36-41 s: Jesus walks to the cross in a clean white robe, no scourging, no thorns. Matt 27:26 says he was scourged first. Sanitised.
   - 11-20 s: Jesus stands unbound, alone in the middle of the crowd. In the text he is a held prisoner before the governor.

## 4. Fairness / harm

- **Words:** clean. "The leaders", not a people. "His blood be on us" is left out. Good.
- **Pictures — real risk at 7-10 s (and 0-3 s panel 3):** the leaders are drawn as dark hooded old men, hunched, hand over mouth, plotting. This is close to the old "scheming Jewish elders" picture. Words are fair; the drawing is not neutral.
- **Crowd 11-20 s:** drawn as a raging fist-waving mob with speed lines. Matthew says the leaders "persuaded the multitude" — the film never shows or says that, so the crowd carries the blame visually.
- **Sensational:** the mob panels are loud but not gory. Acceptable.
- **Reverence:** Jesus is calm and dignified. But the blue-and-orange flame reads as "man on fire" or an anime power-up, not holiness. Worst at 19-20 s (flames fill the frame as the crowd shouts "crucified") and 36-41 s (the cross looks like it is burning). Some viewers will find it odd; some will find it cheap. Not irreverent in intent.
- **34-35 s:** a giant glowing hand in the sky = God the Father shown as a body part. Some traditions object. Also kitsch.

## 5. Pictures and era (by second)

- **0-3 s:** panel 3 — a glowing blue Jesus HEAD floats behind the leaders. Ghost head. Odd.
- **0-10 s vs 11-14 s:** Pilate changes. Young, dark hair (0-10 s). Older, grey, different face (11-14 s). Two Pilates.
- **7-10 s:** Pilate in red military cape and leather armour, torches, indoor stone vault. A governor judging would be in a toga, outside at the judgment seat. Hooded cowls on the leaders look medieval-monk, not 1st-century.
- **15 s:** top panel is an unreadable close-up (cloth, arms, a head). Garbled.
- **15-18 s:** many crowd men wear a white head-cloth with a dark cord band (modern keffiyeh + agal look). Not 1st-century Judea; reads as modern Arab dress.
- **11-20 s:** Jesus free and unguarded inside the mob. No soldiers on him.
- **20 s:** Jesus has a slight smile while the caption says "Let him be crucified." Wrong tone.
- **21-23 s:** words say "Listen: Bar-ABBA" but the picture is the garden prayer, which belongs to the NEXT line. Then at 22 s the panels dissolve into see-through double figures (ghost Barabbas, ghost Jesus). At 23 s a cut-off second Jesus head sits at the bottom edge while Jesus kneels above = two Jesuses in one frame.
- **24-28 s:** words "Jesus called God Abba" but the picture is the invented Barabbas-meets-Jesus scene, under garden trees. Barabbas was never in the garden. Timeline confusion for a newcomer. Loose chains hang from his wrists and end in an odd hook (28 s).
- **29-31 s:** chains burst in sparks as he walks out. Magic, not a jailer unlocking. Shackle bits float in the air. Jesus stands tiny in the background, glowing, at the same moment — fine as an idea, but small.
- **32 s:** tearful look back (invented).
- **33, 36-41 s:** Jesus carries a full Latin cross with the long beam dragging — traditional, acceptable (many hold only the cross-beam was carried). Two or three crosses already stand empty on the hill. Clean robe, no wounds. Soldiers have red crested helmets on every man (crests were for officers) — minor.
- **34-35 s:** giant hand in the sky over a sunrise meadow with flowers. Does not match the era look of the rest; fingers look long and thin. Feels like a different film.
- **Thumbnails:** both use the invented meeting scene. 16x9: a dark band hides both bodies; Barabbas stares up, Jesus stares at the camera — a bit eerie.

## 6. Public text

- **Title:** true. "look who went free" is mild bait but honest and paid off. OK. Lower-case after the colon reads slightly unfinished.
- **Description:** every verse claim checks out. Matt 27:17 quote is verbatim. "The crowd chose him" is fair. Good that sources are named ("Robber" John 18:40, etc.).
- **Missing:** no AI-art disclosure line. Add one, once.
- **Hashtags:** fine, relevant, not spammy.
- **Thumbnails:** "SON OF THE FATHER?" is a good honest question. But the picture promises a Barabbas-Jesus conversation the Bible does not have.
- **No false claim found in the public words.**

## 7. Best version? Worst problem, first change, verdict

- **Best version possible?** No. The words are tight, accurate and nearly lockable. The pictures let them down.
- **ONE worst problem:** The film shows a scene that is not in the Bible as if it happened — Barabbas and Jesus face to face in garden light (24-28 s), then Barabbas crying as he looks back (32 s) — and that same invented scene is the thumbnail. For a channel that says "quoted word for word", this is the easiest thing for a critic to attack.
- **ONE change first:** Replace 24-28 s with the Gethsemane prayer picture (it already exists at 21-23 s, and it matches "Jesus called God Abba"), and cut the tearful look-back at 32 s. Keep the two men apart: one walks out, one walks to the cross.
- **Next, in order:** (2) redraw the hooded scheming leaders 7-10 s to plain, neutral robed men; (3) fix the keffiyeh-style headwear 15-18 s; (4) one Pilate, not two; (5) remove ghost head 0-3 s and double figures 22-23 s; (6) tone the flame down at 19-20 s and on the cross; (7) drop or replace the sky hand 34-35 s; (8) add "Bar = son"; (9) add AI disclosure.

**Count of complaints:** 1 words-level (minor), 4 fairness/reverence, 14 picture/era, 2 public text.
