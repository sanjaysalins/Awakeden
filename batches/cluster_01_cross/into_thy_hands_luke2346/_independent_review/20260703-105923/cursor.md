### Doctrinal soundness
Mostly orthodox. No heresy, no clickbait reading. Two real problems:

**1. Synoptic import onto a Luke anchor.**  
> “The forsaken cry has fallen silent.”

Luke 23:46’s pericope does **not** contain the forsaken cry. That line is Matthew 27:46 / Mark 15:34 / Psalm 22:1. Luke’s death scene runs Father-forgive → paradise → **Father, into thy hands** — a trust arc, not forsaken-then-resolved. The metadata note says “stated modestly” about evening prayer; it does **not** hedge this cross-Gospel splice. For a piece titled **Luke 23:46**, this is the biggest faithfulness flag: you’re narrating Matthew’s emotional beat while claiming to be in Luke’s moment.

**2. Extrabiblical hook stated as fact.**  
> “Hebrew children learned a bedtime prayer.”

Psalm 31:5 *has* been used in Jewish evening/bedtime liturgy, but the spoken line presents it as universal childhood practice — not Scripture, not in the metadata hedge. That’s tradition-as-fact on the hook that carries the whole conceit.

**3. Unused Scripture in the spine.**  
The full Psalm quote includes **“thou hast redeemed me, O LORD God of truth”** — then the narration never touches *redeemed* or *truth*. You paid KJV words for a trust-at-death claim and dropped half the verse. That’s not invented doctrine; it’s **under-grounded** doctrine.

**4. Soft flattening of death.**  
> “He dies like a child falling asleep in his father's arms”  
> “He made death a bedtime with a wake-up.”  
> “So dying and sleeping can be the same thing now.”

The corrective — **“Not because death is small. Because the hands are strong.”** — comes *after* the sleep equation is already stated. A zero-Bible listener may hear “crucifixion ≈ nap” before they hear the qualification. **“Three days later, he woke.”** extends the bedtime metaphor onto resurrection; poetic, but it can sound like Jesus merely slept through death rather than died and was raised — borderline cute for Golgotha.

**5. Rhetorical antitheses not in the text.**  
> “Not judge. Not stranger. Father.”

*Father* is the sharp, text-true observation. *Judge* and *stranger* are invented contrasts — not wicked, but not from Psalm 31 or Luke 23. Adversarial read: filler that sounds deep without exegesis.
