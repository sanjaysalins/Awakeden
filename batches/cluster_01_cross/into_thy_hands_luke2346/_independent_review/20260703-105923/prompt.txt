# Father, Into Thy Hands — Luke 23:46 (with Psalm 31:5)

Series: Words from the Cross · Cluster 1 (The Cross) · style: inked motion-comic (living page)
Note: Psalm 31:5 quoted whole; Luke 23:46 words of Jesus as clause fragment (23:46b, "my spirit" ends at the KJV colon). Evening-prayer tradition stated modestly.

[narrator] Hebrew children learned a bedtime prayer. Words from Psalm 31, prayed before sleep, night after night.
[scripture] Into thine hand I commit my spirit: thou hast redeemed me, O LORD God of truth.
[narrator] Now look at the cross. The darkness has passed. The forsaken cry has fallen silent. Jesus takes one last breath. And what he prays is the children's evening prayer.
[jesus] Father, into thy hands I commend my spirit.
[narrator] But he adds one word the Psalm does not have. Father. Not judge. Not stranger. Father.
[narrator] He dies like a child falling asleep in his father's arms, certain of morning.
[narrator] He made death a bedtime with a wake-up. Three days later, he woke.
[narrator] So dying and sleeping can be the same thing now. You can close your eyes in those same hands. Not because death is small. Because the hands are strong.
[narrator] Commit your spirit to the Father tonight. He has already kept the morning.
