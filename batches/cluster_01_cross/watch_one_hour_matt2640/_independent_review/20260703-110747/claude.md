Cache read wasn't permitted (same as round 1), so KJV verification is from my own knowledge of the AV text — these are heavily attested verses; confidence high.

**Process flag first:** the header carries a fix-note ("Panel r1 fixes applied: cup-prayer fact… invitation added…"). Per the standing unbiased-panel rule the panel should get a CLEAN artifact — this note primes reviewers to check off the old findings instead of reading fresh. I read past it, but strip it next round.

**KJV verbatim — clean.** All three quoted blocks are exact AV, including the frames: "Then saith he unto them, / My soul is exceeding sorrowful, even unto death: tarry ye here, and watch with me." (26:38, correctly split); "And he cometh unto the disciples, and findeth them asleep, and saith unto Peter, / What, could ye not watch with me one hour?" (26:40, including the correct "What," open); 26:41 whole and exact.

**R1 fixes verified as genuinely applied, not cosmetic:** the prayer's object is no longer the disciples ("went back to the prayer they could not stay awake for" is accurate to 26:42/44); an invitation now exists and is built from this piece's own verbs ("So when you cannot watch, pray anyway"); "ever spoken" is gone; the hook now leads with the failed one-hour ask.

**Finding 1 (main) — "the one who prayed that cup to the bottom": "that cup" is never introduced.** The piece quotes 26:38, 40, 41 — the cup-prayer of 26:39/42 is never quoted or narrated. A zero-knowledge listener hits the load-bearing salvation line and meets a definite article pointing at nothing: *what* cup? This fails the clarity-on-first-hearing bar at the single most important sentence. Same root cause as Finding 2 below: the piece skips verse 39 entirely. Also the metaphor is internally muddled — he *prayed about* the cup in the garden and *drank* it at the cross; "prayed that cup to the bottom, and carried it to the cross" says it was emptied in prayer and then still carried. Surgical fix, one narrator line before or inside the salvation turn, e.g.: "Three times he prayed the same prayer — if this cup may not pass, thy will be done." Then "drank that cup to the bottom on the cross" is both anchored and coherent.

**Finding 2 — "He came back" with no going-away.** After "tarry ye here, and watch with me," the next narrator line is "He came back." The listener never heard that he went anywhere (26:39, "he went a little further… and prayed," is skipped). Came back from where? Fixing Finding 1 with the cup-prayer line fixes this for free if placed before "He came back" instead of at the salvation turn. Also minor redundancy at the same spot: narrator says "He came back," then the quote immediately says "And he cometh unto the disciples" — the narrator pre-states the quote's content.

**Finding 3 (minor, carried over) — the hook now pre-spends "hour" anyway.** R1's point was to let the KJV question deliver "one hour" first. The revision moved it out of the narration body but into the hook ("They failed inside an hour"). It IS now grounded in Jesus' own words (the question defines the hour, so "failed inside an hour" is a fair inference, not an invented fact — this is no longer an accuracy problem), but the reveal-force argument is half-undone. I'd accept this as a deliberate trade — the hook needs the concrete stake — noting it, not blocking on it.

**Finding 4 (minor, residual mixed-audience) — "so that in your darkest hour, you are not."** R1 flagged the universal-sounding promise; the revision rephrased it but did not condition it. Softer than before, and the preceding CTA ("pray anyway") makes it read closer to an invitation-promise than a blanket assurance, so I rate this lower-severity than r1 did — but the half-clause fix ("for everyone who rests in him," or tying it to the "pray anyway" of the CTA) is still available at zero cost to the line's rhythm.

**Doctrine otherwise sound.** "Not a curse. A diagnosis full of mercy" is an orthodox reading of 26:41. "He knew what they were made of" rightly echoes Psalm 103:14. "Your salvation does not rest on your ability to stay awake for God" is clean substitutionary-faithfulness framing — no fear, no gain/loss, no works. One spine (watch/hour) runs unbroken hook → middle → CTA → final line; no thread-swap. "Three fishermen" is accurate (Peter and the two sons of Zebedee, all fishermen per Matt 4:18-22).

**HOOK: 8/10.** "Jesus asked three fishermen for one thing. They failed inside an hour." Concrete, specific, real curiosity gap, zero template smell. Not higher because the second line ("The night before the cross. A garden called Gethsemane.") immediately slows back into well-worn scene-setting.

**LANDING: 8/10.** "He watched that hour alone, so that in your darkest hour, you are not." Built from this exact piece's verb and noun — could not close a different piece — and the CTA before it now invites with the piece's own verbs. Docked one for the unconditioned promise (Finding 4) and because the sentence directly before it leans on the unanchored "that cup" (Finding 1), which weakens the run-up on first hearing.
