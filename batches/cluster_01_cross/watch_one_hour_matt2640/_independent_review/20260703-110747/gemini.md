# Independent review — gemini (OK, 81s)

**Doctrinal soundness:** Excellent. The substitutionary framing ("Your salvation does not rest on your ability to stay awake for God. It rests on the one who prayed that cup to the bottom") is deeply evangelical and grace-anchored. It correctly identifies Jesus' solitary faithfulness as the basis of salvation, not our own works.

**KJV verbatim:** Clean. All three quotation blocks (Matthew 26:38, 40, 41) are exact matches, properly split at the clause boundaries.

**Clarity on first hearing:** Very clear. The transition from the disciples' failure to Christ's faithfulness is easy to track.

**Grace-anchored conviction:** Strong. The script completely avoids works-righteousness or guilt-tripping the listener for failing to "stay awake," instead pointing them to the One who stayed awake for them.

**Freshness = faithful depth:** Framing the famous rebuke ("What, could ye not watch with me one hour?") not as a curse but as a "diagnosis full of mercy" is a beautiful, orthodox, and fresh angle. 

**One thread spine:** The "watch/hour" thread runs cleanly from the hook to the landing, though it frays very slightly at the final clause (see Landing below).

**HOOK SCORE:** 8/10. "Jesus asked three fishermen for one thing. They failed inside an hour." It avoids stock templates, creates immediate narrative tension, and establishes a ticking clock. 

**LANDING SCORE:** 8/10. "He watched that hour alone, so that in your darkest hour, you are not." The first half is brilliantly earned from the specific Gethsemane text and couldn't end a different piece. However, the phrase "darkest hour" is a slight Christian cliché that drifts away from the tight "watch" verb that drove the rest of the script.

VERDICT: PASS
TOP FIXES:
1. Fix the thread drift in the final line: "so that in your darkest hour, you are not." The phrase "darkest hour" is a stock cliché. Keep the vocabulary locked to the text's own verbs. (e.g., "He watched that hour alone, so you would never have to watch alone.")
2. Resolve the slight logical collision in the CTA: "So when you cannot watch, pray anyway." Jesus just commanded them to "Watch AND pray" as a paired defense; if you are spiritually asleep, you aren't praying either. Pivot on the text's own diagnosis instead (e.g., "So when your flesh is weak, pray anyway. He is already watching.")
3. Smooth the transition into the substitutionary pivot: "He knew what they were made of. Then he went back..." Changing "Then" to "So" ("So he went back to the prayer...") makes Christ's solitary prayer the direct, merciful answer to their weak flesh.
