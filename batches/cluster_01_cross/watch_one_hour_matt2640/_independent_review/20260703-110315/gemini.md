# Independent review — gemini (OK, 92s)

**Finding 1 (Fatal Textual Error) — Invented narrative regarding Jesus' prayers.** 
"Then he went back to pray for the men who could not stay awake for him. Three times." This is biblically inaccurate. In Matthew 26 (and the other Gospels), Jesus leaves them three times to pray for Himself regarding the cup of wrath ("O my Father, if it be possible, let this cup pass from me..."), not to intercede for the sleeping disciples. He explicitly told *them* to pray for themselves ("Watch and pray, that ye enter not into temptation"). Claiming He went back to pray *for the men* is an invented, contrarian reading that violates the text.

**Finding 2 — The Hook.** 
SCORE THE HOOK: 6/10. 
"The night before the cross, Jesus asked his closest friends for one thing. Stay awake with me." 
This is factually true but functions as standard, predictable Sunday School exposition. It lacks a sharp, surprising, or scroll-stopping entry point. It is too safe. 

**Finding 3 — The Landing / CTA.** 
SCORE THE LANDING: 7/10. 
"He watched that hour alone, so you would never watch one alone." 
As a concluding thought, it perfectly reuses the piece's core KJV verb ("watch") and could not end a different piece, which is excellent. However, it lacks an actual *invitation* or Call to Action (CTA). It delivers a declarative theological comfort but never actually invites the listener to turn to, trust, or come to Jesus with their own "watching" or weariness.

**Finding 4 — KJV Verbatim.** 
Clean. All quoted fragments (Matt 26:38, 40, 41) exactly match the KJV text.

**Finding 5 — Grace-Anchored Conviction.** 
Excellent. "Your salvation does not rest on your ability to stay awake for God... He stayed awake for you" is profoundly grace-anchored and explicitly anti-works.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Fix the biblical error: Jesus went back three times to pray about the cup passing from Him, not to pray "for the men."
2. Add a direct invitation (CTA) to the landing that asks the listener to respond, while retaining the strong "watch alone" imagery.
3. Sharpen the hook to be more arresting and less like standard historical exposition.
