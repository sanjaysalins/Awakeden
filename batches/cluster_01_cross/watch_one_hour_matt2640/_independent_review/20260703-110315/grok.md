# Independent review — grok (OK, 153s)

**KJV check — clean inside quotation marks.** The three Jesus lines match Matthew 26:38, 26:40, and 26:41 KJV. The scripture fragments are clause splits, not altered wording. The hook’s “Stay awake with me” is outside quotes (paraphrase of “watch with me”) — fine for narrator voice.

---

**Finding 1 (main) — “pray *for* the men” is not what Matthew records.**  
Line: *“Then he went back to pray for the men who could not stay awake for him. Three times.”*  
In Matthew 26:39–44 Jesus prays about the cup and the Father’s will — not intercession for sleeping disciples. Luke 22:32 has Peter-specific prayer; this pericope does not. Stated as flat narrative fact, this is witness over-claim — the same failure class flagged on other pieces. A hostile listener will ask: “Where does it say he prayed *for* them?” Fix: separate his lonely vigil from the cup prayer, or drop “for the men.”

---

**Finding 2 — salvation pivot skips the text’s actual subject.**  
Line: *“Your salvation does not rest on your ability to stay awake for God.”*  
Jesus’s line is *“Watch and pray, that ye enter not into temptation”* — temptation/vigilance, not salvation mechanics. The orthodox claim is fine; the jump is abrupt for a zero-Bible listener. They just heard “don’t fall into temptation,” then suddenly “salvation.” One bridging beat (temptation → flesh is weak → you can’t hold yourself → he held the hour for you) would fix clarity without changing doctrine.

---

**Finding 3 — “stayed awake” blurs watch, sleep, and agonized prayer.**  
Lines: *“He stayed awake for you. All the way to the cross.”* and *“He watched that hour alone”*  
The disciples failed to *watch* (stay vigilant); Jesus was in sorrow “unto death” and praying. “Stayed awake” is a wordplay on their nap — cute, but imprecise. He wasn’t mainly winning a vigilance contest; he was submitting to the cup. On first hearing this can sound like the point is insomnia vs. sleep, not the cross.

---

**Finding 4 (minor) — “One hour later” is inference sold as timeline.**  
Line: *“One hour later he returned.”*  
The hour comes from Jesus’s question (“could ye not watch with me one hour?”), not a narrator timestamp. Reasonable homily; stated as chronology it’s a small over-claim. Softer: “When he came back…” then let *his* question supply the hour.

---

**Finding 5 (minor) — “gentlest rebuke ever spoken” is unearned superlative.**  
Line: *“Listen to the gentlest rebuke ever spoken.”*  
Editorial hype, not text. It works emotionally but a skeptic will think of milder or harsher rebukes elsewhere. Cut the contest — the line that follows (“Not a curse. A diagnosis full of mercy.”) already lands.

---

**Finding 6 (tone/clarity) — landing is assurance, thin on invitation.**  
Closing: *“He watched that hour alone, so you would never watch one alone.”*  
Built from this piece’s image — not a stock “come to Jesus” closer. But there’s no second-person verb (trust, watch with him, pray). For a gospel-short that invites, it comforts more than it calls. Not fatal; worth noting.

---

**Doctrine otherwise:** Grace-anchored — no fear, no works-salvation, no gain/loss. One thread (watch one hour → they fail → he holds the hour → you’re not alone). No contrarian reading. Cross named in the penultimate beat.

**HOOK: 7/10** — Strong scene, not a template open; “one thing” is good but not stop-scroll sharp in the first five seconds.

**LANDING: 8/10** — Final sentence is piece-specific; couldn’t swap onto a random short. Slightly declarative vs. invitational.

Hook under 8 → **REVISE** per rubric. Main fix is the unsubstantiated “pray for the men” claim.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Remove or rephrase “pray for the men” — Matthew’s three prayers are cup/submission, not intercession for sleeping disciples; currently stated as fact.
2. Bridge temptation (“enter not into temptation” / “flesh is weak”) to the salvation line so the pivot doesn’t feel like a logic leap on first hearing.
3. Replace “stayed awake” wordplay with language tied to watch/pray/agony and the cross — keep the metaphor, lose the sleep-contest blur.
