# BLIND BRIEF - fresh angle hunt: the Lord turned and looked upon Peter (Luke 22:54-62; read with Luke 22:31-34)

You are ONE independent Bible researcher on a blind panel. Nobody else's work is shown to you.
Do not use tools, do not read files, do not ask questions. Output your answer text only.

## The series
60-second YouTube Shorts. Aim: read the WHOLE Bible (Old + New Testament) with Jesus as the lens.
Every short must LAND ON JESUS. Watched worldwide; many viewers have English as a second language
and no Bible background. KJV only.

## The problem
We want an angle that is OLD IN THE TEXT but NEW TO THE VIEWER - something the Bible itself says
(or plainly supports) that a normal churchgoer has probably never been shown. Never new doctrine.

## KILL LIST - told a million times
The list of common sermons on this passage is included right after this brief (KILL LIST).
Do NOT give any of them, or a re-worded copy of one, as your angle.

## The 4 labels - pick ONE per link, and be honest
- PROPHECY - the OT looks forward and the NT says it was fulfilled in Jesus.
- NT-STATED - the NT itself makes the link, but the OT text was history, a pattern or a vision, not a forecast.
- ECHO - same words or shape in two places. The Bible does not state the link.
- DETAIL - something in the passage itself that most people read past. (DETAIL alone never passes the gate.)
The strongest label sets the headline. An ECHO can never be the headline claim.

## TWO-BOOK RULE
Every angle must link THIS passage to at least one OTHER book of the Bible, with an exact KJV quote from
BOTH books. A detail inside one chapter may be a surprise inside the angle, never the whole angle.
An angle that stays in one book is thrown out. (Other chapters of Luke are the SAME book: they can be part of the story,
but they never count as the second book.)
It is a GOOD answer to write "NO HONEST LINK" for this passage instead of forcing one. A forced link is worse than none.

## WHY-VERSE RULE
For each link, name the ONE verse that gives the MEANING of the link (the verse that says why the two
passages belong together). If no verse says it, say so and label the link ECHO. The film must never
claim more than that verse says.

## The lesson from 70 past angles (read this first)
Past angles almost never failed for being stale. They failed on TRUTH: in 59 of 70 a claim went further
than the verse says, and in 50 of 70 a verse was used out of its own context. So:
- Say ONLY what the KJV verses say. Every claim must be checkable word by word against a quoted verse.
- The text does NOT say what Peter felt beyond "wept bitterly" (Luke 22:62), or what the look meant.
  Do not put thoughts into Jesus or Peter that a verse does not state.
- A link must be joined by a Bible writer HIMSELF to THIS scene or its own words. A verse that joins an Old Testament
  text to Jesus in general (his death, his rising) is not enough to carry a claim about this scene.
- Read the other book's passage WHOLE before you link it. Do not hide what the chapter around it says.
- No claims about Hebrew or Greek words that you cannot show from the KJV text itself.
- No claims about the courtyard, the hour or the customs of the trial unless a Bible verse states them.

## Rules
Prefer links where a Bible writer himself joins the two passages. ECHO links are usually thrown out.
No invented doctrine. No contrarian or clickbait readings. No speculation presented as fact.
No numerology or codes. No "the church got it wrong". No fear or gain/loss framing.
No Hebrew/Greek claims unless they are plain standard lexicon facts.
Do not put motives or feelings into a text that does not state them.
Surprising about the TEXT, never about the TRUTH. Fewer honest angles beat 5 weak ones; "NO HONEST LINK" is allowed.
End with: MY TOP PICK = <title> and one line why.

## Luke 22:54-62
Luke 22:54 Then took they him, and led him, and brought him into the high priest’s house. And Peter followed afar off.
Luke 22:55 And when they had kindled a fire in the midst of the hall, and were set down together, Peter sat down among them.
Luke 22:56 But a certain maid beheld him as he sat by the fire, and earnestly looked upon him, and said, This man was also with him.
Luke 22:57 And he denied him, saying, Woman, I know him not.
Luke 22:58 And after a little while another saw him, and said, Thou art also of them. And Peter said, Man, I am not.
Luke 22:59 And about the space of one hour after another confidently affirmed, saying, Of a truth this fellow also was with him: for he is a Galilaean.
Luke 22:60 And Peter said, Man, I know not what thou sayest. And immediately, while he yet spake, the cock crew.
Luke 22:61 And the Lord turned, and looked upon Peter. And Peter remembered the word of the Lord, how he had said unto him, Before the cock crow, thou shalt deny me thrice.
Luke 22:62 And Peter went out, and wept bitterly.

## Luke 22:31-34

Luke 22:32 But I have prayed for thee, that thy faith fail not: and when thou art converted, strengthen thy brethren.
Luke 22:33 And he said unto him, Lord, I am ready to go with thee, both into prison, and to death.
Luke 22:34 And he said, I tell thee, Peter, the cock shall not crow this day, before that thou shalt thrice deny that thou knowest me.
