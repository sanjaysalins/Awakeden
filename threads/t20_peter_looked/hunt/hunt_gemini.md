1. TITLE: The look that triggered the word
2. HOOK QUESTION: Why did Jesus' look make Peter remember His word?
3. ANCHOR-1: Luke 22:61 "remembered the word of the Lord"
   ANCHOR-2: Psalm 119:49 "Remember the word unto thy servant"
3b. WHY-VERSE: Psalm 119:49 "upon which thou hast caused me to hope."
4. LABEL: ECHO
5. WHAT IT DOES *NOT* PROVE: It does not prove Peter was reciting Psalm 119 while he wept in the dark.
6. FACTS:
   - Peter denied knowing Jesus three times (Luke 22:57-60)
   - The Lord turned and looked upon Peter (Luke 22:61)
   - Immediately, Peter remembered the word of the Lord (Luke 22:61)
   - The Psalmist says remembering the word is what causes us to hope (Psalm 119:49)
7. SO WHAT: Jesus turns to look upon you in your worst shame, bringing His word back to your memory so you find hope. (Backed by Psalm 119:49)
8. WHY MOST CHURCHGOERS HAVE NOT HEARD IT: We focus heavily on Peter's bitter weeping, missing that remembering the word is the biblical anchor for hope. (8/10)
9. HOW IT LANDS ON JESUS: Jesus initiates the look that brings the word of hope back into the mind of His denier.
10. YOUR CONFIDENCE: 9/10

1. TITLE: The snare of the earnest look
2. HOOK QUESTION: Which look actually caused Peter to fail that night?
3. ANCHOR-1: Luke 22:56 "earnestly looked upon him"
   ANCHOR-2: Proverbs 29:25 "The fear of man bringeth a snare"
3b. WHY-VERSE: Proverbs 29:25 "The fear of man bringeth a snare"
4. LABEL: ECHO
5. WHAT IT DOES *NOT* PROVE: It does not prove the maid was a deliberate agent of Satan trying to trap him.
6. FACTS:
   - A certain maid earnestly looked upon Peter by the fire (Luke 22:56)
   - Peter denied Jesus to her, saying "I know him not" (Luke 22:57)
   - The Lord later turned and looked upon Peter (Luke 22:61)
   - Proverbs warns that the fear of man brings a snare (Proverbs 29:25)
7. SO WHAT: Jesus turns and looks upon you to break the snare, even after the fear of human eyes makes you deny Him. (Backed by Proverbs 29:25)
8. WHY MOST CHURCHGOERS HAVE NOT HEARD IT: We notice Jesus looking at Peter, but skip over the maid earnestly looking at him first to trigger the fear. (7/10)
9. HOW IT LANDS ON JESUS: Jesus' look is the only thing that can break the snare created by our fear of others.
10. YOUR CONFIDENCE: 8/10

1. TITLE: Naked and opened to His eyes
2. HOOK QUESTION: What did Jesus' eyes actually do to Peter?
3. ANCHOR-1: Luke 22:61 "looked upon Peter. And Peter remembered"
   ANCHOR-2: Hebrews 4:13 "naked and opened unto the eyes of him"
3b. WHY-VERSE: Hebrews 4:12 "For the word of God is quick, and powerful"
4. LABEL: ECHO
5. WHAT IT DOES *NOT* PROVE: It does not prove the writer of Hebrews was thinking of Peter's denial when writing the epistle.
6. FACTS:
   - The Lord turned and looked upon Peter (Luke 22:61)
   - The immediate result was that Peter remembered the word of the Lord (Luke 22:61)
   - Hebrews says all things are open to the eyes of Him (Hebrews 4:13)
   - Hebrews links those piercing eyes directly to the quick and powerful word of God (Hebrews 4:12-13)
7. SO WHAT: Jesus sees you completely in your failure, but His look points you straight to His word instead of leaving you in the dark. (Backed by Hebrews 4:12-13)
8. WHY MOST CHURCHGOERS HAVE NOT HEARD IT: We reduce Jesus' look to an expression of emotional disappointment, rather than the piercing trigger of the Word. (9/10)
9. HOW IT LANDS ON JESUS: Jesus uses His own eyes to remind us of His own word when we have failed Him.
10. YOUR CONFIDENCE: 9/10

1. TITLE: The prophecy of the distant friend
2. HOOK QUESTION: Why did Luke specifically note where Peter was standing?
3. ANCHOR-1: Luke 22:54 "And Peter followed afar off"
   ANCHOR-2: Psalm 38:11 "my kinsmen stand afar off"
3b. WHY-VERSE: No verse says it.
4. LABEL: ECHO
5. WHAT IT DOES *NOT* PROVE: It does not prove "following afar off" is just a moral warning about backsliding; it is a prophetic picture.
6. FACTS:
   - Jesus was taken and led into the high priest's house (Luke 22:54)
   - Peter, His close disciple, followed afar off (Luke 22:54)
   - Psalm 38 describes a sufferer surrounded by enemies who seek his hurt (Psalm 38:12)
   - The Psalm declares the sufferer's lovers, friends, and kinsmen stand afar off from his sore (Psalm 38:11)
7. SO WHAT: Jesus walked into the high priest's house alone, so that even when fear makes you follow afar off, you are not forsaken. (Backed by Hebrews 13:5)
8. WHY MOST CHURCHGOERS HAVE NOT HEARD IT: We turn Peter standing afar off into a sermon about our own bad habits, missing that it perfectly echoes the abandoned Christ in the Psalms. (9/10)
9. HOW IT LANDS ON JESUS: Jesus walked into the trial knowing even His closest friends would abandon Him exactly as the Psalms described.
10. YOUR CONFIDENCE: 9/10

1. TITLE: The prisoner who turned to show mercy
2. HOOK QUESTION: Who really needed mercy in the high priest's courtyard?
3. ANCHOR-1: Luke 22:61 "the Lord turned, and looked upon Peter"
   ANCHOR-2: Psalm 25:16 "Turn thee unto me, and have mercy upon me"
3b. WHY-VERSE: No verse says it.
4. LABEL: ECHO
5. WHAT IT DOES *NOT* PROVE: It does not prove Peter was consciously praying Psalm 25 at that exact second.
6. FACTS:
   - Peter denied knowing Jesus three times to save himself (Luke 22:57-60)
   - Jesus was the actual prisoner in the high priest's house (Luke 22:54)
   - David prayed for the Lord to turn and have mercy because David was desolate and afflicted (Psalm 25:16)
   - Here, Jesus is the desolate prisoner, but He is the one who turns to look upon Peter (Luke 22:61)
7. SO WHAT: Jesus turns and looks upon you with mercy, even when your failure makes you deny that you know Him. (Backed by Lamentations 3:22)
8. WHY MOST CHURCHGOERS HAVE NOT HEARD IT: We focus on Peter's tears, but miss the staggering grace that the bound prisoner turned to rescue the free man. (9/10)
9. HOW IT LANDS ON JESUS: Jesus extends mercy to His denier even while enduring His own mock trial.
10. YOUR CONFIDENCE: 9/10
