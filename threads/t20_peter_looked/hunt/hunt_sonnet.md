# Blind panel answer (Sonnet seat): Luke 22:54-62

Honest note up front: the strongest-labelled angle is #1 (NT-STATED). Angles 2, 3, 4, 5 are ECHO at best, so none of them can be a headline claim on its own. I give them because the shared KJV words are real and checkable. Freshness scores are guesses.

---

## ANGLE 1

1. TITLE: "Follow afar off" becomes "Follow me"
2. HOOK QUESTION: Peter followed Jesus badly that night. Did Jesus ever tell him to follow again?
3. ANCHOR-1: Luke 22:54 "And Peter followed afar off."
   ANCHOR-2: John 21:19 "And when he had spoken this, he saith unto him, Follow me."
3b. WHY-VERSE: John 13:36 "Whither I go, thou canst not follow me now; but thou shalt follow me afterwards."
4. LABEL: NT-STATED (both ends are in the NT; John himself reports Jesus tying "follow" to Peter in 13:36, and states in 21:19 what the words in 21:18 were "signifying"). No OT link is claimed.
5. DOES NOT PROVE: It does not say Peter's denial was fine or cost nothing. It does not say what Peter felt at "Follow me". John 13:36 does not mention the courtyard; it says only that following was for "afterwards".
6. FACTS:
   - Peter followed "afar off" into the high priest's house: Luke 22:54.
   - Before this, Peter asked "why cannot I follow thee now? I will lay down my life for thy sake": John 13:37.
   - Jesus said Peter could not follow "now" but would "afterwards": John 13:36.
   - Right after, Jesus told Peter he would deny him: John 13:38 (read with Luke 22:34).
   - After the rising, Jesus says to Peter "Follow me": John 21:19.
   - John says the earlier words were "signifying by what death he should glorify God": John 21:19.
7. SO WHAT: Jesus said Peter's following was only waiting, "afterwards"; so when you have followed badly, "afar off" is not the last word He says to you. Backing verse for all believers: John 12:26 "If any man serve me, let him follow me".
8. WHY NOT HEARD: Sermons use "afar off" as a warning (kill list); few set it beside John 13:36 and 21:19 as one promise-and-answer. Freshness 5/10.
9. LANDS ON JESUS: Jesus says "afterwards" before the fall, and says "Follow me" after it.
10. CONFIDENCE: 8/10.

## ANGLE 2

1. TITLE: The denier tells a crowd how to come back
2. HOOK QUESTION: Peter said "I know him not." Later, what did he tell others who denied Jesus?
3. ANCHOR-1: Luke 22:57 "And he denied him, saying, Woman, I know him not."
   ANCHOR-2: Acts 3:14 "But ye denied the Holy One and the Just"
3b. WHY-VERSE: No verse says Peter was thinking of his own denial. The nearest verse that gives the meaning of what he offers: Acts 3:19 "Repent ye therefore, and be converted, that your sins may be blotted out". The link between the scenes is only the shared words, so: ECHO.
4. LABEL: ECHO (the words "denied" and "converted"; Luke 22:32 "when thou art converted" and Acts 3:19 "be converted"). Not a headline claim.
5. DOES NOT PROVE: It does not prove Peter meant his own story. It does not say Peter's denial and the crowd's are the same thing. Acts 3 is also a hard speech: it says "ye ... killed the Prince of life" (3:15); it also says "I wot that through ignorance ye did it" (3:17). I must not hide either.
6. FACTS:
   - Peter is the speaker: Acts 3:12 "Peter answered unto the people".
   - He says the crowd "denied him in the presence of Pilate": Acts 3:13.
   - Jesus told Peter "when thou art converted": Luke 22:32.
   - Peter tells the crowd "be converted": Acts 3:19.
   - Peter denied three times in Luke 22:57, 58, 60 (once to a maid, then "another", then "another").
7. SO WHAT: Jesus turned and looked at the man who said "I know him not", and that same man later told others who denied Jesus there was a way back. Backing verse for all believers: Acts 3:19 (said to a whole crowd, "Repent ye therefore, and be converted").
8. WHY NOT HEARD: Churchgoers know Pentecost (kill list) but rarely read Acts 3 beside Luke 22:32. Freshness 7/10.
9. LANDS ON JESUS: The invitation in Acts 3:19-20 ends with "he shall send Jesus Christ".
10. CONFIDENCE: 7/10 (words are exact; the link is only an echo and I say so).

## ANGLE 3

1. TITLE: Two verses about denial: one hard, one kind
2. HOOK QUESTION: Jesus said He would deny those who deny Him. So what about Peter?
3. ANCHOR-1: Luke 22:61 "Before the cock crow, thou shalt deny me thrice."
   ANCHOR-2: 2 Timothy 2:13 "If we believe not, yet he abideth faithful: he cannot deny himself."
3b. WHY-VERSE: 2 Timothy 2:12 "if we deny him, he also will deny us". The text itself holds both sentences side by side (12 and 13). No verse says how they apply to Peter. ECHO.
4. LABEL: ECHO (shared word "deny"). Matthew 10:33 is the other half: "whosoever shall deny me before men, him will I also deny before my Father which is in heaven."
5. DOES NOT PROVE: It does not prove Peter was "safe" or that denial has no weight. Matthew 10:33 and 2 Timothy 2:12 are real warnings. The Bible does not say in these verses how the two fit for Peter. I will not settle that tension.
6. FACTS:
   - Peter said "I know him not" (Luke 22:57) and "I am not" (22:58), and "I know not what thou sayest" (22:60).
   - Jesus said "whosoever shall deny me before men, him will I also deny": Matthew 10:33.
   - Paul wrote the "faithful saying" in 2 Timothy 2:11-13; v13 follows the warning v12.
   - Peter "wept bitterly": Luke 22:62.
7. SO WHAT: Peter said "I know him not", and the Lord turned and looked on him; and of Jesus the Bible says "he abideth faithful". Backing verse for all believers: 2 Timothy 2:13 ("If we believe not, yet he abideth faithful").
8. WHY NOT HEARD: People quote Matthew 10:33 or 2 Timothy 2:13 alone; few lay them next to Luke 22:57-61. Freshness 6/10.
9. LANDS ON JESUS: "he cannot deny himself" is about Jesus.
10. CONFIDENCE: 6/10 (the weakest on overclaim risk; the film must show both verses and not resolve them).

## ANGLE 4

1. TITLE: The man who remembered, writes "remembrance"
2. HOOK QUESTION: Peter remembered one sentence of Jesus that night. What did he do with remembering later?
3. ANCHOR-1: Luke 22:61 "And Peter remembered the word of the Lord"
   ANCHOR-2: 2 Peter 1:13 "to stir you up by putting you in remembrance"
3b. WHY-VERSE: 2 Peter 3:2 "That ye may be mindful of the words which were spoken before by the holy prophets, and of the commandment of us the apostles of the Lord and Saviour". It says the reason for remembrance; it does not mention the courtyard. ECHO.
4. LABEL: ECHO (shared word "remember / remembrance"). Not a headline claim.
5. DOES NOT PROVE: It does not prove Peter wrote 2 Peter because of that night. The letter does not say that. It only shows Peter, in his own letter, stirring up others by "remembrance".
6. FACTS:
   - Luke 22:61 says Peter remembered "the word of the Lord".
   - The letter opens "Simon Peter": 2 Peter 1:1.
   - He says "I stir up your pure minds by way of remembrance": 2 Peter 3:1.
   - The remembering is of "the words ... spoken before by the holy prophets" and "the commandment of us the apostles": 2 Peter 3:2.
   - Jesus said "strengthen thy brethren": Luke 22:32.
7. SO WHAT: The Lord's word came back to Peter at the worst moment, and the Lord's word is what Peter later passes on. Backing verse for all believers: 2 Peter 3:2 (written to "beloved").
8. WHY NOT HEARD: The "word came back to his mind" point is on the kill list; the link to Peter's own letters is rarely drawn. Freshness 5/10.
9. LANDS ON JESUS: The thing remembered is "the word of the Lord", and the letter closes on "our Lord and Saviour Jesus Christ" (2 Peter 3:18).
10. CONFIDENCE: 7/10.

## ANGLE 5

1. TITLE: "I know him not" and "thou knowest all things"
2. HOOK QUESTION: Peter said he did not know Jesus. Later he said who does know everything?
3. ANCHOR-1: Luke 22:57 "Woman, I know him not."
   ANCHOR-2: John 21:17 "Lord, thou knowest all things; thou knowest that I love thee."
3b. WHY-VERSE: John 10:14 "I am the good shepherd, and know my sheep, and am known of mine." It says who knows whom, but it does not name Peter or the denial. So: ECHO.
4. LABEL: ECHO (word "know / knowest"; Luke 22:34 "that thou knowest me", 22:57 "I know him not", 22:60 "I know not what thou sayest").
5. DOES NOT PROVE: It does not prove Peter was answering his own denial in John 21:17. John says only "Peter was grieved because he said unto him the third time". It adds no reason. It is also close to the "three questions" kill-list item, so the film must not retell the three-for-three pattern.
6. FACTS:
   - Peter said "I know him not" in 22:57 and "I know not what thou sayest" in 22:60.
   - Jesus' own words about it began "that thou knowest me" (Luke 22:34).
   - In John 21:17 Peter is asked a third time "lovest thou me?" and answers "thou knowest all things".
   - John 10:14 gives "know my sheep" in Jesus' own voice.
7. SO WHAT: Peter said "I know him not"; yet when asked later he leaned on what the Lord knows. Backing verse for all believers: John 10:27 "My sheep hear my voice, and I know them, and they follow me".
8. WHY NOT HEARD: The "know" thread is easy to miss in English; most hear only "three denials, three questions". Freshness 4/10.
9. LANDS ON JESUS: The knowing that holds is Jesus' knowing: "I know my sheep".
10. CONFIDENCE: 6/10 (risk: near a kill-list item and ECHO only).
