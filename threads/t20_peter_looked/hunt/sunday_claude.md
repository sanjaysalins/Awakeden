- Even the boldest believer can fall; Peter's "I am ready to go with thee" ended in "I know him not" (Luke 22:33, 22:57)
- Following "afar off" is the first step toward denial; distance from Jesus comes before the fall (Luke 22:54)
- Warming yourself at the enemy's fire, sitting with the wrong crowd, leads to compromise (Luke 22:55)
- Sin grows step by step: a denial, then a second, then a third and stronger one (Luke 22:57-60)
- Jesus prayed for Peter before he fell, so his faith would not fail in the end (Luke 22:32)
- Jesus' look was not anger but love; it broke Peter's heart and brought him to repentance (Luke 22:61)
- Peter's bitter tears show true godly sorrow, not just regret, and set up his restoration (Luke 22:62)
- God's word always comes true; the rooster crowed just as Jesus said (Luke 22:34, 22:60-61)
- Cross-ref: Jesus restores Peter with three "lovest thou me?" questions, matching his three denials (John 21:15-17)
- Cross-ref: Satan asked to sift Peter as wheat, but Jesus prayed for him (Luke 22:31-32)
- Cross-ref: Godly sorrow works repentance; worldly sorrow works death, as with Peter vs Judas (2 Corinthians 7:10; Matthew 27:3-5)
- Cross-ref: "Let him that thinketh he standeth take heed lest he fall" (1 Corinthians 10:12)
- Cross-ref: Peter, once afraid of a servant girl, preaches boldly at Pentecost (Acts 2:14, 2:36-41)
