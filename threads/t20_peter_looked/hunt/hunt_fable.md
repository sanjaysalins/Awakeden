# Fresh angle hunt - Luke 22:54-62 (the Lord turned and looked upon Peter)

HONEST NOTE FIRST
No Bible writer outside the four Gospels names Peter's denial or the look. So no link below is joined to THIS scene by a
writer's own statement. Every cross-book link is therefore labelled ECHO (same words or shape), with the in-passage part
labelled DETAIL. I have not raised any label to PROPHECY or NT-STATED, because that would be an overclaim.
The strongest ones are the Luke -> Acts links: the same writer wrote both books and re-uses his own words about the same man.
All quotes are from memory of the KJV: please check each one word by word.

Links I looked at and REJECTED as forced (do not use):
- Zechariah 12:10 ("look upon me whom they have pierced ... bitterness"): John 19:37 ties it to the piercing, not to Peter.
- Isaiah 22:4 ("I will weep bitterly"): about Jerusalem's ruin, not this scene.
- Amos 9:9 (sifting): a judgment on Israel; the sifting word is in Luke 22:31, not in 22:54-62.
- 2 Timothy 2:12-13 ("if we deny him ... he abideth faithful"): Paul does not mention Peter.

## ANGLE 1

1. TITLE: The denier says "ye denied"
2. HOOK QUESTION: What did Peter tell a crowd that had denied Jesus too?
3. ANCHOR-1: Luke 22:57 "And he denied him, saying, Woman, I know him not."
   ANCHOR-2: Acts 3:14 "But ye denied the Holy One and the Just, and desired a murderer to be granted unto you;"
3b. WHY-VERSE: Acts 3:19 "Repent ye therefore, and be converted, that your sins may be blotted out, when the times of refreshing shall come from the presence of the Lord;"
   (This verse gives the meaning of Peter's sermon. NO verse says Peter was thinking of his own denial. So the link to Luke 22 is ECHO.)
4. LABEL: DETAIL (Luke 22:57 "denied"; Luke 22:32 "converted") + ECHO (Acts 3:13-14, 3:19 - same writer, same two words, not stated as a link)
5. WHAT IT DOES *NOT* PROVE: It does not prove Peter had his own denial in mind, or that he confessed it to the crowd. The crowd's denial "in the presence of Pilate" is a different act from Peter's. We may only show the same words in the same man's story.
6. FACTS:
   - Peter is the speaker in Acts 3. (Acts 3:12 "And when Peter saw it, he answered unto the people")
   - Peter says "denied" twice to the crowd. (Acts 3:13 "denied him in the presence of Pilate"; Acts 3:14 "ye denied the Holy One and the Just")
   - Jesus had used "converted" of Peter before the denial. (Luke 22:32 "when thou art converted, strengthen thy brethren")
   - Peter uses "converted" to the crowd. (Acts 3:19 "be converted")
   - Peter calls the crowd "brethren". (Acts 3:17 "And now, brethren, I wot that through ignorance ye did it")
   - Luke and Acts are addressed to the same man by the same writer. (Luke 1:3 "most excellent Theophilus"; Acts 1:1 "The former treatise have I made, O Theophilus")
   - Peter denied three times. (Luke 22:57, 22:58, 22:60; Luke 22:61 "thou shalt deny me thrice")
7. SO WHAT: Jesus, the one who was "denied", still offers that sins "may be blotted out" - so the shame of having denied him does not have to be the end of your story.
   Verse for all: Acts 10:43 "To him give all the prophets witness, that through his name whosoever believeth in him shall receive remission of sins."
8. WHY NOT HEARD: Sermons jump from the denial to John 21 or to Pentecost; few read Acts 3 next to Luke 22 and hear the same two words. Fresh: 7
9. LANDS ON JESUS: The man who said "I know him not" stands up and names Jesus "the Holy One and the Just", the one through whom sins are blotted out.
10. CONFIDENCE: 7 (the words are plainly there; the link itself is unstated, so the film must only SHOW the words side by side)

## ANGLE 2

1. TITLE: "With him": the same charge, again
2. HOOK QUESTION: What happened when rulers said Peter had "been with Jesus"?
3. ANCHOR-1: Luke 22:56 "and earnestly looked upon him, and said, This man was also with him."
   ANCHOR-2: Acts 4:13 "and they took knowledge of them, that they had been with Jesus."
3b. WHY-VERSE: Acts 4:8 "Then Peter, filled with the Holy Ghost, said unto them, Ye rulers of the people, and elders of Israel,"
   (This verse says what Peter was filled with when he spoke. NO verse says Acts 4 answers Luke 22. So the link is ECHO.)
4. LABEL: DETAIL (Luke 22:56, 22:59 "with him" said twice) + ECHO (Acts 4:6-13, same writer)
5. WHAT IT DOES *NOT* PROVE: It does not prove Peter stood in the same building, or that Luke meant Acts 4 as the "undoing" of the denial. It does not say Peter felt no fear. Only: the same kind of charge, a different answer, and Acts 4:8 says he was filled with the Holy Ghost.
6. FACTS:
   - Peter was accused of being "with him" twice. (Luke 22:56; Luke 22:59 "Of a truth this fellow also was with him")
   - The denial was in the high priest's house. (Luke 22:54 "brought him into the high priest's house")
   - In Acts 4 the high priest and his kindred are present. (Acts 4:6 "And Annas the high priest, and Caiaphas, and John, and Alexander, and as many as were of the kindred of the high priest")
   - This time Peter names Jesus. (Acts 4:10 "by the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth, whom ye crucified, whom God raised from the dead")
   - The rulers saw boldness. (Acts 4:13 "Now when they saw the boldness of Peter and John")
   - John was with Peter in Acts 4. (Acts 4:13)
7. SO WHAT: The Lord "looked upon Peter" when he failed, and later filled him so that men could see he "had been with Jesus" - failure under fear was not the last thing said about him.
   Verse for all: Acts 2:38-39 "ye shall receive the gift of the Holy Ghost. For the promise is unto you, and to your children, and to all that are afar off, even as many as the Lord our God shall call."
8. WHY NOT HEARD: People know "Peter became bold" (Pentecost, Acts 2). Few notice the exact charge "with him" coming back in front of the high priest's own family. Fresh: 5
   WARNING: this is a cousin of the kill-list item "afraid of a servant girl, bold at Pentecost". It uses a different chapter and a word link, but a judge may call it too close.
9. LANDS ON JESUS: The name Peter would not own by the fire is the name he says out loud: "Jesus Christ of Nazareth ... whom God raised from the dead".
10. CONFIDENCE: 7

## ANGLE 3

1. TITLE: "Prison and death": both came later
2. HOOK QUESTION: Did Peter's broken promise to Jesus ever come true?
3. ANCHOR-1: Luke 22:60 "And Peter said, Man, I know not what thou sayest."
   (the boast it breaks: Luke 22:33 "Lord, I am ready to go with thee, both into prison, and to death.")
   ANCHOR-2: Acts 12:5 "Peter therefore was kept in prison: but prayer was made without ceasing of the church unto God for him."
   Second support, for "death": John 21:19 "This spake he, signifying by what death he should glorify God. And when he had spoken this, he saith unto him, Follow me."
3b. WHY-VERSE: John 21:19 (above) gives the meaning of Jesus' words about Peter's death. NO verse says the prison of Acts or the death of John 21 "keeps" the boast of Luke 22:33. So the link is ECHO.
4. LABEL: DETAIL (Luke 22:33 against 22:57-60) + ECHO (Acts 5:18, Acts 12:4-6, John 21:18-19)
5. WHAT IT DOES *NOT* PROVE: The Bible does not record Peter's death, only that Jesus signified it. It does not say Peter "earned back" his promise, or that prison paid for the denial. Do not say Peter went to prison "with" Jesus in the sense he meant that night.
6. FACTS:
   - Peter named two things: prison and death. (Luke 22:33)
   - That same night he said he did not know Jesus. (Luke 22:57, 22:60)
   - Peter was later put in prison with the apostles. (Acts 5:18 "And laid their hands on the apostles, and put them in the common prison.")
   - Peter was put in prison again by Herod. (Acts 12:4 "And when he had apprehended him, he put him in prison")
   - In that prison Peter slept. (Acts 12:6 "the same night Peter was sleeping between two soldiers, bound with two chains")
   - Jesus had prayed for Peter; later the church prayed for Peter. (Luke 22:32 "But I have prayed for thee"; Acts 12:5)
   - Jesus spoke of Peter's death after he rose. (John 21:18-19)
   - Peter himself later wrote of it. (2 Peter 1:14 "Knowing that shortly I must put off this my tabernacle, even as our Lord Jesus Christ hath shewed me.")
   - In John, on the night of the warning, Jesus said: (John 13:36 "Whither I go, thou canst not follow me now; but thou shalt follow me afterwards.")
7. SO WHAT: Jesus "looked upon Peter" on the night his big promise broke, and afterwards said to him "Follow me" - your worst failure does not cancel the purpose he has for you.
   Verse for all: Philippians 1:6 "Being confident of this very thing, that he which hath begun a good work in you will perform it until the day of Jesus Christ:"
   (written to the saints at Philippi - Philippians 1:1; check it is fair to use for all believers.)
8. WHY NOT HEARD: The boast is preached as pride and then dropped. Few follow the two words "prison" and "death" forward into Acts and John 21. Fresh: 7
9. LANDS ON JESUS: Jesus knew the promise would break that night, and still spoke of a day when Peter would "glorify God".
10. CONFIDENCE: 6 (each fact is in the text; the join between them is ours, so the film must say "later", never "fulfilled")

## ANGLE 4

1. TITLE: Looked upon, then seen by name
2. HOOK QUESTION: After Jesus rose, which failed friend is named on his own?
3. ANCHOR-1: Luke 22:61 "And the Lord turned, and looked upon Peter."
   ANCHOR-2: 1 Corinthians 15:5 "And that he was seen of Cephas, then of the twelve:"
   Second support: Mark 16:7 "But go your way, tell his disciples and Peter that he goeth before you into Galilee: there shall ye see him, as he said unto you."
3b. WHY-VERSE: 1 Corinthians 15:3 "For I delivered unto you first of all that which I also received, how that Christ died for our sins according to the scriptures;"
   (This gives the meaning of Paul's list: the gospel. NO verse says WHY Peter is named alone, and none ties it to the look. So the link is ECHO.)
4. LABEL: DETAIL (Luke 22:61, the last thing Luke records between Jesus and Peter before the cross) + ECHO (1 Corinthians 15:5; Mark 16:7)
5. WHAT IT DOES *NOT* PROVE: It does not prove Peter was the first person to see the risen Jesus (Mark 16:9 names Mary Magdalene first). It does not say why Peter is named apart, or what was said when he saw the Lord. Do not say "because he had denied him" as a Bible statement.
6. FACTS:
   - Cephas is Peter. (John 1:42 "thou shalt be called Cephas, which is by interpretation, A stone.")
   - Paul names Cephas first in his list of those who saw the risen Christ. (1 Corinthians 15:5-8)
   - Luke also records a meeting with Peter alone, with no details. (Luke 24:34 "The Lord is risen indeed, and hath appeared to Simon.")
   - The message at the tomb names Peter apart from "his disciples". (Mark 16:7)
   - In Luke, after the look, Peter "went out"; Luke records no word between them before the cross. (Luke 22:62)
   - Paul's list rests on Christ dying "for our sins". (1 Corinthians 15:3)
7. SO WHAT: The Lord "looked upon Peter" in the hour he failed, and after he rose he "was seen of" that same man - Christ died for our sins, so the one who fails him is not left unseen.
   Verse for all: 1 Corinthians 15:3 "Christ died for our sins according to the scriptures"
8. WHY NOT HEARD: "And Peter" (Mark 16:7) is sometimes preached, but Luke 24:34 and 1 Corinthians 15:5 are almost never read beside the look. Fresh: 5
9. LANDS ON JESUS: The same Lord who looked at Peter in the hall is the risen Lord who let Peter see him.
10. CONFIDENCE: 6

## ANGLE 5

1. TITLE: Peter writes the warning he once heard
2. HOOK QUESTION: What did Peter write, years later, about being attacked and falling?
3. ANCHOR-1: Luke 22:61 "And Peter remembered the word of the Lord, how he had said unto him"
   ANCHOR-2: 1 Peter 5:8 "Be sober, be vigilant; because your adversary the devil, as a roaring lion, walketh about, seeking whom he may devour:"
   with 1 Peter 5:10 "But the God of all grace, who hath called us unto his eternal glory by Christ Jesus, after that ye have suffered a while, make you perfect, stablish, strengthen, settle you."
3b. WHY-VERSE: 1 Peter 5:10 (above) gives the meaning of Peter's warning: the God of all grace will strengthen. NO verse says Peter is recalling the night of the denial. So the link is ECHO.
4. LABEL: DETAIL (Luke 22:61 "remembered the word"; Luke 22:31-32) + ECHO (1 Peter 5:8-10)
5. WHAT IT DOES *NOT* PROVE: It does not prove Peter was quoting Jesus or thinking of the hall and the cock. It does not say Peter "strengthened his brethren" BY this letter in fulfilment of Luke 22:32 - no verse says so. Only: four of the same things appear in both places.
6. FACTS:
   - Jesus' warning to Peter had: Satan seeking him, his faith, strengthening, his brethren. (Luke 22:31 "Satan hath desired to have you"; Luke 22:32 "that thy faith fail not ... strengthen thy brethren")
   - Peter's letter has the same four: the devil seeking, faith, strengthen, brethren. (1 Peter 5:8 "seeking whom he may devour"; 5:9 "Whom resist stedfast in the faith, knowing that the same afflictions are accomplished in your brethren that are in the world."; 5:10 "strengthen")
   - The letter is from Peter. (1 Peter 1:1 "Peter, an apostle of Jesus Christ")
   - Peter calls himself a witness of Christ's sufferings. (1 Peter 5:1 "a witness of the sufferings of Christ")
   - Peter remembered Jesus' word at the cock crow. (Luke 22:61)
   - Peter later made "remembrance" his stated aim. (2 Peter 1:12 "I will not be negligent to put you always in remembrance of these things")
7. SO WHAT: Jesus warned Peter and then "looked upon" him when he fell, and the same Peter wrote that "the God of all grace" will "strengthen" you - you are not left alone with your failure.
   Verse for all: 1 Peter 5:10 (written to believers scattered in many lands - 1 Peter 1:1-2)
8. WHY NOT HEARD: 1 Peter 5:8 is famous on its own ("roaring lion"), but it is seldom set beside Jesus' warning to the very man who wrote it. Fresh: 6
9. LANDS ON JESUS: Peter says grace comes "by Christ Jesus" - the same Jesus who prayed for him before he fell.
10. CONFIDENCE: 6
