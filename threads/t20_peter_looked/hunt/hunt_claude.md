# Blind seat — Driver 2 (THE ENCOUNTER): Luke 22:54-62, read with Luke 22:31-34

A note before the angles: only two of my links are carried by a verse in which a Bible writer makes the link himself (Angles 1 and 2). The other three are ECHO links that I've labelled honestly. They are fresh and accurate, but by the rules none can be a headline on its own. I've kept them in so the panel can judge them.

---

## ANGLE 1

1. **TITLE:** The prisoner who looked was "reckoned among transgressors"

2. **HOOK QUESTION:** Which old prophecy did Jesus quote hours before Peter said "I know him not"?

3. **ANCHORS**
   - **ANCHOR-1:** Luke 22:54 "Then took they him, and led him, and brought him into the high priest's house"
   - **ANCHOR-2:** Isaiah 53:12 "he was numbered with the transgressors; and he bare the sin of many, and made intercession for the transgressors."

   3b. **WHY-VERSE:** Luke 22:37 "this that is written must yet be accomplished in me, And he was reckoned among the transgressors: for the things concerning me have an end."

4. **LABEL**
   - Link 1 (Isaiah 53:12a to Jesus held as a prisoner that night): **PROPHECY**. Jesus says it "must yet be accomplished in me."
   - Link 2 (Isaiah 53:12b "made intercession for the transgressors" to Luke 22:32 "I have prayed for thee"): **ECHO**. Jesus quoted only the first half of the verse.

5. **WHAT IT DOES *NOT* PROVE**
   - Luke 22:37 does not call Peter "the transgressor."
   - It does not say the prayer of Luke 22:32 fulfils Isaiah 53:12b. The headline can only say: "Jesus said Isaiah 53 was being fulfilled in him that night, while he was treated as a criminal."

6. **FACTS**
   - Jesus quotes Isaiah 53 at the table after warning Peter, and before going to the mount of Olives (Luke 22:34, 22:37, 22:39).
   - At his arrest Jesus says they came "as against a thief, with swords and staves" (Luke 22:52).
   - He is led into the high priest's house as a prisoner (Luke 22:54).
   - The men who held him mocked him (Luke 22:63).
   - The same Isaiah verse also says "made intercession for the transgressors" (Isaiah 53:12).
   - Jesus had told Peter "I have prayed for thee" (Luke 22:32).

7. **SO WHAT:** The man who "turned, and looked upon Peter" was being treated as a criminal himself, and he had already prayed for Peter before the failure. He still prays for his people.
   - Backing verse for all believers: Hebrews 7:25 "he ever liveth to make intercession for them."
   - Touches: failure, shame.

8. **WHY MOST HAVEN'T HEARD IT:** Luke 22:37 is usually read as being about swords, and few people read on to the end of Isaiah 53:12. **Freshness: 7/10**

9. **HOW IT LANDS ON JESUS:** Jesus said the "transgressors" prophecy was coming true in him on the very night he looked at Peter.

10. **CONFIDENCE:** 8/10 for the PROPHECY link. 5/10 for the ECHO link.

## ANGLE 2

1. **TITLE:** The shepherd's warning that made Peter boast

2. **HOOK QUESTION:** What old prophecy did Jesus give just before Peter said "not I"?

3. **ANCHORS**
   - **ANCHOR-1:** Luke 22:54 "And Peter followed afar off."
   - **ANCHOR-2:** Zechariah 13:7 "smite the shepherd, and the sheep shall be scattered"

   3b. **WHY-VERSE:** Mark 14:27 "All ye shall be offended because of me this night: for it is written, I will smite the shepherd, and the sheep shall be scattered."
   - Mark 14 is the parallel of the warning, not of the denial. It is used only as the why-verse. Zechariah is the second book.

4. **LABEL:** **PROPHECY**. Jesus says "it is written" and "this night."

5. **WHAT IT DOES *NOT* PROVE**
   - Zechariah does not name Peter.
   - Luke does not quote Zechariah.
   - Do not say "following afar off" is the scattering. Mark says only that "all" would be offended and scattered that night.

6. **FACTS**
   - Jesus applies Zechariah 13:7 to "all ye" and to "this night" (Mark 14:27).
   - Peter answers: "Although all shall be offended, yet will not I" (Mark 14:29).
   - In the same answer Jesus says "after that I am risen, I will go before you into Galilee" (Mark 14:28).
   - The shepherd is the one struck, not the sheep (Zechariah 13:7).

7. **SO WHAT:** Jesus called himself the shepherd who would be struck, and he knew the sheep would run. He still "turned, and looked upon" the one who followed "afar off."
   - Backing verse for all believers: 1 Peter 2:25 "ye were as sheep going astray; but are now returned unto the Shepherd and Bishop of your souls."
   - Touches: fear, being unseen.

8. **WHY MOST HAVEN'T HEARD IT:** Zechariah 13:7 is preached about the disciples fleeing at the arrest, but rarely tied to Peter's boast and the look. **Freshness: 5/10**

9. **HOW IT LANDS ON JESUS:** Jesus is the struck shepherd whom Zechariah wrote about.

10. **CONFIDENCE:** 7/10. The weak point is that the why-verse comes from Mark's account of the warning.

## ANGLE 3

1. **TITLE:** Peter "remembered the word of the Lord" twice

2. **HOOK QUESTION:** The first memory made Peter weep. What did the second one do?

3. **ANCHORS**
   - **ANCHOR-1:** Luke 22:61 "And Peter remembered the word of the Lord, how he had said unto him"
   - **ANCHOR-2:** Acts 11:16 "Then remembered I the word of the Lord, how that he said, John indeed baptized with water; but ye shall be baptized with the Holy Ghost."

   3b. **WHY-VERSE:** None. No verse says the two moments belong together, so this is ECHO. Acts is a different book, though the same writer.

4. **LABEL:** **ECHO**. The wording is almost the same, but the link is not stated.

5. **WHAT IT DOES *NOT* PROVE**
   - Luke never says Acts 11 answers Luke 22.
   - Do not claim it was "planned," and do not say what Peter felt in Acts 11.

6. **FACTS**
   - In Luke 22:61 Peter remembers Jesus' warning, then weeps (Luke 22:62).
   - In Acts 11:16 Peter himself tells the story. He remembers Jesus' promise while Cornelius's household receives the Holy Ghost (Acts 11:15).
   - His next words are "what was I, that I could withstand God?" (Acts 11:17).
   - Men "of the circumcision contended with him" (Acts 11:2).

7. **SO WHAT:** The same Lord whose word made Peter go out and weep later gave Peter a word that opened the door to new people.
   - Backing verse for all believers: John 14:26 "he shall teach you all things, and bring all things to your remembrance, whatsoever I have said unto you."
   - Touches: failure, purpose.

8. **WHY MOST HAVEN'T HEARD IT:** Nobody sets the two "remembered the word of the Lord" lines side by side. **Freshness: 8/10**

9. **HOW IT LANDS ON JESUS:** Both times, what Peter remembers is Jesus' own word.

10. **CONFIDENCE:** 9/10 that the quotes are exact. 4/10 as a headline link (ECHO only).

## ANGLE 4

1. **TITLE:** Peter later preaches the word "denied"

2. **HOOK QUESTION:** Why does Peter accuse a crowd of "denying" Jesus?

3. **ANCHORS**
   - **ANCHOR-1:** Luke 22:57 "And he denied him, saying, Woman, I know him not."
   - **ANCHOR-2:** Acts 3:14 "But ye denied the Holy One and the Just" and Acts 3:19 "Repent ye therefore, and be converted"

   3b. **WHY-VERSE:** None. Peter never mentions his own denial in Acts 3.

4. **LABEL:** **ECHO**. In the KJV, "denied" and "converted" match Luke 22:57 and Luke 22:32.

5. **WHAT IT DOES *NOT* PROVE**
   - Peter does not say "I did this too."
   - Do not claim he was thinking of his own fall.

6. **FACTS**
   - Peter is the speaker (Acts 3:12).
   - He says "denied" twice (Acts 3:13, 3:14).
   - He offers "be converted, that your sins may be blotted out" (Acts 3:19).
   - Jesus had said to Peter "when thou art converted" (Luke 22:32).

7. **SO WHAT:** The man who said "I know him not" was later the one telling people who had "denied" Jesus that their sins could be "blotted out." Denying Jesus is not the end of the road.
   - Backing verse for all believers: Acts 3:19 (as quoted above).
   - Touches: shame.

8. **WHY MOST HAVEN'T HEARD IT:** Acts 3 is preached on the healed lame man, not on Peter's word choice. **Freshness: 8/10**

9. **HOW IT LANDS ON JESUS:** Peter calls the man he denied "the Holy One and the Just."

10. **CONFIDENCE:** 8/10 that it is in the text. 4/10 as a headline (ECHO).

## ANGLE 5

1. **TITLE:** Devil, faith, brethren, strengthen: in Peter's own letter

2. **HOOK QUESTION:** Did Peter ever do what Jesus told him that night?

3. **ANCHORS**
   - **ANCHOR-1:** Luke 22:62 "And Peter went out, and wept bitterly."
   - **ANCHOR-2:** 1 Peter 5:8-10
     - 5:8 "your adversary the devil, as a roaring lion, walketh about, seeking whom he may devour"
     - 5:9 "Whom resist stedfast in the faith ... your brethren"
     - 5:10 "make you perfect, stablish, strengthen, settle you"

   3b. **WHY-VERSE:** None. Peter does not cite Luke 22:31-32.

4. **LABEL:** **ECHO**. Four words from Luke 22:31-32 (Satan, faith, brethren, strengthen) appear together in three verses of 1 Peter 5.

5. **WHAT IT DOES *NOT* PROVE**
   - It is not stated that the letter fulfils "strengthen thy brethren."
   - Do not say Peter wrote it "because of" that night.
   - 1 Peter 5:10 says GOD does the strengthening, not Peter.

6. **FACTS**
   - Luke 22:31-32 names Satan, faith, "brethren," and "strengthen."
   - 1 Peter 5:8-10 has "the devil," "the faith," "brethren," and "strengthen."
   - The writer names himself as Peter (1 Peter 1:1) and "a witness of the sufferings of Christ" (1 Peter 5:1).

7. **SO WHAT:** The man who "wept bitterly" later wrote to frightened believers that God would "strengthen" them. Failing did not cancel what he was called to do.
   - Backing verse for all believers: 1 Peter 5:10 (as quoted above).
   - Touches: failure, purpose.

8. **WHY MOST HAVEN'T HEARD IT:** 1 Peter 5:8 is preached on its own, without Luke 22:31-32 beside it. **Freshness: 7/10**

9. **HOW IT LANDS ON JESUS:** The words Jesus spoke to Peter turn up in Peter's own teaching.

10. **CONFIDENCE:** 8/10 that the quotes are exact. 4/10 as a headline (ECHO).

## Rejected

- **Amos 9:9 ("sift... like as corn"):** In Amos it is God who sifts, in judgment. That is a context risk.
- **Judges 6:14 ("the LORD looked upon him"):** A bare phrase echo that would push a meaning onto the look.
- **Job 1-2 (Satan asks):** ECHO only, and close to a kill-list item.
