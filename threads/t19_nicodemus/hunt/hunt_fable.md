# Fresh angle hunt - Nicodemus at the burial (John 19:38-42, read with John 3:1-21 and John 7:45-52)

Blind seat answer. All quotes are KJV. Where I quote a verse that was not printed in the brief, I quote it from memory: please check each one word by word (list at the end).

---

## ANGLE 1

1. TITLE: A Hundred Pounds for an Undecayed Body

2. HOOK QUESTION: Why bring a hundred pounds of spices for a body that never decayed?

3. ANCHOR-1: John 19:39-40 "brought a mixture of myrrh and aloes, about an hundred pound weight. Then took they the body of Jesus, and wound it in linen clothes with the spices, as the manner of the Jews is to bury."
   ANCHOR-2: Psalm 16:10 "For thou wilt not leave my soul in hell; neither wilt thou suffer thine Holy One to see corruption."

3b. WHY-VERSE: Acts 2:31 "He seeing this before spake of the resurrection of Christ, that his soul was not left in hell, neither his flesh did see corruption."
    (Support, same link from Paul: Acts 13:37 "But he, whom God raised again, saw no corruption.")

4. LABEL: PROPHECY (Psalm 16:10 -> Acts 2:31; Peter says David "seeing this before spake of the resurrection of Christ"). The pairing of the psalm with the hundred pounds of spices is my own placing side by side: DETAIL.

5. WHAT IT DOES *NOT* PROVE: It does not prove what the spices were for, or what Nicodemus expected or believed. John only says they buried him "as the manner of the Jews is to bury". It does not prove the spices were wasted or wrong. We may only say: he was buried as the dead are buried, and God had already said his flesh would not see corruption.

6. FACTS
   - Nicodemus brought the myrrh and aloes; the amount was "about an hundred pound weight" (John 19:39).
   - "They" (Joseph and Nicodemus) wound the body in linen with the spices (John 19:38-40).
   - The burial followed "the manner of the Jews" (John 19:40).
   - Psalm 16 is "of David" and speaks in the first person (Psalm 16 title; 16:10).
   - Peter says David himself "is both dead and buried, and his sepulchre is with us unto this day" (Acts 2:29), so the psalm is not finally about David.
   - Peter says David spoke of Christ's resurrection (Acts 2:30-31).
   - Paul makes the same point: David "saw corruption" but the one God raised "saw no corruption" (Acts 13:36-37).
   - Nothing in John 19 says Nicodemus knew or thought of Psalm 16.

7. SO WHAT: Jesus was wound in linen with spices and laid in a sepulchre like any dead man, yet his body did not see corruption - so for those who belong to him, the grave is a loss but not the end.
   Verse for all believers: 1 Corinthians 15:42 "So also is the resurrection of the dead. It is sown in corruption; it is raised in incorruption." (also 1 Corinthians 15:20 "But now is Christ risen from the dead, and become the firstfruits of them that slept.")

8. WHY NOT HEARD: Psalm 16 is preached at Easter and at Pentecost; the spices are preached as "costly love". The two are rarely put in the same frame. Fresh: 6/10.

9. LANDS ON JESUS: He is the "Holy One" whose flesh God would not let see corruption, even while men were burying him as dead.

10. CONFIDENCE: 8/10.

## ANGLE 2

1. TITLE: The Law Teacher Buried the Cursed Man

2. HOOK QUESTION: Why did the law say a hanged man must be buried that day?

3. ANCHOR-1: John 19:38 "He came therefore, and took the body of Jesus." (and John 19:42 "There laid they Jesus therefore because of the Jews' preparation day")
   ANCHOR-2: Deuteronomy 21:23 "His body shall not remain all night upon the tree, but thou shalt in any wise bury him that day; (for he that is hanged is accursed of God;)"

3b. WHY-VERSE: Galatians 3:13 "Christ hath redeemed us from the curse of the law, being made a curse for us: for it is written, Cursed is every one that hangeth on a tree:"

4. LABEL: NT-STATED (Paul quotes Deuteronomy 21:23 and applies it to Christ; the Deuteronomy text is a law, not a forecast). The "bury him that day" half of the same verse matching the same-day burial in John 19 is ECHO - no verse says John 19 fulfils it.

5. WHAT IT DOES *NOT* PROVE: John does not quote Deuteronomy 21. The reason John gives for the haste is the sabbath and the preparation day (John 19:31, 19:42), not this law. We must not say Nicodemus buried him "to keep Deuteronomy 21" or that he understood the curse. And Deuteronomy 21:22 speaks of a man who "committed a sin worthy of death" - Jesus is not that; Galatians says he was made a curse "for us".

6. FACTS
   - Nicodemus was "a man of the Pharisees... a ruler of the Jews" and Jesus called him "a master of Israel" (John 3:1, 3:10).
   - In John 7 the Pharisees say of the crowd: "this people who knoweth not the law are cursed" (John 7:49).
   - In the very next verses Nicodemus speaks about "our law" (John 7:50-51).
   - Jesus was buried the same day he died, before the sabbath (John 19:31, 19:42).
   - The body was taken down and laid in a sepulchre (John 19:38-42; Acts 13:29 "they took him down from the tree, and laid him in a sepulchre").
   - The law joins two things in one verse: the hanged man is "accursed of God" and must be buried "that day" (Deuteronomy 21:23).
   - Paul says Christ was "made a curse for us" and quotes that law (Galatians 3:13).
   - Pilate said "I find no fault in him" (John 19:6) - so the sin was not his own.

7. SO WHAT: Jesus hung on the tree where the law says "cursed", and men took his body down - he took the curse that was ours, so your shame does not have to stay on you.
   Verse for all believers: Galatians 3:13 "Christ hath redeemed us from the curse of the law, being made a curse for us".

8. WHY NOT HEARD: Galatians 3:13 is preached about the cross, almost never about the burial; and the word "cursed" in John 7:49, spoken right before Nicodemus talks, is read past. Fresh: 6/10.

9. LANDS ON JESUS: The one the rulers' own law would mark as "accursed" was carrying the curse for the people those rulers had called cursed.

10. CONFIDENCE: 8/10 for Deuteronomy -> Galatians; 5/10 if the film leans on the "that day" echo as if John states it.

## ANGLE 3

1. TITLE: They Touched the Dead

2. HOOK QUESTION: What did the law say happened to a man who touched a dead body?

3. ANCHOR-1: John 19:40 "Then took they the body of Jesus, and wound it in linen clothes"
   ANCHOR-2: Numbers 19:11 "He that toucheth the dead body of any man shall be unclean seven days."

3b. WHY-VERSE: Hebrews 9:13-14 "For if the blood of bulls and of goats, and the ashes of an heifer sprinkling the unclean, sanctifieth to the purifying of the flesh: How much more shall the blood of Christ, who through the eternal Spirit offered himself without spot to God, purge your conscience from dead works to serve the living God?"

4. LABEL: NT-STATED (Hebrews 9:13-14 names the Numbers 19 rite - "the ashes of an heifer sprinkling the unclean" - and says Christ's blood does "much more"; Numbers 19 is law, not forecast). That Joseph and Nicodemus came under Numbers 19:11 is a plain reading of the law applied to John 19:40, but John does not say it: DETAIL / inference.

5. WHAT IT DOES *NOT* PROVE: John never says the two men became unclean, missed the feast, or counted any cost. He never gives their reason. We must not say they "gave up the Passover" or "chose defilement for love" - no verse says that. We may only say: the law says what it says about touching the dead, and John says they took and wound the body.

6. FACTS
   - "They" who took and wound the body are Joseph and Nicodemus (John 19:38-40).
   - Earlier that day the leaders "went not into the judgment hall, lest they should be defiled; but that they might eat the passover" (John 18:28) - same book, so story colour only.
   - The law: touching "the dead body of any man" = unclean seven days (Numbers 19:11).
   - The cure in that chapter is the ashes of a heifer with water, called "a purification for sin" (Numbers 19:9).
   - Hebrews names that same rite and says the blood of Christ does "much more" (Hebrews 9:13-14).
   - Hebrews says Christ "offered himself without spot" (Hebrews 9:14).
   - Nicodemus was a Pharisee and a ruler (John 3:1), so the law of Numbers 19 was his own law ("our law", John 7:51).

7. SO WHAT: Two men took the body of Jesus in their hands, the one thing the law said makes a man unclean - and that body is the one whose blood makes people clean, so shame does not have to keep you away from him.
   Verse for all believers: 1 John 1:7 "the blood of Jesus Christ his Son cleanseth us from all sin." (and Hebrews 9:14 "purge your conscience")

8. WHY NOT HEARD: Sermons stay on the men's courage; the law about dead bodies sits in Numbers, a book few preach, and John 18:28 is not read next to John 19:40. Fresh: 7/10.

9. LANDS ON JESUS: The body the law would count as defiling is the offering "without spot" that cleans the conscience.

10. CONFIDENCE: 7/10 (the Hebrews -> Numbers link is solid; "they became unclean" is an inference and must be said as "the law says", not "John says").

## ANGLE 4

1. TITLE: How Can a Man Be Born Again?

2. HOOK QUESTION: Did Nicodemus ever get an answer to his night question?

3. ANCHOR-1: John 19:39 "Nicodemus, which at the first came to Jesus by night" (with John 19:41-42 "a new sepulchre, wherein was never man yet laid. There laid they Jesus")
   ANCHOR-2: 1 Peter 1:3 "Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, which according to his abundant mercy hath begotten us again unto a lively hope by the resurrection of Jesus Christ from the dead,"

3b. WHY-VERSE: 1 Peter 1:3 (as above): the new birth comes "by the resurrection of Jesus Christ from the dead".
    (Support: Colossians 1:18 "who is the beginning, the firstborn from the dead")

4. LABEL: None of the four fits cleanly, and I say so. Peter states plainly how people are "begotten again", but Peter does not mention Nicodemus or John 3. John himself (19:39) points the reader back to the night talk of John 3. Joining John's pointer to Peter's sentence is my link: ECHO by the brief's rules. The surprise inside it (John reminds us of the night visit at the tomb) is DETAIL.

5. WHAT IT DOES *NOT* PROVE: It does not prove Nicodemus was born again, believed, or understood. It does not prove John meant the tomb as a picture of a womb - no verse says that, so do not say it. Only: Nicodemus asked "How can a man be born when he is old?" (John 3:4); Peter says God has "begotten us again... by the resurrection of Jesus Christ from the dead"; and Nicodemus is named at the grave that resurrection came from.

6. FACTS
   - Nicodemus asked two "how" questions: John 3:4 and John 3:9 ("How can these things be?").
   - John does not record any reply from Nicodemus after verse 9 (John 3:9-21).
   - Jesus told him "Ye must be born again" (John 3:7).
   - John names the night visit all three times Nicodemus appears (John 3:2; 7:50; 19:39).
   - Nicodemus is not named again in John after 19:39.
   - Peter ties being "begotten again" to the resurrection (1 Peter 1:3) and uses the words "born again" in 1 Peter 1:23.
   - Paul calls the risen Jesus "the firstborn from the dead" (Colossians 1:18).

7. SO WHAT: Jesus was laid in a sepulchre and rose from it, and by his rising God gives people a new birth - so it is not too late for you to begin again, even "when he is old".
   Verse for all believers: 1 Peter 1:3 "hath begotten us again unto a lively hope".

8. WHY NOT HEARD: "Born again" is taught from John 3 alone; few are shown that Peter says how it happens - by the resurrection - or that the man who asked "how" stood at that grave. Fresh: 6/10.

9. LANDS ON JESUS: His rising from the dead is the way the new birth he spoke of at night is given.

10. CONFIDENCE: 6/10 (each verse is sound; the join between them is mine, not a Bible writer's).

## ANGLE 5

1. TITLE: The King's Clothes Smell of Myrrh, Aloes

2. HOOK QUESTION: Where else does the Bible put myrrh and aloes on one man?

3. ANCHOR-1: John 19:39 "brought a mixture of myrrh and aloes, about an hundred pound weight"
   ANCHOR-2: Psalm 45:8 "All thy garments smell of myrrh, and aloes, and cassia, out of the ivory palaces, whereby they have made thee glad."

3b. WHY-VERSE: Hebrews 1:8 "But unto the Son he saith, Thy throne, O God, is for ever and ever: a sceptre of righteousness is the sceptre of thy kingdom." (quoting Psalm 45:6; Hebrews 1:9 goes on to quote Psalm 45:7)
    NO verse says Psalm 45:8 points to the burial.

4. LABEL: Two links, two labels. Psalm 45:6-7 -> the Son: NT-STATED (Hebrews 1:8-9). Psalm 45:8 myrrh and aloes -> John 19:39 myrrh and aloes: ECHO (same two words; the Bible does not join them). The ECHO cannot be the headline. The honest headline is only: "The song God speaks to the Son dresses that king in myrrh and aloes - and those are the two spices on Jesus' body."

5. WHAT IT DOES *NOT* PROVE: It does not prove Psalm 45:8 foretells the burial. Psalm 45 is a glad wedding song for a king (Psalm 45:1 "touching the king"; the title calls it "A Song of loves"), not a death scene, and it adds "cassia", which John does not. It does not prove Nicodemus chose these spices because of the psalm.

6. FACTS
   - Hebrews quotes Psalm 45:6-7 and says it is said "unto the Son" (Hebrews 1:8-9).
   - The very next verse of the psalm (45:8) names myrrh and aloes on the king's garments.
   - The king of the psalm has a throne "for ever and ever" (Psalm 45:6).
   - John names exactly two spices: myrrh and aloes (John 19:39).
   - Myrrh and aloes together also appear in Proverbs 7:17 and Song of Solomon 4:14, neither about a king's death. (I believe these four are the only KJV places with the pair - please check with a concordance.)

7. SO WHAT: The body they wound with myrrh and aloes belongs to the one whose throne is "for ever and ever" - Jesus went into death and was not ended by it, so loss is not the last word for those who come to God by him.
   Verse for all believers: Hebrews 7:25 "Wherefore he is able also to save them to the uttermost that come unto God by him, seeing he ever liveth to make intercession for them."

8. WHY NOT HEARD: Myrrh is always tied to the wise men's gift (kill list); Psalm 45 is rarely preached, and its verse 8 almost never. Fresh: 7/10.

9. LANDS ON JESUS: The one buried in myrrh and aloes is the Son to whom God says, "Thy throne, O God, is for ever and ever."

10. CONFIDENCE: 5/10 as an angle (the Hebrews link is 9/10 sound; the spice link is a true word-match but only an ECHO, so by the rules it likely fails the gate).
