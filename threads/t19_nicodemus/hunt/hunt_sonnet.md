# Blind hunt: Nicodemus at the burial (John 19:38-42), seat = Sonnet

Honest note up front: five angles, but only A, B and C have a real Bible writer joining the two passages. D is a weaker link. E is an ECHO and will not pass alone.

---

## ANGLE 1 - "The Law Said Bury Him Today"

1. TITLE: The Law Said Bury Him Today
2. HOOK: Why did the burial have to happen in a rush that very evening?
3. ANCHOR-1: John 19:38 "besought Pilate that he might take away the body of Jesus"
   ANCHOR-2: Deuteronomy 21:23 "His body shall not remain all night upon the tree, but thou shalt in any wise bury him that day; (for he that is hanged is accursed of God;)"
3b. WHY-VERSE: Galatians 3:13 "Christ hath redeemed us from the curse of the law, being made a curse for us: for it is written, Cursed is every one that hangeth on a tree:"
4. LABEL: NT-STATED (Galatians 3:13 itself quotes Deuteronomy 21:23 and applies it to Christ)
5. DOES NOT PROVE: That Joseph or Nicodemus acted because of Deuteronomy 21. John gives a different stated reason (the preparation day, 19:42). It does not prove they understood Jesus as "cursed."
6. FACTS:
   - Jesus was put to death on a cross; the passage calls the burial place a "sepulchre" (John 19:41).
   - The Jews wanted the bodies taken down because of the preparation and the sabbath (John 19:31).
   - Joseph asked Pilate for the body (John 19:38).
   - "They" took the body and wound it in linen with spices (John 19:40). The text does not name who "they" are beyond Joseph (19:38) and Nicodemus (19:39).
   - Galatians 3:13 says Christ was "made a curse for us."
7. SO WHAT: They laid Jesus in a grave on the very day the Law said a hanged man must be buried, and Galatians 3:13 says he took that curse so it would not stay on us; shame is not the last word on you. (Verse for all believers: Galatians 3:13, "redeemed us.")
8. WHY NOT HEARD: Most tellings of the burial stop at "Jesus was buried," and skip the Law's rule about a hanged man. Fresh guess: 6.
9. LANDS ON JESUS: The man the Law's words call "accursed" is the one who took that curse for us.
10. CONFIDENCE: 8

---

## ANGLE 2 - "A Hundred Pounds of Spice, and Peter Never Mentions It"

1. TITLE: Spices Wrapped Him; God's Word Kept Him
2. HOOK: What does the Bible say kept Jesus' body from decay?
3. ANCHOR-1: John 19:40 "Then took they the body of Jesus, and wound it in linen clothes with the spices"
   ANCHOR-2: Psalm 16:10 "For thou wilt not leave my soul in hell; neither wilt thou suffer thine Holy One to see corruption."
3b. WHY-VERSE: Acts 2:31 "He seeing this before spake of the resurrection of Christ, that his soul was not left in hell, neither his flesh did see corruption."
4. LABEL: PROPHECY (Acts 2:31 says David spoke ahead of the resurrection of Christ)
5. DOES NOT PROVE: That the spices were useless or that Nicodemus's gift was wasted. Neither Acts nor John says what the spices did or did not do.
6. FACTS:
   - Nicodemus brought "a mixture of myrrh and aloes" (John 19:39).
   - Jesus' body was wound in linen with the spices (John 19:40).
   - Peter says David "is both dead and buried, and his sepulchre is with us unto this day" (Acts 2:29), then says David spoke of Christ (Acts 2:30-31).
   - Acts 2:31 says "neither his flesh did see corruption," and Peter ties it to David's words (Acts 2:30-31).
7. SO WHAT: Two men wrapped Jesus in linen with spices, yet Peter names God's promise, not a spice, as the reason his flesh saw no corruption; and Romans 8:11 says the One who raised Jesus "shall also quicken your mortal bodies." (Verse for all believers: Romans 8:11.) Touches loss.
8. WHY NOT HEARD: The spices are usually told as a love story; Psalm 16 is told as a resurrection proof text; the two are rarely set side by side. Fresh guess: 6.
9. LANDS ON JESUS: The Holy One in the borrowed grave is the one God did not leave.
10. CONFIDENCE: 9 (safest angle on truth). Overlaps the kill-list item "Jesus truly died and was buried," so the film must lead with the spice-versus-promise contrast, not the resurrection proof.

---

## ANGLE 3 - "They Touched the Dead, Before Passover" (MY TOP PICK)

1. TITLE: Two Men Touched the Dead at Passover
2. HOOK: What did the Law say about handling a dead body at Passover?
3. ANCHOR-1: John 19:40 "Then took they the body of Jesus, and wound it in linen clothes"
   ANCHOR-2: Numbers 19:11 "He that toucheth the dead body of any man shall be unclean seven days."
   (Support: Numbers 9:6 "certain men, who were defiled by the dead body of a man, that they could not keep the passover on that day")
3b. WHY-VERSE: Hebrews 9:13-14 "For if the blood of bulls and of goats, and the ashes of an heifer sprinkling the unclean, sanctifieth to the purifying of the flesh: How much more shall the blood of Christ ... purge your conscience from dead works to serve the living God?"
4. LABEL: NT-STATED (Hebrews 9 joins the Numbers 19 cleansing for the unclean to the blood of Christ)
5. DOES NOT PROVE: That Nicodemus or Joseph missed the Passover meal, were forbidden to join it, or knew what they were risking. It does not prove their motives. John's timing and the other Gospels' timing of the meal are not settled by this angle.
6. FACTS:
   - They took the body and wound it (John 19:40), so they handled it.
   - It was "the preparation of the passover" (John 19:14) and "the Jews' preparation day" (John 19:42).
   - Numbers 19:11 says touching a dead body means seven days unclean.
   - Numbers 9:6 tells of men defiled by a dead body who could not keep the passover "on that day."
   - In John 18:28 the accusers stayed out of Pilate's hall "lest they should be defiled; but that they might eat the passover." That was about the accusers and a Gentile house, not Nicodemus.
7. SO WHAT: Two men took hold of a dead body the Law calls unclean, and the dead man in their hands is the one whose blood Hebrews 9:14 says can "purge your conscience"; so what the Law made unclean, he cleanses. (Verse for all believers: Hebrews 9:14, "your conscience.") Touches shame.
8. WHY NOT HEARD: Most talks on this scene are about courage and spices; the rule about touching the dead is not usually raised. Fresh guess: 7.
9. LANDS ON JESUS: The body they handled belongs to the one whose blood cleans a conscience.
10. CONFIDENCE: 7. Main risk is overclaiming on Passover timing; keep it to what Numbers 9 and 19 say and what John 19:14 states.

## ANGLE 4 - "Search, and Look: Galilee"

1. TITLE: The Man Told to Search Galilee
2. HOOK: What did the prophet Isaiah say about Galilee?
3. ANCHOR-1: John 19:39 "Nicodemus, which at the first came to Jesus by night"
   (read with John 7:50-52: "Art thou also of Galilee? Search, and look: for out of Galilee ariseth no prophet.")
   ANCHOR-2: Isaiah 9:1-2 "in Galilee of the nations. The people that walked in darkness have seen a great light: they that dwell in the land of the shadow of death, upon them hath the light shined."
3b. WHY-VERSE: Matthew 4:14-16 "That it might be fulfilled which was spoken by Esaias the prophet ... The people which sat in darkness saw great light"
4. LABEL: PROPHECY (Matthew 4:14 says Isaiah's words were fulfilled)
5. DOES NOT PROVE: That Nicodemus knew Isaiah 9, thought of it, or acted at the burial because of it. John 7 records no answer from him. It does not prove the rulers' "no prophet" claim is false; it only shows what Isaiah said about Galilee.
6. FACTS:
   - Nicodemus is called "he that came to Jesus by night, being one of them" (John 7:50).
   - His colleagues said "Art thou also of Galilee? Search, and look" (John 7:52).
   - Isaiah 9:1-2 speaks of light in Galilee of the nations.
   - Matthew 4:13-16 says Jesus dwelt there and quotes Isaiah.
   - Jesus told Nicodemus "light is come into the world" (John 3:19).
7. SO WHAT: Others waved Galilee away, yet Isaiah said light would shine there, and Jesus went there first; so being looked down on does not put you outside his reach ("whosoever," John 3:16). Touches being unseen.
8. WHY NOT HEARD: The Galilee jab in John 7 is usually read as a put-down, not set beside Isaiah 9. Fresh guess: 5.
9. LANDS ON JESUS: The light Isaiah promised for Galilee is the Jesus Nicodemus came to by night.
10. CONFIDENCE: 7. Weakest tie to the burial scene itself; the scene is only the anchor on Nicodemus.

## ANGLE 5 - "Myrrh and Aloes in the King's Robes"

1. TITLE: Where Else Do Myrrh and Aloes Appear?
2. HOOK: Where else in the Bible do myrrh and aloes go together?
3. ANCHOR-1: John 19:39 "a mixture of myrrh and aloes, about an hundred pound weight"
   ANCHOR-2: Psalm 45:8 "All thy garments smell of myrrh, and aloes, and cassia, out of the ivory palaces"
3b. WHY-VERSE: Hebrews 1:8-9 "But unto the Son he saith, Thy throne, O God, is for ever and ever" (quotes Psalm 45:6-7). No verse joins the myrrh-and-aloes of Psalm 45:8 to John 19:39.
4. LABEL: ECHO (the Psalm is tied to the Son by Hebrews, but the myrrh-and-aloes match is only a shared pair of words)
5. DOES NOT PROVE: That Nicodemus meant a royal robe, or that John is pointing back to Psalm 45. Hebrews quotes verses 6-7, not verse 8. Psalm 45 has cassia, John 19 does not.
6. FACTS:
   - John 19:39 names myrrh and aloes.
   - Psalm 45:8 names myrrh, aloes and cassia, on a king's garments.
   - Hebrews 1:8-9 quotes Psalm 45:6-7 and says it is said "unto the Son."
7. SO WHAT: The Son the Psalm calls King is the one laid in a borrowed grave, so even his lowest hour was the King's. (Verse for believers: none that speaks to this directly; keep this as a story beat only.)
8. WHY NOT HEARD: Rarely noticed pair. Close to the kill-list "burial fit for a king." Fresh guess: 3.
9. LANDS ON JESUS: The King of Psalm 45, named as the Son in Hebrews 1, is the one wrapped with myrrh and aloes.
10. CONFIDENCE: 5 as a pure echo. Would not pass the gate alone.

---

MY TOP PICK = Angle 3, "Two Men Touched the Dead at Passover" - it is the freshest, has a Bible writer (Hebrews 9:13-14) joining the two passages, and lands on cleansing by Jesus' blood. Safest runner-up is Angle 2 (confidence 9) if the Passover timing in Angle 3 proves too hard to keep clean.
