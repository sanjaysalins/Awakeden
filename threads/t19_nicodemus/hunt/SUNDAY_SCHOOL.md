## KILL LIST - the common sermons on John 19:38-42 (Nicodemus brings myrrh and aloes to bury Jesus; read with John 3:1-21 and John 7:45-52). DO NOT give any of these as your angle, and do not give a re-worded copy of one.

- Nicodemus first came to Jesus "by night" out of fear, but at the cross he finally comes out into the open as a disciple (John 3:2; John 19:39)
- Secret faith is not enough; real faith must one day stand up and be seen, as Joseph and Nicodemus did (John 19:38; John 12:42-43)
- Nicodemus shows that faith often grows slowly: from a curious question, to a quiet defence, to open devotion (John 3:4; John 7:50-51; John 19:39)
- When the bold disciples ran away, two fearful, secret followers were the ones who showed courage (John 19:38-39; Mark 14:50)
- The huge amount of spices, about a hundred pounds, shows great love and costly devotion, a burial fit for a king (John 19:39)
- Jesus really died and was truly buried, which sets up the truth of the resurrection (John 19:40-42; 1 Corinthians 15:3-4)
- Jesus was laid in a new tomb in a garden where no one had ever been laid, so there could be no mix-up about whose body rose (John 19:41)
- God uses people of rank and wealth too; even members of the ruling council can come to Christ (John 19:38-39; John 7:48)
- Cross-ref: Joseph of Arimathea was a rich man, so Jesus was buried "with the rich" as foretold (Isaiah 53:9; Matthew 27:57-60)
- Cross-ref: "As Moses lifted up the serpent," Nicodemus heard it at night and then saw it fulfilled at the cross (John 3:14-15; Numbers 21:8-9)
- Cross-ref: Joseph was a good and just counsellor who had not agreed to the council's plan (Luke 23:50-51; Mark 15:43)
- Cross-ref: Many rulers believed but would not confess him, for fear of being put out of the synagogue (John 12:42-43)
- Cross-ref: Myrrh as a gift at his birth and spices at his burial frame Jesus' life (Matthew 2:11; Mark 16:1)
- Joseph of Arimathaea, once a secret disciple, came forward openly to care for Jesus’ body (John 19:38).
- Nicodemus, who had first come to Jesus by night, now publicly helped bury him (John 19:39; cf. John 3:1–2).
- A costly gift can express love and devotion to Christ (John 19:39).
- Jesus truly died and was buried; his body was wrapped and laid in a tomb (John 19:40–42).
- The burial preparations fulfilled the Jewish custom of honoring the dead (John 19:40).
- God can bring faith and courage to people who once hesitated or feared others (John 19:38–39; cf. John 7:50–52).
- Jesus was buried in a new tomb in a garden near the place of crucifixion (John 19:41–42).
- Cross-ref: Isaiah foretold that the suffering servant would be with the rich in his death (Isaiah 53:9).
- Cross-ref: Joseph of Arimathaea asked Pilate for Jesus’ body and laid it in his own tomb (Matthew 27:57–60).
- Cross-ref: Nicodemus had earlier come to Jesus by night and heard of the need to be born again (John 3:1–21).
- Cross-ref: Nicodemus had defended hearing Jesus before judging him (John 7:50–52).
- Cross-ref: The women saw where Jesus was laid and prepared spices for his burial (Luke 23:50–56).
