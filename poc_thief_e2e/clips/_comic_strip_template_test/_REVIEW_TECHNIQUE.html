Panel-animation technique test — same source page, two prompt strategies

Frames pulled at start / mid / end of each 5s clip to see whether panels genuinely change over time, not just the camera moving.

Variant 1 — static camera, amped environmental motion, all-panels mandate

Dust genuinely grows top+bottom panels; mist crosses Goliath's face by frame 3. David close-up stays inert. David's arm pose drifts in the bottom panel (rule break).

Variant 2 — panel motion described first, camera rack demoted to afterthought

Invents a lightning bolt not in the source; David's sling goes into active motion; by the last frame the Goliath close-up panel is replaced entirely with an invented new shot. Hallucination, not animation.

Variant 3 — same as v1, but every reference to hair/cloth/held objects removed; motion strictly sky/clouds/distant ground; freeze rule hardened to explicitly name hair/cloth/objects

Mildest leak of the three, but still real: a scratch mark appears on Goliath's helmet that was never in the source, David's expression shifts from calm to furrowed, and two birds appear in the bottom panel's sky. No panel replacement, no limb drift -- but the "invent nothing" rule still breaks even with zero figure-adjacent language.

Variant 4 — same image passed as BOTH --start-image and --end-image (forced loop)

Zero drift, zero invention -- but also zero visible motion. All 5 sampled frames across the full clip (start/quarter/mid/three-quarter/end) are visually identical to the source. Forcing a return-to-start apparently made "don't move much" the safest path for Kling, so this trades away the aliveness to buy the cleanliness.
