## Independent adversarial review — *The Suffering Servant* (Isaiah, eyewitness-short)

---

### EW-INV-4 — KJV verbatim + attribution

**Quotes are word-exact.** All three bold spans match KJV substrings:
- Isa 53:5 and 53:6 align with `passage.txt`.
- `"preached unto him Jesus."` is exact Acts 8:35.

**Attribution is thin or wrong on every quote.**

1. **First quote** — *"The blows fell — and every wound was ours, not his own:"* → **`"But he was wounded for our transgressions..."`**  
   No speaker/source named before the span. Isaiah’s own scroll lines need an explicit frame (*I wrote / the vision said / thus saith the LORD*) — especially in a multi-voice cast where KJV routes to `scripture`.

2. **Second quote** — *"The LORD pressed it plainer"* is the only real attribution line. Acceptable but still light.

3. **Third quote** — *"Philip read my words and"* → **`"preached unto him Jesus."`**  
   This is **Luke’s narrative summary**, not Philip’s spoken words. The long-form review on this same piece already flagged this. Also: **who is “him”?** Acts means the Ethiopian eunuch — invisible to a first-time listener. Attribution should name Acts/Luke (or Philip’s *action* as recorded), not read like direct speech.

---

### EW-INV-2 — CTA-on-Jesus

**Mostly passes.** Jesus is named; responses are named (`Come`, `believe`, `Trust`); substitution is grace-anchored (*"You were never made to carry your wandering home — he has already borne it"*).

**Soft flags:**
- *"Trust the One wounded for your transgressions, and be healed by his stripes"* is not the banned bare *“will you trust Him?”* — but after three beats of identical substitution vocabulary it lands **template-adjacent**.
- *"be healed by his stripes"* is KJV-faithful, not fear/gain-loss — but it is the one line that edges toward **benefit** framing rather than invitation-to-person.

Not a blocking fail on CTA alone.

---

### EW-G7 — Reveal earned

**Partially earned, largely asserted.**

**Strong hinge:** Acts 8 — scroll read → someone asks who the Servant is → **`"preached unto him Jesus."`** → *"That was his name."* That is the best text-driven identification in the piece.

**Weak / announced / imported:**

- *"I tell you now from the far side of my days, by the light that came seven hundred years after me"* — **announces** the turn before showing it. Same failure mode as Noah’s *“I see it now, by a light…”*
- *"They led a Man away to be killed, and he opened not his mouth; and the death I watched turn to life ended in an empty tomb"* — crucifixion/resurrection detail **not in Isaiah’s vision or quoted KJV**. It is imported hindsight, not walked out of 53:5–6. Isaiah did not watch an empty tomb.
- *"Jesus — the Servant, the arm of the LORD made bare"* — **asserted label**. Isa 53:1 asks *“to whom is the arm of the LORD revealed?”* — the piece never earns that link from the binding verses; it stamps it on at the end.
- Beat 1 promises a vision *"I never understood"* — but Beat 6 resolves by **time-jump omniscience**, not by the sheep/iniquity/stripes logic Isaiah actually wrote.

The substitution spine in Beat 3 does real work; the Christ-identification in Beat 6 mostly **leaps** there.

### EW-G8 — Wrestling (steel-man)

**Absent — critical.**

Beat 1 teases struggle: *"a vision on me I never understood"* / *"I could not yet tell his name"* — then **never pays it off** in Isaiah’s voice.

What’s missing (and what the long-form version of this same witness actually had):
- *How can the innocent bear the guilty’s iniquity?* (Isa 53’s real scandal)
- *Who hath believed our report?* (53:1 — disbelief as steel-man)
- Acts 8:34’s question — *of himself, or of some other man?* — mirrored in the eunuch’s mouth, not Isaiah’s

*"He laid our guilt on a Servant who had none. I wrote it, not knowing the One it was about"* is **resolution**, not wrestling. Same pattern flagged on Noah and Abraham shorts: beats 2/4/5 deleted, EW-G8 deleted with them.

### EW-G9 — Doctrine

**Substitution is named clearly and soundly** — the piece’s strongest axis:
- *"every wound was ours, not his own"*
- full 53:5–6 quotes
- *"He laid our guilt on a Servant who had none"*
- *"he has already borne it"*

No supersession, no contrarian reading. Resurrection mention is orthodox.

**Minor fuzz:** *"he bore us away"* after the sheep verse shifts from **straying** to **carrying** — the KJV says iniquity was **laid on** him, not that he bore the sheep away. Small, but it blurs the substitution image right where precision matters.

### EW-G10 — Voice / character

**Fails the eyewitness contract — the biggest structural problem.**

Isaiah is a **prophet who received a vision and wrote it**. He was not at Golgotha, Philip’s chariot, or the empty tomb. The script repeatedly puts him in scenes he never inhabited:

| Line | Problem |
|------|---------|
| *"I watched him come up despised, wanted by no one"* | Prophetic vision recast as physical eyewitness |
| *"The blows fell"* | Isaiah did not see blows fall |
| *"They led a Man away to be killed"* | Passion narrative, not Isaiah’s lived moment |
| *"the death I watched turn to life"* | Grammar breaks; factually false for this witness |
| *"My scroll was carried down a desert road, where Philip read my words"* | Isaiah was not there |
| *"the same hand I watched gather our iniquity"* | Metaphor from writing, not watching |

Beat 1 establishes **writing a vision**; Beat 3–6 switch to **costume-wearing essay narrator** with 700-year hindsight. Compare the blind-man / Jonah good shorts: typology applied from **what they lived**, not from scenes they never attended.

For Isaiah specifically, the honest eyewitness frame is: *I saw it in the spirit, I wrote it, I did not know his name — and here is how the text itself names the wound-bearer.* This piece skips that discipline.

### First-hearing clarity

**Weak for zero-Bible listeners.**

- *"Servant"* — never explained as God’s suffering substitute until late.
- *"the arm of the LORD made bare"* — opaque without Isa 53:1 context (which isn’t quoted).
- **`"preached unto him Jesus."`** — *who* is “him”? Why does that sentence name Jesus?
- *"believe the report I wrote"* — clever for those who know 53:1; opaque for newcomers.
- *"wanted by no one"* — paraphrase of 53:2–3 without the KJV frame; a listener cannot connect it to Scripture.

Hook (*"a vision on me I never understood"*) grips. Spine (substitution → Jesus) holds. But clarity drops sharply in Beat 6.

---

### SHORT form — tightness / single moment

**Word count ~300–310 spoken words — in range for ~90s.**

**But structurally loose for a “single moment” short:**
- Binding core is **53:5–6** (wounds + sheep/iniquity).
- Beat 6 adds **crucifixion + silence + resurrection + empty tomb + Philip/eunuch** — a second thread crowding the one moment.
- Beats labeled **1, 3, 6, 7** — 2/4/5 gone; the wrestling beat was sacrificed to fit two KJV blocks + a mini-passion summary.

The single moment does not fully **land**; it **sprawls** in the reveal.

---

### Hook / spine

**Hook:** adequate — mystery + grief in line 1.  
**Spine:** one thread (Servant wounded for us → Jesus), no swap.  
**Execution:** spine stated well in Beat 3, undermined in Beat 6 by genre drift.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. **Restore EW-G8 steel-man wrestling in Isaiah’s own voice** — e.g. *each soul bears its own sin* vs *the LORD laid our iniquity on a guiltless Servant* / *who hath believed our report?* / *of whom speaketh the prophet?* — so *"not knowing the One it was about"* is earned, not skipped.
2. **Stop pseudo-eyewitness passion scenes (EW-G10 / EW-G7)** — cut or rewrite *"I watched… blows fell… led a Man away… empty tomb"*; keep Isaiah in the **vision-and-scroll** he actually lived; let Acts 8:35 do the Christ-naming without Isaiah attending the desert road.
3. **Fix KJV attribution before every quote (EW-INV-4)** — frame Isa 53 lines as *what I was given to write* / *thus the vision ran*; frame Acts 8:35 as *Luke records Philip preached unto him Jesus* (and one clause naming the eunuch so *“him”* is intelligible).
