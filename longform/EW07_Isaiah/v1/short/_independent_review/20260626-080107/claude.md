# Independent review — claude (OK, 72s)

I reviewed the artifact against the eyewitness lens. The KJV spine and doctrine are mostly solid, but there's a real structural hole.

## KJV verbatim + attribution (EW-INV-4)
- Isa 53:5 quote — **exact KJV.** ✓
- Isa 53:6 quote — **exact KJV.** ✓
- `"preached unto him Jesus."` — verbatim fragment of Acts 8:35. ✓ BUT the embedding is shaky: *"where Philip read my words and 'preached unto him Jesus.'"* In the KJV the **"him" is the eunuch** (Philip preached Jesus *to him*), not the scripture. With no eunuch introduced in the line, the pronoun **"him" dangles** — a zero-Bible listener has no antecedent and may parse it as "preached him [=the scripture] Jesus." Either introduce the eunuch or trim to land the name cleanly.

## The WRESTLING (EW-G8) — the headline failure
The 7-beat spine is exposed and shows **beats 2, 4, and 5 are simply missing** (it jumps 1 → 3 → 6 → 7). The honest-doubt / steel-man beat is gone. All that remains is a faint murmur: *"a vision on me I never understood"* and *"not knowing the One it was about."* That's mild puzzlement, **not a genuine internalized objection** in Isaiah's voice. The strongest real question — *how can a righteous Servant justly bear another's guilt? why would God crush the innocent?* — is never voiced or wrestled. This is the gate that fails hardest.

## The REVEAL (EW-G7) — earned, mostly
Beat 6 does *show* it (the silent Man led away = "he opened not his mouth"; the desert road; Philip naming Jesus) rather than baldly asserting it. Acceptable. Minor: Isaiah claims *"the death I watched turn to life... an empty tomb"* — he saw a vision, not the event; the "far side of my days / by the light that came seven hundred years after me" framing mostly licenses this, but the **repeated "I watched"** (3×) overclaims literal eyewitnessing of the crucifixion/resurrection.

## CTA-on-Jesus (EW-INV-2) — passes
Names Jesus, names the response (*"Come to Jesus; believe the report I wrote"* / *"Trust the One..."*), grace-anchored, no fear/gain-loss/manufactured pressure, no tired bare "will you trust Him?". ✓

## Doctrine (EW-G9) — passes
Substitution is clearly named — penalty + what Christ bore: *"every wound was ours, not his own"*, *"He laid our guilt on a Servant who had none."* No supersession, no contrarian reading. ✓ (Watch *"be healed by his stripes"* reads slightly toward physical-healing out of context, but it's quoting 53:5 and the surrounding frame keeps it about iniquity — fine.)

## Voice (EW-G10) — passes
Consistent, characterful first-person: *"I tell you now from the far side of my days..."* Not the essay narrator in costume. ✓

## Length / tightness — over cap
I count **~325 spoken words**, at/over the 320 ceiling for a ~90s short. The double full-verse quote back-to-back in Beat 3 is the densest stretch; trim the connective prose (e.g. *"He laid our guilt on a Servant who had none. I wrote it, not knowing..."* partly restates the quote you just read).

## First-hearing clarity — one snag
*"the arm of the LORD made bare"* (Isa 52:10/53:1) is opaque to a zero-Bible listener and lands in the reveal where clarity matters most. Either cut it or make it plain.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Restore the wrestling beat — give Isaiah a genuine first-person objection (how can the innocent justly bear the guilty / why would the LORD bruise his own Servant) and let the reveal answer it; right now EW-G8's steel-man is absent (beats 2/4/5 dropped).
2. Fix the dangling "him" in *"Philip read my words and 'preached unto him Jesus'"* — introduce the eunuch or restructure so the verbatim fragment lands with a clear antecedent.
3. Trim to ≤320 words (cut restated prose around the two Isaiah quotes) and replace/explain *"the arm of the LORD made bare"* for first-hearing clarity.
