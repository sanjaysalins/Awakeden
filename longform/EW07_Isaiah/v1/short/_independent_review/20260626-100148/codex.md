# Independent review — codex (OK, 28s)

Findings:

- **EW-INV-4 attribution issue:** The Isaiah 53:5 quote is KJV-exact, but the attribution before it is weak: “every wound was ours, not his own:” introduces interpretation, not the speaker/source. Attribute it as Isaiah’s written prophecy or “the LORD showed me.”
- **EW-INV-4 attribution issue:** “Yet the vision pressed plainer:” before Isaiah 53:6 is not proper attribution. The quote is exact KJV, but “the vision” is not a speaker.
- **EW-INV-4 misattribution risk:** “Philip ran to him and **"preached unto him Jesus."**” quotes Luke’s narration from Acts 8:35, not Philip’s spoken words. The KJV phrase is exact, but this framing makes it read like Philip said those exact words.
- **Eyewitness spine problem:** “A report reached me” and “My scroll was carried down a desert road…” shifts Isaiah beyond his lived moment into posthumous omniscience. For a first-person eyewitness, “from the far side of my days” is a theological device, not a witnessed scene.
- **Reveal partly asserted, not fully earned:** “That was his name. Jesus — the Servant…” lands strongly, but the bridge depends on reported later events rather than showing how Isaiah 53 itself compels the Christ turn. “did not stay among the dead” is asserted without textual development.
- **Wrestling is decent but underdeveloped:** “How can the innocent justly bear the guilty?” is the right objection. But it resolves too quickly with “the vision pressed plainer,” which dodges the moral weight instead of letting Isaiah feel the scandal of substitution.
- **Doctrine mostly sound:** “He laid our guilt on a Servant who had none” and “he has borne it” name substitution. But the close says “carry your wandering home,” which softens the penalty/guilt language. The penalty Christ bore should be clearer.
- **CTA issue:** “Will you trust the report I was given to write?” is very close to the tired bare “will you trust Him?” template the lens forbids. “Come to Jesus” and “Be healed by his stripes” are better, but the response should be named more distinctly: come in faith, receive His grace, turn to Him.
- **First-hearing clarity gap:** “arm of the LORD made bare” is biblical, but unexplained. A zero-Bible listener may not know it means God’s saving power revealed.
- **Short form:** It is tight enough, but the Acts 8 desert-road thread crowds the Isaiah vision moment. The single witnessed moment weakens once the narration pivots to Philip and the Ethiopian.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Fix quote attribution, especially the Acts 8:35 fragment, so KJV narration is not framed as Philip’s spoken words.
2. Make the reveal arise from Isaiah 53 itself before bringing in Jesus, rather than relying on “a report reached me.”
3. Strengthen the CTA by naming the response clearly and replacing the “Will you trust…” template.
