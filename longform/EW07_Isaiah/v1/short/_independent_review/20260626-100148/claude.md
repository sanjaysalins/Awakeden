# Independent review — claude (OK, 60s)

I read the artifact closely against the eyewitness lens. The KJV spine is clean — both Isaiah quotes are verbatim and the Acts 8:35 fragment is exact. But there are real defects.

## Findings

**1. CTA leans on the explicitly-banned template (EW-INV-2).** The close reads: *"Come to Jesus. Will you trust the report I was given to write? Be healed by his stripes."* — "Will you trust the report…" is a cosmetic reskin of the flagged "will you trust Him?" template. The lens specifically bans the tired bare "will you trust" form; rewording the object doesn't escape it. The good news is the rest of the CTA is strong (grace-anchored, "Come to Jesus" names the response, "wandering home / he has borne it" is felt), so this one sentence is the weak link — cut or recast it as an invitation, not a yes/no trust-question.

**2. Beat-number gaps leak scaffolding (1 → 3 → 6 → 7).** Beats 2, 4, 5 are missing from the headers. Either the 7-beat spine is incomplete, or these section labels are build-scaffolding that shouldn't survive into the locked narration at all. As shown it reads unfinished. Strip the beat headers from the final, or fill the spine.

**3. Slightly long for short form (~330 spoken words vs the ~220–320 target).** Beat 3 carries two full KJV blocks + a two-question wrestle + two framing lines; it's the densest section and could shed ~15–20 words without losing the moment.

**4. Watch-item — the post-mortem device (EW-G9 / clarity).** *"I tell you from the far side of my days, by a light seven hundred years after me… A report reached me"* has Isaiah narrating the crucifixion and resurrection from beyond his death. It's a literary conceit and reads as one, but a careless listener could hear a dead prophet *receiving reports* in the afterlife (spiritualist tinge). It's defensible, but it's the one spot a hostile reader will poke. Consider grounding the device as prophetic sight ("the LORD let me see past my own grave") rather than "a report reached me."

**5. Minor first-hearing opacity.** *"the arm of the LORD made bare"* (Isa 52:10/53:1) lands on a zero-Bible listener as a non-sequitur — it's the only phrase that assumes prior knowledge. "Philip" and "an Ethiopian eunuch" arrive cold but the scene carries them; "the arm of the LORD made bare" doesn't.

## What's working (so the revise doesn't break it)
- KJV verbatim is exact on all three quotes; attribution is clean (the vision/scroll for Isaiah, Philip for the Acts fragment).
- The wrestle is a genuine steel-man — *"How can the innocent justly bear the guilty? Why would the LORD bruise His own righteous Servant?"* is the actual strongest objection to penal substitution, in the witness's own voice (EW-G8 ✓).
- Substitution is named concretely — *"every wound was ours, not his own… He laid our guilt on a Servant who had none"* (EW-G9 ✓).
- ONE witness, ONE thread (the unnamed wounded Servant → "That was his name. Jesus"). No swap, no second moment crowding it. The reveal is shown via the text he lived, not announced (EW-G7 ✓).
- First-person voice is consistent and characterful, not the essay narrator in costume (EW-G10 ✓).

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Recast the CTA's "Will you trust the report I was given to write?" — it's the banned bare "will you trust" template reskinned; make it a named invitation, not a yes/no trust-question.
2. Remove the leaked beat-number scaffolding (gaps at 2/4/5) from the locked narration, or complete the spine.
3. Reframe the "a report reached me / far side of my days" device as prophetic sight rather than a post-mortem report, and trim Beat 3 to bring it under ~320 spoken words.
