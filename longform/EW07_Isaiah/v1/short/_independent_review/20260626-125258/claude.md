# Independent review — claude (OK, 64s)

Reviewed adversarially against the eyewitness invariants. This is a strong piece — great hook, earned reveal, clean spine. But there are real, specific gaps.

**KJV / attribution (EW-INV-4)**
- The bold quote **"preached unto him Jesus."** is exact Acts 8:35 ("...and preached unto him Jesus") and is correctly attributed to Philip. Clean. ✓
- BUT in Beat 3: "asked the very question I had asked: **of whom speaketh the prophet this?**" — that phrase is verbatim KJV (Acts 8:34, the eunuch's line) yet it is NOT marked as a quote, and it is half-misattributed. The *stranger* (eunuch) asked it; the clause "the very question **I** had asked" re-assigns the eunuch's actual spoken words to Isaiah. So you have unmarked KJV scripture being claimed as Isaiah's own question. Either mark/attribute it as the eunuch's words, or paraphrase it out of KJV so it isn't mistaken for Isaiah's quote.

**The wrestling is thin (EW-G8) — the main weakness.** The "honest doubt" is only disquiet: "I could not make it lie quiet in me." That's wonder, not the *strongest objection*. The genuine steel-man Isaiah's vision raises is the **justice** of it — how can the guiltless bear the guilty's penalty and that be right? The narration asserts the substitution ("every wound was ours... He had done no wrong; I had") but never lets Isaiah *press* on it. It's the one beat that doesn't earn its steel.

**Substitution named but penalty soft (EW-G9).** Doctrine is sound — guilt transferred, "he has borne it already," no supersession, no contrarian reading. ✓ But the *penalty* component is vague: we hear "guilt" and "wandering" but never what that guilt incurred (judgment/death). "wounded for our transgressions" is implied, not landed. Name what was owed, so "he has borne it already" has weight.

**CTA (EW-INV-2).** "Come to Jesus, and be healed by His stripes." — names Jesus, names the response ("Come"), grace-anchored, not the tired "will you trust Him?" template. ✓ Watch only that "be healed" doesn't tip toward self-interest; it's saved here by being the text's own promise.

**Voice (EW-G10).** Consistent, characterful first-person ("I went to my grave never knowing his name," "Centuries after my hand fell still"). ✓ The Beat 4 shift to second-person ("You were never made...") is an acceptable CTA pivot but is the one place the witness frame thins.

**Clarity / length.** Zero-Bible listener follows it; "Philip" and "a stranger" carry by context. ✓ Length ~200 spoken words — at the *short* end of the band; given the thin wrestling, you have room to add 2–3 lines there without bloating.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Fix the unmarked/misattributed KJV line "of whom speaketh the prophet this?" — it's the eunuch's verbatim words, not Isaiah's; either attribute/mark it as his question or paraphrase it so it isn't read as Isaiah's quote.
2. Deepen Beat 2's wrestling (EW-G8) into the *real* objection — the justice of the innocent bearing the guilty's penalty — in Isaiah's own voice, instead of vague disquiet.
3. Name the penalty in the substitution (EW-G9): say what the guilt incurred (judgment/death we were owed) so "he has borne it already" lands with full weight.
