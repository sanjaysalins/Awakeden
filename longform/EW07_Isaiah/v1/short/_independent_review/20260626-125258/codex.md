# Independent review — codex (OK, 23s)

Concrete findings:

- **EW-INV-4 KJV attribution problem:** `**"preached unto him Jesus."**` is exact KJV from Acts 8:35, but the narration does not attribute it cleanly. The quoted words are Luke’s narrative report, not Philip’s spoken words. The lead-in `Then Philip sat beside him, began at my own words, and` makes the quote feel like Philip’s action-language, but it should be attributed as Scripture/Luke says before quotation.
- **Potential KJV styling inconsistency:** `of whom speaketh the prophet this?` is KJV wording from Acts 8:34, but it is not placed in the required `**"..."**` quote format. If this is intended as Scripture quotation, it needs exact quote treatment and attribution. If paraphrase, it is too close to KJV to dodge the rule.
- **Reveal is partly announced, not fully earned:** `That was the name I was never given.` is a strong turn, but the Christ connection rests mostly on Acts 8:35 being quoted. The piece says Philip “began at my own words,” but does not show how the wounds/guilt/stripes specifically resolve in Jesus beyond assertion.
- **Wrestling is underdeveloped:** `I could not make it lie quiet in me` hints at inner trouble, but the strongest objection is not really voiced. Isaiah’s hard question should be something like how the innocent Servant can bear the guilty without injustice. Here the doubt is atmospheric, not steel-manned.
- **Substitution is present but could be sharper:** `every wound was ours, not his own` and `lay the guilt of us all... onto a Servant` are good, but the close softens into `carry your wandering home`. The penalty and what Christ bore are not named as plainly as the lens asks.
- **First-hearing clarity risk:** `my scroll was carried down a desert road` and `Philip sat beside him` assume knowledge of Acts 8. A zero-Bible listener may not know who Philip is, who the stranger is, or why that desert road matters.
- **Voice mostly works, but occasionally becomes theological essay:** `the Servant wounded for our transgressions, fulfilled in Christ and named on the desert road` in the header and `The report is true` feel more like doctrinal framing than Isaiah’s lived first-person witness.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Attribute the Acts 8:35 KJV quote correctly and handle the Acts 8:34 wording consistently.
2. Strengthen the earned reveal by showing how Isaiah’s wounded, guilt-bearing Servant is fulfilled in Jesus, not just naming Him.
3. Give Isaiah a real internal objection: how the innocent can justly bear the guilty.
