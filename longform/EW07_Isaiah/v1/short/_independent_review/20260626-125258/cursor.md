## Independent adversarial review — *The Suffering Servant* (Isaiah, eyewitness-short)

### EW-INV-4 — KJV verbatim + attribution: **PASS (narrow)**

The only `**"..."**` quote is **`"preached unto him Jesus."`** — exact KJV Acts 8:35 substring. Verbatim is clean.

Attribution is shaky, not wrong on the words:

- **`Then Philip sat beside him, began at my own words, and`** → **`"preached unto him Jesus."`** — those words are **Luke’s narrative summary**, not Philip’s spoken dialogue. The lead-in makes it read like Philip’s action-language. For EW-INV-4 this is borderline; for a zero-Bible listener it sounds like Philip *said* that exact sentence.
- **`of whom speaketh the prophet this?`** is verbatim KJV (Acts 8:34, minus *“I pray thee”* and *“of himself, or of some other man?”*) but is **not** in `**"..."**` format and is **not attributed to the eunuch**. If it’s Scripture, it should be quoted and attributed; if it’s paraphrase, it’s too close to KJV to dodge scrutiny.

No Isa 53 KJV is quoted at all — paraphrases like **`sheep gone astray, each turned his own way`** (53:6) and **`healed by His stripes`** (53:5) are outside quote marks, so EW-INV-4 doesn’t flag them, but the **load-bearing OT moment never gets Scripture’s exact wording**.

### EW-G8 — Wrestling: **FAIL (absent)**

There is no steel-man in Isaiah’s own voice. The only interior trouble is atmospheric:

- **`though I could not make it lie quiet in me`**

That is unease, not objection. Isaiah’s genuine strongest doubts are never voiced:

- How can the **innocent** Servant bear the **guilty** without injustice? (*the LORD lay the guilt of us all… onto a Servant who carried none of it* asserts the scandal but never lets Isaiah *wrestle* with it)
- **`Who hath believed our report?`** (53:1) — skepticism that anyone would believe this
- **`of whom speaketh the prophet this?`** — identity of the Servant

Beat 3 imports the identity question from the **eunuch** (*`asked the very question I had asked`*), but Beat 2 never gives Isaiah’s version of that doubt first. The steel-man is **borrowed externally**, not internalized — same failure pattern flagged on Moses/Noah shorts.

Spec §2: SHORT compresses beats but wrestling is still part of the DNA (EW-G8 is a named gate).

### EW-G7 — Reveal earned: **FAIL / weak**

**What works:** Beat 2 *shows* transfer — **`every wound was ours, not his own`**, **`the LORD lay the guilt of us all`**, **`onto a Servant who carried none of it`**. Substitution is planted from the vision, not only at the close.

**What fails:** The **type→Christ identification** is not earned from that vision. It arrives via posthumous report:

- **`Centuries after my hand fell still, my scroll was carried down a desert road`**
- **`Philip sat beside him, began at my own words`**
- **`"preached unto him Jesus."`**
- **`That was the name I was never given.`**

The hinge line is strong, but Isaiah did not live Acts 8. EW-INV-11 allows a signposted cross-time device — **`Centuries after my hand fell still`** signposts it — yet the block still reads as **omniscient commentary**: Isaiah narrates Philip, a stranger, and a desert road he never saw, then **`The report is true`** **asserts** fulfillment rather than **showing** how the wounded/guilt-bearing Servant he wrote is the same Jesus Philip preached. A zero-Bible listener gets a name-drop, not a demonstrated turn.

The punch partially re-earns substitution (**`he has borne it already`**, **`healed by His stripes`**), but **`carry your wandering home`** softens *iniquity/guilt* into metaphor without reconnecting to the vision’s wound-transfer.

### First-hearing clarity: **FAIL / weak**

A listener with zero Bible knowledge will stumble on:

- **`a stranger`** — who? (Ethiopian eunuch never named)
- **`Philip`** — who is he, why does his preaching prove anything?
- **`of whom speaketh the prophet this?`** — without one grounding clause, this is opaque Elizabethan syntax
- **`preached unto him Jesus`** — *who* is *“him”*? Antecedent is **`stranger`**, but the quote’s *him* is the eunuch; easy to misparse.

The Servant/wound/guilt thread in Beat 2 is clear; the Acts-8 proof block is not.
