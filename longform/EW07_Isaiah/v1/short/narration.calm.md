## Beat 1 — I was there
I am Isaiah, son of Amoz. The LORD laid a vision on me I never understood, and I wrote it word for word. I saw a Servant, and grief came over me, for I could not tell his name.

## Beat 3 — The act
In the vision he came up despised, wanted by no one. The blows fell — and every wound was ours, not his own:

**"But he was wounded for our transgressions, he was bruised for our iniquities: the chastisement of our peace was upon him; and with his stripes we are healed."**

But I wrestled. How can the innocent justly bear the guilty? Why would the LORD bruise His own righteous Servant? Yet the vision pressed plainer:

**"All we like sheep have gone astray; we have turned every one to his own way; and the LORD hath laid on him the iniquity of us all."**

He laid our guilt on a Servant who had none, and I wrote it, not knowing the One it was about.

## Beat 6 — The reveal
I tell you from the far side of my days, by a light seven hundred years after me. The vision I wrote was fulfilled in a Man led to die, who opened not his mouth; and the Servant I had seen wounded — the Spirit testifies — did not stay among the dead. My scroll was carried to an Ethiopian eunuch; and Philip ran to him and

**"preached unto him Jesus."**

That was his name. Jesus — the Servant, the arm of the LORD made bare.

## Beat 7 — The invitation
Be still, and hear me. I carried this vision to my grave without his Name. You have it now. The same hand I saw gather our iniquity is open toward you still. You were never made to carry your wandering home — he has borne it. The report I was given to write is true, and the Servant has a name. Come to Jesus, and be healed by his stripes.
