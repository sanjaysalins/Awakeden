## Beat 1 — I was there
I am Isaiah, the son of Amoz, and I prophesied in Judah in the days of her kings. I saw many things in my years — a throne high and lifted up, a far-off light over a people who walked in darkness. But there was one vision the LORD laid on me that I never wholly understood, and I wrote it word for word, for the words were not mine to soften or change. I saw a Man — a Servant — and as I watched him a grief came over me I cannot describe, for I could not yet tell his name. And the very first words rose in me like a cry torn out of my chest:

**"Who hath believed our report? and to whom is the arm of the LORD revealed?"**

Even as I wrote it, I knew. What I had been shown would be too hard for most who heard it to believe.

## Beat 2 — The world
You who hear me — understand the world I stood in. I was a man of unclean lips, dwelling in the midst of a people of unclean lips, in a small land that trembled between great empires. And we were waiting — for the arm of the LORD to be made bare, for our God to roll back His sleeve and come down and save us. When a whole people waits for God to show His strength, they look for a warrior — a king upon a horse, with sword and crown and the armies of heaven behind him. That is the salvation I expected too; I had been set as a watchman, and thought I knew the shape our rescue would take. Then the LORD turned my eyes to a single Servant, and the strength of God came down before me not as a sword lifted up, but as a body broken low.

## Beat 3 — The act
Let me tell you what was put into my mouth, for I invented not one line of it. The word of the LORD came first like a king's decree, and at first hearing it filled me with hope:

**[the LORD]** **"Behold, my servant shall deal prudently, he shall be exalted and extolled, and be very high."**

Exalted, extolled, very high — so I looked for splendour. But the vision that followed turned in my hands like a knife: I saw him come up small, from poor ground, wanted by no one:

**"For he shall grow up before him as a tender plant, and as a root out of a dry ground: he hath no form nor comeliness; and when we shall see him, there is no beauty that we should desire him."**

And I saw how men would look on him — or look away:

**"He is despised and rejected of men; a man of sorrows, and acquainted with grief: and we hid as it were our faces from him; he was despised, and we esteemed him not."**

And then came the line my hand trembled to set down — for it told me whose sorrow this was:

**"Surely he hath borne our griefs, and carried our sorrows: yet we did esteem him stricken, smitten of God, and afflicted."**

Ours — not his own. He carried what belonged to us, though to every eye that watched it looked as though God were striking him for his own sin. And the LORD pressed it plainer still:

**"But he was wounded for our transgressions, he was bruised for our iniquities: the chastisement of our peace was upon him; and with his stripes we are healed."**

Wounded for ours — and, in the same breath, a healing I could not explain.

## Beat 4 — The strange detail
Here is the riddle that followed me to the end of my days. This Servant had done no wrong, yet every blow that fell on him, some other man had earned — the innocent, crushed for the guilty; and most terrible of all, it was the LORD Himself who willed it done:

**"All we like sheep have gone astray; we have turned every one to his own way; and the LORD hath laid on him the iniquity of us all."**

The LORD laid it on him — our guilt, bound onto a Servant who had none. I wrote it down, though I could not make the words lie down quiet in me. And then I saw a thing stranger yet, that should not stand inside one vision. I saw the Servant truly die — cut off, given over to a grave:

**"he was cut off out of the land of the living: for the transgression of my people was he stricken."**

And in that same vision, on the far side of that grave, I saw him living — satisfied, long of days, with a seed of his own: not children of his body, but an offspring won out of his dying:

**"he shall see his seed, he shall prolong his days, and the pleasure of the LORD shall prosper in his hand."**

Cut off out of the land of the living, yet prolonging his days — dead, and alive beyond it. How could one Man be both? I did not understand it. I only knew the LORD had said it, and so it was true.

## Beat 5 — The wrestling
And I will be honest with you, for a prophet who covers his own ignorance with fine words is no prophet at all. Two things I could not lay to rest.

The first was not who he was, but how it could be just. All my life I had taught that each soul must bear its own sin — that a righteous Judge will by no means lay the guilt of the wicked upon the innocent. Yet here was that very Judge doing the one thing I had said He never would. Was God then unjust? Or was there a love deeper than the law I knew — a love that would sooner be wounded itself than let the guilty perish? I could not see to the bottom of it; I only knew the LORD had said it, and the Judge of all the earth does right.

The second was who. I read my own scroll like a stranger and searched. Of myself? I had a guilty mouth like any man. Of Hezekiah our king? He died and stayed dead. Of Israel my own people? That reading I wrestled longest, for the LORD had called Israel His servant a hundred times, and we had suffered long among the nations. But the words would not bend to it: this Servant was wounded for the transgression of my people — he suffered for us; he could not be us. We were the sheep gone astray; he was the Lamb that bore us away. And in whose mouth was there no deceit? Not one man of my generation fit the words. I laid down my pen still searching — I believed the report I had written, but I did not yet know the One it was about.

## Beat 6 — The reveal
I did not see the answer in my own lifetime. I tell you now from the far side of my days, by the light that came long after my hand fell still — by One who came seven hundred years after me, whose name I was never given to write.

The whole vision was a shadow — and a shadow is cast by a body that is coming. The body came. There was a Man; they led him away to be killed, and he answered nothing, exactly as I had seen:

**"he is brought as a lamb to the slaughter, and as a sheep before her shearers is dumb, so he openeth not his mouth."**

They gave him a criminal's death and a rich man's tomb in the same day:

**"And he made his grave with the wicked, and with the rich in his death."**

They counted him among thieves, and on him lay the wrong that was never his:

**"he was numbered with the transgressors; and he bare the sin of many."**

And the death I had seen turn into life — the Spirit testifies he did not stay in that grave. He prolonged his days; he sees his seed even now. And here is how the name was given: the very scroll I wrote was carried down a desert road by a stranger from Ethiopia, who read my words aloud and asked the question I myself had asked — of whom speaketh the prophet this? And Philip the evangelist sat beside him, and beginning at my own words, he

**"preached unto him Jesus."**

That was his name. Jesus. He was the Servant. He was the arm of the LORD, made bare at last for all the world to see.

## Beat 7 — The invitation
Hear me, you who listen across the years — be still. Do not look to me. I was only a watchman given a vision to write; I never saw the Servant's face, never stood at his cross. I saw it from afar, the night the LORD set before me — wounded for our transgressions, and the LORD laid on him the iniquity of us all, mine and yours. I carried those words to my grave without the comfort of his name. But you have the name I was not given.

Sit with that. The same hand I watched gather the iniquity of us all is open toward you still, and Jesus is the One it was laid upon.

So I ask the question I asked over my own scroll — now there is no dread in it, for the name has come: will you trust the report I was given to write? Not on my word, but on his — the Servant wounded for your transgressions, alive beyond the grave. Believe the report I was given to write: His name is Jesus, and by His stripes you are healed.
