# The Suffering Servant — Awakeden eyewitness (long)

---
## Beat 1 — I was there
I am Isaiah, the son of Amoz, and I prophesied in Judah in the days of her kings. I saw many things in my years — a throne high and lifted up, the fall of nations, a far-off light over a people who walked in darkness. But there was one vision the LORD laid on me that I never wholly understood, and I wrote it down word for word, because the words were not mine to soften or to change. I saw a Man — a Servant — and as I watched him a grief came over me that I cannot describe, for I could not yet tell you his name. And the very first words rose in me like a cry torn out of my chest:

**"Who hath believed our report? and to whom is the arm of the LORD revealed?"**

Even as I wrote it, I knew. What I had been shown would be too hard for most who heard it to believe.

## Beat 2 — The world
You who hear me — understand the world I stood in. I was a man of unclean lips, dwelling in the midst of a people of unclean lips, in a small land that trembled between great empires. And we were waiting. We were waiting for the arm of the LORD to be made bare — for our God to roll back His sleeve and come down and save us. When a whole people waits for God to show His strength, I will tell you what they look for. They look for a warrior. They look for a king upon a horse, with a sword and a crown and the armies of heaven behind him. That is the salvation I expected too. That was the weight I carried for years: I had been set as a watchman, and I thought I knew the shape our rescue would take. Then the LORD turned my eyes to a single Servant, and the strength of God came down before me not as a sword lifted up, but as a body broken low.

## Beat 3 — The act
Let me tell you what was put into my mouth, for I invented not one line of it; my hand only wrote what I was given to write. The word of the LORD came over the Servant first as a king's decree, and at the first hearing it filled me with hope:

**[the LORD]** **"Behold, my servant shall deal prudently, he shall be exalted and extolled, and be very high."**

Exalted. Extolled. Very high. So I looked for splendour. But the vision that followed turned in my hands like a knife. I saw him come up small, and from poor ground, and wanted by no one:

**"For he shall grow up before him as a tender plant, and as a root out of a dry ground: he hath no form nor comeliness; and when we shall see him, there is no beauty that we should desire him."**

I saw men cover their faces and turn from him in the street:

**"He is despised and rejected of men; a man of sorrows, and acquainted with grief."**

And then came the line my hand trembled to set down — for it told me whose sorrow this truly was:

**"Surely he hath borne our griefs, and carried our sorrows."**

Ours. Not his own. He was carrying what belonged to us. And the LORD pressed it plainer and harder still, until I could not mistake it:

**"But he was wounded for our transgressions, he was bruised for our iniquities."**

Wounded for ours. Bruised for ours. And then, in the same breath, a healing I had no way to explain:

**"the chastisement of our peace was upon him; and with his stripes we are healed."**

## Beat 4 — The strange detail
Here is the riddle that followed me to the end of my days. This Servant had done no wrong — and yet every blow that fell upon him was a blow that some other man had earned. I had taught the law of God for years: that each soul shall bear its own sin and die for its own guilt. But this was the opposite. This was the innocent crushed for the guilty — and most terrible of all, it was the LORD Himself who willed it done:

**"All we like sheep have gone astray; we have turned every one to his own way; and the LORD hath laid on him the iniquity of us all."**

The LORD laid it on him. The Judge of all the earth gathered up our guilt — yours and mine — and bound it onto His own Servant's back. And then I saw a thing stranger yet, a thing that should not be able to stand inside one vision. I saw the Servant truly die — cut off, taken away, given over to a grave:

**"he was cut off out of the land of the living: for the transgression of my people was he stricken."**

And in that very same vision, on the far side of that grave, I saw him living. Satisfied. Long of days. With children of his own:

**"he shall see his seed, he shall prolong his days."**

Cut off out of the land of the living — yet prolonging his days. Dead, and somehow alive again beyond it. How could one Man be both? I wrote it down. I did not understand it. I only knew that the LORD had said it, and so it was true.

## Beat 5 — The wrestling
And I will be honest with you, for a prophet who covers his own ignorance with fine words is no prophet at all. I did not know who he was. I read my own scroll like a stranger, and searched. Of whom did the Spirit within me speak? Of myself? I had a guilty mouth like any man. Of Hezekiah our king? He died and stayed dead. Of Israel my own people? But we were the sheep gone astray, not the Lamb that bore us. Not one man of my generation fit the words. Where was the man who had done no violence, in whose mouth was found no deceit, who opened not his mouth when they struck him, and who bore the sin of many and never once his own? I looked for him under the whole of heaven, through all my years, and did not find him. I had been handed a face I could not name, and a death that ended in life I could not explain. I laid down my pen still searching. I believed the report I had written — but I did not yet know the One the report was about.

## Beat 6 — The reveal
I did not see the answer in my own lifetime. I tell you now from the far side of my days, by the light that came long after my hand fell still — by the One who came some seven hundred years after me, whose name I was never given to write, but which I will say to you plainly now: Jesus.

The whole vision was a shadow — and a shadow is cast by a body that is coming. The body came. There was a Man, and they led him away to be killed, and he answered them nothing, exactly as I had seen:

**"he is brought as a lamb to the slaughter, and as a sheep before her shearers is dumb, so he openeth not his mouth."**

They handed him a criminal's death and a rich man's tomb, both in the same day:

**"And he made his grave with the wicked, and with the rich in his death."**

They counted him among thieves, and on him lay the wrong that was never his:

**"he was numbered with the transgressors; and he bare the sin of many."**

And the death I had seen turn into life — they came to his tomb and it was empty. He prolonged his days. He sees his seed even now. Hear this wonder, then: the very scroll I wrote was carried down a desert road in the hands of a stranger from Ethiopia, who read my words aloud and asked the one question I myself had asked — of whom speaketh the prophet this? And a man named Philip went up and sat beside him, and beginning at my own words, he

**"preached unto him Jesus."**

That was his name. Jesus. He was the Servant. He was the arm of the LORD, made bare at last for all the world to see.

## Beat 7 — The invitation
So hear me now, you who listen across all these years. I was given to write that this Servant would justify not a few, but many — and it was the LORD Himself who spoke it over him:

**[the LORD]** **"by his knowledge shall my righteous servant justify many; for he shall bear their iniquities."**

Many. That word is wide enough to hold you. I confessed it over all of us — that we like sheep had gone astray, every one of us turned to his own way — and the same hand I watched gather up all that iniquity is open toward you still. You were never made to carry your own wandering home; there is no need. He has already borne it.

Come to Jesus, and believe the report I was given to write. Do not wait any longer for a warrior on a horse; the strength of God has already come down as a wounded Servant, and he has finished it. Trust the One who was wounded for your transgressions, and let your sins be among the sins laid on him. Believe him — and be counted among the many he justifies. Come, and be healed by his stripes.
