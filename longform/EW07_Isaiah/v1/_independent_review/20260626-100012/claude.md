# Independent review — claude (OK, 84s)

Read the artifact closely against KJV and the EW gates. It's strong work — but there's one real factual overreach and a couple of softer issues. Concrete findings:

**KJV verbatim (EW-INV-4) — clean.** I checked all 13 quoted fragments against the KJV:
- Isa 53:1, 52:13, 53:2, 53:3, 53:4, 53:5, 53:6, 53:8 (frag), 53:10 (frag), 53:7 (frag), 53:9 (frag), 53:12 (frag), and Acts 8:35 ("preached unto him Jesus") — all verbatim, no altered words. Good.

**Attribution — one soft spot.** Beat 3 frames Isa 53:4–6 with **"And the LORD pressed it plainer still"** immediately before *"But he was wounded for our transgressions..."* In the text these verses are the confession of the redeemed *"we,"* not God's first-person decree. It's defensible inside the dramatic conceit (the LORD showing/speaking the whole vision), and no KJV word is altered, so it's not a hard violation — but a careful reader will notice the voice is assigned to the LORD when Scripture gives it to the community. Worth a light touch.

**FACTUAL OVERREACH (the one real defect) — Beat 6.** *"And a man named Philip, who had followed the Servant himself and seen him risen, sat beside him..."* This is not supported and likely conflates two people. The Philip of Acts 8 is **Philip the evangelist, "one of the seven"** (Acts 6:5; 21:8) — a deacon, *not* the apostle Philip and not established anywhere as a follower of Jesus' earthly ministry or a resurrection witness. In a piece whose entire brand is *eyewitness accuracy*, inventing "followed the Servant himself and seen him risen" for Philip is exactly the kind of unsupported detail the doctrine-soundness gate (EW-G9: no invented reading) exists to catch. Fix: drop the claim or recast it ("a man named Philip, a preacher of the risen Lord") so nothing rests on a fabricated biography.

**CTA (EW-INV-2) — passes, and handles the template trap well.** *"will you trust the report I was given to write?... Come to Jesus, and be healed by his stripes."* The named response is **"Come to Jesus"** — concrete, grace-anchored, no fear/gain-loss. The bare "will you trust Him?" template is deliberately *recast* as the question Isaiah asked over his own scroll ("now there is no dread in it"), which earns it rather than recycling it. Good.

**Wrestling (EW-G8) — genuinely strong steel-man.** The two doubts are the *actual* strongest objections: (1) the justice of penal substitution — *"Was God then unjust?"* and (2) the corporate-Israel reading — *"Of Israel my own people? That reading I wrestled longest..."* — which is the real historic counter-interpretation, not a strawman, and it's answered from the text (*"he suffered for us; he could not be us"*) without supersession. Excellent.

**Doctrine (EW-G9) — substitution named, no supersession.** Penalty + what He bore is explicit (*"the innocent, crushed for the guilty"*, *"the LORD hath laid on him the iniquity of us all"*). The Israel distinction avoids replacement theology. Sound.

**Reveal earned (EW-G7) — yes.** The turn isn't announced; it's *shown* via the mechanism of Isaiah's own scroll being read down the Gaza road and Philip naming the name — the witness's own text doing the revealing.

**Voice (EW-G10) — consistent first-person throughout**, no essay-narrator leak.

**Length / sag — mild redundancy, not fatal.** Beats 6–7 re-state the same core line twice (*"wounded for our transgressions, and the LORD laid on him the iniquity of us all"* appears in both), and the "name I was never given to write" motif lands three times (Beat 6 + twice in Beat 7). It's contemplative-close repetition, mostly earned, but the third "name not given" beat could be trimmed so the runtime doesn't tread water at the very end.

**First-hearing clarity — good.** *"unclean lips"* (Isa 6) and *"arm of the LORD... roll back His sleeve"* are either self-explained or harmless to a zero-Bible listener.

Net: one genuine accuracy error (Philip), one soft attribution choice, one mild end-of-piece repetition. The Philip claim alone is enough to send it back, given the eyewitness-accuracy brand.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Beat 6 — fix the unsupported claim that Philip "had followed the Servant himself and seen him risen." Acts 8's Philip is Philip the evangelist (one of the seven deacons), not an apostle/resurrection witness; recast or delete the biography so nothing rests on an invented detail.
2. Trim the end-of-piece repetition (the duplicated "wounded for our transgressions... iniquity of us all" line and the triple "name I was never given" motif) so Beats 6–7 keep advancing instead of restating.
3. Soften the Beat 3 framing "And the LORD pressed it plainer still" before Isa 53:4–6, since those verses are the confession of the "we," not the LORD's direct speech — adjust the attribution cue to avoid assigning the community's voice to God.
