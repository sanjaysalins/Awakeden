# Independent review — codex (OK, 95s)

Checked KJV wording against BibleGateway’s KJV text for Isaiah 52:13-53:12 and Acts 8:32-35. The quoted wording is mostly exact; the larger failures are attribution, witness logic, and ending shape.

**Findings**

- **EW-INV-4: attribution is too loose.** The opening quote `**"Who hath believed our report?...` is introduced by “the very first words rose in me,” not a clear speaker/source attribution. Later quotes are introduced by vague narration like “then came the line” and “I saw a thing stranger yet.” If the rule is strict, each bold KJV quote needs attribution before it, e.g. Isaiah/LORD/Scripture records.

- **Possible quote-attribution mismatch:** `**[the LORD]** **"Behold, my servant shall deal prudently..."**` is acceptable as divine speech, but most Isaiah 53 quotes are collective prophetic confession/report, not all direct speech from the LORD. The piece blurs that distinction with “the LORD pressed it plainer still.”

- **Eyewitness frame breaks at the reveal.** Isaiah says, “I did not see the answer in my own lifetime,” then narrates Jesus’ death, empty tomb, Philip, and the Ethiopian: “the very scroll I wrote was carried down a desert road…” That is not eyewitness narration from a moment Isaiah lived. It becomes posthumous omniscient narrator.

- **The spine swaps witnesses.** The thread starts as Isaiah receiving/wrestling with the Servant vision, but the reveal depends on “a stranger from Ethiopia” and “a man named Philip.” That is a different eyewitness scene. The piece should either stay with Isaiah’s prophetic vision or formally make Acts 8 the witnessed reveal through the Ethiopian/Philip.

- **CTA violates the tired-template warning.** The close says, “will you trust the report I was given to write?” It is more specific than bare “trust Him,” but still lands in the prohibited template zone. “Come to Jesus” is better, but the final emotional mechanism is still a familiar trust-question.

- **Substitution is present but not fully named as penalty.** Strong phrases: “wounded for our transgressions,” “the iniquity of us all,” “on him lay the wrong that was never his.” But the doctrinal requirement asks for the penalty and what Christ bore. The piece never plainly says Jesus bore the judgment/penalty/death due to sinners.

- **Justice wrestling is strong but under-resolved.** “Was God then unjust? Or was there a love deeper than the law I knew” risks implying love bypasses justice. Evangelical substitution needs justice satisfied, not God doing “the one thing I had said He never would” and then waving it through because love is deeper.

- **Voice sometimes turns essay-like.** “The whole vision was a shadow — and a shadow is cast by a body that is coming. The body came” sounds like a theological explainer, not Isaiah son of Amoz. Same with “not children of his body, but an offspring won out of his dying.”

- **First-hearing clarity has gaps.** A zero-Bible listener will not know why “rich man’s tomb,” “numbered with the transgressors,” Philip, or Ethiopia matter. The piece names them but does not make the evidential force clear enough.

- **Long-form sag:** Beat 4 and Beat 5 repeat the same unresolved note: “I could not make the words lie down quiet in me,” “I did not understand it,” “Two things I could not lay to rest.” The wrestling is real, but the runtime would tighten if each repetition added a new turn instead of restating bewilderment.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Fix the eyewitness-frame break: keep Isaiah inside what he actually lived, or change the reveal witness to Philip/the Ethiopian.
2. Repair quote attribution so every bold KJV quote is explicitly sourced/speaker-attributed before it.
3. Strengthen the close: name repentance/faith/receiving Christ without the “will you trust…” template, and state that Jesus bore the penalty/judgment due to sinners.
