## Independent adversarial review — *The Suffering Servant* (eyewitness long)

### EW-INV-4 — KJV verbatim + attribution

**Attribution is inconsistent and often missing.** EW-INV-4 requires the speaker to be identified *before* each quoted block. Only one quote is properly tagged:

- Beat 3: `**[the LORD]** **"Behold, my servant shall deal prudently..."**` — correct.
- Beat 1: `**"Who hath believed our report?..."**` — preceded only by “the very first words rose in me,” not a routable speaker label or clear “the LORD put in my mouth.”
- Beat 3 (after the first block): `**"For he shall grow up..."**`, `**"He is despised..."**`, `**"Surely he hath borne our griefs..."**`, `**"But he was wounded..."**` — no `[the LORD]`, no “I wrote,” no re-attribution.
- Beat 4: all four quote blocks — no attribution.
- Beat 6: `**"he is brought as a lamb to the slaughter..."**`, grave/transgressors quotes — no attribution (“exactly as I had seen” is commentary, not a speaker frame).

**Misattributed “dialogue.”** In Beat 6:

> “And a man named Philip… sat beside him, and beginning at my own words, he **"preached unto him Jesus."**”

Those five words are Luke’s narrative summary of Philip’s preaching (Acts 8:35), not Philip’s direct speech. Tagging them as something *Philip said* blurs attribution. If quoted at all, they belong to scripture/Luke’s narration, not `[Philip]`.

**Partial quotes are verbatim substrings** (checked against the project’s `passage.txt` and KJV cache) — no altered words found in the bold blocks themselves.

### EW-G7 — Reveal earned vs. announced

**Earned:** Beat 6’s lamb-to-slaughter, grave-with-wicked/rich, and numbered-with-transgressors mapping tracks the vision Isaiah already received. Beat 5’s corporate-Israel rejection (“We were the sheep… he was the Lamb”) is text-driven.

**Not earned — imported NT briefing:**

- “**I did not see the answer in my own lifetime.**” / “**by the light that came long after my hand fell still**”
- “**they came to his tomb and found it empty.**”
- “**He prolonged his days; he sees his seed even now.**”

Isaiah’s scroll gives the death/life *riddle* in 53:10 (“cut off… prolong his days… see his seed”), but the empty tomb is not in his pericope. Beat 6 jumps from the riddle to Easter fact without quoting or re-walking 53:10–11 in the reveal. The turn becomes **posthumous omniscience**, not discovery from what he wrote.

**Typology asserted, not shown:**

> “**The whole vision was a shadow — and a shadow is cast by a body that is coming.**”

No line from Isaiah 52–53 supports “shadow”; it reads like sermon glue between vision and Passion recap.

**Philip/Ethiopia is Acts 8 recap**, not something Isaiah witnessed. Fine as a *signposted* reflective device (per EW-INV-11), but the piece doesn’t keep that boundary tight — “**And here is how I came to know his name**” presents it as Isaiah’s knowledge path, then **“That was his name. Jesus.”** announces rather than demonstrates.

### EW-G8 — Wrestling (strong, with one soft landing)

**Steel-man (good):**

- Justice: “**Was God then unjust?**” / “**that very Judge doing the one thing I had said He never would**” — real scandal of vicarious bearing.
- Identity: corporate Israel as servant vs. individual innocent Lamb — the strongest live alternative reading, rejected on textual grounds (“**he could not be us**”).

**Soft spots:**

- “**All my life I had taught that each soul must bear its own sin — that a righteous Judge will by no means lay the guilt of the wicked upon the innocent**” — this is Ezekiel 18–style formulation; Isaiah (8th c. BC) predates Ezekiel’s explicit argument. The objection is theologically right but **historically thin in Isaiah’s mouth** unless tied to Torah he’d actually cite (e.g. Deut 24:16).
- Beat 5 ends honestly unresolved (“**I laid down my pen still searching**”), but Beat 6 resolves everything cleanly — the wrestling is **undercut by neat post-death certainty**.

### EW-G9 — Doctrine

**Sound core:** Substitution is named repeatedly and correctly — “**wounded for our transgressions**,” “**the LORD hath laid on him the iniquity of us all**,” “**he bare the sin of many**,” stripes/healing. No supersessionism; Israel-as-servant reading is rejected on text. Philip → Jesus is standard evangelical fulfillment.

**Gaps:**

- The pericope’s sharpest penal language — 53:10 “**when thou shalt make his soul an offering for sin**” — is **never quoted**. Substitution is named, but the **sin-offering / penalty-mechanism** is under-specified compared to what the text offers.
- “**chastisement of our peace was upon him**” is quoted but never unpacked; a zero-Bible listener may not hear *penalty borne for our shalom*.
- Beat 5’s justice tension resolves with “**a love deeper than the law I knew**” and “**the Judge of all the earth does right**” without showing *how* vicarious bearing satisfies justice — doctrine elsewhere, but the steel-man gets a devotional soft landing.

---

### EW-INV-2 — CTA on Jesus

**Passes baseline:** Names **Jesus**; “**Come to Jesus**” names a response.

**Weak:**

- “**will you trust the report I was given to write?**” — clever 53:1 echo, but still a **trust-framed question**; “Come” does the real invitational work almost as a tag-on. Reads template-adjacent per the lens (even if it misses the deterministic banned list).
- “**Sit with that.**” — modern contemplative filler; no new turn.
- “**be healed by his stripes**” foregrounds outcome over Person; borderline benefit-forward for grace-anchored CTA.

---

### EW-G10 — Witness voice

Strong in Beats 1–5: “**I am Isaiah, the son of Amoz**,” trembling hand, pen laid down still searching.

**Breaks:**

- Beat 6–7: post-death knowledge of empty tomb, Philip, risen Servant, seed “**even now**” — an eyewitness who “**never saw the Servant’s face**” and “**carried those words to my grave without the comfort of his name**” cannot naturally narrate Acts 8/Easter as memory. Becomes **essayist-in-costume with future knowledge**.
- “**you who listen across the years**” — timeless podcast host, not Judah’s prophet.
- “**Sit with that.**” — breaks period voice entirely.

---

### First-hearing clarity

- “**arm of the LORD**” gets a workable gloss (“**roll back His sleeve**”).
- Sheep/Lamb distinction in Beat 5 helps.
- Ethiopia/Philip block assumes familiarity with eunuch, desert road, Philip — no one-line grounding for a listener with zero Bible knowledge.
- “**chastisement of our peace**” — opaque without a plain gloss.

---

### Hook, spine, long-form runtime (~1623 spoken words — in budget)

**Hook:** “**one vision… I never wholly understood**” + disbelief cry — grips.

**Spine:** One witness, one thread — holds.

**Sag:**

- **Beat 3** is a long scripture chain (“**Exalted, extolled, very high**” → despised → borne griefs → wounded) with thin interior turns between quotes; reads like dramatized public reading more than lived discovery. Substitution language repeats without new questions.
- **Beat 7** largely re-states Beat 3–4 (“**wounded for our transgressions**,” “**iniquity of us all**,” stripes) without a fresh beat — runtime not fully earned in the close.
- **Beat 2** world-building (empires, warrior-king expectation) is competent but generic; Beat 5 already carries the messianic-expectation tension more sharply.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Add explicit speaker attribution before every `**"..."**` block (re-tag `[the LORD]` / scroll / vision as appropriate); reframe Acts 8:35 fragment as Luke’s narration, not Philip’s spoken line.
2. Rebuild Beat 6 so Christ is shown from the vision’s own riddles (53:10–11 death/life, lamb, numbered with transgressors) inside Isaiah’s voice; treat empty tomb/Philip as Spirit-given confirmation, not post-death eyewitness recap — cut or reframe “far side of my days” omniscience.
3. Sharpen Beat 7: lead with a named response to Jesus (come/receive/turn), demote or replace “will you trust the report,” cut “Sit with that,” quote or gloss 53:10’s “offering for sin” so substitution’s penalty is fully named.
