# Independent adversarial review — *The Suffering Servant* (eyewitness-long)

## KJV verbatim + attribution (EW-INV-4)

**Punctuation altered on excerpted verses.** Several bold spans end with a full stop where KJV continues with a colon — presented as complete sentences but not exact KJV:

- **"He is despised and rejected of men; a man of sorrows, and acquainted with grief."** — KJV 53:3 has `grief:` not `grief.`
- **"Surely he hath borne our griefs, and carried our sorrows."** — KJV 53:4 has `sorrows:` not `sorrows.`
- **"But he was wounded for our transgressions, he was bruised for our iniquities."** — KJV 53:5 has `iniquities:` not `iniquities.`

The deterministic gate may normalize punctuation away; the **review lens asks for exact KJV inside `**"..."**`**. These fail that bar.

**Attribution is thin on most quotes.** Only two lines carry explicit speaker tags:

- `**[the LORD]** **"Behold, my servant shall deal prudently..."**`
- `**[the LORD]** **"by his knowledge shall my righteous servant justify many..."**`

Everything else is framed by Isaiah’s prose but not attributed before the bold span. Examples:

- Beat 1: *"And the very first words rose in me like a cry torn out of my chest:"* → **`"Who hath believed our report?..."`** — no speaker/source named immediately before the quote.
- Beat 3: *"I saw men cover their faces and turn from him in the street:"* → quote — the KJV voice is not identified.
- Beat 6: *"...beginning at my own words, he"* → **`"preached unto him Jesus."`** — this is Luke’s narrative (Acts 8:35), not Philip’s direct speech; attribution is vague and the fragment is mid-sentence.

For a multi-voice cast (`scripture` voice on every KJV line), this is a routing problem as well as a fidelity problem.

**No blocking word changes found** — fragments like **`"he was cut off out of the land of the living..."`** and **`"the chastisement of our peace was upon him; and with his stripes we are healed."`** are valid substrings of the binding passage.

## CTA-on-Jesus (EW-INV-2)

**Passes the core test.** Jesus is named; responses are named (`Come`, `believe`, `Trust`); substitution language is grace-anchored:

- *"You were never made to carry your own wandering home; there is no need. He has already borne it."*
- *"Come to Jesus, and believe the report I was given to write."*

**Mild pressure:** *"Do not wait any longer for a warrior on a horse"* edges toward manufactured urgency. Not fear/gain-loss, but the lens explicitly flags pressure — soften or reframe.

**Template-adjacent close:** *"Trust the One who was wounded for your transgressions"* is not the banned bare *"will you trust Him?"* but sits close to a formula after three beats of the same substitution vocabulary (`wounded`, `stripes`, `laid on him`). The CTA earns less because Beat 3/6/7 all say the same thing.

## Reveal earned (EW-G7)

**Partially earned, partially asserted.**

**Strong:** Beat 4’s death/life riddle pays off in Beat 6:

- *"Cut off out of the land of the living — yet prolonging his days."* → *"they came to his tomb and it was empty. He prolonged his days."*

Parallel lines in Beat 6 (`lamb to the slaughter`, `grave with the wicked... rich`, `numbered with the transgressors`) map Isa 53 to the passion with concrete detail.

**Acts 8 is the best earned hinge:** *"who read my words aloud and asked the one question I myself had asked — of whom speaketh the prophet this?"* → **`"preached unto him Jesus."`** That is text-driven identification.

**Weak / essay-narrator:**

- *"The whole vision was a shadow — and a shadow is cast by a body that is coming. The body came."* — theological summary, not discovery from what Isaiah lived.
- *"they came to his tomb and it was empty"* — crucifixion/resurrection detail **not in Isaiah’s vision or quoted KJV**; imported by reflective hindsight (allowed per EW-INV-11) but **not shown from the text the witness lived**.
- *"He sees his seed even now"* — asserted; **`"he shall see his seed, he shall prolong his days."`** was already quoted in Beat 4 without the Beat 6 payoff being walked through again.

**Missed earning from the binding passage:** `passage.txt` includes 52:14–15 (`visage marred`, `sprinkle many nations`) — never quoted or turned in the reveal. The piece runs Isa 53 twice instead of mining unused material.

## Wrestling — steel-man (EW-G8)

**The real objection is started in Beat 4, then abandoned.**

- *"I had taught the law of God for years: that each soul shall bear its own sin and die for its own guilt. But this was the opposite."*
- *"This was the innocent crushed for the guilty — and most terrible of all, it was the LORD Himself who willed it done"*

That is the steel-man: **How can the Judge transfer guilt to an innocent Servant?** Isaiah never sits in it. The next line resolves by assertion:

- *"The Judge of all the earth gathered up our guilt — yours and mine — and bound it onto His own Servant's back."*

**Beat 5 pivots to a softer question:** *"I did not know who he was"* — identity search (self? Hezekiah? Israel?), not *whether* this vision is just or true.

**Corporate-Servant reading is straw-manned:**

- *"Of Israel my own people? But we were the sheep gone astray, not the Lamb that bore us."*

The strongest historical objection is not “Israel is the lamb” but **Israel as the suffering servant-nation** (53:4’s *"we did esteem him stricken, smitten of God"* — also **omitted from the quoted text**). The piece dismisses Israel in one line without Isaiah voicing why a hearer might still read the chapter corporately.

**Beat 5 ends honestly** (*"I laid down my pen still searching"*) but the doubt is epistemic (who?), not moral (can God do this?). EW-G8 wants the **genuine strongest objection internalized** — here it is split across beats and resolved too fast.

## Doctrine (EW-G9)

**Broadly sound.** Substitution is named repeatedly and clearly:

- *"Wounded for ours. Bruised for ours."*
- *"the LORD hath laid on him the iniquity of us all"*
- *"he bare the sin of many"*
- CTA: *"let your sins be among the sins laid on him"*

No supersession. Isaiah is seer, not Servant — status note is correct.

**Friction points:**

1. *"With children of his own"* glosses **`"he shall see his seed"`** as biological offspring; evangelical reading is overwhelmingly **spiritual seed** (disciples/nations). Not heresy, but imprecise for a careful listener.
2. **`"each soul shall bear its own sin and die for its own guilt"`** is paraphrase of Ezek 18:20 territory, not KJV — fine in prose, but the piece treats it as settled law then jumps to substitution without acknowledging **Levitical sacrifice** as the within-canon precedent Isaiah would actually know. That weakens both doctrine and wrestling.
3. Omission of 53:4b (*"yet we did esteem him stricken, smitten of God, and afflicted"*) skips the false-accusation beat — that the Servant looked **punished for his own sin** when he bore **ours**. Important for substitution clarity.

## Voice / character (EW-G10)

**Strongest in Beats 1, 4, 5:**

- *"I read my own scroll like a stranger"*
- *"my hand trembled to set down"*
- *"a prophet who covers his own ignorance with fine words is no prophet at all"*

**Slips toward polished essay/sermon:**

- *"When a whole people waits for God to show His strength, I will tell you what they look for."* — second-person lecture, not a shaken seer.
- *"Hear this wonder, then:"* — pulpit cadence.
- *"The body came."* — three-word thesis statement; not Isaiah’s voice.
- *"You who hear me — understand the world I stood in"* / *"you who listen across all these years"* — direct address works, but stacked with the above it feels **performed** rather than **remembered**.

Cross-time device is properly signposted: *"I tell you now from the far side of my days, by the light that came long after my hand fell still"* — good EW-INV-11 discipline; no banned *"at last I understood."*

## First-hearing clarity

**Mostly followable**, but assumes church literacy in the reveal:

- **`"report"`** in 53:1 is never glossed — zero-Bible listener won’t know what “report” means.
- **`"arm of the LORD"`** is partially explained (*"roll back His sleeve"*) — adequate.
- **Philip / Ethiopia / desert road** — a listener who doesn’t know Acts 8 gets *that something happened later*, but not **why that proves identity** rather than one more preacher’s claim. Needs one plain sentence: a foreign reader of Isaiah’s scroll, centuries later, was told the prophet spoke of **Jesus crucified**.

**Hook grips:** *"I am Isaiah, the son of Amoz"* + un-named Servant + opening cry — strong.

**One witness, one thread** — holds.

**Runtime sags in the middle-back:**

| Beat | Issue |

| Beat 3 | Long quote chain; *"Wounded for ours. Bruised for ours."* repetition |

Beats 3→6→7 cover **wounded / stripes / transgressions / laid on him** four times. The dead-alive riddle (Beat 4) and Acts 8 hinge (Beat 6) earn length; the **re-quoted Isa 53 stack** does not.
