# Independent review — claude (OK, 103s)

I read the artifact closely and checked every bracketed quote against the KJV.

## KJV verbatim + attribution (EW-INV-4) — clean
I verified all 14 quotations. Isa 53:1 (Beat 1), 52:13 (Beat 3, correctly attributed **[the LORD]**), 53:2 / 53:3 / 53:4 / 53:5 (Beat 3), 53:6 / 53:8 / 53:10 (Beat 4), 53:7 / 53:9 / 53:12 (Beat 6), Acts 8:35 "**preached unto him Jesus.**" (Beat 6), 53:11 (Beat 7, attributed **[the LORD]**). Every one is exact. The "preached unto him Jesus" fragment is verbatim — the Acts 8:35 elision that bit a prior Isaiah pass is NOT repeated here. Good.

**Minor convention note:** the eunuch's line in Beat 6 — *"of whom speaketh the prophet this?"* — is verbatim Acts 8:34 but is woven into narration *unmarked* (no bold/quotes, no attribution to the eunuch). Using verbatim KJV outside quote-marks isn't a violation, but it's inconsistent with the rest of the piece, where every Scripture word is set off. Either mark+attribute it or leave it — pick one.

## CTA-on-Jesus (EW-INV-2) — clean
Beat 7 names Jesus, the response is NAMED (come / believe / trust / "be counted among the many he justifies / be healed by his stripes"), grace-anchored ("the same hand… is open toward you still"), and it explicitly disarms the warrior-king expectation rather than manufacturing pressure. No fear / gain-loss. It avoids the banned bare "will you trust Him?" Good.

## Doctrine (EW-G9) — sound
Substitution is named explicitly with both halves — the penalty (wounded, bruised, stricken, iniquity laid on him) and that it was *ours* He bore. No supersession: Beat 5 keeps Israel distinct from the Servant ("we were the sheep gone astray, not the Lamb that bore us") rather than replacing Israel. Death→resurrection is grounded in 53:8 + 53:10, then the empty tomb. Solid.

## Reveal (EW-G7) — earned, but leans on a device
The turn is shown, not just asserted: silence before accusers (53:7), criminal's death + rich man's tomb in one day (53:9), the empty tomb, and the scroll itself carried down the Gaza road into Philip's hands. That last move is the strongest earning beat. **Caveat:** the framing — dead Isaiah narrating the empty tomb and the eunuch from "the far side of my days" — does some of the work by *fiat* (post-mortem omniscience) rather than letting the lived text drive him to the name. The fulfillment details rescue it, but the "I tell you now… Jesus" announcement arrives slightly ahead of what the witness could have known in-life.

## Wrestling (EW-G8) — real, but THIN for the runtime
This is my main concern. Beat 5 is the shortest substantive beat yet carries the steel-man. The genuinely strongest objection to a messianic Isaiah 53 is the **corporate-Israel** reading (Israel is repeatedly called "my servant" — 41:8, 44:1). The piece raises it ("Of Israel my own people?") and rebuts it in a single clause. The rebuttal is the *correct* one (the Servant suffers *for* "my people," 53:8, so cannot *be* them) — but compressed to one line in a ~10-minute long-form, it reads more as a checklist (self / Hezekiah / Israel) than a lived grapple. Also note the doubt is *identity-search* ("I did not know who he was"), not doubt that the vision was *true* — appropriate for the format, but it's the lighter of the two possible objections.

## Voice / clarity / spine
- **Voice (EW-G10):** consistent first-person Isaiah, characterful — "man of unclean lips" (his ch.6 call), "set as a watchman," "I read my own scroll like a stranger." Not the essay-narrator in costume. Good.
- **First-hearing clarity:** "arm of the LORD made bare" is unpacked ("roll back His sleeve"); substitution is plain. The Philip/eunuch episode may land slightly cold for a zero-Bible listener (an unnamed "stranger from Ethiopia," a "man named Philip" appearing without setup), but it's followable.
- **Spine:** one witness, one thread, no swap. Holds.

## Sag check
Mostly earns its length. The "warrior on a horse" motif recurs in Beats 2, and 7 — purposeful bookending, not padding. The death-then-life paradox is touched in Beat 4 and Beat 5; there's mild overlap but each adds a distinct turn (Beat 4 = the paradox; Beat 5 = the unsolved identity). Tightest opportunity to cut/condense is the back half of Beat 4.

Nothing here is broken — KJV is exact, doctrine and CTA are clean, voice and spine hold. The flags are depth/polish, not defects.
