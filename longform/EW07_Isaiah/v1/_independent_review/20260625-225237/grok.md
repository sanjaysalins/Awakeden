## Independent adversarial review — Isaiah / Suffering Servant (eyewitness-long)

### EW-INV-4 — KJV verbatim + attribution (systemic problems)

**Attribution:** Only two quotes are tagged. Everything else drops KJV into Isaiah’s mouth with no speaker frame.

- `**"Who hath believed our report? and to whom is the arm of the LORD revealed?"**` (Beat 1) — no `[Isaiah]` / narrator attribution before the quote.
- Same pattern through Beat 3: `**"For he shall grow up before him as a tender plant..."**`, `**"He is despised and rejected of men..."**`, `**"Surely he hath borne our griefs..."**`, `**"But he was wounded for our transgressions..."**`, etc.
- Beat 6: `**"he is brought as a lamb to the slaughter..."**`, `**"And he made his grave with the wicked..."**`, `**"he was numbered with the transgressors..."**` — all unattributed.
- `**"preached unto him Jesus."**` — KJV-exact (Acts 8:35), but it is Luke’s narrative summary, not words Philip spoke. Putting it in quote marks treats narration as dialogue; needs a clear scripture/narrator frame, not a phantom Philip line.

**Punctuation drift on partial verses** (if gates require exact KJV substrings, these fail):

- `**"He is despised and rejected of men; a man of sorrows, and acquainted with grief."**` — KJV continues with a **colon**, not a period (`…grief: and we hid…`).
- `**"Surely he hath borne our griefs, and carried our sorrows."**` — KJV has a **colon** before `yet we did esteem him stricken…`
- `**"But he was wounded for our transgressions, he was bruised for our iniquities."**` — KJV has a **colon** after `iniquities`, not a period.

**Deliberate omission that weakens the text:** 53:4 continues `yet we did esteem him stricken, smitten of God, and afflicted` — the misread that God was punishing *him* for *his* sin. That is the passage’s own steel-man. The piece quotes 53:4 halfway, then never wrestles with it.

### EW-G7 — Reveal earned?

**Works when it stays on Isaiah’s lines:**

- Beat 4 plants `cut off out of the land of the living` vs `he shall prolong his days` — Beat 6 pays it off with `They came to his tomb and it was empty. He prolonged his days.` That is a real earned turn.
- Beat 6’s Isa 53 stack (`lamb to the slaughter`, `grave with the wicked`, `numbered with the transgressors`) maps details, not just a name-drop.

**Weak / asserted:**

- `The whole vision was a shadow — and a shadow is cast by a body that is coming` — theological summary. Not something Isaiah saw or reasoned to; essay voice.
- `The body came` — same. Announcement, not demonstration.
- Naming `Jesus` arrives before the textual case is closed: `whose name I was never given to write, but which I will say to you plainly now: Jesus` — then the proof quotes follow. Order is tell-then-show.
- Acts 8 (`a stranger from Ethiopia` … `preached unto him Jesus`) is used as *external proof* that Isaiah did not live. EW-INV-11 allows a signposted cross-time device (`from the far side of my days, by the light that came long after my hand fell still`), but the Ethiopia/Philip block still reads like omniscient commentary, not Isaiah re-reading his own scroll and recognizing the Servant.

### EW-G8 — Wrestling: real steel-man?

Beat 5 is competent but safe.

**Strongest objection is planted then abandoned:**

- Beat 4: `I had taught the law of God for years: that each soul shall bear its own sin and die for its own guilt. But this was the opposite.` — That is the scandal: the **Judge** laying guilt on an innocent Servant (`the LORD hath laid on him the iniquity of us all`).
- Beat 5 never sits in that. It only asks **who** (`Of Hezekiah our king?` … `Of Israel my own people?` … `Not one man of my generation fit the words`). Identity riddle, not justice riddle.
- Resolution in Beat 4 is assertion: `The Judge of all the earth gathered up our guilt — yours and mine — and bound it onto His own Servant's back.` Isaiah does not wrestle; he narrates the doctrine.

Also skipped: 53:4’s `we did esteem him stricken, smitten of God` — the witness’s own community misreading punishment as deserved. That would be lived, first-person steel.

### EW-G9 — Doctrine

**Solid:** Substitution is named repeatedly — `wounded for our transgressions`, `bruised for our iniquities`, `the LORD hath laid on him the iniquity of us all`, `he bare the sin of many`, CTA `let your sins be among the sins laid on him`. No supersession. `Israel my own people? But we were the sheep gone astray, not the Lamb that bore us` is careful.

**Fuzz:**

- `With children of his own` (Beat 4, paraphrasing `he shall see his seed`) — risks a biological misread of Jesus. Evangelical typology wants *seed* as spiritual offspring; this line nudges literal.
- `he shall see his seed, he shall prolong his days` is quoted without `He shall see of the travail of his soul, and shall be satisfied` (53:10–11). Satisfaction after suffering is central to the chapter; dropping it thins the substitution payoff.

### EW-G10 — Voice / character

**Strong:** Beats 1–5 mostly sound like Isaiah — `I read my own scroll like a stranger`, `my hand trembled to set down`, `I laid down my pen still searching`.

**Slips:**

- `Hear this wonder, then` — pulpit cadence, not a prophet’s raw voice.
- `the very scroll I wrote was carried down a desert road` — polished explainer; Acts 8 tourism.
- Posthumous omniscience without enough “I see it now by the Spirit’s light, not by my own eyes” guardrails on `They came to his tomb and it was empty` and Philip’s sermon.

---

### First-hearing clarity

- `the arm of the LORD` — well set up in Beat 2 (`roll back His sleeve`).
- Ethiopia / Philip / `of whom speaketh the prophet this?` — thin for a zero-Bible listener. Why does a random desert ride *prove* the Servant is Jesus? The piece assumes Acts 8 literacy.
- Warrior-on-a-horse expectation is clear and pays off in the CTA.

---

### Hook / spine

- Hook grips: `one vision the LORD laid on me that I never wholly understood` → immediate 53:1 cry.
- One witness, one thread (Servant → Christ). No swap.

---

### Long-form runtime — sag vs earn

- **Earns:** Beat 4 death/life paradox; Beat 5 identification search; Beat 6 partial payoff.
- **Sags:** Beat 3 repeats the same turn four times (`Ours. Not his own.` / `Wounded for ours. Bruised for ours.` / same substitution idea, no new beat). Beat 2 warrior-king setup overlaps Beat 1’s `arm of the LORD` expectation. Beat 5 is short relative to Beat 3’s quote marathon — wrestling should be the heaviest long-form beat; it isn’t.

### EW-INV-2 — CTA

Mostly grace-anchored: `Come to Jesus`, `believe`, `be counted among the many he justifies`, `be healed by his stripes` — response is named, not vague.

**Pressure-adjacent:** `Do not wait any longer for a warrior on a horse` — mild manufactured urgency against the spec’s ban on pressure phrasing.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Add explicit speaker attribution before every `**"..."**` quote; fix end-punctuation on partial verses to match KJV (colons, not invented periods); reframe `**"preached unto him Jesus."**` as scripture narration, not Philip dialogue.
2. Deepen Beat 5: make Isaiah *feel* the justice scandal he raised (`each soul shall bear its own sin` vs `the LORD hath laid on him the iniquity of us all`) — include and wrestle with 53:4’s `we did esteem him stricken, smitten of God` before the reveal resolves it.
3. Cut essay lines (`The whole vision was a shadow`, `The body came`, `Hear this wonder, then`); let Beat 6 earn Jesus from Isaiah’s own riddles (cut-off/prolong-days, silent lamb, grave with wicked) before naming Him, and scaffold Acts 8 for listeners who do not know Philip or the Ethiopian.
