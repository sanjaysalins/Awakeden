# Independent review — codex (OK, 29s)

Findings:

- **KJV quote punctuation is repeatedly altered.** If `**"..."**` must be exact KJV, several partial quotations insert periods where KJV has continuing punctuation: `**"He is despised and rejected of men; a man of sorrows, and acquainted with grief."**`, `**"Surely he hath borne our griefs, and carried our sorrows."**`, `**"But he was wounded for our transgressions, he was bruised for our iniquities."**`. These are excerpted mid-verse but presented as complete sentences.

- **Attribution is inconsistent.** `**[the LORD]** **"Behold, my servant shall deal prudently..."**` and `**[the LORD]** **"by his knowledge shall my righteous servant justify many..."**` are attributed, but the opening `**"Who hath believed our report?... "**` has no explicit speaker attribution before the quote. If the rule requires speaker attribution before quoting, this fails.

- **The strongest wrestling is started, but softened too quickly.** The best objection is here: `I had taught the law of God for years: that each soul shall bear its own sin and die for its own guilt. But this was the opposite.` That is promising, but it never really wrestles with the apparent moral scandal of substitution. It resolves by assertion: `The Judge of all the earth gathered up our guilt... and bound it onto His own Servant's back.` The piece needs Isaiah to feel the justice problem more sharply before accepting the mystery.

- **Reveal is mostly earned, but the phrase `The whole vision was a shadow` weakens it.** The reveal works best when it ties details to Jesus: `he answered them nothing`, `a criminal's death and a rich man's tomb`, `he was numbered with the transgressors`. But `The whole vision was a shadow — and a shadow is cast by a body that is coming` is theological summary language, not eyewitness discovery. It sounds like an essay narrator stepping in.

- **Voice slips toward sermon-essay.** Lines like `Hear this wonder, then`, `the very scroll I wrote was carried down a desert road`, and `The body came` feel polished and detached rather than Isaiah’s own astonished first-person witness. The voice is strongest in `I read my own scroll like a stranger` and weakest when it becomes explanatory bridgework.

- **First-hearing clarity is decent, but assumes too much in the reveal.** `a stranger from Ethiopia`, `a man named Philip`, and `preached unto him Jesus` are clear only if the listener already knows Acts 8. A zero-Bible-knowledge listener may not understand why that episode proves the identification rather than merely being another later claim.

- **Doctrine is broadly sound and substitution is named.** `Wounded for ours. Bruised for ours.` and `Trust the One who was wounded for your transgressions, and let your sins be among the sins laid on him` clearly name substitution. No supersessionist claim appears; `Israel my own people? But we were the sheep gone astray, not the Lamb that bore us` is careful enough.

- **CTA names Jesus and the response, but has one pressure-adjacent line.** `Come to Jesus`, `believe`, and `Trust` are named responses, and the close is grace-anchored. But `Do not wait any longer` risks manufactured urgency. It is mild, not fatal, but the instruction explicitly bans pressure; this should be softened.

- **Long-form runtime mostly earns itself, but Beat 2 repeats expected-warrior setup.** `They look for a warrior. They look for a king upon a horse... That is the salvation I expected too` is useful, but the beat lingers on a familiar contrast before the actual text arrives. The strongest material is Beats 3-6; Beat 2 could be tighter.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Fix every KJV quote to exact KJV form and add explicit speaker attribution before each quote.
2. Deepen the moral wrestling over innocent substitution before resolving it.
3. Replace essay-like reveal/CTA lines with Isaiah’s concrete first-person recognition of Jesus from the text.
