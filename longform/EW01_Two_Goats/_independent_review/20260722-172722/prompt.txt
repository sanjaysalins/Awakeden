# PROPOSAL: adopt a "depth-composition discipline" as the standard for our inked still prompts

## The change
Add four composition rules to every inked still prompt (the `subject_block` we write for
each scene), borrowed from the ArkAIology vox-blend SHOT formula — WITHOUT adopting their
paper-diorama/collage *look*:

1. **Name at least two explicit depth layers** (foreground / mid-ground / background) — their
   claim: "if depth isn't stated, the model flattens it."
2. **Shallow depth of field** — one plane sharp, others soft.
3. **A foreground element crossing close to the lens** (silhouettes, hands, an object).
4. **One committed camera angle** (low three-quarter, macro, crane) — not a flat centered shot.

Our inked style block (`VISUAL_STYLE_BASE_GN` / `_TAIL_GN`), model (seedream via HF), and the
frozen-tableau content stay identical. Only the spatial composition language changes.

## Evidence so far (small, possibly biased)
A/B on 2 scenes (same style block, same model, only the depth language added):
- Scene 1 (Aaron before the court): flat centered postcard → deep establishing panel (foreground
  crowd silhouettes, tiny priest, towering tent). Clear improvement.
- Scene 7 (the lots): flat medium → hero macro (lots huge in the palms, priest soft behind, goats deep).
Both stayed fully in our inked graphic-novel look. **BUT n=2, scenes chosen for obvious depth
potential, and one of the two grew an anachronistic cross finial (a 50% period-slip in this tiny sample).**

## Claimed advantages (please pressure-test)
1. Fixes flatness at the SOURCE — deep stills give the camera real layers to move through, and make
   our `parallax_25d` 2.5D tool actually work (a flat still has nothing to separate).
2. "Comic level" at zero marginal cost — it's composition, not a new art style or a new tool.
3. Repeatable + consistent — a prompt-system rule applied to every still in every episode.
4. Anti-slideshow variety across a long-form (varied deep compositions, not 20 similar flat tableaux).

## Risks / open questions I want attacked
- **DoF → photoreal drift:** "shallow depth of field" is a lens/3D concept; real comics build depth
  with linework/overlap, not blur. Does this quietly erode our INK purity toward "AI photo + comic filter"?
- **Reverent beats:** does an assertive foreground-macro/low-angle CHEAPEN the holy, still moments
  (Christ enthroned, the veil, the landing)? Should sacred scenes be exempt or use a calm variant?
- **Animation-safety regression:** more depth = more elements = more for the i2v model to invent/move —
  a foreground figure near the lens is prime for the model to animate. Does this undo the frozen-tableau
  discipline that exists to stop invented motion?
- **QC/defect rate:** more complex compositions may raise period-slip / extra-figure / anatomy defects
  and re-roll cost — is the "$0 going forward" claim honest?
- **Generalization:** does it hold on multi-figure and reverent scenes, or only easy "action" ones?

## The decision
Adopt this as the standard for all NEW inked still work? And separately, is it worth retrofitting
EW01's 25 existing stills (~$7.50 + re-animation)? Reject or bound the change if the risks outweigh it.
