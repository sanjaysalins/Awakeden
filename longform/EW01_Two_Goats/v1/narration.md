## Beat 1 — I was there
I am Aaron, the first high priest of Israel. Once a year — only once — I went where no other man on earth was permitted to go.

On that one morning I laid aside my garments of gold and glory. I washed my body with water, and I put on plain white linen, like a servant, like a man with nothing to boast of. And then I walked toward the veil with my heart in my throat. Behind that heavy curtain was the mercy seat, and above it, in the cloud, the presence of the living God. I went in alone. The whole nation stood outside, holding its breath — and every year, as the curtain fell shut behind me, I was not certain I would come out alive.

## Beat 2 — The world
You who hear me — I buried two of my own sons. Nadab and Abihu, my boys, died in that holy place, for bringing strange fire before the LORD that He had not commanded. I carried them out myself. So when the LORD gave His warning concerning me, I did not take it lightly; I heard it in my bones every time I reached for the veil. The LORD spoke it to my brother Moses, and Moses brought me the word:

**[the LORD]** **"Speak unto Aaron thy brother, that he come not at all times into the holy place within the vail before the mercy seat, which is upon the ark; that he die not: for I will appear in the cloud upon the mercy seat."**

That was my charge — to stand at the veil for the people and not be consumed. Understand me: I did not bear their guilt. I could not; I had to bring blood for my own sin before ever I brought it for theirs. I only carried the blood in, and came out alive. For the law the LORD gave was plain, and it ran red through everything I did:

**"it is the blood that maketh an atonement for the soul."**

Something innocent had to die, or none of us could draw near.

## Beat 3 — The act
Let me tell you what I did with my own hands, for I did it year after year and could have done it in the dark.

They brought me two goats for the people's sin offering, and as the law commanded I cast lots over them:

**"And Aaron shall cast lots upon the two goats; one lot for the LORD, and the other lot for the scapegoat."**

The first goat I killed. I took its blood in a basin and carried it where no ordinary man could follow — through the veil, into the thick dark, and I sprinkled it before the mercy seat:

**"Then shall he kill the goat of the sin offering, that is for the people, and bring his blood within the vail."**

But the second goat I did not kill. I laid both my hands upon its living head, and I confessed over it — out of my own mouth, sin by sin — all the wrong of all the people, and laid it upon the creature:

**"And Aaron shall lay both his hands upon the head of the live goat, and confess over him all the iniquities of the children of Israel, and all their transgressions in all their sins, putting them upon the head of the goat, and shall send him away by the hand of a fit man into the wilderness."**

And then I stood at the edge of the camp and watched it go, smaller and smaller, carrying it all away into a land where no one would ever find it:

**"And the goat shall bear upon him all their iniquities unto a land not inhabited."**

## Beat 4 — The strange detail
Here is the thing that never let me rest. It was one sin offering — yet it took two goats.

**"And he shall take of the congregation of the children of Israel two kids of the goats for a sin offering."**

Two goats, for a single offering. One I killed; the other I sent away alive. And I asked myself, year upon year: why two? Why not put both to death? One goat died to pay; the other lived to carry it off — as though God meant to show us two things at once that no single creature could hold. I performed it faithfully, and the riddle of it followed me home every time.

## Beat 5 — The wrestling
And I will be honest with you, for an honest man must be honest before God. I obeyed, and I believed. The people went home clean — for the LORD Himself had said that on that day we were cleansed from all our sins before Him. That cleansing was real.

And yet it never stayed finished. Every year I came back and did the whole thing again — the same blood, the same two goats, the same confession over the same people for the same sins. A cleansing you must make again every year is true, but it is not done; the stain came back with the new year, as faithful as the feast. And in my old age a quiet fear pressed on me, one I dared not speak aloud at the altar: that for all the blood I poured out, I was only ever pointing — pointing at some greater atonement that my own hands could never reach. I was a sign. I did not yet know of what.

## Beat 6 — The reveal
I did not see the answer in my own day. I tell you now from the far side of my life — by the light that came long after I laid down my office, and by the word that was written after me — what every Day of Atonement was always reaching toward.

That later word says plainly what I had only feared: that the blood I carried could never finish it.

**"there is a remembrance again made of sins every year."**

**"For it is not possible that the blood of bulls and of goats should take away sins."**

It was a shadow — and a shadow waits for the body that casts it. The body came. There came a Man, Jesus, and with His own blood He did what my basin of blood only pictured: He entered once, and it was finished.

**"but by his own blood he entered in once into the holy place, having obtained eternal redemption for us."**

He was the blood that paid the price — and He was the One upon whom the guilt was laid and carried clean away, exactly as I had laid the people's sin upon the head of the goat I sent into the wild:

**"and the LORD hath laid on him the iniquity of us all."**

That is why there were two. No single creature could show both halves at once — the price paid and the guilt carried off — and both halves were finished in one Priest. They led Him outside the city to die, and His body was taken without the gate, as the sin offering's body was always carried outside the camp and burned:

**"Wherefore Jesus also, that he might sanctify the people with his own blood, suffered without the gate."**

And He did the one thing I never could. I always stood at my work, for it was never done; there was no chair behind that veil. But this Priest finished it, and sat down:

**"But this man, after he had offered one sacrifice for sins for ever, sat down on the right hand of God."**

And the veil I trembled before — the curtain that shut every man but one out, one day a year — was torn from the top the very hour He died, by no human hand:

**"And, behold, the veil of the temple was rent in twain from the top to the bottom."**

## Beat 7 — The invitation
So hear me. Do not come to me. Do not come to a goat, or a yearly altar, or a priest in linen who is himself a dying man and must wash before he dares draw near. Come to Jesus — the High Priest who finished it.

If you will come to Him, your sin is not held down for another year — it is carried away, as far as the east is from the west, for so the Psalm had promised long before:

**"As far as the east is from the west, so far hath he removed our transgressions from us."**

The curtain I trembled before is torn. The way in is thrown wide open — and not for one man one day a year, but for you:

**"Having therefore, brethren, boldness to enter into the holiest by the blood of Jesus."**

You do not need a priest like me to go in for you any longer. Come to Him yourself, and trust the One who finished what I only ever began. Come — and be carried clean.
