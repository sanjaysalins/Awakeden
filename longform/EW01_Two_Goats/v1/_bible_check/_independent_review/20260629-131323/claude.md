# Independent review — claude (OK, 105s)

I reviewed this fact sheet against the KJV text of Leviticus 16 and the cited Exodus passages. It is unusually disciplined — buckets are mostly honest, historical notes are clearly flagged secondary, and the Christ-lens (slain goat = price paid / scapegoat = sin carried away, Aaron as mediator-not-substitute) is intact and explicitly guarded. But there is one real citation-truth defect in a SPECIFIED fact, repeated, plus a couple of precision issues and a coverage gap.

## Findings

**1. CITATION OVER-REACH (most important) — "he-goats … mature, not baby kids" is not in the cited KJV and partly contradicts it.**
- World fact 4 ([SPECIFIED] "Two he-goats … Visual: … mature he-goats, not baby kids") and Scene 7 ([SPECIFIED] "Two he-goats stood together") both cite only **Lev 16:5 / 16:7**.
- The quoted KJV says **"two kids of the goats"** (16:5) and **"the two goats"** (16:7). Neither verse states the animals are **male** ("he-goats") or **mature**. Maleness comes from the Hebrew *sa'ir*, not the shown English text. Worse, the visual instruction "**not baby kids**" actively reads against the KJV word **"kids."**
- This is licence/Hebrew-derived detail sitting in the **SPECIFIED** bucket attributed to a verse that doesn't say it, and it drives a binding "show mature / not kids" rule. Fix: either move the male/mature qualifier to a secondary (Hebrew) note, or soften the visual so it doesn't contradict "kids." "Two goats together" is genuinely SPECIFIED; "mature he-goats" is not.

**2. PRECISION — "seven times" attached to the wrong action (Scene 8, SPECIFIED).**
- Scene 8: "sprinkled with the finger **upon and before** the mercy seat, **seven times**." Cites Lev 16:14/16:15.
- KJV 16:14 actually assigns the seven times only to the **before**-the-mercy-seat sprinkling ("before the mercy seat shall he sprinkle of the blood with his finger seven times"); the "upon the mercy seat eastward" sprinkle is not numbered. The sheet conflates the two. Immaterial to a painting (you can't depict a count), but it's an over-statement of what the verse says — tighten the wording.

**3. MINOR — "two blood-entries" (World fact 7).** Worded carefully and defensible (bullock blood 16:14 + goat blood 16:15), but note the chapter also has the prior incense entry (16:12–13); "two blood-entries" is fine as written, just don't let it imply only two entries total. No change required.

**COVERAGE GAP (suggestion, not a defect):** Scene 9 (scapegoat) has no guard against the common **traditional scarlet thread/cloth on the scapegoat's head** (Mishnah Yoma, not in Scripture). Since artists routinely add it and it would read as biblical, a "must NOT show a scarlet thread/marker on the scapegoat as if Scripture-stated" guard would close an obvious, checkable hole — consistent with how the sheet already excludes other Mishnaic staging (e.g. multiple gold/linen changes).

**Things that are correctly done (verified, not flags):** linen-garments SPECIFIED (16:4/16:23) ✓; gold-before-rite honestly bucketed FREE with Yoma flagged ✓; veil colours + cherubim SPECIFIED (Ex 26:31) ✓; ark stays behind veil ✓; both-hands-on-live-goat SPECIFIED (16:21) with the "not one hand" guard ✓; scapegoat sent *after* reconciling (16:20) ✓; mercy-seat cherubim facing each other, wings up (Ex 25:18/20) ✓; "no man in the tabernacle" correctly scoped to the tent not the court (16:17) ✓; breastplate/ephod/gold plate (not a European crown) ✓.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Demote "mature he-goats, not baby kids" out of the SPECIFIED bucket in World fact 4 and Scene 7 — the cited KJV ("two kids of the goats") states neither maleness nor maturity, and "not baby kids" contradicts the word "kids"; keep only "two goats together" as specified, move the rest to a secondary Hebrew note.
2. Fix the Scene 8 "seven times" claim — per Lev 16:14 the seven-times applies to the *before*-the-mercy-seat sprinkling, not the "upon" sprinkle; reword so it doesn't over-state the verse.
3. Add a Scene 9 "must NOT show" guard against the traditional scarlet thread on the scapegoat (Mishnaic, not biblical), closing an obvious detail a painting could get wrong with no fact guarding it.
