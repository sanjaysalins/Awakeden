# Independent review — codex (OK, 38s)

Findings:

- **World fact: “[CONSTRAINED] The sanctuary was the portable Tabernacle — a tent of curtains and skins…” cites Exodus 26:1 and Exodus 40:2.** The quoted verses support curtains and “tent of the congregation,” but **do not mention skins**. If skins are required visually, cite Exodus 26:14. As written, “skins” is an uncited specified visual detail.

- **World fact / Scene 2 / Scene 3 / Scene 7 / Scene 9: repeated claim “plain white linen” or “simple undyed white linen.”** Leviticus 16:4 says “holy linen” coat, breeches, girdle, and mitre. The quoted KJV **does not say white, plain, or undyed**. “Not gold vestments” is supported by the linen requirement, but “white/undyed” should not be treated as specified from this citation.

- **Scene 1: “[CONSTRAINED] Gold vestments belong to the ordinary approach OUTSIDE the veil… Gold vestments are acceptable here… before the linen change.”** Leviticus 16:4 does **not** state that Aaron wore gold vestments earlier that morning or in the outer court before changing. That is a plausible reconstruction from normal priestly garments, but this citation only specifies linen for the service. This should not license gold vestments as biblically constrained unless another passage or a non-biblical production note supports it.

- **Scene 2 depiction: “garments laid upon a worn stone bench.”** This is visually dangerous against the world rule forbidding stone temple/building cues. No cited text supports a stone bench in the Tabernacle setting. It should be marked free at most, and probably avoided because it can imply later temple architecture.

- **Scene 3 depiction: “a dim colonnaded passage” with “hanging oil lamps.”** The fact correctly says “Must NOT show… stone colonnade / masonry hall,” but the scene description itself says “colonnaded passage.” That is internally contradictory and risks violating the Tabernacle setting. Also, “hanging oil lamps” is not supported by the cited veil texts; Exodus has the candlestick/lampstand, not hanging lamps.

- **Scene 7 depiction: “before the bronze altar.”** Leviticus 16:7 says the goats are presented “before the LORD at the door of the tabernacle of the congregation,” not specifically before the bronze altar. The altar may be nearby in the court, but this is not a specified biblical fact from the cited verses.

- **Scene 7: “Aaron alone, no other priests” / fact sheet does not guard the congregation’s role.** Leviticus 16:5 says the goats are taken “of the congregation of the children of Israel,” and 16:7 says Aaron presents them. The text does not say Aaron was alone in the court for the lot-casting. Leviticus 16:17 only excludes others from the tabernacle while he makes atonement inside, not necessarily from the outer court during the goat presentation.

- **Scene 9: “a single robed attendant” is treated under a specified fact.** Leviticus 16:21 says “a fit man,” but not that he is robed, waiting quietly, singularly visible at the edge, or already positioned there during the confession. “An attendant present” is reasonable, but “robed” is artistic licence.

- **Missing coverage: the goat for the LORD is killed as a sin offering, while the scapegoat remains alive.** Leviticus 16:9-10 and 16:15 are central to the two-goat distinction. The sheet guards “don’t show a goat already slain at lot-casting,” but it does not specify the post-lot distinction: one goat is for the LORD and killed; the other is presented alive for the scapegoat.

- **Missing coverage: the live goat bears the sins to “a land not inhabited.”** Scene 9 includes wilderness, but the fact could more explicitly guard against showing the goat merely released inside or beside the camp. Leviticus 16:22 specifies “unto a land not inhabited” and “in the wilderness.”

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Remove or downgrade “white/undyed/plain” as specified unless properly supported; Leviticus 16:4 only says linen.
2. Fix Scene 1’s gold-vestment claim; Leviticus 16:4 does not state Aaron wore gold before changing.
3. Remove Tabernacle-inconsistent visuals like the stone bench, colonnaded passage, and hanging lamps, or clearly mark them as free and non-contradictory.
