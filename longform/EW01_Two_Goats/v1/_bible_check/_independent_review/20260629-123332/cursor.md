## Independent adversarial review — EW01_Two_Goats biblical fact sheet

### CITATION TRUTH — systematic over-reach on linen color

**“Plain white linen” / “undyed white linen” / “simple undyed white”** appears repeatedly as **SPECIFIED** (world fact visual line 18; Scene 2 line 59; Scene 3 lines 65–68; Scene 7 line 99; Scene 9 lines 118–119).

**Leviticus 16:4** names **“holy linen”** garments (coat, breeches, girdle, mitre). It does **not** say white, undyed, plain, or uncolored. Treating whiteness as **specified** is invention dressed as Scripture. If artists must avoid blue/purple/scarlet on atonement linen, cite a verse that actually forbids it — 16:4 does not.

Same pattern on the negative: **“not the gold vestments”** (world fact line 15) is hung only on **16:4**, but **16:4** only states what to put on, not what to take off. The gold-vs-linen contrast is inferential (Exodus 28 + **Leviticus 16:23–24**). Reasonable as **constrained**, not **specified** on that citation alone.

---

### CITATION TRUTH — “gold garments” (Scene 2)

**Scene 2 [SPECIFIED]:** “The high priest's **gold garments** included an embroidered ephod and a breastplate” (line 50), citing **Exodus 28:4**.

**Exodus 28:4** lists garment types (breastplate, ephod, robe, broidered coat, mitre, girdle) and does **not** call them “gold.” Gold on the breastplate is explicit in **28:15** (cited). “Gold garments” as a blanket label over-reaches **28:4**. “Embroidered ephod” also over-reaches: **28:4** puts **“broidered coat”** as its own item, not the ephod.

---

### BUCKET ERROR — Leviticus 16:17 stretched beyond the verse

**World fact [SPECIFIED]** (lines 26–29) and **Scene 3 [SPECIFIED]** (lines 76–79): “no other man was in the tabernacle” / “Aaron alone.”

**Leviticus 16:17** applies when Aaron **“goeth in to make an atonement in the holy place”** — i.e. the inner atonement entry/work, until he has finished atonement for himself, household, and congregation. It does **not** forbid other people in the **court**, and it does **not** require Aaron alone at every moment of the day.

**Scene 7** depiction and facts (line 83: “Aaron alone, no other priests”) apply **16:17** to lot-casting at the altar/court. That is a **misapplication**: goats are presented **“at the door of the tabernacle”** (**16:7**); **16:17** does not govern that moment. Other priests could be in the court; the text only empties the **tabernacle interior** for the holy-place atonement phase.

**Scene 3** at the veil threshold is closer to the verse, but still slightly ahead of “goeth in”; scope should be tightened to **inner sanctum / after entry**, not every veil-adjacent frame.

---

### CITATION TRUTH — Scene 1 gold vestments on the Day of Atonement

**Scene 1** depicts Aaron in **golden vestments** before the tabernacle (line 33). **Scene 1 [CONSTRAINED]** (lines 35–38) says gold is acceptable in the outer court **“before the linen change.”**

**Leviticus 16:4** is the Day-of-Atonement entry instruction: **wash**, then put on **linen** — it does not describe a gold phase that morning. Gold ordinary vestments (Exodus 28) are plausible **before** the linen change in narrative order (Scene 2), but **16:4** does not authorize Scene 1’s gold-on-Atonement-morning image. The constrained fact cites **16:4** for gold acceptability; that citation does not support it.

### CONTRADICTION RISK — veil color vs “plain white” veil

**Scene 3 [CONSTRAINED]** (lines 70–75) correctly cites **Exodus 26:31**: the veil is **blue, purple, scarlet, and fine twined linen** with cherubim.

The scene depiction (line 63) says only **“a great heavy veil.”** No **specified** or **constrained** fact requires the veil to show those colors. Meanwhile linen is repeatedly locked as **white**. Artists can easily render a plain white veil and satisfy the linen facts while **violating 26:31**. Missing positive guard: veil must **not** be plain white undyed linen.

---

### CONTRADICTION RISK — Scene 3 “colonnaded passage” vs tent architecture

Scene depiction (line 63): **“dim colonnaded passage.”** **Scene 3 [CONSTRAINED]** (line 75) forbids **“stone colonnade / masonry hall.”** Good guard, but the **Depicts** line still invites stone-temple architecture the world facts reject. Facts should either drop colonnade from the canonical depiction or add an explicit **must NOT** for colonnades/passages implying permanent masonry.

**Scene 2** (line 48): **“worn stone bench”** — same tension with tabernacle-as-tent world facts (lines 9–14). No fact flags a **stone bench** inside a fabric sanctuary.

---

### NO INVENTED FACTS — lot-stones and bronze vessel (Scene 7)

**Scene 7 [SPECIFIED]** visual (line 94): **“lot-stones over a bronze vessel.”** **Leviticus 16:8** says Aaron **“cast lots”** — not stones, not a vessel, not their material. Lot mechanics are **free** artistic licence, not **specified**. Putting them in the **specified** visual line overstates what Scripture states.

**Scene 7** depiction: **“bronze altar.”** Burnt-offering altar overlay is brass/bronze (**Exodus 27:2**), but the fact sheet never cites or guards it. Not necessarily wrong, but **16:7** places goats at the **door of the tabernacle**, not explicitly at the altar — location is slightly loose.

---

### NO INVENTED FACTS — Scene 9 attendant

**Scene 9 [SPECIFIED]** (lines 110–114): **“An attendant present.”** **16:21–22** require a **“fit man”** to **send the goat away** after confession. The text does not require him to be **visible during** hand-laying/confession, nor **robed**. “Single robed attendant waits quietly at the edge” mixes a composite tableau with **specified** facts. Safer: **constrained** — fit man for the sending; **free** — robe, position, simultaneity with confession.

### COVERAGE — obvious missing guards (paintings will get these wrong)

1. **Once a year** — Scene 1 title and narration hinge on this; no fact cites **Leviticus 16:34** (“once a year”) or **Hebrews 9:7** (“once every year”). Easy to depict generic priestly routine instead of the annual rite.

2. **Wash before linen** — **16:4**: “wash his flesh in water, and so put them on.” Scene 2 shows linen beside gold but no **specified/constrained** wash step.

3. **Aaron’s bullock** — **16:3, 6, 11**: young bullock for Aaron’s own sin offering before/at the start of the rite. Missing entirely; artists may show only goats.

4. **Ram for burnt offering** — **16:5**: “one ram for a burnt offering” alongside the goats. Scene 7 **must NOT** only forbids substituting sheep for goats, not showing the ram that **16:5** explicitly requires.

5. **The killed goat + blood** — Core of the typology and narration (Beat 3): **16:9, 15–16** — LORD’s goat killed; blood brought within the veil and sprinkled on the mercy seat. **No scene facts** guard this half of “price paid + sin carried away.” Scene 9 covers only the live goat.

6. **Scapegoat presented alive before the LORD** — **16:10** before the wilderness sending. Not guarded.

7. **Bodies burned outside the camp** — **Leviticus 16:27** (slain sin offering) vs scapegoat sent alive — narration explicitly distinguishes these; fact sheet does not. Christ-lens risk: conflating crucifixion “outside the gate” (slain offering) with scapegoat wilderness.

8. **Mercy seat, ark, cloud** — **16:2, 12–14** for inner scenes (Scene 4 in plan, not covered here). Veil/inner work without blood/cloud guards leaves the holiest-place typology unprotected.

9. **Veil colors** (above) — **26:31**.

10. **“Kids of the goats”** — **16:5** says **“two kids of the goats”** (young goats). Facts only say “two goats” — minor, but artists may show mature billies.

---

### CHRIST-LENS

The sheet protects the **scapegoat** half (live goat, both hands, wilderness, not killed) reasonably well. It **under-guards the blood half** (slain goat, mercy seat, outside-camp burning of the **body**). For an episode whose type→fulfillment is **“price paid + sin carried away — both halves finished in one Priest, Christ,”** that omission is doctrinally significant: paintings can look orthodox on Leviticus 16 while **silently dropping the substitutionary death/blood** half the Christ connection depends on.

No fact **distorts** typology, but **incomplete** guarding on the slain-goat/blood thread is a Christ-lens failure by omission.

---

### WHAT IS SOLID

- Tabernacle as tent, not stone temple (**Exodus 26:1, 40:2, 27:9**) — sound **constrained**.
- Two goats together for the sin offering (**16:5, 7**) — sound **specified**.
- Cast lots: one for LORD, one for scapegoat (**16:8**) — sound **specified** (minus lot-stone detail).
- Both hands on live goat; confess; send to wilderness (**16:21–22**) — sound **specified** for the hand/confession/living-goat elements.
- Veil as hanging curtain, not door (**26:31, 33**) — sound **constrained**.
- Linen garment **types** for atonement service (**16:4**) — sound **specified** if color language is stripped.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Demote all **“white / undyed / plain”** linen color claims from **specified** to **free** (or drop color entirely); cite only **“holy linen”** garment types from **16:4**; add **specified** veil colors from **Exodus 26:31** so artists cannot paint a white veil.
2. Tighten **Leviticus 16:17** to **inner holy-place atonement only**; remove “Aaron alone, no other priests” from Scene 7 court/lot-casting; do not use **16:17** for altar/court scenes.
3. Add **specified** facts for the **slain goat, blood within the veil, mercy seat**, and **outside-the-camp burning of the slain offering’s body** (**16:9, 15–16, 27**) plus **once a year** (**16:34**) and **wash before linen** (**16:4**) — the missing half of the two-goats Christ typology and the Scene 1 annual frame.
