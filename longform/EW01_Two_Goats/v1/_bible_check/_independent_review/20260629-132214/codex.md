# Independent review — codex (OK, 34s)

Findings:

1. **Overreach: “Two goats were taken together for the people's single sin offering”**  
   Citation: Leviticus 16:5, 16:7.  
   The verse says “two kids of the goats for a sin offering,” but it does **not** say “single sin offering.” That may be a theological/systematic reading, but the cited wording supports “two goats for a sin offering,” not the tighter “single” formulation. Use the biblical wording.

2. **Wrong bucket / overclaim: “the high priest may not come into the holy place within the veil at other times” as [SPECIFIED] “once-a-year Day of Atonement rite”**  
   Citation: Leviticus 16:2, 16:34.  
   Leviticus 16:2 says Aaron must not come “at all times”; 16:34 says atonement is made once a year. Together they support the annual rite, but “may not come…at other times” is broader than the cited text unless qualified as ordinary/unauthorized entry. “Not at all times” is not identical to “never under any other divinely commanded circumstance.”

3. **Misleading specified claim: “Its blood was…sprinkled with the finger upon the mercy seat, and before the mercy seat seven times”**  
   Citation: Leviticus 16:15, 16:14.  
   Leviticus 16:15 says the goat blood is treated “as he did with the blood of the bullock,” and 16:14 says the bullock blood is sprinkled “upon the mercy seat eastward” and “before the mercy seat…seven times.” This is probably right, but the fact should make clear the “seven times” is imported through “as he did,” not stated directly of the goat in 16:15.

4. **Unsupported visual detail: “Aaron’s weathered hands” / “two weathered hands”**  
   Scenes 2 and 7.  
   No cited Scripture states Aaron’s hands were weathered. This is fine as artistic licence, but the sheet should not let it sit inside scene descriptions without flagging it as free. The fact sheet is supposed to guard biblical facts; this is invented visual characterization.

5. **Unsupported visual detail: “worn wooden bench”**  
   Scene 2.  
   The bench/tableau is correctly labeled [FREE], but “wooden” and “worn” are still invented. The note says “keep furnishings portable/wood,” but Leviticus 16:4 does not support a bench at all, and “wood” here is staging, not biblical fact.

6. **Possible overrestriction: “Must NOT show: a scarlet thread or cloth on the scapegoat's head presented as biblical”**  
   The warning is good, but the “Must NOT show” wording is too broad unless it keeps the qualifier “presented as biblical.” Scripture does not forbid a marker; it simply does not specify one. The correct rule is: do not put it in [SPECIFIED], and do not make it necessary. A production may still use tradition if clearly treated as non-biblical licence.

7. **Citation gap: “The sanctuary was the portable Tabernacle”**  
   Citation: Exodus 26:1, 26:14, 40:2.  
   These prove tent/curtains/coverings and setup, but not “portable” directly. Portability is true from broader Torah context, but the quoted verses do not establish it. Either cite transport passages or remove “portable” from the fact claim.

8. **Bucket concern: “The mercy seat was a gold lid”**  
   Citation: Exodus 25:18, 25:20.  
   The cited verses prove the cherubim are gold and placed at the ends of the mercy seat, but the exact “gold lid” wording is not fully established by the quoted verses alone. Exodus 25:17 would be the cleaner citation for “mercy seat of pure gold.” Add it.

9. **Coverage gap: the bronze altar / altar of burnt offering is under-guarded**  
   Several scenes occur in the court and around sacrifices, but there is no explicit guard that the outdoor altar is the altar of burnt offering, not the ark or mercy seat. The sheet says the ark must not be used as a fire altar, but it should also positively guard the court altar if sacrifice staging is shown.

10. **Coverage gap: the “fit man” later washes after releasing the goat**  
   Leviticus 16:26 says the man who lets go the scapegoat washes his clothes and bathes before returning to camp. If the image set includes aftermath or return, this is a specified checkable detail currently unguarded.

11. **Coverage gap: Aaron changes clothes and washes again after the live-goat phase**  
   Leviticus 16:23-24 says Aaron puts off the linen garments, washes, puts on his garments, and offers burnt offerings. The world fact mentions removal, but not the second washing / change / burnt offerings. If later scenes imply the end of the rite, this matters.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Tighten overreaching specified claims so they say only what the cited KJV text actually states, especially “single sin offering” and “may not come…at other times.”
2. Add missing citations where the fact is true but not supported by the quoted verses, especially “portable” and “gold lid.”
3. Separate invented staging from biblical facts more aggressively, especially weathered hands, bench/tableau details, and traditional scapegoat markers.
