# Independent review — codex (OK, 34s)

**Findings**

1. **Overreach: “no gold is worn for … scapegoat acts” is not directly stated.**  
   World fact: “the inner Day-of-Atonement actions are done in linen, so no gold is worn for the inner blood/incense/scapegoat acts…” cites Ex 28:2, Ex 28:4, Lev 16:4. Lev 16:4 supports linen garments for the rite, but the sheet’s phrasing mixes “inner” actions with the scapegoat action, which is not inner/within the veil. The conclusion may be reasonable from Lev 16 sequence, but the cited verses do not explicitly say “no gold” or specify every costume transition for the live-goat moment. Keep it constrained, but tighten the claim.

2. **Unsupported visual addition: “two blood-entries.”**  
   World fact: “the bullock’s blood and the goat’s blood are each brought in on that one day - two blood-entries” cites Lev 16:2 and 16:34. Those verses support “not at all times” and “once a year,” but not the number of blood entries. Lev 16:14-15 supports bullock and goat blood actions; cite those or remove the “two blood-entries” detail from this fact.

3. **Likely wrong bucket: mercy-seat details are explicit, not merely constrained.**  
   World fact: “[CONSTRAINED] The mercy seat was a gold lid bearing two cherubim…” cites Ex 25:18 and Ex 25:20, which explicitly state two gold cherubim, beaten work, wings on high, faces toward one another/toward the mercy seat. This should be **[SPECIFIED]**, at least when the mercy seat is visible.

4. **“Gold vestments … at the veil” may be too broad.**  
   Scene 3 fact: “Must NOT show: gold vestments at the veil; bare head” cites Lev 16:4. If the scene is specifically Aaron at the threshold for the atonement rite, linen is supported. But “at the veil” by itself is broader than the verse; Exodus 28 priestly garments could exist elsewhere in ordinary ministry. Make the prohibition “gold vestments during this Day-of-Atonement entry/atonement action,” not a universal veil-location rule.

5. **Citation does not support “bronze vessel/basin.”**  
   Scene 7 depicts “two small marked lots over a bronze vessel,” then the fact says the form of lots, “stones, a vessel, the bronze basin,” is licence. Lev 16:8 only says Aaron cast lots. The artifact correctly marks it free, but should not call it “the bronze basin” unless it is clearly production shorthand. “Bronze basin” could be confused with the laver from Exodus; no cited text links lots to a bronze vessel.

6. **Scene 8 may under-guard a specified sequence: incense before blood.**  
   Lev 16:12-13 places the censer/incense cloud before the blood sprinkling of Lev 16:14-15. The fact sheet says the inner approach is veiled by incense, but does not explicitly guard the order. A painting showing blood brought in before incense would contradict the cited sequence. Add a constrained/spec detail: incense cloud is established before the blood sprinkling.

7. **Missing specified fact: the live goat is presented alive before the LORD before confession/sending.**  
   Lev 16:10 states the scapegoat is “presented alive before the LORD, to make an atonement with him, and to let him go for a scapegoat into the wilderness.” Scene 9 covers “live goat” from Lev 16:21-22, but the sheet never cites Lev 16:10. This would guard against depicting the live goat as merely an ordinary animal waiting at the camp edge rather than part of the presented rite.

8. **Missing specified fact: the bullock and goat blood are applied to the mercy seat and before it, with eastward/sevenfold detail for the bullock.**  
   Scene 8 cites Lev 16:14-15 and says goat blood was sprinkled “upon the mercy seat, and before the mercy seat seven times (as he did with the bullock’s blood).” The seven times is explicit in Lev 16:14 for the bullock; Lev 16:15 says goat blood is treated “as he did with the blood of the bullock,” so the inference is defensible. Still, the “eastward” detail from Lev 16:14 is omitted from the visual constraints. If orientation is visible, it is a checkable specified detail.

9. **Missing coverage: the altar reconciliation after the inner blood work.**  
   Scene 9 correctly cites Lev 16:20, but the sheet never guards the intervening altar blood rite in Lev 16:18-19. Since Scene 9 depends on “after he hath made an end of reconciling … the altar,” the missing altar step could let the sequence visually jump from mercy seat to scapegoat as if nothing happens between.

10. **Christ-lens note is mostly intact, but one phrase is too interpretive for a fact sheet.**  
   World fact historical note: “this slain half is the ‘price paid’ alongside the scapegoat’s ‘sin carried away’ - the substitution the Christ-type rests on.” This is theological interpretation, not a visual biblical fact. It is labelled secondary, which helps, but “price paid” is not stated in Lev 16 and risks sounding like the cited passage itself says that. Keep the guard “do not omit the slain goat/blood,” but soften or separate the doctrinal gloss.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Tighten overreaching claims where the cited verses do not explicitly support the wording, especially “two blood-entries,” “no gold” for all scapegoat acts, and theological “price paid” language.
2. Rebucket explicit mercy-seat/cherubim details as specified, and add missing sequence guards for incense before blood and the altar reconciliation before the scapegoat.
3. Add Lev 16:10 and Lev 16:18-19 coverage so the live goat and altar-reconciliation steps are biblically guarded, not skipped.
