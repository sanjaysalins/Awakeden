# Independent review — codex (OK, 170s)

**Findings**

- **Scene 2 overstates the “laid aside gold” moment as biblical.** The line “*a golden breastplate, ephod and priestly garments laid upon a worn wooden bench... hands releasing the gold; plain folded linen waiting beside it*” is not stated in Leviticus 16:4. The verse says Aaron puts on linen after washing; it does not say he first removed gold garments, placed them on a bench, or had folded linen beside them. This staging should be marked free/traditional, not allowed to ride under **[SPECIFIED]** facts.

- **The cited texts do not support “gold-set ephod” in the world fact.** The fact says “*the gold-set ephod, breastplate, robe, etc. are described in Exodus 28*,” but the quoted Exodus 28:2 and 28:4 only say “glory and beauty” and list garments. They do not state gold in the ephod. Add the actual ephod gold citations, e.g. Exodus 28:6-8, or remove “gold-set” from the supported claim.

- **Scene 2’s “breastplate set with gold” is under-cited.** Exodus 28:15 says the breastplate is made “of gold...” and Exodus 28:21 gives twelve stones, but the quoted text does not prove the stones are “set with gold.” The fact needs Exodus 28:17-20 or similar support.

- **“Any gold belongs only outside the rite” is too broad.** The world fact says “*the Day-of-Atonement rite itself is performed in linen, so any gold belongs only outside the rite*.” But Leviticus 16:23-24 has Aaron remove the linen, wash, put on “his garments,” and offer burnt offerings on the same Day of Atonement sequence. The real constraint is: no gold for the inner blood/incense/scapegoat actions, especially not within the veil.

- **Scene 1 gold vestments before the rite remain traditional staging, not biblical fact.** The line “*Aaron’s gold vestments here in the open court (before the rite) are his regular Exodus 28 garments*” is not stated by Exodus 28:2 or Leviticus 16:4. The artifact admits this in the historical note, but the fact heading still sounds stronger than the citation allows.

- **“Scene 3 — Plain white linen, like a servant” conflicts with the sheet’s own caution.** The world fact correctly says “*The COLOUR of the linen is not stated by Scripture*.” The scene title nevertheless asserts “white,” and “like a servant” is devotional interpretation, not a Leviticus 16 fact.

- **The “Historical” note on the slain goat is not historical.** The line “*Theologically this slain half is the ‘price paid’... the substitution the Christ-type rests on*” is theology, not history, and Leviticus 16:9, 15-16 do not say “price paid.” Keep the guard against omitting blood atonement, but do not present that wording as a cited biblical/historical fact.

- **Scene 8’s “shallow bronze basin” is invented unless marked free.** Leviticus 16:15 says Aaron brings the goat’s blood within the veil and sprinkles it; it does not specify a basin, shallowness, or bronze material.

- **Scene 9’s “camp gate” is invented and potentially misleading.** Leviticus 16:21-22 says the goat is sent “into the wilderness” and “unto a land not inhabited.” It does not describe a camp gate. A gate could visually imply a walled city or settled architecture foreign to the wilderness tabernacle setting.

- **Coverage gap: the rams are quoted but not guarded.** Leviticus 16:5 mentions “*one ram for a burnt offering*,” and Leviticus 16:3 also has Aaron’s ram. If any wider court scene shows sacrificial animals, the sheet should guard against omitting or substituting those rams.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Rebucket the gold-removal still-life, bench, folded linen, white linen, basin, and camp gate as artistic licence/traditional staging, not specified biblical fact.
2. Fix incomplete/misapplied citations for gold garments, especially “gold-set ephod” and “breastplate set with gold.”
3. Tighten the gold/linen rule to “no gold in the inner atonement actions / within the veil,” not “gold only outside the rite.”
