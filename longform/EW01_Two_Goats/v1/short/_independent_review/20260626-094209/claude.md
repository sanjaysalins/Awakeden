# Independent review — claude (OK, 85s)

I read the narration closely against the eyewitness lens. It's strong — but here are the concrete findings, skeptic-first.

## KJV verbatim + attribution (EW-INV-4)
All four quotes check out as exact KJV:
- **"And Aaron shall cast lots upon the two goats; one lot for the LORD, and the other lot for the scapegoat."** — Lev 16:8, exact. ✓
- **"And the goat shall bear upon him all their iniquities unto a land not inhabited."** — Lev 16:22 (clean partial), exact. ✓
- **"and the LORD hath laid on him the iniquity of us all."** — Isa 53:6, exact. ✓
- **"Having therefore, brethren, boldness to enter into the holiest by the blood of Jesus."** — Heb 10:19, exact. ✓

**Attribution seams (real, minor):**
- The first quote is introduced by **"I cast lots over them:"** — but the verse then refers to **"Aaron"** in the *third person*. So first-person Aaron is quoting a law *about* Aaron. It reads as the essay-narrator reading Scripture, not Aaron speaking — a small VOICE (EW-G10) crack at the one spot the device is most exposed.
- The Isaiah and Hebrews quotes are framed only as **"By the light that came long after me"** and **"The word long after me says"** — no source named. Defensible as the pre-NT witness device, but it's vague attribution; nothing anchors them as prophet/apostle.

## CTA-on-Jesus (EW-INV-2)
Names Jesus, names the response ("Trust," "Come"), grace-anchored ("be carried clean"), and avoids the banned bare "will you trust Him?" — "Will you come in?" is earned off the torn-curtain image. **But the landing is cluttered:** *"Will you come in? Now, on His blood. Trust the One who finished what I only foreshadowed. Come — and be carried clean."* Four imperatives/questions stacked — it diffuses the single clean turn. **"Now, on His blood."** as a clipped fragment also leans faintly toward manufactured urgency rather than invitation. Tighten to one breath.

## Reveal earned (EW-G7) — passes
The "two for one — why?" puzzle is planted in Beat 1, the mechanics shown in Beat 3 (one pays in blood, one carries away, *but I came back every year*), then resolved in Beat 6 (one Priest does *both* halves, and "sat down" = finished). Genuinely shown, not announced. Beat 6's "I see what every Day of Atonement reached toward" tips slightly toward telling, but it's backed by mechanics already shown.

## Wrestling (EW-G8) — passes
The honest objection is real and in-voice: *"Yet I came back — same blood, same goats, same sins… real, but never finished."* That's the actual Hebrews argument (repetition proves incompleteness), not a strawman.

## Doctrine (EW-G9) — sound
Substitution named (God's own Son bearing your sin + Isa 53:6 + "blood did what no goat could do"); penalty present ("bearing your sin into a death none could undo"); no supersession — the old rite is called **"real, but never finished,"** i.e. fulfilled, not abolished.

## First-hearing clarity (the soft spots)
- **"one Priest who sat down"** is a gorgeous Hebrews allusion but opaque to a zero-Bible listener — they won't know the priest *never* sat = work done. It's load-bearing on the reveal yet uncashed.
- **"beyond the veil"** / **"the holiest"** lean on the later "curtain… is torn / way stands open" to carry meaning — it just barely lands. Watchable, not broken.

## Length
~305–310 spoken words — inside the band but at the very top. The CTA trim above also buys you margin.

Bottom line: no doctrinal or KJV error; the reveal and wrestling are genuinely earned. The defects are polish-level — a cluttered landing, the third-person-Aaron seam, and one uncashed allusion.

VERDICT: PASS
TOP FIXES:
1. Declutter the close — collapse the four stacked CTA beats ("Will you come in? Now, on His blood. Trust the One… Come — and be carried clean.") into one clean invitation, and soften "Now, on His blood." so it reads as welcome, not pressure.
