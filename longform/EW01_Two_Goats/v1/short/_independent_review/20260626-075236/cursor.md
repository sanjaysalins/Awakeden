## Independent adversarial review — *The Two Goats* (Aaron, short)

### EW-INV-4 — KJV verbatim + attribution

**Verbatim:** The four bold spans match `passage.txt` as substrings. No word-level corruption found.

**Attribution: FAIL on all four quotes.** EW-INV-4 requires the speaker to be named before each quote; sibling shorts use `**[the LORD]**`, `**[Jesus]**`, or an explicit “the law commanded” frame. This artifact drops straight from Aaron’s prose into Scripture with no tag:

- *“I cast lots over them:”* → **`"And Aaron shall cast lots upon the two goats..."`** — no law/Moses/scripture attribution.
- *“I watched it go into a land where no one would find it:”* → Lev 16:22 — same gap.
- Beat 6: Isaiah 53:6 fragment **`"and the LORD hath laid on him the iniquity of us all."`** — no “Isaiah wrote” / prophet frame; listener cannot tell this is a later text, not Aaron’s Day-of-Atonement ledger.
- Beat 7: **`"Having therefore, brethren, boldness to enter into the holiest by the blood of Jesus."`** — no Hebrews/writer attribution; reads as Aaron’s own words.

**Voice collision on quote 1:** Aaron says *“I cast lots over them”* then quotes third-person *“And **Aaron** shall cast lots…”* — the law speaking about him, unattributed. Awkward and fails the project’s `[Speaker]` convention.

### EW-G8 — Wrestling (steel-man)

**Thin / strawman.** The only doubt is curiosity, not the strongest honest objection:

- *“I never stopped asking why.”*
- *“Why would one need two?”*

That is a riddle, not Aaron’s real steel-man. The locked long names what a high priest would actually carry: the rite was **real but never finished** (*“Every year I came back… the same blood, the same two goats”*), plus the fear that *“the blood of bulls and of goats”* could not finally take away sin (Heb 10:4). None of that survives the short cut.

A listener should feel Aaron’s **stakes** (veil terror, sons dead in the holy place, annual repetition) before the reveal. Here the doubt is too light to earn the turn.

### EW-G7 — Reveal earned

**Partially earned, mostly announced.**

**Earned:** Beat 3 does show the two halves — *“One died to pay. The other lived to carry it off.”* — from Aaron’s hands. Good.

**Unearned / asserted:**

- *“By the light that came long after me, I tell you what every Day of Atonement was reaching toward.”* — cross-time signposting is allowed (EW-INV-11), but this **announces** fulfillment instead of **walking** the listener from the goat Aaron already quoted (*“bear upon him all their iniquities”*) to Christ. The Isaiah line lands as imported proof, not as logic drawn from the ritual just described.
- *“That is why there were two: no single creature could hold both halves”* — typology stated, not demonstrated from Lev 16:5’s *“two kids of the goats for a **sin offering**”* (one offering, two goats). The long earns this; the short asserts it.
- *“who entered once and **sat down**”* — Hebrews 10:12 theology with **no quote, no setup**. Zero–Bible-knowledge listener will not know why “sat down” matters (no chair behind the veil). Unearned punchline.

The reveal leans on post-Aaron texts (Isaiah, implied Hebrews) before fully mining Aaron’s **own** Leviticus moment.

### EW-G9 — Doctrine

**Mostly sound**, but two friction points:

- Substitution is named (*“price paid”* + *“guilt was laid and carried clean away”*) — passes.
- *“Trust the One who finished what **I only began**”* — without the long’s *“cleansing was real… but not done”* guardrail, this can sound like Aaron’s God-given rite **failed**, edging toward dismissive supersession rather than shadow→substance. The long is careful; the short is not.
- *“So do not come to me, nor to a goat, nor to a yearly altar”* — standard typological close, acceptable if the rite is honored as shadow first (currently under-weighted).

No contrarian reading detected.

### EW-G10 — Voice / character

**Generic priest costume in places.**

- Beat 1 is functional but lacks Aaron’s distinctive terror (veil, Nadab and Abihu) that makes the long’s Aaron **this** Aaron.
- Beat 6 slips into essay voice: *“every Day of Atonement was reaching toward”*, *“There came a Man, Jesus”*.
- **Continuity break:** Beat 7 says *“The curtain **I trembled before** is torn”* — **trembling is never established in this short.** That detail is borrowed from the long without being planted here. Reads as costume, not lived memory.

### Hook / spine / tightness

**Hook:** *“Two — for one. I never stopped asking why.”* — competent, not gripping compared to peer shorts (Moses’s dying people, Abraham’s three-day climb).

**Spine:** One witness, one thread (two goats → Christ). Holds.

**Length:** ~300 spoken words — inside 220–320. No second thread crowding the moment. Beat 3 carries most of the weight; sag is minimal, but **compression cost** is the missing steel-man, not bloat.
