# Independent review — claude (OK, 64s)

I read this adversarially against the eyewitness lens. The KJV text is clean, the doctrine holds, the CTA is strong — but two real gaps keep it from a PASS.

## KJV verbatim — PASS
All four quotes check out word-for-word:
- **Lev 16:8** "And Aaron shall cast lots upon the two goats; one lot for the LORD, and the other lot for the scapegoat." ✓
- **Lev 16:22** "And the goat shall bear upon him all their iniquities unto a land not inhabited." ✓ (verbatim partial)
- **Isa 53:6** "and the LORD hath laid on him the iniquity of us all." ✓
- **Heb 10:19** "Having therefore, brethren, boldness to enter into the holiest by the blood of Jesus." ✓

## Attribution (EW-INV-4) — the main problem
The two *cross-testament* quotes float into Aaron's mouth with no speaker named, and a zero-Bible listener cannot tell they belong to other people centuries apart:

- **Isa 53:6** is dropped mid-sentence — "the One on whom the guilt was laid and carried clean away: **"and the LORD hath laid on him the iniquity of us all."**" Nothing tells the listener this is a *prophet* speaking ~700 years after Aaron. The umbrella line "By the light that came long after me" gestures at it but doesn't attribute *this* quote.
- **Heb 10:19** is worse: the quote contains "**brethren**," which is the apostolic writer addressing *Christians* — but as placed, it reads as **Aaron** calling his hearers "brethren." That's a borderline misattribution. It needs an explicit frame ("as one would later write to the church…") so the voice doesn't claim apostolic words as his own.

## Wrestling (EW-G8) — too thin
The "honest doubt" is just "**Why would one need two?**" (repeated). That's a liturgical *curiosity*, not the genuine strongest objection. A real steel-man for this witness is deeper — *does the blood actually work? does the sin actually stay gone in that empty land, or does it come back next year?* The repeated yearly offering is itself the unanswered ache (cf. Heb 10:1-4) and would be a far stronger internalized doubt than mere puzzlement at the number two.

## Reveal (EW-G7) — borderline, leans announced
"I tell you what every Day of Atonement was reaching toward" and "That is why there were two" are partly *told*. It's rescued by the genuinely earned payoff — "no single creature could hold both halves" — which does the work from the text he lived. Keep that line; trim the announcing.

## Minor
- Beat numbering jumps **1 → 3 → 6 → 7**. Looks like material is missing; relabel or close the gaps.
- **Voice/CTA are good:** "The curtain I trembled before is torn"; "Come — and be carried clean" names the response (come/trust), Jesus-anchored, grace-only, no fear/gain-loss. Substitution is named (price paid + guilt carried away). No supersession — it's fulfillment ("finished what I only began").
- Length ~290 words — tight, lands the single moment, no second thread. ✓

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Attribute the cross-testament quotes in narrator/witness frame — name the prophet before Isa 53:6 and the apostolic writer before Heb 10:19, so the "brethren" in Hebrews isn't put in Aaron's own mouth (EW-INV-4).
2. Deepen the wrestling from "why two goats?" to the real ache Aaron lived — that the blood had to be brought *again every single year* and the sin never finally stayed gone — so the steel-man is genuine doubt, not curiosity (EW-G8).
3. Soften the announced reveal ("I tell you what… was reaching toward" / "That is why there were two") and let the earned "no single creature could hold both halves" carry the turn (EW-G7); also fix the Beat 1→3→6→7 numbering gaps.
