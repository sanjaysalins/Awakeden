# Independent review — codex (OK, 158s)

KJV/source check used: Bible Gateway KJV for [Leviticus 16](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Leviticus%2016&version=KJV), [Leviticus 17:11](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Leviticus%2017%3A11&version=KJV), [Hebrews 9:12](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Hebrews%209%3A12&version=KJV), [Hebrews 10](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Hebrews%2010&version=KJV), [Hebrews 13:12](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Hebrews%2013%3A12&version=KJV), [Isaiah 53:6](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Isaiah%2053%3A6&version=KJV), [Psalm 103:12](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Psalm%20103%3A12&version=KJV), and [Matthew 27:51](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Matthew%2027%3A51&version=KJV).

Major findings:

- EW-INV-4 has strictness problems. Several quotes are clipped mid-verse and repunctuated as if complete: **"And he shall take... for a sin offering."**, **"And the goat shall bear... unto a land not inhabited."**, **"And, behold, the veil... from the top to the bottom."**, and **"Having therefore... by the blood of Jesus."** If excerpts are allowed, they still need clean source labels and non-misleading punctuation/ellipsis. **"The gospel records it:"** is also too vague; the quoted wording is Matthew 27:51, so name Matthew before quoting.

- Invented ritual detail: **"I confessed over it — out of my own mouth, sin by sin — all the wrong of all the people"** over-specifies Leviticus 16:21. The text says Aaron confessed all Israel’s iniquities/transgressions/sins, not a literal itemized “sin by sin” confession. That is dramatic padding and may be impossible for the national rite.

- The wrestling is close, but not yet the real steel-man. **"I was only ever pointing — pointing at some greater atonement"** is already Christian typological vocabulary, not Aaron’s strongest internal objection. The stronger conflict is buried in **"The people went home clean... That cleansing was real. And yet it was never the end."** Make that the wound: if God truly cleansed them, why did the same blood have to return every year?

- The reveal partly turns into a Bible lecture in costume. **"That later word — the letter to the Hebrews — says plainly..."**, **"It was a shadow — and a shadow waits for the body that casts it"**, and **"That is why there were two"** sound like an expositor, not Aaron. The typology is basically coherent, but the narration announces the answer more than making Aaron discover it through the goats, the blood, the hand on the head, the wilderness, and the repeated return.

- Doctrine needs sharper substitution language. **"He was the blood that paid the price"** is imprecise; Christ is not “the blood,” His blood/death pays. More importantly, the lens asks for the penalty named. **"price paid"** and **"guilt was laid"** are close, but the piece never clearly says Christ bore sin’s penalty in His death.

- **"none of it ever stayed done"** risks undercutting the earlier, better line **"That cleansing was real."** The old covenant rite was real but not final; saying it “never stayed done” can sound as if God’s stated cleansing wore off.

- Voice weakens in Beat 6. The early Aaron voice has blood, sons, linen, veil. The later voice becomes abstract: **"Type→fulfillment"**, **"shadow"**, **"body"**, **"one Priest"**, **"letter to the Hebrews"**. The first-person witness frame survives structurally, but the character fades at the most important turn.

- First-hearing clarity has some fog. Terms like **"mercy seat"**, **"atonement"**, **"without the gate"**, and **"the holiest"** are emotionally usable but not always clear to a listener with no Bible background. The closing phrase **"Walk in — the holiest is open"** may land as poetic rather than intelligible.

- Long-form sag: the same annual-repetition idea is hit too many times: **"year after year"**, **"year upon year"**, **"Every year..."**, **"the same blood, the same two goats"**, **"always I came back to do it again"**. One of those beats needs to add a new turn or be cut.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Fix strict KJV/source handling: name the source before each quote and stop repunctuating clipped verses as complete.
2. Rework the wrestling/reveal so Aaron’s question is internal and earned, not a Hebrews lecture in Aaron’s mouth.
3. Sharpen substitution: say Christ bore sin’s penalty in His death, while preserving that Levitical cleansing was real but not final.
