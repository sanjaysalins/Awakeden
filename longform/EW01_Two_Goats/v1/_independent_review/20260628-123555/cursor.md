**Strictness nits (minor):**

**Attribution: still fails on the OT blocks.** Only one quote is speaker-tagged:

| Quote | Lead-in | Problem |
|---|---|---|

## EW-G9 — Doctrine

**What holds:**
- Aaron explicitly denies bearing the people’s guilt: *“I did not bear their guilt. I could not”* — correct mediating-priest theology, not Aaron-as-substitute.
- Nadab/Abihu corrected: *“my cousins carried their bodies out… while I stood at the door of the tabernacle, forbidden… to leave my place”* — matches Lev 10:4–7.
- Beat 5 no longer says the prior cleansing failed; *“Every year the people sinned afresh”* / *“A cleansing you must make again every year is true, but it is not finished”* — sound Hebrews-shaped tension.
- Outside-the-camp typology is grounded on the **slain** offering’s body (Heb 13:11), not the scapegoat: *“as the sin offering's body was always carried outside the camp and burned”* — doctrinally careful.
- Substitution **partially** named: **`He was the blood that paid the price`** + **`the LORD hath laid on him the iniquity of us all`** + **`carried clean away`**.

**What’s thin or missing:**
- **Penalty under-named (EW-INV-7).** Death/blood is pictured; **what was due to us** (judgment, wrath, the death sin earns) is never stated in Aaron’s voice. *“They led Him outside the city to die”* implies penal substitution via Hebrews 13, but the listener must supply the logic. Compare to the spec bar: *penalty + what Christ bore* — only the second half is explicit.
- **Missed anchor in Aaron’s own text:** Lev 16:10 says the live goat is presented *“to make an atonement with him.”* The piece never quotes or uses that line. Beat 4’s riddle (*“One goat died to pay; the other lived to carry it off”*) is Aaron’s inference, but the **live goat’s atoning role** — right in his chapter — would let the two-halves reveal stay in-period longer instead of leaning on later synthesis.

No supersession smell; *“Do not come to me… Come to Jesus”* is typological fulfillment, not “Israel replaced.”

## EW-G7 — Reveal earned?

**Earned:**
- Beat 4 is the spine’s best turn: *“It was one sin offering — yet it took two goats”* → *“One goat died to pay; the other lived to carry it off — as though God meant to show us two things at once that no single creature could hold.”* That is shown from what Aaron lived.

**Still asserted more than shown:**
- Beat 6 opens with hindsight announcement: *“I did not see the answer in my own day. I tell you now looking back from the far side of my life — by the light that came long after I laid down my office, and by the word that was written after me.”* Cross-time is allowed (EW-INV-11), but this is **signpost + delivery**, not derivation.
- After Beat 4’s riddle, Beat 6 becomes a **seven-quote NT stack** glued with thesis lines:
  - *“It was a shadow — and a shadow waits for the body that casts it. The body came.”*
  - *“He was the blood that paid the price — and He was the One upon whom the guilt was laid…”*
  - *“That is why there were two.”*
- **`That is why there were two`** lands as **announcement** after Hebrews/Isaiah/Matthew do the work — not a stepwise walk showing how **one Priest** completes **both halves** from Leviticus alone (especially 16:10’s live-goat atonement). The reveal is **half-earned**: Beat 4 sets it up; Beat 6 mostly **confirms** via later writers.

## EW-G8 — Wrestling (steel-man)

**Improved** vs earlier drafts: annual repetition is framed correctly (*fresh sin*, not failed cleansing).

**Still not the fullest steel-man in Aaron’s own era:**
- Beat 5 is **one summary paragraph** — no dramatized memory (a particular year, a particular confession over the goat’s head, hands still smelling of blood, a specific dread at the veil).
- The stated doubt — *“in my old age a quiet fear pressed on me… that for all the blood I poured out, I was only ever pointing”* — is already **the sermon’s answer**. It hurts less than it should because it resolves into foreshadowing (*“I was a sign. I did not yet know of what.”*) instead of staying unresolved.
- Beat 1–2 invest heavily in **veil-death dread** (Nadab/Abihu, *“that he die not”*, *“I was not certain I would come out alive”*). Beat 5 **barely uses it**. The annual-repetition objection is biblical but **essay-level**; the stronger lived objection — *God declared them clean this day; am I impious to feel it is never finished?* — is gestured (*“The people went home clean”*) but not **wrestled**.
- Beat 4’s *“why two?”* riddle never becomes a **doubt beat** — Aaron asks it *“year upon year”* but never voices impiety, fear of getting the rite wrong, or terror that the live goat’s role makes no sense to him.

## EW-G10 — Voice / character

**Strengths:** Beats 1–4 are vivid Aaron — *“heart in my throat,”* *“could have done it in the dark,”* linen/washing detail, hand-work on the goats.

**Costume slips:** Beats 6–7 drift **sermonic**. The Aaron who trembled at the veil yields to a Hebrews expositor wearing his name:
- *“It was a shadow — and a shadow waits for the body that casts it”* — literary/modern, not priest-memory.
- Beat 6’s quote density reads like **audio Bible + commentary**, not a man recounting what his hands did.

**Interpretive dramatization (acceptable if labelled, but blurs first-hearing clarity):**
- *“I stood at the edge of the camp and watched it go”* — Lev 16 sends the goat by a **fit man** into the wilderness; Aaron watching from the edge is plausible color, not text, and *“by the hand of a fit man”* in the quote is never glossed.
