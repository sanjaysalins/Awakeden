## The real problem — anachronistic self-quotation (Beat 5, voice/timeline)
Beat 5 is framed as in-life wrestling — "As the years wore on… in my old age the thought grew sharper" — and inside that present-tense wrestling the priest quotes **Hebrews 10:3** and **Hebrews 10:4** as his own thoughts. But the retrospective vantage that licenses NT quotes ("I tell you now from the far side of my life, by the light that came long after I had laid down my office") doesn't arrive until Beat 6. So in Beat 5 a pre-Christ high priest is verbatim-reaching the *conclusion of the book of Hebrews* before establishing he's speaking from beyond. The verbatim NT citation is the tell. Either pull the "far side of my life" frame forward to open Beat 5, or recast the Beat 5 doubt as the *felt* insufficiency he lived ("a cure I must take again every year is no cure") and reserve the Hebrews verbatim for the reveal where the frame already covers it. This is the one thing that should be tightened.

## Attribution (EW-INV-4) — uneven
Only the LORD quote carries a `**[speaker]**` tag. The reveal quotes (Hebrews, Matt 27:51) float as scripture the priest could not author. Beat 6's "by the light that came long after" is a soft frame and *mostly* covers it, but Matt 27:51 in particular ("torn open from the top the very hour He died… by no human hand" → quote) leans on reportage of an event he names but didn't witness. Acceptable, but a hair under-framed.

## Minor — "thy brother" (Beat 2)
The Lev 16:2 quote "Speak unto Aaron **thy brother**" is the LORD addressing *Moses*. In the mouth of a later high priest (Aaron's successor, not his brother) the "thy brother" can read as a momentary snag for a zero-Bible listener. Verbatim and correctly attributed to the LORD, so not a violation — just a clarity nit.
