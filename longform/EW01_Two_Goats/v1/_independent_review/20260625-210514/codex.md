# Independent review — codex (OK, 144s)

**Findings**

- EW-INV-4 attribution fails. The KJV wording I checked is broadly exact, but most quotes are not attributed before use. Only `**[the LORD]**` is tagged. Blocks like `**"it is the blood that maketh an atonement for the soul."**`, `**"there is a remembrance again made of sins every year."**`, and `**"For it is not possible that the blood of bulls and of goats should take away sins."**` appear as floating authority. Worse, the Hebrews lines are placed inside the priest’s old-age interior wrestling, though they are later apostolic commentary, not something this witness could have quoted then. KJV references checked: Leviticus 16, Leviticus 17:11, Hebrews 10, Hebrews 9:12, Hebrews 13:12. ([biblegateway.com](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Leviticus+16&version=KJV)) ([biblegateway.com](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Leviticus+17%3A11&version=KJV)) ([biblegateway.com](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Hebrews+10&version=KJV)) ([biblegateway.com](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Hebrews+9%3A12&version=KJV)) ([biblegateway.com](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Hebrews+13%3A12&version=KJV))

- The witness is not actually a named biblical witness. `**Witness:** the high priest of Israel` is a role, not a named person. The narration also dodges identity: `Two of Aaron's own sons had died` implies the speaker is not Aaron, but Leviticus 16 is specifically addressed to Aaron. Then `I did it forty times in my life` invents a biographical claim without grounding. This breaks the “named biblical witness” premise.

- Serious doctrinal imprecision: `somebody had to answer for it. That somebody, on that day, was me.` The high priest did not bear Israel’s penalty as substitute. He mediated the commanded sacrifice and also needed atonement for himself. This line gives the priest a role that belongs to Christ and muddies substitution.

- The substitution is close but not fully named. `He paid the price in His own blood, and He carried your sin away` names blood and removal, but the “penalty” itself is not explicit. Under EW-G9, the piece should name what Christ bore: the guilt/judgment/penalty due to sinners, not merely “price” in vague devotional terms.

- The wrestling is strong in concept but anachronistic in execution. `A cure you must take again every year is not truly a cure` is not really the high priest’s strongest faithful objection; it sounds like Hebrews 10 turned into a modern analogy. It risks making God’s appointed ceremony sound defective rather than provisional, ceremonial, and intentionally forward-pointing.

- The reveal is partly earned, but the key turn is over-announced. `I tell you now from the far side of my life... what every Day of Atonement was always reaching toward` and `He was both my goats` tell the interpretation before the witness has fully shown it. The later links are good, especially blood, guilt-bearing, outside the gate, and veil, but the reveal still feels like sermon thesis more than awakened recognition.

- Voice drifts into essay-preacher mode. Phrases like `stand in the gap between a guilty people and a holy God`, `the true and final High Priest`, `once and for all`, and `The work is done` are doctrinally familiar but not character-specific. The priest’s hands, fear, linen, blood, and silence are vivid; the reveal increasingly sounds like a detached explainer.

- CTA mostly satisfies EW-INV-2, but has one doctrinal/clarity risk. `Come to Jesus` and `trust the One` name the response, and the close is grace-oriented. But `Your sin is carried away` stated to every listener before/without tying it to coming by faith can sound like automatic universal application. Better anchor the assurance to those who come/receive/trust.

- Long-form runtime is only partly earned. Beat 4 and Beat 5 add a real interpretive turn, but repetition starts to sag: `year upon year`, `the same blood, the same two goats, the same confession...`, then the reveal restates the two-goat logic almost exactly. The long version needs more distinct lived pressure or a sharper inner conflict, not more restatement of the same riddle.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Make the witness a named, text-grounded person and remove anachronistic Hebrews quotations from his pre-reveal interior life.
2. Add proper attribution before every KJV quote and fix quote-fragment punctuation/citation discipline.
3. Replace `That somebody... was me` and explicitly name Christ bearing the sinner’s penalty by faith.
