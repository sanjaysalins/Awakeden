# Independent review — claude (OK, 87s)

Reviewed adversarially against the eyewitness invariants and gates. I checked every `**"..."**` quote against KJV.

## KJV verbatim (EW-INV-4) — clean
I verified all 14 quotes word-for-word. They are exact KJV:
- Lev 16:2, 16:8, 16:15, 16:21, 16:22, 16:5; Lev 17:11
- Heb 10:3, 10:4, 9:12, 13:12, 10:12, 10:19
- Isa 53:6; Ps 103:12; Matt 27:51

No altered words, no smuggled paraphrase inside the quote marks. This is the strongest part of the piece.

## Doctrine (EW-G9) — sound
Substitution is named with both halves and the penalty borne: *"He was the blood that paid the price — and He was the One upon whom the guilt was laid and carried clean away."* The shadow→body framing is Hebrews' own (9–10), not supersession of Israel. The self-sin point is correct and honest: *"I did not bear their guilt… I had to bring blood for my own sin before ever I brought it for theirs."*

## Reveal earned (EW-G7) — yes
The "why two goats?" riddle is planted in Beat 4 and genuinely paid off in Beat 6: *"That is why there were two. No single creature could show both halves at once."* The turn is shown, not announced. Spine holds — one witness, one thread, no swap.

## Wrestling (EW-G8) — genuine
*"it never stayed finished… A cleansing you must make again every year is true, but it is not done."* That IS Hebrews' actual argument from repetition — the real steel-man, in Aaron's voice, not a strawman.

## CTA (EW-INV-2) — passes
Names Jesus and the response: *"Come to Jesus — the High Priest who finished it… Trust the One who finished what I only ever foreshadowed. Come — and be carried clean."* Fear is explicitly removed (*"only now there is no fear left in it"*), grace-anchored, no gain/loss. *"will you come in?"* dodges the banned bare template because it's recontextualized as the question he asked behind the veil.

---

## Real weaknesses (none fatal, but worth fixing)

**1. Beat 6 is a proof-text catena — the voice thins to essay (EW-G10 + SAG).** Seven scriptures fire in rapid succession (Heb 10:3 → 10:4 → 9:12 → Isa 53:6 → Heb 13:12 → 10:12 → Matt 27:51) with only thin connective tissue. For ~6 sentences Aaron stops being a man remembering and becomes a citation engine. This is the one stretch where the first-person witness recedes behind a sermon. Thin it to the 3–4 load-bearing quotes (9:12 entered once / Isa 53:6 laid on him / 10:12 sat down / 27:51 veil rent) and carry the rest in his own remembered voice.

**2. First-hearing clarity: how does Aaron know the New Testament?** The frame — *"by the light that came long after I laid down my office, and by the word that was written after me"* — is mystical and vague. A listener with zero Bible knowledge may stumble on a dead high priest quoting the Gospel of Matthew and Hebrews. The device is legitimate, but the bridge could be one line clearer.

**3. Minor attribution drift (EW-INV-4 attribution half).** The Levitical quotes are well-framed ("the LORD said," "the law commanded"). But Isa 53:6 and the Hebrews quotes are blanketed under "the word that was written after me" — no prophet/writer named. Isaiah in particular (*"the LORD hath laid on him the iniquity of us all"*) lands without a "the prophet wrote" frame. The LORD-content inside it covers it, but naming the source would tighten it.

This is a high-quality piece. The verdict is a pass on the binding invariants; the fixes are craft improvements, chiefly the Beat 6 density.

VERDICT: PASS
TOP FIXES:
1. Trim Beat 6's seven-quote catena to the 3–4 load-bearing scriptures and let Aaron carry the connective lines in his own remembered voice, so the first-person witness doesn't collapse into a proof-text sermon.
2. Sharpen the "how do I know the New Testament?" bridge in Beat 6 — make the after-the-fact vantage one line clearer for a zero-Bible listener.
3. Name the source on at least Isaiah 53:6 ("the prophet wrote") so the cross-witness quotes aren't all blanketed under the vague "word written after me."
