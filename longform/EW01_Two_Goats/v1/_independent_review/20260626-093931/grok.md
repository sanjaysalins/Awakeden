# Independent review — grok (OK, 57s)

Checking the eyewitness spec for exact attribution and quote rules before delivering the review.
## Independent adversarial review — *The Two Goats* (Aaron, long)

### EW-INV-4 — KJV verbatim + attribution

**Only one quote is formally attributed.** Lev 16:2 has `**[the LORD]**` before the quote. Every other KJV block is unattributed:

- Beat 2: `**"it is the blood that maketh an atonement for the soul."**` — no speaker/source (Lev 17:11).
- Beats 3–4: all five Leviticus 16 quotes (`cast lots`, `kill the goat`, `lay both my hands`, `bear upon him all their iniquities`, `two kids of the goats`) — no `**[Moses]**`, `**[the law]**`, or equivalent before the quote.
- Beat 6: six post-OT quotes (Hebrews, Isaiah, Matthew) — no attribution; Aaron says *"by the word that was written after me"* once, but each quote still needs its own frame per the rule.
- Beat 7: Psalm 103:12 and Hebrews 10:19 — same gap.

Substring KJV looks fine on spot-check; the failure is **attribution**, not wording.

---

### EW-G9 — Doctrine / ritual grounding

**Beat 7 misstates what Aaron actually did with sin.**

> *"Year after year I carried the people's sin in past that veil, and year after year I carried it back out again, because I could never set it down for good."*

Beat 3 is explicit: blood goes **in** through the veil; guilt goes **out** on the living goat, sent away *"by the hand of a fit man into the wilderness"* — not carried back by Aaron through the veil. This line **collapses the two goats into one image** and implies the scapegoat rite failed to remove sin. That fights the piece’s own typology (*price paid* vs *guilt carried off*) and mis-teaches Leviticus 16 to a first-time listener.

**Beat 5 softens the same problem:**

> *"the stain came back with the new year, as faithful as the feast"*

Leviticus 16:30 and Hebrews 10:3 point to **annual remembrance** and **ongoing sin**, not the Day’s cleansing failing and the stain “returning.” A zero-Bible listener may hear: *Yom Kippur didn’t really work.*

**Beat 5 also overstates repetition:**

> *"the same confession over the same people for the same sins"*

Each year addresses **new** transgressions; framing it as the *same sins* every year skews the steel-man.

---

### EW-G7 — Is the reveal earned?

**Strong in Beats 3–4.** Aaron’s hands-on split — one goat killed, one sent away alive — earns *"That is why there were two"* and *"both halves finished in one Priest."* Good.

**Weaker from mid–Beat 6 onward.** After the two-goat logic, the turn leans on a **quote stack** (Hebrews 9/10/13, Isaiah 53, Matthew 27) more than on ritual memory:

- Veil torn, Priest **sat down**, “outside the gate” — true and sound, but **not shown from the two-goat act Aaron lived**; they read as appended NT proof, not as something his riddle forced.
- *"It was a shadow — and a shadow waits for the body"* is assertion; the next lines **name** Jesus quickly without walking the listener through *how* the live-goat half maps to Christ’s death-outside-the-camp (the scapegoat wasn’t killed; Christ was — that tension is never wrestled).

Reveal is **partially earned**, **partially announced** once Beat 6 turns into exposition.

---

### EW-G8 — Wrestling: real steel-man?

**Beat 5’s core doubt is legitimate:** yearly repetition → *"not done"* → *"only ever pointing."* That matches Hebrews’ argument. Not a strawman.

**But the text’s sharpest puzzle is underused.** Beat 4 asks *"why two? Why not put both to death?"* — the live goat is the strangest detail. Beat 5 never doubts **that**; it only doubts **repetition**. A steel-man Aaron might also ask: *If sin is truly carried away, why must I kill one goat at all?* The wrestling follows the sermon outline, not the ritual’s weirdest edge.

**"quiet fear pressed on me"** (Beat 5) is honest interiority, but it edges toward mood-padding unless tied tighter to the two-goat riddle.

---

### EW-INV-2 — CTA on Jesus

**Passes on naming:** *"Come to Jesus"*, *"will you come in?"*, *"Trust the One who finished"*, *"Come — and be carried clean"* — response verbs are named; not the bare *"will you trust Him?"* template.

**Flags:**

- *"the thing I waited my whole life to understand"* (Beat 7) — close cousin to the banned *"at last I understood"* stinger (EW-G12 variant): summary verdict, not lived turn.
- *"Sit with that a moment"* appears **twice** (Beats 7) — host cadence, not Aaron-at-the-altar voice; slight manufactured pause.

No gain-loss framing; grace-anchored overall.

---

### EW-G10 — Voice / character

**Beats 1–3:** Strong Aaron — Nadab and Abihu, veil dread, hands on the goat, watching it shrink. First-person, specific, consistent.

**Beats 6–7:** Voice thins into **epistle narrator**: *"That later word says plainly"*, scripture dump, repeated veil image, *"Sit with that a moment."* Signposted cross-time frame (EW-INV-11) is fine; still, half the close sounds like a Hebrews commentary wearing Aaron’s name.

---

### First-hearing clarity

- **"scapegoat"** appears in KJV (`other lot for the scapegoat`) with **no plain-language gloss** for listeners who don’t know the term.
- **Two-goat mechanics** are clear; **blood-goat vs live-goat** distinction gets muddied again in Beat 7’s *"carried in… carried back out"* line.
- Ritual order is compressed (no bull for Aaron’s own sin in Beat 3) — acceptable for length, but a knowledgeable listener will notice.

---

### Hook, spine, long-form pacing

**Hook holds:** *"where no other man on earth was permitted to go"* — immediate stakes.

**Spine holds:** one witness, one thread (two goats → one Priest). No swap.

**Sag / padding (~9–11 min budget):**

- Beat 6: long quote run with little new turn between quotes.
- Beat 7: **veil torn twice** (Beat 6: *"rent in twain from the top to the bottom"*; Beat 7: *"torn from top to bottom the hour He died"*) — same beat, no new insight.
- *"Sit with that a moment"* ×2 — runtime without a new hinge.
- Beats 1–5 earn their minutes; **6–7 repeat rather than deepen.**

---

### What works (so the verdict isn’t a blanket FAIL)

- Substitution is **named**: blood pays; guilt laid on the head and borne away; Christ as both.
- Aaron **does not** claim to bear the penalty as substitute (Beat 2) — sound.
- Two-goat typology in Beats 3–4 is the right evangelical reading, not contrarian.
- No supersession; “do not come to me” targets **mediation fulfilled**, not Israel replaced.

---

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. **Rewrite Beat 7’s *"carried the people's sin in past that veil… carried it back out again"*** — keep blood-in and scapegoat-away separate; match Beat 3’s ritual or the two-goat typology breaks for first-time listeners.
2. **Attribute every KJV quote before the `**"..."**` block** (Leviticus law, Hebrews, Isaiah, Psalm, Matthew) — only Lev 16:2 complies today.
3. **Tighten Beat 5–6–7:** fix *"stain came back"* / *"same sins"* imprecision; cut the second veil paragraph; earn Christ-as-both-halves without leaning on an unattributed NT stack; let Beat 4’s *"why two?"* feed the wrestling, not only annual repetition.
