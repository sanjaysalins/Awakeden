### EW-G9 — Doctrine (most serious flag)

**Internal contradiction + ritual error in the CTA (Beat 7):**

Beat 2 is explicit and correct:

> `Understand me: I did not bear their guilt. I could not; I had to bring blood for my own sin before ever I brought it for theirs. I only carried the blood in, and came out alive.`

Beat 7 undoes that:

> `Year after year I carried the people's sin in past that veil, and year after year I carried it back out again, because I could never set it down for good.`

Leviticus 16 does not work this way. Aaron carried **blood** through the veil; guilt was confessed onto the **live** goat and sent away by a fit man (`**"And Aaron shall lay both his hands upon the head of the live goat…"**` / `**"by the hand of a fit man into the wilderness."**`). Sin was not shuttled in and out past the mercy seat. This is not a stylistic slip — it mis-teaches the rite the witness claims to have performed with his own hands (Beat 3), and blurs **mediator** vs **substitute** right at the invitation.

**Diminishing the Day of Atonement’s real efficacy (Beat 5):**

You do honor `That cleansing was real.` — good. But then:

> `the stain came back with the new year, as faithful as the feast.`

Lev 16:30-style same-day cleansing is **real for that year**; what returns is **new sin / the need for another annual rite**, not the prior atonement failing and “stain” reappearing. Hebrews’ point is *not final*, not *sham*. `"A cleansing you must make again every year is true, but it is not done"` is the stronger line; `"the stain came back"` still sounds like the LORD’s declared cleansing didn’t hold.

**Outside-the-camp (Beat 6):** Fixed from earlier drafts — `as the sin offering's body was always carried outside the camp and burned` correctly tracks Heb 13:11 / Lev 16:27, not the scapegoat’s wilderness route. Keep that; don’t regress.

**Substitution:** Adequately named — `"He was the blood that paid the price"`, `"the LORD hath laid on him the iniquity of us all"`, `"carried clean away"`. Penalty could be sharper (wrath/judgment due), but EW-G9 bar is mostly met **except** the Beat 7 sin-through-veil error.

### EW-G7 — Reveal earned?

**What earns it:** Beat 4’s riddle is the spine’s best turn:

> `It was one sin offering — yet it took two goats.`
> `One goat died to pay; the other lived to carry it off — as though God meant to show us two things at once that no single creature could hold.`

Beat 6 pays off with `That is why there were two.` and `"both halves were finished in one Priest"` — better than the old `"both my goats"` mapping.

**What is still asserted more than shown:** After the signpost (`I tell you now from the far side of my life — by the light that came long after…`), Beat 6 becomes a **NT quote stack** glued with typological thesis lines:

> `It was a shadow — and a shadow waits for the body that casts it. The body came.`
> `He was the blood that paid the price — and He was the One upon whom the guilt was laid and carried clean away`

The cross-time device is allowed (EW-INV-11), but EW-G7 wants the **two-goat logic the witness lived** to carry more of the Christ-turn before Hebrews/Isaiah/Matthew do the heavy lifting. The listener gets `"That is why there were two"` as announcement, not a stepwise walk through how **one** Priest completes **both** halves without 1:1 live-goat = crucified-man geometry.

### EW-G8 — Wrestling: steel-man or strawman?

**Improved** vs earlier draft: Hebrews 10:3–4 now sit under Beat 6’s `"That later word says plainly what I had only feared"` frame, not inside pre-signpost interior monologue.

**Still not the fullest steel-man in Aaron’s own era.** Beat 2 gives you Nadab/Abihu and `"that he die not"` — the priest’s **strongest** lived dread is failing at the veil and being consumed. Beat 5 barely uses that; it pivots to annual repetition and `"I was only ever pointing"`. A faithful Aaron wrestling Lev 16:30 would also voice:

*God said we are cleansed this day — am I impious to feel it is never finished?*

You gesture at that (`The people went home clean`) but don’t **wrestle** the tension — you resolve it quickly into shadow/pointer language (`I was a sign. I did not yet know of what.`). That’s honest, but closer to essay foreshadowing than the hardest objection internalized in Aaron’s voice.
