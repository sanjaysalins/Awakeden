# Independent review — codex (OK, 33s)

**Findings**

1. **EW-INV-2 CTA violation: the close uses the tired bare template.**  
   The line **“will you come in?”** is close to the forbidden bare “will you trust Him?” pattern. It is better than vague trust alone because it later says **“Trust the One…”**, but the actual question still leans on a familiar pressure-close rather than a fresh, grace-anchored invitation.

2. **EW-G9 substitution is present, but the penalty is under-named.**  
   The piece says **“the price paid and the guilt carried off”** and **“He was the blood that paid the price”**, but it never clearly states what the price was: wrath, judgment, death, curse, or penalty for sin. For this lens, “price” is too clean and abstract. It needs to name that Christ bore the penalty sinners deserved.

3. **EW-G8 wrestling is honest, but not the strongest objection.**  
   The objection is mostly ritual incompleteness: **“A cleansing you must make again every year is true, but it is not done.”** That is valid, but the stronger internal crisis for Aaron would likely be more terrifying: “How can I, a sinful priest whose sons died before the LORD, stand between a holy God and a guilty people?” The piece gestures at this with **“I buried two of my own sons”** and **“I had to bring blood for my own sin”**, but then drops that steel-man and settles into a Hebrews-style explanation.

4. **Voice partially slips into sermon essay.**  
   Lines like **“what every Day of Atonement was always reaching toward”**, **“It was a shadow — and a shadow waits for the body that casts it”**, and **“both halves were finished in one Priest”** sound like a preacher-theologian explaining typology, not Aaron recounting what he lived. The witness frame weakens in the reveal.

5. **First-hearing clarity problem: “far side of my life” is ambiguous.**  
   **“I tell you now from the far side of my life — by the light that came long after I laid down my office”** will confuse a listener with zero Bible knowledge. Is Aaron dead? resurrected? narrating from heaven? The artifact’s “eyewitness” premise bends here without explaining the storytelling frame.

6. **KJV attribution is inconsistent.**  
   The first direct command is attributed: **“The LORD spoke it to my brother Moses…”** before the quote. But several later quotes are introduced with vague formulas rather than clear attribution: **“That later word says plainly…”**, **“for so the Psalm had promised…”**, and no explicit speaker/book attribution before **“there is a remembrance again made of sins every year.”** If the standard requires speaker/source attribution before every quote, these fail.

7. **Possible overclaim: “no hand will ever sew it shut again.”**  
   **“no hand will ever sew it shut again”** is rhetorically strong but not a quoted biblical claim. It is doctrinally defensible as access fulfilled in Christ, but as phrased it risks sounding like invented narrative detail about the physical veil rather than theological meaning.

8. **Long-form sag: Beat 7 repeats Beat 6 instead of turning.**  
   Beat 7 repeats already-established images: **“year after year”**, **“the curtain I trembled before”**, **“torn from top to bottom”**, **“one man one day a year.”** The invitation is clear, but the added runtime does not add a new emotional or theological turn. It re-says the reveal.

9. **Reveal is mostly earned, but partly announced.**  
   The two-goat logic is shown well through **“One I killed; the other I sent away alive”** and **“why two?”** But the transition **“The body came. There came a Man, Jesus…”** still jumps from ritual to fulfillment by assertion. The Hebrews and Isaiah quotes support it, but Aaron’s own lived scene could do more showing before the doctrinal conclusion lands.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Name the penalty Christ bore, not only the “price.”
2. Replace the closing question/template with a stronger named invitation to come/receive/rest in Jesus by faith.
3. Tighten the reveal and Beat 7 so Aaron’s voice stays embodied and the ending stops repeating the same veil/yearly-work beats.
