SHORT · Psalm 22:1 · narration.md
5-CLI panel: cursor: REVISE claude: REVISE gemini: — codex: REVISE grok: REVISE  reviews
narrative-fact — Matt 27:46 (cf. Luke 23:34, 23:43 — earlier sayings)
The phrase 'did not speak His own words first' reads as a chronological claim that the cry of dereliction (Matt 27:46) was Jesus' opening utterance from the cross. In the harmonized gospel record the dereliction cry is the FOURTH of the seven sayings; 'Father, forgive them' (Luke 23:34) and the word to the penitent thief ('To day shalt thou be with me in paradise', Luke 23:43) precede it. Matthew/Mark record only this cry, so 'first' is not supportable. The intended point is that the cry was borrowed Scripture rather than original speech — fix the wording so it does not assert a false order.
before
From the cross, Jesus did not speak His own words first — He cried a psalm David had written a thousand years before.
after
From the cross, in His darkest hour, Jesus reached for words already written — a psalm David had penned a thousand years before.
