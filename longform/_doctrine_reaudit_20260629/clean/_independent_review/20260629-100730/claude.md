I read the whole thing against KJV and for doctrine/clarity. This is a strong, careful piece — the typology is orthodox and the quotes are clean. Here's what survives adversarial scrutiny.

## Doctrine — sound
The two-goat typology (goat 1 = blood/price/propitiation behind the veil; scapegoat = guilt-borne-away/expiation; both fulfilled in Christ as priest-and-offering) is standard evangelical, not contrarian. It carefully keeps the *blood/price* on the slain goat and the *removal* on the live goat — it never claims the bloodless scapegoat made propitiation. It also avoids the contested "Azazel = demon/place" rabbit-hole entirely. The CTA is grace-anchored ("you do not have to keep paying… you have only to come"), no fear/gain-loss/works framing. Nadab/Abihu (Mvmt 1), Lev 16:16 stain-on-the-place (Mvmt 2), Lev 16:27 burned-outside (Mvmt 4) all grounded. Isaiah 53 "of the Messiah, seven centuries before" is the orthodox reading, not overclaim.

## Real findings (polish, not blockers)

**1. The hook foregrounds the wrong thread for a piece titled "The Two Goats."** Mvmt 1 opens on *one man, one day, one curtain* — the high-priest/veil thread. The titular two goats don't appear until Mvmt 3, ~a third in. There are effectively two braided threads (two-goats *and* veil/access); they're both faithful and both land on Christ, but the cold-open promises the veil, not the distinctive payload. For an engagement product, the freshest, most ownable image (one sin offering — *two* goats) should be previewed in the first breath, not held back.

**2. Mvmt 5's answer partly dodges the objection it raises.** The skeptic's charge is two-pronged: "primitive superstition" *and* "you're reading Christ *back* into a livestock ceremony." The reply (Heb 10:3–4: the ritual was self-confessedly provisional) crisply answers the *first* prong but only indirectly the *second*. The "you're retrojecting Christ" charge is actually answered back in Mvmt 4 (the *NT authors*, not us, draw the line) — Mvmt 5 should name that ("the line isn't ours — Hebrews and Isaiah drew it") so the rebuttal lands fully.

**3. Minor clarity nit for a zero-Bible listener.** "mercy seat" appears inside the Lev 16:2 quote and is leaned on, but is only obliquely glossed ("above the ark"). One five-word aside ("the gold lid over the ark, where God met them") would close the gap. "the ark" itself is also undefined.

None of these touch doctrine or KJV. They're the difference between a strong lock and a sharper one.

VERDICT: REVISE
