# Independent review — codex (OK, 26s)

Concrete findings:

- **KJV issue:** `“And he shall take ... two kids of the goats for a sin offering.”` truncates Leviticus 16:5 before `“and one ram for a burnt offering.”` That is acceptable only if partial quotations are allowed, but the punctuation turns a comma into a period. If the rule is strict KJV verbatim, flag the punctuation as non-verbatim.

- **Doctrinal overstatement:** `“Something innocent must die in the place of the guilty.”` risks flattening the sacrificial system into a simple penal-substitution formula at a point where Leviticus is also about purification/cleansing of the sanctuary. It is not heretical, but it is imprecise and makes the later `“stain ... on the very place”` claim less carefully connected.

- **Possible contested typology stated too strongly:** `“two goats, because one could not show both halves of what God does with sin”` is a plausible evangelical typological reading, but the wording presents an interpretive synthesis as the explicit reason for the ritual. Safer would be “together they show...” rather than “because.”

- **Over-clean “place fits” claim:** `“He died outside the city wall, where that day's offering was taken to be burned.”` compresses the connection too tightly. Hebrews explicitly connects Jesus suffering “without the gate” to the bodies burned “without the camp,” but “where that day's offering was taken” can sound geographically identical across tabernacle/temple/Jerusalem settings. The typology is biblical; the wording is overconfident.

- **Clarity problem for zero Bible knowledge:** `“mercy seat,” “ark,” “Most Holy Place,” “vail,” “scapegoat,” “without the gate”` arrive quickly. Some are explained, but “mercy seat” and “ark” are not. A totally new listener may follow the emotional outline but not the objects.

- **Hook is strong but generic:** `“Once a year — only once — a whole nation held its breath for a single man.”` grips, but it slightly oversells the drama with cinematic language before grounding the scene. Not a major failure, but “held its breath” is inferred rather than textual.

- **Landing partly reverts to familiar CTA:** `“You have only to come — to trust the One...”` and `“Come to Him”` are orthodox, but the final move is still close to the generic trust/come ending the prompt warned against. The stronger new work is `“nothing was left to add”`; the close should land more specifically on access through the torn veil and guilt carried away.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Tighten the typology so interpretive claims are framed as biblical fulfillment, not as the stated reason the ritual existed.
2. Fix strict KJV quotation handling, especially truncated verses with changed punctuation.
3. Make the closing do more specific work from the two-goat/torn-veil thread instead of settling into a generic “come/trust Him” CTA.
