# Independent review — claude (OK, 60s)

I read this as a hostile auditor. Doctrine is largely sound and both KJV quotes are exact — but there are real craft and one nuance issue.

**KJV verbatim — clean.**
- Psalm 22:1: "My God, my God, why hast thou forsaken me?" — exact (the quoted clause; the verse continues "why art thou so far…"). ✓
- Matthew 27:46: "My God, my God, why hast thou forsaken me?" — exact (the Greek-then-gloss verse's English gloss). ✓
- "at the ninth hour" — Matt 27:46 says "about the ninth hour"; dropping "about" is acceptable paraphrase in the narrator frame, not inside quotes. Fine.

**Doctrine — mostly sound, one nuance to tighten.**
- "the sinless Son taking our place, and bearing the forsaking that our sin deserved" — penal substitution, orthodox evangelical. ✓
- "even forsaken, He still calls God 'my God'" — careful, faithful reading; avoids the "the Trinity broke" overclaim. ✓
- "Psalm twenty-two opens in the voice of a forsaken man; Jesus made it His own" — well-guarded; doesn't flatten David into pure prediction. ✓
- ⚠️ **"He was forsaken so that you never will be."** Read flatly, this is an *unconditional* promise to every listener. The never-forsaken promise (Heb 13:5, "I will never leave thee, nor forsake thee") is covenantal — to those in Christ. The invitation that follows ("the way home is open") softens it, but the cause-effect sentence lands *before* the condition. Tie the promise to coming home so it can't be heard as "everyone is automatically safe."
- "David's very first line" — accurate; Psalm 22:1 is the opening line. (Note: it does NOT claim this was Jesus's first word from the cross — good, since it was the fourth saying. The prior re-audit "first" flag appears correctly resolved.)

**Craft problems (the real weaknesses).**
1. **Hook is a slow wind-up.** "From the cross, in His darkest hour, Jesus reached for words already written…" buries the actual grip — the *same words, a thousand years apart* — behind two expository clauses. The shocking fact arrives only after the second quote. Lead with the collision.
2. **Redundancy in the middle.** You show both identical quotes, then the narrator says "The very same words." — the visual/audio already made the point; the line is filler. Also "a thousand years before" + "A thousand years later" repeats the figure twice in close range.
3. **Freshness is thin.** The David→Jesus quote link is the most familiar Psalm 22 connection. It's faithful, but nothing here surprises about the *text* (e.g., that the psalm that opens forsaken ends in triumph/resurrection vindication — Ps 22:22ff — would add faithful depth and even feed the landing). Right now the depth is shallow for a "freshness = faithful depth" bar.

**Landing — good.** "He opened it from the dark" echoes "darkest hour" and does new work; not a generic "will you trust Him?" close. Grace-anchored, no fear/gain-loss framing. ✓

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Re-cut the hook to lead with the collision (same cry, a thousand years apart) instead of the slow "From the cross, in His darkest hour…" wind-up; move the grip into the first 5 seconds.
2. Tighten the CTA so "you never will be [forsaken]" is bound to the invitation/coming-home, not stated as an unconditional promise to every listener.
3. Cut the redundant "The very same words." line and the doubled "thousand years"; optionally add faithful depth by noting the same psalm ends in vindication (Ps 22:22ff) to lift freshness.
