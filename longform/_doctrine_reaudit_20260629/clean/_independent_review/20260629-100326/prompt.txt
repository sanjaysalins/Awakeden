# Joseph — Awakeden eyewitness (long)


## Beat 1 — I was there

I am Joseph, son of Jacob. And I stood at the right hand of Egypt's throne, with a famine swallowing the earth and the bread of nations under my seal.

For thirteen years before that, I had been a slave and a prisoner, sold by my own blood and left to rot in a dungeon. And now princes bowed as my chariot passed.

But on that day, ten travel-worn men came begging me for grain, not knowing the ruler they bowed to. They were my brothers — the very brothers who had sold me, whose faces I had carried in the dark for half my life. And when I could hold it in no longer, I sent every Egyptian out of the room, and I wept aloud until the house of Pharaoh heard me, and I said the words they had dreaded for twenty years:

**"I am Joseph your brother, whom ye sold into Egypt."**

## Beat 2 — The world

Let me tell you who I was before the pit. I was the son of my father's old age, and he did not hide that he loved me best.

**"Now Israel loved Joseph more than all his children, because he was the son of his old age: and he made him a coat of many colours."**

That long bright coat was a wound to my brothers every time I passed them in it.

**"And when his brethren saw that their father loved him more than all his brethren, they hated him, and could not speak peaceably unto him."**

And I, a boy, made it worse. God gave me dreams — sheaves bowing to my sheaf, the sun and moon and eleven stars bowing to me — and instead of holding my peace, I told them aloud to the very men those dreams said would one day kneel.

**"And his brethren said to him, Shalt thou indeed reign over us? or shalt thou indeed have dominion over us? And they hated him yet the more for his dreams, and for his words."**

I was seventeen, and did not yet know that a dream of rising can come true only by a long road down.

## Beat 3 — The act

Hear now what was done to me, for I lived every hour of it. My father sent me to my brothers in the field, and when they saw me coming in that coat, they seized me.

**"that they stript Joseph out of his coat, his coat of many colours that was on him"**

They tore the sign of my father's love off my back, threw me into a dry well, and sat down to eat while I cried up from the bottom of it.

**"And they took him, and cast him into a pit: and the pit was empty, there was no water in it."**

Then a caravan came over the hills, bound for Egypt, and my brother Judah set a price on his own brother's head.

**[Judah]** **"What profit is it if we slay our brother, and conceal his blood? Come, and let us sell him to the Ishmeelites, and let not our hand be upon him; for he is our brother and our flesh."**

And so my own flesh and blood handed me over for the price of a slave:

**"sold Joseph to the Ishmeelites for twenty pieces of silver"**

In Egypt it only grew darker. I served faithfully and was repaid with a lie, thrown into prison for the crime of my own innocence. And yet, in that pit beneath a pit, I was not alone.

**"But the LORD was with Joseph, and shewed him mercy, and gave him favour in the sight of the keeper of the prison."**

## Beat 4 — The strange detail

Here is the thing I could never explain, the riddle I turned over in the dark for years.

Every time I was thrust downward, the LORD turned the low place into a road that climbed. The pit carried me to Egypt. The slander carried me to prison. The prison carried me to the throne. And in one morning, a forgotten Hebrew convict was lifted from a cell to the side of Pharaoh himself.

**[Pharaoh]** **"Thou shalt be over my house, and according unto thy word shall all my people be ruled: only in the throne will I be greater than thou."**

**[Pharaoh]** **"See, I have set thee over all the land of Egypt."**

And there it was, the strangest thing of all: the boy's dream of his brothers bowing came true — but only because the road to the throne ran straight through the pit. I could see that it had happened, but not yet why it had to.

## Beat 5 — The wrestling

And I will be honest with you. There were long years when I did not feel like a man God was keeping, but a man God had forgotten.

The word you heard me quote says the LORD was with me in that prison — and it was true. But hear me: a man can be held by God and still feel let go. I had done no wrong, and I was sold. I had done right, and I was imprisoned. And I lay in the dark and wrestled the bitter question that every betrayed man knows by heart: where was God when my own brothers ate their bread while I screamed from the bottom of a pit? No answer came in the pit. The dreams did not return; the door did not open the next morning, nor the next year. There was only silence — and a faith I had to hold long before I was given any reason for it.

And then, hardest of all, the day came when the power was finally in my hand. Those same brothers stood trembling before me, starving, not knowing my face, and the sword of vengeance lay ready in my fist. One word from me, and I could have ended every one of them — and every wound in me screamed for it. That was the heaviest hour of my life. Not the pit. The choice in the palace, with my betrayers on their knees and my grief insisting they deserved to die.

## Beat 6 — The reveal

I did not raise that sword. I forgave them, and told them what I had come to see — that a hand far greater than my brothers' had been working through all their evil from the very start.

**"But as for you, ye thought evil against me; but God meant it unto good, to bring to pass, as it is this day, to save much people alive."**

I gave them bread, and kept them alive through the famine. But I tell you now, by a light given long after me, a word written after I was laid in my grave, that my whole life had been a shadow thrown ahead of Someone greater. I saved bodies from a famine of bread, but I could not reach the deeper hunger underneath; I could keep my brothers breathing, but I could not lift the guilt off a single soul.

There came a Man, Jesus, and the very shape of my life was the shadow of His. He came to His own people, and His own would not receive Him.

**"He came unto his own, and his own received him not."**

I was sold by my brothers for twenty pieces of silver; He was sold by Judas, one of His own twelve, for thirty.

**"And said unto them, What will ye give me, and I will deliver him unto you? And they covenanted with him for thirty pieces of silver."**

He was falsely condemned, then brought lower than any pit I knew — down into death itself. And as I was raised from prison to Pharaoh's right hand to keep one nation alive, God raised Him from the grave to the right hand of heaven to save the world. So the word written long after me says of Him:

**"Him hath God exalted with his right hand to be a Prince and a Saviour, for to give repentance to Israel, and forgiveness of sins."**

And here is the wonder that breaks me still: like me, He turned and saved the very ones who sold and slew Him. Over the men driving the nails, He prayed:

**"Father, forgive them; for they know not what they do."**

What I could only do with bread, He did with His own blood. I told my brothers their evil had been meant unto good; He took the worst evil ever done — His own murder — and made it the price of our pardon, dying in the place of the sinners who hated Him. That same word says it plainly:

**"But God commendeth his love toward us, in that, while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us."**

## Beat 7 — The invitation

So hear me now, you who listen long after — be still with me a moment.

I know what it cost. When my betrayers knelt before me, I held bread, not a sword — and forgiveness was the heaviest thing my hands ever lifted. I could feed them, but I could not lift the guilt off one soul. I could not. He can.

You have wronged Him too — we are the brothers who sold Him. Yet He does not wait with a sword, but stands with arms thrown wide, the marks still in them.

Sit with that. He carried your guilt down where my hands could never reach — into death itself — and rose holding pardon for the hands that nailed Him.

So I leave you the question my life kept asking — now with no sword behind it, no fear:

**"Fear not: for am I in the place of God?"**

I was only the shadow; He is the substance. Trust Jesus, the Brother who holds out bread, not a sword — not when you are clean enough, but now, as you are.
