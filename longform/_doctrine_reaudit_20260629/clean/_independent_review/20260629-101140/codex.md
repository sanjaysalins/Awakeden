# Independent review — codex (OK, 26s)

Findings:

- **EW-INV-4: KJV quote passes.** The quoted line, **"Behold the Lamb of God, which taketh away the sin of the world."**, matches KJV John 1:29 exactly. Attribution comes before it: “A man named John pointed:”. Minor clarity issue: for first-hearing listeners, “John” should probably be “John the Baptist,” but this is not a KJV violation.

- **EW-G9: substitution is undernamed for Christ.** The ram substitution is clear: “it died in my son's place.” But Christ’s substitution is mostly implied: “He was the Lamb” and “taketh away the sin of the world.” The lens asks that “the penalty + what Christ bore” be named. This never plainly says Jesus bore our sin, judgment, curse, wrath, or penalty in our place.

- **EW-G9: “No voice stopped His Father’s hand” is risky shorthand.** The phrase is vivid, but it can blur agency and sound like the Father personally “knifed” the Son. It needs more careful doctrinal phrasing: the Father gave the Son; the Son willingly offered Himself; sinners’ penalty fell on Him.

- **EW-G8: the wrestling is too thin.** Abraham’s strongest internal objection is not really voiced. “God gave me one son… then told me…” is gripping, but the piece does not steel-man the agony: How can God keep His promise through Isaac if Isaac dies? How can the God who gave the son command his death? The artifact narrates the crisis but does not internalize the strongest objection.

- **EW-G7: reveal is mostly earned, but the turn leans on assertion at the key point.** The type is shown well through “wood on Isaac’s back,” “where the lamb was,” and “died in my son’s place.” But “because He was the Lamb” is more announced than demonstrated. The piece should more directly connect John’s quote to Abraham’s unanswered question.

- **First-hearing clarity: “Lay your knife down” is unclear.** The phrase sounds forceful, but a listener with zero Bible knowledge may not know what action it means. The CTA does name “receive Him,” which is good, but “Lay your knife down” risks becoming a vague metaphor instead of a clear gospel invitation.

- **Voice: strong but slightly over-polished.** Lines like “The Lamb God provided has a name” sound more like a preacher’s crafted close than Abraham’s living voice. The first-person witness is mostly consistent, but the ending slips toward sermon voice.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Name Christ’s substitution explicitly: the penalty for sin and what Jesus bore in our place.
2. Strengthen Abraham’s real internal wrestling around Isaac, promise, and God’s command.
3. Make the CTA clearer and less metaphor-dependent while keeping “receive Him” named.
