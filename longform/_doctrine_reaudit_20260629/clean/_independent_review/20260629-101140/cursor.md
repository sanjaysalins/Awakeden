## EW-G8 — Wrestling (steel-man): **FAIL**

The hook states crisis (*“God gave me one son… then told me to carry the knife up a mountain and kill him”*) but **never voices Abraham’s strongest internal objection in his own voice**.

Missing steel-man (the real agony of Genesis 22 + Hebrews 11):

- *How can God keep His promise through Isaac if Isaac dies on that altar?*
- *Did the God who swore “in Isaac shall thy seed be called” just undo His own word?*

Beat 2 runs the action (*bound him, raised the knife*) without one sentence of lived doubt. That is **narrated crisis, not wrestled crisis** — a strawman-sized gap where the piece needs the genuine objection internalized. SHORT form compresses beats, but the lens still asks for steel-man; this doesn’t deliver it.

## EW-G9 — Doctrine: **REVISE-level problems**

**Ram substitution — clear:** *“it died in my son's place.”* Good.

**Christ substitution — undernamed:** The lens requires **penalty + what Christ bore**. You get *“taketh away the sin of the world”* (quoted) and *“He was the Lamb”* (asserted). You never plainly say **our sin/judgment/death fell on Him in our place** the way the ram’s death is named for Isaac. Compare the bar set elsewhere in this series (e.g. Joseph short: *“dying in the place of the sinners who hated Him”*) — this piece stops at typology and quote, not substitution spelled out.

**Typological foundation weakened by paraphrase:** Genesis 22:8 KJV has Abraham say God will provide **“himself”** a lamb. The artifact flattens this to *“I told him God would provide one.”* That drops the divine self-provision hint — the deepest layer of “the lamb I spoke of was never found” — and replaces it with generic provision language Abraham could have meant literally.

**Risky shorthand:** *“No voice stopped His Father's hand”* — vivid parallel to Moriah, but doctrinally sloppy. It can sound like the Father personally executed the Son (knife/hand imagery carried from Abraham’s scene). The Father **gave** the Son; the Son **offered Himself**; sinners’ penalty **fell on Him** — that nuance isn’t here, and the line blurs agency.

**No supersession, no contrarian reading** — credit where due.

## EW-G7 — Reveal earned: **mixed / leaning REVISE**

**Earned well from Abraham’s lived details:**

- Wood on the son’s back.
- *“where the lamb was”* vs ram in the thicket.
- *“died in my son's place”* vs *“No ram was caught for Him.”*
- *“the lamb I spoke of was never found on that mountain”* → *“I came down delivered, and still waiting.”*

**Unearned at the pivot:** *“because He was the Lamb”* is **announced**, not demonstrated from Abraham’s mountain. The John quote is the real reveal engine, but it arrives **after** Abraham has already named Jesus, the hill, and the Father’s non-stopping hand — so Beat 3 partly **asserts the fulfillment before the text proves it**.

**Chronology undercuts the earn in Beat 4:** *“My boy asked me: where is the lamb?”* repeats Isaac’s question **after** *“Generations later… A man named John pointed.”* A first-time listener can’t tell whether Abraham is back on Moriah, speaking prophetically, or breaking frame. The callback is rhetorically clever but **muddies the single-moment spine**.

## EW-INV-2 — CTA on Jesus

**Partial pass.** Names Jesus; names the response (*“receive Him”*); avoids bare *“will you trust Him?”*; no obvious fear/gain-loss pressure.

Problems:

- *“Lay your knife down”* — **opaque for a zero-Bible listener.** Whose knife? Lay down what? Abraham held the knife; the audience didn’t. Metaphor without setup reads as vague imperative, not gospel clarity.
- *“who climbed the mountain you never could”* — substitution hint is OK, but stacked on Moriah + knife + mountain metaphors **without explaining what the “mountain” is** (God’s holy demand? death we owed?). Risk of sounding like **performance gap** (“you couldn’t climb”) rather than grace-anchored invitation.
- *“The Lamb God provided has a name”* — polished sermon cadence; less Abraham-at-the-fire than preacher closing.

## EW-G10 — Voice / character: **slips at the turn**

Beat 1–2: credible Abraham — *“my only son,”* *“my boy,”* *“still waiting.”*

Beat 3 breaks witness frame: *“Generations later, another beloved Son — Jesus… A man named John pointed.”* Abraham didn’t live to see John or Golgotha. ## First-hearing clarity

A listener with no Bible background gets:

- A gripping father-and-son sacrifice story (clear).
- Then a sudden stack: **Jesus, John, Lamb of God, sin of the world, mountain you never could, lay your knife down** — **without routing any of it through explained links.**

Weakest links: who John is; what “receive Him” receives them **from**; what “lay your knife down” means.

## Minor factual / texture flags

- *“carry the knife up a mountain”* — Genesis 22 commands the offering; the knife is dramatized, not quoted commanded language.
