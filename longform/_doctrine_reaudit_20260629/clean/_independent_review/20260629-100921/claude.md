## The reveal is earned (EW-G7) — STRONG
The hinge is genuinely text-grounded, not announced: the nearer kinsman refuses "**lest I mar mine own inheritance**" (Ruth 4:6) → Christ "was willing to be **marred**." That single word ties the lived act to the cross from within the text. The genealogy Obed→Jesse→David→Bethlehem→Jesus is the literal bridge. Earned.

## Doctrine (EW-G9) — SOUND
Substitution is named with both halves: penalty ("the penalty was a curse") and what He bore ("the curse that should have fallen on us fell on Him instead" + Gal 3:13). Incarnation-as-kinsman basis is correct (He "took on our flesh and became our near kinsman"). No supersession — "the outsider brought all the way in" is Gentile inclusion, not replacement.

## Where it's actually weak (the real findings)

**1. Chronology seam between Beat 3 and Beat 5.** Beat 3 projects instant resolve — "I would have done it **that hour**." Then Beat 5 announces "I wrestled **before** I went up to that gate." A close listener feels a retcon: was he eager at the threshing floor or wrestling at the gate? It can be psychologically true (heart says yes at night, head wrestles by day), but as written the wrestling arrives as news after we already watched him act with full confidence. The steel-man (EW-G8) is genuine (the Moab/tenth-generation reproach, Deut 23:3, is the real objection) — but it loses force landing *after* the deed is done.

**2. The nearer-kinsman's cost-refusal is used twice.** Beat 4 ("He could do arithmetic… he would not spend himself") and Beat 5 ("do exactly what the nearer kinsman did") run the same beat — costly redemption refused — from two angles. The thread is one (cost) but the device repeats, which is the closest thing to sag in an otherwise tight piece.

**3. Bride applied to the individual.** "gives you His name and brings you home as His **bride**" (CTA) and "took the redeemed to be His own bride, the way I took Ruth." Eph 5 makes the *church corporately* the bride; mapping it onto the individual "you" is common evangelical shorthand but slightly imprecise. Low severity — flag, not a blocker.

**4. Length / a missed textual beat.** Reads ~1,300 words → ~8.5–9 min, the low end of the long target. The text itself crowns the redemption around **Naomi** ("a restorer of thy life," Ruth 4:14–15) — a redemption beat the narration omits entirely while leaning all-in on Ruth-as-bride. Adding it would deepen, not pad.

## CTA (EW-INV-2) — PASS
Names Jesus, invites concretely ("let the Redeemer pay what you cannot, and be His own… Come to Jesus… Be redeemed"), grace-anchored ("The redeemed never pay"), explicitly disclaims fear ("now without fear"). Not the bare "will you trust Him?" template.

## Voice (EW-G10) & first-hearing clarity — PASS
Consistent, characterful Boaz ("with my own hands," "the riddle I turned over for the rest of my days"). A zero-Bible listener can follow it — the threshing-floor request, the shoe custom, and "redeem" are all explained in-line.

Net: doctrine sound and Bible-grounded, KJV airtight — the project's non-negotiable bar is met. The remaining issues are craft polish, not faithfulness errors.

VERDICT: PASS
TOP FIXES:
1. Resolve the Beat 3 / Beat 5 chronology seam — either move the wrestling before the gate-confidence, or have Beat 3 acknowledge the unresolved cost so Beat 5's doubt doesn't read as a retcon.
2. Differentiate Beat 4 and Beat 5 so the nearer-kinsman's cost-refusal isn't run twice; let Beat 5 carry the Moab/law tension alone.
3. Earn the full long-form runtime by adding the text's own Naomi-restoration beat ("a restorer of thy life," Ruth 4:14–15), and tighten the individual-as-"bride" phrasing toward the corporate church.
