# Independent review — grok (OK, 38s)

## Findings

### Doctrinal soundness

**"From the cross, Jesus did not speak His own words first — He cried a psalm David had written a thousand years before."**

This is the biggest problem. Luke records Jesus’ first spoken words on the cross as *"Father, forgive them"* (Luke 23:34), before the ninth-hour cry. So “did not speak His own words first” is not safe as a blanket claim. It only holds if you silently ignore Luke and treat Matthew/Mark as the whole story.

Also, quoting Psalm 22 on the cross **is** Jesus speaking. Calling it “not His own words” blurs the line between David’s psalm, messianic prophecy, and Christ’s voice. That is a clever hook, but it risks sounding like Jesus was a mouthpiece for someone else’s lines, not the Word fulfilling Scripture.

**"It was the sinless Son taking our place, and bearing the forsaking that our sin deserved."**

Broadly orthodox substitution language. Fine for evangelical shorts. A picky reviewer might want more care around “God forsook Jesus” (Trinitarian mystery), but this line is standard and not invented doctrine.

**"He was forsaken so that you never will be."**

The logic is gospel-shaped, but it states an absolute promise without any guardrail. Psalm 22 itself is a cry *while* forsaken-feeling, not a denial that believers ever feel abandoned. A listener who feels forsaken today may hear this as contradiction, not comfort.

---

### KJV verbatim

Both quoted lines look exact KJV:

- Psalm 22:1: *"My God, my God, why hast thou forsaken me?"* — correct.
- Matthew 27:46: *"My God, my God, why hast thou forsaken me?"* — correct.

No altered words inside quotation marks.

---

### Clarity on first hearing

**"did not speak His own words first"** — confusing on first pass. A zero-Bible listener will not know what counts as “His own words” vs a psalm He is speaking.

**"Jesus made it His own"** — vague. Made what own? The psalm? The forsaken cry? The suffering?

**"at the ninth hour"** — time-code jargon. Many listeners will not know that means roughly 3 p.m.

**"The very same words"** after giving the line twice — clear enough, but slightly redundant for a 60s short.

The David → Jesus → substitution thread is followable. The opening conceit is what muddies it.

---

### Grace-anchored conviction

Mostly invites rather than threatens.

**"However far you've run, the way home is open"** — grace-anchored, no works, no fear lever. Good.

**"He was forsaken so that you never will be"** — mild benefit framing (“so that you…”), but normal gospel indicative, not a scare tactic. Acceptable here.

No gain/loss manipulation or “trust or else” pressure.

---

### Freshness = faithful depth

The intended freshness is the opening line: Jesus’ first cross words are a thousand-year-old psalm.

That is surprising about the **text** — but it leans on a contested factual setup (Luke order) and a fuzzy “own words” distinction. Freshness here trades on a shaky foundation.

**"even forsaken, He still calls God 'my God.'"** — solid, but a very common sermon point. Not wrong; not especially fresh.

**"He opened it from the dark"** — the best fresh image in the piece. Ties cross-darkness to an open way home.

---

### One thread spine

Single thread holds: Psalm 22 on the cross → same words → substitution → open way home.

No thread-swapping.

---

### Landing does new work

**"However far you've run, the way home is open — and He opened it from the dark."**

Better than a bare “will you trust Him?” It echoes forsakenness/darkness from the middle.

Still partly familiar prodigal/runner language. The “from the dark” line does the real new work; the first half is more stock.

---

### Hook grips in first ~5 seconds

The cross + “did not speak His own words first” is attention-grabbing.

But grip comes with confusion and a factual snag. A sharp listener may disengage rather than lean in.

---

VERDICT: REVISE

TOP FIXES:
1. Replace **"Jesus did not speak His own words first"** — it conflicts with Luke 23:34 and confuses whose words Jesus is speaking; anchor the hook on fulfillment (“David wrote it; Jesus cried it from the cross”) without the “not His own words” trick.
2. Nuance **"He was forsaken so that you never will be"** so it does not flatly deny that believers can cry out feeling forsaken; keep Psalm 22’s “my God” faith-in-the-cry as the model.
3. Swap **"at the ninth hour"** for plain speech (e.g. “hours into the crucifixion”) so a first-time listener is not lost on Bible time-code.
