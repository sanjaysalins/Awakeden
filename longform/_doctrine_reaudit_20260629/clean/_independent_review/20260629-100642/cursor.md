## Independent adversarial review — *The Forsaken Cry*

### Doctrinal soundness

**Hook is a factual overreach.**  
*"From the cross, Jesus did not speak His own words first — He cried a psalm David had written a thousand years before."*  

On a plain hearing, **"first"** means chronology. In the harmonized passion record, Jesus speaks **before** the ninth-hour cry: e.g. *"Father, forgive them"* (Luke 23:34) and *"To day shalt thou be with me in paradise"* (Luke 23:43). Matthew/Mark only report the dereliction cry, but the narration does not signal it is using a Matthean-only timeline — so the hook teaches a sequence the Gospels do not support. That is not a clever fresh angle; it is a **grounding error** dressed as a hook.

**"Jesus made it His own"** (*"Psalm twenty-two opens in the voice of a forsaken man; Jesus made it His own."*) tilts toward **appropriation**, not **fulfillment**. Evangelical Christology here is that David’s psalm was written *about* this moment; Jesus did not adopt someone else’s lament as a convenient line. Wording matters: *made it His own* sounds like creative reuse, not prophetic completion.

**"bearing the forsaking that our sin deserved"** is broadly orthodox (penal substitution), but compressed in a 60s piece it can imply the Father **stopped relating to** the Son in a way that sounds like Trinitarian rupture. The line does not say *judgment*, *curse*, *wrath borne*, or *in our place* with enough precision to keep a careful listener from hearing ontological abandonment. Not heresy — but **doctrinal compression risk**.

**"He was forsaken so that you never will be"** is standard evangelical comfort (cf. Heb 13:5), but it **overpromises on first hearing**. Believers still cry out forsaken (this psalm is proof). The line only works if the listener already knows you mean *eternally/separation-from-God* forsakenness. A zero-Bible-knowledge listener may hear a contradiction: *If He was forsaken for me, why do suffering Christians still feel abandoned?*

No contrarian readings or invented doctrine beyond the hook’s chronology problem.

### Clarity on first hearing

**Worst offender:** *"did not speak His own words first."* A listener with no Bible background cannot tell whether **first** means time order, importance, or “not original speech.” All three readings lead to different meanings; two are wrong.

**"at the ninth hour"** assumes crucifixion-time literacy. Secular listeners may not connect it to darkness / near-death without a half-beat of context.

**"The very same words"** is clear and strong — good middle beat.

**Logic gap:** Hook claims He did not speak His own words → body shows Him speaking → explanation jumps to substitution without ever resolving what *own words* meant. The conceit is **clever-but-confusing**, not crisp.

---

### Grace-anchored conviction

Mostly clean. No fear lever, no works ladder, no “do better” CTA.

*"However far you've run, the way home is open"* is invitation-shaped, not guilt-shaped — pass on grace tone.

Minor note: *"bearing the forsaking that our sin deserved"* is **descriptive** theology, not manipulative, but it is the most **punishment-flavored** line in an otherwise gentle close. Acceptable in context; not the main problem.

---

### Freshness = faithful depth

The **David → Jesus verbatim echo** is faithful and central — but it is also **the default sermon move** for Psalm 22:1 / Matthew 27:46. Not clickbait, but not deeply fresh.

The one attempt at freshness — the hook’s *own words / first* framing — misfires factually and clarity-wise.

Faithful depth **left on the table:** Psalm 22 does not end in forsakenness. The psalm that opens with this cry moves to vindication and praise. This short stops at verse 1. Even as a distilled slice, the landing could have done **psalm-specific** work (e.g. the cry that begins in dereliction is the same psalm that ends in declaration) instead of a generic *way home* appeal. That would be surprising about the **text** without bending doctrine.

---

### One thread spine (hook → middle → CTA)

Thread is coherent: **David’s opening line → Jesus’ cross cry → substitution → you won’t be ultimately forsaken.** No mid-script thread swap.

But hook and middle **fight slightly**: hook stresses *not His own words / first*; middle stresses *identical words across a millennium*. Those are related ideas that were never welded into one clear sentence.

---

### Landing — does new work?

**Partial pass, mostly fail on constitution’s portability test.**

*"He was forsaken so that you never will be"* — clear substitution payoff; connects to the thread.

*"However far you've run, the way home is open — and He opened it from the dark"* — **"However far you've run, the way home is open"** could close a prodigal-son short, a repentance short, or half the catalogue unchanged. *"from the dark"* nods to Golgotha/darkness but is vague — not a concrete psalm-22 image (e.g. *my God* held in forsakenness, or the psalm’s turn toward praise).

Not a banned *"Will you trust Him?"* — but still **heard-before** and not tightly earned from **this** verse’s unique detail.

---

### Hook — first ~5 seconds

**Grabs attention, fails honesty/clarity.**  
The opening line is scroll-stopping — then a knowledgeable listener immediately tripped by **first**, and a naive listener confused by **own words**. A strong hook that **mis-teaches** is worse than a dull accurate one.

---

VERDICT: REVISE  
TOP FIXES:  
1. Rewrite the hook so it does not imply the dereliction cry was Jesus’ first cross utterance — state plainly that He cried **Scripture David wrote**, not that He did not speak “His own words first.”  
2. Replace *"Jesus made it His own"* with fulfillment language (prophesied / written for this hour / David’s opening line became the Son’s cry) and tighten *"forsaken"* so it reads as **judgment borne in our place**, not Father-Son relational break.  
3. Rebuild the landing from **Psalm 22’s specific material** (e.g. forsaken cry still says *my God*; or the psalm’s arc toward vindication) — cut or rework the portable *"way home"* close so the last line could not belong to another episode.
