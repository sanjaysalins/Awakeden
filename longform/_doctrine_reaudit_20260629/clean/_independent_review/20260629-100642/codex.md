# Independent review — codex (OK, 23s)

Concrete findings:

1. **Potentially inaccurate hook:** “Jesus did not speak His own words first” is confusing. “First” sounds chronological, but this was not Jesus’ first saying from the cross in the Gospel sequence. If “first” means “not original to Him,” say that plainly.

2. **Overstated agency:** “Jesus made it His own” could imply He repurposed David’s words rather than fulfilled Scripture. Better to frame it as fulfillment, not appropriation.

3. **Doctrinal compression risk:** “bearing the forsaking that our sin deserved” is broadly evangelical, but in a short narration it can sound like the Father stopped loving or abandoned the Son ontologically. Add precision if possible: judgment, curse, wrath, or place-bearing without implying rupture in the Trinity.

4. **Landing is familiar:** “He was forsaken so that you never will be” is clear and orthodox, but “However far you’ve run, the way home is open” is a fairly generic gospel close. It does not do much new work from Psalm 22 specifically.

5. **KJV check:** The quoted lines appear verbatim KJV for the quoted portion: “My God, my God, why hast thou forsaken me?” No altered words found inside the main Scripture quotation marks.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Fix “did not speak His own words first” so it does not sound chronologically false.
2. Clarify “forsaken” so it does not imply a break in the Trinity.
3. Make the landing arise more specifically from Psalm 22 rather than a generic return-home appeal.
