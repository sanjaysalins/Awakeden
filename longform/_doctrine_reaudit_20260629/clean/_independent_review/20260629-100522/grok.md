# Independent review — grok (OK, 41s)

**KJV verbatim + attribution (EW-INV-4)**  
- The quoted line is **exact partial KJV** (Isaiah 53:6): **"and the LORD hath laid on him the iniquity of us all."**  
- **FAIL on attribution:** Nothing names who spoke/wrote it before the quote. Beat 3 jumps from *"Long after me a Man named Jesus did what my two goats only pictured"* straight into Isaiah with no *"as the prophet said"* / *"as it was written"* — so the listener cannot tell if this is the priest’s words, Scripture, or Jesus speaking.

**CTA-on-Jesus (EW-INV-2)**  
- **Mostly passes:** *"Jesus is already inside. Walk in."* names Jesus and gives a concrete response (enter/come), grace-toned, no fear or gain-loss.  
- **Minor weakness:** *"Walk in"* leans on temple imagery; a zero-Bible listener may not connect *"walk in"* to *faith in Christ* — it’s spatial, not clearly *to Him*.

**Reveal earned (EW-G7)**  
- **Announced, not shown:** *"I know now. No single creature could hold both halves."* is exactly the flagged pattern — insight declared, not walked out from the lived ritual.  
- The years of asking (*"For years I asked why it took two."*) never get a beat where the **two-goat day itself** teaches the answer; the logic is asserted in summary.  
- **Anachronism without bridge:** *"The curtain I trembled behind tore in two the hour He died."* — this witness lived centuries before the crucifixion. *"Long after me"* admits the gap but does not explain **how** he knows the tear happened *"the hour He died"* — so the reveal feels imported, not witnessed.

**Wrestling / steel-man (EW-G8)**  
- **Thin objection:** *"For years I asked why it took two."* is a curiosity about ritual design, not the priest’s real terror. Beat 1 sets stronger steel: *"I was never sure I would walk back out"* — but Beat 2–3 never wrestle **“Did this actually make me right with God?”** / **“What if I’m not clean enough?”** / **“Does driving a goat away really remove guilt?”**  
- The strongest line in the piece is abandoned for a weaker puzzle.

**Doctrine (EW-G9)**  
- **Passes core typology:** substitution is named — *"He was the price paid, and the One the guilt was laid on"* plus the Isaiah line; one Christ for both goats; *"once"* / *"Finished"* — sound, not supersessionist.  
- **Small fuzz:** *"He carried it away Himself"* blurs the scapegoat (priest **drove** it out) vs Christ; still defensible typologically, but slightly loose on the ritual detail.

**Voice / character (EW-G10)**  
- Beat 1 is strong first-person: *"Once a year I walked behind a curtain to meet God — and I was never sure I would walk back out."*  
- Beat 3–4 slip into essay voice: *"I know now."*, *"I was only the sign; He is the substance."*, *"no hand will ever sew it shut"* — polished sermon lines, not a trembling priest remembering **one** Yom Kippur.

**First-hearing clarity**  
- Two-goat ritual is clear (*"One to pay. One to carry it away."*).  
- The Isaiah quote lands without setup — a new listener won’t know why those words appear or who said them.

**Hook / spine**  
- Hook grips. One witness, one thread (two goats → Christ). **No thread swap.**  
- **Sag / repeat:** *"The curtain I trembled behind"* three times; Beat 3 *"tore in two"* and Beat 4 *"torn from top to bottom"* say the same beat twice.

**Short form**  
- ~195–200 spoken words — **under** the ~220–320 band; fine for “punchy” but thin for ~90s.  
- Single moment mostly holds; curtain reprise in Beat 4 is the main crowding.

---

VERDICT: REVISE  
TOP FIXES:  
1. Attribute the KJV quote before **"and the LORD hath laid on him the iniquity of us all."** (e.g. name Isaiah / “as it was written”) so EW-INV-4 is satisfied and new listeners aren’t lost.  
2. Replace *"I know now"* with a reveal **shown from the ritual** (e.g. one goat dies, one bears sin away — neither alone finishes the job) and either cut or justify *"the hour He died"* so the priest isn’t an unexplained NT eyewitness.  
3. Steel-man the doubt already in the hook (*"never sure I would walk back out"*) instead of the soft *"why it took two"* — and trim the repeated curtain lines so the close lands once, with a clearer grace-anchored invite **to Jesus** (not only “walk in”).
