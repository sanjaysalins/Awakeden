# Independent review — claude (OK, 80s)

I read the artifact closely against the KJV source (Leviticus 16; Isaiah 53:6; the Synoptic veil; Hebrews 10). It's strong, tight (~215 words), the voice is consistent, the typology is sound and non-supersessionist. But there are two real flags — one of them the exact class of error your panel caught on EW01 before.

**1. Factual over-claim — "I … drove it into the desert" (Beat 2).** Lev 16:21-22 is explicit: the high priest *laid his hands* and *confessed*, then "shall send him away **by the hand of a fit man** into the wilderness… and **he** shall let go the goat in the wilderness." The high priest did NOT personally drive/lead the scapegoat out — a designated man did. The witness claiming "I… drove it into the desert to be lost" assigns to himself an action Scripture assigns to another. This is the same defect pattern as the prior EW01 catch ("I carried them out myself" vs. Lev 10:4-7). Verbatim-clean ≠ factually-grounded. Fix: "I laid my hands on its head, confessed every sin of the people over it — and it was led away into the desert to be lost."

**2. Attribution gap on the KJV quote (Beat 3).** **"and the LORD hath laid on him the iniquity of us all."** is exact KJV (Isaiah 53:6 — verified verbatim, LORD caps correct). But EW-INV-4 requires the speaker/source be attributed *before* quoting, and the lead-in ("the One the guilt was laid on:") never says who wrote it. To a zero-Bible listener it's a floating sentence with no source. Fix: name it — e.g. "the prophet Isaiah had already seen it:" before the quote.

**3. Minor — "two goats for one sin" (Beat 2).** Loose. Lev 16:5 makes the two goats together "a sin offering" for *all* the people's sins, not "one sin." Tighten to "two goats for the sins of a whole nation" or "two goats, one sin offering."

Lesser notes (not blocking):
- **Wrestling (EW-G8) is a bit thin.** "For years I asked why it took two" is an honest *intellectual* question, but the deeper steel-man for a high priest is efficacy — the dread of *coming back every year* because the blood never finished it (Heb 10:1-4, 11). The "once… and sat down. Finished." payoff would land harder if the doubt were "did any of it ever truly take sin away?" rather than only "why two?" Optional.
- **CTA "Walk in"** passes — names Jesus, grace-anchored, no fear/gain-loss, not the tired "will you trust Him?" template — but the named response is an *entry* metaphor. It's concrete enough; fine as is.

The hook grips ("I was never sure I would walk back out"), the spine holds one witness/one thread, the reveal is earned from the two-goat structure, and the substitution is named (price paid + guilt laid on + carried away). The two flags above are the gate-failures.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Beat 2 — remove the false personal action "I … drove it into the desert"; Scripture (Lev 16:21) has the scapegoat led away "by the hand of a fit man," not by the high priest. Reword to "it was led away into the desert."
2. Beat 3 — attribute the Isaiah 53:6 quote before dropping it (e.g. "the prophet Isaiah had already seen it:"), per EW-INV-4.
3. Beat 2 — fix "two goats for one sin": it was one sin *offering* for the sins of the whole people, not a single sin.
