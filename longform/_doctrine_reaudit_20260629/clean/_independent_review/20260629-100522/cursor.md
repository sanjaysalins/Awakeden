### EW-INV-4 — KJV verbatim + attribution

**Verbatim:** The sole quoted span — **"and the LORD hath laid on him the iniquity of us all."** — matches Isaiah 53:6 KJV exactly (mid-verse fragment). No altered words inside the marks.

**Attribution: FAIL.** Nothing names the source before the quote. The lead-in is witness paraphrase only:

> *"Long after me a Man named Jesus did what my two goats only pictured — He was the price paid, and the One the guilt was laid on:"*

There is no *“Isaiah wrote”*, *“the prophet said”*, or `[scripture]` tag. A zero-Bible listener cannot route voice or book. Under strict EW-INV-4, one unattributed quote is enough to fail the gate.

**Binding-text gap:** The witness’s core ritual (Leviticus 16) is entirely paraphrased — no KJV from the passage Aaron actually lived. Beat 2’s *“I laid my hands on its head, confessed every sin of the people over it, and drove it into the desert”* tracks Lev 16:21–22, but none of it is quoted or attributed. For an eyewitness piece, the lived text never enters the room as Scripture.

**Unmarked KJV leakage:** *“torn from top to bottom”* (Beat 4) is Matthew 27:51 language used as narration, not inside quote marks — fine if intentional, but it blurs witness memory vs. later gospel record without the honest cross-time frame other drafts use (*“by a light given long after me”*).

### EW-G8 — Wrestling (steel-man): effectively absent

The only doubt line is:

> *"For years I asked why it took two."*

That is a **curiosity**, not a steel-man. It never voices:
- the hook’s terror (*“never sure I would walk back out”* — planted Beat 1, **never returned**),
- impiety at unfinished cleansing (Lev 16:30 *“clean this day”* vs. doing it all again next year),
- fear of getting the rite wrong with God’s life in your hands,
- or why a **live** goat sent to Azazel can “carry” sin at all.

Beat 2’s *“why it took two”* resolves in Beat 3 with *“I know now”* — the objection is already the sermon’s answer. No unresolved weight, no dramatized memory (one specific year, hands on the goat’s head, blood on the fingers). **EW-G8 fails.**

SHORT compresses to hook → act → reveal → invitation, but it does not eliminate wrestling — it must compress it. Here it was cut entirely.

### EW-G7 — Reveal earned?

**Partially set up, mostly asserted.**

**Earned:** Beat 2’s *“One to pay. One to carry it away.”* — clean type logic from what Aaron lived.

**Asserted / announced:**
- *"I know now."* — hindsight declaration, not derivation.
- *"No single creature could hold both halves."* — theological thesis in essay voice; not shown step-by-step from Leviticus.
- *"Long after me a Man named Jesus did what my two goats only pictured"* — typology stated, not walked out from Aaron’s text.
- *"He sat down. Finished."* — Hebrews 10:11–12 imported without source, attribution, or bridge from the goats.
- *"The curtain I trembled behind tore in two the hour He died."* — gospel event asserted into Aaron’s mouth with no framing.

**Missed in-period anchor:** Lev 16:10 — the live goat is presented *“to make an atonement with him.”* That line would let the two-halves reveal stay inside Aaron’s chapter longer. Instead the turn jumps straight to Isaiah + curtain tear.

The reveal **names** Christ; it does not **show** the type→fulfillment path from the witness’s lived detail the way Beat 4 of the long-form draft did (*“one sin offering — yet two goats”*).

### EW-G9 — Doctrine

**Present but thin on penalty.**

Substitution is partially named:
- *“He was the price paid”* (blood/death half),
- *“the One the guilt was laid on”* + Isaiah 53:6 (imputation half),
- *“He carried it away Himself — once.”*

**Missing:** what **we owed** — judgment, death earned by sin, wrath, curse. *“Price paid”* implies it; a zero-Bible listener may not connect blood-shed to **penalty borne in our place**. EW-INV-7 asks for penalty **and** what Christ bore; only the second half is explicit.

**Sound:** No supersession. Two-goat typology (slain + scapegoat → one Christ) is standard evangelical reading. Aaron is not presented as bearing guilt himself. No contrarian inventiveness.

**Minor precision:** Lev 16’s scapegoat is **driven away**, not voluntary self-bearing. *“He carried it away Himself”* is true of Christ but skips how the **type** differed — could confuse careful listeners about what the live goat actually did.

### EW-G10 — Voice / character

**Strong opening, essay drift in the turn.**

Hook lands in Aaron’s skin:
> *"Once a year I walked behind a curtain to meet God — and I was never sure I would walk back out."*

**Voice breaks:**
- *"No single creature could hold both halves"* — systematics, not memory.
- *"I was only the sign; He is the substance"* — Pauline shorthand (*sign/substance*), not Aaron’s idiom.
- *"no hand will ever sew it shut"* — rhetorical flourish; no witness anchor.
- *"Long after me a Man named Jesus"* — anachronism without the honest device (*“word written after I was laid in my grave”*) that keeps first-person integrity.

**Witness never named.** *“I”* is inferentially the high priest; a zero-Bible listener may not know who is speaking until Beat 2’s ritual clues arrive.

**Repetition:** *“The curtain I trembled behind”* appears three times (Beats 1, 3, 4) — in a ~204-word piece that reads as filler, not deepening dread.
