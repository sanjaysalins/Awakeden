# Independent review — claude (OK, 45s)

I read this adversarially. It's mostly sound — KJV is clean, the substitution doctrine is orthodox, the landing does real work. But there is one genuine problem and a couple of smaller ones.

**Doctrinal imprecision — the hook (strongest flag):**
> "Jesus cried words that were not His own — a psalm David had written a thousand years before."

"Words that were not His own" is theologically loose. Scripture teaches the *Spirit of Christ* was in the prophets when they wrote (1 Peter 1:11) — so in the truest sense Psalm 22 *was* His own word before David ever penned it. Saying they "were not His own" quietly undercuts inspiration to score a hook. The fix is small: "words David wrote a thousand years before — words that were always His."

**Internal tension / near-contradiction on first hearing:**
The script opens "words that were **not His own**" and then immediately, twice, says "Jesus **made it His own**" (beats 1→2). A zero-Bible listener hears a flip-flop in the first ten seconds. The intended arc (David's psalm → Christ claims it) is good, but the "not His own" framing fights it. Drop the negation and the arc lands clean.

**Redundancy:**
> "Jesus made it His own." (beat 1) … "Jesus made it His own." (beat 2)

The exact phrase repeats back-to-back across two beats. One of them should carry new freight (e.g. the second could move straight to "the voice of a forsaken man — and on the cross, that man was Him").

**Things that hold up (not flags, for the record):**
- KJV verbatim: Psalm 22:1 and the English of Matthew 27:46 are both exact. Clean.
- "at the ninth hour" — faithful to Matt 27:46.
- "the sinless Son taking our place… bearing the forsaking that our sin deserved" — sound penal substitution, no gain/loss or fear framing.
- "He was forsaken so that you never will be" — specific, fresh, grace-anchored landing (echoes Heb 13:5), not a generic "will you trust Him?" close. Strong.
- Thread spine is single and intact: forsaken cry → identical words → not lost faith → substitution → never-forsaken invitation.

The freshness lever (David's verbatim line on Christ's lips, 1000 years apart) is faithful and grips. The only thing standing between this and a PASS is the "not His own" line, which is both doctrinally soft and self-contradicting.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Replace "words that were not His own" — it undercuts that the Spirit of Christ inspired David (1 Pet 1:11) and contradicts the script's own "made it His own." Reframe as words always His, that He now spoke as the forsaken man.
2. Remove the back-to-back duplicate "Jesus made it His own" — give the second beat new content.
3. Smooth the opening arc so a first-time listener isn't whipsawed between "not His own" and "made it His own" inside the first ten seconds.
