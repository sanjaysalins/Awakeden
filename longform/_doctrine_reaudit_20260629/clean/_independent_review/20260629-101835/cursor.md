## Doctrinal soundness

**Mostly orthodox evangelical penal substitution — but two precision gaps.**

- **"From the cross, Jesus cried words that were not His own"** — The hook trades on a paradox the text never resolves. In evangelical theology Jesus is the divine author of the Psalms via inspiration; David’s line is not alien speech Jesus borrowed. A careful listener (or hostile reviewer) can read this as **performance of someone else’s script**, not **fulfillment of His own Word**. The next line (“Jesus made it His own”) only partly repairs it; the hook still plants the wrong frame first.

- **"He was forsaken so that you never will be"** — Substitution is sound, but **"never will be"** is stated as a blanket outcome with **no faith / union-with-Christ guard**. That reads like universal assurance to every listener, including those who have not come to Him. The long-form on this same beat explicitly reworked penal-sub language after panel pushback; this short keeps the **beneficiary-avoidance cadence** (“so that you never will be”) that the long-form was told to drop.

- **No reconciliation with Psalm 22’s own arc** — The long-form adds that the same psalm moves from forsaken cry to “he heard” / “raised him up” (v.24 territory). This short asserts real forsaking as judicial bearing **without any beat from the psalm’s turn**. Not heresy, but it **over-asserts dereliction in isolation** in a way the backing study already flagged as needing balance.

**"This was no cry of lost faith — even forsaken, He still calls God 'my God.'"** — Good guardrail; orthodox, text-grounded.

## Clarity on first hearing

**Several lines assume Bible literacy or tolerate ambiguity.**

- **"words that were not His own"** — A zero-Bible-knowledge listener hears: whose words? Did Jesus not mean them? The hook is clever but **logic-friction on first pass**, not instant clarity.

- **"at the ninth hour"** — Never explained (~3 p.m.). Fine for church people; **opaque on first hearing** for the stated audience.

- **"He opened it from the dark"** — **"the dark"** is undefined. Cross darkness (Matt 27:45)? Hell? Sin? A poetic listener may admire it; a cold listener may not know what was opened, from what, or how.

- **"Psalm twenty-two opens in the voice of a forsaken man"** — Assumes the listener knows what a psalm is and that it has a “voice.” Workable in-series, thin for a total outsider.

## Grace-anchored conviction

**Mixed — invitation tone is mostly grace, but one line snaps the anchor.**

- **"However far you've run, the way home is open"** — Grace register; prodigal echo without fear-selling. Good.

- **"bearing the forsaking that our sin deserved"** — Bearing language (Christward), not “do better.” Good.

- **"He was forsaken so that you never will be"** — This is **gain/loss / personal benefit framing**: Christ’s forsaking positioned as securing *your* exemption. Constitution explicitly warns against cadences where the payoff is “so you would never have to…” The long-form panel removed that pattern; **this short reintroduces it in the spoken close.**

No works-framing, but the **transactional benefit line** is the weakest grace link.

- **"The very same words"** — states the obvious after back-to-back identical quotes; low new information.
- **"even forsaken, He still calls God 'my God'"** — standard pulpit point; faithful but **not fresh depth**.

For beat #3 (“Jesus QUOTES it Himself”), the script proves citation but **does not exploit what makes this link unique** (deliberate quotation vs. mockers’ accidental match in short #2).

## Landing does NEW work

**FAILS the project’s landing bar — soft, partially generic, weak CTA-to-Jesus.**

- Final line: **"However far you've run, the way home is open — and He opened it from the dark."**
  - Does not **name Jesus** in the landing (after a Jesus-centric proof block).
  - Does not **invite** (come, trust, turn, receive) — only declares an open way. Constitution: *“Invite the viewer to Jesus — to trust, to come, to follow.”*
  - **Portability test fails:** “However far you've run, the way home is open” could close a prodigal parable short, a generic grace short, or several other episodes unchanged.
  - **"He opened it from the dark"** adds mood, not new doctrinal work tied to *this* cry.

Compare sibling short #1 in the same series, which lands: **“The suffering man of Psalm twenty-two has a name: Jesus”** + **“laying down His life to win you back.”** This landing does less person-directed work.

(Note: the file metadata claims the landing was tightened to an “audible Heb 13:5 promise (‘a Father who will never let you go’).” **That phrase is not in the spoken artifact.** If that was the intended fix, the script and metadata diverge.)
