# Independent review — codex (OK, 69s)

Findings:

- **EW-INV-4 attribution breach:** `**"And Abraham stretched forth his hand, and took the knife to slay his son."**` is exact KJV, but it is not attributed before quotation. Abraham cannot simply quote the narrator without a cue like “Moses wrote” or “Genesis says.”

- **Possible KJV/paraphrase confusion:** `I could only answer that God would provide Himself a lamb.` closely echoes Genesis 22:8 but is not marked as quotation. Capitalizing `Himself` pushes a Christological reading into Abraham’s mouth before the reveal.

- **Weakest required element is the wrestling:** `not knowing if he would come back down` is the only real doubt line. It gestures at tension but does not steel-man the hardest objection: God promised through Isaac, then commanded Isaac’s death. It also risks clashing with Genesis 22:5 / Hebrews 11:19, where Abraham expects God to bring Isaac back somehow.

- **Typology overclaims geography:** `up that same hill` is too definite. Genesis gives Moriah; the crucifixion site is not clearly “that same hill.” This sounds like an invented precision.

- **Reveal is partly earned, partly announced:** `the lamb I named was never found on my mountain` is a strong turn, but `By the light that came long after me, I see what that hill was reaching toward` announces the meaning instead of letting the lived details carry more of it.

- **Doctrine mostly holds, but needs sharper substitution:** `He bore the death we had earned` names penalty, but it should more clearly name sin and substitution. As written, it is close but a little thin for EW-G9.

- **First-hearing clarity gap:** `in whom my whole promise hung` does not explain what promise. A listener with no Bible knowledge may not understand why Isaac matters beyond being Abraham’s son.

- **Voice slips toward essay narration:** `Generations later a Man taught in Jerusalem and named me` and `John pointed at Him` move Abraham into a detached theological narrator voice. The first-person witness is strongest in `my boy`; it weakens in the reveal.

- **CTA is acceptable but slightly vague at the final effect:** `Come to Him` names the response and `Jesus` is named, but `be carried home` is soft and undefined. It is grace-toned, not fear-based, but could land more clearly as faith/receiving Christ.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Add real Abraham-level wrestling around God’s promise through Isaac versus God’s command to sacrifice Isaac.
2. Fix quotation attribution and avoid quasi-quoting Genesis 22:8 with loaded capitalization.
3. Remove or qualify `that same hill`, and sharpen the substitution line to name sin and the death Christ bore.
