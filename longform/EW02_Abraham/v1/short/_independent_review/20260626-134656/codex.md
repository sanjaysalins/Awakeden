# Independent review — codex (OK, 24s)

Findings:

- **EW-INV-4 KJV quote:** The quoted line is exact KJV from John 1:29: **"Behold the Lamb of God, which taketh away the sin of the world."** Speaker attribution exists before it: “A man named John pointed:”. No altered word found.

- **EW-G9 doctrine weakness:** “the substitute God provided so you never have to climb that mountain” undernames substitution. It says “substitute,” but not the penalty, sin, wrath, curse, judgment, or what Christ bore. John’s quote mentions sin, but the narration’s own doctrinal close does not explain that Jesus bore the sinner’s penalty.

- **CTA-on-Jesus issue:** “Come to Him, wanted, and be carried home” names a response, but it slides sentimental. “wanted” is not grounded in the text’s logic, and “be carried home” is vague. The call should still be gracious, but clearer: come to Christ as the Lamb who bore sin in your place.

- **Reveal mostly earned, but one line overclaims:** “No voice stopped His Father’s hand” is powerful, but risky. It implies the Father’s direct hand killed the Son in the same way Abraham would have killed Isaac. The intended substitutionary point is sound, but the phrasing can become theologically blunt and imprecise.

- **First-hearing clarity gap:** “No ram was caught for Him — because He was the Lamb” works for Bible-literate listeners, but a zero-Bible listener may not understand why “ram” and “Lamb” matter unless the substitute logic is made explicit: the ram died instead of Isaac; Jesus died instead of sinners.

- **Voice/character:** Abraham’s first-person voice is present in “My boy asked,” “I had no name to give him,” and “I came down delivered, and still waiting.” But “Generations later, another beloved Son — Jesus — carried His own wood” shifts into polished sermon narration. It remains coherent, but the witness voice thins there.

- **Short form:** It is tight and focused. One witness, one thread, one witnessed moment. No major sag.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Name the substitution more fully: Jesus bore sin’s penalty in the sinner’s place.
2. Make the closing CTA clearer and less sentimental than “wanted, and be carried home.”
3. Sharpen the Christ turn so “No voice stopped His Father’s hand” does not overstate the Father/Abraham parallel.
