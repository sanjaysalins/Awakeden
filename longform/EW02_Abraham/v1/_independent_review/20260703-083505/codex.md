# Independent review — codex (OK, 33s)

**Findings**

- EW-INV-4 issue: `**[the LORD]** **"Take now thy son...` is misattributed against KJV wording. Genesis 22:1 identifies the speaker as `God`, not `the LORD`. If this standard is strict, that attribution fails even though the quoted words themselves are KJV.

- EW-INV-4 issue: several KJV quotes lack a direct attribution tag before the quote: `**"Abide ye here with the ass...``, `**"My son, God will provide himself a lamb...``, `**"Accounting that God was able...``, `**"Behold the Lamb of God...``, `**"He that spared not his own Son...``, and `**"For God so loved the world...``. The prose often implies attribution, but the rule says the speaker must be attributed before quoting.

- EW-G9 concern: `On His mountain the Father did not stay His hand` is theologically risky wording. It can sound as if the Father directly “raised the knife” over Christ in the same mechanical way Abraham did, flattening the crucifixion’s human agency and Trinitarian unity. The substitution is named well elsewhere, but this phrasing needs tightening.

- EW-G9 concern: `He stood where I deserved to stand, where you deserve to stand` names substitution, but the penalty is still somewhat generalized as `the death guilty men had earned`. Stronger doctrinal clarity would name sin, judgment/wrath, and curse more precisely, without turning manipulative.

- EW-G7 mostly works, but the reveal leans on typological parallels that may feel overdrawn: `who carried the wood of His own offering up a hill in that same country, exactly as I had bound the wood onto Isaac.` “Exactly” overclaims. Isaac carried wood for a burnt offering; Jesus carried the cross. The parallel is legitimate, but “exactly” makes it feel asserted beyond the text.

- EW-G8: the wrestling is one of the stronger parts: `The same God who swore the promise would run through Isaac was now telling me to end Isaac's life.` That is the real objection. But it resolves too cleanly and quickly into Hebrews 11: `I had only one place left to stand.` For a long-form interior narration, this could bear more lived pressure before the theological resolution arrives.

- Voice issue: phrases like `the later Scripture says it of me more kindly than I could` and `by the word that was written after I was dust` push Abraham into essay-narrator mode. The conceit permits far-side-of-life knowledge, but these lines sound like a Bible teacher explaining sources, not Abraham remembering.

- First-hearing clarity issue: `in a figure`, `the father in the shadow`, and `only in figure` are insider phrases. The piece eventually explains them, but a zero-Bible listener may not track “figure” as “preview/picture” on first hearing.

- Long-form pacing: Beat 4 and Beat 6 both repeat the lamb/ram contrast: `God had given a ram... The lamb I had named had not yet been brought` and later `The lamb I had promised Isaac and never found... God provided Him on His`. The repetition is thematically central, but the second pass needs a new turn or sharper escalation to justify the runtime.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Fix all KJV attribution problems, especially `[the LORD]` for Genesis 22:2 and unattributed quote blocks.
2. Tighten the substitution language so the penalty Christ bore is doctrinally precise without risky Father/knife phrasing.
3. Cut or deepen repeated lamb/ram beats so the long form earns its length rather than circling the same reveal.
