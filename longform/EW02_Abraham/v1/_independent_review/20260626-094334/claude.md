# Independent review — claude (OK, 107s)

I read the full narration and checked every KJV quotation against the text.

## KJV verbatim + attribution (EW-INV-4) — clean
I verified all eleven quotes character-by-character. Every one is exact KJV:
- Gen 22:2 (the LORD), 22:5 (Abraham, first-person), 22:7 (Isaac), 22:8 (Abraham), 22:10 (Scripture), 22:12 (the angel of the LORD) — all verbatim ✓
- Heb 11:19, John 8:56 (Jesus), John 1:29 (John the Baptist), Rom 8:32, John 3:16 — all verbatim ✓

This is rare and well done. One **minor attribution nit**: the closing John 3:16 is dropped after "the love I saw in figure, now given in full:" with no named speaker. Everywhere else you tag the speaker (Jesus / John / Scripture); here the most famous verse rides in unattributed. A two-word frame ("for God's own word says:") would close the gap.

## Doctrine (EW-G9) — sound, substitution explicit
"Jesus the Lamb bore the death guilty men had earned — He stood where I deserved to stand, where you deserve to stand." Penalty named (death earned by the guilty), what Christ bore named, the substitution stated crisply ("There was no substitute for Him, because He was the substitute"). No supersession; the witness reads the canon through Christ rather than replacing Israel. The ram-vs-lamb riddle ("I had promised the boy a lamb, and God had given a ram… the lamb I had named had not yet been brought") is a legitimate, text-grounded reading resolved at John 1:29. Good.

## The reveal (EW-G7) — earned
The turn is *shown*, not announced: the only beloved son, the wood laid on the son's shoulders, Moriah / "that same country," "no voice came out of heaven to stop the hand, and no ram was caught in the thicket." You also wisely avoid overclaiming Golgotha = Moriah exactly ("that same country," not "that same mountain"). Strong.

## The wrestling (EW-G8) — real, but it picks the in-world objection over the listener's
The doubt is genuine and in-voice: "the God who cannot lie [commanding] the death of His own word… If the boy died, the oath died with him," resolved by the Hebrews 11 resurrection logic. That is Abraham's *authentic* dilemma. **But** the objection a zero-Bible modern listener actually brings to Genesis 22 is the moral one — *how can a good God command a father to kill his child?* The narration never internalizes the moral scandal; it steel-mans only the covenantal contradiction. In-character this is defensible, but the strongest objection for the audience goes untouched. Consider one line of honest moral horror inside Beat 5.

## CTA (EW-INV-2) — grace-anchored, named, not the tired template
"His name is Jesus… Come to Him not driven but wanted… not when worthy, but now, freely. Come, and be carried home." Response is named (come / let Him be your substitute), no fear/gain-loss/pressure, and the "where is the lamb?" callback to the hook is genuinely good. Not the bare "will you trust Him?" template.

## Voice (EW-G10) — consistent, characterful
"Be still with an old man a moment." "I have gone back over it ten thousand times since." First-person Abraham holds throughout. A few phrases tilt essayistic ("A picture of something larger than itself") but it never collapses into the detached narrator.

## Weaknesses worth flagging
1. **Beat 6 → Beat 7 redundancy.** The invitation restates the reveal's images nearly wholesale — "He gave His beloved Son, who carried the wood and did not come down, the Lamb I never found" repeats Beat 6's "carried the wood of His own offering… the lamb I had promised Isaac and never found." Some recapitulation is fine for a CTA, but this is close to verbatim re-tread; tighten Beat 7 so it does new emotional work rather than re-summarizing.
2. **"I had no other" (Beat 2).** Ishmael existed; the KJV's own "thine only son" means only son of Sarah/of the promise, and your "Sarah bore him" qualifier mostly covers it — but the flat "I had no other" could mislead a complete novice. A small tightening ("no other by Sarah, no other of the promise") removes the snag.
3. **ram/lamb hinges on a distinction a novice may miss.** The whole riddle depends on the listener feeling that a *ram* is not the *lamb* promised. "A full-grown ram" helps; one more beat of contrast would secure it for the zero-Bible ear.

## Length
Reads ~10 min at the contemplative pace; each beat lands a new turn (world → act → strange detail → wrestling → reveal). The only sag is the Beat 6/7 overlap above. No padding elsewhere.

VERDICT: PASS
TOP FIXES:
1. Cut the near-verbatim repetition between Beat 6 and Beat 7 so the invitation advances rather than re-summarizes the reveal.
2. Add a brief attribution frame before the closing John 3:16 (every other quote is tagged; this one isn't).
3. In Beat 5, internalize the *moral* objection (a good God commanding a child's death), not only the covenantal contradiction — that's the doubt the listener actually carries; and soften the standalone "I had no other" re Ishmael.
