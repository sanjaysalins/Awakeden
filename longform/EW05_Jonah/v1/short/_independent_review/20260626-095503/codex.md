# Independent review — codex (OK, 22s)

Concrete findings:

- **EW-INV-4 KJV:** The two quoted passages appear verbatim KJV and are attributed before quoting. Jonah’s quote is introduced by “I sentenced myself,” and Jesus’ by `**[Jesus]**`. No altered word found inside the quoted KJV.

- **EW-G9 Doctrine / substitution is under-named:** `“He went down innocent, bearing ours”` leaves the object vague. Bearing our what? The lens requires the penalty and what Christ bore. `“died in your place”` is substitutionary, but still does not clearly name sin, guilt, judgment, wrath, or penalty.

- **EW-G7 reveal partly asserted instead of shown:** `“I tell you now, by the mouth of the One who claimed those days”` jumps into interpretation. The connection is real, but the narration does not linger enough on the textual shape: Jonah guilty and cast down, Christ innocent and self-given; Jonah delivered from deserved death, Christ rising after bearing sinners’ death. It states that contrast, but the turn still feels compressed.

- **EW-G8 wrestling is not the strongest objection:** `“I had chosen death — why was I breathing?”` is interesting, but Jonah’s central scandal is God’s mercy to enemies. The opening names it: `“I feared His mercy was too wide”`; the wrestling in the fish shifts to personal survival. The stronger Jonah objection would be: “If mercy reaches Nineveh, what becomes of justice?” That is the steel-man.

- **First-hearing clarity issue:** `“the prophet the sea swallowed and gave back alive”` is vivid but imprecise. The sea did not give him back; the fish did. A zero-Bible listener may not understand what literally happened.

- **CTA:** The close names the response clearly: `“Come to Jesus”` / `“Will you stop running?”` It avoids the bare “will you trust Him?” template. But `“Come up out of the deep, and live”` risks sounding slightly gain-loss/self-preservation unless anchored more plainly in receiving mercy from Christ.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Name what Christ bore: our sin, guilt, judgment, and penalty.
2. Make Jonah’s wrestling the real objection: mercy to guilty enemies without denying justice.
3. Tighten the reveal so the Jonah-to-Christ turn is shown from the lived moment, not announced.
