### EW-G10 — Voice / character: **FAIL at the pivot**

Most beats stay first-person Jonah. Then Beat 3 breaks the eyewitness contract:

> *"Then, long after I was dust, scribes demanded a sign — Jesus gave them only my name:"*

Jonah did not live to see scribes, Pharisees, or Jesus. This is redemptive-history summary in Jonah’s mouth — the “essay narrator wearing a costume” failure the spec flags. A cross-time signpost is allowed; this line still ** narrates events Jonah never witnessed** as if he were there.

Same problem immediately after the quote:

> *"He went down innocent, bearing what was ours; He gave Himself."*

Jonah could infer guilt/innocence from **his** storm and casting. He could **not** eyewitness Christ’s voluntary atonement. *"He gave Himself"* is sermon commentary, not memory from the fish.

---

### EW-G7 — Reveal earned: **FAIL / weak**

The Matthew 12:40 quote does real work — Jesus Himself names the sign. After that, the Christ-turn is largely **asserted**, not walked out from what Jonah survived.

**What is earned:**
- *"I went down guilty — the storm was mine"* — ties to Jonah 1 (tempest for his sake).
- *"The grave no more held Him than the deep held me"* — strong typological rhyme **if** the listener already knows Jonah came up alive.

**What is not earned from Jonah’s lived text:**
- **Substitution** is imported, not shown. Matthew 12:40 claims **three days in the earth**, not penal substitution. The jump from the quote to *"bearing what was ours; He gave Himself"* is applied theology, not Jonah reasoning from the belly.
- Jonah’s **deliverance up** — the half of the type that makes resurrection land — is almost absent. Beat 3 ends *"He rose"* about Christ but never has Jonah say **he** was vomited up alive. The punch *"Come up, and live"* echoes resurrection, but the witness never **showed** his own “come up” moment, so the parallel is announced at the end rather than demonstrated through the single moment.
- Typological slippage: Jonah was **preserved alive** in the fish (“buried alive”); Christ **died** and rose. The piece says *"died in your place"* in the CTA but the middle analogy (*"the deep held me"* / *"buried alive"*) blurs death vs. entombment. A careful listener can hear Jonah-as-near-death and Christ-as-actual-death conflated.

---

### EW-G8 — Wrestling (steel-man): **FAIL / too thin**

Jonah’s real objection is present but **compressed into retrospective summary**, not internalized wrestling:

> *"I ran from God because His mercy was too wide."*  
> *"I'd sooner drown than see her spared."*

That is the correct steel-man (mercy on enemies Jonah wanted destroyed). But there is **no line of doubt in Jonah’s voice at the moment** — no *“How can God spare them?”*, no anguish in the dark, no honest question the text must answer. It reads as confessed backstory, not live wrestle.

For a zero-Bible listener, *"the city I hated"* also fails to load the steel-man: **why** Nineveh? Without one concrete phrase (violent enemy, oppressor, Assyria), the objection can sound petty rather than morally dangerous — which weakens both EW-G8 and first-hearing clarity.

---

### EW-G9 — Doctrine: **CONDITIONAL / under-specified**

No supersession, no contrarian reading, typology via Matthew 12:40 is mainstream evangelical. But EW-G9 requires substitution with **the penalty + what Christ bore** named:

> *"bearing what was ours"*  
> *"died in your place"*

Substitution is **gestured**, not named. What was ours? Sin? Guilt? Wrath? Judgment? The storm was **Jonah’s** — Christ bearing **ours** needs the penalty explicit, especially because Matthew 12:40 alone does not teach substitution; the piece must carry that freight itself, and currently doesn’t.

### EW-INV-2 — CTA on Jesus: **PASS with misalignment**

**Passes:**
- Names Jesus: *"Come to Jesus"*
- Named response (come), not bare *"will you trust Him?"*
- Grace-anchored tone; *"fallen too far"* fits mercy, not fear/gain-loss framing

**Problem — CTA doesn’t answer Jonah’s steel-man:**

Jonah’s wrestle is **mercy too wide for enemies he hated**. The close pivots to:

> *"You who are sure you've fallen too far — that is how far His mercy reaches."*

That addresses the **runner** (*"Stop running"*) more than Jonah’s actual objection (**God sparing people I wanted judged**). The spine wobbles at the landing: Beat 2 sets enemy-mercy scandal; Beat 4 invites the ashamed sinner. Related themes, but not the same question — listener may not see how Nineveh’s mercy connects to *"fallen too far."*

- *"Nineveh"* — unnamed significance.
- *"scribes demanded a sign"* — who, why, and what a “sign” means unexplained.
- *"only my name"* — significance unclear before/unless Matt 12:40 lands perfectly.

Broad arc (ran → storm → fish → Jesus → come) is followable; **key causal links are Bible-coded**.
