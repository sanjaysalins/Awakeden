# Independent review — codex (OK, 23s)

Concrete findings:

- **EW-INV-4 KJV quote passes.** The line attributed to Jesus is exact KJV Matthew 12:40: **"For as Jonas was three days and three nights..."** Speaker attribution is correct.

- **Doctrine is under-specified.** “He went down innocent, bearing what was ours” and “died in your place” gesture at substitution, but the required penalty is not named. What did Christ bear: sin, guilt, wrath, curse, judgment? As written, substitution is present but too vague for EW-G9.

- **The steel-man objection is compressed.** “I ran from God because His mercy was too wide” is strong, but “the city I hated” gives a first-hearing listener no reason why Jonah’s hatred felt morally justified. The real objection was not generic dislike; Nineveh represented violent enemies. Without that, Jonah’s wrestling sounds petty instead of dangerous and morally loaded.

- **First-hearing clarity has gaps.** “Nineveh,” “the lot fell on me,” and “scribes demanded a sign” assume Bible knowledge. A zero-knowledge listener can follow the broad arc, but not why Nineveh matters or why “only my name” is significant.

- **Reveal is mostly earned, but slightly too neat.** “I went down guilty — the storm was mine. He went down innocent…” is the strongest move and it works. But “The grave no more held Him than the deep held me” risks over-smoothing the type: Jonah was preserved from death; Christ truly died and rose. The piece does say “died,” but the analogy needs care.

- **Voice is decent but not fully characterful.** “Then, long after I was dust, scribes demanded a sign” sounds more like a narrator summarizing redemptive history than Jonah’s lived voice. Still first-person overall, but the costume thins there.

- **CTA is good but could be sharper.** “Come to Jesus, who died in your place and rose” names Jesus and the response. “Stop running” fits Jonah and avoids the tired trust-template. It is grace-anchored, though “Come up, and live” is a little poetic/vague compared with the clearer “Come to Jesus.”

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Name the substitution fully: Christ bore our sin/guilt/penalty/judgment, not just “what was ours.”
2. Steel-man Jonah’s objection by making Nineveh’s evil/enemy status clear in one tight phrase.
3. Clarify first-hearing details without adding a second thread: why Nineveh mattered, what the sign meant, and keep Jonah’s voice through the Christ reveal.
