# Independent review — codex (OK, 110s)

**Findings**

- **KJV attribution problem:** “the record they kept of that day tells it plainly:” before **"So they took up Jonah..."** makes it sound like the sailors kept/wrote the record. That is narrative Scripture, not sailor testimony. Attribution should be “the record says” or similar.

- **KJV punctuation/verbatim risk:** **[Jesus]** **"there shall no sign be given to it, but the sign of the prophet Jonas"** omits the KJV terminal colon and presents a fragment as a complete sentence. Same issue with **"they repented at the preaching of Jonas; and, behold, a greater than Jonas is here."** being excerpted without the surrounding judgment context. Word sequence is KJV, but “verbatim” standards may require exact punctuation/context handling.

- **Core-text mismatch:** Header says **“Core text: Jonah 1–2”**, but the piece depends heavily on Jonah 3–4: **“Nineveh heard, and turned, and was spared”**, **“I went out and sat down in my anger”**, **“God left me there with a question”**. Either the core text is wrong or the narration is under-sourced.

- **Historical overclaim / invented motive risk:** **“the seat of the people who slaughtered mine”** and **“the very people who had butchered mine”** are strong claims not stated in Jonah. Jonah’s biblical objection is God’s mercy to Nineveh, not a named personal/national massacre. This may be defensible from Assyrian brutality, but here it is asserted as Jonah’s lived motive.

- **Reveal is partly over-announced:** The hook says **“Whom those three days were always about”** before the story earns the Christ turn. Later the reveal is stronger because Jesus explicitly says **“the sign of the prophet Jonas”**, but the opening weakens the discovery.

- **The wrestling is strong but slightly unstable:** **“That was the scandal that found me in the dark”** suggests Jonah learned the mercy lesson inside the fish, yet later says **“I never did fully make my peace with it”** after Nineveh. That tension can work, but here it reads like two Jonah arcs stitched together.

- **First-hearing clarity issue:** **“I was a dead man who was carried home”** and **“a dead man walking”** are vivid, but for a zero-Bible listener they blur whether Jonah literally died. Since the Christ fulfillment depends on actual death and resurrection, Jonah’s “as good as dead” status needs cleaner distinction.

- **CTA is close but too metaphor-dependent:** **“Will you stop running, and let it carry you up?”** is Jonah-shaped, but vague. **“Come to Jesus — and live”** names Jesus and “come,” which helps, but the response should more explicitly name faith/receiving mercy/turning to Christ without leaning only on “let it carry you.”

- **Long-form sag:** The “deep / three days / wide mercy” insight repeats without enough new turn: **“Three days I lay in the belly of the deep”**, **“Three days and three nights. Buried alive”**, **“Three days I lay in the deep”**; likewise **“mercy... too wide”**, **“same wide mercy”**, **“His mercy runs lower and farther”**. Some repetition is thematic, but several beats restate rather than advance.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Fix source/attribution discipline: update the core text to Jonah 1–4, clean narrative quote attribution, and remove or qualify unsupported “butchered mine” claims.
2. Tighten the Jonah arc so the fish-prayer wrestling and later Nineveh anger do not contradict each other.
3. Sharpen the close by naming the response plainly: come to Jesus by faith, receive His mercy, and turn from running.
