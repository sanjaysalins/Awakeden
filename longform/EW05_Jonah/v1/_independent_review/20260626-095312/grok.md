# Independent Review — *The Sign of Jonah* (eyewitness-long)

Skeptical read. Findings below cite exact lines/phrases.

---

## EW-INV-4 — KJV verbatim + attribution

**Mostly clean on wording.** Spot-checked Jonah 1:2, 1:9, 1:12, 1:15, 1:17, 2:2, 2:4, 2:6, 2:9, 2:10, Matthew 12:39–41 — fragments match KJV.

**Attribution gaps (several quotes):** EW-INV-4 wants the speaker named *before* the quote block. These lean on context only:

- After *"I told them the truth I had been fleeing:"* — no `[Jonah]` tag on **"I am an Hebrew; and I fear the LORD..."**
- After *"With my own mouth I pronounced my own sentence..."* — no tag on **"Take me up, and cast me forth into the sea..."**
- Prayer lines in Beat 5 — **"out of the belly of hell cried I..."**, **"I am cast out of thy sight; yet I will look again toward thy holy temple."**, etc. — no `[Jonah]` (or equivalent) before each block.

Jesus lines are tagged correctly: **[Jesus]**.

**Minor:** **"out of the belly of hell cried I..."** is mid-verse KJV (Jonah 2:2); verbatim, but a listener may not know it is Jonah praying unless attribution is tighter.

---

## EW-G7 — Reveal must be *earned*, not announced

**Main weakness.** Beat 6 opens with:

> *"I did not understand it in my own day. I tell you now from the far side of my life, by the word written long after me — and by the mouth of the One who spoke it."*

That admits the Christ-turn is **imported from Matthew 12**, not drawn out of what Jonah lived. Beat 4 sets up *"Why three?"* and *"waiting for something I could not see"* — but never ties three days, burial, or deliverance to a forward shadow from Jonah’s own experience. Beat 5 ends with *"I did not yet know that those three days in the dark were a sign of Someone far greater than me"* — again **deferred**, not shown.

The typology works **doctrinally** once Jesus speaks; it is **not earned inside the witness’s lived text** before the external quote. For EW-G7, that is a real miss.

---

## EW-G8 — Wrestling / steel-man

**Strong overall.** Beat 2 nails the biblical objection:

> *"I would sooner drown than watch God show mercy to men I hated"* / *"fear that the God of heaven would spare her. I knew Him to be gracious and slow to anger, and I did not want His grace spent on my enemies."*

That is Jonah 4:2 steel-manned, not a strawman.

**Slip:** After quoting **"I am cast out of thy sight; yet I will look again toward thy holy temple."**, the line *"That was my doubt"* mislabels the quote. In KJV that line is **hope** (*"yet I will look again toward thy holy temple"*), not doubt. The honest objection is mercy-too-wide (next paragraph), not “cast out of thy sight.” Calling the whole quote “doubt” blurs the prayer’s shape.

---

## EW-G9 — Doctrine

**Substitution is named clearly** in Beat 6:

> *"The penalty I had earned — to be cast out, cut off, swallowed by death — He took upon Himself, though He had earned nothing, and He bore it in our place."*

Innocent vs guilty contrast is also clear: *"I was guilty... He was innocent."*

**Typological blur in Beat 3:** *"One man given up to the deep, and the storm fell silent over all the rest"* reads like substitution for the sailors **before** Beat 6 corrects the frame. Jonah accepted **his own** storm; he did not bear the sailors’ guilt. A zero-Bible listener could carry the wrong picture for three beats.

**No supersession, no contrarian invention.** Nineveh mercy and Jonah’s anger track the text. Sound.

---

## EW-INV-2 — CTA on Jesus

**Passes.** Jesus is named: *"Come to Jesus — and live."* Response is named: *"Will you stop running, and let it carry you up?"* / *"Come up out of the deep."* Grace-anchored; tied to Jonah’s running and mercy, not fear or gain-loss.

**Borderline:** *"Come to Jesus — and live"* is plain, but the beat before personalizes it enough that it does not feel like a bare trust template.

**Good guard:** *"Do not come to me; I am the sign, never the Saviour"* — keeps the landing on Christ.

---

## EW-G10 — Voice / character

**Consistent first-person Jonah** throughout — prophet, runner, angry at mercy, not a costume essayist. Beat 6 honesty (*"when I saw that city spared, I was not glad"*) and *"I never did fully make my peace with it"* keep character integrity. **Strong.**

---

## First-hearing clarity

Mostly followable. **Beat 6** drops *"certain scribes and Pharisees stood before a Teacher and demanded a sign"* with little setup — a cold listener may not know who they are or why that scene matters before Jesus speaks.

*"The scandal"* / *"the very gospel I had been made to picture"* assume church vocabulary without one plain line of gloss.

---

## Hook, spine, long-form runtime

**Hook grips:** *"I am the man the sea swallowed whole and then gave back alive"* — immediate, vivid.

**One witness, one thread:** three days + mercy-too-wide holds start to finish. No thread swap.

**Sag / padding:** *"mercy too wide"* / fleeing grace because it reaches enemies repeats across Beats 2, 5, 6, and 7 with limited new turns after Beat 5. Beat 4 (*"The strange detail"*) is thin for long-form — one verse, *"Why three?"*, then mostly wait until Matthew. Beat 5→6 overlap on Nineveh mercy scandal. Runtime feels **~earned through storm/belly/prayer**, but **middle beats recycle** rather than add fresh hinges.

VERDICT: REVISE  
TOP FIXES:  
1. **Earn the reveal from Jonah’s lived experience before Matthew** — use Beat 4’s *"Why three?"* / burial / deliverance / “given up to the deep” to *show* the death-burial-resurrection shadow in Jonah’s own voice; treat Matthew 12 as confirmation, not the first time the listener hears the turn (EW-G7).  
2. **Tag every KJV block with explicit speaker attribution** (`[Jonah]`, `[the LORD]`, `[Jesus]`, or “the record says”) immediately before each quote (EW-INV-4).  
3. **Cut or compress repeated “mercy too wide” beats and thicken Beat 4** with one new, non-repeated turn (e.g. what “three days” meant to Jonah *before* Christ) so long-form runtime buys fresh material, not recap.
