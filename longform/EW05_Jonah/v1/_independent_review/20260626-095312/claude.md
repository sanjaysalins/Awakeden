# Independent review — claude (OK, 86s)

I read the artifact closely and checked every `**"..."**` quote against the KJV.

## KJV verbatim + attribution (EW-INV-4) — CLEAN
All thirteen quotes are exact KJV and properly attributed:
- Jonah 1:2 (LORD), 1:9, 1:12, 1:15, 1:17, 2:2, 2:4, 2:6, 2:9, 2:10 — all verbatim.
- Matt 12:39, 12:40, 12:41 (Jesus) — all verbatim, including the partial pickups ("there shall no sign be given to it, but the sign of the prophet Jonas" and the mid-verse "they repented at the preaching of Jonas…"). The partials quote contiguous KJV words, so they're legitimate.

No altered words, no misattributions. The strongest defensible reading is that the LORD speech (1:2) gets a speaker tag and the sailor-scene quotes are framed in narration ("I told them the truth," "the record they kept… tells it plainly"). Good.

## Doctrine (EW-G9) — SOUND
Substitution is explicitly named: "The penalty I had earned — to be cast out, cut off, swallowed by death — He took upon Himself, though He had earned nothing, and He bore it in our place." Penal substitution, clean. The type is handled carefully — it never claims Jonah literally died; it stays in shadow language ("My great fish was only the shadow of it"). No supersession ("a greater than Jonah," not "replacement of Israel"). No contrarian reading.

## CTA (EW-INV-2) — PASSES
Names Jesus ("Come to Jesus — and live"), names the response ("stop running," "let it carry you up," "come"). Grace-anchored, and it pre-empts the fear charge: "now with no fear left in it." "Will you stop running…?" is a variant of the banned bare template but it's earned by the running-motif spine, not generic. Acceptable.

## Reveal (EW-G7) — EARNED
Beat 4 plants the question hard ("Why three? … the dark itself were marking time, waiting for something I could not see"), and Beat 6 pays it off through Jesus' own words in Matthew. Not announced — shown.

## Voice (EW-G10) — STRONG
Consistent, characterful first person. "I would sooner drown than watch God show mercy to men I hated" is the witness, not the essay narrator.

## Real weaknesses (concrete)

**1. The wrestling and the climax sit on different axes.** The honest doubt in Beat 5 is the mercy-scandal ("I fled because I knew it was too wide"). But the climactic reveal in Beat 6 is the resurrection sign (three days). So the steel-man objection and the type-payoff are on two different threads. The piece declares both types in its header ("three days… and of mercy reaching the worst of cities") and reconnects them in Beat 6 ("that was the scandal I could not stomach — and it was the very gospel I had been made to picture"), which mostly holds it together — and it's faithful, since Jesus invokes BOTH in Matt 12. But the seam is real: a listener feels the wrestling resolve on mercy while the visual/structural climax resolved on resurrection. This is the one thing keeping it from airtight.

**2. Beat 7 repeats the refrain.** "Too small / too wide" appears in Beat 5, Beat 6, and three more times in Beat 7 ("The mercy was never too small — always too wide… I knew how wide it was… His mercy runs lower and farther"). It's the thematic spine, but by the invitation it's been said enough that the close mildly treads water instead of doing new work. Tighten the redundant restatements in Beat 7.

**3. Beat 6 is overloaded.** It carries the resurrection reveal, the Nineveh repentance, AND Jonah's unresolved Jonah-4 anger ("I went out and sat down in my anger"). That's three turns in one beat while Beat 7 then re-states. Consider letting the Nineveh/anger material breathe earlier so Beat 6 lands the resurrection cleanly.

None of these violate an invariant — they're quality refinements. The doctrine, KJV, CTA, reveal, and voice all clear the bar.

VERDICT: PASS
TOP FIXES:
1. Tighten the thread seam: make the mercy-scandal wrestling (Beat 5) and the three-days resurrection reveal (Beat 6) feel like ONE spine, not two — lean on the bridge line ("the very gospel I had been made to picture") earlier so the resurrection climax and the mercy resolution land as one turn.
2. Cut the repeated "too small / too wide" restatements in Beat 7 so the invitation does new work instead of re-summarizing.
3. Rebalance Beat 6 — it's carrying resurrection + Nineveh repentance + Jonah's anger at once; move the anger/Nineveh material slightly earlier so the resurrection reveal isn't crowded.
