# Independent review — cursor (OK, 71s)

## Independent adversarial review — *The Sign of Jonah* (eyewitness-long)

---

### EW-INV-4 — KJV verbatim + attribution

**Verbatim:** Every bold quote checks as a substring of the episode `passage.txt`. No altered words inside the quote spans themselves.

**Attribution — multiple failures.** The lens requires the speaker to be named *before* each quote. Several KJV lines are third-person **narrator** text from Jonah, not Jonah’s speech, yet they appear with no `[scripture]` tag and immediately after Jonah’s first-person narration — so they will read as Jonah quoting himself or speaking narrator lines:

| Quote | Problem |
|-------|---------|
| **"So they took up Jonah, and cast him forth into the sea; and the sea ceased from her raging."** (Beat 3) | Jonah 1:15 narrative. Jonah did not say this; the sailors’ action is described in third person. |
| **"Now the LORD had prepared a great fish to swallow up Jonah. And Jonah was in the belly of the fish three days and three nights."** (Beat 4) | Jonah 1:17 narrative. Not Jonah’s speech. |
| **"And the LORD spake unto the fish, and it vomited out Jonah upon the dry land."** (Beat 5) | Jonah 2:10 narrative. Not Jonah’s speech. |

**Jonah’s own lines also lack tags** where other episodes tag speakers (e.g. Moses tags `[the people]`, `[the LORD]`):

- Beat 3: **"I am an Hebrew; and I fear the LORD..."** and **"Take me up, and cast me forth into the sea..."** — Jonah 1:9 / 1:12, no `[Jonah]` (or scripture) before the quote.
- Beat 5: five prayer fragments (Jonah 2:2, 2:4, 2:6, 2:9) — Jonah’s prayer, but none tagged before quoting.

Only Beat 2 (`**[the LORD]**`) and Beat 6 (`**[Jesus]**`) meet the attribution rule cleanly. This is a cast-routing problem as well as a fidelity problem: EW-INV-4 expects a dedicated **scripture** voice on every KJV line.

**Partial Jesus quotes** (Matt 12:39, 12:41) are verbatim substrings, but they drop the opening clauses (“An evil and adulterous generation…” / “The men of Nineveh shall rise in judgment…”). Acceptable as excerpts only if the production treats them as clipped quotes; a listener may not know these are Jesus answering Pharisees, not standalone pronouncements.

### EW-INV-2 — CTA-on-Jesus

**Mostly passes.** Beat 7 names **Jesus Christ**, invites with named responses (**Turn**… **come to Him**… **Trust**… **Come to Jesus — and live**). No banned bare “will you trust Him?” template. Grace-anchored, not fear/gain-loss.

**Minor friction:**

- **"Do not run the road I ran."** — Imperative framed on Jonah’s mistake, not divine wrath; acceptable, but edges toward “avoid my error” pressure rather than pure invitation.
- **"with your name upon His lips"** — Poetic, not Scripture; vague anthropomorphism for a zero-Bible listener (“what does that mean?”).
- **"The mercy you fear is too good to be true, the mercy I tried to outrun, is held out to you now in Jesus Christ"** — Comma splice; awkward when spoken aloud.

CTA is not the weakest link.

---

### EW-G7 — Reveal earned

**Partially earned; partly announced and imported.**

**Telegraphed in the hook (undercuts earning):**
Beat 1 ends: **"and Whom those three days were always about."** Capitalized *Whom* tells the listener the ending before Jonah lives the story. That is preacher foreshadowing, not witness ignorance — and it violates the piece’s own guardrail that Jonah **"did not yet know"** (Beat 5) and **"did not understand it in my own day"** (Beat 6).

**Cross-time import is signposted** — **"I tell you now from the far side of my life, by the word written long after me — and by the mouth of the One who spoke it"** — which satisfies EW-INV-11 structurally. But the **type→Christ turn is not shown from what Jonah lived alone.** The burial/resurrection parallel arrives almost entirely via:

**[Jesus]** **"For as Jonas was three days and three nights in the whale's belly; so shall the Son of man be three days and three nights in the heart of the earth."**

Then Beat 6 **asserts** the meaning: **"There it is — the meaning I could never read in my own dark."** The substitution paragraph (**"The penalty I had earned… He took upon Himself… He bore it in our place"**) is sound doctrine but **applied exposition after the NT quote**, not a chain of deductions from Jonah’s lived details (fish, storm, cast-out language, three-day count).

**What *is* earned from lived text:** three days (Beat 4), cast-out / depths language (Beat 5), mercy reaching the bottom (Beat 5). **What is not:** tomb, resurrection, substitution — those are imported and then labeled.

**Missing lived beat:** Jonah never preaches to Nineveh in this piece. Beat 6 says **"Nineveh heard, and turned, and was spared"** — but the listener never heard Jonah cry against the city or saw the repentance. The mercy-to-enemies thread is **told in retrospect**, not shown.

### EW-G8 — Wrestling (steel-man)

**Beat 2 is the real steel-man — Beat 5 is a softer, generic doubt.**

Beat 2 nails Jonah’s distinctive objection: **"I would sooner drown than watch God show mercy to men I hated"** / **"I knew Him to be gracious and slow to anger, and I did not want His grace spent on my enemies."** That is the book’s actual scandal (cf. Jonah 4:2).

Beat 5 reframes doubt as: **"does the mercy of God reach all the way down to the floor of the sea, to a man who had earned the storm with both hands?"** — honest, but **generic “have I fallen too far?”** Every repentance story gets this doubt. The piece **does** tie it back (**"I had fled His grace because I would not have it given to Nineveh — and now I needed that very grace"**), which saves it from pure strawman — but the **Nineveh mercy scandal is not wrestled to resolution in Beat 5**; it is skipped and retroactively resolved in Beat 6.

**Character honesty problem:** Scripture’s Jonah is **still furious** after Nineveh repents (Jonah 4 — gourd, “I do well to be angry”). This piece has posthumous Jonah say: **"That was the scandal I could not stomach, and it was the very gospel I had been made to picture."** That reads as **full reconciliation the biblical Jonah never reached in his lifetime.** The witness voice becomes wiser-than-Scripture at the end — essayist hindsight, not the man who sat east of the city wishing death.

---

### EW-G9 — Doctrine

**Passes on core evangelical claims.** Substitution is explicitly named: **"to be cast out, cut off, swallowed by death — He took upon Himself… He bore it in our place."** Innocent Christ / guilty Jonah distinction is clear. No supersession. Jesus names the sign Himself (Matt 12) — orthodox.

**Nits:**

- **"heart of the earth"** = tomb/resurrection is standard evangelical reading of Matt 12:40, but unexplained for first-hearing clarity.
- **"A greater than Jonah has come"** (Beat 6 narration) vs KJV **"Jonas"** in the quote — spelling inconsistency in witness voice vs Scripture.
- **"the seat of the people who slaughtered mine"** — historical coloring not in Jonah’s text; risks invented specificity presented as Jonah’s lived memory.

---

### EW-G10 — Voice / character

**Strong opening and storm sequence.** First-person Jonah is mostly vivid: **"the dead sleep of a man running from God,"** **"With my own mouth I pronounced my own sentence."**

**Costume slips in Beat 6:** **"Generations after I was dust, certain scribes and Pharisees stood before a Teacher and demanded a sign from heaven"** — detached historical narrator, not a man telling his story. **"the Lord Jesus reached back across the centuries"** — omniscient commentary. Compare Beat 5’s shore moment (in character) vs Beat 6’s Gospel Harmony tone (out of character).

Beat 1’s **"Whom those three days were always about"** also breaks character — Jonah-the-prophet speaking with post-resurrection knowledge at the open.

---

### First-hearing clarity

Generally followable. Weak spots:

- **Jonas / Jonah / whale / fish** — name and creature shift without explanation.
- **"heart of the earth"** — no plain gloss for burial.
- Nineveh’s repentance is **asserted**, not dramatized — a zero-Bible listener may not grasp why the city matters to the ending.

---

### Hook, spine, long-form pacing (~1550 spoken words — in budget)

**Hook:** Strong — **"the man the sea swallowed whole and then gave back alive."**

**Spine:** One witness, one thread (flight → storm → deep → fish → reveal → invitation). Holds.

**Sag / repetition:**

- Beat 1 and Beat 4 both deliver **three days, weeds, deep, dead-man** imagery — intentional echo, but at ~9–11 min it **re-treads** rather than advancing.
- Beat 3 ending (**"waters closed over my head, and down I went"**) and Beat 4 opening (**"I went down to the bottoms of the mountains, the weeds about my head"**) **overlap** the descent beat.
- **Jonah 3–4 entirely absent** — for long form, that’s not padding, but it **leaves a hole**: the runtime is filled with storm/fish/prayer repetition while the book’s **second half** (preaching, repentance, Jonah’s ongoing anger) is compressed into one retrospective sentence in Beat 6. That’s structural sag: **long on catastrophe, short on the scandal the book is actually about.**

---

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. **Fix KJV attribution on every quote** — tag narrator lines (1:15, 1:17, 2:10) for **scripture**, tag Jonah’s speech (1:9, 1:12, Jonah 2 fragments) for **Jonah** or scripture; never let Jonah’s voice read third-person narrative as his own words.
2. **Remove or rewrite Beat 1’s "Whom those three days were always about"** — it telegraphs the reveal and breaks witness ignorance; let Beat 4’s "Why three?" and Beat 5’s "Someone far greater" carry foreshadowing instead.
3. **Honest Jonah 4 wrestling or dramatize Nineveh** — either show Jonah preaching and still raging when mercy wins (steel-man mercy-for-enemies), or stop claiming **"That was the scandal I could not stomach, and it was the very gospel I had been made to picture"** as if Jonah fully embraced it; the biblical witness never got there in life.
