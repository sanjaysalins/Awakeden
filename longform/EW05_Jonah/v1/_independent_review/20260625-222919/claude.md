## The real problem — the WRESTLING is not Jonah's wrestling (EW-G8)
This is my biggest finding. The book of Jonah records exactly what Jonah's strongest objection was, and it is the **opposite** of the doubt this script gives him. The text's Jonah is not a man afraid mercy won't reach *him* — he is a man **furious that mercy reaches his enemies** (Jonah 4:1–3, "I knew that thou art a gracious God… therefore I fled"). The script even states this correctly in Beat 2 ("I did not want His grace spent on my enemies").

But Beat 5 then swaps that for a generic, universally-relatable doubt: *"does the mercy of God reach all the way down to the floor of the sea, to a man who had earned the storm with both hands?"* That is a real and honest objection — but it is **a sinner's objection placed in Jonah's mouth, not Jonah's own.** For a format whose entire value is *authentic first-person testimony*, relocating the wrestling from its true seat is the central weakness. The strongest steel-man here is "why should God spare the people who slaughtered mine?" — and the script flinches from sitting in it.

## Related — chapter 4 is smoothed over (voice / steel-man)
The piece ends Jonah's arc clean: *"a greater than Jonah has come, and His mercy runs lower and farther than mine ever could."* But the book of Jonah ends with Jonah **angry under a withered gourd**, God asking "Doest thou well to be angry?", and the question left hanging. By skipping ch. 4, the witness conveniently becomes a humble preacher of grace when Scripture leaves him resentful and unresolved. *"That was the scandal I could not stomach"* is past-tensed away. The most uncomfortable, honest beat of this man's life is omitted — and it's exactly the beat that would make the testimony land as confession rather than sermon.

## Minor — third-person self-quotes break first-person voice (EW-G10)
Twice the first-person witness quotes verses that name him in the **third** person: *"So they took up **Jonah**, and cast **him** forth…"* and *"Now the LORD had prepared a great fish to swallow up **Jonah**."* It's faithful KJV citation, but the witness narrating "they took up Jonah… and *down I went*" creates a small grammatical seam in the I-was-there illusion. Consider framing these as the written record he's reading ("the record says…") rather than his own recollected speech.

## Doctrine / CTA / reveal — sound
- Substitution named cleanly (EW-G9): *"The penalty I had earned — to be cast out, cut off, swallowed by death — He took upon Himself… and He bore it in our place."* Penalty + what Christ bore, both present. No supersession; the type/antitype contrast (Jonah guilty / Christ innocent) is handled, not flattened. ✓
- Reveal earned (EW-G7): the turn rests on Jesus himself naming "the sign of the prophet Jonas," not on an asserted "at last I understood." Genuinely earned. ✓
- CTA (EW-INV-2): names Jesus, names the response (turn / come / trust), grace-anchored, no fear/gain-loss, avoids the bare "will you trust Him?" template. ✓ One nit: *"came up on the third day with your name upon His lips"* is a poetic stretch with no textual basis — fine as figure, but it's the one invented flourish.

## Runtime
Word count looks ~1,250–1,350. At narration pace with the pauses, that's roughly 8–9 min — at or slightly **below** the ~9–11 min long-form target. It does not sag (Beat 4's "why three?" rumination earns its keep as setup for Beat 6), but there is room: the omitted ch. 4 anger is the obvious place to **add** an earning beat rather than pad elsewhere.

## First-hearing clarity — good
A zero-Bible listener can follow it. One slightly abstract line — *"My great fish was only the shadow; His empty tomb is the very body that cast it"* — is apt typology (shadow/substance) but may read dense on first hearing; acceptable.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Make the wrestling Jonah's *actual* strongest objection — his fury that grace is spent on the enemies who slaughtered his people (the ch. 4 steel-man) — instead of substituting a generic "will mercy reach me?" doubt; let the scandal of grace-to-enemies be the honest internal steel before it turns to Christ.
2. Stop smoothing over Jonah 4: don't past-tense his anger away into a tidy humble-preacher close; either dramatize the unresolved gourd-and-question and let grace answer *it*, or explicitly acknowledge the anger was never fully cured — that confession is what makes the testimony land.
3. Reframe the two third-person self-quotes ("they took up Jonah…", "a great fish to swallow up Jonah") as the written record he reads, not his own recalled speech, to keep the first-person voice seamless.
