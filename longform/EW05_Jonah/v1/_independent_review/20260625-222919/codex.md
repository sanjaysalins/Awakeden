# Independent review — codex (OK, 72s)

**Findings**

1. **EW-INV-4: KJV wording appears accurate, but attribution is inconsistent.**  
   I do not see altered KJV wording inside the quoted fragments. The problem is attribution. Several narrative quotations have no explicit speaker/source before them: **"So they took up Jonah, and cast him forth into the sea..."**, **"Now the LORD had prepared a great fish..."**, and **"And the LORD spake unto the fish..."**. Those are not Jonah speaking; they are biblical narration. If the rule requires attribution before every quote, these need explicit source/narrator attribution.

2. **EW-G8: the wrestling is not Jonah’s strongest objection.**  
   The piece says Jonah’s doubt was: **"does the mercy of God reach all the way down... to a runaway who deserved the deep."** But Jonah’s sharpest internal conflict is not mainly “can God forgive me?” It is “how can God spare Nineveh?” The artifact names that earlier — **"I would sooner drown than watch God show mercy to men I hated"** — but then dodges the hardest version: justice for victims, hatred of Assyria, and resentment that mercy could reach the cruel. That makes the wrestling softer than Jonah’s actual scandal.

3. **The reveal is mostly earned, but the substitution turn overreaches the Jonah scene slightly.**  
   **"One man given up to the deep, and the storm fell silent over all the rest"** is a good setup, but Jonah is the guilty cause of the storm, not an innocent substitute. The later contrast helps — **"I was guilty... He was innocent"** — but the piece should be careful not to make Jonah’s casting overboard itself sound substitutionary in the same way Christ’s death is.

4. **Doctrine is broadly sound, but one line is sentimental invention.**  
   **"came up on the third day with your name upon His lips"** is not grounded in the quoted text and risks manufactured emotional pressure. The substitution line is much stronger: **"He bore it in our place."** Keep the doctrine there; lose the invented flourish.

5. **Voice weakens in Beat 6.**  
   The opening sounds like Jonah. Beat 6 often sounds like a sermon narrator: **"There it is — the meaning I could never read in my own dark"** and **"My great fish was only the shadow; His empty tomb is the very body that cast it."** That is theological essay language, not a characterful first-person Jonah voice.

6. **First-hearing clarity has a few cloudy phrases.**  
   **"the very body that cast it"** will not land for a listener with zero Bible knowledge. Also **"I tell you now from the far side of my life, by the word written long after me"** raises a genre question: how exactly is Jonah narrating later Scripture? The piece can do awakened narration, but this phrasing calls attention to the mechanism instead of clarifying it.

7. **Long form sags through repetition of the same imagery.**  
   The repeated cluster **"dark / deep / dead / down"** starts strong but becomes predictable: **"darkness no living man..."**, **"belly of the deep"**, **"dead man"**, **"cold dark"**, **"living dark"**, **"floor of the sea"**. Beat 4 and Beat 5 partially cover the same emotional ground. The added runtime would be better earned by deeper wrestling with Nineveh, not more descent imagery.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Make Jonah’s real objection the center: God’s mercy toward Nineveh, not only mercy toward Jonah.
2. Fix quote attribution for narrative KJV lines and keep every quoted fragment clearly sourced.
3. Tighten Beat 6 so the Christ reveal stays in Jonah’s voice and removes invented/sentimental lines.
