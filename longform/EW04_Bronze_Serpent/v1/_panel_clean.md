# Look and Live — Awakeden eyewitness (long)

---
## Beat 1 — I was there
I am Moses. For forty years I led Israel through the wilderness, and of all the strange works these two hands were given to do — a rod that became a serpent, a sea split dry, water struck from a rock — there is one that follows me still into the dark of my old age. A pole driven into the sand. And upon it, a serpent of bronze.

I was there the day the people were dying faster than I could dig the graves. And I was there when God told a dying man that he could be healed by nothing in his hands, nothing in his own strength — by nothing more than a look.

## Beat 2 — The world
You must understand who we were. We were a freed people who had forgotten how to be grateful. We had come up from mount Hor, going the long way round to compass the land of Edom, and the road was bitter and seemed to have no end. The manna fell as faithfully as the morning, and still we despised it; and the Scripture does not flatter us — it tells the plain truth:

**"the soul of the people was much discouraged because of the way."**

Discouraged turned to anger, and anger turned its mouth against heaven. They came at me, and through me at God Himself:

**[the people]** **"Wherefore have ye brought us up out of Egypt to die in the wilderness? for there is no bread, neither is there any water; and our soul loatheth this light bread."**

I had heard that voice before — at the sea, at the rock, at the foot of the mountain where they cast a calf of gold while I stood in the cloud. But this time the answer came not in thunder, but in the dust between our tents, in a thing that slid and struck:

**"And the LORD sent fiery serpents among the people, and they bit the people; and much people of Israel died."**

Fiery, we named them, for the bite burned like a coal beneath the skin. I watched strong men fall. I watched mothers cradle children who had been laughing an hour before. And there was no remedy — not in all the wisdom of Egypt I had been schooled in, not in anything I knew to do.

## Beat 3 — The act
Then the people came to me, and for once their words were not complaint but confession:

**[the people]** **"We have sinned, for we have spoken against the LORD, and against thee; pray unto the LORD, that he take away the serpents from us."**

So I did the one thing a mediator can do — I prayed for the people. I expected the LORD to take the serpents away. He did not. Instead He gave me a command so strange that I have turned it over in my mind every year since:

**[the LORD]** **"Make thee a fiery serpent, and set it upon a pole: and it shall come to pass, that every one that is bitten, when he looketh upon it, shall live."**

He did not promise to take the serpents away. He did not hand me a medicine for the wound. He told me to forge the image of the very thing that was killing us — a serpent — and lift it high where every eye could find it. And so, with these hands, I did exactly as I was told:

**"And Moses made a serpent of brass, and put it upon a pole, and it came to pass, that if a serpent had bitten any man, when he beheld the serpent of brass, he lived."**

A man would feel the fire spreading in his blood, lift his eyes to that bronze upon the pole — and live. No payment. No climbing. No cure of his own making. He looked, and the death let go of him.

## Beat 4 — The strange detail
Here is the thing that never let me rest. Why a serpent?

The serpent was the curse itself. It was the very shape of what was striking them down. I would gladly have lifted up anything else — a rod, a lamb, the tables of the law in my arms. But God told me to lift up the likeness of the killer, high and plain, in the middle of the camp. As though the only cure for the poison were to look full upon the image of the poison, raised over their heads, and trust.

And the cure was a look — only a look. Not a sacrifice they brought, not a wound they nursed, not one thing they carried up the pole. The strong and the weak, the child and the dying elder, were all healed the same poor way: by lifting their eyes. I obeyed it, and the riddle of it walked home with me every evening. Why save a people by the sight of the very thing that slays them?

## Beat 5 — The wrestling
And I will be honest with you, for a man this near his grave has no reason to be otherwise.

Not everyone looked. Some, I am sure of it, lay in their tents too proud to turn their heads — it was too simple, too foolish, a grown man staring at a figure of brass like a child. And they died with the cure in plain sight of their own door. That has grieved me longer than the serpents did.

But there was a deeper trouble in me, and it was this: I knew the bronze had no power in it. I had ground a calf of gold to bitter powder and made Israel drink it; I of all men knew that metal saves no one. It was not the serpent of brass that healed — it was God's word, and the look that took Him at His word. So what, then, was the bronze for? Why a sign at all? I felt as though I were holding up a signpost in the wilderness, pointing past itself, down a long road of years, to something I could not yet see. I forged it. I could not have told you what it meant.

## Beat 6 — The reveal
I did not see the answer in my own day. I tell you this now from the far side of my life — by the light that came long after I laid down my staff, and by the word that was written after me — what that pole in the sand was always reaching toward.

For there came a Teacher in Israel, and to a man who sought Him by night He spoke of me, and of my serpent, and said the very thing I had been waiting for without knowing it:

**[Jesus]** **"And as Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, even so must the Son of man be lifted up:"**

**[Jesus]** **"That whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have eternal life."**

There it was. My serpent was never the point of it. It was a shadow, and a shadow waits for the body that casts it. The body came. They lifted Jesus up upon a Roman pole of wood, between the earth and the sky, where every eye could find Him — the very picture my bronze had held up in the wilderness.

And mark why it had to be a serpent — the cursed thing, the killer's own image. For when they hung Him there, the spotless Son of God was made to be the curse that was killing us:

**"being made a curse for us: for it is written, Cursed is every one that hangeth on a tree:"**

He became, upon that pole, the very poison of our sin, lifted up — so that we who are bitten and dying need only look to Him and live. He said it Himself, of the death He was going to die:

**[Jesus]** **"And I, if I be lifted up from the earth, will draw all men unto me."**

And the riddle of the look was answered too. It was never the bronze; it was the looking that God honoured — the faith that takes Him at His word. I know that now better than I once wished to. For long after me, when men forgot, they kept that serpent and burned incense to the metal of it, until a good king of Judah broke it to pieces and called it Nehushtan — a nothing, a scrap of brass. He was right to break it. The power was never in my handiwork. The power was in the God who said look and live — and in the Son He always meant the look to find:

**"For God so loved the world, that he gave his only begotten Son, that whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have everlasting life."**

## Beat 7 — The invitation
So hear me, you who are bitten — and that is every one of us, for the venom of sin is in us all, and we are dying of it the same. Do not despair of it. The cure has been lifted up.

Do not look to me; I was only the man who raised the pole. Do not look to the bronze, nor to any relic, nor to your own strength to climb up out of the poison — you cannot, and you were never asked to. The cure was never something you carry up. It is a look. Lift your eyes to Jesus, lifted up for you, and believe on Him.

You do not have to clean the wound first. You do not have to be strong enough, or worthy enough, or to understand it all as I did not understand it. The dying child in our camp knew enough to turn his head. Turn yours. Look to the Son of man lifted up; trust the One who was made your curse and carried it clean away.

Look, and live. Come to Him — and feel the death let go of you.
