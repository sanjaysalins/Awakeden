## Independent adversarial review — *Look and Live* (Moses / bronze serpent, long)

---

### EW-INV-4 — KJV verbatim + attribution

**Verbatim:** All bold `**"..."**` spans I checked match KJV substrings in `passage.txt` (Numbers 21:4–9, John 3:14–16, John 12:32, Galatians 3:13). No altered words inside the quote marks.

**Attribution is uneven — and that is a real defect under this format.** Several KJV lines float without a named speaker/source before the quote, while `[the people]`, `[the LORD]`, and `[Jesus]` lines are tagged correctly:

| Quote | Issue |
|-------|-------|
| **`"And the LORD sent fiery serpents among the people..."`** | No `[the LORD]` / scripture frame immediately before the quote — reads as Moses ventriloquizing narrative text. |
| **`"And Moses made a serpent of brass..."`** | Same — narrator-voice scripture with no attribution. |
| **`"being made a curse for us: for it is written, Cursed is every one that hangeth on a tree:"`** | Mid-verse excerpt of Gal 3:13 with no `[Paul]` / `[scripture]` tag; the subject *Christ* is stripped from the quote itself, so the line is grammatically orphaned if heard as a standalone citation. |
| **`"For God so loved the world..."`** | John 3:16 unattributed at the end of Beat 6 — after three tagged `[Jesus]` lines, a listener cannot tell who is speaking. |

The Beat 2 line **"and the Scripture does not flatter us — it tells the plain truth:"** partially attributes the *previous* fragment (`"the soul of the people was much discouraged..."`), but the pattern is inconsistent: some quotes get speaker tags, others do not. Under EW-INV-4 and the pattern flagged across sibling episodes (EW01–EW03), that is not cosmetic — it is a format failure.

### EW-INV-2 — CTA on Jesus

**Mostly passes.** Beat 7 names Jesus explicitly (**"Lift your eyes to Jesus"**), names the response (**look / believe / trust / come**), and stays grace-anchored (**"You do not have to clean the wound first"** / **"You do not have to be strong enough, or worthy enough"**). No bare **"will you trust Him?"** template. No fear/gain-loss pressure.

**Minor clarity risk:** **"Come to Him — and feel the death let go of you"** ties assurance to felt experience; the text’s own logic is *look and believe*, not guaranteed sensation. Not a hard fail, but the close could over-promise to a hurting listener.

### EW-G7 — Reveal earned

**Partial pass, with a structural weakness.**

**What works:** Beat 4 plants a real riddle from Moses’ lived moment — **"Why a serpent?"** / **"the very shape of what was striking them down"** / **"Why save a people by the sight of the very thing that slays them?"** That is shown from the Numbers event, not imported.

**What sags:** Beat 6 opens with an announcement frame — **"I did not see the answer in my own day. I tell you this now from the far side of my life"** — then leans heavily on apostolic/NT dump (John 3:14–15, Gal 3:13 fragment, John 12:32, John 3:16). The serpent→curse→cross link is *asserted* in Moses-voice prose (**"the spotless Son of God was made to be the curse that was killing us"**) and then proof-texted. Jesus naming the type (John 3:14) is legitimate, but the turn is more **"here is what was written after me"** than **"the day itself forced this meaning."**

**Best earned beat in the reveal:** Nehushtan — **"they kept that serpent and burned incense to the metal of it, until a good king of Judah broke it to pieces and called it Nehushtan"** — directly pays off Beat 5’s **"I knew the bronze had no power in it."** That lands. It arrives late and is historical postscript, not wilderness-lived.

### EW-G8 — Wrestling (steel-man)

**Mixed — one strong thread, one weak one, one invented claim.**

**Strong:** Moses-specific doubt — **"I knew the bronze had no power in it. I had ground a calf of gold to bitter powder..."** / **"Why a sign at all?"** That is the genuine post–golden-calf tension an Israelite mediator would carry. Good voice, right objection class.

**Weak / strawman-adjacent:** Opening Beat 5 paragraph — **"Not everyone looked. Some, I am sure of it, lay in their tents too proud to turn their heads"** — is not in Numbers 21. It shifts wrestling from *Moses’* doubt to *Israel’s* pride, and **"I am sure of it"** presents invention as eyewitness certainty (EW-INV-11 hazard).

**Under-developed for long-form:** The idolatry-of-the-sign objection (the actual steel-man after Ex 32) gets one sentence before resolving into **"I felt as though I were holding up a signpost... pointing past itself."** Beat 5 is ~150 spoken words in a ~1634-word piece — the thinnest interior beat. For a 9–11 min long that promises wrestling earns runtime, this beat does not carry its weight.

**Also thin:** Beat 3 raises **"I expected the LORD to take the serpents away. He did not."** — a real steel-man — but Beat 5 never returns to wrestle *why God leaves the serpents in the camp*. That question is dropped.

---

### EW-G9 — Doctrine

**Sound direction; substitution naming is incomplete by this spec’s standard.**

- Typology is mainstream (John 3:14, Gal 3:13, curse on the tree). No supersession. No contrarian reading.
- **Curse** is named clearly: **"made to be the curse"** / **"made your curse and carried it clean away."**
- **Penalty/substitution swap is under-specified.** The piece maps bite → sin (**"the venom of sin is in us all"**) and lift-look → faith, but never crisply states what Christ *bore in the sinner’s place*: the **death/judgment** due to sin. **"Carried it clean away"** is removal language, not *He died under the bite that was ours* / *He stood where the dying man stood*. Sibling reviews (EW03) flagged the same gap when guilt/curse were named but penalty was not.

**Metaphor stretch to watch:** **"He became, upon that pole, the very poison of our sin"** — typologically intended, but a zero-Bible listener may hear modalism-of-poison rather than penal substitution. Needs the prose guardrail that He bore the *penalty*, not that He literally became sin-substance.

### EW-G10 — Voice / character

**Mostly Moses; not fully consistent.**

**Strong Moses markers:** **"these two hands"**, Egypt schooling, gold-calf powder, mediator praying, old-age honesty (**"a man this near his grave has no reason to be otherwise"**).

**Essayist slips:** **"Discouraged turned to anger, and anger turned its mouth against heaven"** — polished literary pivot, not an old man remembering. **"A shadow waits for the body that casts it"** — sermon craft. Beat 2–3 occasionally sound like a narrator writing *about* Israel rather than Moses inside the camp.

**Invented vivid detail (borderline EW-INV-11):** **"I watched mothers cradle children who had been laughing an hour before"** — emotionally effective, historically unstated, presented as lived memory.

---

### First-hearing clarity

**Generally clear.** The look-to-live mechanic is repeated enough that a novice can follow.

**Stumbles:**
- Beat 1: **"God told a dying man that he could be healed by nothing more than a look"** — God told *Moses* the command; dying men heard it secondhand. Minor but real confusion for a first-time listener.
- Beat 6: **"a Teacher in Israel, and to a man who sought Him by night"** — Nicodemus context without the name; fine for Bible readers, opaque for zero-knowledge listeners.
- **"Nehushtan — a nothing, a scrap of brass"** — good gloss, but arrives fast in a dense reveal beat.

---

### Hook, spine, long-form pacing

**Hook:** **"I am Moses... A pole driven into the sand. And upon it, a serpent of bronze."** — grips. One witness, one thread; no swap.

**Runtime (~1634 words):** Inside budget, but **Beat 5 underfills** while Beats 3–4–6–7 **repeat the same turn** without always advancing it:
- **"only a look"** / **"lift their eyes"** / **"look and live"** — Beats 3, 4, 6, 7
- **"the death let go of him"** (Beat 3) → **"feel the death let go of you"** (Beat 7) — bookend or padding?
- Bronze-has-no-power / don’t worship the sign — Beat 5 and again in Nehushtan (Beat 6)

The piece does not catastrophically sag, but the middle **re-states** more than it **complicates** before the NT reveal.
