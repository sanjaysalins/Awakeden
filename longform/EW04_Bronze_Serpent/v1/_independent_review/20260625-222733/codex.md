# Independent review — codex (OK, 56s)

Findings:

- EW-INV-4 attribution failure: **"being made a curse for us: for it is written, Cursed is every one that hangeth on a tree:"** is KJV wording from Galatians 3:13, but it is quoted with no speaker/writer attribution. Attribute Paul before the quote.
- EW-INV-4 attribution failure: **"For God so loved the world..."** is quoted with no explicit speaker attribution. If treating John 3:16 as Jesus speaking in the Nicodemus discourse, label it. If treating it as John’s narration, label that. Right now it is unattributed.
- Possible EW-INV-4 formatting weakness: **[the people]** and **[the LORD]** are labels, but the artifact often introduces narrative quotes without equivalent attribution, e.g. **"And the LORD sent fiery serpents..."** and **"And Moses made..."**. If the rule is “speaker before quoting,” narrative citations need consistent source attribution such as Scripture / Numbers.
- Invented eyewitness certainty: **"Not everyone looked. Some, I am sure of it..."** is not in Numbers 21. It may be plausible, but “I am sure of it” turns inference into claimed eyewitness fact. That is risky for an eyewitness narration.
- The wrestling partly straw-mans the refusal: **"too proud to turn their heads — it was too simple, too foolish"** imports a pride-based motive not supplied by the text. The stronger objection already present is better: why would God heal through the image of the very killer, especially after the golden calf?
- Doctrine needs tightening: **"He became, upon that pole, the very poison of our sin"** is rhetorically strong but imprecise. Christ bore sin, curse, wrath, and penalty; He did not become sinful or become “poison” in His nature. This needs clearer substitution language: what penalty He bore and what was credited/laid on Him.
- Substitution is present but under-specified. **"trust the One who was made your curse and carried it clean away"** names curse, but the penalty is not as clearly named as the lens asks. “Carried it clean away” is vivid but vague.
- The reveal is mostly earned through John 3, but this line over-announces: **"what that pole in the sand was always reaching toward."** The text earns “lifted up” and “look/believe/live”; the “serpent = curse = Christ made curse” move needs one more shown step, not just **"And mark why it had to be a serpent"**.
- First-hearing clarity problem: **"Son of man"**, **"Teacher in Israel"**, **"Nehushtan"**, and **"only begotten Son"** are dropped with little explanation. A zero-Bible listener can follow the main story, but these terms create fog at the reveal.
- Voice sometimes turns into essay narration: **"It was a shadow, and a shadow waits for the body that casts it. The body came."** That sounds like theological commentary more than Moses’ embodied first-person memory.
- Long-form sag: Beat 4 and Beat 5 repeat the same idea several times: **"Why a serpent?"**, **"Why save a people by the sight of the very thing that slays them?"**, **"So what, then, was the bronze for? Why a sign at all?"** Some repetition is useful, but this beat lingers without enough new turn.
- CTA is mostly compliant because it names Jesus and the response: **"Lift your eyes to Jesus... and believe on Him"**, **"Come to Him"**. The weak spot is **"feel the death let go of you"**, which risks promising a felt experience rather than anchoring the invitation in Christ’s finished work.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Fix quote attribution, especially Galatians 3:13 and John 3:16.
2. Tighten substitution language so Christ bears sin’s curse/penalty without sounding like He becomes evil.
3. Remove or qualify the invented “Not everyone looked” material and sharpen the real Moses-level objection.
