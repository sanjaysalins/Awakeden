### EW-INV-4 — KJV verbatim + attribution

**Verbatim:** No altered words inside the quote marks that I could find. Numbers 21:4–9, John 3:14–15, John 12:32, John 3:16, and the Gal 3:13 excerpt all match KJV substrings.

**Attribution still fails on multiple quotes** — the pattern is inconsistent: `[the people]`, `[the LORD]`, and `[Jesus]` lines are tagged; others float:

| Quote | Problem |
|-------|---------|
| **`"And the LORD sent fiery serpents among the people..."`** | No `[scripture]` / narrator frame immediately before the block — Moses appears to speak narrative text as his own voice. |
| **`"And Moses made a serpent of brass..."`** | Third-person Scripture about Moses, with no **"the record says"** / `[scripture]` frame. First-person witness suddenly quotes a verse that names him in the third person — voice rupture plus EW-INV-4 breach. |
| **`"being made a curse for us: for it is written, Cursed is every one that hangeth on a tree:"`** | Preceded only by *"For so the apostle wrote of Him:"* — no `[Paul]` tag on the quote itself; the excerpt drops the opening **"Christ hath redeemed us from the curse of the law,"** so **Christ** is absent from the spoken citation. Heard alone, the line is grammatically orphaned. |
| **`"For God so loved the world..."`** | Preceded by *"For so the gospel was written:"* — prose frame, but no `[John]` / `[scripture]` tag; after three tagged `[Jesus]` lines, a listener cannot tell who is speaking. |

The Beat 2 fragment **`"the soul of the people was much discouraged because of the way."`** is partially framed by *"and the Scripture does not flatter us — it tells the plain truth:"* — better, but still no dedicated scripture voice tag. Under this format’s binding rule (attribute **before** quoting, dedicated scripture voice), that is not enough when sibling episodes are held to bracket tags.

### EW-INV-2 — CTA on Jesus

**Passes.** Beat 7 names **Jesus** explicitly (*"but to Jesus, who was made your curse and carried it clean away"*), names the response (**look** — not vague trust), and stays grace-anchored (*"costs you nothing but a look"* / *"You cannot climb out of the poison — you were never asked to"*). No bare *"will you trust Him?"* template. No fear/gain-loss pressure; *"only now there is no fear left in it"* explicitly disclaims fear framing.

**Minor:** CTA is **look**-typed; John 3:15’s **believe** is quoted in Beat 6 but not echoed in the close. A zero-Bible listener could hear “look” as physical glance without the faith link — Beat 6’s *"the faith that takes Him at His word"* helps, but the invitation itself does not bridge look → believe.

### EW-G7 — Reveal earned

**Mixed — improved, not fully earned from Moses’s lived moment.**

**What works:** Beat 4 plants a real riddle from the event — *"Why a serpent?"* / *"the very shape of what was striking them down"* / *"Why save a people by the sight of the very thing that slays them?"* Beat 5’s signpost dread (*"pointing past itself, down a long road of years to something I could not yet see"*) sets up the turn. Nehushtan pays off Beat 5’s idol-sign fear: *"they kept that serpent and burned incense to the metal of it... and called it Nehushtan."*

**What sags:** Beat 6 opens by **announcing** the reveal — *"I did not see the answer in my own day. I tell you this now from the far side of my life — by the light that came long after I laid down my staff, and by the word that was written after me — what that pole in the sand was always reaching toward."* Then stacks four NT quotes (John 3:14–15, Gal 3:13 fragment, John 12:32, John 3:16). The serpent→curse→cross link is **asserted** in Moses-voice prose (*"the spotless Son of God was made to be the curse that was killing us"*) and proof-texted, rather than **walked out** from Beat 4’s riddle before Jesus speaks. *"There it was"* after John 3:14 is tell, not show.

John 3:14 is a legitimate hinge (Jesus names the type). But for EW-G7 the wilderness material does less of the lifting than the post-hoc NT dump — acceptable as a signposted device (EW-INV-11), not fully **earned**.

### EW-G8 — Wrestling (steel-man)

**Mostly passes — one weak paragraph, one dropped thread.**

**Strong (Moses-specific):** *"With these same hands I had ground a calf of gold to powder... I, who had carried down the commandment to make no graven thing, was now told to make one and set it on high. It kept me awake: had God bidden the very sin I had just broken?"* — genuine post–golden-calf steel-man, internalized in Moses’s voice. Good payoff via Nehushtan.

**Weak opening:** *"And I feared for any who would not look. A cure so simple is its own stumbling-block: a proud heart can scorn to turn its head for a thing so plain..."* — this is Moses worrying about **others’** pride, not **his** honest doubt. It reads pastoral/strawman-adjacent compared to the graven-image dread that follows. Not invented certainty (prior draft’s *"I am sure of it"* is gone — good), but not the witness’s strongest internal objection.

**Dropped steel-man:** Beat 3 raises *"I expected the LORD to take the serpents away. He did not."* — a real objection (*why leave the killers in the camp?*) — but Beat 5 never returns to wrestle it. The piece resolves the sign; it never resolves the ongoing snakes.

### EW-G9 — Doctrine

**Sound.** Typology is mainstream (John 3:14, Gal 3:13). No supersession. Substitution is **named** with both halves: *"He bore, upon that pole, the judgment our sin had earned, taken in our place"* plus *"made to be the curse"* / *"made your curse and carried it clean away."*

**Watch this phrase:** *"the spotless Son of God was made to be the curse that was killing us"* — typologically intended (serpent = image of what kills; Christ = curse-bearer), but a first-hearing listener may hear **modal confusion** (Christ literally became the serpent-poison) rather than **penal substitution**. The next line (*"judgment our sin had earned"*) guards it; the line still runs hot.

### EW-G10 — Voice / character

**Mostly Moses; several essayist slips.**

**Strong:** *"these two hands"*, Egypt schooling, calf ground to powder, mediator praying, *"a man this near his grave has no reason to be otherwise."*

**Breaks:**
- **`"And Moses made a serpent of brass..."`** — third-person Scripture in first-person mouth (see EW-INV-4).
- **`"Sit with that."`** (Beat 7) — modern mindfulness/director register; not an old wilderness leader’s idiom.
- **`"So hear me — be still."`** — stage-direction essayist, same family flagged on Abraham/Aaron siblings.
- **`"A shadow waits for the body that casts it."`** — polished sermon craft, not campfire memory.
- **`"a Teacher in Israel, and to a man who sought Him by night"`** — Nicodemus context without the name; fine for Bible readers, opaque for zero-knowledge listeners.

**Borderline EW-INV-11:** *"I watched mothers cradle children who had been laughing an hour before"* — vivid, unstated in Numbers 21, presented as lived memory.

### Hook, spine, long-form pacing

**Hook:** *"I am Moses... A pole driven into the sand. And upon it, a serpent of bronze."* — grips. One witness, one thread; no swap.

**Word budget:** ~1627 spoken words — inside 1300–1650.

**Sag/repetition:** Better than prior draft, but the **look / no climbing / poison-in-you** turn still restates without always complicating:
- Beat 3: *"No payment. No climbing. No cure of his own making."*
- Beat 4: *"only a look"* / *"by lifting their eyes"*
- Beat 6: *"need only look to Him and live"*
- Beat 7: *"You cannot climb out of the poison"* / *"costs you nothing but a look"*

Beat 5 now earns its runtime (graven-image dread). Beats 3–4–7 still **re-state** the same gospel logic more than they **turn** it before the invitation. Not catastrophic sag, but the back half could tighten one full lap of the “only a look” motif.
