# Independent review — grok (OK, 45s)

## Findings (adversarial)

### EW-INV-4 — KJV verbatim + attribution

**Altered KJV (not verbatim):**

1. **Numbers 21:6** — artifact uses a semicolon; KJV has a comma:
   - **"And the LORD sent fiery serpents among the people, and they bit the people; and much people of Israel died."**
   - KJV: `...they bit the people, and much people of Israel died.`

2. **Numbers 21:8** — **"a fiery serpent"** vs KJV **"an fiery serpent"**. Changing `an` → `a` is an altered word, not a harmless modernization.

3. **Numbers 21:9** — **"when he beheld the serpent of brass"** vs KJV **"when he looked upon the serpent of brass"**. `beheld` ≠ `looked`.

**Missing or weak attribution before quotes:**

4. **"the soul of the people was much discouraged because of the way."** — framed as Scripture, but no speaker tag (`[Scripture]`, `[Moses reading]`, etc.) immediately before the quote. Rule requires attribution *before* the quoted words.

5. **"And the LORD sent fiery serpents among the people..."** — no `[the LORD]` or `[Scripture]` tag; it drops into narrative without attribution.

6. **"And Moses made a serpent of brass..."** — same: no attribution before the block.

7. **"being made a curse for us: for it is written, Cursed is every one that hangeth on a tree:"** — Paul/Galatians 3:13 is quoted with no speaker tag. The line **"For so the apostle wrote of Him:"** comes *after* the setup but the quote itself still lacks the required `[Paul]` / `[the apostle]` marker on the quote line.

8. **"For God so loved the world..."** — John 3:16 quoted with no `[Jesus]` / `[Scripture]` attribution on the quote itself (**"For so the gospel was written:"** is too indirect).

---

### EW-G7 — Reveal must be earned, not announced

9. **"I did not see the answer in my own day. I tell you this now from the far side of my life — by the light that came long after I laid down my staff, and by the word that was written after me"** — this *announces* delayed understanding instead of *showing* the type→Christ turn from what Moses lived. The bronze-serpent logic is set up well in Beats 3–5, but the Christ link arrives by posthumous receipt of John 3:14, not by Moses threading his own experience to the cross.

10. **"what that pole in the sand was always reaching toward"** — retrospective certainty. Beat 5 ends honestly: **"I forged it. I could not have told you what it meant."** Beat 6 then jumps to full typological confidence. The turn is asserted from “far side” hindsight more than demonstrated from Numbers.

11. **"My serpent was never the point of it. It was a shadow, and a shadow waits for the body that casts it."** — polished essay line; the *idea* is orthodox, but Moses deduces it from later texts, not from the wilderness event itself. Listener gets the answer because the narrator now has John 3:14, not because the witness’s lived detail *proved* Christ.

---

### EW-G8 — Wrestling: steel-man vs strawman

12. Beat 5 opens with generic pastoral doubt: **"I feared for any who would not look. A cure so simple is its own stumbling-block: a proud heart can scorn to turn its head..."** — plausible, but not *Moses’s* strongest Numbers-specific objection and not internalized from the account. It reads like inserted application, not the witness’s deepest trouble.

13. The real steel-man — **"I, who had carried down the commandment to make no graven thing, was now told to make one and set it on high. It kept me awake: had God bidden the very sin I had just broken?"** — is strong and Moses-specific. Problem: it’s buried *after* the weaker pride paragraph, so the wrestling beat leads with straw-ish material and only then gets to steel.

---

### EW-G9 — Doctrine

14. Mostly sound: substitution is named — **"He bore, upon that pole, the judgment our sin had earned, taken in our place"** — penalty + bearing in our place. Good.

15. Slight imprecision: **"the spotless Son of God was made to be the curse that was killing us"** — risks conflating (a) fiery-serpent *judgment* with (b) Christ *becoming a curse for us* (Gal. 3:13). Christ bore the curse; He was not “made to be” the serpent-curse itself. A careful listener could mishear modalism of curse = Christ rather than substitution *for* curse.

16. Nehushtan section is sound and anti-idolatry: **"they kept that serpent and burned incense to the metal of it... He was right to break it."** — no supersession problem.

---

### EW-INV-2 — CTA on Jesus

17. Close names Jesus and names the response (look): **"Look to Him, and live."** — not a bare “will you trust Him?” template. Grace-anchored: **"costs you nothing but a look"**, **"You cannot climb out of the poison — you were never asked to."**

18. Minor weakness: **"will you lift your eyes and look?"** echoes the piece’s refrain heavily; the CTA is on-theme but not fresh. Still passes the letter of the rule because Jesus and the action are named.

---

### EW-G10 — Voice / character

19. First-person Moses is mostly consistent and characterful: **"these hands"**, **"all the wisdom of Egypt I had been schooled in"**, golden-calf guilt.

20. Drift toward detached essay in Beat 6: **"a shadow waits for the body that casts it"** and **"The body came."** — abstract typology language, less the old man who dug graves and forged bronze.

---

### First-hearing clarity

21. Golden-calf thread assumes prior knowledge: **"ground a calf of gold to powder and made Israel drink it"** — one line, no scene-setting. Zero-Bible listener may not grasp why that makes a bronze serpent-on-a-pole terrifying for Moses.

22. **"Nehushtan — a nothing, a scrap of brass"** — vivid for insiders; outsiders get the gloss but not *why* a king broke it or when. Not fatal, but the beat leans on Bible literacy.

---

### Hook, spine, long-form runtime

23. Hook grips: **"A pole driven into the sand. And upon it, a serpent of bronze."** — strong. One witness, one thread; no swap.

24. **Runtime sag / repetition** — same turn restated without new information:
   - Beat 3: **"No payment. No climbing. No cure of his own making. He looked, and the death let go of him."**
   - Beat 7: **"The cure is not in you... costs you nothing but a look."**
   - Beat 4 + Beat 5 both circle **"Why a serpent?"** / **"Why a sign at all?"**
   
   Beat 7 invitation largely re-preaches Beat 6 rather than adding a final distinct turn. For ~9–11 min, Beats 6–7 could be tighter; Nehushtan earns its place, the invitation beat less so.

---

### What works (for balance, not praise)

- Numbers narrative spine is clear and follows the text.
- Golden-calf vs bronze-serpent tension is the best original beat in the piece.
- John 3:14 → cross → look/faith chain is standard, orthodox typology.
- Anti-idolatry via Nehushtan is a useful late turn.

3. Restructure Beat 5 so the graven-image / second-commandment wrestling leads (steel-man first); trim generic pride-stumbling-block padding; tighten Beat 7 so the close adds one new landing beat instead of repeating look/live/cure-not-in-you.
