# Independent review — grok (OK, 35s)

## Findings

### KJV verbatim + attribution (EW-INV-4)
**Clean on the formal quotes.** Both attributed speakers match the text, and the quoted words are exact KJV.

- **`[the LORD]`** — **"Make thee a fiery serpent, and set it upon a pole: and it shall come to pass, that every one that is bitten, when he looketh upon it, shall live."** — Numbers 21:8, verbatim.
- **`[Jesus]`** — **"And as Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, even so must the Son of man be lifted up:"** — John 3:14, verbatim.

**Soft paraphrase, not a flagged quote violation:** *"the God who said look and live"* compresses *"when he looketh upon it, he shall live"* — fine in narration, but it is not a KJV line.

**Beat 1 accuracy slip:** *"And God told a dying man he could be healed"* — God told **Moses** the command; the bitten looked afterward. A zero-Bible listener may think God spoke directly to one victim.

---

### CTA-on-Jesus (EW-INV-2)
**Mostly strong.** Jesus is named; the response is named (*believe*, *turn*, *look*, *come*), not a bare *"will you trust Him?"* template. *"You need not clean the wound first"* is grace-anchored.

**Minor pressure risk:** *"So hear me, you who are bitten — and that is every one of us"* works as metaphor, but *"every one of us"* slightly universalizes before the cross logic is fully shown for a first-time listener.

---

### The reveal is earned (EW-G7) — **main weakness**
The turn is **announced, then proof-texted**, not shown from what Moses lived.

- *"For years I could not tell what it meant."* — understanding is declared missing, not dramatized.
- *"I speak now from the far side of my life, by the light that came after."* — retrospective essay frame, not witness discovery.
- *"A Teacher rose in Israel; by night a man sought Him, and He spoke of my serpent:"* — Moses reports a scene he never saw; the reveal rides on **John 3:14 quoted secondhand**, not on the pole moment itself.
- *"My serpent was only a shadow; Christ was the body that cast it."* — typology **asserted**, not walked out from bronze + bite + look.

Beat 3 sets up the puzzle (*"a command so strange I have pondered it ever since"*), but Beat 6 **does not pay off that pondering** from Numbers 21. It jumps to later revelation.

---

### The wrestling is the real steel-man (EW-G8) — **missing**
There is **no dedicated wrestling beat** (Beat 2 / 4 / 5 absent). The strongest objections are not internalized in Moses's voice.

What we get instead:
- *"I prayed for the serpents to be taken away — but the LORD did not."* — real tension, then dropped.
- *"A command so strange I have pondered it ever since"* — mild wonder, not steel-man doubt.

**Unvoiced steel-man objections Moses would actually carry:**
- Why forge the **image of the very thing killing us**?
- Why heal by **looking at a likeness**, not at the LORD directly?
- Why leave the serpents in the camp at all?

The piece never lets Moses argue with God in his own voice. That leaves EW-G8 thin.

---

### Doctrine sound (EW-G9)
**Core typology is evangelical and sound:** lifted image, faith-response, substitution language (*"made to be the curse, bearing the judgment our sin had earned"*). No supersession problem spotted.

**Invented detail:** *"The dying child knew enough to turn his head."* — Numbers 21 has no child, no age-specific victim, no head-turning vignette. This is **fabricated scene dressing** dressed up as biblical memory. Weakens fidelity and witness credibility.

**Interpretive color:** *"Roman pole"* is editorial, not text-derived — minor, but it nudges toward sermon-commentary rather than lived memory.

---

### Voice / character (EW-G10)
Beat 1–3: convincing Moses — *"dying faster than I could dig the graves"* is vivid and in-character.

Beat 6 **breaks first-person witness discipline:**
- *"A Teacher rose in Israel; by night a man sought Him"* — detached third-person Gospel report.
- Moses becomes a **typology essayist with posthumous omniscience**, not a man who lived the wilderness day and is now wrestling it through.

That costume-slip undercuts the eyewitness contract.

---

### First-hearing clarity
- Opening crisis is clear.
- *"shadow… body that cast it"* may lose listeners with no Bible background.
- *"made to be the curse"* is doctrinally right but **unexplained** for a first-time hearer.
- The invented *"dying child"* may confuse: listeners will assume it is in Scripture.

---

### Hook, spine, short-form
**Hook:** strong — graves, serpents, dust, immediate stakes.

**Spine:** one thread (pole → lifted Christ → look/live). No crowding second story.

**Length:** ~260 spoken words — inside the ~220–320 band; tight enough.

**Land:** the single moment mostly lands, but **sag is in the missing wrestle** between act and reveal; Beat 6's essay pivot slows the earned turn.

---

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Add a real wrestling beat in Moses's voice — strongest doubt voiced and internalized (why an image of the killer; why not remove the serpents; why look at bronze instead of God).
2. Earn the reveal from the wilderness moment itself before/alongside John 3:14 — pay off *"pondered it ever since"* with insight from bite/look/lifted curse-image; cut or rework *"For years I could not tell"* / secondhand Nicodemus report.
3. Delete *"The dying child knew enough to turn his head"* (invented); fix Beat 1's *"God told a dying man"* to God commanding Moses for the bitten; keep Beat 6 in first-person witness memory, not third-person Gospel narration.
