# Independent review — codex (OK, 31s)

Findings:

- **KJV / attribution: no quote violation found.**  
  `**[the LORD]** **"Make thee a fiery serpent..."**` is exact KJV from Numbers 21:8 after the attribution clause. `**[Jesus]** **"And as Moses lifted up..."**` is exact KJV from John 3:14 and correctly attributed.

- **Reveal is somewhat asserted, not fully shown.**  
  The turn depends on `“My serpent was only a shadow; Christ was the body that cast it.”` That is true, but it arrives as explanation. The artifact shows “lifted up” and “look/live,” but it does not linger enough on the scandal Moses actually faced: looking at the image of the judged thing. The best bridge is present in `“the image of the very thing that was killing us”`, but the reveal should make that line carry the Christ-turn more directly.

- **The wrestling is too light.**  
  `“For years I could not tell what it meant”` is plausible, but it is not the strongest objection in Moses’ voice. The real difficulty is sharper: Why would God command the likeness of the serpent, when serpents were the judgment? Why would healing come by looking instead of removing the snakes? The piece names strangeness, but does not fully internalize the objection.

- **Potential first-hearing confusion.**  
  `“A Teacher rose in Israel; by night a man sought Him”` assumes knowledge of Nicodemus and John 3. A zero-Bible listener may not know who this man is or why the night matters. It adds a second mini-thread without helping the short form much.

- **Doctrine is mostly sound, but substitution could be more explicit.**  
  `“made to be the curse, bearing the judgment our sin had earned”` is strong, but the penalty is not named as clearly as the lens asks. “Judgment” is close; “death/curse/penalty in our place” would make substitution unmistakable.

- **Voice is good but occasionally essay-like.**  
  `“Christ was the body that cast it”` sounds theological and polished rather than Moses recounting a lived memory. The strongest voice is in `“faster than I could dig the graves”` and `“Turn yours.”`

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Make the reveal earn itself through the scandal of looking at the judged serpent, not just “shadow/body” assertion.
2. Strengthen Moses’ wrestling: why this command, why not remove the serpents, why the image of judgment?
3. Clarify substitution by naming that Jesus bore the curse/death penalty for sin in our place.
