## EW-INV-4 — KJV verbatim + attribution

**The two formal quotes pass.** Both are tagged before the bold span and match `passage.txt` / KJV:

- `**[the LORD]** **"Make thee a fiery serpent, and set it upon a pole: and it shall come to pass, that every one that is bitten, when he looketh upon it, shall live."**` — Numbers 21:8, verbatim.
- `**[Jesus]** **"And as Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, even so must the Son of man be lifted up:"**` — John 3:14, verbatim.

**Non-quote accuracy slip:** Beat 1 — *"God told me there was healing only by a look"* — compresses the command. In the text God told **Moses** to make the sign; the bitten looked afterward. Not a quote violation, but a zero-Bible listener may think God spoke healing directly to victims, not through Moses’s obedience.

## EW-G7 — Reveal earned — **weak / partly asserted**

John 3:14 is the right hinge (Jesus names Moses’s serpent). But the turn **around** it is announced and explained, not walked out from what Moses lived.

- *"For years its meaning escaped me"* — understanding is **declared missing**, not dramatized from the pole moment.
- *"I speak now from the far side of my life, by the light that came after"* — retrospective essay frame, not witness discovery.
- *"a night I never saw: one they called Teacher answered a seeker"* — Moses reports a scene he **never witnessed**; the Christ link rides on secondhand Gospel report, not the wilderness day.
- *"My serpent was only a shadow; Christ was the body that cast it"* — typology **asserted** in polished theological language, not shown from bite → lifted curse-image → look → live.

Beat 3 plants the puzzle — *"Forge the image of the very thing killing us"* / *"heal by looking at bronze, not by clearing serpents away?"* — but Beat 6 **does not pay that off** before jumping to shadow/body commentary and *"Roman pole."* The best bridge (*image of what was killing them, lifted, looked at, lived*) is stated in Beat 3 and abandoned in favor of post-hoc NT receipt.

Cross-time reveal is signposted (EW-INV-11), but under EW-G7 the wilderness material does too little lifting.

## EW-G8 — Wrestling — **present but thin**

Improved over earlier cuts: Beat 3 now voices real tension:

- *"I prayed for the serpents to be taken away — but the LORD did not."*
- *"Forge the image of the very thing killing us — a graven thing?"*
- *"heal by looking at bronze, not by clearing serpents away?"*

**Still underdeveloped for Moses specifically.** The strongest steel-man for **this** witness — the man who smashed the golden calf and carried *"Thou shalt not make unto thee any graven image"* — is only hinted at by *"a graven thing?"* Two rhetorical questions, then immediate *"Yet I obeyed"* with no beat sitting in the dread. The prayer-refused objection (*serpents stay; mercy comes a stranger way*) is raised and dropped for the image puzzle.

Resolution (*"It was never the bronze that healed them, but the God who said look and live"*) is sound but rushes past the objection. For SHORT, wrestling is non-zero; for EW-G8 it is not yet the **genuine strongest** objection internalized in Moses’s own voice.

## EW-G9 — Doctrine — **orthodox typology, substitution under-named / ambiguous**

**Sound:** lifted-image typology, faith-as-look, no supersession, no contrarian reading.

**Substitution not fully named** per EW-INV-7 / EW-G9 (penalty + what Christ bore):

- *"They lifted Jesus on a Roman pole, made the curse our sin had earned"* — grammatically muddy. Who *made* the curse? Reads like crowd/Roman action, not **Christ bearing the curse/penalty in our place** (contrast Gal 3:13 in `passage.txt`: *"being made a curse for us"*).
- CTA *"the curse borne"* — passive; does not name **what** was borne **for whom**.
- Penalty side (serpent-bite death / earned judgment) is clear in Beat 1; the **in-our-place** half on Christ is implied, not nailed.

*"Roman pole"* is editorial commentary, not Moses’s lived vocabulary — minor sermon bleed.

## EW-G10 — Voice / character

**Beats 1–3:** Strong Moses — *"died faster than I could dig the graves"* is vivid, first-person, in-character. Wrestling lines feel like a man who forged bronze in the dust.

**Beat 6 breaks the eyewitness contract:**

- *"one they called Teacher answered a seeker"* — detached third-person Gospel narration.
- *"My serpent was only a shadow; Christ was the body that cast it"* — essayist typology, not testimony.
- *"They lifted Jesus on a Roman pole"* — posthumous omniscience Moses did not live.

Moses becomes a **typology essayist wearing a Moses mask** in the reveal beat, not a witness threading his own day to Christ.
