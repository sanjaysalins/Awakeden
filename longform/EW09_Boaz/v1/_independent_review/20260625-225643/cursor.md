## Independent review — *The Kinsman-Redeemer* (Boaz, long)

### EW-INV-4 — KJV verbatim + attribution

**No altered KJV words detected** in bold quotes against `passage.txt`. Spot-checks pass as substrings (Ruth 2:8–12, 3:9/12, 4:6–10/17; Gal 3:13; Titus 2:14; Eph 5:25/27).

**Attribution / framing problems remain:**

1. **Beat 2 — `"under whose wings thou art come to trust,"`** — Words are fine, but framing is off. Boaz says he *“prayed that the God of Israel”* would repay her; in Ruth 2:12 this is **Boaz’s direct blessing spoken to Ruth** (“The LORD recompense thy work… under whose wings thou art come to trust”), not a private prayer *about* God. The quote is attributed to the wrong speech-act.

2. **Beat 5 — `"how thou hast left thy father and thy mother…"`** — Also Boaz’s **field speech to Ruth** (Ruth 2:11), but it’s placed under *“I only knew what I had seen”* as if it were observed fact. Same speaker, wrong moment — reads like mis-timed testimony.

3. **Beat 3 — `"have I not charged the young men that they shall not touch thee?"`** — KJV-accurate, but prose says *“And I charged my young men to leave her unharmed”* then quotes Boaz’s **rhetorical question to Ruth**. A zero-Bible listener may not catch that this is reassurance, not a separate legal charge.

4. **Beat 3 — `"a man plucked off his shoe, and gave it to his neighbour."`** — Ruth 4:7’s **generic legal description**, not Boaz’s act (4:8: *“So he drew off his shoe”*). Not a verbatim fail, but the witness says *“I did the old custom”* then quotes third-person statute language — thin attribution for a first-person “I was there” account.

### EW-INV-2 — CTA on Jesus

**Passes.** Beat 7 names **Jesus** / **Christ**, invites **come**, **receive**, **trust**, and **be redeemed** — not the banned bare “will you trust Him?” template. Grace-anchored (*“You cannot earn it; the redeemed never pay”*). No fear/gain-loss lexicon.

Minor weakness: *“Trust the Kinsman who would not count the cost”* rhymes with Beat 5’s interior theme but doesn’t add a fresh turn at the close.

---

### EW-G7 — Reveal earned

**Mixed — partly earned, partly announced.**

**Earned well:**
- Beat 4’s economics (*“lest I mar mine own inheritance”* → Boaz’s costly love) maps cleanly to Christ *“willing to be marred.”*
- Genealogical bridge: *“he is the father of Jesse, the father of David”* → Bethlehem → Jesus.
- Bride/outsider thread: Moabitess taken in → church presented without spot.

**Not fully earned:**
- *“He is the true Kinsman-Redeemer, and He did what I only acted out in a barley field”* — **assertion before demonstration**. The turn is named, then illustrated.
- *“I had no idea, in that gate, that I was sketching the gospel”* — retrospective editorial; the Ruth scene doesn’t **show** “gospel,” it shows goel law. The Pauline curse-frame is imported, not walked out of the gate.
- *“That is the substitution my barley field could never show you”* — honest limit, but it admits Beat 4’s type **doesn’t** carry substitution; Gal 3:13 then does the doctrinal work from outside Ruth. The reveal leans on epistle quotes more than re-litigating the gate in Christ-shaped terms.

Signposting (*“by the light of the word written long after me”*) is correct per EW-INV-11; still, the Christ-turn is **half typology from lived detail, half NT overlay**.

---

### EW-G8 — Wrestling (steel-man)

**Weakest beat — likely the primary defect.**

Beat 4 already delivers the text’s real steel-man via the nearer kinsman: *“I cannot redeem it for myself, lest I mar mine own inheritance.”* That is the strongest objection **in Ruth**.

Beat 5 pivots to **Moab / tenth-generation exclusion** — material not in Ruth’s gate scene and **never voiced by Boaz in Scripture**, where he is immediately generous (field → threshing floor → gate).

**Chronology breaks character:**
- Beat 5: *“I wrestled **before I went up to that gate**.”*
- Beat 3 already has Boaz protecting Ruth in the field, saying at the threshing floor *“I would have done it that hour,”* and only then going to the gate.

So the Moabite-law doubt is both **post-hoc** and **mis-timed**: the witness acts as though resolved before he claims to have wrestled. That undercuts first-person credibility (EW-G10) and makes the objection feel **installed**, not lived.

The piece also skips the sharper Deuteronomic tension a steel-man might use: **how does a Moabitess enter the messianic line if the law held Moab at a distance?** Beat 5 gestures at the law but never wrestles *Ruth’s* inclusion — only Boaz’s generic “kindness vs letter.”

---

### EW-G9 — Doctrine

**Mostly sound.** Substitution is named (*“The curse that should have fallen on us fell on Him instead”* + Gal 3:13). Bride/redeemer typology is standard. No supersession — *“A Moabitess in the line of the Messiah — the outsider brought all the way in”* is inclusion, not replacement.

**Soft spot:** Jump from **goel land/levirate economics** to **“curse of the law”** is Pauline, not Ruth-native. Faithful for evangelical preaching, but the piece **asserts** the bridge (*“We owed the law a debt we could not pay, and the penalty was a curse”*) rather than deriving it from what Boaz actually bought at the gate. A skeptical listener may hear two sermons stitched together.

---

### EW-G10 — Voice / character

**Strong in Beats 1, 3, 4, 7** — Boaz sounds like a wealthy gate-elder with memory and cost.

**Essay bleed:**
- *“Redemption is costly, and only love will pay it — the price always falls on the redeemer”* (Beat 4) — thesis sentence, not a man remembering a morning.
- *“Let me tell you the weight of it”* / *“Now let me tell you what I did with my own hands”* — presenter pivots.
- *“sketching the gospel”* — modern meta for an OT witness, even with signposting.

Beat 5’s mis-timed wrestling is the biggest **character-consistency** hit.

---

### First-hearing clarity

**Mostly followable**, but:

- Beat 1 **spoils the gate** (*“I bought it all… took a Moabitess to be my wife”*), then Beat 3 **re-tells** the same gate at length. Zero-Bible listeners get the ending twice.
- *“a name that went back to Elimelech”* — unclear (family tie? lineage?). Elimelech is kin, not Boaz’s namesake.
- **Mahlon** appears in a quote (Beat 3) without setup.
- Threshing-floor scene is **helpfully glossed** (*“spread thy skirt”* = redeem) — good.

---

### Hook / spine

**Hook grips** (Beat 1 gate + shoe + Moabitess). **One witness, one thread** (goel redemption → Christ). No swap. Spine holds.

---

### Long-form pacing (~9–11 min)

**Borderline sag.** Beats 2→3→4 repeat the same nouns (foreign widow / stranger / costly redeemer) without enough new turns between them. Beat 4 asks *“why I would”*; Beat 5 asks again in different clothing. Beat 1 + Beat 3 **double-cover the gate**. Runtime is bought partly by recap, not only by new interior steel or dialogue.

Word count looks ~1,350–1,450 — in budget, but on the **lower** end; repetition makes it feel shorter than 9–11 minutes of earned material.

---

VERDICT: REVISE  
TOP FIXES:  
1. **Rebuild Beat 5** — either move wrestling **before** first kindness (field) or reframe it around the **inheritance-cost** objection Beat 4 already proves in-text; drop or sharply compress the Moab/tenth-generation thread unless Boaz can wrestle it **without contradicting** Beat 3’s “I would have done it that hour.”  
2. **De-spoil Beat 1 / compress Beat 3** — gate outcome once, not twice; cut duplicate “why would I redeem?” between Beats 4 and 5.  
3. **Earn the curse/substitution turn from the gate economics** (*mar / inheritance / name-not-mine*) before Gal 3:13, so the reveal shows Christ from Boaz’s riddle rather than announcing *“true Kinsman-Redeemer”* then importing Paul.
