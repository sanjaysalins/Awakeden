# Independent review — codex (OK, 125s)

KJV checked against Ruth 2-4, Galatians 3:13, Titus 2:14, and Ephesians 5:25-27. The main quoted words are mostly exact, but attribution and framing are not always clean. Findings:

- EW-INV-4 attribution weakness: **"Christ hath redeemed us from the curse of the law, being made a curse for us."** is introduced only as “as the Scripture says,” while the next quote is introduced “for Paul wrote.” If the rule requires speaker/writer attribution before quoting, this should name Paul/Galatians before the quote.

- EW-INV-4 attribution/context issue: **"a man plucked off his shoe, and gave it to his neighbour"** is not spoken by Boaz or the nearer kinsman; it is narrator explanation of the custom. The piece says, “And to seal it, by the old custom — [quote] — the nearer kinsman drew off his shoe and gave it to me.” That blurs generic narrator text into the concrete action.

- Textual accuracy problem: “First I gave her my field” is false or at least badly overstated. Boaz lets Ruth glean in his field; he does not give her the field. This matters because the whole type depends on redemption/purchase later at the gate.

- Factual/theological overstatement: “Redemption was not free — it cost the redeemer everything, and bought him nothing.” Boaz did not literally lose everything, and the text itself gives him Ruth, Obed, and public blessing. “Bought him nothing” fights Ruth 4 rather than illuminating it.

- Voice slips into essay mode: “That is the substitution my barley field could never show you” and “I tell you now by the light of the word written long after me” sound like a preacher explaining typology, not Boaz remembering and then awakening to Christ. The witness costume thins out in the reveal.

- The wrestling is partly strong but partly manufactured: “was kindness to the outsider a breach of the law, or the very heart the law had been pointing at all along?” is a good objection, but it needs sharper grounding. Israel’s law also commands care for strangers and gleaners, so the piece risks making “law” sound colder than the Ruth text itself.

- Doctrine is mostly sound, especially “The curse that should have fallen on us fell on Him instead” and “the penalty was a curse; He stepped into the gate in our place and bore it Himself.” But “will you let the Redeemer pay what you cannot” is sloppy: Christ has already paid. The invitation should call the hearer to come/receive/believe, not “let” Him pay.

- Reveal is earned in concept but too announced in execution. “He had the right… He had the wealth… He was willing to be marred” is the strongest Christ-turn. But “I had no idea, in that gate, that I was sketching the gospel” tells the reveal after the piece has already explained it.

- First-hearing clarity is mostly intact, but “old custom of the shoe,” “raise up the name of the dead,” and “curse of the law” are loaded terms. The piece explains some, but not enough for a listener with zero Bible knowledge.

- Long-form sag: Beat 4, Beat 6, and Beat 7 repeat the same cost/payment language: “cost the redeemer everything,” “the price always falls on the redeemer,” “Jesus paid His own blood,” “The redeemed never pay.” The repetition does not always add a new turn.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Correct the factual overstatements around Boaz giving the field, redemption costing “everything,” and redemption buying “nothing.”
2. Tighten the reveal so Boaz’s lived details show Christ before the narration announces the typology.
3. Fix quote attribution/context and revise the CTA from “let the Redeemer pay” to a clear call to come to Jesus by faith/receive His redemption.
