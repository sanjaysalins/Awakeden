# Independent review — claude (OK, 57s)

Read the artifact closely against the gates. The KJV quote checks out and the doctrine is sound, but two real problems keep it from done.

## What holds up

- **KJV verbatim (EW-INV-4):** `**"Christ hath redeemed us from the curse of the law, being made a curse for us."**` is exact KJV (Gal 3:13). Truncating before the colon is fine — no words altered. Attribution "Scripture says:" is present before the quote. Clean.
- **CTA-on-Jesus (EW-INV-2):** Names Jesus, names the response ("Come to Jesus"), grace-anchored ("the redeemed never pay… Let the Redeemer pay what you cannot"). No fear, no gain/loss, not the tired "will you trust Him?" template. Passes.
- **Substitution named (EW-G9):** "the curse we owed fell on Him" — penalty + what Christ bore are both stated. No supersession; "He takes the poor outsider for His bride" reads as Gentile inclusion, not replacement of Israel. Doctrine sound.
- **Hook grips, spine holds:** ONE witness (Boaz), ONE thread (kinsman-redeemer), no swap. Length ~230 spoken words — inside the short band.

## What fails

**1. The wrestling is absent (EW-G8) — the biggest gap.** Boaz never voices an honest doubt or the real objection in his OWN voice. The strongest objection — *redemption costs me my own inheritance* — is handed to the **other** kinsman ("it would mar his inheritance… he walked"), while Boaz himself only declares "joy worth every coin." That's a strawman: the steel-man cost lands on a foil, and the witness sails through cost-free. The form requires the witness to internalize the genuine objection. Right now Beat 2 has no steel for Boaz.

**2. The reveal leans on an equivocation, so it's partly asserted not earned (EW-G7).** "marred where the nearer kinsman would not be" puns two different "mars": the kinsman feared marring his **inheritance** (Ruth 4:6, financial), but Christ was **marred in body/visage** (Isa 52:14). The wordplay sounds earned but the two senses don't connect logically — the nearer kinsman never declined to be *physically* marred. The turn needs the link built from what Boaz lived, not from a pun on one word.

**3. Hook overstates the text.** "asked me to buy back all she'd lost" — at the threshing floor Ruth asked for the kinsman's covering/redemption (Ruth 3:9); the land was Naomi/Elimelech's, and the instruction was Naomi's. "knelt at my feet" — she *lay* at his feet. Minor, but the hook dramatizes beyond the record; tighten to stay defensible to a Bible-literate viewer.

Lesser note: Beat 3 ("He took our flesh, paid not silver but His own blood…") drifts toward the essay-narrator register — least first-person of the four beats (EW-G10). A "I" beat would keep Boaz in the room.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Give Boaz the wrestling (EW-G8): move the real cost-objection into HIS voice — let him feel "this redemption empties my own name/inheritance" before he pays — instead of parking it entirely on the nearer kinsman.
2. Earn the reveal without the pun (EW-G7): rebuild the type→Christ link from what Boaz lived (paid the full price the nearer man refused), not from equivocating on "marred" (inheritance vs. visage).
3. Tighten the hook to the text: Ruth lay at his feet and sought the kinsman's redemption (Ruth 3:9) — don't have her "ask to buy back all she'd lost."
