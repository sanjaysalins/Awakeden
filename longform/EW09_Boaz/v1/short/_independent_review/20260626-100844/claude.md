# Independent review — claude (OK, 70s)

I read the narration closely and checked the four KJV quotes against the text. Findings below, most material first.

## KJV verbatim + attribution (EW-INV-4) — PASS
All four quotes are exact KJV and properly attributed before the quote:
- Ruth 3:9 — *"I am Ruth thine handmaid: spread therefore thy skirt over thine handmaid; for thou art a near kinsman."* ✓ (attributed: "Ruth asked it of me")
- Ruth 4:6 — *"I cannot redeem it for myself, lest I mar mine own inheritance."* ✓ (attributed: "He weighed it and walked")
- Ruth 4:9 — *"Ye are witnesses this day, that I have bought all that was Elimelech's."* ✓ (Boaz, first person)
- Gal 3:13 — *"Christ hath redeemed us from the curse of the law, being made a curse for us."* ✓ ("Scripture says")

Nit: "Elimelech’s" uses a curly apostrophe while the rest of the file uses straight quotes — inside a KJV quote, so cosmetic, but inconsistent.

## The WRESTLING (EW-G8) — WEAK, the main problem
The genuine objection is **externalized onto the nearer kinsman, not internalized in Boaz's own voice.** The steel-man ("it's not worth thinning my estate for a foreign widow") is spoken by *him*, who "weighed it and walked." Boaz only says *"I weighed the same, and paid it"* — he reports the conclusion but we never hear *his* honest struggle. That's the difference between a witness wrestling and a witness narrating that someone else wrestled. The short skips the beat labeled for it (beats jump 1 → 3 → 6 → 7). One or two lines of Boaz's own counted cost-and-doubt would fix this and make the steel-man real.

## The REVEAL (EW-G7) — PASS, genuinely earned
The "mar / marred" thread is the strongest thing here: the nearer kinsman refuses *"lest I mar mine own inheritance,"* and the reveal lands on *"He took our flesh, marred where that kinsman would not."* That turn is shown from the lived text, not announced. Good.

## CTA-on-Jesus (EW-INV-2) — PASS
Names Jesus, names the response (*"Come to Jesus… and be His own"*), grace-anchored (*"The redeemed never pay"*), no fear/gain-loss. *"Will you let the Redeemer pay what you cannot?"* is fresh enough to dodge the tired "will you trust Him?" template. Slightly doubled (rhetorical question + "Come to Jesus") but acceptable.

## Doctrine (EW-G9) — PASS
Substitution named with penalty + what Christ bore: *"made a curse so you need never be"* / *"His own blood."* Bride-of-Christ analogy (Eph 5) sound. No supersession.

## Voice (EW-G10) — mostly PASS
Consistent first-person, some character (*"I will not hide the cost," "He weighed it and walked"*). But Beat 6 *"I tell you now by the light of the word written long after me"* is a slightly essayish framing device — necessary to license the NT reveal, but it's the seam where the witness thins toward narrator.

## Length / tightness — SLIGHTLY OVER
~340–350 spoken words, above the ~320 ceiling. Beat 3 is the dense one (three KJV quotes plus the cost exposition). It doesn't *sag* — single moment holds — but it's not tight. Trimming Beat 3's framing would buy room for the missing wrestling line without going longer.

## Hook — soft
*"I am Boaz of Bethlehem, the richest man in town."* Clear, but a mild grip, not a scroll-stopper. "The richest man in town" undersells the hook when the real tension is a rich man *choosing to make himself poorer* for an outsider — lead with the stakes, not the status.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Internalize the wrestling in Boaz's own voice (EW-G8): give him one or two honest lines of *his* counted cost/doubt before he pays, instead of letting the nearer kinsman carry the whole objection.
2. Tighten to ~320 words — compress Beat 3's exposition around the three quotes — and use the room to land the hook on the stakes (a rich man making himself poorer for a foreign widow), not on "richest man in town."
3. Soften the Beat 6 narrator seam (*"by the light of the word written long after me"*) so the reveal stays in the witness's voice rather than stepping outside it.
