# Independent review — cursor (OK, 65s)

## Independent adversarial review — *The Kinsman-Redeemer* (Boaz, eyewitness-short)

---

### EW-INV-4 — KJV verbatim + attribution

**Inside the quote marks:** All four spans are verbatim KJV substrings (Ruth 3:9, Ruth 4:6 partial, Ruth 4:9 partial, Gal 3:13 partial). No altered words detected inside `**"..."**`.

**Attribution — mixed:**

- **`"I am Ruth thine handmaid: spread therefore thy skirt over thine handmaid; for thou art a near kinsman."`** — *"Ruth asked it of me:"* names the speaker before the quote. Passes, but barely; no dialogue tag for voice routing (`**[Ruth]**`), and *"asked it of me"* collapses Ruth’s threshing-floor plea with the later gate scene (see clarity below).

- **`"I cannot redeem it for myself, lest I mar mine own inheritance."`** — *"He weighed it and walked:"* — the antecedent is recoverable (*"A nearer kinsman came first"*), but *"He"* is weak attribution for a quoted speaker the listener never meets by name. Should be explicit (*the nearer kinsman said*) before the scripture voice reads it.

- **`"Ye are witnesses this day, that I have bought all that was Elimelech's."`** — Boaz-as-narrator at *"I paid it at the gate:"* — acceptable self-attribution.

- **`"Christ hath redeemed us from the curse of the law, being made a curse for us."`** — *"Scripture says:"* before the quote fixes the attribution gap an earlier draft had. Pass.

**Non-quote drift:** Beat 1 — *"the richest man in town"* vs Ruth 2:1 / header *"mighty man of wealth"* — not a KJV violation, but it reads like modern gloss, not witness idiom.

**Under-quoted gate moment:** The type→fulfillment header promises bride-redemption; the only gate quote is land purchase. Ruth 4:9–10’s marriage half (*"Moreover Ruth the Moabitess… have I purchased to be my wife"*) is absent. The story Boaz actually lived at the gate is therefore misrepresented in the quoted moment.

### EW-G8 — Wrestling (headline failure)

SHORT may compress Beats 2/4/5, but **steel-man doubt must survive inside the kept beats**. It does not.

The real objections in Ruth — *why mar my inheritance for a Moabitess?*, *can a foreign widow belong in Israel’s line?*, *why pay when the nearer kinsman already refused?* — are voiced only by **the other kinsman**, then dismissed in one line:

> *"I weighed the same, and paid it at the gate"*

That is **resolution without struggle**, not internalized wrestling. Boaz narrates cost (*"my inheritance thinned to raise another man's line"*) but never owns the doubt. The nearer kinsman’s refusal becomes a contrast prop for Beat 6’s *"marred where that kinsman would not"* — clever, not lived.

Compare the locked long-form Beat 5, which actually wrestles Moab’s reproach and the law’s cold letter. None of that steel survives the cut.

---

### EW-G7 — Reveal earned vs. announced

**Announced / imported:**

- *"I tell you now by the light of the word written long after me"* — signposted cross-time device (per EW-INV-11), but still a **generic explainer**, same family as the banned *"at last I understood"* stinger the spec warns against.

- *"From the son Ruth bore me came David's line, and a greater Kinsman"* — genealogy **asserted**, not shown from what Boaz witnessed in the kept beats. Boaz did not live to see David crowned.

- Gal 3:13’s **curse of the law** drops in without a shown step from *goel* mechanics (land, name, marriage at personal cost). A zero-Bible listener hears “kinsman bought dead man’s land” then suddenly “curse of the law.”

**Partially earned:**

- Nearer kinsman’s *"lest I mar mine own inheritance"* vs. Christ’s willingness — the best bridge in the piece, but it lives in Beat 3 as **his** line, not Boaz’s earned insight.

**Not earned in the act beat:**

- *"And He takes the redeemed outsider for His bride"* — bride typology is central to the header’s type→fulfillment, but Beat 3 never shows Boaz **taking Ruth as wife** (only land purchase is quoted; *"a Moabitess for my wife"* is narratorial summary, not witnessed/quoted act). The reveal’s bride claim outruns the moment the short actually stages.

### EW-G9 — Doctrine

**Sound core:** goel → Christ Kinsman-Redeemer; willing cost; blood not silver; grace not earning; no supersession in the text; Gal 3:13 correctly applied.

**Gaps / risks:**

- **Substitution under-named.** Beat 7 has *"He paid not silver but His own blood, made a curse so you need never be"* — blood and curse are named, but **what penalty** (sin/judgment under the law that we owed) is never plainly stated. Jonah’s short names it explicitly (*"The penalty I had earned, He took in my place"*). Here it stays generic.

- **Broken line:** *"made a curse so you need never be"* — grammatically incomplete (*be what?* cursed? under the law?). A listener cannot latch the doctrine.

- **"Marred" wordplay:** Beat 6 — *"He took our flesh, marred where that kinsman would not"* — fuses Ruth 4:6’s *mar mine own inheritance* (financial/legal harm) with Christ’s suffering. Related themes, not the same act or word in the text. Reads clever rather than text-grounded.

- Bride/outsider echo is standard Eph 5 typology; fine at this length, but slightly over-tidy without the Gentile-inclusion thread Gal 3:14 actually carries.

---

### EW-INV-2 — CTA on Jesus

**Passes on essentials:** Names *"Jesus, the Christ"*; *"Come to Jesus"* names the response; *"The redeemed never pay"* is grace-anchored; *"Will you let the Redeemer pay what you cannot?"* is specific enough to avoid the bare *"will you trust Him?"* template; no fear/gain-loss pressure.

**Soft fail on grounding:** *"If you feel like that woman in my field — poor and far off"* — the invitation points at a scene **never staged** in Beats 1/3/6 (see clarity). Mood-matching replaces substitution as the reason to come.

---

### EW-G10 — Voice / character

Boaz is present in Beats 1 and 3 (*"I was that man,"* *"I will not hide the cost"*). Beat 6 slides into **systematic-theology narrator**:

> *"He took our flesh, marred where that kinsman would not. He is Jesus, the Christ."*

That is catechism in first person, not a wealthy Bethlehem elder recounting a costly gate morning and the Moabite widow he chose. Beat 1’s *"richest man in town"* also sounds contemporary, not characterful.

---

### First-hearing clarity

- **Orphan CTA image:** *"that woman in my field"* — Ruth 2’s field never appears. The listener is asked to identify with a vignette they never heard.

- **Chronology compression:** *"Ruth asked it of me"* (night/threshing floor) → *"A nearer kinsman came first"* (gate) with no transition. Zero-Bible listeners may think Ruth pleaded at the gate.

- **Untranslated mechanics:** *"spread thy skirt,"* *"raise his name,"* *"goel"* — partially glossed, but **why a relative’s word at the gate decides a foreign widow’s future** remains thin until Beat 3, and even then the marriage half of redemption is summary-only.

- **David’s line** — assumes Bible literacy; not fatal, but the bridge is asserted, not narrated.

---

### SHORT form — tightness / single moment

~310–320 spoken words — at the **upper budget ceiling**, acceptable.

One witness, one thread (Ruth redemption → Christ) — **PASS** on spine; no second episode crowding.

But the piece packs **two OT scenes** (night plea + gate refusal/payment) **plus** David genealogy **plus** Gal 3 Christology into ~90s while **omitting the field** the CTA still references and **omitting Boaz’s wrestling**. Tight on word count; loose on moment unity and listener orientation.

---

### Hook

*"At the gate, a poor widow's whole future hung on one man's word. I was that man."* — stakes work; identity clear. Not a gripping first-hearing hook for a cold audience (declarative biography, not a sensory or paradox opening). Improved over a gate-spoiling Beat 1, but still flat.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. **Add Boaz’s steel-man wrestling in his own voice** inside Beat 1 or 3 (inheritance marred, Moabitess outsider, why pay when the nearer kinsman refused) — replace *"I weighed the same, and paid"* with honest doubt answered by the reveal, not contrast-wordplay with the other kinsman alone.
2. **Earn the bride + curse bridges from the staged moment:** quote or clearly witness the marriage half at the gate; walk one explicit step from goel-cost to Gal 3:13’s curse; fix *"made a curse so you need never be"* and name substitution plainly (the penalty/judgment Christ bore in our place).
3. **Fix listener clarity:** either stage the field in Beat 1/3 or cut *"that woman in my field"* from the CTA; attribute the nearer kinsman by name before his quote; reorder/compress so night-scene vs gate-scene doesn’t whiplash a zero-Bible listener.
