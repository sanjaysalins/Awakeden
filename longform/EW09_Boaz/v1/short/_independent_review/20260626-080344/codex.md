# Independent review — codex (OK, 26s)

Concrete findings:

- EW-INV-4 issue: the quotes are not explicitly attributed with verse/source. Example: `**"I am Ruth thine handmaid..."**`, `**"I cannot redeem it..."**`, `**"Ye are witnesses this day..."**`, and `**"Christ hath redeemed us..."**` have speaker context, but no KJV reference attribution like `Ruth said...` / `the nearer kinsman said...` / `Paul wrote...`. The lens asks for “KJV verbatim + attribution.”
- KJV wording appears mostly exact, but `**"Ye are witnesses this day, that I have bought all that was Elimelech’s."**` is only a clipped fragment of Ruth 4:9 and uses curly apostrophe in `Elimelech’s`. If enforcing strict KJV text presentation, use straight KJV punctuation/possessive style and identify it as Boaz speaking to elders and people.
- EW-G8 weak: the wrestling is underdeveloped. `It gained me nothing but a poor outsider. Only love pays such a price.` gestures at cost, but Boaz’s strongest internal objection is never really voiced. The real objection would be inheritance, reputation, marrying a Moabitess, risking his own line, or whether this foreign widow should receive covenant kindness.
- EW-G7 partially earned but a little announced. `He is the true Kinsman-Redeemer` is asserted after the genealogy line. The strongest textual bridge is present in `near kinsman`, `redeemed`, `paid`, `mar mine own inheritance`, but the turn from Boaz’s act to Christ’s substitution needs more showing before the label lands.
- EW-G9 has a substitution gap. `paid not silver but His own blood` and `being made a curse for us` are sound, but the penalty is not plainly named. The lens asks for “the penalty + what Christ bore.” Say He bore the curse/judgment/penalty of our sin in our place.
- Possible doctrinal overstatement: `Then He took the redeemed outsider as His bride and gave her His name.` This is theologically familiar, but in this short form it risks making Ruth individually map too neatly onto “the bride” without clarifying grace to Jew and Gentile together. Not supersessionist as written, but watch the “outsider only” framing.
- Voice is competent but somewhat polished-essay-like. `I tell you now by the light of the word written long after me` sounds like an explainer narrator, not Boaz recounting a lived wound and wonder.
- First-hearing clarity is decent, but `goel`, `near kinsman`, and `redeemed` are not explained inside the narration. A zero-Bible listener may not know why a relative can buy back land/name/marriage rights.
- Short-form tightness is good. This likely fits ~90 seconds. The single thread holds: Ruth, the gate, redemption, Christ. No major second-thread crowding.
- CTA is mostly compliant. `Come to Jesus... receive what He has done` names the response and avoids fear pressure. However, `if you feel like that woman... poor, far off, a stranger with nothing to bring` leans on self-identification more than universal need; make the grace invitation broader and explicitly sin/substitution anchored.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Add explicit KJV speaker/source attribution before each quote and tighten exact KJV presentation.
2. Strengthen Boaz’s real internal wrestling around cost, inheritance, Moabite outsider status, and name.
3. Name substitution more plainly: the curse/judgment/penalty of our sin that Christ bore in our place.
