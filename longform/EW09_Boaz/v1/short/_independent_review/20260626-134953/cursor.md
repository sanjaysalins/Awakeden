# Independent adversarial review — *The Kinsman-Redeemer* (Boaz, eyewitness-short)

Default posture: skeptical. This reads like a typology sketch wearing Boaz’s name, not a witness remembering one lived moment.

---

### EW-INV-4 — KJV verbatim + attribution: **REVISE**

**The one quoted line — words only: PASS (narrow).**  
**`"Christ hath redeemed us from the curse of the law, being made a curse for us."`** matches Galatians 3:13 KJV through the colon (legitimate truncation).

**Attribution: FAIL.** The lead-in is only **`Scripture says:`** — not Paul, not Galatians, not even “the apostle.” EW-INV-4 requires the speaker to be identified *before* the quote. A zero-Bible listener gets anonymous “Scripture,” not a routed voice with a named source.

**The witnessed hinge never gets KJV.** The load-bearing gate scene — the nearer kinsman’s refusal — is all paraphrase. Ruth 4:6’s exact line **`"I cannot redeem it for myself, lest I mar mine own inheritance"`** is what Beat 3 later wordplays on (`marred where the nearer kinsman would not be`), but it never appears in quotes with **`[the kinsman]`** attribution. The eyewitness act is never sealed by Scripture’s own words.

---

### EW-G8 — Wrestling (steel-man): **FAIL**

There is **no wrestling beat at all**. SHORT compresses the spine, but EW-G8 is still a named gate — and this piece has **zero** internalized doubt in Boaz’s voice.

Beat 2 reports that the nearer kinsman **`walked — it would mar his inheritance`**, but that is **his** objection, parked on a foil — not Boaz’s steel-man. Boaz never voices the hardest honest questions this story demands:

- Why should a **Moabitess** have any claim on Israel’s inheritance?
- Why bind **my** house and name to a dead man’s debt when the law lets a nearer man refuse?
- Is mercy to the outsider a breach of the law, or its point?

The locked long form *does* wrestle this (`"So which was it — was kindness to the outsider a breach of the law..."`). The short cut it entirely. **`joy worth every coin`** resolves cost-free — wonder-free exposition, not wrestling.

---

### EW-G9 — Doctrine / grounding: **REVISE**

**Direction is mostly sound:** substitution is named (**`paid not silver but His own blood`**, **`the curse we owed fell on Him`**, Gal 3:13); **`He takes the poor outsider for His bride`** reads as inclusion typology, not supersession. No invented divine speech. Good direction.

**But the opening misstates Ruth — a real grounding defect for an eyewitness piece.**

Beat 1:

> **`A penniless foreign widow came to me and asked me to buy back all she'd lost`**

That is **not what Ruth 2–4 records.**

- Ruth first **gleaned in the field** (Ruth 2) and asked **`"Why have I found grace in thine eyes... seeing I am a stranger?"`** — gratitude, not a redemption plea.
- The goel request came **at the threshing floor** (Ruth 3:9): **`"spread therefore thy skirt over thine handmaid; for thou art a near kinsman."`**
- Land redemption happened **at the gate** (Ruth 4), with Naomi selling the parcel — not a widow arriving to ask Boaz to “buy back all she’d lost.”

This is not poetic compression; it **invents a scene** the witness did not live (EW-INV-11 hazard).

**Economics mis-told.** **`gained Ruth as my bride — joy worth every coin`** frames Boaz as a happy purchaser. Ruth’s law point is the opposite: redemption **cost the redeemer** and **did not enlarge his estate** — it raised **another man’s name**. The long form gets this right (`"gained no profit — nothing but her"`). The short flattens the type’s cost logic and weakens the Christ parallel.

**Hook tension mis-aimed:** **`though it would cost me my own inheritance`** — the nearer kinsman feared marring *his* inheritance (Ruth 4:6); Boaz’s act was costly in a different way (name/money poured into Elimelech’s line). Conflating the two blurs the steel-man.

---

### EW-G7 — Reveal earned: **REVISE / weak**

Some machinery is genuinely shown:

- Nearer kinsman refuses over inheritance → Christ **`marred where the nearer kinsman would not be`**
- Outsider bride → **`He takes the poor outsider for His bride`**
- Silver at the gate → **`paid not silver but His own blood`**

That parallel work is real — but underdeveloped and partly smuggled.

**The curse-of-the-law turn is imported, not walked from what Boaz lived.** Ruth is about **land, name, levirate redemption, and a foreign widow grafted in** — not “curse of the law” on the page. Beat 3 jumps to **`the curse we owed fell on Him`** and **asserts** the link via **`Scripture says:`** + Gal 3:13. Legitimate typology for the episode’s spine, but **announced**, not shown: debt you cannot pay → redeemer steps in at the gate → penalty falls on him instead of you. A zero-Bible listener hears “curse” with no Ruth-grounded explanation of *what* was owed.

**`marred where the nearer kinsman would not be`** puns two different “mars”: the kinsman feared marring his **inheritance** (financial/name), but Christ was **marred in body/visage** (Isa 52:14 energy). Clever for insiders; **opaque and equivocal** on first hearing — the nearer kinsman never declined to be *physically* marred.

**`Long after, One came from David's line, born in my Bethlehem`** is an acceptable reflective signpost (EW-INV-11), not the banned **“at last I understood”** stinger — but it still leans on **time-skip assertion** more than demonstration from the gate facts.

### EW-G10 — Voice / character: **REVISE**

Boaz never names himself in the spoken text (header only). Beat 2 especially reads like a **Bible-study handout**, not a man remembering his gate:

> **`In our law the goel — the near kinsman — could redeem a dead man's land and take his widow to raise his name.`**

Third-person legal exposition, not **`I sat down in the dust and called ten elders`**. Sprinkled **`I paid`** / **`my bride`** are not enough; this is the essay-narrator-in-costume pattern the gates are meant to catch. Beat 4’s direct second-person address (**`So if you feel poor, far off, a stranger`**) further breaks the first-person witness frame.

---

### EW-INV-2 — CTA-on-Jesus: **PASS (narrow)**

**`Come to Jesus.`** — names Jesus, names the response (**Come**), not the banned bare **“will you trust Him?”** template.

**`the redeemed never pay. Let the Redeemer pay what you cannot.`** — grace-anchored, no fear/gain-loss/manufactured pressure. **`So if you feel poor, far off, a stranger`** ties listener to Ruth without crass self-interest. Thin, but acceptable.

---

### First-hearing clarity: **REVISE**

- **`goel`** is glossed — good.
- **`One stood nearer than I`** — followable with context.
- **`marred where the nearer kinsman would not be`** — likely lost on a zero-Bible listener; requires Ruth 4:6’s “mar” logic already paraphrased but not quoted in KJV terms.
- **`the curse we owed`** — unexplained until Paul; the Ruth story’s felt debt is **poverty, foreignness, dead name, lost land** — not “curse of the law” until Gal 3. The short assumes Paul before the listener has Ruth.

---

### Hook / spine / SHORT form: **REVISE**

**Hook:** **`came to me and asked me to buy back all she'd lost`** — wrong scene; weak for anyone who knows Ruth, misleading for anyone who doesn’t.

**Spine:** ONE witness, ONE thread (goel → Christ) — holds. No mid-piece swap.

**Tightness:** Spoken body ≈ **200 words** — **below EW-INV-8’s 220–320 floor**. Too thin to carry wrestling + earned reveal; what remains is compressed typology, not tight witness testimony.

**Single moment:** Crowded across mis-hook + law lecture + gate + Christ turn; the **gate** should be the one moment — the hook should open *there*, not on a fabricated plea.

---

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. **Replace the invented hook** — open on the gate (or threshing-floor goel plea with Ruth’s actual KJV ask); drop **`came to me and asked me to buy back all she'd lost`**; quote Ruth 4:6 (and ideally Ruth 3:9) with named speakers before paraphrase.
2. **Add real steel-man wrestling in Boaz’s voice** — Moab/inheritance/law cost vs mercy to the outsider — then let Beat 3’s Christ turn *answer that doubt*, not only the nearer kinsman’s arithmetic.
3. **Earn the Gal 3:13 turn from the gate facts, hit word budget, fix attribution** — walk debt → redeemer pays at the gate → penalty on him; attribute **`Paul wrote in Galatians`** (or equivalent); expand ~20–120 words so substitution and the Ruth→curse chain are shown, not asserted via **`Scripture says:`** alone.
