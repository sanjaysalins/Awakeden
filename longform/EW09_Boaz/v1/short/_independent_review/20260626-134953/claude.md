# Independent review — claude (OK, 62s)

I read it closely against the eyewitness lens. Doctrine and CTA are basically sound, but there's a real internal contradiction that muddles the very type the piece is built on.

## Findings

**1. The hook contradicts Beat 2 — and misattributes the cost (factual + spine).**
Beat 1: *"asked me to buy back all she'd lost — though it would cost me my own inheritance."*
Beat 2: the **nearer** kinsman is the one who feared *"it would mar his inheritance"* and walked, while for Boaz it was *"joy worth every coin."* So the hook puts the inheritance-loss fear on Boaz, but the text (Ruth 4:6 — *"lest I mar mine own inheritance"*) puts it on the man who refused. This is the central contrast of the whole story (the kinsman who **won't** pay vs. the one who **will**), and the hook inverts it. It manufactures a stake that the narrative itself then denies two lines later. Fix this — it's the biggest hole.

**2. The reveal leans on a pun, not the text (EW-G7).**
*"marred where the nearer kinsman would not be"* depends on the listener connecting "mar mine inheritance" to Christ being "marred" (Isa 52:14). That's a writerly conceit doing the work of the turn, and because finding #1 has already scrambled who feared the marring, the parallel lands confused rather than earned. Earn it from what Boaz lived (he paid the full price gladly at the gate; the nearer man would not) → Christ paid the fuller price the same way.

**3. No wrestling / honest doubt beat (EW-G8) and the voice goes essayistic.**
Boaz never voices a genuine objection or question — there's no steel-man. By Beat 4 (*"So if you feel poor, far off, a stranger…"*) the first-person witness has dissolved into a preacher; Boaz's character is gone (EW-G10). At minimum keep the close in Boaz's mouth.

**4. KJV check — passes.** *"Christ hath redeemed us from the curse of the law, being made a curse for us"* is exact KJV (Gal 3:13), and *"Scripture says:"* is an acceptable attribution frame. Substitution is named (penalty = the curse of the law; Christ *"made a curse," "the curse we owed fell on Him"*) — EW-G9 satisfied. CTA names the response (*"Come to Jesus,"* *"Let the Redeemer pay"*), grace-anchored, no fear/gain-loss, not a tired template — EW-INV-2 satisfied.

**5. Minor first-hearing snags.** *"raise his name"* and *"goel"* are dense for a zero-Bible listener; *goel* is glossed, *"raise his name"* isn't. Workable but on the edge. Word count ~230 — tight and fine.

The piece is well-built and doctrinally clean; it fails on a correctness inconsistency at its hinge, not on heresy.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Resolve the inheritance contradiction — Boaz's redemption did NOT cost him his inheritance; the "mar mine own inheritance" fear belongs to the nearer kinsman who refused (Ruth 4:6). Rewrite the hook so it doesn't invert the story's core contrast.
2. Earn the reveal from what Boaz lived (paid the full price gladly where the nearer kinsman would not) rather than the "marred inheritance → Christ marred" pun, which currently reads as asserted and confused.
3. Keep the close in Boaz's first-person voice (and add at least a beat of his honest wrestling) instead of dropping into a detached preacher in Beat 4.
