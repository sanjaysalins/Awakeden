# Gallery Plan — EW09_Boaz (Awakeden eyewitness SHORT)

**Witness:** Boaz of Bethlehem, the kinsman-redeemer (go'el) · **Core text:** Ruth 2–4 → Gal 3:13
**Engine:** Locked "gallery hard-cut" — one rich Baroque oil painting per beat; eye sees the whole canvas, then hard-cuts to the NAMED elements (the tour targets). ~70s, 9:16 vertical. **Lands on the risen Christ.**
**Standing subject_block stem (every painting):** *Baroque oil painting, deep chiaroscuro, Caravaggio/Rembrandt lighting, ancient biblical-period Near-Eastern setting, vertical 9:16, no modern elements, no text.*

Doctrine guardrails honored: Beat 3 is a signposted reflective device (Boaz speaks "by the light of the word written long after me," never claiming he knew Christ) — the Christ paintings stand as their own canvases, Boaz never "sees" them. ## 1. [Hook] [0–9s] [NEW]
**Subject_block:** Baroque oil painting, deep chiaroscuro, Caravaggio/Rembrandt lighting, ancient biblical-period Near-Eastern setting, vertical 9:16, no modern elements, no text. ONE dominant scene: a penniless foreign widow in a dust-grey mourning veil kneels low at the feet of a tall, weathered landowner, one hand lifted toward him in plea; behind them a barley field fades into warm shadow. Negative space of golden dusk-light above. Two faces only.
**Named tour elements (in order):** (1) the widow's pleading lifted hand, (2) the widow's upturned eyes under the grey veil, (3) the landowner's grave, considering face, (4) the lifted hem of barley stalks she has gleaned at his feet.
**Lands on Christ:** opens the redemption question — someone with nothing asks to be bought back — the human ache Christ will answer.

## 2. [Strange thing — the goel law set up] [9–20s] [NEW]
**Subject_block:** Baroque oil painting, deep chiaroscuro, Caravaggio/Rembrandt lighting, ancient biblical-period Near-Eastern setting, vertical 9:16, no modern elements, no text. MULTI-STORY canvas of 3 soft-edged vignettes bleeding into one warm scene: centre — an abandoned, sun-cracked field with a fallen stone boundary marker (Elimelech's land); upper-left in cool shadow — a distant grave-cairn on a barren Moab ridge; lower-right — an old kinsman's open weathered hand holding a single silver coin over the soil. Crowd/background entirely in shadow. Generous negative space.
**Named tour elements (in order):** (1) the fallen stone boundary marker on the cracked field, (2) the shadowed grave-cairn on the Moab ridge, (3) the open hand holding the silver coin, (4) the dry barley stubble catching low light.
**Lands on Christ:** establishes the law of the near kinsman — only blood-kin with the right and the means may buy back the lost; the frame Christ will fill.

## 3. [Strange thing — the nearer kinsman walks away] [20–31s] [NEW]
**Subject_block:** Baroque oil painting, deep chiaroscuro, Caravaggio/Rembrandt lighting, ancient biblical-period Near-Eastern setting, vertical 9:16, no modern elements, no text. ONE dominant figure: a well-robed nearer kinsman turning his back and stepping away into darkness, his face half-lit in refusal, one hand clutching a fold of his rich mantle close to his chest. A single shaft of light he is leaving behind. Two faces at most; the spurned widow only a dim silhouette in the far light.
**Named tour elements (in order):** (1) the kinsman's averted, refusing face, (2) the hand clutching the rich mantle to himself, (3) the back of his shoulder turning into shadow, (4) the dim widow-silhouette in the abandoned light.
**Lands on Christ:** the one who *could* redeem but won't — because the cost would "mar his inheritance" — sets the dark foil for the Redeemer who pays everything.

## 4. [Strange thing — Boaz pays the full price at the gate] [31–42s] [NEW]
**Subject_block:** Baroque oil painting, deep chiaroscuro, Caravaggio/Rembrandt lighting, ancient biblical-period Near-Eastern setting, vertical 9:16, no modern elements, no text. ONE dominant action at a torchlit city gate of stone: the kinsman-redeemer's open hand pressing a full purse of silver into the scene, a removed leather sandal resting on the worn threshold stone beside it, elder witnesses receding into warm shadow. Strong single torch source. One clear face (the redeemer), the rest in shadow.
**Named tour elements (in order):** (1) the redeemer's hand pouring out the full purse of silver, (2) the removed leather sandal on the threshold stone, (3) the redeemer's resolved, open face in torchlight, (4) the shadowed faces of the elder witnesses.
**Lands on Christ:** the full price paid publicly, gladly — the shadow-act of a redeemer who counts the cost worth it.

## 5. [Strange thing resolved — Boaz gains Ruth as bride, joy] [42–51s] [NEW]
**Subject_block:** Baroque oil painting, deep chiaroscuro, Caravaggio/Rembrandt lighting, ancient biblical-period Near-Eastern setting, vertical 9:16, no modern elements, no text. ONE intimate scene: the redeemer drawing the once-grey-veiled widow up from her knees, her mourning veil now warm-lit and lifted from her face, their two hands clasped, joy breaking on both faces in golden light. Soft negative space of dawn behind. Two faces only.
**Named tour elements (in order):** (1) the two clasped hands lifting her up, (2) the bride's uncovered, relieved face, (3) the redeemer's joyful eyes, (4) the warm-lit veil falling back from her shoulders.
**Lands on Christ:** "joy worth every coin" — the redeemer gains a bride; the picture of Christ rejoicing over the soul He buys.

## 6. [Turn — the greater Kinsman pays His own blood, the curse falls on Him] [51–62s] [NEW]
**Subject_block:** Baroque oil painting, deep chiaroscuro, Caravaggio/Rembrandt lighting, ancient biblical-period Near-Eastern setting, vertical 9:16, no modern elements, no text. ONE dominant reverent figure: Christ in the flesh, head bowed under a darkened, storm-torn sky, His open wounded hand lifted — not silver but His own blood the price; a heavy shadow of the curse pressing down on Him while a single shaft of holy light holds His face. Deeply reverent, not gory; one face. Generous dark negative space above.
**Named tour elements (in order):** (1) Christ's bowed, sorrowing face in the shaft of light, (2) His open wounded hand offered as the price, (3) the storm-torn darkness of the curse pressing on His shoulders, (4) the single shaft of holy light breaking through.
**Lands on Christ:** the substitution itself — "Christ hath redeemed us from the curse of the law, being made a curse for us" (Gal 3:13); the greater Kinsman pays in blood what Boaz paid in silver.
