## Independent adversarial review — *The Passover Lamb* (DRAFT v1.2)

### Doctrinal soundness
- **M4 — “Faultless, and killed anyway, so that judgment would pass over a guilty house.”** “Guilty house” overstates the Exodus setup. Death threatens Israelite homes too, but the text’s logic is *substitution for the firstborn*, not “this family is morally guilty like Egypt.” A careful listener may hear penal guilt where the passage is about a death that must fall *somewhere* that night.
- **M4 — “Jesus is crucified at Passover, the very feast where every family in the city is killing a lamb.”** Poetic, but chronologically loose. Jesus dies at the ninth hour; Passover lambs were killed *in the evening* (Exodus 12:6). Families were not mid-slaughter at the cross in any straightforward sense. Not heresy — but it is a **freshness stretch** dressed as eyewitness fact.
- **M4 — “Pilate, the judge who sentenced Him, said he found no fault in Him at all.”** Pilate’s “no fault” lines are real (e.g. John 19:4, 19:6), but **“at all”** is rhetorical padding, not KJV. Fine in narration — yet the beat leans on Pilate as the inspection climax, which is thinner than the “four days of inspection” parallel built in M3.

### KJV verbatim (inside quotation marks)
Checked against project KJV cache. Problems:

| Quoted phrase | Issue |
|---|---|
| **“When I see the blood, I will pass over you…”** (M3) | KJV Exodus 12:13 reads **“and when I see the blood…”** — leading **“and”** dropped. |
| **“I will pass through the land of Egypt this night,”** (M2) | KJV Exodus 12:12 begins **“For I will pass through…”** — **“For”** dropped. |
| **“Christ our passover is sacrificed for us.”** (M4, M6) | KJV 1 Corinthians 5:7 has **“For even Christ our passover…”** — **“For even”** dropped. |

Truncation with ellipsis is acceptable; **altered interior words are not.** The header claims quotes are “verbatim (verified vs cache)” — that claim is **not fully true** for at least Exodus 12:13.

Non-quoted paraphrase is fine: *“You were not redeemed, Peter says, with silver or gold”* (KJV: “corruptible things, as silver and gold”).

### Clarity on first hearing
- **M7 — “The blood on the doorpost did no good *outside* the house.”** This is the biggest clarity failure. Doorposts are **on** the house exterior. A zero-Bible listener hears: blood on the doorpost → useless outside → must be applied → on their own door. That sounds **self-contradictory** before the later “security is outside of them” explanation arrives. The intended point (objective blood, not inner feeling / not someone else’s lamb) is good; this sentence fights the image.
- **M1 — “read closely, they stop sounding like rules for a meal.”** This is a **written-text** conceit in spoken narration. Audio listeners are not “reading closely.”
- **M3 — quotes “the whole assembly of the congregation of Israel shall kill it,” then walks it back.** The correction is responsible, but the **KJV line still lands first** and paints one national slaughter of one lamb. Many listeners will not recover when the gloss comes two sentences later.
- **M1 hook — “a description of a man who would not be born for another fourteen hundred years.”** Intriguing for Bible-aware ears; opaque for the stated zero-knowledge listener. *Which man?* The hook assumes payoff knowledge the audience may not have.

### Grace-anchored conviction
- **Mostly clean** after the v1.2 rebuild. M6–M7 stay on blood-received, not merit-earned.
- **M7 — “the way a frightened family in Egypt simply trusted the blood and stayed under it through the dark.”** “Frightened” describes the historical scene, not a threat to the listener — acceptable.
- **M6 — “the judgment you and I have earned would look at the blood — and pass over.”** “Earned” is doctrinally standard (deserved wrath), not works-salvation. Not a fail; just watch tone.

### Freshness = faithful depth
- **Bone-not-broken spine** is orthodox typology, not clickbait.
- **M4 Passover simultaneity** (see above) is the one place **poetic alignment outruns text**.
- **M5 objection block** is strong — actually tests coincidence rather than asserting typology by vibes.

### One thread spine
- **Holds.** Lamb specs → blood on wood → bone rule → John at the cross → substitute → applied blood → invitation. No thread swap.

### Landing — does new work?
- **Yes, mostly.** “Security rests on blood you cannot see from inside” is fresher than a generic altar call.
- **But the final line partly retreats:** “rest under the blood of the Lamb who was slain for you” is solid gospel language, yet **“rest under the blood”** is familiar enough that the fresh “outside/inside” insight does not fully carry through the last sentence.

### Hook — first ~5 seconds
- Opening is literate, not gripping: *“There is a set of instructions in the book of Exodus so oddly specific…”* No death, no lamb, no stakes yet.
- The real hook (**meal rules → portrait of a future man**) takes **well past 5 seconds** and needs context a new listener lacks.

---

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Rewrite M7’s “blood on the doorpost did no good outside the house” — it contradicts the literal doorpost image; separate “applied to *your* door” from “security outside your feelings” in plain speech.
2. Fix KJV-strict quotes: restore **“and when I see the blood”** (Ex 12:13), and either restore **“For”** / **“For even”** on other partial quotes or stop claiming full verbatim lock.
3. Reorder M3’s “whole assembly” beat: give the per-household gloss **before** (or instead of leading with) the easily-misheard KJV line; tighten M4’s Passover timing so it does not sound like families are slaughtering lambs at the moment of crucifixion.
