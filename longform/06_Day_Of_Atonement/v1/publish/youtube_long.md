## TITLE
Why Did One Sacrifice Need Two Goats? (Leviticus 16 Explained)

## DESCRIPTION
In Leviticus 16, on the Day of Atonement, the high priest does not sacrifice one goat. He needs two, and only one of them dies.

The first goat is killed, and its blood is carried behind the veil, into the Holy of Holies itself. The second goat is never touched by a blade. Aaron lays his hands on its head, confesses the sin of the people over it, and sends it alive into the wilderness, never to return (Leviticus 16:15-22).

Why two goats for one offering? Because forgiveness has two halves that no single animal could picture at once: the penalty that has to be paid, and the guilt that has to be carried away. One goat shows the price. The other shows the distance sin is removed to.

Leviticus 16:15 says it plainly: "Then shall he kill the goat of the sin offering, that is for the people, and bring his blood within the vail, and do with that blood as he did with the blood of the bullock, and sprinkle it upon the mercy seat, and before the mercy seat:"

Every year, the priest does this again. The blood never finally finishes the job, until one Priest does both halves in a single death, and sits down, because the work is actually done (Hebrews 9:12; 10:10-12). What two goats needed a year to picture, the cross did once, for all.

If your sin is on Him, it is not just covered for another year. It is carried away, as far as the east is from the west. You have only to come.
