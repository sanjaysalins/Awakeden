## Independent adversarial review — *The Two Goats* (Day of Atonement)

Prior-panel issues (Heb 9:22 mis-attributed as “the Law’s answer,” scapegoat/outside-the-camp conflation in M4, stacked “debt/bill” language) appear **fixed** in this artifact. What remains are smaller but real problems — especially landing precision and a few places where typology outruns the Leviticus text.

---

### Hook (first ~5 seconds)
**Pass.** *“Once a year — only once — a whole nation held its breath for a single man”* is immediate stakes. Grip softens in M1 as Aaron’s sons, linen, cloud, and mercy seat stack up before the human tension returns.

---

### KJV verbatim
**Mostly clean on words.** I did not find substituted KJV vocabulary in the quoted spans checked against the project cache (Lev 16:2,5,8,10,15,21,22; Lev 17:11; Heb 10:3,4,10,11–12,19; 13:12; Isa 53:6; Mt 27:51; Ps 103:12).

**Flag — possible word-level issue:**  
**“but by his own blood he entered in once into the holy place, having obtained eternal redemption for us.”** (M4)  
Standard Cambridge/Oxford KJV (Heb 9:12) reads **“the holy places”** (plural). Your cache has singular; if lock is against published KJV, not cache, this fails.

**Punctuation (if LF-G1 is strict):**  
- **“Having therefore, brethren, boldness to enter into the holiest by the blood of Jesus.”** — KJV continues with a comma after *Jesus*, not a period.  
- **“And, behold, the veil of the temple was rent in twain from the top to the bottom.”** — KJV continues with a semicolon.

Truncated mid-clause quotes (lowercase **“but by his own blood…”**, **“there is a remembrance again made of sins every year.”**) are word-accurate fragments but presented as complete verse units — borderline on strict quote discipline.

---

### Doctrinal soundness
**Orthodox evangelical typology overall.** No contrarian readings, no invented Messiah, no clickbait twist on Lev 16. M5’s steel-man (*“primitive superstition… reading Christ back into a livestock ceremony”*) answered from Heb 10:4 is strong.

**1. Lev 16:30 tension still audible (M5 / M7).**  
M5: **“The two goats were never the real thing — they were a picture God drew, year after year, of a cleansing they could not finally make.”**  
Leviticus 16:30 says the priest makes atonement **“that ye may be clean from all your sins before the LORD.”** The **“could not finally make”** qualifier helps, but **“never the real thing”** still risks sounding like Yom Kippur was empty theater — exactly the supersession slippage M5 tries to avoid. One explicit line that Israel’s cleansing **that day was real but not final** would close the gap.

**2. M4 overstatement on “outside the camp.”**  
**“He died outside the city wall, where the sin offering was always taken.”**  
Leviticus sends **specific** sin-offering bodies outside the camp when blood entered the sanctuary (16:27; cf. Heb 13:11). Most sin offerings were not handled that way. **“always”** over-reads the ritual law in a piece that sells itself on the detail the eye slides over.

**3. Live goat / dead Christ asymmetry (M6) — unaddressed.**  
The scapegoat is **“presented alive before the LORD”** and **“let go.”** Christ is **killed** and cast out. M6 asserts **“Both goats fulfilled in one Man”** without showing how the **alive-and-released** half maps onto a crucified Messiah. A careful listener will feel the mismatch; M5’s skeptic could reuse it.

**4. M7 re-conflates two distinct ritual motions.**  
**“Removed — to a place it can never be found, by the One led outside the gate bearing it for you.”**  
Removal-to-wilderness (Lev 16:22 / Ps 103:12) and **“outside the gate”** (Heb 13:12 / burned slain offering) are **different** ritual actions you correctly separated in M4 — then merged again in the landing. That blurs the two-goat architecture at the climax.

---

### Clarity on first hearing
**1. M2 primes death; M3 introduces a goat that doesn’t die — without a bridge.**  
**“Something innocent must die in the place of the guilty.”** (M2)  
Then M3: **“One goat was killed. The other was not.”**  
A zero-Bible listener may hear contradiction before M4’s “two halves” payoff. One bridging clause in M3 (“death alone could not show the whole picture”) would prevent the stall.

**2. Lev 16:10 pronoun left opaque (M3).**  
Quote ends: **“to make an atonement with him.”**  
Script jumps to **“Why would one sin offering need two goats?”** without glossing **“him”** (the LORD, in context). At the central mystery, five words of plain English would keep the thread.

**3. M7 conditional weakens the invitation.**  
**“Your sin, if it is on Him…”**  
For someone who doesn’t yet know imputation/faith, **“if”** lands as uncertainty, not assurance — right before **“Come.”**

**4. Movement title vs. spoken line (M4).**  
Title: **“The Centuries-Early Match.”** Opens: **“Centuries later.”** Sloppy for production; minor but confusing on read-through.

---

### Grace-anchored conviction
**Mostly pass.** The anti-works triad is well done:
- **“You do not have to keep paying for what has already been carried away.”**
- **“You do not have to earn your way behind a curtain…”**
- **“You do not have to wait until you feel clean enough to come.”**

**Watch item:** **“the price paid in full”** (M7) reintroduces a transactional register after debt/bill language was trimmed. Not a fail, but it sits next to grace language and can feel commercial to sensitive ears.

No fear countdown, no gain-loss sell, no works ladder — good.

---

### Freshness = faithful depth
**Pass with caveat.** The two-goat split (penalty + removal), standing/sitting priest, top-down veil, and M5 calendar argument (**“A cure you must repeat every year is not a cure”**) are text-driven, not novelty-chasing. The surprise is anchored in Lev 16’s paired goats — orthodox, not contrarian.

**Caveat:** This is still a **well-worn evangelical sermon move**. Freshness comes from execution (M5 objection, sat-down beat), not from a novel claim.

---

### One thread spine (hook → middle → CTA)
**Pass.** Single spine holds: one man / one day / one veil → two goats → both halves in Christ → shadow not substance → once-for-all → enter boldly. M3’s held question resolves in M4; M5 strengthens M6; M7 mirrors veil / removal imagery from the opening. No thread-swapping.

---

### Landing — does it do NEW work?
**Partial pass.** Avoids the banned *“Will you trust Him?”* template. **“Boldness to enter into the holiest by the blood of Jesus”** + the anti-works triad tie back to torn veil and finished priesthood — not generic.

**Still thin:** **“The way in is open. The work is finished. The sin is gone. Come.”**  
**“Come”** alone does not tell a zero-Bible listener **come how** (draw near through His blood / in faith — Heb 10:22 immediately follows the quoted verse). The landing names **access** but under-specifies **response** for an 8-minute gospel piece. Combined with **“if it is on Him,”** the close feels open-ended rather than invitingly concrete.

---

### Summary
Competent, orthodox, largely KJV-clean typology with a strong hook, a real M5 objection, and a coherent spine. Major prior errors appear repaired. It is **not lock-ready** without revision: the **M7 outside-the-gate / wilderness merge**, the **live-goat vs. crucified Christ gap**, and the **under-specified “Come”** are the sharpest remaining problems; **M5/M7 vs. Lev 16:30** and **M4’s “always taken”** are the sharpest textual ones.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Split M7’s removal beat from “outside the gate” — keep wilderness carrying-away (Ps 103:12 / scapegoat) separate from Heb 13:12’s slain-offering/outside-the-camp echo; don’t merge them in one sentence.
2. Address the live-goat/dead-Christ asymmetry in M6 (one line: both removal and penalty fulfilled in His one offering, though He died rather than walked away) and replace M7’s **“if it is on Him”** with assured imputation language; land **“Come”** with one concrete how (draw near through His blood / in faith).
3. Qualify M5’s **“never the real thing”** so Lev 16:30’s real same-day cleansing isn’t denied; trim M4’s **“where the sin offering was always taken”** to the specific Lev 16/Heb 13 pattern, not all sin offerings.
