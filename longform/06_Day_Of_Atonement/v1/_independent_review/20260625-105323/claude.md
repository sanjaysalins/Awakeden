# Independent review — claude (OK, 95s)

I read the whole narration and checked every quoted verse against KJV. Here's what I found.

## KJV verbatim — all clean
I verified all 18 quotations. Every word inside quotation marks is exact KJV — Lev 16:2, 17:11, 16:5/8/10/15/21/22; Heb 9:12, 10:3/4/10/11–12/19, 13:12; Isa 53:6; Matt 27:51; Psa 103:12. The partial quotes (e.g. cutting 16:5 before "and one ram", 16:15 before "and do with that blood") are honest cuts, not alterations. **No KJV problems.**

## Doctrine — sound
- The two-goats-as-one-offering reading (price paid / guilt carried away, both fulfilled in Christ) is mainstream evangelical typology, not a contrarian stretch. Good restraint: it does **not** take the "scapegoat = Azazel/Satan" contested route.
- "outside the camp / without the gate" link (Lev 16:27 → Heb 13:12) is accurate.
- Nadab/Abihu reference (Movement 1) is faithful to Lev 16:1.
- Conviction is genuinely grace-anchored — "You do not have to keep paying… earn… wait until you feel clean enough." No fear, no gain/loss. Clean.

## Real findings (concrete)

**1. The landing never says HOW sin gets "on Him."** Movement 7 hinges on a condition — *"Your sin, **if it is on Him**…"* — and closes with *"Come."* But the piece never names the means of appropriation: trust, believe, turn to Him, receive. A listener with zero Bible knowledge hears a beautiful exposition and an invitation to "come," but is not told *how to respond*. For a CTA-to-Jesus model this is the one substantive gap — the door is opened but the threshold isn't named.

**2. Movement 5's rebuttal answers the wrong half of the objection.** The skeptic's charge is two-pronged: (a) "this is just primitive ritual" and (b) *"you're reading Christ back into a livestock ceremony."* The answer (Heb 10:4 + the annual repetition = "not a cure, a reminder") rebuts (a) powerfully but only grestures at (b) by asserting "a picture God drew." It slightly assumes the type rather than showing the text points forward. Not fatal — but the "reading Christ back in" line is left half-answered.

**3. Minor: opening sequence compresses the vesting.** Movement 1 — "took off his garments of gold, washed, and put on plain white linen." Lev 16:4 has him bathe *then* don the holy linen; the "garments of gold" framing is interpretive shorthand. Acceptable, but it's a small liberty on a frozen detail.

## Strengths (so the fix doesn't break them)
Hook grips ("a whole nation held its breath for a single man"). One thread spine — two goats → two halves → one Man → way open — never swaps. Landing does NEW work via Psalm 103:12 ("removed… east from west") tied back to the scapegoat, not a tired "will you trust Him?" The veil "from the top down… by God, from His own side" is a strong, accurate beat.

Net: this is a strong, faithful, verbatim-clean piece. The only thing keeping it from a clean pass is that the evangelistic close invites without naming the means of response.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. In Movement 7, before/within "Come," name the means of appropriation explicitly — that sin comes to rest on Him by trusting Him / turning to Him in faith — so a zero-Bible listener knows how to respond, not just that a door is open.
2. Strengthen Movement 5's answer to the "reading Christ back in" half of the objection (e.g. lean on the text's own forward-pointing insufficiency + Hebrews naming Christ as the fulfillment), so the rebuttal doesn't appear to assume the type.
3. Trim or soften the "garments of gold" vesting shorthand in Movement 1 to match Lev 16:4's bathe-then-linen order.
