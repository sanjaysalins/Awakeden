# Independent review — codex (OK, 75s)

Findings:

- Doctrinal overstatement: **“Scripture never claims the goats actually took sin away; it says the opposite, plainly”** is too absolute. Leviticus 16 does present real covenantal atonement and cleansing, even though Hebrews says animal blood could not finally remove sin. This needs “finally,” “in themselves,” or “as the ultimate reality.”

- Internal tension: **“the debt settled before God”** conflicts with **“a promise with the bill still unpaid.”** You are using “settled” for the goat, then “unpaid” for the same system. The distinction between typological/covenantal atonement and final atonement is not clear enough on first hearing.

- The line **“A cure you must repeat every year is not a cure”** is rhetorically sharp but risks making God’s appointed sacrifices sound fake or useless. Hebrews’ point is finality, not that the Day of Atonement was empty theater.

- The first goat is framed mainly as **“the price paid”** and **“the debt settled before God.”** Leviticus 16’s immediate emphasis is cleansing/purging the sanctuary because of Israel’s uncleanness and transgressions. Penal/payment language is orthodox in the larger NT frame, but here it is imposed too early and too flatly.

- KJV words: I do not see altered KJV words in the quoted Scripture. However, several quotes are truncated fragments made to look like complete sentences: **“two kids of the goats for a sin offering.”**, **“bring his blood within the vail.”**, **“unto a land not inhabited.”**, **“Having therefore, brethren, boldness to enter into the holiest by the blood of Jesus.”** If strict verbatim presentation includes punctuation/completeness, this needs ellipses or references.

- Clarity issue for non-Bible listeners: **“remission,” “mercy seat,” “vail,” “sanctify,” “holiest”** appear in quotations with limited unpacking. The narration mostly explains the ideas, but the KJV density may lose a first-time listener in the middle.

- Strong thread overall: the spine from **two goats → two aspects of atonement → Christ → open way in** holds. The CTA is not generic; **“You do not have to earn your way behind a curtain God Himself has already torn open”** lands the actual image.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Qualify the “goats never actually took sin away / not a cure / bill unpaid” language so it does not deny Leviticus’ real covenantal atonement.
2. Soften the “price/debt” framing around the first goat and let Leviticus’ cleansing emphasis stay visible.
3. Tighten quote discipline for truncated KJV fragments with ellipses, references, or exact punctuation.
