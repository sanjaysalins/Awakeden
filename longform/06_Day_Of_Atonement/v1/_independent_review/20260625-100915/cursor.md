# Independent review — cursor (OK, 110s)

## Independent adversarial review — *The Two Goats* (Day of Atonement)

### Hook (first ~5 seconds)
**Strong.** *"Once a year — only once — a whole nation held its breath for a single man"* lands tension, stakes, and singularity immediately. No fix needed here.

---

### KJV verbatim
**Words: clean.** Every quoted span matches the cached KJV corpus (Lev 16:2,5,8,10,15,21,22; Heb 9:12,22; 10:3,4,10,11–12,19; 13:12; Isa 53:6; Mt 27:51; Ps 103:12). No substituted words found.

**Punctuation (if your lock is strict):** Mt 27:51 ends `"…from the top to the bottom."` but KJV continues with a semicolon; Heb 10:19 ends with a period where KJV has a comma after *"Jesus,"*; Heb 10:12 ends with a period where KJV has a semicolon. Words only → pass; punctuation-strict LF-G1 → flag.

---

### Doctrinal soundness
Mostly orthodox evangelical typology (penalty + removal, shadow → substance, Hebrews’ own critique of animal blood). Three real problems:

**1. “Outside the camp” is mis-mapped to the scapegoat (Movement 4).**  
*"That day, all that carried the people's sin was sent outside the camp"* — then Hebrews 13:12. In Leviticus, the **live scapegoat** is sent *into the wilderness* by a fit man (16:21–22), not “outside the camp.” **Outside the camp** is where the **bodies of the slain sin-offering animals** are burned (Lev 16:27; Heb 13:11). Hebrews 13:11–12 is tying Christ to the **burned offering bodies**, not primarily to the wilderness goat. This blurs two distinct ritual actions and over-reads the scapegoat into a verse about different beasts. Not contrarian, but **textually imprecise** in a narration that sells itself on the detail the eye slides over.

**2. OT Day-of-Atonement cleansing is undercut (Movement 7).**  
*"Your sin… is not merely covered — held down, hidden, waiting to surface again next year"* sits awkwardly against Lev 16:30 (*"the priest make an atonement for you, to cleanse you, that ye may be clean from all your sins before the LORD"*) and against Movement 5’s own fair point that the ritual was a **real** picture, not nothing. The fix in M5 (*"picture God drew… bill still unpaid"*) is good; M7’s *"not merely covered"* risks sounding like Leviticus lied about what God accomplished that day — classic supersession slippage, not heresy, but **needs one clarifying line** that the cleansing was real-but-typological, not fake.

**3. Overclaim on NT explicitness (Movement 4).**  
*"the New Testament names the One in whom both goats meet"* — Hebrews never says that. It says goat-blood cannot take away sin, Christ entered by His own blood, Isaiah lays iniquity on Him, He suffered outside the gate. The **two-goats-in-one-Man** synthesis is standard typology, but the line presents inference as if the NT **names** it. That is exactly the eisegesis charge M5 steel-mans; M4 partially triggers it.

**4. Live goat / dead Christ asymmetry (unaddressed).**  
One goat is **killed**; the scapegoat is **presented alive** and released. Christ is **killed** and cast out. A careful listener will feel the mismatch. *"Both goats fulfilled in one Man"* (Movement 6) asserts resolution without showing it — especially since M4 emphasized the goat that *walked away alive*.

---

### Clarity on first hearing
**1. Hebrews 9:22 presented as “the Law’s answer” (Movement 2).**  
*"So how can a guilty people live in His presence and not be consumed? The Law's answer is stark:"* → **`"and without shedding of blood is no remission."`**  
That is **Hebrews 9:22**, not Leviticus. A zero-Bible listener will think Leviticus said it. The *idea* is lawful; the **attribution is wrong on first hearing** and violates the project’s own rule to attribute quotes before speaking them.

**2. Lev 16:10 left opaque (Movement 3).**  
The quote ends with *"to make an atonement with him"* — then the script jumps to *"Why would one sin offering need two goats?"* Who is *"him"*? The LORD? The goat? KJV ambiguity is fine to quote, but **without a five-word gloss** a first-time listener loses the thread at the central mystery.

**3. “Seven centuries before” (Movement 4).**  
*"as Isaiah foretold of the Messiah seven centuries before"* — before **what**? Before Christ, not before Leviticus. Spoken at pace, this sounds like Isaiah predates the ritual. Minor, but fixable with *"seven centuries before He came."*

**4. Conditional landing (Movement 7).**  
*"Your sin, if it is on Him"* — **if** lands as uncertainty for someone who doesn’t yet know imputation/faith. Combined with the one-word close *"Come."*, a listener may not know **come how, or as what**.

---

### Grace-anchored conviction
**Mostly pass.** The anti-works triad is well done:
- *"You do not have to keep paying for what has already been carried away."*
- *"You do not have to earn your way behind a curtain…"*
- *"You do not have to wait until you feel clean enough to come."*

*"Keep paying"* is negated obligation, not gain-loss selling — acceptable.

**Watch item:** *"debt settled"* / *"bill still unpaid"* / *"price paid in full"* is standard substitution language, not self-help, but stacked financial metaphors can still **feel transactional** to sensitive ears when repeated across M4–M7. Not a fail; worth one metaphor trim in M7.

No fear-pressure, no “before it’s too late,” no works-selfhelp landmines hit.

---

### Freshness = faithful depth
**Pass with one caveat.** The two-goats split (penalty vs. removal), the standing/sitting priest, veil rent top-down, and M5’s calendar argument (*"A cure you must repeat every year is not a cure"*) are genuinely text-driven, not clickbait. M5’s skeptic voice (*"primitive superstition… reading Christ back into a livestock ceremony"*) is a **real** steel-man, well answered from Hebrews 10:4 — strong LF-G8 work.

The freshness wobbles only where M4 over-claims NT explicitness and mis-assigns “outside the camp.”

---

### One thread spine (hook → middle → CTA)
**Pass.** Single spine throughout: one man / one day / one veil → two goats → both halves in Christ → shadow not substance → once-for-all → enter boldly. No thread-swapping. M3’s held question resolves in M4; M5 strengthens M6; M7 mirrors veil/blood/removal imagery from the opening.

---

### Landing — does it do NEW work?
**Mostly yes, but thin.** It avoids the banned *"Will you trust Him?"* template. *"Boldness to enter into the holiest by the blood of Jesus"* + *"The way in is open. The work is finished. The sin is gone. Come."* ties back to torn veil and finished priesthood — **not generic**.

**Problem:** *"Come."* alone does not tell a zero-Bible listener what “enter” means in practice (draw near through faith/blood — Heb 10:22 continues immediately after the quoted verse). The landing **names access** but not **response**. For an 8-minute gospel piece, that’s a missed beat — not a recycled close, but **under-specified**.

---

### Movement title noise (minor)
Movement 4 is titled **"The Centuries-Early Match"** but opens *"Centuries later."* Sloppy metadata; could confuse production, not doctrine.

---

### Summary judgment
This is **competent, orthodox, KJV-clean typology** with a strong hook, a real M5 objection, and a coherent spine. It is **not** ready to lock without revision: the **outside-the-camp / scapegoat conflation** is the sharpest textual error; **Hebrews-as-“Law’s answer”** is the sharpest clarity error; **M7’s “not merely covered”** needs nuance so Leviticus 16:30 isn’t contradicted on audio.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Repair Movement 4’s “outside the camp” beat: separate wilderness scapegoat from beasts burned outside the camp; align Hebrews 13:11–12 with the slain offering’s body, not the live goat’s desert journey.
2. Fix Movement 2 attribution: name Hebrews 9:22 (or quote Leviticus’ blood logic from the chapter itself) instead of presenting a NT verse as “the Law’s answer.”
3. Soften Movement 7’s “not merely covered” with one line that Lev 16’s cleansing was real but typological—then land “Come” with one concrete how (draw near through His blood / in faith), not veil imagery alone.
