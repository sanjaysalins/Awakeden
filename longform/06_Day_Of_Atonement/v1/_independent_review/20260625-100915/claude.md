## Doctrine — sound, with one typological seam to tighten
The both-goats typology (killed goat = penalty/propitiation in the blood; live goat = guilt borne away/expiation; both fulfilled in one Christ) is mainstream evangelical and handled carefully — it never claims the scapegoat *died*, and it grounds the move in Heb 9:12 + Isa 53:6 rather than asserting the NT "names" the scapegoat directly. Good restraint.

**The one real imprecision — Movement 4, the "outside the camp" link.** The narration writes: *"That day, all that carried the people's sin was sent outside the camp — and Hebrews catches the echo… He died outside the city wall, cast out where the sin-bearer always went,"* then quotes Heb 13:12 ("suffered without the gate"). But Hebrews 13:11–12 keys "without the camp / without the gate" specifically to the **burned body of the killed sin-offering goat** (Lev 16:27), not to the scapegoat. The line *"cast out where the sin-bearer **always** went"* maps the *scapegoat* (the live, guilt-bearing goat that went to the wilderness) onto Christ's crucifixion location — but Heb 13 is pointing at the *other* goat's fate. Both animals did leave the camp, so this isn't false, but the sentence quietly fuses the two goats' destinations and over-tightens the match. A careful listener tracking the two-goats logic could trip on it. Worth one clause of precision.

## Argument-from-silence stated as flat fact — Movement 6
*"He always stands… there was no chair in that tabernacle."* This is a beloved preaching point and broadly defensible (the tabernacle furniture lists no priestly seat, and Heb 10:11 says the priest "standeth daily"), but it's an argument from silence delivered as a hard architectural fact. Minor — soften to "no seat was given him" or lean on the Heb 10:11 "standeth" rather than the furniture claim.

This is a strong piece. The only finding with doctrinal/textual teeth is the Heb 13:12 / scapegoat-vs-burned-goat conflation, and it's a precision tweak, not an error or a heresy. Nothing here blocks a lock.
