Round 2 — Seedance 1.5 Pro on 3 HARDER stills

Same model, same locked frozen-tableau discipline, but multi-figure/crowd/action compositions instead of one calm hero portrait. 4.8 credits each.
FINDINGS — Seedance 1.5 Pro invents real motion once the scene gets hard: On the easy single-figure hero portrait (round 1) it was clean bar a minor light bloom. On all 3 harder stills below it broke the "painting stays still, only camera moves" rule in a visible, specific way: 1. Snakebite camp (7 figures + serpent) — the coiled red serpent visibly UNCOILED and extended into a slithering pose toward the man's foot by the last frame. That's invented creature motion, not camera reframing. 2. Pleading crowd (20+ figures) — Moses's arm/hand moved from extended-forward at chest height (source pose) to raised up near his head by the end of the clip. Invented body motion on the focal figure. 3. Hezekiah's hammer strike (mid-action, debris+smoke) — the hardest test: the hammer visibly continued its downward swing closer to the serpent, and the suspended debris shifted/fell further. It animated the STRIKE ITSELF rather than holding the frozen instant — the one thing the locked-discipline skill exists to prevent. Conclusion: Seedance 1.5 Pro's low price comes with a real cost on anything busier than a single calm portrait — it reads multi-figure/action compositions as motion cues and animates them, exactly the vector-proximity hallucination the kling-director-locked skill's token discipline was built to defeat. Worth re-testing WITH that skill's strict state-only prompt wording before ruling it out, but on a plain descriptive prompt it's not safe for action or crowd beats.
