## TITLE
Look, And Live: The Bronze Serpent That Was A Portrait Of The Cross

## DESCRIPTION
In Numbers 21, a whole camp is dying of snakebite, and God's remedy is not to kill the snakes, heal the wounds, or hand out an antidote. He tells them to look at a piece of metal on a pole. Look, and live. It is one of the strangest cures in the Bible.

Israel is worn down by the wilderness years, the old bitterness boils over, and "the LORD sent fiery serpents among the people" (Numbers 21:6). The people run to Moses and confess, "We have sinned," but even their confession only reaches outward, begging for the danger to be taken away, while the venom is already in their blood.

God does not do what they asked. He does not take the serpents away. Instead He tells Moses to make a bronze serpent and lift it on a pole: a fiery serpent for the fiery serpents, so that everyone who is bitten and looks at it will live. The cure is shaped like the curse.

Move forward roughly fourteen hundred years, into a quiet night conversation. Nicodemus comes to Jesus in the dark, and Jesus reaches back past the temple and past the law, to a snake on a pole in the desert: "And as Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, even so must the Son of man be lifted up: That whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have eternal life" (John 3:14-15).

This deep dive follows the bronze serpent from Numbers 21 to the cross: the strange detail, the honest objections (isn't a bronze snake just an idol? isn't a serpent a strange picture of Christ?), and the exchange at the centre of it all. The cure was never inside the dying man. It hung entirely outside him, on a pole.

"Who his own self bare our sins in his own body on the tree." (1 Peter 2:24)

You do not have to get the poison out first, or feel strong, or be certain your faith is large enough. You only have to stop staring at the bite, and look up. Lift your eyes from the poison to the Saviour. What He has done on that cross is enough.
