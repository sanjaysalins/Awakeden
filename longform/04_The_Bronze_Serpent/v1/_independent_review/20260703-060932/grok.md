## Independent adversarial review

### KJV verbatim

Most quotes check out against KJV (Num 21:5–9, John 3:14–15, 12:32–33, 2 Kgs 18:4, Gal 3:13). A few deserve scrutiny:

- **Num 21:7** — **"We have sinned… pray unto the LORD, that he take away the serpents from us."** The ellipsis drops *"for we have spoken against the LORD, and against thee."* Words shown are exact; still a partial quote inside quote marks.
- **2 Cor 5:21** — **"hath made him to be sin for us, who knew no sin"** drops the opening **"For he"**. Narration supplies God as speaker, but the quoted fragment is not a complete KJV clause.
- **1 Pet 2:24** — **"his own self bare our sins in his own body on the tree."** KJV opens **"Who his own self bare our sins…"** — **"Who"** is missing from the quoted string.

No smuggled paraphrase inside the marks, but the lens is strict: three quotes start mid-verse without marking that cut.

### Doctrinal soundness

Generally orthodox. Jesus’ own typology (John 3:14) carries the load; M5 guardrail is solid: **"Christ did not become a sinner. He was lifted up bearing our curse, in the likeness of the judgment we deserved."**

One phrase wobbles:

- **M6:** **"The curse fell on the lifted One, so that it would pass by everyone who looks to Him."** *Pass by* sounds like Passover (Exodus 12), not bronze-serpent logic. In Numbers, the bitten are already dying; the cure is look-and-*live*, not judgment skipping over them. Sloppy typology on first hearing.

M5’s serpent-as-curse argument is sound and guarded. Not contrarian or clickbait.

### Clarity on first hearing

- **M1:** **"And then the ground itself turns against them."** The LORD sent the serpents (Num 21:6). *Ground turns against them* is poetic filler that can mislead a listener with no Bible background.
- **M4:** **"will draw all men unto me"** sits unglossed before **"God so loved the world."** A naive ear can hear universal salvation in *draw all men* — scope is never clarified.
- **M2 → application jump:** **"when the poison is already within"** pivots from literal snake venom to universal sin before Jesus names the type (M4). The move works for church folks; a zero-knowledge listener may not know we left the camp scene.
- **M2 logical stretch:** **"That would not save a single person already bitten"** — if God can command a bronze cure, He can remove snakes *and* neutralize venom. The homily point (they asked for external relief, not internal cure) is good; the absolute overstates the text.

### Grace-anchored conviction

Clean. **"You do not have to get the poison out first, or feel strong, or be certain your faith is large enough"** and **"Faith is not a great work you produce"** invite; no fear, gain-loss, or works pressure. **"It cost the bitten Israelite nothing but a turn of the eyes. It cost the Son everything."** lands well.

### Freshness = faithful depth

Strong where it stays on the text: external remedy vs internal venom (M2), cure shaped like curse (M3), Jesus naming the pole Himself (M4), Hezekiah smashing the relic (M5). Not manufactured novelty.

**M4:** **"The snake on the pole and 'God so loved the world' sit in the same breath of Scripture"** — safer than claiming Jesus spoke 3:16, but still tightens a contested speaker boundary (many scholars end Jesus’ speech at v.15). Rhetorical overreach if presented as settled fact.

### One thread spine

Single spine holds: strange external cure → look/lifted up → cross → invitation. No thread swap.

### Landing — new work?

Yes. **"You can be too weak to stand and still lift your eyes"** / **"stop staring at the bite"** / **"the empty-handed look of someone who has stopped trying to save themselves"** is not a generic *will you trust Him?* close. **"What He has done on that cross is enough"** does finished-work landing.

Weakness: M6 and M7 restate the same beat three times — outside cure, turn of eyes, empty-handed look — so the close feels earned in content but padded in delivery.

### Hook (~5 seconds)

**"A whole camp is dying of snakebite — and God's remedy is not to kill the snakes, or heal the wounds, or hand out an antidote. He tells them to look at a piece of metal on a pole. Look, and live."** Grips fast. **"It is one of the strangest cures in the Bible"** is tell-not-show, but the opening line does the work.

### Pacing / craft

**M5:** **"Now, be fair to the doubts, because there are two."** Shifts from meditation to debate structure; densest stretch; most likely drift point before the cross exchange.

**Look vs believe mismatch:** John 3:15 quotes **"whosoever believeth in him"**, but M7 leans almost entirely on *look*. Standard typology, but a first-time hearer may think salvation is a physical glance, not trust in Christ — never bridged explicitly.

---

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. M6: Replace **"so that it would pass by everyone who looks to Him"** with language that matches the serpent type (*live*, *healed*, *the venom spent on Him*) — not Passover *pass by*.
2. Trim the M6→M7 seam: the outside-cure / turn-of-eyes / empty-handed-look idea is stated three times back-to-back; cut one pass so the landing hits once, hard.
3. Gloss **"draw all men unto me"** (draw toward, not save all) or lean proof weight onto **"signifying what death he should die"** so zero-Bible listeners don’t hear universalism right before John 3:16.
