# Independent review — gemini (OK, 52s)

This is an exceptionally strong, doctrinally precise, and well-crafted script. The KJV quotes are flawless, and the theological care taken in Movement 5 ("Christ did not become a sinner. He was lifted up bearing our curse, in the likeness of the judgment we deserved") is excellent. The grace-anchored CTA in Movement 7 ("You can be too weak to stand and still lift your eyes") is profound and perfectly aligned with the constraints.

However, applying adversarial skepticism, here are a few minor issues to tighten:

1. **Mixed Typology (Movement 6):** You write, "The curse fell on the lifted One, so that it would **pass by** everyone who looks to Him." The phrase "pass by" introduces Passover imagery (the angel of death passing over the blood on the doorposts). In the Bronze Serpent narrative, the judgment didn't "pass by" them—they were already bitten and dying. The typology is about looking and *living* despite the venom. 
2. **Slight Logical Stretch (Movement 2):** You write, "That would not save a single person already bitten; the venom is already in the blood." While this is a brilliant homiletical point about their request focusing on the external threat rather than the internal poison, it relies on a slight absolute (if God removed the snakes, He certainly had the power to miraculously neutralize the venom too). It works well for the narrative, but it is a slight stretch of the text's literal mechanics.
3. **Momentum Pause (Movement 5):** The detour into Hezekiah destroying the Nehushtan ("brake in pieces the brasen serpent") is historically accurate and answers a skeptical objection, but it briefly interrupts the powerful momentum building from the John 3 reveal toward the Galatians 3 atonement explanation. It recovers well, but it is a slight speed bump in the pacing.

VERDICT: PASS
TOP FIXES:
1. Movement 6: Change "so that it would pass by everyone who looks to Him" to "so that everyone who looks to Him might live" to avoid mixing the Bronze Serpent typology with Passover imagery.
2. Movement 2: Consider softening "That would not save a single person" to something like "That alone would not save a single person..." to avoid the logical absolute while keeping the powerful point about the internal venom.
3. Movement 5: Keep the Hezekiah reference if you feel the idol objection is critical for your audience, but recognize it slightly slows the drive to the cross. No mandatory change required.
