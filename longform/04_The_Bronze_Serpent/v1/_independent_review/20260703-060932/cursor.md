## Independent adversarial review — *The Bronze Serpent* (long-form narration)

### What holds up

- **Doctrine is orthodox and guarded.** The dangerous edge — serpent/cross typology sounding like Christ *became* a sinner — is explicitly blocked: *"Christ did not become a sinner. He was lifted up bearing our curse, in the likeness of the judgment we deserved."* That is anchored to 2 Cor 5:21 and Gal 3:13, not a contrarian read. Jesus (not a later preacher) is made the authority in M4: *"This is Jesus Himself, naming that pole as a portrait of His own cross."*
- **KJV substrings are word-exact** on every fragment I checked against the project cache (Num 21:5–9; John 3:14–15; John 12:32–33; 2 Kings 18:4; 2 Cor 5:21; Gal 3:13; 1 Pet 2:24). No substituted words inside the quote marks.
- **One thread spine holds:** poison already inside → wrong cure imagined (remove serpents) → God’s cure is outside, lifted up → look/believe → cross sufficiency. No thread-swap.
- **Grace-anchored conviction:** *"Faith is not a great work you produce"* / *"You do not have to get the poison out first"* — invitation, not fear or works bargaining.
- **Hook grips fast:** *"A whole camp is dying of snakebite — and God's remedy is not to kill the snakes…"* lands the strangeness inside ~5 seconds.

---

### Findings (specific, cited)

**1. KJV — Num 21:7 ellipsis is a strict-gate risk**

> **"We have sinned… pray unto the LORD, that he take away the serpents from us."**

The words shown are KJV, but the elided middle — *"for we have spoken against the LORD, and against thee;"* — is load-bearing confession detail. A punctuation-strict auditor will treat this as a non-contiguous verse span, not a clean lock. If you keep the ellipsis, the surrounding frame should carry what was dropped; as written, a listener never hears that they sinned *against the LORD and Moses*, only the serpent-removal plea.

**2. Clarity — John 12:32 can sound universalist to a zero-Bible ear**

> **"And I, if I be lifted up from the earth, will draw all men unto me."**

You pair it with 12:33 (*"signifying what death he should die"*), which proves *lifted up = cross*. But **"draw all men"** still gets no gloss. A first-time listener can easily hear *all are saved* right before *"For God so loved the world…"* — the two lines stack toward universalism unless the ear already knows *draw ≠ save*.

**3. Clarity / fidelity — the NT fulfillment word is *believe*, but the close is almost entirely *look***

John 3:14–15 is quoted correctly with **"believeth."** After that, the piece runs on look-metaphor: *"the cure was a look,"* *"lift your eyes,"* *"empty-handed look,"* *"stop staring at the bite, and look up."* That is powerful homiletics, but Jesus’ own typology in John 3 does not say *look* — it says the Son of Man must be lifted up so that **whosoever believeth** should not perish. A zero-Bible listener may finish thinking salvation is defined by a physical upward glance rather than trust in the lifted Christ. M7 never explicitly weds **look = believe**.

**4. Clarity — M4 compresses two timelines into one breath**

Nicodemus-by-night (John 3) → *"Later, John tells us"* (John 12) works. Then:

> **"And the passage flows straight on into the verse the whole world knows: 'For God so loved the world…'"**

Smart avoidance of the contested 3:16 speaker — but a novice can still hear one continuous night conversation with Jesus. *"The passage"* is doing technical work the ear will not catch.

**5. Repetition — M6→M7 still triple-states the same beat**

- M6: *"nothing but a turn of the eyes"* / *"It cost the Son everything."*
- M7: *"the cure was a look"* / *"too weak to stand and still lift your eyes"* / *"empty-handed look"* / *"stop staring at the bite, and look up."*
- Close: *"Lift your eyes from the poison to the Saviour."*

Same idea, four passes back-to-back. For 6–8 minutes that is the most likely drift point — not heresy, but editorial slack.

**6. Doctrine-adjacent imprecision — *"pass by"* in M6**

> **"The curse fell on the lifted One, so that it would pass by everyone who looks to Him."**

Penal substitution is **borne**, not **bypassed**. *"Pass by"* evokes serpents passing in the camp, not imputed curse absorbed on the tree. Careful listeners may hear dodging rather than substitution.

**7. Text-fidelity friction — *"the ground itself turns against them"***

> **"And then the ground itself turns against them."**

Num 21:6 says **"the LORD sent fiery serpents."** Poetic, but in a project that polices Scripture framing, this is an invented causal image — the ground did not turn; God sent judgment.

**8. OT attribution is thin on early quotes (project skill requirement)**

Num 21:5 and 21:6 drop into quoted speech after scene-setting, without an explicit *the people said* / *Scripture records* frame. M2 does better (*"They come to Moses and confess"*). For a listener with zero Bible knowledge, who is speaking in M1 is unclear until the content itself gives it away.

**9. Landing — better than generic, but the final motion is still look-language, not sufficiency-language**

The sufficiency beat is strong: *"already finished… What He has done on that cross is enough."* That is real new work. But the last imperative before it is again **"Lift your eyes…"** — so the ear’s final *action* is still the look-metaphor, not the finished work. The sufficiency line lands as theology; the look lands as CTA. Slight competition on the final bar.

**10. Minor KJV framing — 2 Cor 5:21**

> God **"hath made him to be sin for us, who knew no sin"**

Quoted words are exact KJV substring; KJV opens **"For he hath made him…"** Narrator supplies **"God"** as subject. Defensible in long-form, but strict attribution purists will note the verse is mid-sliced and re-headed.

### Non-findings (checked and cleared)

- *"near the end of the wilderness years"* — placement after Aaron’s death is defensible.
- *"this light bread"* = despised manna — faithful to Num 21:5.
- Hezekiah / brasen serpent — substring exact; no Nehushtan etymology overreach.
- M5 idol objection — steel-manned, Scripture-answered, sign-vs-Savior guarded.
- No fear/gain-loss/works CTA detected.
- Hook does not depend on a clever-but-opaque conceit.

---

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Explicitly marry **look → believe** in M7 (one sentence tying Num 21’s look to John 3’s *believeth*), and gloss **"draw all men"** so a zero-Bible ear cannot hear universal salvation.
2. Trim the **M6→M7 look/outside-cure** restatement to two beats max; let the sufficiency close (*"already finished… enough"*) carry the landing without a fourth *lift your eyes* imperative.
3. Restore **Num 21:7** as a contiguous KJV span (or drop quote marks and paraphrase with attribution), and fix framing drift: **"the LORD sent"** not *"the ground… turned against them"*; clarify M4’s **John 3 vs John 12** timeline for first-time listeners.
