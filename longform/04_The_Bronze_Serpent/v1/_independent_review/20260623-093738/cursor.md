### Clarity on first hearing — two logic slips

**1. M2 undercuts its own quote.** You have Israel confess **"We have sinned… pray unto the LORD, that he take away the serpents from us."** Then immediately: **"Take away the serpents — fix the surroundings, make the threat go away."** and **"we beg God to change our circumstances, when the thing that is killing us is already under our own skin."**

The text they just quoted *includes confession of sin*. The narration treats them as if they only asked for circumstantial relief and missed the internal problem. A listener who heard the quote will feel a skip — you're arguing against a straw request while leaving the confession on the floor. Either acknowledge the confession and show why removing serpents still misses the already-bitten, or don't quote **"We have sinned"** if you're going to ignore it.

**2. Internal vs. external tension (M2 → M6).** M2: **"The danger outside was never the deepest problem; the poison inside was."** M6: **"the cure was never *in* the dying man… It hung entirely outside him, on a pole."**

Both can be true typologically (sin within; remedy without), but a zero-Bible listener gets whiplash: first you're told outside isn't the real problem, then salvation is entirely outside. The bridge — *the poison is inside, but the cure God gives is outside, received by looking* — is never stated plainly in one sentence. Right now it reads like two clever moves, not one clear argument.

**3. M3 imprecision:** **"The thing lifted up is a serpent — the very image of what is killing them."** Fiery serpents are killing them; the bronze serpent is a *representation* Moses made. Calling it **"the very image of what is killing them"** blurs sign and agent. Minor, but it weakens the idolatry objection you answer in M5.

**4. M1 embellishment:** **"And then the ground itself turns against them."** Numbers 21:6 — **"the LORD sent fiery serpents."** God sends judgment; the ground doesn't turn. Poetic, but not text-faithful on first hearing.

**5. Look vs. believe (M4/M7):** John 3:14–15 pairs the serpent with **"whosoever believeth in him."** The script collapses everything to **look** — never once says *believe* in the invitation. Typologically fair (Jesus makes the link), but a listener who knows John 3:16 from culture may wonder if *looking* is enough. One explicit **look / believe** tie would close the gap.
