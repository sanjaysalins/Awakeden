# Independent review — codex (OK, 42s)

Findings:

- KJV issue: **“We have sinned… pray unto the LORD, that he take away the serpents from us.”** is not verbatim KJV. Numbers 21:7 reads **“We have sinned, for we have spoken against the LORD, and against thee; pray unto the LORD…”** The ellipsis removes inspired words inside a quotation, so it fails KJV-strict.

- KJV issue: **“For God so loved the world, that he gave his only begotten Son…”** is truncated inside quotation marks. If this is presented as KJV verbatim, ellipsis is not KJV text.

- Possible doctrinal overreach: **“The wilderness pole was always pointing at Calvary.”** Jesus explicitly makes the comparison in John 3:14, but “always” risks claiming more about original intent than the narration proves. Better: Jesus reveals its fulfillment in His lifting up.

- Clarity issue: **“the cure is shaped like the curse”** and **“He lifts up the image of the judgment itself”** are vivid but abstract. A first-time listener may not understand whether the serpent represents sin, judgment, Satan, curse, or Christ. The script later explains it, but the first pass is slippery.

- Doctrinal precision issue: **“We are all bitten; the poison of sin is already in us”** shifts the Numbers image from external judgment after rebellion to an internalized sin-nature metaphor. That can work homiletically, but it blurs the typology and risks making the “serpent” equal to “sin inside us,” which the script had already tried to avoid.

- Weak grace framing: **“You only have to stop staring at the bite, and look up.”** This is close to works language by making the decisive movement sound like self-managed attention. It is not fatal, but the line should anchor more clearly in God’s provision and Christ’s sufficiency.

- Landing is partly generic: **“Look to Him, and live.”** It is biblically apt, but as a final line it repeats the whole conceit rather than doing much new work. The stronger fresh insight is earlier: God gives a cure weak enough for dying people to receive.

- Hook is solid in concept, but **“one of the strangest cures in the Bible”** is a familiar Bible-video opener. The sharper hook is already inside the next sentence: God does not remove the snakes.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Fix all non-verbatim KJV quotations, especially the ellipses inside Scripture quotes.
2. Tighten the serpent/poison/curse typology so the symbol does not blur into invented doctrine.
3. Rewrite the landing so it adds fresh gospel force instead of only repeating “look, and live.”
