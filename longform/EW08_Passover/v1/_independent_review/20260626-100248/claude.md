# Independent review — claude (OK, 110s)

I read the full narration and checked every `**"..."**` quote against the KJV, plus the doctrine, reveal, wrestling, voice, and CTA.

## What holds up (verified, not flattery)

**KJV verbatim — all 14 quotes are exact.** I checked each against the text:
- Ex 12:12, 12:3, 12:5, 12:6, 12:22 (hyssop + "none of you shall go out…"), 12:13, 12:23, 12:46, 12:30 — all verbatim, including the KJV spelling **"bason"** inside the quote (good — and note he correctly uses modern "basin" in his own prose, "I caught his blood in a basin," keeping the quote clean).
- NT: 1 Pet 1:19, John 19:33, John 19:36, 1 Cor 5:7 — all verbatim.
- Attribution is present before each quote (**[the LORD]**, "the command had come down through Moses," "the word written of Him says," "the Scripture says"). No misattribution.

**The reveal is genuinely earned (EW-G7).** The two "strange details" — blood struck *on the outside where it can be seen*, and *not a bone broken* — are planted as unsolved riddles in Beat 4 ("they followed me to my grave… I obeyed it. I did not see why"), then paid off in Beat 6 with the exact KJV hooks (1 Pet "without blemish and without spot" = "the very words of my own command"; John "they brake not his legs"). That's a shown turn, not an announced one.

**Doctrine sound + substitution named (EW-G9).** "The death I deserved fell on Him instead… The firstborn stood under sentence; the lamb died in his place. We all stand under sentence; the Lamb of God died in ours." Penalty + what Christ bore are explicit. No supersession (Israel's night is the *shadow* fulfilled, never denigrated). "A deliverance that need never be done again" rightly echoes once-for-all.

**CTA is on-Jesus and not the banned template (EW-INV-2).** "Come home to Jesus" names Him; the response is named and concrete ("make His blood your own… hide behind Him"); grace-anchored ("Not because you are good — I was not"). It is *not* the tired bare "will you trust Him?" — it's a characterful question rooted in the doorpost image.

## Real weaknesses (adversarial)

**1. The wrestling sidesteps the actually-strongest objection (EW-G8).** The doubt he voices is the *efficacy* of substitution: "How could the blood of a lamb stand in for the life of my son?" That's genuine and not a strawman — but the harder objection is sitting right in his own narration and he walks past it. In Beat 5 he *hears* "mothers weeping over sons as dear to them as mine" and feels only relief — he never wrestles with the **justice** of the judgment that killed Egypt's firstborn children. A modern listener's strongest stumbling block at Passover is exactly that, and the piece raises it and drops it. The steel-man would be stronger if his doubt touched "why them and not me, when I am no better?" — which he sets up early ("I was no better than any Egyptian") and then never honestly grapples with.

**2. The witness is deliberately unnamed — a format tension.** "I will not give you my name — I withhold it on purpose… let me be every father of Israel." The format's premise is a *named* biblical witness telling what they lived. The archetypal choice is defensible (no individual father is named in Ex 12) and it's handled gracefully, but it trades the specific-person intimacy the eyewitness device runs on for a representative voice. Flagging for the user's call — it's a creative deviation, not a clear pass.

**3. Mild repetition / length drag.** The "blood had to be applied where the judgment could see it" point is made three times (Beat 4, Beat 6, Beat 7). It's motif, but by Beat 7 ("the blood that had to be struck where the judgment could see it") it's restating, not advancing. The piece runs long; one pass to tighten Beats 6–7 would lose nothing.

**4. Minor logic strain in the reveal image.** "a shadow is cast only by a body that comes after" — physically a shadow isn't cast by a body that comes *later*; it's poetic license on Col 2:17 ("a shadow of things to come; but the body is of Christ"). Doctrinally anchored, but the line invites a "that's not how shadows work" snag. Optional.

None of these are KJV errors, doctrinal failures, or CTA problems — the spine is solid. The wrestling gap (#1) is the one I'd hold the LOCK on.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Let the witness wrestle with the genuine hardest objection he himself raises — the justice of the firstborn judgment falling on Egypt's children when "I was no better than any Egyptian" — instead of feeling only relief at the weeping next door; this makes EW-G8's steel-man real.
2. Surface the deliberately-unnamed/archetypal witness choice for the user's decision (it diverges from the "named witness" premise) — keep it only if intended.
3. Tighten the "blood applied where it can be seen" motif (stated 3×) and the Beat 6–7 overlap to cut runtime drag without losing the payoff.
