# Independent review — codex (OK, 135s)

Findings:

- Fatal witness-format breach: the artifact is not a named biblical witness. It explicitly says, `“I will not give you my name”`, `“I was not one man only”`, and `“Let me stand for them all; let me be every father of Israel on that night.”` That breaks the premise of one named eyewitness and weakens the spine. This is a composite sermon persona, not a biblical witness recounting his own lived moment.

- KJV wording appears mostly exact, but attribution fails in several places. `**"with the precious blood of Christ..."**` is introduced only by `“The word written of Him says”`; `**"when they came to Jesus..."**` by `“the Scripture says”`; `**"A bone of him shall not be broken"**` by `“it stands written”`; and `**"Christ our passover is sacrificed for us"**` is dropped in without speaker/book attribution. EW-INV-4 requires attribution before quoting, not vague Scripture-language.

- The line `“Without blemish, without spot — the very words of my own command”` is inaccurate as phrased. Exodus 12:5 gives `“without blemish”`; `“without spot”` is from 1 Peter 1:19, not “the very words” of the Passover command.

- The reveal is partly earned by real textual details: blood applied to the door, the lamb’s death, and no broken bone. But the turn still leans on announcement: `“Only now do I see...”`, `“my lamb without blemish was His shadow”`, and especially `“a shadow is cast only by a body that comes after.”` That last line is clever but confusing, and it explains typology rather than letting the witness’s lived details carry the weight.

- The wrestling is real but not the strongest version. `“How could the blood of a lamb stand in for the life of my son?”` is a good internal objection. But the harder objection is opened and then dodged: `“mothers weeping over sons as dear to them as mine”` plus `“I was no better than any Egyptian.”` The steel-man question should be: why my son spared and theirs not, if I had no moral claim? The piece retreats into mechanism: `“A lamb had died in his place.”`

- Doctrine is broadly sound on substitution: `“the death I deserved fell on Him instead”` and `“The Lamb of God died in ours”` name the exchange. But the invitation muddies response-language with `“will you make His blood your own”` and `“hide behind Him.”` It finally says `“Come home to Jesus”`, but the main ask is metaphorical rather than clearly naming faith, receiving, or turning to Christ.

- Voice breaks repeatedly into detached essay/preacher mode: `“The record of that night would say it plainly,”` `“The word written of Him says,”` `“the Scripture says,”` and `“This was the substitution my doorpost had been whispering all my life.”` Those are not the natural voice of an Israelite father; they are a narrator wearing the father as a frame.

- First-hearing clarity is decent on the basic story, but some phrases are opaque for a zero-Bible listener: `“destroyer,”` `“bason,”` `“Christ our passover,”` and `“make His blood your own.”` The artifact explains the Passover event better than it explains the response to Christ.

- The hook is slowed by meta-disclaimer. `“I was a father in Israel...”` has force, but then `“I will not give you my name... I was not one man only”` turns the opening into format-explanation instead of immediate lived danger.

- Long-form runtime is not fully earned. The best added beats are Beat 4’s blood/bone questions and Beat 5’s midnight cry. But the close repeats earlier material: `“I did not earn it”`, `“a lamb lay dead where my own son should have lain”`, `“Something spotless died so my boy could live”`, and `“blood on the wood”` have already been established. `“Sit with that.”` adds pause, not a new turn.

VERDICT: FAIL
TOP FIXES:
1. Replace the composite “every father” narrator with one named biblical witness, or change the artifact type.
2. Add clear source/speaker attribution before every KJV quote, especially the New Testament fulfillment quotes.
3. Rework the final invitation so the response is explicitly named as coming to/receiving/trusting Jesus by faith, without vague “make His blood your own” language.
