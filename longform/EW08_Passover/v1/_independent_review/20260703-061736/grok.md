# Independent review — grok (OK, 79s)

## Findings

### EW-INV-4 — KJV verbatim + attribution (multiple failures)

**Missing speaker attribution before quotes.** Only two blocks name the speaker (`**[the LORD]**`). The rest drop straight into KJV with no “Moses said” / “the LORD commanded” frame, which the lens requires.

Examples:
- Beat 3: `**"they shall take to them every man a lamb, according to the house of their fathers, a lamb for an house"**`
- Beat 3: `**"Your lamb shall be without blemish, a male of the first year"**`
- Beat 3: `**"the whole assembly of the congregation of Israel shall kill it in the evening"**`
- Beat 3: `**"ye shall take a bunch of hyssop..."**` / `**"none of you shall go out at the door of his house until the morning"**`
- Beat 4: `**"neither shall ye break a bone thereof"**`
- Beat 6: `**"when they came to Jesus, and saw that he was dead already, they brake not his legs"**`
- Beat 6–7 (twice): `**"Christ our passover is sacrificed for us"**`

**Mis-framed quote at midnight.** Beat 5 sets up something *heard*:

> “And I heard it — not in my house, but across the whole of Egypt — a cry such as I had never heard:”

Then it quotes Exodus narrative summary, not a cry or spoken word:

> `**"there was not a house where there was not one dead"**`

That line is narrator report in Exodus 12:30, not audio the father could quote as KJV speech. It breaks both attribution and first-person credibility.

**Weak NT attribution.** Beat 6:

> “the word written of Him says He was **`"the precious blood of Christ, as of a lamb without blemish and without spot"`**”

No book, no writer, no `[Peter]` / `[Paul]` / `[John]` — too vague for EW-INV-4.

(KJV substrings themselves look mostly exact; the main issue is attribution and the midnight misuse.)

---

### EW-G7 — Reveal not fully earned; partly announced/imported

Beat 4 earns two live riddles (blood on the *outside*; unbroken bones). Good setup.

Beat 6 then undercuts the eyewitness turn:

> “**I did not learn the answer in my lifetime.** I tell you it now from the far side of my days, by a light that fell long after my night, **in words written in an age I never saw.**”

> “**I did not know His name** when I painted my door.”

The Christ link is not *shown from the night he lived*; it is **imported posthumously** from NT texts he never saw (`1 Peter`, `John`, `1 Corinthians`). The bones thread is the one strong earned bridge; the rest is assertion via outside scripture, including:

> “There came another Lamb… a Man named **Jesus**”

That is revelation-by-annotation, not “the night itself opened to Christ.”

---

### EW-G10 — Voice breaks in the reveal

Beats 1–5: consistent, characterful Israelite father.

Beat 6–7: he stops sounding like a man of that night and reads like a typology essay in first-person costume — citing crucifixion detail, “precious blood of Christ,” soldiers at the cross, Pauline “Christ our passover.” A real witness would wrestle with what the lamb *meant*; he delivers a finished sermon with NT footnotes.

---

### EW-G8 — Wrestling (mostly strong; one soft spot)

Steel-man doubt is real and in his voice:

> “How could the blood of a lamb stand in for the life of my son?”

> “was this small creature the end of it, or only a sign, pointing at something I could not yet see?”

That holds.

Soft spot: after the plague he says the doubt “**only deepened**” while his son is alive and the lamb clearly worked. A harder steel-man might ask why God demands recurring blood year after year if one lamb already saved the firstborn — the piece gestures at that but moves quickly to relief.

---

### EW-G9 — Doctrine (mostly sound; one stretch)

**Substitution is named clearly:**

> “Something had to die, or my son would have died. A life for a life.”

> “the death I deserved fell on Him instead, exactly as the death my son deserved had fallen on the lamb”

> “We all stand under sentence; the Lamb of God died in ours.”

No supersession problem; Passover → Christ is standard typology.

Stretch: Beat 6 fuses lamb and Christ on a four-day detail:

> “He was the spotless male of the first year I had held only four short days.”

That leans on Palm-Sunday arithmetic the text never gives the father. Typology-by-counting, not text-earned.

---

### EW-INV-2 — CTA on Jesus (passes, minor template risk)

Jesus is named; invitation is grace-anchored; response is partly named (“Come… take His blood… shelter under it”).

Slight template drift at the close:

> “**Come inside, and trust the Lamb** who was slain for you.”

“Trust the Lamb” is better than bare “trust Him,” but still close to a tired trust-closer after a strong “come / take / shelter” line.

---

### Long-form runtime — some sag / repetition

**Earned:** ritual walk-through (Beat 3), two strange details (Beat 4), midnight wrestling (Beat 5).

**Sags / repeats:**
- Beat 3 lists every Passover step in sequence — necessary for newcomers, but long for anyone who knows the story; little new *turn* per step.
- Blood-must-be-*applied* lesson appears in Beat 4, again in Beat 6, again in Beat 7 (“It is not enough only to know that He died”).
- `**"Christ our passover is sacrificed for us"**` twice (Beat 6 and 7) without a new beat between.

Beat 7 adds rest (“deliverance that will never need to be done again”) but mostly re-preaches Beat 6.

---

### Hook / clarity / spine (strengths, for balance)

Hook grips:

> “before the sun rose, death would walk down our street, and would not pass our house unless I did a strange and bloody thing with my own two hands.”

One witness, one thread (door, blood, firstborn, lamb). A zero-Bible listener can follow the stakes. Those strengths are why this is **REVISE**, not **FAIL**.

---

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Earn the Christ reveal from what the father lived and kept (blood on the doorpost, unbroken bones, firstborn spared) — cut or radically reframe “I did not learn the answer in my lifetime / words written in an age I never saw”; stop having him quote NT as personal memory.
2. Fix EW-INV-4 everywhere: attribute every KJV quote before the quote; replace the midnight “cry” line with what he actually heard (wailing across Egypt), not Exodus 12:30 summary as if it were heard speech.
3. Restore Beat 6–7 voice: father’s deduction and dread, not crucifixion footnotes; trim repeated blood-application / “Christ our passover” beats so Beat 7 lands one fresh invitation, not a second sermon.
