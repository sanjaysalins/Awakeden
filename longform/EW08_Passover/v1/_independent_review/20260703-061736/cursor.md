## Independent adversarial review — *The Passover Lamb* (eyewitness-long)

---

### EW-INV-4 — KJV verbatim + attribution

**Hard failure (altered KJV inside quotes):**

- Beat 4: **`"when he seeth the blood upon the lintel, and on the two side posts, the LORD will pass over the door, and will not suffer the destroyer to come in unto you to destroy you"`**
  - KJV Exodus 12:23 reads: *"…come in unto **your houses to smite you**."*
  - *"you to destroy you"* is not a substring of the KJV. This is an altered quote, not a trimming issue.

**Attribution gaps (most command/Scripture blocks):**

Only two quotes carry a speaker tag (`**[the LORD]**` in Beats 2 and 3). Under a strict EW-INV-4 read, the rest drop in unattributed:

| Location | Quote | Problem |
|----------|-------|---------|
| Beat 3 | `"Your lamb shall be without blemish…"`, `"the whole assembly…shall kill it"`, hyssop/lintel quote, `"none of you shall go out…"` | One Moses/LORD setup line, then four quotes with no repeated tag |
| Beat 4 | bone quote `"neither shall ye break a bone thereof"` | No Moses/LORD frame after *"Two things in that command I never understood"* |
| Beat 5 | `"there was not a house where there was not one dead"` | No source at all — not Moses, not Scripture, not *"the record says"* |
| Beat 6 | `"Christ our passover is sacrificed for us"` (first use) | Colon after *"exactly as the death my son deserved had fallen on the lamb:"* is not attribution |

**Mis-framing (quote verbatim, meaning bent):**

- Beat 6: *"and the word written of Him says He was"* → **`"the precious blood of Christ, as of a lamb without blemish and without spot"`**
  - Spoken aloud this becomes *"He was the precious blood of Christ"* — not 1 Peter 1:19’s sense (*"But **with** the precious blood of Christ…"*). The substring is KJV-clean; the witness frame distorts it.

### EW-INV-2 — CTA on Jesus

**Passes minimum bar:** Beat 7 names **Jesus** (*"Look to Jesus, the Lamb that God Himself has provided"*) and names responses: **Come** (*"Come, and take His blood for your own"*, *"Come inside"*) plus **trust** (*"trust the Lamb who was slain for you"*). Grace framing is present: *"not because you are good, but because He is your Lamb."*

**Problems:**

- *"Come inside, and **trust** the Lamb"* — not the banned bare *"will you trust Him?"* template, but **trust** is the weakest/nakedest invitation verb here; **come / shelter / apply** do the real work.
- *"take His blood for your own"* — vivid for insiders; a zero-Bible listener may hear sacrament or magic, not faith/shelter. **Come** is named; **believe/receive** less so.
- Beat 7 **re-quotes** **`"Christ our passover is sacrificed for us"`** already used in Beat 6 — reads like recap, not a fresh invitation turn.

---

### EW-G7 — Reveal earned

**Earned (strong):**

- Beat 4’s bone riddle → Beat 6’s John 19:33/36 stack: *"My lamb's unbroken bones had been pointing across the centuries to His"* is **shown** from a detail the witness obeyed without understanding.

**Asserted / under-walked:**

- *"I did not learn the answer in my lifetime. I tell you it now from the far side of my days, by a light that fell long after my night, in words written in an age I never saw"* — signpost satisfies EW-INV-11, but the turn is still **announced**, not walked.
- *"The command that my lamb be without blemish was a shadow, and a shadow waits for the body that casts it"* — typology label dropped in; less *I lived this* than *let me explain shadows*.
- *"He was the spotless male of the first year I had held only four short days"* — forces a parallel (four days with the lamb → Christ) that the text does not give the witness; feels **engineered**, not discovered.
- The piece’s sharpest lived mystery — *"It was not enough that the lamb had died. The blood had to be applied"* — resolves in Beat 6 by assertion: *"was His blood, poured out and **applied** to everyone who shelters under it"* with **no attributed NT verse** on sheltering/application (unlike the bone thread, which stacks John). The doctrinal center is preached, not quote-shown.
- *"This is the substitution my doorpost had been whispering all along"* — summary rhetoric. The doorpost whispered **firstborn-for-lamb**, not **Christ-for-the-world**, until Beat 6 asserts the extension.

### EW-G8 — Wrestling (steel-man)

**Genuine opening:**

*"How could the blood of a lamb stand in for the life of my son? What was a pen animal to the God who made heaven and earth, that a smear of its blood should turn aside His own judgment?"* — real, period-appropriate, internalized. Not a strawman.

**Deflated before Beat 6:**

- Same beat answers the steel-man empirically: *"The difference was not that I was righteous. The difference was a lamb that had died in his place, and the blood of it on my door"* / *"Something had to die, or my son would have died. A life for a life."* Midnight **proves substitution worked**; the intellectual objection is largely overridden by outcome.
- What survives is softer: *"was this small creature the end of it, or only a sign, pointing at something I could not yet see?"* — reads as a **setup question for Beat 6**, not a doubt the piece truly wrestles with. Beat 6 answers immediately with NT montage.
- *"And I will be honest with you, as a man must be honest before God"* — throat-clearing; adds no new turn.

### EW-G9 — Doctrine

**Sound core:** Spotless lamb, blood turning away judgment, substitution named (*"the lamb died in his place"* → *"the Lamb of God died in ours"*), **Christ our passover**, no supersession, CTA redirects to Jesus not the annual lamb.

**Issues:**

1. **Universal atonement leap** — *"We all stand under sentence; the Lamb of God died in ours"* extends **firstborn/destroyer** to **sin-judgment** without a shown step. Orthodox, but **asserted** the way John 19 **earns** the bone.
2. **Christ-side penalty thin** — firstborn plague / destroyer is vivid; what Christ **bore** is named (*"death I deserved"*, *"judgment"*) but not anchored to a supporting verse (Romans, Isaiah 53, etc.).
3. Beat 2: *"I was no better than the Egyptian in the next field. If the judgment went by who deserved it, my door had no claim on mercy"* — collapses **blood-marker salvation under the same plague** into **moral equivalence with Egypt**. Works as grace-not-works setup, but a careful listener may wonder why Israel needed blood if the issue were Egyptian guilt alone.
4. Beat 6 predicate glitch (*"He was the precious blood"*) — minor grammar, but it blurs the redemption claim at the doctrinal pivot.

### EW-G10 — Voice / character

**Strengths:** *"I painted my doorframe with blood"*, four days feeding the lamb, *"I obeyed it. I did not see why"* — hands-on, first-person, not essay-costume for long stretches.

**Weaknesses:**

- **Archetypal, not one man:** *"I will not give you my name"* / *"Let me stand for them all"* — justified composite, but the voice stays **Every Father** rather than one remembered man; less characterful than named witnesses.
- Beat 6–7 drift to **catechism voice:** *"a shadow waits for the body that casts it"*, *"The lesson of my night was never that the lamb was enough simply by being killed"* — correct theology, less **I was there** than **let me explain the type**.
- Beat 2: *"Moses came back from Pharaoh with his face set like flint"* — *"face set like flint"* is Isaiah/Ezekiel idiom, not Exodus Passover narrative detail; small **costume-anachronism** in an otherwise grounded voice.

---

### First-hearing clarity

- Hook and stakes are clear: *"death would walk down our street, and would not pass our house unless I did a strange and bloody thing with my own two hands."*
- **Destroyer** appears in the Beat 4 quote (*"will not suffer the destroyer"*) but is **never explained** for a listener with zero Bible background.
- Beat 6 assumes **Jesus + crucifixion + soldiers breaking legs** without grounding who He is or what crucifixion is.
- Beat 1 composite framing (*"I was not the only one"*) may confuse before the story starts.

---

### Hook, spine, LONG runtime

**Hook:** Strong — immediate stakes, bloody act, firstborn son.

**Spine:** One witness, one thread (blood + unbroken bone + spotless lamb). No swap.

**LONG sag:** Beat 3 ritual walkthrough earns some runtime, but Beat 5 **re-proves** Beat 3’s moral (*"The difference was not that I was righteous"* vs *"We were not saved by being good"*), and Beat 7 **re-teaches** blood-application/shadow already landed in Beats 4 and 6. The piece reads **complete around ~8–9 min**, not **deep at 10–11** — thin for the long-form band unless pace is very slow. Likely at or below the 1,300-word floor once quotes are counted.
