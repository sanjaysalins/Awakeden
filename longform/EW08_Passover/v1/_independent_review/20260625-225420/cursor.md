# Independent review — cursor (OK, 68s)

## Independent adversarial review — *The Passover Lamb* (eyewitness-long)

---

### EW-INV-4 — KJV verbatim + attribution

**Verbatim:** All bold quotes appear to be KJV substrings against `passage.txt`. No altered words detected inside the quote spans themselves.

**Attribution gaps (still problems under the lens):**

1. **Exodus 12:30 without a named source** — Beat 5: *"Then midnight came. And I heard it — not in my house, but across the whole of Egypt — a cry such as I had never heard:"* then **`"there was not a house where there was not one dead"`**. That line is Moses’s narrative summary, not the witness’s live report. A zero-Bible listener hears it as his eyewitness words. Needs explicit framing (*as Moses later wrote*, *the record says*, etc.) before the quote.

2. **Beat 3 command cluster** — After *"The command had come down through Moses from the LORD:"* only the first quote is clearly sourced. The blemish, kill-it-in-the-evening, hyssop, and stay-inside quotes follow with no repeated attribution. Acceptable for a listening ear only if you treat the opening line as covering the block; strict reading of EW-INV-4 wants attribution before *each* quote or a tagged **`[Moses]`** line.

3. **Beat 6 NT quotes** — **`"when they came to Jesus, and saw that he was dead already, they brake not his legs"`** and **`"A bone of him shall not be broken"`** drop in after prose (*"when they came to Him they stopped"* / *"The riddle… was answered on a hill I never saw"*) with no *Scripture says* / *it was written* frame. The bone thread is the strongest part of the reveal; unattributed NT drops weaken both attribution and first-hearing clarity.

**Mis-framing (worse than a missing “But”):**

4. Beat 6: *"The body came — a Man named Jesus, and the word written of Him says He was"* **`"the precious blood of Christ, as of a lamb without blemish and without spot"`**. KJV 1 Peter 1:19 is *"But with the precious blood of Christ, as of a lamb without blemish and without spot"* — redemption **with** His blood, not *He was the precious blood*. The substring is verbatim; the **surrounding predicate alters what the verse says**. That is misattribution in the review lens even if EW-G1 passes.

### EW-INV-2 — CTA on Jesus

**Passes the bar:** Beat 7 names **Jesus** (*"Look to Jesus, the Lamb that God Himself has provided"*) and names the response: **Come** (*"Come, and take His blood for your own"*), **trust** (*"Come inside, and trust the Lamb who was slain for you"*). No banned bare *will you trust Him?* template.

**Borderline:**

- *"the judgment that is real — as real as the cry I heard at midnight — will pass over you"* — grace-qualified (*not because you are good*), but it re-invokes the midnight death-cry as the motivator. Not full fear/gain-loss, but it leans on terror-echo more than invitation.

- *"Come, and take His blood for your own"* — vivid for insiders; a first-hearing listener may not know what “take His blood” means practically (faith? sacrament?). The CTA names **come/trust** but not **believe/receive** as clearly as the bone thread names John 19.

### EW-G7 — Reveal earned

**Earned well:**
- Unbroken-bone riddle (Beat 4) → John 19:33/36 (Beat 6) is the best work in the piece. *"My lamb's unbroken bones had been pointing across the centuries to His"* is shown, not merely announced.
- Spotless lamb / without blemish parallel is text-grounded.

**Asserted, not shown:**

- *"There came another Lamb"* — pivot by announcement; the witness did not live to see a second lamb. Signposting (*"from the far side of my days"*) helps per EW-INV-11, but the turn still **tells** more than **walks**.

- The **blood-must-be-applied** strand is the piece’s sharpest lived mystery (Beat 4: *"It was not enough that the lamb had died. The blood had to be applied"*), but Beat 6 resolves it with assertion: *"His blood, poured out and applied to everyone who shelters under it"* — **no NT verse** on sheltering/applying/faith (contrast EW01’s stacked Heb/Eph quotes). The application→Christ link is the doctrinal center of Passover typology; here it is preached, not quoted-shown like the bone thread.

- *"The death I deserved fell on Him instead"* — jumps from **firstborn under plague** to **personal desert** without a step for a zero-Bible listener. Beat 2’s *"If the judgment went by who deserved it, my door had no claim on mercy"* partially sets it up, but Beat 6 does not re-anchor before the universal claim *"We all stand under sentence."*

- *"This is the substitution my doorpost had been whispering all along"* — summary rhetoric; the doorpost whispered **firstborn-for-lamb**, not **Christ for the world**, until Beat 6 asserts it.

### EW-G8 — Wrestling (steel-man)

**Genuine in Beat 5 opening:** *"How could the blood of a lamb stand in for the life of my son? What was a pen animal to the God who made heaven and earth, that a smear of its blood should turn aside His own judgment?"* — real, internalized, period-appropriate.

**Deflated before the reveal:**

- Same beat answers it empirically: *"The difference was a lamb that had died in his place"* / *"Something had to die, or my son would have died. A life for a life."* Midnight proves **substitution worked**; the intellectual objection is overridden by outcome. What remains is the softer typology question: *"was this small creature the end of it, or only a sign, pointing at something I could not yet see?"* — almost a setup question for Beat 6, not a steel-man the piece truly wrestles with.

- Beat 5 also repeats Beat 3’s moral (*"The difference was not that I was righteous"*) — the wrestling beat spends runtime restating what the act beat already landed.

**Strawman avoided:** The doubt is not fake skepticism. It’s just **resolved too early**, leaving Beat 6 to deliver typology answers to a question the witness already half-accepts.

### EW-G9 — Doctrine

**Sound core:** Spotless lamb, blood turning away judgment, substitution (*"the lamb died in his place"* → *"the Lamb of God died in ours"*), **Christ our passover**, no supersession in the CTA redirect (*"Do not look to a lamb… Look to Jesus"*).

**Issues:**

1. **1 Peter mis-framing** (above) — makes Christ *identical with* “the precious blood” rather than the spotless Lamb whose blood redeems.

2. **Universal atonement leap** — *"We all stand under sentence; the Lamb of God died in ours"* without tying the Egypt **destroyer/firstborn** type to **sin-judgment** in the listener’s ear. Orthodox, but the piece **asserts** the extension rather than **showing** it from the type (cf. how EW01 stacks verses for each half of the type).

3. **Substitution named but penalty thin on Christ side** — firstborn plague / destroyer is vivid; what Christ **bore** is named (*death I deserved*, *judgment*) but not anchored to a verse (Romans, Isaiah 53, etc.) the way John 19 anchors the bone.

### EW-G10 — Voice / character

**Strengths:** Mud-brick house, children feeding the lamb four days, *"I painted my doorframe with blood"*, *"I obeyed it. I did not see why"* — hands-on, first-person, not essay-costume for long stretches.

**Weaknesses:**

- **Archetypal, not named:** *"I will not give you my name. I was not the only one… Let me stand for them all"* — justified composite, but the voice stays **Every Father** rather than one remembered man (Abraham, Boaz, etc.). Less characterful than the format’s best episodes.

- Beat 6–7 shift toward **catechism voice:** *"The command that my lamb be without blemish was a shadow, and a shadow waits for the body that casts it"* / *"The lesson of my night was never that the lamb was enough simply by being killed"* — correct theology, less **I was there** than **let me explain the type**.

- *"And I will be honest with you, as a man must be honest before God"* — slightly preacher-to-audience; breaks the night’s immediacy.

---

### First-hearing clarity

- Hook and plague logic are clear.
- **Crucifixion leg-breaking** is explained briefly — OK.
- **Destroyer** in the Beat 4 quote (*"will not suffer the destroyer to come in"*) is never explained; a new listener may not know who that is.
- Beat 6 assumes **Jesus + cross** without grounding who He is or what the cross is for someone with zero Bible background.
- **"Take His blood for your own"** may confuse without a one-line gloss (faith / shelter under Him).

---

### Hook, spine, LONG runtime

**Hook:** Strong — *"before the sun rose, death would walk down our street, and would not pass our house unless I did a strange and bloody thing with my own two hands."*

**Spine:** One witness, one thread (blood + unbroken bone). No swap.

**LONG sag:** Spoken body is ~**1,305 words** (floor of the 1,300–1,650 band) — thin for a confident 9–11 min at natural multi-voice pace.

**Padding / repetition:**
- **`"Christ our passover is sacrificed for us"`** quoted in Beat 6 **and** Beat 7 — second use adds no new turn.
- Beat 7 largely **re-teaches** Beat 4’s application theology (*blood had to be applied* → *"It is not enough only to know that He died"*) without a fresh beat.
- Beat 5 **re-proves** righteousness-not-the-difference already clear in Beat 3 (*"We were not saved by being good. We were saved by being inside, under the blood"*).
- Beat 3’s ritual walkthrough earns some runtime; Beats 5–7 do not all earn theirs — the piece feels **complete at ~8 min**, not **deep at 10**.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Repair Beat 6’s 1 Peter line — do not predicate *"He was"* on *"the precious blood of Christ…"*; quote KJV faithfully (*redeemed with* / *with the precious blood*) and attribute 1 Peter before the quote.
2. Earn the **blood-applied** reveal the way the **unbroken bone** is earned: add a attributed NT cross-ref for sheltering under Christ’s blood (and/or faith), and stop Beat 5 from fully resolving substitution (*"a life for a life"*) so the steel-man carries into Beat 6.
3. Cut or rework Beat 7 sag — drop the duplicate *"Christ our passover is sacrificed for us"*; add a new closing turn (or ~200 words of earned material elsewhere) so LONG hits runtime without repeating Beat 4’s application lesson.
