# Independent review — claude (OK, 142s)

I read the full narration closely and verified all fourteen scripture quotes against the KJV.

## KJV verbatim + attribution (EW-INV-4) — CLEAN
I checked every word inside `**"..."**`:
- Ex 12:12 "I will pass through the land of Egypt this night, and will smite all the firstborn in the land of Egypt" ✓
- Ex 12:3 "they shall take to them every man a lamb, according to the house of their fathers, a lamb for an house" ✓
- Ex 12:5 "Your lamb shall be without blemish, a male of the first year" ✓
- Ex 12:6 "the whole assembly of the congregation of Israel shall kill it in the evening" ✓
- Ex 12:22 hyssop/bason quote (incl. KJV spelling "bason") ✓ and "none of you shall go out at the door of his house until the morning" ✓
- Ex 12:13 "when I see the blood, I will pass over you…" ✓
- Ex 12:23 "when he seeth the blood upon the lintel…" ✓
- Ex 12:46 "neither shall ye break a bone thereof" ✓
- Ex 12:30 "there was not a house where there was not one dead" ✓

- John 19:36 "A bone of him shall not be broken" ✓
- 1 Cor 5:7 "Christ our passover is sacrificed for us" ✓

No altered words. **Attribution is also principled, not sloppy:** the two `**[the LORD]**` tags fall on first-person divine speech ("I will pass through…", "when I see the blood, I will pass over you"), while the impersonal law/command quotes ("ye shall take…", "the whole assembly… shall kill it") are introduced as "the command," "the word," "as we had been told." That's a clean divine-voice vs scripture-reader split that the multi-voice cast can honor. Not a defect.

## Reveal earned (EW-G7) — STRONG
The two puzzles are planted concretely in Beat 4 ("why must I put the blood on the outside?" and the guarded bone) and both pay off from lived detail in Beat 6 — the four-day spotless lamb → 1 Pet 1:19, the unbroken bone → John 19:33/36, the applied blood → 1 Cor 5:7. Crucially it avoids the banned "and at last I understood" announce: "I did not learn the answer in my lifetime… I know now what my trembling hands were truly doing." The post-mortem framing ("by a light that fell long after my night") earns the NT citations from a Bronze-Age father. This is the best beat in the piece.

## Wrestling (EW-G8) — GENUINE
"How could the blood of a lamb stand in for the life of my son? What was a pen animal to the God who made heaven and earth…?" is the real strongest objection (efficacy of substitution), in his own voice, and it *deepens* after deliverance ("was this small creature the end of it, or only a sign?"). Not a strawman.

## Doctrine (EW-G9) — SOUND
Substitution is explicitly named with penalty + what Christ bore: "the death I deserved fell on Him instead… The firstborn stood under sentence; the lamb died in his place. We all stand under sentence; the Lamb of God died in ours." Typology stays as shadow→fulfillment ("a shadow waits for the body that casts it"), not supersession. No invented reading.

## Voice / hook / spine — HOLD
Consistent characterful first-person ("I will not give you my name… Let me stand for them all"). One witness, one thread, no swap. First-hearing clear (slavery, nine plagues, the lamb all explained).

## Real weaknesses (polish, not structural)
1. **Mild redundancy / slight Beat-7 sag.** The "blood must be *applied*, not just shed" doctrine is fully landed at the end of Beat 6, then Beat 7 re-explains it again ("The blood had to be applied — struck on the door, claimed… It is not enough only to know that He died"). For a ~11-min read this is acceptable reinforcement, but the invitation restates rather than advancing. Tighten Beat 7's first half.
2. **"Not by merit" repeats three times** — Beat 3 "We were not saved by being good," Beat 5 "not that I was righteous," Beat 7 "not because you are good." Core gospel beat, but vary or thin one.
3. **CTA self-interest watch-point.** "the judgment that is real… will pass over *you*" is the closest the close comes to a self-interest/escape appeal. It's defensible because the judgment is the literal substance of the Passover the witness lived (not manufactured pressure) and it's immediately grace-reframed ("not because you are good, but because He is your Lamb") and relational ("trust the Lamb who was slain for you"). Keep, but make sure the read lands on *coming to Him*, not on *escaping*.

This is a faithful, well-built piece. The reveal and wrestling genuinely earn the runtime; the few nits are polish.

VERDICT: PASS
TOP FIXES:
1. Tighten Beat 7's opening — it re-explains the "blood must be applied" doctrine already fully landed at the end of Beat 6; let the invitation advance (toward coming to Him) rather than restate.
2. Thin the triple "not by merit" repetition (Beats 3/5/7) so the grace-anchor stays sharp instead of routine.
3. Soften the CTA's "the judgment… will pass over you" so the close rests on coming to / trusting the Lamb rather than on escaping judgment (avoid any self-interest read).
