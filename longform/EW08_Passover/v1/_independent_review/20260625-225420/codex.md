# Independent review — codex (OK, 133s)

KJV source checks: Exodus 12, John 19, 1 Peter 1:19, and 1 Corinthians 5:7. ([biblegateway.com](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Exodus+12&version=KJV)) ([biblegateway.com](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John+19&version=KJV)) ([biblegateway.com](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1+Peter+1%3A19&version=KJV)) ([biblegateway.com](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1+Corinthians+5%3A7&version=KJV))

Findings:

- Hard format failure: this is not a named biblical witness. The piece says, “I will not give you my name” and “Let me stand for them all.” That violates the premise of a named eyewitness and turns him into a composite narrator.

- KJV wording appears exact in the quoted snippets I checked, but attribution is not consistently compliant. Examples: “there was not a house where there was not one dead” has no source attribution before it; “the precious blood of Christ…” is introduced only as “the word written of Him says”; “A bone of him shall not be broken” and “Christ our passover is sacrificed for us” are not attributed to John/Paul/Scripture before quotation.

- The CTA names Jesus and gives a response, but it violates the “NO fear / gain-loss” rule. “the judgment that is real… will pass over you” and “be passed over” make the appeal land as escape-from-judgment pressure rather than a grace-anchored invitation.

- The reveal is partly earned through blood, blemish, Passover, and unbroken bones, but it also announces too much: “I know now what my trembling hands were truly doing” and “The command… was a shadow” are essay statements, not a discovery shown through the witness’s lived memory.

- Doctrinal overreach: “He was the spotless male of the first year I had held only four short days.” Christ fulfills the spotless lamb, but calling Jesus “the male of the first year” is an awkward literalizing of the type beyond the NT wording.

- “the death my son deserved had fallen on the lamb” is too blunt. The father may confess shared guilt, but the text has not earned “my son deserved” as an individual moral claim; it risks making the child’s death-sentence sound invented rather than tied to the plague/judgment.

- The strongest wrestling is not steel-manned. “How could the blood of a lamb stand in for the life of my son?” is real, but the harder objection is the Egyptian firstborns. The line “In every home without the blood, the firstborn lay dead” moves past that moral horror too quickly.

- Voice drifts from father to preacher-commentator. Phrases like “a shadow waits for the body that casts it,” “This is the substitution,” and “He is the true Passover” sound like doctrinal narration, not the character’s own first-person voice.

- First-hearing clarity weakens in the reveal/CTA. “applied,” “take His blood for your own,” “shelters under it,” and “stand under His blood” are insider metaphors that a zero-Bible listener may not know how to obey.

- Long-form sag: Beat 3, Beat 4, and Beat 7 all repeat the same “not enough that the lamb died / blood had to be applied” point. The repetition does not add a new turn each time.

VERDICT: FAIL
TOP FIXES:
1. Recast this through a named biblical witness, not an anonymous composite father.
2. Rewrite the CTA so it names coming to/receiving/trusting Christ by grace without fear-pressure.
3. Tighten the reveal into lived discovery and remove over-literalized or essay-like type language.
