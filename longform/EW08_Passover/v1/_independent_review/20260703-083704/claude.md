# Independent review — claude (OK, 119s)

I reviewed the artifact line by line, checking every quote against KJV and the eyewitness gates. The quotes themselves are clean — all 13 KJV excerpts are verbatim (including the correct KJV spelling "bason"), the reveal is genuinely earned by the two planted riddles, the wrestling is a real steel-man (it even anticipates Hebrews 10's "repeated feast" problem), substitution is named plainly, and the CTA is grace-anchored with a named response ("come home to Jesus," "hide behind Him"). But I found real problems.

## Findings

**1. Over-claim inside the reveal (worst problem).** Beat 6: *"Without blemish, without spot — the very words of my own command, now spoken of a Man."* They are NOT the very words of his command. Exodus 12:5 says only **"without blemish"** — "without spot" is 1 Peter 1:19's addition (and Numbers' language for other offerings). The witness claims Scripture said something it didn't, at the exact moment the piece stakes its typology on verbal precision. This is the corpus's known recurring failure (witness over-claims vs the source text — the EW01 Lev 10 pattern). Fix: *"Without blemish — the very word of my own command; without spot — and more."* or similar.

**2. Bone-command chronology over-claim.** Beat 4: the witness says the no-broken-bone command came *"when the LORD gave the law of this lamb"* and *"I obeyed it. I did not see why."* But Exodus 12:46 sits in the ordinance of 12:43–49, which the narrative places AFTER the departure (12:37–42), not in the first-night instructions (12:1–13, 21–23). A witness claiming he heard and obeyed it *that night* goes beyond the text. Soften: let him learn it as the feast's standing law ("the law God fixed over every Passover lamb after," cf. Numbers 9:12) rather than claiming first-night obedience — the riddle-payoff in Beat 6 survives intact.

**3. Anonymous composite witness breaks the format.** Beat 1: *"I will not give you my name… let me be every father of Israel."* The eyewitness format is a NAMED biblical witness. This is an intentional, well-executed everyman device — but it's a format deviation, and per the standing rules that's a user decision, not mine or the writer's. Surface it; don't ship silently.

**4. Misattributed-by-omission quote.** Beat 4's Exodus 12:23 quote (*"when he seeth the blood upon the lintel…"*) has no speaker frame. In the text these are Moses' words to the elders. Every other quote gets an attribution frame; this one should too ("Moses had told us" / "the word through Moses was").

**5. Small wording wobble, first line.** *"the night the LORD passed over Egypt"* — the LORD passed **through** Egypt (Ex 12:12) and passed **over** the blood-marked houses. Conflating them in line 1 slightly muddies the very distinction the piece teaches. Say "passed through Egypt" or "passed over Israel's doors."

**Minor / no action:** the speaking-from-beyond-the-grave device ("I tell you it now from the far side of my days") is a narrative liberty, but it's framed as an OT believer now seeing what he trusted in shadow (Heb 11:39–40 territory), not post-mortem second-chance salvation — acceptable. Runtime (~1,600 words ≈ 10 min) is on target; Beat 3 is long but every step carries a new quote, so it doesn't sag. "Four days he lived under my roof" (10th→14th day) is a fair inference.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Beat 6 — fix *"Without blemish, without spot — the very words of my own command"*: Exodus 12:5 says only "without blemish"; don't claim "without spot" was in his command.
2. Beat 4 — stop the witness claiming he received/obeyed the bone command that first night (Ex 12:46 is given after the departure); reframe it as the standing law of the feast (Num 9:12) whose riddle he carried.
3. Beat 4 — add a speaker frame to the Ex 12:23 quote (Moses' words to the elders); and put the anonymous-composite-witness format deviation (Beat 1) to the user for an explicit decision.
