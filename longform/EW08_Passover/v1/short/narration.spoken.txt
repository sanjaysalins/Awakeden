Death was coming for my firstborn son that night — and the only thing between him and the grave was blood on my door. I give you no name; I am every father in Israel. I took a spotless lamb, killed it, and struck its blood on the doorposts where only God would see. Not one of its bones we broke. A lamb died so my son would not — I was saved not by being good, but by hiding under the blood. For years it haunted me — why a spotless lamb, why a bone never broken, why blood God alone looked on? I tell you now, by a light that fell long after. There came another Lamb — a Man named Jesus — spotless, His bones unbroken, His blood poured out. My lamb was only His shadow. As the Scripture written after me says: Christ our passover is sacrificed for us Do not look to a painted door. Take His blood for your own, and hide under Jesus as my children hid behind that door — not because you are good, but because He is the Lamb slain for you. No one who hides there is ever turned away.
