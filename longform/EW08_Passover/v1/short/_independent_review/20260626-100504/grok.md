### EW-G7 — Reveal earned vs. announced

**Partial planting, still mostly asserted.**

Beat 3 seeds oddities (*outside* blood, unbroken bones). Beat 6 lists them back. That is better than a bare punchline.

But the Christ turn is still **told**, not **walked**:

- *"I tell you from the far side of my days, by a light that fell after"* — reflective signpost that **announces** later understanding; it does not dramatize how the night itself opened the door.
- *"There came another Lamb — a Man named Jesus"* — not from Exodus memory; imported from later revelation.
- *"My lamb was His shadow"* — label dropped after a parallel list. The bone/blood parallels are real typology, but *shadow* is still asserted, not demonstrated step-by-step from what he **saw that night**.
- The midnight payoff — *"A lamb had died in my son's place"* — lands substitution for **that night**. It does **not** bridge to Christ. Beat 6 jumps centuries via Paul.

The reveal earns **Passover substitution**. It does **not** earn **Christ** from the witness’s lived moment without the NT quote doing the work.

---

### EW-G8 — Wrestling (steel-man)

**Present but thin — doubt is raised and immediately closed.**

- *"But my hands shook — blood on a wooden door, can that turn death from my son?"* — fair objection, in his voice.
- *"Yet I dipped the hyssop, as we were told"* — obedience swallows the doubt in the same beat. No deepening at the midnight cry, no unresolved strangeness carried into Beat 6 from **fear**, only from *"strangeness."*

The steel-man is not a strawman, but it is **one sentence**, not the episode’s real tension. A first-time listener never hears why blood-on-wood should answer for a **firstborn** under plague judgment — only *can it turn death from my son?*

Missing Beat 2 (and 4–5) is fine for SHORT compression — but what got cut was **sustained** wrestling, not filler.

---

### EW-G9 — Doctrine

**Mostly orthodox Passover → Christ typology. Two slips:**

1. *"the innocent for the guilty"* (Beat 7) — **misframes the night.** Exodus 12 is **firstborn plague judgment**, not moral guilt of the boy. The son is not “guilty”; death was coming on firstborns regardless. Importing penal *guilty* language without the witness having owned personal guilt in his voice is sloppy substitution theology **in his mouth**.

2. **Christ’s bearing of penalty** is implied (blood, passover, shelter) but never **named** for the listener: what judgment fell, what Christ took. *"Christ our passover is sacrificed for us"* does heavy lifting; the witness never says in his own words that **death/judgment meant for us fell on Him** — only that his lamb died for his son.

No supersession. No contrarian reading. Substitution for the **night** is clear (*"A lamb had died in my son's place"*).

---

### EW-G10 — Voice / character

**Archetype reads as essayist in a costume.**

- *"No name — I stand for every father bent over his slain lamb"* — editorial framing, not lived memory.
- **Chronology break:** Beat 1 has him *"bent over his **slain** lamb"* before Beat 3 slaughters the lamb. Signals narrator shaping the story, not one man remembering one night in order.
- *"For the word that came after says"* — commentary voice, not an Israelite father.
- *"Hear me. Be still a moment"* — pulpit register; breaks first-person witness intimacy.
- *"Do not look to a **painted** door"* — he **struck** blood with hyssop (Beat 3). *Painted* is the narrator’s metaphor, not the witness’s act. Voice inconsistency.

### First-hearing clarity

**Improved in Beat 1** — *"death was coming for every firstborn"* and *"Only blood on my door could turn that death away"* are clear.

**Still muddy:**

- Opening: *"the night the LORD **passed over** Egypt"* — zero-Bible listener may hear “passed over” as “skipped Egypt,” not “moved through to judge.” The spare/skip sense only appears inside the LORD quote.
- *"painted door"* (Beat 7) — unexplained; contradicts the hyssop strike they just heard.
- *"hyssop"* — no grounding for a cold listener.

### Hook / spine / SHORT form

**Hook:** Strong — slave-father, firstborn inside, blood as only shelter.

**Spine:** ONE witness, ONE night, ONE thread. No swap.

**Tightness:** ~310–320 spoken words — inside 220–320, at the ceiling ✓. No second thread crowding ✓.

**Single moment:** The **Passover night** lands. The **Christ turn** does not land as witnessed moment; it lands as Beat 6 commentary. Compression kept theology, not dramatic steel.

VERDICT: REVISE

TOP FIXES:
1. **Fix Beat 1 chronology and voice** — remove *"bent over his slain lamb"* (lamb not slain yet); open with the boy inside / death coming, slaughter still ahead.
2. **Attribute every KJV block** — tag Beat 3’s command quote (Moses / the LORD) and Beat 6’s *"Christ our passover…"* (Paul / Scripture) **before** the bold text; drop vague *"as we were told"* / *"the word that came after says."*
3. **Earn Christ from one lived oddity through midnight** — let doubt or strangeness (outside blood, unbroken bone) **deepen** at *"A lamb had died in my son's place"* and **pay off** in Beat 6 without *"shadow"* / *"by a light that fell after"*; replace *"the innocent for the guilty"* with language true to firstborn plague judgment, then name what Christ bore.
