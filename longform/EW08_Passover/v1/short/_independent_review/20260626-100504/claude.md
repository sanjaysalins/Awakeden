# Independent review — claude (OK, 94s)

I read the narration closely against the eyewitness lens. It's a genuinely strong piece — but it's not clean. Specific findings:

## KJV verbatim + attribution (EW-INV-4) — PASS
All three quotes are exact KJV:
- **"strike it on the two side posts and on the upper door post"** — Exod 12:7, verbatim. ✓
- **"when I see the blood, I will pass over you, and the plague shall not be upon you to destroy you"** — Exod 12:13, verbatim, and explicitly tagged **[the LORD]**. ✓
- **"Christ our passover is sacrificed for us"** — 1 Cor 5:7, verbatim (a clean mid-sentence pickup after "For even"). ✓

Attribution is adequate throughout ("as we were told", "[the LORD]", "the word that came after says"). This is the strongest part of the piece — no elision, no misquote.

## The reveal is earned (EW-G7) — PASS, and it's the craft highlight
The three payoff details in Beat 6 ("a lamb without spot, blood God alone looked on, a bone never broken") are each genuinely *planted* in Beat 3: "a lamb without spot" → "seen only by the LORD" → "We broke not one of its bones." The turn is **shown**, not announced. Good.

## Doctrine — substitution under-named at the Christ level (EW-G9) — the real weakness
The Passover-level substitution is concrete ("A lamb had died in my son's place", "the innocent for the guilty"). But at the *Christ* level the substitution is **asserted, not landed**. Beat 6 establishes Jesus is the matching Lamb (spotless / unbroken / blood seen by the Father) — but never says **what Christ bore for whom**: that the firstborn's death is the judgment *I* deserve, and Christ took *that* in *my* place. The listener has to transfer it themselves. The gate wants the penalty + what Christ bore named explicitly; right now Beat 7's "make His blood your own" leans on inference.

## The wrestling is thin (EW-G8)
The witness's doubt — "blood on a wooden door, can that turn death from my son?" — is real and in-voice, and it pays off ("A lamb had died in my son's place"). But it's a *logistical* doubt (can this physical act work?), not the strongest objection a thinking listener raises at Passover: **why must an innocent die for the guilty — is that just?** As a steel-man it's serviceable, not the strongest steel.

## CTA (EW-INV-2) — borderline, watch the escape-framing
"hide behind Him as my children hid behind that door" runs close to self-interest / escape-from-judgment logic. It IS mitigated by the reframe "You were never fleeing the dark — you were coming home. Come, and rest in Him" — and the response is named (come / rest). I'd let it stand, but the dominant felt-logic should be *coming home*, not *saving your own skin*; keep an eye on it.

## Length — slightly over ceiling
By my count ≈324 spoken words (incl. quotes) vs the ~220–320 target. Marginal, but trim Beat 6's "I tell you from the far side of my days, by a light that fell after" (the hindsight device is already carried by "the word that came after").

## Cosmetic
Beat labels jump 1 → 3 → 6 → 7. That reads as a spine artifact, not a finished deliverable — renumber clean.

Minor: "Before dawn, death was coming" then "At midnight a cry rose" — the later precise "midnight" makes "Before dawn" loose; harmonize.
