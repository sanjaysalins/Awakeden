# Independent review — claude (OK, 69s)

I read it closely against the eyewitness invariants and gates. It's strong work — the hook grips, the three-detail typological mapping is the best thing here, and the CTA dodges the tired template. But there are real, specific gaps.

**KJV verbatim + attribution (EW-INV-4) — PASS.** `**"Christ our passover is sacrificed for us"**` is an exact contiguous excerpt of 1 Cor 5:7 ("For even Christ our passover is sacrificed for us:"). Attributed as "the Scripture written after me." Clean. The paraphrases ("Not one of its bones we broke," Ex 12:46) sit outside the quote marks, so they don't trip the verbatim rule.

**The reveal earned (EW-G7) — mostly earned, one soft seam.** The mapping is genuinely shown, not asserted: the three things the witness actually *lived* — spotless lamb / unbroken bone / blood — each land on Christ ("spotless, His bones unbroken, His blood poured out"). That's the right machinery. BUT "I tell you now, **by a light that fell long after**" is an *announced* understanding doing the hinge work — it's the one place the turn is told rather than shown. The mapping rescues it, but the phrase is vague (a zero-Bible listener doesn't know what "light" means — later revelation? a vision?).

**The wrestling (EW-G8) — WEAK. This is the main problem.** "For years it haunted me — why a spotless lamb, why a bone never broken, why blood God alone looked on?" That's *puzzlement about ritual details*, not the genuine strongest objection. The real steel-man a skeptic brings to Passover is: *how can an animal's blood save anyone — isn't smearing blood on a door just superstition? Why should God spare* me*?* The line "I was saved not by being good, but by hiding under the blood" gestures at the works-vs-grace objection but states it as a resolved conclusion — it's never wrestled. The honest doubt isn't internalized as doubt.

**Doctrine / substitution (EW-G9) — sound but the LISTENER'S penalty is never named.** Substitution is clear *for the son* ("A lamb died so my son would not"). No supersession ("My lamb was only His shadow" = typology, correct). But in the turn to the listener, the penalty *they* face is never spoken. "hide behind Jesus" — from *what*? The death/judgment the firstborn faced is the whole engine of the type, yet the application leaves the listener's own judgment-for-sin unstated. Name it so the substitution is complete on their side too.

**Minor accuracy flag:** "blood on my door **where only God would see**" / "blood **God alone looked on**." The blood was struck on the outer doorposts and lintel (Ex 12:7) — externally visible, called a "token" (Ex 12:13). "Only God would see" is a poetic liberty that's slightly off the text; the point is *God looks at the blood*, not that it was hidden.

**Witness identity:** the deliberate "I give you no name; I am every father in Israel" is defensible (Passover has no single named father), and the voice stays consistently first-person and characterful — but it does soften the format's "named witness" specificity. Acceptable, noting it.

**Length/spine:** ~220 spoken words — tight, ~70-75s, at the low end. One witness, one thread, no swap. Good.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. **Make the wrestling the real objection (EW-G8):** replace the "why these details" puzzlement with the genuine doubt a skeptic feels — *how can blood on a door save anyone; why should death pass over me?* — voiced honestly before the turn resolves it.
2. **Name the listener's penalty (EW-G9):** in Beat 4, say plainly what they're hiding *from* (the judgment/death their own sin earns), so the substitution is complete on their side, not only the son's.
3. **Tighten the reveal hinge (EW-G7):** replace the vague "by a light that fell long after" with something concrete (e.g. the later-written Scripture / the cross itself) so the turn is shown, not announced.
