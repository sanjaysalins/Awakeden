## TITLE
Isaiah 53: The Death Described 700 Years Before It Happened

## DESCRIPTION
Isaiah 53. Seven hundred years before a Roman nail was ever driven, a prophet in Jerusalem wrote down the death of a man he had never met (Isaiah 52:13-53:12): the wounds, the silence, the grave, the rich man in his death, and what the wounds were for.

"But he was wounded for our transgressions, he was bruised for our iniquities: the chastisement of our peace was upon him; and with his stripes we are healed" (Isaiah 53:5, KJV).

This deep-dive walks the whole fourth Servant Song, movement by movement: the report no one believed; the Servant exalted and then marred more than any man; the exchange at the chapter's centre, where every line is a transaction; the lamb silent before her shearers; the honest question thoughtful Jewish readers raise, whether the Servant is Israel, taken seriously and answered from the text itself; the moment in Acts 8 when a traveller reading this very chapter asks, "of whom speaketh the prophet this?" (Acts 8:34), and Philip "began at the same scripture, and preached unto him Jesus" (Acts 8:35); the line heavier than all the wounds: "it pleased the LORD to bruise him" (Isaiah 53:10); and the quiet turn toward morning, a grave that could not be the end of him.

The arm of the LORD is not a thing. It is a person, and he has your name in verse six: "All we like sheep have gone astray" (Isaiah 53:6, KJV). Already carried. Already taken away. Come as you are.

The report is true. The arm of the LORD has a name. Have you believed the report?
