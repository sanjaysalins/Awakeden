# Independent review — codex (OK, 26s)

Findings:

- **KJV quote passes EW-INV-4.** `Jesus calls:` correctly attributes the quoted words before the quote, and `**"Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest."**` matches KJV Matthew 11:28 exactly.

- **The reveal is mostly earned, but one turn is asserted too quickly.** `But I was only a shadow. There came a greater Brother.` is true enough typologically, but it announces the category before showing it. The stronger shown thread is already there: `sold`, `falsely condemned`, `raised to save`, `pardon`, `bread / blood`. Lead with that logic, not the abstract label.

- **The wrestling is present but slightly softened.** `had they died in the pit my brothers dug?` is a real Joseph question. But `I could — for God had meant their evil unto good` resolves the steel-man almost instantly. The hardest objection is not only “can I forgive?” but “was God absent or cruel during the years of betrayal, slavery, false accusation, and prison?”

- **Doctrine is sound and substitution is named.** `bearing the death our sin had earned, dying in the place of the sinners who hated Him` clearly names penalty and substitution. No supersession problem.

- **First-hearing clarity has one risk.** `You are the brother who sold Him` is rhetorically sharp, but a zero-Bible listener may not know how they personally “sold” Jesus. The prior line names `our sin`, but the final accusation could use one clearer bridge from Joseph’s brothers to the hearer’s sin.

- **Voice is decent but sometimes becomes sermon narrator.** `What I did with bread, He did with His own blood` is strong in Joseph’s mouth. `But I was only a shadow` and `There came a greater Brother` sound more like theological commentary than Joseph remembering and interpreting.

- **Length and spine pass.** One witness, one thread, one moment: brothers bowing before Joseph. It is tight enough for short form.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Earn the “greater Brother” reveal through Joseph’s lived details before naming the typology.
2. Strengthen the wrestling so Joseph’s hardest question about God’s hiddenness is not resolved too quickly.
3. Clarify `You are the brother who sold Him` so first-hearing listeners understand how their sin is in view.
