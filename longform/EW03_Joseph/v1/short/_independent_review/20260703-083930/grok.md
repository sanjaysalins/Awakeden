## Independent adversarial review — Joseph eyewitness (short)

### EW-INV-4 — KJV verbatim + attribution
**Pass on the one formal quote.** `**"Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest."**` matches KJV Matthew 11:28. `Jesus calls:` attributes before the quote.

**Minor gap:** no scripture-voice tag on the quote block (compare the good fixture’s `**[the Pharisees]**` pattern). Production issue, not a wording fail.

**Not flagged as quote violation:** `God had meant their evil unto good` is paraphrase, not in `**"..."**`. Close to Genesis 50:20, not presented as KJV.

---

### EW-G8 — Wrestling (still thin)
Wrestling exists now, but both doubts collapse in one line each:

1. `had they died in the pit my brothers dug?` → instantly answered by `Then He lifted me to Pharaoh's right hand`
2. `could I forgive what could not be undone?` → instantly answered by `I could — for God had meant their evil unto good`

The **steel-man is missing**: Joseph had **power** when they bowed. The real fight is not only “can I forgive?” but “**should I?**” — revenge, justice, whether their evil gets a pass, whether God was cruel or absent through slavery, false accusation, and prison. None of that is voiced. `I could — for God had meant their evil unto good` reads like a proof-text landing, not a wrestled-through conviction.

---

### EW-G7 — Reveal asserted, not earned
The Christ turn is **announced**, not drawn from what Joseph lived:

- `But I was only a shadow.` — typology label before the lived scene earns it
- `There came a greater Brother.` — asserted, not discovered

Beat 2 ends on Joseph’s forgiveness (`Every pit became a road that climbed`), then Beat 3 **jumps** to a NT checklist: `I was sold for twenty; Jesus was sold by Judas... for thirty` / `Falsely condemned, killed` / `the men who drove the nails`. That is **parallel exposition**, not something Joseph **saw** in the bowing moment.

**Missing earned hinge:** Genesis already gives Joseph the turn-in-hand — `God meant it unto good` at the reconciliation. The piece paraphrases that in Beat 2 but does **not** use it to **show** Christ (evil meant for salvation → greater Brother who turns betrayal into pardon). Instead it pivots to abstract shadow language and Judas arithmetic.

---

### EW-G10 — Voice breaks in Beat 3
Beat 1–2 sound like Joseph remembering. Beat 3 becomes sermon-in-costume:

- `Jesus was sold by Judas, one of His own twelve, for thirty`
- `the men who drove the nails`
- `Jesus calls:` + Matthew 11:28

Joseph did not live Judas, crucifixion, or that quote. Per EW-INV-11, cross-time reveal must be a **signposted reflective device** (“I did not know then…”). Here NT facts are dropped in first person with no signpost — the witness frame cracks.

`What I did with bread, He did with His own blood` is strong Joseph voice. `But I was only a shadow` is theological commentary.

---

### SHORT form — one moment does not land
Word count is fine (~260 words), but **time scope is not one moment**.

The hook alone spans **years**: sold → bowed at my feet. Beat 2 stacks a **mini-biography**: `Sold for twenty pieces of silver. Falsely accused. Forgotten in a dungeon.` → Pharaoh → `savior of the very brothers` → bowing → forgiveness.

For ~90s eyewitness short, that crowds the single scene that should carry the piece (brothers bowing / Joseph’s heart at their need). Sag is structural, not length.

---

### EW-INV-2 — CTA on Jesus (borderline)
Jesus is named. Grace is good: `Not when you're clean enough — now, as you are.`

**Problems:**
- Matthew invites **Come** (`Come unto me…`); the close invites **Trust** (`Trust the Brother who forgives you still`) — response not tied to the quoted invitation
- `Trust the Brother` is close to a bare trust template; it does not name **come / receive pardon / take the bread** from the scene just told
- `You are the brother who sold Him` is sharp but assumes guilt without walking from Joseph’s brothers → the listener’s sin

---

### EW-G9 — Doctrine (mostly sound; one soft spot)
**Pass on substitution:** `bearing the death our sin had earned, dying in the place of the sinners who hated Him` names penalty + substitute. No supersession, no contrarian reading.

**Soft spot:** `made me the savior of the very brothers who threw me away` — “savior” language for Joseph, right before the Christ parallel, risks flattening Christ’s unique work into “I forgave my brothers.” The shadow line tries to fix that but comes too late and too abstract.

---

### First-hearing clarity
- `But I was only a shadow` — typology jargon; zero-Bible listener won’t know what that means
- `twenty… thirty` — sounds like accounting without setup
- `You are the brother who sold Him` — who is “you”? Bridge from Joseph’s brothers to the hearer is thin

---

### Hook / spine
**Pass with caution.** `My own brothers sold me for silver` grips. One witness, one thread (betrayal → exaltation → forgiveness → Christ). Spine holds until Beat 3 swaps to NT exposition.

---

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Earn the Christ turn from the bowing/forgiveness moment (Gen 50:20 logic: evil meant for salvation → greater Brother) before labels like `only a shadow` / `greater Brother`; cut or defer the Judas/thirty/nails checklist.
2. Steel-man the forgiveness beat: Joseph with power asking whether they **deserve** bread/pardon and whether God was absent — let him wrestle, not resolve in `I could — for God had meant…`.
3. Tighten to **one witnessed scene** (brothers bowing / Joseph’s heart) and close with a grace-anchored **come/receive pardon** tied to bread/blood — not a generic `Trust the Brother`.
