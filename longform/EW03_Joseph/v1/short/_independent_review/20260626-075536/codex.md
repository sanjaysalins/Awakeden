# Independent review — codex (OK, 46s)

Concrete findings:

- **EW-INV-4 attribution issue:** `**"sold Joseph to the Ishmeelites for twenty pieces of silver"**` is KJV wording, but it is **Genesis narration**, not Joseph’s spoken words. The setup `my own blood set my price:` blurs attribution and may imply Joseph said it or that the brothers directly performed every action in the quoted clause. Attribute it as Scripture/narrator before quoting.

- **EW-G9 doctrinal gap:** `who carries the guilt my hands never could` is too vague for substitution. It names guilt, but not penalty or what Christ bore. The lens asks for substitution named: sin’s penalty, wrath/judgment/curse/death borne by Christ in the sinner’s place.

- **EW-G8 weak wrestling:** The strongest internal objection is underdeveloped. `When the power to destroy them lay in my fist, I did not raise the sword` gives a dramatic choice, but Joseph never voices the real moral pressure: why should traitors live? why should evil brothers receive bread? The steel-man doubt is mostly skipped.

- **EW-G7 reveal partly asserted:** `my life was a shadow of a greater Brother` announces the typology. Some links are shown afterward, especially `sold... for thirty` and `raised... to save`, but the reveal would be more earned if Joseph’s own witnessed moment of feeding/forgiving guilty brothers drove the turn, not the abstract label `shadow`.

- **First-hearing clarity risk:** `by a light given long after me` is insider phrasing. A listener with zero Bible knowledge may not understand whether Joseph is time-traveling, receiving revelation, or narrating after death.

- **Possible overclaim:** `He too saved the ones who slew Him` is true in one sense for some who repented, but phrased broadly it may sound like all who killed Jesus were saved automatically. Needs tighter gospel wording.

- **CTA is close but slightly familiar:** `Come to Jesus` and `Come to the Brother...` names the response and avoids the tired trust-template. But `You have wronged Him — we are the brothers who sold Him` leans accusatory before grace. It can work, but the close should land more clearly on grace through Christ’s substitution, not just analogy.

- **Short form:** Likely within range and mostly single-threaded. The Joseph moment holds, though `Betrayed for silver. Falsely condemned.` introduces “falsely condemned” without showing where in the witnessed moment it happened, so it feels like a second thread imported from Joseph’s wider life.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Fix KJV attribution for the Genesis narration quote and do not imply Joseph spoke it.
2. Name substitution clearly: the penalty for sin and what Christ bore in our place.
3. Strengthen Joseph’s internal wrestling so the forgiveness/reveal is earned, not merely announced.
