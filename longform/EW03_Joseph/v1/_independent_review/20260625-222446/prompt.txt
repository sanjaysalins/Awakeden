# Joseph — Awakeden eyewitness (long)

---
## Beat 1 — I was there

I am Joseph, the son of Jacob, the son of Isaac, the son of Abraham. And I stood at the right hand of the throne of Egypt, with a famine swallowing the earth and the bread of nations under my seal.

For thirteen years before that, I had been a slave and then a prisoner — sold by my own blood, dragged into a land that did not know my name, left to rot in a dungeon. And now princes bowed as my chariot passed.

But on that day, ten travel-worn men came begging me for grain, not knowing the ruler they bowed to. They were my brothers — the very brothers who had sold me, whose faces I had carried in the dark for half my life. And when I could hold it in no longer, I sent every Egyptian out of the room, and I wept aloud until the house of Pharaoh heard me, and I said the words they had dreaded for twenty years:

**"I am Joseph your brother, whom ye sold into Egypt."**

## Beat 2 — The world

Let me tell you who I was before the pit. I was the son of my father's old age, and he did not trouble to hide that he loved me best of all his sons.

**"Now Israel loved Joseph more than all his children, because he was the son of his old age: and he made him a coat of many colours."**

That long bright coat was a wound to my brothers every time I walked past them in it.

**"And when his brethren saw that their father loved him more than all his brethren, they hated him, and could not speak peaceably unto him."**

And I, a boy, made it worse. God gave me dreams — sheaves of grain bowing to my sheaf, the sun and the moon and eleven stars bowing down to me — and instead of holding my peace, I told them aloud to the very men those dreams said would one day kneel.

**"And his brethren said to him, Shalt thou indeed reign over us? or shalt thou indeed have dominion over us? And they hated him yet the more for his dreams, and for his words."**

I was seventeen, and did not yet know that a dream of rising can come true only by a long road down.

## Beat 3 — The act

Hear now what was done to me, for I lived every hour of it. My father sent me out to find my brothers in the field, and when they saw me coming in that coat, they seized me.

**"that they stript Joseph out of his coat, his coat of many colours that was on him"**

They tore the sign of my father's love off my back, threw me into a dry well, and sat down to eat their bread while I cried up to them from the bottom of it.

**"And they took him, and cast him into a pit: and the pit was empty, there was no water in it."**

Then a caravan came over the hills, bound for Egypt, and my brother Judah set a price on his own brother's head.

**[Judah]** **"What profit is it if we slay our brother, and conceal his blood? Come, and let us sell him to the Ishmeelites, and let not our hand be upon him; for he is our brother and our flesh."**

And so my own flesh and blood handed me over for the price of a common slave.

**"sold Joseph to the Ishmeelites for twenty pieces of silver"**

In Egypt it only grew darker. I served faithfully and was repaid with a lie — accused of the very evil I had refused, and thrown into prison for the crime of my own innocence. And yet, in that pit beneath a pit, I was not alone.

**"But the LORD was with Joseph, and shewed him mercy, and gave him favour in the sight of the keeper of the prison."**

## Beat 4 — The strange detail

Here is the thing I could never explain, the riddle I turned over and over in the dark for years.

Every time I was thrust downward, the LORD turned the low place into a road that climbed. The pit carried me to Egypt. The slander carried me to prison. The prison carried me to the foot of the throne. And in one morning, a forgotten Hebrew convict was lifted out of a cell and set at the side of Pharaoh himself.

**"Thou shalt be over my house, and according unto thy word shall all my people be ruled: only in the throne will I be greater than thou."**

**[Pharaoh]** **"See, I have set thee over all the land of Egypt."**

And there it was, the strangest thing of all: the boy's dream of his brothers bowing did come true — but only because the boy had first been hated, betrayed, sold, and buried. The road to the throne ran straight through the pit. I could see that it had happened, but not yet why it had to.

## Beat 5 — The wrestling

And I will be honest with you. There were long years when I did not feel like a man God was keeping, but a man God had forgotten.

I had done no wrong, and I was sold. I had done right, and I was imprisoned. And I lay in the dark and wrestled the bitter question that every betrayed man knows by heart: where was God when my own brothers ate while I screamed from a pit?

And then, hardest of all, the day came when the power was finally in my hand. Those same brothers stood trembling before me, starving, not knowing my face, and the sword of vengeance lay ready in my fist. One word from me, and I could have ended every one of them — and every wound I carried was screaming for that word. That was the heaviest hour of my life. Not the pit. The choice in the palace, with my betrayers on their knees and my grief insisting they deserved to die.

## Beat 6 — The reveal

I did not raise that sword. I broke, and I wept, and I forgave them, and told them the thing I had finally come to believe — that a hand far greater than my brothers' had been working through all their evil from the very start.

**"But as for you, ye thought evil against me; but God meant it unto good, to bring to pass, as it is this day, to save much people alive."**

I gave them bread, and kept them alive through the famine. But I tell you now, from the far end of my life — by a light given long after me, a word written after I was laid in my grave — that my whole life had been a shadow thrown ahead of Someone greater. I saved bodies from a famine of bread, but I could never reach the deeper hunger underneath it; I could keep my brothers breathing, yet I could not lift the guilt off a single soul.

There came a Man, Jesus, and everything that happened to me happened to Him — only truer, deeper, and forever. He came to His own people, and His own sold Him.

**"He came unto his own, and his own received him not."**

I was sold by my brothers for twenty pieces of silver; He was sold by one of His own twelve for thirty.

**"And said unto them, What will ye give me, and I will deliver him unto you? And they covenanted with him for thirty pieces of silver."**

He was falsely condemned, as I was, then brought lower than any pit I knew — down into death itself. And as I was raised from prison to the right hand of Pharaoh to keep one nation alive, God raised Him from the grave to the right hand of heaven to save the world.

**"Him hath God exalted with his right hand to be a Prince and a Saviour, for to give repentance to Israel, and forgiveness of sins."**

And here is the wonder that breaks me still: like me, He turned and saved the very ones who sold and slew Him. Over the men driving the nails through His hands, He prayed:

**"Father, forgive them; for they know not what they do."**

What I could only do with bread, He did with His own blood. I told my brothers their evil had been meant unto good; He took the worst evil ever done — His own murder — and made it the very price of our pardon, dying in the place of the sinners who hated Him.

**"But God commendeth his love toward us, in that, while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us."**

## Beat 7 — The invitation

So hear me, you who listen so far away and so long after. I said to my trembling brothers, fear not — for though they had sold me, I had been made their rescue and not their judge.

**"Fear not: for am I in the place of God?"**

Now hear that same word from a far greater Brother. You have wronged Him — every one of us has; we are the brothers who sold Him and turned our backs. But He is not waiting for you with a sword. He stands holding bread, His arms thrown wide, calling the very ones who betrayed Him to come home and be fed and forgiven.

Do not come to me; I am only the shadow. Come to Jesus, who is the substance. He has carried your guilt where my hands could never reach, and to every weary and heavy-laden soul He says:

**"Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest."**

Come to Him. Trust the Brother who was sold for you, who was raised for you, who forgives you still. Come — and be kept alive forever.
