# Independent review — codex (OK, 166s)

KJV spot-check: I found no altered words in the quoted KJV fragments. The failure is attribution consistency.

Findings:

- EW-INV-4 attribution misses: `**"Thou shalt be over my house..."**` is Pharaoh speaking, but only the next quote gets `[Pharaoh]`. The attribution must come before this quote too.
- More EW-INV-4 misses: `**"He came unto his own..."**`, `**"Him hath God exalted..."**`, and `**"But God commendeth..."**` have no source/speaker attribution before the quote. If the format demands attribution before every KJV quote, the Beat 2 narrator quotes also fail.
- `**"And said unto them, What will ye give me..."**` obscures attribution. The speaker is Judas, but the setup only says `one of His own twelve`; it should name Judas before the quoted words.
- The reveal is partly earned, but overclaims with `everything that happened to me happened to Him`. That is too broad; the piece should map selected parallels, not imply a one-for-one correspondence.
- The substitution is asserted more than shown: `What I could only do with bread, He did with His own blood` is strong rhetoric, but Joseph’s lived text does not itself show penal substitution. The NT line needs to carry that weight more explicitly.
- EW-G9 is close but incomplete: `dying in the place of the sinners who hated Him` names substitution, but the required penalty is not clearly named. `carried your guilt` is not quite the same as naming Christ bearing the penalty/judgment due to sin.
- Potential anti-Jewish overcompression: `He came to His own people, and His own sold Him` moves from John’s `received him not` to corporate “sold Him.” Later `we are the brothers` universalizes guilt, but this line still needs guardrails.
- The wrestling is vivid but simplified. `I did not raise that sword. I broke, and I wept, and I forgave them` skips the actual testing arc with the brothers, Benjamin, and Judah. That flattening makes Joseph’s forgiveness feel cleaner than the Genesis narrative.
- The strongest objection is raised but not fully answered: `I could see that it had happened, but not yet why it had to.` The reveal answers with typology, but not with a deeper wrestling over providence using evil without excusing evil.
- Voice weakens after `by a light given long after me`. From there, phrases like `Someone greater`, `the substance`, `deeper hunger`, and `price of our pardon` sound like a sermon narrator, not Joseph’s characterful first-person witness.
- First-hearing clarity issue: `one of His own twelve`, `right hand of heaven`, and `shadow... substance` assume church vocabulary. A zero-Bible listener can follow Joseph, but may lose footing in the Christ turn.
- Long-form sag: Beat 4 and Beat 6 repeat the same ladder: pit/prison/throne, hated/betrayed/sold/buried, raised to rule/save. The runtime would be better earned by dramatizing the brother-testing/reconciliation sequence instead of summarizing the same pattern twice.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Fix KJV attribution before every quote, especially Pharaoh/Judas/NT apostolic narration.
2. Strengthen the Christ reveal by showing precise Joseph-to-Christ parallels without overclaiming.
3. Name the penalty Christ bore and deepen Joseph’s real wrestling through the Genesis reconciliation arc.
