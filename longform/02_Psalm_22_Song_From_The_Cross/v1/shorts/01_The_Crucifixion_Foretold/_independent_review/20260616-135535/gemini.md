# Independent review — gemini (OK, 30s)

The script is very close, hitting the doctrinal marks and maintaining an excellent, tight focus on the garments prophecy. The KJV usage is exact. However, there is a glaring structural oversight in the narrative logic regarding the "reveal" of Jesus, and a minor clunkiness in how a quote is integrated. 

**Critique Findings:**

- **Clarity / Narrative Logic (The False Reveal):** 
  In the fourth beat, the narrator explicitly identifies the subject: *"soldiers dividing **Jesus'** clothes"*. 
  Two beats later, the landing attempts a dramatic reveal: *"The suffering man of Psalm twenty-two has a name: **Jesus**."* 
  This falls completely flat. You cannot dramatically reveal a name you already explicitly gave 15 seconds earlier. It makes the script feel disjointed, as if the final beat was written in isolation and forgot the preceding context. 

- **Clarity on first hearing (Clunky quote integration):**
  In the fourth beat: *"soldiers dividing Jesus' clothes, then casting lots for the seamless coat — 'that the scripture might be fulfilled'."* 
  Tacking the exact KJV fragment onto the end of the sentence with a dash reads like a textual footnote, not a spoken sentence. A voice actor will struggle to make that dash sound natural without a lead-in (e.g., "...seamless coat, recording 'that the scripture might be fulfilled'").

- **Landing does NEW work:** 
  Aside from the false reveal, the actual hook of the landing is strong: *"They rolled dice for the clothes off His back, never seeing what He was really doing — laying down His life to win you back."* This brilliantly contrasts the executioners' petty greed with Christ's cosmic grace. If you remove the redundant name reveal, this contrast shines perfectly.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Cut the false reveal in the final beat ("The suffering man of Psalm twenty-two has a name: Jesus"). Start the landing directly on the contrast: "As they rolled dice for the clothes off His back..."
2. Smooth the spoken integration of the KJV fragment in Beat 4 so it sounds like natural dialogue rather than a parenthetical citation (e.g., "...seamless coat, noting 'that the scripture might be fulfilled'").
3. Ensure the transition into the landing maintains the grace-anchored contrast without backtracking to re-identify the subject.
