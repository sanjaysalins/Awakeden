# Independent review — codex (OK, 23s)

Concrete findings:

- **Overstated doctrinal claim:** “David himself was never executed; he died an old man. He was describing someone else” is too flat. Psalm 22 can be read as David’s own lament with prophetic fulfillment in Christ. Say it *points beyond David* rather than implying David had no real first-person referent.
- **KJV quotations:** Psalm 22:18 appears exact KJV. “that the scripture might be fulfilled” also matches KJV wording from John 19:24 as a phrase, though it is a fragment.
- **Clarity issue:** “a song recorded exactly how a dying man’s clothes would be divided up” is gripping, but “song recorded” may sound odd on first hearing. “Psalm” or “prayer-song” would be clearer.
- **Weak landing:** “laying down His life to win you back” is orthodox in broad shape, but generic. It does not do much new work from the specific image of the garments/lots. The close should land the irony more sharply: the soldiers took what was beneath them while Christ was giving Himself for them.
- **No clear CTA:** The script ends devotionally, but not with a distinct invitation or turn. “win you back” gestures at response, but it is not as concrete as the prompt asks.
- **Hook:** Strong enough for first five seconds. “Ten centuries before the cross…” gives immediate tension and specificity.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Soften “David was describing someone else” into a typological/prophetic claim that does not erase David’s original lament.
2. Make the landing do fresh work from the garments/lots image instead of ending on generic “win you back” language.
3. Add a clearer grace-anchored invitation or response without fear, gain-loss, or works framing.
