## Independent adversarial review

### Doctrinal soundness

**Oversimplified David claim.** *"David wrote it in the first person — but David himself was never executed; he died an old man. He was describing someone else"* flattens Psalm 22 into a single referent. Evangelical typology holds that David’s lament can be his own *and* point beyond him to Christ. Stating he was *only* describing someone else risks sounding like invented supersession, not standard messianic reading.

**Lecture beat, not shown truth.** *"A thousand years early, God had it written down — so when it happened, you'd know the cross was no accident. It was the plan."* tells the listener the theological conclusion instead of letting the garment/lots image carry it. Per your charter, *"Notice the order…" / "This teaches us…"* style narration is a failure mode — and *"you'd know the cross was no accident. It was the plan"* is that same explanatory move.

**Soldiers’ spiritual ignorance is asserted, not text-grounded.** *"never seeing what He was really doing"* imposes inner blindness on the soldiers. John 19:24 records their actions and fulfillment; it does not say they failed to grasp Christ’s purpose. Small, but it is interpretive padding dressed as fact.

**Otherwise orthodox.** Psalm 22 → cross fulfillment → Christ laying down His life is sound. No contrarian readings, no broken bones/thirst landmines, no works-salvation framing in the body.

### Clarity on first hearing

**Hook phrasing is slightly opaque.** *"a song recorded exactly how a dying man's clothes would be divided up"* — a zero-Bible listener may not know what *"recorded"* means here (written down? sung? preserved how?). *"Psalm"* or *"prayer-song"* would land faster.

**David/“someone else” logic needs a beat.** The script says David died an old man, then immediately *"He was describing someone else: stripped, surrounded, his life poured out."* A cold listener may ask: how do we know it is execution, not metaphor? You never name cross, nails, or death explicitly before the Jesus reveal — the middle leans on assumed Psalm-22 literacy.

**John at the cross is clear.** *"soldiers dividing Jesus' clothes, then casting lots for the seamless coat"* is concrete and followable.

### Grace-anchored conviction

**Gain/self-interest tint on the close.** *"laying down His life to win you back"* frames the atonement as Christ *winning you* — a benefit delivered *to you*. That edges toward *"what you get"* framing, which your charter explicitly forbids (*"Do not sell 'what you get'"*).

**No invitation; Spirit convicts but script does not invite.** The piece ends declaratively. There is no grace-anchored turn onto the viewer — no *look*, *come*, *receive*, *see Him*, or even a question tied to the garments irony. Conviction is asserted, not opened.

**No fear/works pressure** — clean on those axes.

### Freshness = faithful depth

**Hook has real text-specific grip.** *"Ten centuries before the cross, a song recorded exactly how a dying man's clothes would be divided up"* is a strong, concrete entry.

**Body freshness is thin.** Garment-division fulfillment is one of the most circulated Psalm-22 proofs in apologetics/Sunday school. After the hook, the short does not surprise a Bible-aware listener with a lesser-known textual detail (e.g., seamless garment specificity in John, the *vesture* word, soldiers’ indifference vs. the psalm’s first-person *my*).
