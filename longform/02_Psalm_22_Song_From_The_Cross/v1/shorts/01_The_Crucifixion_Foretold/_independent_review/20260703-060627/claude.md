# Independent review — claude (OK, 72s)

**Findings — Psalm 22 short #1 ("The Crucifixion Foretold")**

**KJV check — clean.**
- "They part my garments among them, and cast lots upon my vesture." — exact KJV, Psalm 22:18. ✔
- "that the scripture might be fulfilled" — exact KJV, John 19:24. ✔

**Doctrine — sound, one soft spot.**
- The core claim (Psalm 22:18 fulfilled at the cross, John 19:23-24) is the plain NT reading. "The cross was no accident. It was the plan" = Acts 2:23. Solid.
- "David himself was never executed... He was describing someone else" — the apologetic is standard, but stated this flatly it implies the *whole* psalm has zero reference to David's own distress. Much of Psalm 22 is David's real anguish that God shaped as prophecy. One word ("in this line, he was describing someone else") keeps it honest without losing the punch. Minor — not an error, an over-flat claim.
- "died an old man" — 1 Chron 29:28. ✔

**The real problem — the landing declares but never invites.**
- The close is "laying down His life to win you back." That names Jesus, it's personal, it's grace-anchored — good. But it stops there. No invitation, no turn toward Him at all. The lens (and the locked model) is CTA-to-Jesus: "the Spirit convicts, the script invites." This script never invites. That's the one structural miss.

**Clarity nits.**
- "cast lots upon my vesture" is archaic; the narrator pre-explains it, good — but the *dice* explanation ("They rolled dice") only arrives in the last line. A zero-knowledge listener sits through "cast lots" twice before learning what it means. Move the plain-words gloss earlier (e.g. "cast lots — gambled for them").
- "the seamless coat" — its significance (why lots instead of tearing it) is never explained, so it's a dangling detail for a first-time hearer. Either give it 4 words ("too good to tear") or cut "seamless."
- "Ten centuries before" / "A thousand years later" / "A thousand years early" — the same fact three times in ~45 seconds. The third repetition is deliberate bookending; the second and third back-to-back feel padded in a 60s budget.

**Hook, spine, freshness — good.**
- Hook grips inside 5 seconds ("a song recorded exactly how a dying man's clothes would be divided up"). ✔
- One thread (the garments/lots detail) runs hook → verse → fulfillment → landing with no swap. ✔
- Fresh about the text, orthodox in the claim. ✔

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Add a real invitation to the landing — the close names Jesus but never invites the listener to Him; one grace-anchored line (not a stock "will you trust Him") completes the required CTA-to-Jesus.
2. Soften "He was describing someone else" to scope it to the line being quoted (e.g. "in this line he was describing someone else"), so it doesn't flatly deny David's own anguish in the rest of Psalm 22.
3. Explain "cast lots" in plain words at first use and either justify or drop "seamless" — right now the gloss ("rolled dice") arrives 20 seconds too late and the coat detail dangles.
