## Visual reading
The arc opens on the killing thirst itself — the parched, cracked-lipped face of a crucified man gasping two words — then steps back to David at his lamp, glimpsing a death not his own, and lays out the psalm's three dying images: strength dried to a potsherd, the tongue cleaving to the jaws, the body sinking into the dust of death. The proof beat brings the cross John records and holds David and Calvary a thousand years apart in one dusk. The conviction turns on the central irony — the Maker of every river and sea hanging parched with nothing in His hands while, behind Him, all the waters He made dim into shadow; the well of John 4 and the offer of living water sit beside Him. Two OT water-echoes the NT names (the smitten rock that was Christ, 1 Cor 10:4; the open invitation 'every one that thirsteth, come', Isaiah 55:1 / 44:3) carry the landing toward a weary soul drinking and never thirsting again, and the cut lands on the hero: the crucified Christ with living water drawn from His own pierced side — that water is Himself.

## Writer's own red-team notes
Jaded Viewer worries: (1) too many crucified-Christ frames could blur — answered by varying framing hard (close parched face / mid cry / low-angle full body / wide pierced-side hero) and giving each a distinct macro payload. (2) The potsherd and the tongue-cleaving close-ups risk being grim medical shots — kept reverent and held-as-state, not gore. (3) The Ps 69 landmine: I deliberately do NOT depict the vinegar sponge (its fulfilment formula belongs to Ps 69:21) — the cross scenes show thirst, not the sponge, so no visual implies a Ps 22 citation. (4) The water-echo scenes could be a Sunday-school water montage — anchored each to a real NT-named echo (the Rock = Christ, 1 Cor 10:4; the thirst-invitation Isaiah 55:1/44:3 behind John 7:37) so they read as study, not slideshow. (5) Rejected the obvious cliche auto-completes: a literal glowing chalice, a dripping faucet, a desert mirage, a single falling teardrop standing for the whole psalm.
