# Independent review — claude (OK, 66s)

Findings, specific to the text:

**KJV verbatim — clean.** I checked both quotes word-by-word:
- "…they shake the head, saying, He trusted on the LORD that he would deliver him: let him deliver him…" — exact KJV Psalm 22:7-8, elisions honestly marked with ellipses.
- "He trusted in God; let him deliver him now…" — exact KJV Matthew 27:43.
- The narrator framing is also factually right: the head-wagging is the passers-by (Matt 27:39) and the "He trusted in God" taunt is the chief priests/scribes/elders (27:41-43) — the script keeps those two straight instead of blending them. Good.

**Finding 1 — no CTA (charter violation).** The locked model is viral-hook + **CTA-to-Jesus**. The landing — "He could have come down in an instant — He chose to stay, with you in view." — is a contemplative close but contains **zero invitation**. Nothing asks the listener to respond to Jesus. That's the no-CTA "Attenborough" model the project explicitly rejected. It doesn't need a templated "come to Jesus" verb (corpus-diversity applies), but it needs *some* turn toward response — e.g. land the "deliver" wordplay on the listener: the deliverance He stayed for is offered to *you*, receive it.

**Finding 2 — "And He wasn't delivered" is doctrinally imprecise as stated.** Flat "He wasn't delivered" is contradicted by Psalm 22 itself ("he heard," v24) and Hebrews 5:7 ("was heard in that he feared") — He wasn't delivered **from the cross**, He was delivered **through it** in resurrection. A zero-Bible listener hears "God didn't deliver Him," full stop. One clause fixes it ("not delivered from the cross") and the resurrection is actually the strongest answer to the mockers' taunt — the script leaves that punch on the table.

**Finding 3 — "David, describing a mocked and dying man" flattens the psalm's voice.** Psalm 22 is first-person — David writes "all they that see **me**." The line makes it sound like David is narrating a third-party scene. Minor, but "David, writing as the mocked and dying one" (or similar) is both more accurate and *more* striking, since the first-person voice is exactly what makes the prophecy eerie.

**What works:** hook lands inside 5 seconds and is text-true; one thread (the "deliver" taunt) runs unswapped from hook to close; conviction is grace-anchored ("It was love… with you in view") with no fear or gain-loss framing; freshness is faithful depth (the taunt itself pre-recorded, not a contrarian reading); multi-voice split (david / mocker) matches house style.

**Clarity on first hearing:** the deliver/deliver pivot ("let him deliver him" → "staying was the only way to deliver you") is followable and is the freshest line in the piece — but it currently ends the argument instead of opening the door, which is Finding 1.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Add a real CTA-to-Jesus in the landing — turn "He chose to stay, with you in view" into an invitation to respond (non-templated verb; land the "deliver" wordplay on the listener).
2. Qualify "He wasn't delivered" → "wasn't delivered *from the cross*" (and ideally let the resurrection answer the taunt) so it doesn't read as "God didn't hear Him," contra Ps 22:24 / Heb 5:7.
3. Fix "David, describing a mocked and dying man" → reflect the psalm's first-person voice (David writes as the sufferer), which is both more accurate and a sharper hook-support.
