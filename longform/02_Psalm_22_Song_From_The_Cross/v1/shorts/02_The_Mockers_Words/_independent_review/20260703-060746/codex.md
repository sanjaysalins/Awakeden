# Independent review — codex (OK, 22s)

Findings:

- **KJV quote risk:** `"…they shake the head, saying, He trusted on the LORD that he would deliver him: let him deliver him…"` is materially KJV, but it is a stitched fragment across Psalm 22:7-8 with ellipses inside the quotation. If the standard is “every word inside quotation marks must be exact KJV,” the ellipses and excerpting make this vulnerable. Use the full KJV wording or label it clearly as an excerpt.

- **Doctrinal phrasing is slightly loose:** `"He stayed because staying was the only way to deliver you."` The claim is orthodox in intent, but “only way” needs care. Evangelical doctrine would ground this in the Father’s will, atonement, and Scripture’s fulfillment, not merely Jesus’ choice to remain physically on the cross.

- **Strong text-thread:** The hook, Psalm 22, Matthew 27, and landing all stay on one spine: mockers unknowingly echoing prophecy. No major thread swap.

- **Landing is emotionally familiar:** `"He chose to stay, with you in view."` is clear and grace-oriented, but it does not do much new work beyond the prior sentence `"He stayed because staying was the only way to deliver you."` It lands warmly, but repeats rather than deepens.

- **Clarity is good:** `"The same gesture. The same taunt"` is easy to follow for a zero-knowledge listener. The Psalm-to-cross connection is direct.

- **Hook grips:** `"At the cross they shook their heads and sneered the very taunt a psalm had recorded a thousand years before"` is strong and immediate.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Make the KJV quotations unquestionably exact, preferably full enough to avoid ellipsis ambiguity.
2. Tighten “only way to deliver you” with clearer atonement/obedience language.
3. Make the final line add a fresh turn instead of repeating “He stayed.”
