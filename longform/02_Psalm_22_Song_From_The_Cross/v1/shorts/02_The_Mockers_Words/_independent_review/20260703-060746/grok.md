## Independent adversarial review — *The Mockers' Own Words*

### Doctrinal soundness
**Mostly sound.** The voluntary-substitution claim is orthodox and well guarded: *"not because Jesus lacked the power to come down; He had every power"* denies impotence. The Ps 22 ↔ Matthew correspondence is standard fulfillment reading, and *"The same gesture. The same taunt"* correctly avoids overclaiming verbatim identity across *"on the LORD"* vs *"in God."*

**Watch:** *"And He wasn't delivered"* lands first as agreement with the mockers before the reversal. That is safer than the old *"They were right"* heresy, but on one hearing it can still sound like the narrator is saying God failed Him — the fix depends on the next clause arriving fast and clear.

### KJV verbatim
**Quoted blocks appear exact** for the words spoken (ellipses OK for excerpts):

- Ps 22:7–8 excerpt: *"they shake the head, saying, He trusted on the LORD that he would deliver him: let him deliver him"* — matches authoritative KJV (comma after *"head"* is correct; bible-api/cache omits it — not an artifact error).
- Matt 27:43 excerpt: *"He trusted in God; let him deliver him now"* — exact KJV opening.

**Unmarked echo:** *"Let him deliver him," they sneered* is narrator prose, not tagged KJV, but the phrase itself is verbatim — fine.

No invented wording inside the marked KJV quotes.

### Clarity on first hearing
**Friction for a zero-Bible listener:**

- *"Psalm twenty-two. David…"* and *"Matthew records"* assume Bible literacy. Who David is, and why Matthew matters, are never grounded.
- The *"deliver"* wordplay needs work: mockers mean *deliver him off the cross*; the turn means *deliver you* (salvation). A first-time listener may hear contradiction, not irony: *"He wasn't delivered… staying was the only way to deliver you."*
- *"A thousand years later"* repeats the hook’s *"a thousand years before"* — same beat twice, not new information.

### Grace-anchored conviction
**Pass on fear/gain-loss.** No *"what you lose,"* no works pressure. *"with you in view"* is personal but not manipulative.

Borderline: *"the only way to deliver you"* is benefit-framed, but it reads as substitution (gospel), not self-interest salesmanship.

### Freshness = faithful depth
**Strong in the middle.** The mockers unknowingly quoting prophecy is faithful depth, not clickbait. *"people who never saw what they were echoing"* is the freshest line.

**Weak at the close:** *"It was love. He could have come down… He chose to stay, with you in view"* is solid theology but familiar Passion-language — not a fresh textual surprise.

### One thread spine
**Held.** Hook → taunt recorded early → same taunt at cross → *deliver* pivot → love/voluntary staying. No thread-swap (good — v2’s Matt 27:42 detour is gone).

### Landing does new work
**FAIL on this lens.** The artifact **ends without a landing or CTA-to-Jesus.** Gospel Five-Beat requires conviction **and** a grace-anchored close that points to Christ. This stops at observation (*"with you in view"*) — no invitation, no new closing move, no named response to Jesus. For a 60s viral short, that is structurally incomplete.

### Hook (~5 seconds)
**Functional, not gripping.** *"At the cross they shook their heads and sneered the very taunt a psalm had recorded a thousand years before"* creates intrigue, but it opens mid-scene (cross-first) rather than a sharp textual hook. Compare the de-templated line *"written down, word for word, a millennium early"* — this version is slower and less specific.

---

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. **Add a landing/CTA-to-Jesus** — the script has no closing beat; it cannot ship as a gospel short without a grace-anchored invitation that lands on Christ (not just *"with you in view"*).
2. **Smooth the *"deliver"* wordplay** — rephrase so *"He wasn't delivered [off the cross]"* cannot be heard as doctrinal confusion before the salvation turn (or signpost the double meaning in plain words).
3. **Sharpen the hook or cut repetition** — either open on the textual shock (*word-for-word / millennium early*) or drop the second *"A thousand years later"* so the middle does new work instead of echoing the opener.
