## Independent adversarial review — *The Mockers' Own Words*

### Doctrinal soundness

**"And He wasn't delivered"** is the sharpest problem. On a first hearing, that line sounds like God failed Jesus — or that Psalm 22’s sufferer never gets vindicated. The script *means* “not rescued off the cross the way the mockers demanded,” but it never says that. A listener who knows Psalm 22 ends in deliverance will hear a contradiction; a listener who doesn’t may simply conclude Jesus lost.

**"He stayed because staying was the only way to deliver you"** is orthodox in intent (voluntary substitution), but **"the only way"** compresses too much. It reads as a human strategic choice, not grounded in the Father’s will, atonement, or resurrection vindication. Not heresy — but loose enough that a skeptical ear can push back.

**"He could have come down in an instant"** is inference, not text. Matthew’s mockers challenge Him to come down (27:40); Matthew 26:53 supports His power. Fair evangelical shorthand, but **"in an instant"** is rhetorical padding the Bible doesn’t give you.

The Psalm 22 ↔ cross correspondence itself is standard, undisputed typology — not clickbait, not a contested reading. Good.

### KJV verbatim

**Psalm block** — labeled `Psalm 22:7-8`:
> "…they shake the head, saying, He trusted on the LORD that he would deliver him: let him deliver him…"

- Stitches two verses with **leading/trailing ellipses inside the quote marks**. Under a strict “every word in quotes = exact KJV” rule, those ellipses are not KJV text.
- Omits **"seeing he delighted in him"** (22:8) with no indication that’s deliberate clipping of a single verse.
- The spoken words that *are* present match KJV (including **"saying,"** with comma — fine if your standard is 1769 KJV).

**Matthew block** — labeled `Matthew 27:43`:
> "He trusted in God; let him deliver him now…"

- Spoken portion is exact KJV; trailing ellipsis clips **"if he will have him: for he said, I am the Son of God."**

**Narrator echo in quotes:**
> "Let him deliver him," they sneered.

- Exact KJV phrase from 22:8 / 27:43. Clean.

### Clarity on first hearing

**Strong:** *"The same gesture. The same taunt — flung at Him by people who never saw what they were echoing."* Clear even with zero Bible background.

**Weak:** The **"deliver"** wordplay is doing all the structural work, but the hinge line **"He wasn't delivered"** forces the listener to hold two meanings of “deliver” at once without help. That’s a logic trick, not plain speech.

**"Matthew records both — the passers-by wagging their heads, and the religious leaders jeering"** then quotes **only** 27:43 (rulers). Passers-by get gesture narration, not their words (27:40). A careful listener may wonder why one group is quoted and the other isn’t — minor, but slightly asymmetric.

**"Psalm twenty-two. David, describing a mocked and dying man"** assumes the listener knows what a psalm is and who David is. Not fatal for your audience, but not zero-knowledge clean either.

---

### Grace-anchored conviction

No fear framing, no gain/loss (“trust Him or lose”), no works pitch. **"with you in view"** and **"It was love"** stay on grace/love, not leverage. Passes this lens.

---

### Freshness = faithful depth

**"people who never saw what they were echoing"** is the best line in the piece — surprising about the *text*, orthodox in claim. The mockers discredit Him while accidentally quoting prophecy. That’s real depth, not a hot take.

---

### One thread spine

Hook → Psalm taunt → cross echo → voluntary staying for “you” — single **"let him deliver him" / deliver** thread throughout. No swap to a different beat (e.g. “himself he cannot save”). Coherent.

---

### Landing does NEW work

**Fails structurally.** For a Gospel Five-Beat ~60s short, there is no explicit **CTA-to-Jesus** — no invitation to the Person, only an observation.

The last two narrator beats also **repeat** rather than deepen:
- *"He stayed because staying was the only way to deliver you."*
- *"He chose to stay, with you in view."*

Same claim twice: He voluntarily remained for your sake. The final line is warmer but doesn’t turn the screw or invite response. Constitution-wise: strong hook, soft landing.

---

### Hook (~first 5 seconds)

**"At the cross they shook their heads and sneered the very taunt a psalm had recorded a thousand years before."** — Immediate scene, time-gap intrigue, concrete gesture. Grips. Passes.

---

### Length / beat coverage

At ~130 spoken words, this reads like **~35–45 seconds**, not a full 60s Gospel Five-Beat arc (Hook → Point → Proof → Conviction → **Landing/CTA**). Conviction is present; landing/CTA is underdeveloped or absent.

---

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Qualify **"He wasn't delivered"** immediately (e.g. not from the cross *that hour* / not the rescue *they* meant) so it doesn’t contradict Psalm 22’s vindication or sound like God abandoned Him.
2. Add a **distinct landing beat with CTA-to-Jesus** — the close currently stops at observation (**"with you in view"**) without inviting the listener to Christ.
3. Tighten the final movement so the last line **does new work** (resurrection vindication, invitation, or a fresh turn on the taunt) instead of restating **"He stayed… to deliver you"** in different words.
