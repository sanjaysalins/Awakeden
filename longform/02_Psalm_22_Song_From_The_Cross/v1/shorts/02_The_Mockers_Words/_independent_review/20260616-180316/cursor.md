## Independent adversarial review — *The Mockers' Own Words* (Psalm 22 short #2)

---

### Doctrinal soundness

**Mostly sound**, but one line still carries the impotence echo the metadata says v3 fixed.

- **"And He wasn't delivered — not because Jesus lacked the power to come down"** — On first hearing, *"He wasn't delivered"* lands as agreement with the mockers' taunt before the denial arrives. The v1→v3 notes explicitly reject a close that *concedes* mockers' claims about inability; this doesn't concede doctrinally, but it **replays their frame for a beat**. A hostile ear still hears: *mockers said "let him deliver him" → he wasn't → oh, but he had power.* That's a clarity/heresy-adjacent stumble, not a full doctrinal break.
- **"He stayed because staying was the only way to deliver you"** — Orthodox voluntary substitution. Fine.
- **"At the cross they shook their heads and sneered the very taunt a psalm had recorded"** — Soft prophetic/implied-fulfillment tone. Metadata correctly guards against Matthew-style *"that the scripture might be fulfilled"* language, but spoken lines like **"recorded"** and **"never saw what they were echoing"** still nudge the listener toward *scripted prophecy* rather than *striking correspondence*. Not heresy, but the guard in the header is tighter than the spoken voice.

No invented doctrine, no contrarian reading. Predictive correspondence is legitimate.

---

### KJV verbatim

**Quoted spans appear correct** against the project's pinned 1769 corpus (comma before *saying* in Ps 22:7 is intentional per `pipeline/kjv_strict.py`, not a word change).

- **Ps 22:7–8 excerpt:** *"…they shake the head, saying, He trusted on the LORD that he would deliver him: let him deliver him…"* — Verbatim splice across the verse break; trailing clip of *"seeing he delighted in him"* is honestly marked.
- **Matt 27:43 excerpt:** *"He trusted in God; let him deliver him now…"* — Exact; honest truncation before *"if he will have him."*

**Watchpoint (not a misquote, but a honesty gap):**

- Spoken line **"The same gesture. The same taunt"** overstates textual sameness. Ps 22:8 is *"He trusted **on the LORD that he would deliver him**: let him deliver him"*; Matt 27:43 is *"He trusted **in God**; let him deliver him **now**."* Metadata admits the openings differ; the **spoken** line does not. Shared kernel = *"let him deliver him"*; calling the full utterances *"the same taunt"* is a paraphrase claim dressed as proof.

**Inline echo (not quoted KJV):** *"Let him deliver him," they sneered* — acceptable narrator echo of the shared phrase.

### Clarity on first hearing (zero Bible knowledge)

**Several friction points:**

1. **Hook fuses, then splits.** Line 1: *"At the cross **they** shook their heads and sneered…"* — one crowd. Line 46: *"**passers-by** wagging their heads, and the **religious leaders** jeering"* — two groups. A cold listener may not know why two groups matter or which one spoke the quoted line.
2. **"The very taunt"** (line 37) — names nothing. The taunt isn't heard until ~20+ seconds in. Hook promises a punchline it hasn't shown.
3. **"He wasn't delivered"** — ambiguous without the next clause: not rescued from the cross? Didn't save himself? God didn't deliver him? All three blur together for ~2 seconds.
4. **Psalm setup:** *"David, describing a mocked and dying man, even writes his tormentors' words"* — assumes David = author, psalm = predictive, verse 8 = mockers' speech. Standard evangelical reading, but dense for a listener with no framework.

### Grace-anchored conviction

**No fear/gain-loss/works framing.** Good.

- **"deliver you"** / **"with you in view"** — substitution framed as love, not transaction. No *"win you back"* (which v1 sibling short still uses).
- **Conviction beat is thin.** After proof (*"never saw what they were echoing"*), the script jumps straight to substitution mechanics. There's no explicit grace-conviction line (compare short #3's *"This was no cry of lost faith"*). The middle tells you *what happened*; it doesn't invite the Spirit's work—it **explains Jesus's choice**.

### Freshness = faithful depth

**Strong core idea:** mockers unknowingly recite Psalm 22's gesture + taunt. Faithful, surprising about the *text*, orthodox landing concept.

**Weakened by repetition:** The fresh insight is spent by line 52. Lines 55 and 58–58 both restate *He had power → He chose to stay → for you/love* without a new image or turn.

### One thread spine (hook → middle → CTA)

**"Deliver" thread holds** — Ps 22:8 → Matt 27:43 → landing wordplay. v3 correctly dropped Matt 27:42 (*"himself he cannot save"*) with no Psalm parallel. Good fix.

**Thread held; CTA missing** (see below).

### Landing does NEW work

**Partial pass, then stall.**

- **"They thought weakness pinned Him to that cross. It was love."** — New work. Concrete, thread-specific. Good.
- **"He could have come down in an instant — He chose to stay, with you in view."** — Mostly restates line 55 (*"He had every power… He stayed because staying was the only way to deliver you"*). Portability test: this could close several substitution shorts unchanged.

**Critical metadata drift:** DEPTH claims the landing includes **"That's Jesus — come to Him."** That line is **not in the spoken script**. The artifact ends at *"with you in view."* For a format labeled **CTA-to-Jesus / Gospel Five-Beat**, the v2 pilot in this repo explicitly closes **"Come to Him."** — this LOCKED v3 removed the invitation entirely while the header still claims it.

### Hook grips in ~5 seconds?

**Weak relative to series siblings.**

- Line 37 (~18 words): abstract setup — cross, heads, unnamed taunt, unnamed psalm, "a thousand years."
- Compare short #3 opener: *"The loneliest sentence Jesus ever spoke wasn't His own line — He was quoting."* — immediate tension + paradox.
- This hook **summarizes the thesis** instead of **forcing a scroll-stop**. A listener at 5s still doesn't know *what* was said or why it's shocking.

### Additional findings

- **Redundant closing beats** waste ~8–10 seconds in a 60s cap — same point twice weakens punch.
- **Voice plan:** Matt 27:43 is direct mockers' speech; narrator-only is permitted but flattens the scene (metadata flags this; still a missed dramaturgical lever, not a doctrine fail).
- **"Religious leaders"** — acceptable paraphrase of Matt 27:41's chief priests/scribes/elders; not a KJV issue (narrator prose).

### What the gauntlet likely under-weighted

The 5-CLI history focused on v1's *"They were right"* heresy echo and v2's Matt 27:42 thread-swap — both real, both fixed. What's still loose: **(a)** missing spoken CTA despite LOCK metadata, **(b)** *"He wasn't delivered"* first-hearing stumble, **(c)** hook abstraction, **(d)** landing repetition without invitation.

VERDICT: REVISE

TOP FIXES:
1. **Restore a spoken, thread-specific CTA** — metadata promises *"That's Jesus — come to Him"* but the script never says it; add a grace invitation tied to *deliver/stay* (e.g. the One who wouldn't come down to deliver you — come to Him), not a bare *"Will you trust Him?"*
2. **Rewrite the landing opener** — replace *"And He wasn't delivered"* with phrasing that never momentarily agrees with the mockers (e.g. lead with *"He wouldn't deliver Himself"* or *"They demanded a rescue He refused"*), then deny impotence in the same breath.
3. **Sharpen the hook and cut the duplicate close** — open on the taunt itself or the accidental echo (*"They sneered 'let him deliver him' — a line Psalm twenty-two had already written"*), and merge lines 55 and 58 into one landing beat so the final seconds do new work instead of restating power/stay/for-you.
