## Independent Review — Upload Kit: The Mockers' Own Words

### Finding 1 — CRITICAL: The Psalm 22 quote is not KJV Psalm 22 (UK-G2, confirmed and worse than flagged)

Every platform carries this as the Psalm 22:7-8 quote:

> "they shake the head, saying, He trusted on the LORD that he would deliver him: **let him deliver him**."

KJV Psalm 22:8 actually ends: **"seeing he delighted in him."**

The phrase "let him deliver him" is from **Matthew 27:43** ("let him deliver him now"), not from Psalm 22. The kit has grafted the Matthew taunt onto the end of the Psalm quote and presented the hybrid as verbatim KJV Psalm 22. This appears on YouTube Short, YouTube Long, Facebook — every platform that carries a scripture block. The gate flagged UK-G2 FAIL but didn't name the splice; this is it. A viewer checking their Bible will find the discrepancy immediately and it undermines the entire parallel argument the kit is built on.

Fix: Quote Psalm 22:8 as written ("seeing he delighted in him"), then present Matthew 27:43 separately as the fulfillment.

---

### Finding 2 — "exact taunt" / "exact words" overstates the case (TikTok, Instagram, YT Short)

- **TikTok:** "sneered the exact taunt Psalm 22 had recorded"
- **Instagram:** "sneered the exact taunt Psalm 22 had written"
- **YT Short description:** "the exact taunt — 'let him deliver him now' (v.43)"

The parallel is real and striking — but "exact" invites a precision test the kit currently fails (because the Psalm quote itself contains the Matthew words, not the other way around). Even after fixing Finding 1, the Psalm 22 / Matthew 27 correspondence is semantic/thematic, not a word-for-word Greek/Hebrew match. "Same taunt," "same gesture, same words," or "the taunt David had already recorded" are defensible. "Exact" as a standalone claim is not.

---

### Finding 3 — "David wasn't writing a song about himself" is a theological overclaim (YT Long description)

> "David wasn't writing a song about himself."

Psalm 22 is headed "A Psalm of David" and reflects real Davidic anguish — the abandoned cry, the bone-dislocation imagery, the scorning crowd. Standard evangelical hermeneutics reads it as simultaneously Davidic lament AND messianic prophecy, not as exclusively messianic. The flat "wasn't writing about himself" negation will attract a fair counter-comment from anyone who knows the psalm's heading and context. The red-team already flagged this; I confirm it.

Fix (already suggested by the red-team): "David was writing more than a personal lament — he recorded a scene he couldn't have witnessed."
