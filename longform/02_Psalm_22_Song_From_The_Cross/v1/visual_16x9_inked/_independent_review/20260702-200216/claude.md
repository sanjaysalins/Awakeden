# Independent review — claude (OK, 104s)

Independent adversarial review of the Psalm 22 long-form narration + on-screen text layer. Findings below, most serious first.

## Findings

**1. 🔴 Citation/text mismatch on the Hebrews red bar (#72, [302s]).**
The bar is tagged "RISEN CHRIST - Hebrews 2:12" but the text shown is **Psalm 22:22 KJV** ("…in the midst of the congregation will I praise thee"). KJV Hebrews 2:12 actually reads: "…**in the midst of the church will I sing praise unto thee**." So the screen attributes Psalm wording to a Hebrews reference — a verbatim-citation error, the exact class the panel exists to catch. Fix: tag it `PSALM 22 - v.22 (quoted of Christ in Hebrews 2:12)`, or show the true Hebrews 2:12 KJV text. (The narration's spoken claim "the New Testament puts this very line in the mouth of the risen Christ" is substantively fine — Hebrews 2:12 does quote this verse of Christ — but "this very line" is slightly loose given the KJV wording differs.)

**2. 🟡 "The forsaking fell on him so that it would never fall on you."**
Spoken to a general, unconditioned "you," this states the benefit as already secured for every listener regardless of response — it drifts toward universalism before the invitation lands. The CTA that follows ("come and join…") implies the condition, but this line doesn't carry it. One phrase fixes it: "…so that it need never fall on you" or "…on everyone who comes."

**3. 🟡 Partial verses shown as complete on red bars.**
The narration marks truncation with ellipses; the on-screen bars don't:
- Bar #18 (Ps 22:7) drops the trailing "**, saying**" and shows no ellipsis — presented as the full verse.
- Bars #27 (v.14), #29 (v.15, starts mid-verse), #31 (v.16, starts mid-verse) likewise show fragments with clean punctuation and no ellipsis.
Since these bars are explicitly "presented as Scripture," add leading/trailing ellipses so a fragment never reads as the whole verse. (The fragments themselves are word-perfect KJV — I checked every bar; #8, #17, #20, #36, #39, #79, #81, #85, #88, and both Matthew 27:46 bars are exact.)

**4. 🟢 Caption #52 hardens a hedge.** Narration says "**almost** two centuries before Jesus"; the caption says flat "two centuries before Jesus." The LXX Psalms dating is debated — keep the "almost."

**5. 🟢 "…almost a thousand years before that morning."** The piece opens at "the ninth hour" (~3 PM), then says "that morning." Defensible (he was nailed in the morning, Mark 15:25) but momentarily confusing on first hearing. "Before that day" is cleaner.

**6. 🟢 "When a first-century Jew quoted the opening line of a psalm, he was rarely reaching for just one line."** This incipit-citation claim is common in evangelical scholarship but not undisputed. The hedging ("rarely," "the most natural way to hear it") keeps it honest — acceptable as written, just noting it's the piece's one load-bearing extra-biblical claim.

## What holds up well

- **Hook**: "At the ninth hour, nailed to a Roman cross and unable to fill his lungs" — grips inside 5 seconds. ✓
- **The pierced/ka'ari concession** is the strongest section — it concedes the dispute cleanly, rebuilds on the convergence, then plays the LXX card. Fresh, honest, no clickbait. ✓
- **One spine** (cry → quotation → whose death? → forensic detail → objection → whole-psalm claim → the turn → "he hath done this"/"It is finished" → come) with no thread-swapping. ✓
- **Landing does new work**: "the congregation it ends in still has room for you. Come and join the ones who praise him" — tied to Ps 22:22's own image, not a generic close. ✓
- **Grace-anchored**: "You do not have to make yourself worth saving before you come" — no fear/gain-loss framing anywhere. ✓ (Finding 2 is the one soft spot.)
