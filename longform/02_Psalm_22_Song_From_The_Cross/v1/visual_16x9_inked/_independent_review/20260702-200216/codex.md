# Independent review — codex (OK, 137s)

**Findings**

- Hard KJV failure: `[302s] "RISEN CHRIST - Hebrews 2:12" - "I will declare thy name unto my brethren: in the midst of the congregation will I praise thee."` That is Psalm 22:22 wording, not Hebrews 2:12 KJV. Hebrews has “church” and “will I sing praise unto thee.” The displayed words are altered for the cited reference.

- Overclaim: `"He was placing himself as the I of Psalm twenty-two. Every line."` This flattens typology into a one-to-one claim. Psalm 22 is Davidic lament fulfilled in Christ; “Every line” invites avoidable problems and sounds more absolute than the text requires.

- The line `"This one is about me"` is too blunt. Jesus’ quotation does point hearers to Psalm 22, but this phrasing makes the whole interpretive argument sound like a direct self-label rather than a biblically argued fulfillment.

- Internal tension: `"The cry was real — on the cross he truly bore the forsaking..."` versus `"neither hath he hid his face from him; but when he cried unto him, he heard."` The script gestures at holding both together, but a first-time listener may hear contradiction: was he forsaken, or did God not hide his face? This needs clearer theological framing.

- Unqualified promise: `"The forsaking fell on him so that it would never fall on you."` Addressed to every listener without qualification, this can sound like automatic universal assurance. The gospel invitation should attach the promise to coming to Christ / belonging to him.

- Apologetic overheat: `"The song turns forensic. It stops sounding like poetry and starts sounding like a witness statement from a cross"` conflicts with the later concession: `"this is poetry."` It is poetry throughout. Calling it “forensic” chases force at the cost of accuracy.

- Historical/textual overconfidence: `"David himself never came near a death like the one he describes"` and `"written first-person, by a king who suffered none of it"` are too sweeping. David did suffer persecution, betrayal, danger, and public shame. The point should be that the psalm exceeds David, not that David “suffered none of it.”

- The pierced discussion is too neat: `"The reading was there, in Jewish hands, centuries before there was a cross to defend."` The Septuagint point is useful, but “centuries before there was a cross to defend” sounds like courtroom rhetoric. Also, dating “the Greek Old Testament” as a unit is more complicated than the script admits.

- Clever phrase likely unclear in audio: `"placing himself as the I of Psalm twenty-two."` On first hearing, “the I” is abstract and grammatical. “The speaker” would be clearer.

- The ending is stronger than a generic CTA, but `"Come and join the ones who praise him"` still needs sharper gospel clarity. Join how? By faith, repentance, receiving Christ? The grace note is there, but the response is under-specified.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Correct the Hebrews 2:12 / Psalm 22:22 KJV quotation and audit every Scripture tag.
2. Replace overclaimed apologetic language with precise typological fulfillment language.
3. Clarify the forsaken/heard tension and qualify the final promise as received in Christ.
