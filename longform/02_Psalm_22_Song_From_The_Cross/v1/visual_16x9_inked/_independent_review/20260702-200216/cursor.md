## Independent adversarial review — Psalm 22 motion-comic narration

### Doctrinal soundness

**1. “He hath done this” ↔ “It is finished” is presented as the same “note,” but the psalm’s grammar does not support that cleanly.**

- Narration: *“Look at the very last line David writes… ‘…he hath done this.’ A thousand years later… He receives the vinegar, and says: ‘It is finished.’ Done. He hath done this.”*
- In KJV Psalm 22:31 the full line is: *“They shall come, and shall declare **his** righteousness unto a people that shall be born, that **he hath done this**.”* The proximate “his/he” is God’s righteousness and God’s accomplished deed — not the crucified speaker saying “I am done.”
- Thematic echo is fine in evangelical typology; **verbal fulfillment is not.** A hostile listener will call this proof-texting dressed as precision.

**2. Real forsakenness on the cross is asserted without guarding Trinitarian nuance.**

- *“On the cross he truly bore the forsaking that our sin had earned, in our place.”* and *“The forsaking fell on him so that it would never fall on you.”*
- That is standard substitutionary language, but paired with Psalm 22:24 — *“neither hath he hid his face from him; but when he cried unto him, he heard”* — a careful listener may hear contradiction: forsaken **and** face-not-hidden, unless you explicitly time-stamp **cross-moment vs resurrection-outcome**. The script resolves it later (“how it ends… raised him up”) but the **tension is left sharp too long** for a first-hearing audience.

**3. “Safe in his own bed” is rhetorical, not Scripture.**

- *“By a king who died old and safe in his own bed.”*
- KJV gives “full of days” / “good old age” (1 Kings 2:10–11; 1 Chron. 29:28), not bed, not “safe.” Small, but under an adversarial lens it’s **invented color** on a contrast that should stay text-grounded.

**4. Whole-psalm citation via the opening line is stated too absolutely.**

- *“When a first-century Jew quoted the opening line of a psalm, he was rarely reaching for just one line. The most natural way to hear it was as a pointer to the whole song.”*
- Common in Christian reading (especially Psalm 22); **not** “most natural” as a flat historical claim. Jewish practice varied; some scholars treat the cry as anguish first, allusion second. Over-certainty weakens the “honest objection” tone you earn earlier.

**5. Soldiers “fulfilling one anyway” risks sounding like they had no agency.**

- *“The Roman soldiers gambling for his clothes were not following a script. They were fulfilling one anyway.”*
- Doctrinally salvageable (providence), but phrased without “in God’s purpose” it can sound like puppet-theology to a skeptical ear.

### Clarity on first hearing (zero Bible knowledge)

**6. Mid-section cognitive load is high.**

From *“So whose death is he describing?”* through the LXX/pierced digression (~89s–230s in captions), a new listener must track: David ≠ crucifixion victim → forensic details → scholarly dispute → convergence argument → Greek OT → back to cross. The **logic holds**, but the thread is easy to lose without visual scaffolding.

**7. “Witness statement from a cross that would not be invented for centuries” overcompresses time.**

Crucifixion as a Roman method postdates David; yes. *“Centuries early”* / *“would not be invented for centuries”* may sound like **Romans invented crucifixion** rather than **David’s world didn’t use that form of execution**. One confused listener mishears the claim.

**8. Poetry concession, then pierced returns — feels like a gotcha.**

- *“Take pierced off the table entirely, just for a moment.”* … later: *“The reading was already there: pierced”* and on-screen *“The reading was already there: pierced.”*
- Intellectually fair (convergence first, then LXX). Rhetorically, a skeptical listener hears: **concede dispute → smuggle dispute back in.**

**9. “A dead man declares nothing” is clear; “mere rescue from the edge of death?” may not be.**

- Zero-Bible listener may not know what “edge of death” rescue means in psalm genre vs resurrection. You answer it, but the **question lands faster than the answer.**

### Grace-anchored conviction

**Mostly strong.**

- *“You do not have to make yourself worth saving before you come.”*  
- *“Not what you must do, but with what he hath done.”*  
- *“The congregation… still has room for you.”*

**Minor pressure edge:**

- *“So it would never fall on you”* is relief-by-substitution, not works — but “fall on you” still images **divine abandonment as what you’d otherwise get**. Grace-anchored, but one notch toward **loss-avoidance** framing; could be softened to “borne for you” without “never fall on you.”

No gain/works preaching detected.

### Freshness = faithful depth

**Strengths:** David-life vs psalm-body mismatch; mockers’ exact words; honest pierced dispute; psalm **turn** (congregation/nations) as missed half — **faithful and not clickbait.**

**Weak spots:**

- Whole-psalm pointer and **he hath done this / It is finished** pairing lean on familiar evangelical moves presented as tighter than the text allows.
- *“Forensic”* / *“witness statement”* is fresh voice but blurs **lament poetry vs prophecy** even after the poetry objection — exactly where a hostile reader pounces.

**Single spine holds:** cry → Psalm 22 quotation → David cannot be describing himself → crucifixion-shaped details → Jesus claims the *I* → psalm turns to life/worship/nations → substitution → congregational invitation.

No thread swap. The LXX/pierced block is detour but returns to *“Back to the cross.”*

### Landing — does new work?

**Yes, mostly.** Closing ties invitation to **Psalm 22’s ending congregation** and **“he hath done this,”** not a generic “will you trust Him?”

**Deduction:** If the **22:31 / “It is finished”** link is shaky (see #1), part of the landing’s “new work” rests on the weakest exegetical peg.

### Hook (~first 5 seconds)

**Effective.**

- *“At the ninth hour, nailed to a Roman cross and unable to fill his lungs, Jesus of Nazareth gathered his strength and cried out into the dark.”*
- Visceral, immediate, stakes clear before the first quoted cry. Caption layer reinforces (0s NAILED, 4s CRIED OUT, 8s quote).

### Additional nitpicks

- **Hebrews 2:12** tagged “RISEN CHRIST” — defensible Christological use; Hebrews applies the line to Christ as pioneer of salvation, not only post-resurrection utterance. Tag slightly over-specifies.
- **David “never came near a death like the one he describes”** — David was hunted, mocked, in mortal danger (1 Sam.). The **crucifixion-shaped public execution** point stands; “never came near” is **too absolute** without “like this.”

VERDICT: REVISE  
TOP FIXES:  
1. **Reframe or soften the Psalm 22:31 / “It is finished” link** — either quote enough of v.31 to show “he hath done this” refers to **God’s accomplished righteousness**, or call it thematic completion rather than “the same note.”  
2. **Time-stamp the forsaken / face-not-hidden tension** — one explicit bridge: real abandonment **at the cross**, heard and vindicated **on the far side of death** (before v.24), so v.24 doesn’t sound like a contradiction.  
3. **Tone down certainty on whole-psalm citation** (“most natural way”) and trim “safe in his own bed” / “never came near” to strictly text-safe contrast (old age, buried, not executed) so the David mismatch argument stays bulletproof under skepticism.
