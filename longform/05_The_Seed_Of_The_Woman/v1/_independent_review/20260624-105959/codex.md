# Independent review — codex (OK, 41s)

Findings:

1. **KJV attribution/quotation problem:** The draft uses quoted fragments without verse attribution or ellipsis marking, e.g. **"Where art thou?"**, **"The serpent beguiled me, and I did eat."**, **"till thou return unto the ground."** These are KJV words, but the brief requires KJV-strict handling and per-verse attribution when quoting across Genesis 3:14–15. The narration reads smoothly, but auditability is weak.

2. **Potential overclaim in the hook:** **"The very first sermon of the gospel in the Bible is preached to a snake."** This is punchy, but it risks sounding like the serpent is the intended recipient of gospel grace. The locked spine says grace is announced *to the enemy* before the guilty pair are sentenced, not that the snake receives a sermon of gospel invitation.

3. **Too much confidence in “her seed” linkage:** **"it is a clue the New Testament reaches back and picks up"** followed by Galatians 4:4. That sounds stronger than the brief allows. Galatians 4:4 says Christ was “made of a woman,” but it does not clearly “reach back” to Genesis 3:15. Better to frame this as canonical resonance, not NT pickup.

4. **KJV wording creates a clarity snag:** **"And notice the deliverer is singular — it shall bruise the serpent's head — one who finishes the job."** The draft tries to move from KJV “it” to a singular personal deliverer, but on first hearing “it” sounds impersonal. This needs clearer explanation, or it feels like a logic jump.

5. **Objection section omits one locked objection:** The brief specifically requires: **"'The woman' is just Eve; 'her seed' need not imply abnormal paternity."** The draft does not really steel-man this. It says **"the word 'seed' is collective"** and **"the serpent here is only a serpent"**, but skips the Eve/ordinary-descendants objection.

6. **Doctrinal overstatement:** **"The head that was crushed stayed down."** This is rhetorically strong but the draft itself later says Satan’s final end is still future. As stated, it may flatten the already/not-yet tension the brief explicitly requires.

7. **Landing is partly generic:** **"You only have to come to the One... and trust Him."** The final paragraph does mirror the spine, but the actual CTA still lands close to the tired generic “trust Him” close the brief warned against. The stronger fresh landing is the enemy-hearing promise kept, not the familiar trust-language.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Fix the overclaims around “sermon to a snake” and “NT reaches back and picks up” so they stay canonical but not overstated.
2. Add the missing Eve/ordinary “her seed” objection and clarify the KJV “it” to singular deliverer move.
3. Sharpen the landing so it does new work beyond a generic “come and trust Him” CTA.
