# Independent review — codex (OK, 110s)

Not a pass. The core reading is broadly evangelical, but the narration overclaims in a few places and fails strict quotation discipline.

**Findings**

- **KJV verbatim problem:** I do not see a Scripture quote with a substituted KJV word, but strict verbatim is still broken by punctuation/quote handling. `"...every beast of the field."` in Genesis 3:14 ends with a period where KJV has a semicolon and continues. Same issue with `"...made under the law."` from Galatians 4:4, where KJV has a comma, and `"...that is, the devil."` from Hebrews 2:14, where KJV has a semicolon.

- **Non-KJV phrase inside quotation marks:** `says "fulfilled today."` is not KJV Scripture. If this script’s rule is “every word inside quotation marks must be exact KJV,” this violates the format even though it is hypothetical speech.

- **Overclaim / false phrasing:** `the very first words of that judgment are not aimed at the guilty couple at all. They are a promise` is inaccurate as written. The first words are `Because thou hast done this, thou art cursed...` The promise comes next. The stronger claim is that the first gospel promise comes before the judgments on Eve and Adam.

- **Potentially contested handling of Romans 16:20:** `the church does crush Satan, but only under the feet of the One who crushed him first` is a theological synthesis, not what the quoted line itself says. It is orthodox-leaning, but it needs to be framed as inference, not as the obvious meaning of the verse.

- **Confusing eschatology:** `his final end, in God's word, is still coming "shortly"` ties Romans 16:20 directly to Satan’s final end at Christ’s return. That is not self-evident, and “shortly” will confuse a first-time listener after two millennia.

- **Doctrinal clarity risk:** `He is... not waiting to sentence you` can sound like judgment is simply off the table. Evangelically, the better landing is that Christ comes as the promised rescue from judgment, not that sentencing is unreal or irrelevant.

- **Clickbait freshness tone:** `almost everyone reads straight past — and once you see it, you cannot unsee it` sounds like secret-insight marketing. The actual observation is good; the packaging risks sounding contrarian.

- **Thread swap in Movement 5:** `First... Second... Third... And a fourth...` turns the narration into an apologetics lecture. The spine shifts from “God seeks the hidden guilty through the promised seed” to “defending a canonical reading of Genesis 3:15.” For zero Bible knowledge, this is too much too late.

- **Weak first-five-seconds hook:** `Something has just gone terribly wrong in the garden` is clear but generic. The actual grip is later: God speaks the first gospel promise to the serpent before addressing Adam and Eve.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Fix strict KJV quotation discipline, including punctuation and non-Scripture double quotes.
2. Correct the overclaim that the first words of judgment “are a promise.”
3. Simplify Movement 5 so the narration keeps one clear gospel spine instead of detouring into four objections.
