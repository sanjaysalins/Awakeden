### M3 — hook precision wobbles (clarity + fidelity to locked spine)

The sentencing-order fix is real (**"When God begins to pronounce the sentence"** / **"before one word to Adam about thorns and dust"**). But M3 still overstates what the quoted text actually says:

> **"And the very first words of that judgment are not aimed at the guilty couple at all. They are a promise"**

You just quoted 3:14 ending in **"thou art cursed above all cattle, and above every beast of the field."** Those *are* judgment words aimed at the serpent — curse, not promise. The protoevangelium promise is 3:15 (**"And I will put enmity…"**), which you quote next. On first hearing, **"They are a promise"** reads as if the curse line itself is the promise. That blurs the locked hook (promise embedded *inside* the curse block) into “the curse is a promise,” which is sloppier than the spine allows.

Also: metadata thread line (not spoken, but shows residual drift):

> **"God's very first word of consequence is not a threat to the guilty couple"**

M1 already has God addressing the guilty couple (**"Where art thou?"**). The defensible hook is **sentencing order** (3:15 before 3:16–19), not “first consequence-word period.” Spoken M3 mostly gets this; the header still doesn’t.

### M4 — honesty, then overclaim

The honest paragraph is strong:

> **"No New Testament writer ever stops, quotes Genesis 3:15 word for word, and says 'fulfilled today.'"**

But the very next beat undercuts it:

> **"Page three planted a promise — and the rest of the Bible reads like its answer."**

That’s trajectory dressed as direct fulfillment. A skeptical listener will hear “the NT answers page three” — exactly the **explicit application/citation** claim the brief forbids. Keep the trajectory frame; lose the “reads like its answer” certainty.

Luke 1:35 linkage is brief-required, but the clipped quote + **"names what kind of child she carried"** sits close to the demoted virgin-birth-proof lane. You never cross into grammar-proof, but you don’t re-state the guardrail (**clue, not proof**) at the Luke beat the way you do at Gal 4:4.
