## Real flags (precision/clarity, not doctrine-breaking)

**1. M3 — the hook line contradicts the verse it just quoted (strongest flag).**
M3 quotes Gen **3:14** first — *"Because thou hast done this, thou art cursed above all cattle..."* — which is a **curse**, not a promise. Then immediately: *"And the very first words of that judgment are not aimed at the guilty couple at all. They are a promise."* But the very first words of that judgment, which you just read aloud, are the serpent's *curse*. The promise (3:15) comes a beat later, woven *into* the serpent-address. A hostile listener catches the seam: you said "the first words are a promise" right after quoting words that aren't. The airtight claim (and the one your locked spine actually rests on) is narrower: the first **grace/rescue** word is spoken into the serpent's curse, *before Eve and Adam are sentenced.* Tighten to that — e.g. "and woven into that curse, before one word of sentence falls on the man or the woman, comes the first promise of rescue." Don't let "the very first words... are a promise" stand next to the 3:14 quote.

**2. M6 — Col 2:15 is pinned to the wrong event.**
*"And the empty tomb proved it: 'And having spoiled principalities and powers, he made a shew of them openly, triumphing over them in it.'"* In context Col 2:14–15, the "it" Christ triumphs *in* is **the cross** ("nailing it to his cross... triumphing over them in it"), not the resurrection. Introducing it with "the empty tomb proved it" mismatches verse to event. Either reframe the lead-in away from the resurrection ("the triumph was made open and public") or, if you want the resurrection beat, anchor it on a resurrection text and keep Col 2:15 attached to the cross.

**3. Minor — "Page three planted a promise" (M4).**
Casual idiom; Genesis 3 isn't literally "page three" in any given Bible, and for a zero-Bible-knowledge listener it's a throwaway that can read as imprecise. Low stakes, but it's the one place the register slips into cute.

**4. Minor — "The heel that was bruised got up" (M6).**
Clever, but the metaphor breaks (a heel doesn't "get up"). It half-lands the resurrection. Consider "The One whose heel was bruised rose."
