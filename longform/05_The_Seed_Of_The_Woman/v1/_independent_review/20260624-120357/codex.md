# Independent review — codex (OK, 33s)

Findings:

1. **Hook overclaims “first word of consequence.”**  
   Phrase: “God's very first word of consequence is not a threat to the guilty couple…”  
   Before the serpent’s curse, God has already interrogated Adam and Eve. This is safer as “first word of sentencing” or “first word of curse,” not “first word of consequence.”

2. **Doctrinal risk: “the enemy’s own deadly blow.”**  
   Phrase: “taking the enemy's own deadly blow.”  
   This can sound like Satan inflicted the decisive wrath/death, when Movement 6 rightly says Christ bore “what we had earned.” Keep Satan’s strike subordinate to God’s judgment and Christ’s substitution.

3. **KJV issue: added modern casing inside quotation.**  
   Phrase: **"that old serpent, called the Devil, and Satan."**  
   KJV Revelation 12:9 has “called the **D**evil, and **S**atan,” but this is a fragment from a longer sentence. If KJV-strict includes punctuation/context, this needs verse attribution and exact casing/punctuation from the pinned corpus.

4. **KJV issue: Galatians 4:4 punctuation is altered.**  
   Phrase: **"But when the fulness of the time was come, God sent forth his Son, made of a woman, made under the law."**  
   KJV has the verse continuing; this fragment should either preserve the comma before the cut or mark the truncation. Same concern applies to other partial-verse quotations ending as complete sentences.

5. **Clarity problem: “Page three planted a promise.”**  
   Phrase: “Page three planted a promise…”  
   A zero-knowledge listener may not know this means Genesis 3. It also risks sounding clever rather than clear. Say “Genesis 3” or “the third chapter of Scripture.”

6. **Potential overstatement: “the church does crush Satan, but only under the feet of the One who crushed him first.”**  
   The claim is orthodox in substance, but Romans 16:20 says “under your feet shortly,” and the script adds a “crushed him first” formulation without supporting it cleanly from the quoted text. It should tie union with Christ to the church’s participation more explicitly.

7. **Landing partly drifts into generic appeal.**  
   Phrase: “So step out from behind the trees… Come out, and be found by Him.”  
   This is better than “will you trust Him,” but “behind the trees” leans on the hiding image more than the serpent-addressed promise. The landing should do more new work with the strange fact that the promise was spoken to the enemy before the sinners were sentenced.

8. **Possible contested phrase: “set his prisoners free.”**  
   Phrase: “a deliverer who would crush the serpent and set his prisoners free.”  
   Genesis 3 does not yet frame Adam and Eve as the serpent’s prisoners. The brief allowed bondage-to-death, but warned against over-claiming serpent dominion. This should be phrased through death/sin bondage, not serpent ownership.

9. **Freshness weakens in Movement 4.**  
   Phrase: “a clue that lights up when the New Testament tells how the Saviour finally came.”  
   This is acceptable, but familiar. The fresher locked insight is not “woman’s seed = virgin birth hint”; it is the rescue promise being spoken into the serpent’s curse. Movement 4 should stay more clearly subordinate so it does not feel like the spine swapped.

10. **Multi-voice constraint is only metadata, not reflected in narration.**  
   Phrase: “Multi-voice: **the_LORD** voices Gen 3:14–15.”  
   The artifact says this in status notes, but the actual narration does not tag or format the LORD’s direct speech for production. If this is the narration artifact, that constraint is not actually implemented in the spoken/production text.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Tighten all KJV fragments so quotation punctuation/cuts are exact and ellipses or partial-verse handling is explicit.
2. Remove or qualify wording that makes Satan’s blow sound like the decisive cause of Christ’s penal suffering.
3. Strengthen the landing so it mirrors the hook: the promise spoken to the enemy before the guilty are sentenced, now fulfilled in Christ.
