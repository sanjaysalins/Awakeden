## Movement 1 — The Picture

Something has just gone terribly wrong in the garden. The two people God made have believed a lie, eaten what He forbade, and now — for the first time in the world — they are hiding from Him.

**"And they heard the voice of the LORD God walking in the garden in the cool of the day: and Adam and his wife hid themselves from the presence of the LORD God amongst the trees of the garden."**

And God comes looking. Not with armies, not yet with judgment — with a question. **"Where art thou?"** Then the blame goes around the circle. The man blames the woman, and the woman blames the serpent: **"The serpent beguiled me, and I did eat."** Everything good is coming apart in real time. This is the moment the rest of the Bible exists to answer — and God's answer begins in the most unexpected place.

## Movement 2 — The Problem

And judgment has to fall. Eden cannot simply carry on as if nothing happened. Innocence is gone, death has entered, and the people made for God's presence are now afraid of it, crouched in the trees.

But underneath the fear is a deeper trouble. In trusting the serpent over their Maker, the man and the woman have taken the creature's word against the Creator's. And there is no human way back. You cannot un-eat the fruit, or argue your way back into the garden, or stop the death that has now entered the world. If rescue is going to come, it has to come from God's side of the breach — or it does not come at all.

## Movement 3 — The Strange Detail

Now here is the thing almost everyone reads straight past — and once you see it, you cannot unsee it.

When God begins to pronounce the sentence, He does not turn to Adam first. He does not turn to Eve first. He turns to the *serpent*. **"And the LORD God said unto the serpent, Because thou hast done this, thou art cursed above all cattle, and above every beast of the field."** And the very first words of that judgment are not aimed at the guilty couple at all. They are a promise — fired straight over the serpent's head:

**"And I will put enmity between thee and the woman, and between thy seed and her seed; it shall bruise thy head, and thou shalt bruise his heel."**

Let that land. Before God says one word to Eve about sorrow, before one word to Adam about thorns and dust — both of which come *after* this — the first promise of the gospel in the Bible is spoken into the enemy's curse: a deliverer is coming who will crush him.

## Movement 4 — The Centuries-Early Match

And the promise is strangely specific. It speaks of **"her seed"** — the woman's offspring. That is an unusual way to put it; across the rest of Scripture a line is most often traced through fathers. Here the hope hangs on the woman's seed. It is a clue, not a proof — but it is a clue that lights up when the New Testament tells how the Saviour finally came.

**"But when the fulness of the time was come, God sent forth his Son, made of a woman, made under the law."** *Made of a woman.* And the angel's word to Mary names what kind of child she carried: **"that holy thing which shall be born of thee shall be called the Son of God."**

Now be honest about what kind of match this is. No New Testament writer ever stops, quotes Genesis 3:15 word for word, and says "fulfilled today." This is not a citation; it is a trajectory — the whole story bending toward one point. What the New Testament *does* do is name the players. The serpent? **"that old serpent, called the Devil, and Satan."** The mission? **"For this purpose the Son of God was manifested, that he might destroy the works of the devil."** And the crushing itself? Paul writes to a church: **"And the God of peace shall bruise Satan under your feet shortly."** Page three planted a promise — and the rest of the Bible reads like its answer.

## Movement 5 — The Honest Objection

Now slow down, because there are real objections here, and they deserve a real answer — not a dodge.

First: *you are reading Jesus into a snake story.* And it is true — no verse quotes this line outright. Second: *the word "seed" is collective; it just means descendants — humanity in general beating back evil. Even Paul says Satan is crushed under "your feet," the church's, not one man's.* Third: *the serpent here is only a serpent; Genesis never says "Satan."* And a fourth, sharper still: *"her seed" is nothing remarkable — Scripture speaks of a woman's "seed" elsewhere too; this is just Eve and her ordinary children.* On the surface, every one of those is fair.

So here is the honest answer. The case does not stand on a single Hebrew word or one clever proof-text. It is true that "her seed" can simply mean descendants — which is exactly why we never rest the whole case on that phrase. The case rests on the trajectory of the whole canon — the same canon whose later writers call that serpent the Devil, say the Son was sent to destroy his works, and place a woman's child at the heart of the rescue. And yes — the church does crush Satan, but only under the feet of the One who crushed him first. You do not have to force any of this. You only have to follow the line that runs from Eden to the cross.

## Movement 6 — The Exchange

And here the promise turns out to cost far more than it first sounds. Look again at *how* the deliverer wins: **"it shall bruise thy head, and thou shalt bruise his heel."** Two wounds — but they are not equal. The serpent strikes the heel: real, agonizing, the kind of strike that can kill. The seed crushes the head: final.

That is the cross. The strike to the heel is Calvary — and it was no glancing blow. The Son of God hung there bearing what we had earned; the death that was our wages fell on Him. He stepped into the very judgment we deserved and took it: **"that through death he might destroy him that had the power of death, that is, the devil."**

Do you see the turn? The blow that looked like the serpent winning — the death of the Son — was the blow that crushed the serpent's head. And the empty tomb proved it: **"And having spoiled principalities and powers, he made a shew of them openly, triumphing over them in it."** The heel that was bruised got up. The head that was crushed will never rise to win. He is a beaten enemy already — though his final end, in God's word, is still coming "shortly": defeated at the cross, finished forever at the King's return.

## Movement 7 — The Invitation

So here is the oldest promise in the world, and it is still open.

Think about where it was spoken. Not over an altar, not from a mountain — into a curse, in the hearing of the enemy, while two guilty people stood waiting to be sentenced. Before there was a temple, a prophet, or a single drop of sacrificial blood, God had already named the future: a deliverer who would crush the serpent and set his prisoners free.

And the serpent still whispers his oldest lie — that God is against you, that your safest move is to stay hidden in the trees. But that promise has been kept. The deliverer came; the heel was struck on a cross; the serpent's head is broken — and his end is now only a matter of time.

So you do not have to climb your own way back into the garden. You do not have to get clean first, feel strong, or be sure your faith is large enough — the dying do not heal themselves, and they were never asked to. The promise was made in your enemy's hearing, long before you ever took his side. So step out from behind the trees: the One whose heel was struck to break the serpent's hold is not waiting to sentence you — He is the rescue God named from the very beginning. Come out, and be found by Him.
