## Hidden risks, false assumptions, single points of failure

### 1. Smuggles C while demoting C
Recommendation line:

> **"The first gospel: a Rescuer is coming — the woman's seed — and He will crush the serpent..."**

**“The first gospel”** is Candidate C’s entire lever (“The **first word of grace**… spoken **to the serpent**”). Yet the brief says C is **“better distilled into the spinoff shorts, not the spine of the long-form.”** That contradicts both itself and the slate headline **“The **first gospel promise** — big-picture spine of the whole Bible”** (`LONGFORM_TYPES_SHADOWS_SLATE.md` L18). You are fusing C into the tagline while officially excluding C.

### 2. Slate / spinoff funnel is inverted
Slate shorts for this topic (`LONGFORM_TYPES_SHADOWS_SLATE.md` L54):

- “the first gospel in the curse” → **C**
- “her seed” → **A**
- “heel vs head” → **B**
- “serpent-crusher promised in Eden” → **A+B**

If the long-form spine is A+B, **three of four spinoffs overlap the long**; only C stays distinct. Longs-first funnel logic (`/study` L19) normally makes the long the **broadest** frame (C: grace announced to the serpent before Adam/Eve are sentenced), with A and B as movements inside it — not the reverse.

### 3. “NT’s own explicit application” — overstated (M5 answer risk)
M5 answer claims **“the NT's own explicit application; the canonical trajectory.”**  
**False for Gen 3:15 specifically.** No cited verse quotes the protoevangelium. Bronze Serpent can say “Jesus Himself, naming that pole” (`narration.md` M4). This episode cannot. Entire long-form rests on typology + echo — one weak link for LF-G8 if M5 pretends explicit NT citation exists.

### 4. “No human father” from “her seed” — doctrinal overreach
Candidate A:

> “points centuries forward to a birth with **no human father**”

Gal 4:4 says **“made of a woman”** — incarnation, not virgin birth stated. Luke 1:35 supports virgin birth but is **not in Genesis’ semantic field**; Eve is “the woman.” Orthodox messianic reading is fine; jumping to **“no human father” as the grammar’s point** is eisegesis the brief asks the panel to flag, then does anyway. Safer: singular seed / messianic pronoun **“it”** (KJV) → one Deliverer, with virgin birth as **corroboration**, not deduction.

### 5. Rom 16:20 undercuts singular-hero M6
Brief uses Rom 16:20 for B (“bruise Satan”). KJV (`kjv_cache.json`):

> **“And the God of peace shall bruise Satan under your feet shortly.”**

**“Your feet”** = believers, corporate victory. That fights a clean M6 Exchange centered on **one** heel-wounded Rescuer unless M5/M6 address the Christ-vs-church tension. Brief ignores it.

### 6. M6 vs. LF-DOCTRINE-PENAL-CLEAR
Mapped M6:

> “the heel-wound **is** the head-crushing”

`LONGFORM_SPEC.md` / `narrate-long`: M6 must name **substitution penalty**, not victory metaphor alone. Heb 2:14 (“through death… destroy him that had the power of death”) helps, but the brief’s M6 is **combat imagery**, not **sin/death borne**. Risk of LF-DOCTRINE-PENAL-CLEAR FAIL at narration lock — especially after Bronze Serpent’s penal clarity (2 Cor 5:21, Gal 3:13 in M5–M6).

### 7. Serpent = Satan — assumed, not in the text
Rev 12:9 identifies the serpent as Satan **retroactively**. Genesis 3:14–15 addresses **the serpent** in its curse. Brief leans on Rev 12 / 1 John 3:8 without flagging that **identification is canonical theology, not exegesis of the Eden sentence itself**. M5 should steel-man: “This is just a snake curse; Satan isn’t named until much later.”
