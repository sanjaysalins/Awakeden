## Findings

**1. "Only here is it 'her seed'" is likely factually wrong — and it's the load-bearing claim.**
Candidate A's entire freshness rests on: *"Everywhere else… lineage is reckoned through the man… Only here is it 'her seed.'"* That overstates it. Scripture does attach "seed" to women elsewhere — e.g. Hagar (Gen 16:10, "I will multiply *thy* seed") and Rebekah (Gen 24:60, "let *thy* seed possess"). So the construct "a woman's seed" is **not unique to Gen 3:15**. If the hook (Movement 3) is "why *her* seed, when seed always runs through the man?", and that premise is demonstrably not airtight, the freshest beat is built on sand. This is the single biggest risk and it violates the locked principle "surprising about the *text*, never contrived."

**2. The verse's actual contrast is woman-vs-serpent, not woman-vs-man.**
The grammar in 3:15 sets "*thy* seed [serpent's] and *her* seed" in opposition — the foil is the **serpent's** seed, not Adam's line. Candidate A imports a woman-vs-man contrast the verse doesn't make, then derives "no human father" from it. That's the chain: *her seed → breaks man-lineage → virgin birth*. The middle link isn't in the text. Gal 4:4 / Luke 1:35 / Isa 7:14 are a legitimate **retrospective, NT-informed** reading — but the brief presents the virgin-birth inference as if it rises from Gen 3:15's own grammar. State it as canonical/typological, not grammatical, or M5's "you're reading Jesus into a snake story" objection lands cleanly and your answer doesn't refute it.

**3. The "singular pronoun" proof is weaker than the brief assumes.**
Movement 4 and the M5 answer both lean on *the singular pronoun ("it/he shall bruise")*. Two problems: (a) KJV reads **"it shall bruise"**, not "he" — write it as the text reads. (b) Hebrew *zera* is masculine and collective; a collective noun **naturally takes a singular pronoun**, so the singular alone does not individuate a single Messiah. The real strength is the NT's explicit application + canonical trajectory (which you have) — not the grammar. Don't let the steel-man rest its weight on the pronoun, or a competent panelist refutes it.

**4. The hook→landing mirror has a seam — this brushes the "never swap threads" lock.**
Locked principle: *one thread spine, mirrored hook → climax → close.* Your hook (M3) is a **grammatical puzzle** ("why her seed?"). Your climax (M6) is the **heel/head exchange** (the cross). The landing answers *how He wins*, not *why her seed* — so the question you opened on is never paid off at the close. A+B may be "front and back of one promise" at the sentence level, but at the *beat* level the hook and the landing are answering different questions. Either resolve the "her seed" question at the landing, or open on the exchange. As written, it reads like a soft thread-swap mid-piece.

**5. Candidate C is lower-risk than the brief admits — reconsider demoting it.**
C ("the first word of grace is spoken *to the serpent*, before Eve or Adam are sentenced") rests on the **plain narrative order** — unassailable, and genuinely fresh. It carries none of A's grammatical exposure. The brief's own goal is "freshest *AND* airtight"; on *airtight*, C beats A. You've surfaced this as question #1, which is right — but you under-weight that A's risk profile is the reason to lead with C (or to make C the hook and B the landing, with A as supporting evidence in M4 rather than the spine's entry point).

**6. Minor:** M2 "humanity now under the serpent's power" reads Heb 2:14's developed demonology back into Genesis 3 — defensible as bondage-to-death, but don't overclaim a settled "serpent's dominion" the Genesis text doesn't yet state.
