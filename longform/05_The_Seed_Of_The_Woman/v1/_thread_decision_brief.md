## The text (KJV, verbatim — fetched, not paraphrased)

**Genesis 3:14–15** — *"And the LORD God said unto the serpent, Because thou hast done this,
thou art cursed above all cattle, and above every beast of the field; upon thy belly shalt
thou go, and dust shalt thou eat all the days of thy life: And I will put enmity between
thee and the woman, and between thy seed and her seed; it shall bruise thy head, and thou
shalt bruise his heel."*

Spoken by God **to the serpent**, embedded in the serpent's curse — before God sentences
Eve (3:16) or Adam (3:17–19).

## The four candidate spines

### Candidate A — "Her seed" (original-language / overlooked-detail lever)
Everywhere else in Scripture, lineage is reckoned through the **man** ("the seed of Abraham,"
"the seed of David"; every genealogy is fathers begetting). Only here is it **"her seed"** —
the *woman's*. The grammar breaks the pattern, and it points centuries forward to a birth
with **no human father**.
- **Match:** Gal 4:4 "made of a woman"; Luke 1:35 (virgin birth); Isa 7:14 echo.
- **Freshness:** the grammatical anomaly itself reads as prophecy.

### Candidate B — "The wound that wins" (NT-confirmed echo / the exchange)
"It shall bruise thy head, and thou shalt bruise his heel." Same verb for both wounds, but
they are **not equal**. The serpent strikes the **heel** (painful, even deadly — the cross);
that very strike crushes his **head** (the decisive death-blow — the victory). The Rescuer
wins not in spite of the wound but **through** it.
- **Match:** Rom 16:20; Heb 2:14 ("through death... destroy him that had the power of
  death"); Col 2:15.
- **Freshness:** the cross reframed as the promised head-crushing.

### Candidate C — "The gospel preached to the serpent" (overlooked-detail lever)
The **first word of grace in the whole Bible** is not spoken to the guilty couple — it is
spoken **to the serpent**, buried inside his curse, **before** Eve or Adam are sentenced.
God's first gospel is a judgment on the enemy.
- **Match:** 1 John 3:8; Heb 2:14; Rev 12 (the whole NT war on the devil).
- **Freshness:** surprising about *who it is addressed to*.

### Candidate D — "Enmity as mercy" (overlooked-detail lever)
After the fall, humanity had effectively sided with the serpent (believed him over God).
God's first act of grace is to **break that alliance** — "I will put enmity." The hostility
between us and evil is a gift God installed; without it we would still be the serpent's
friends.
- **Match:** the new birth; James 4:4; regenerate hatred of sin.
- **Freshness:** enmity reframed as mercy.

## My recommendation (to be tested by the panel): A + B, fused into ONE spine

> **"The first gospel: a Rescuer is coming — the woman's seed — and He will crush the
> serpent by taking the serpent's own deadly blow."**

A and B are not two threads; they are the **front and back of one promise**:
- **How** God gets a sinless man into the world: *her* seed — born of a woman, not of a
  fallen father's line in the ordinary way (Gal 4:4 "made of a woman"; Luke 1:35 "called the
  Son of God").
- **What** that man does: crushes the serpent's head by absorbing the heel-wound (the cross).

Mapped to the 7-movement spine:
1. **The Picture** — Eden, the fall, the LORD walking in, the sentence begins.
2. **The Problem** — humanity now under the serpent's power; enmity, death, exile.
3. **The Strange Detail (hook)** — "*her* seed" — why the woman's, when seed always runs
   through the man?
4. **The Centuries-Early Match** — Gal 4:4 "made of a woman"; the singular pronoun ("it/he
   shall bruise"); Luke 1:35.
5. **The Honest Objection** — "you're reading Jesus into a snake story; Hebrew *zera* is just
   collective descendants; this is etiology for why people fear snakes." Steel-man, then
   answer (singular pronoun; the NT's own explicit application; the canonical trajectory).
6. **The Exchange** — the heel-wound **is** the head-crushing; Rom 16:20, Heb 2:14, Col 2:15.
7. **The Invitation** — come to the One whose bruised heel broke the serpent's head;
   grace-anchored, the Spirit convicts, the script invites.

C ("told to the serpent") and D ("enmity as mercy") are strong but, in my judgment, are
better distilled into the **spinoff shorts**, not the spine of the long-form.
