## Movement 1 — The Picture

Something has just gone terribly wrong in the garden. The two people God made have believed a lie, eaten what He forbade, and now — for the first time in the world — they are hiding from Him.

**"And they heard the voice of the LORD God walking in the garden in the cool of the day: and Adam and his wife hid themselves from the presence of the LORD God amongst the trees of the garden."**

And God comes looking. Not with armies, not yet with judgment — with a question. **"Where art thou?"** Then the blame goes around the circle. The man blames the woman, and the woman blames the serpent: **"The serpent beguiled me, and I did eat."** Everything good is coming apart in real time. This is the moment the rest of the Bible exists to answer — and God's answer begins in the most unexpected place.

## Movement 2 — The Problem

And judgment has to fall. Eden cannot simply carry on as if nothing happened. Innocence is gone, death has entered, and the people made for God's presence are now afraid of it, crouched in the trees.

But underneath the fear is a deeper trouble. In trusting the serpent over their Maker, the man and the woman have taken the creature's word against the Creator's. And there is no human way back. You cannot un-eat the fruit, or argue your way back into the garden, or stop the death that has now entered the world. If rescue is going to come, it has to come from God's side of the breach — or it does not come at all.

## Movement 3 — The Strange Detail

Now watch closely, because the key detail is one the eye slides right over.

When God begins to pronounce the sentence, He does not turn to Adam first. He does not turn to Eve first. He turns to the *serpent*. **"And the LORD God said unto the serpent, Because thou hast done this, thou art cursed above all cattle, and above every beast of the field."** That much is pure curse. But listen to what God weaves into it next — still speaking to the serpent, still before He has said a single word of sentence to the man or the woman:

**"And I will put enmity between thee and the woman, and between thy seed and her seed; it shall bruise thy head, and thou shalt bruise his heel."**

Let that land. Before God says a word to Eve about sorrow, before a word to Adam about thorns and dust — both of which come *after* this — the first promise of rescue in the Bible is spoken into the enemy's own curse: a deliverer is coming who will crush him.

## Movement 4 — The Centuries-Early Match

And the promise is strangely specific. It speaks of **"her seed"** — the woman's offspring. That is an unusual way to put it; across the rest of Scripture a line is most often traced through fathers. Here the hope hangs on the woman's seed. It is a clue, not a proof — but it is a clue that lights up when the New Testament tells how the Saviour finally came.

**"But when the fulness of the time was come, God sent forth his Son, made of a woman, made under the law."** *Made of a woman.* And the angel's word to Mary names what kind of child she carried: **"that holy thing which shall be born of thee shall be called the Son of God."**

Now be honest about what kind of match this is. No New Testament writer ever stops, quotes Genesis 3:15 word for word, and declares it fulfilled in so many words. This is not a citation; it is a trajectory — the whole story bending toward one point. What the New Testament *does* do is name the players. The serpent? **"that old serpent, called the Devil, and Satan."** The mission? **"For this purpose the Son of God was manifested, that he might destroy the works of the devil."** And the crushing itself? Paul writes to a church: **"And the God of peace shall bruise Satan under your feet shortly."** The opening pages of Scripture planted a promise — and everything that follows leans toward its keeping.

## Movement 5 — The Honest Objection

Now slow down, because a fair-minded skeptic has real objections here — and they deserve a real answer, not a dodge.

The biggest one: *you are simply reading Jesus into a snake story.* And it is partly fair — no verse quotes this line outright, and the word "seed" can just mean ordinary descendants. So we do not rest the case on that one phrase. We rest it on the shape of the whole canon — the same Scripture whose later writers are the ones who call that serpent the Devil, who say the Son came to destroy his works, and who put a woman's child at the heart of the rescue. The thread was not forced onto Genesis from outside; it was followed out from within.

And what of the line that says Satan is crushed **"under your feet"** — the church's feet, not one man's? Both are true. The church shares the victory, but only because it stands on the One who won it first. You do not have to force any of this. You only have to follow the line that runs from Eden to the cross.

## Movement 6 — The Exchange

And here the promise turns out to cost far more than it first sounds. Look again at *how* the deliverer wins: **"it shall bruise thy head, and thou shalt bruise his heel."** Two wounds — but they are not equal. The serpent strikes the heel: real, agonizing, the kind of strike that can kill. The seed crushes the head: final.

That is the cross. The strike to the heel is Calvary — and it was no glancing blow. The Son of God hung there bearing what we had earned; the death that was our wages fell on Him. He stepped into the very judgment we deserved and took it: **"that through death he might destroy him that had the power of death, that is, the devil."**

Do you see the turn? The blow that looked like the serpent winning — the death of the Son — was the blow that crushed his head. The victory was won *in* the cross itself, not in spite of it: **"And having spoiled principalities and powers, he made a shew of them openly, triumphing over them in it."** And the empty tomb three days later put it beyond all doubt — the One whose heel was struck rose. The serpent's head will never lift to win again. He is a beaten enemy already — though his final end, in God's word, is still coming "shortly": defeated at the cross, finished forever at the King's return.

## Movement 7 — The Invitation

So here is the oldest promise in the world, and it is still open.

Think about where it was spoken. Not over an altar, not from a mountain — into a curse, in the hearing of the enemy, while two guilty people stood waiting to be sentenced. Before there was a temple, a prophet, or a single drop of sacrificial blood, God had already named the future: a deliverer who would crush the serpent and free those he held by sin and death.

And the serpent still whispers his oldest lie — that God is against you, that your safest move is to stay hidden in the trees. But that promise has been kept. The deliverer came; the heel was struck on a cross; the serpent is a beaten enemy whose end is now only a matter of time.

So you do not have to climb your own way back into the garden. You do not have to get clean first, feel strong, or be sure your faith is large enough — the dying do not heal themselves, and they were never asked to. The promise was spoken in your enemy's hearing, long before you were ever born. So step out from behind the trees: the One whose heel was struck to break the serpent's hold did not come to sentence you — He is the rescue God named from the very beginning. Come out, and be found by Him.
