# Independent review — codex (OK, 31s)

Findings:

- EW-INV-4 attribution failure: `**"By faith Noah, being warned of God of things not seen as yet, moved with fear, prepared an ark to the saving of his house"**` is not attributed before quoting. “men who knew the whole story wrote down” is vague, and Hebrews is not identified.

- EW-INV-4 attribution failure: `**"Make thee an ark of gopher wood"**` follows “He gave me the very pattern,” but the speaker is not explicitly named right before the quote. Same problem with `**"the door of the ark shalt thou set in the side thereof"**`.

- EW-INV-4 format problem: `**[the LORD]** **"Come thou and all thy house into the ark..."**` attributes the speaker, but the bracketed attribution is awkward and not narration. The lens asks speaker attributed before quoting, not inserted as a tag.

- EW-INV-4 attribution failure: `**"and the LORD shut him in."**` is KJV text, but no speaker/source is attributed before the quote. Also it is narrative Scripture, not the LORD speaking, so attribution needs care.

- EW-INV-4 attribution failure: `**"And every living substance was destroyed which was upon the face of the ground"**` lacks source/speaker attribution before the quote.

- EW-INV-4 quote-fragment risk: `**"The like figure whereunto even baptism doth also now save us"**` is exact KJV wording, but the fragment is doctrinally hazardous without the immediate clarification: “not the putting away of the filth of the flesh, but the answer of a good conscience toward God.” As used, it can sound baptismal-regenerationist.

- EW-G9 doctrine concern: `**"The like figure whereunto even baptism doth also now save us"**` is quoted and then summarized only as “A figure. A likeness.” That does not guard the evangelical reading enough. The passage itself qualifies what baptism “save us” means; the artifact omits that guardrail.

- EW-G9 substitution is mostly present, but the penalty language is slightly generalized: `“The wrath that my sin had earned... broke over Him, and He bore it down into death”`. This names wrath and death, but “penalty” is not explicitly named. Stronger would be the penalty of sin/death borne by Christ.

- EW-G7 reveal partly asserted: `“The door. He is the door I cut into the side of the ark”` is a strong connection, but `“And the ark itself was Him”` jumps from door to whole ark with less textual earning. The piece shows “one door” well; the full “ark itself was Him” claim needs more careful grounding.

- EW-G8 wrestling is not the strongest objection. `“who am I, that I should float while they sink?”` is morally serious, but the harder objection is God’s judgment itself: how can the LORD be righteous and merciful while the world drowns? The current wrestling mostly protects Noah’s humility, not the scandal of judgment.

- First-hearing clarity issue: `“by the light that came long after I was laid in the ground, and by the word that was written when the body arrived that my ark of wood had only ever been the shadow of”` is dense and confusing for a zero-Bible listener. “the body arrived” is especially unclear.

- Voice fracture: `“when the body arrived that my ark of wood had only ever been the shadow of”` sounds like theological essay narration, not Noah’s first-person witness. Same with `“My ark was the shadow; He is the substance that cast it.”`

- Possible internal overclaim: `“the sign of a God whose heart is to save and not to drown”` risks flattening the flood judgment the entire piece has just narrated. God did drown the world in the story. Better to say His mercy keeps covenant after righteous judgment, not imply drowning was contrary to His heart.

- Long-form sag: Beat 4 and Beat 6 repeat the same theological point several times: `“I did not shut the door”`, `“The LORD shut me in”`, `“I did not shut my own door”`, `“you cannot shut yours”`, `“He is the One who holds the door.”` The motif is good, but the runtime is padded by restatement rather than fresh turn.

- CTA is mostly compliant and better than the tired template: `“Come to Jesus — the Ark, and the open door — and let Him be the refuge the storm breaks over in your place.”` It names Jesus and the response “Come.” But `“That same mercy is holding the door open to you while I speak”` adds time-pressure language. It is not fear-heavy, but it edges toward manufactured urgency.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Fix every KJV quote attribution and add doctrinal guardrails around the 1 Peter baptism quotation.
2. Strengthen the wrestling so Noah faces the hardest objection: righteous judgment and mercy amid mass death.
3. Cut repeated “door/shut in” restatements and make the ark-to-Christ reveal more shown, less asserted.
