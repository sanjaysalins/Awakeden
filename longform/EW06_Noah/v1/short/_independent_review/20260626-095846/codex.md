# Independent review — codex (OK, 31s)

Concrete findings:

- **EW-INV-4 attribution failure:** `**"and the LORD shut him in"**` is exact KJV wording from Genesis 7:16, but it is not attributed before quoting. The setup says only `a thing I did not do:`. For KJV quote discipline, it needs something like “Moses records…” or “Genesis says…” before the quote.

- **EW-INV-4 partial attribution weakness:** `God gave me one instruction...: **"the door of the ark shalt thou set in the side thereof"**` is exact KJV wording from Genesis 6:16 and God is the speaker in context, so this is basically sound. But the artifact should cite/attribute more cleanly because the quote is a fragment from a larger divine instruction.

- **EW-G8 wrestling is underpowered:** `And was I righteous enough to deserve the dry side of it?` is a real theological question, but it is not Noah’s strongest lived objection. The stronger steel-man would be the horror of being saved while the world perished, or the apparent narrowness of one door under divine judgment. This line turns quickly into “I did not save myself,” so the wrestling gets resolved before it bites.

- **EW-G7 reveal is mostly earned, but slightly announced:** `That door was a shadow of a Man to come — Jesus` is an asserted typological label. The better-earned part comes after: `One door. I never closed it. The LORD shut me in.` That is strong. But the sentence `That door was a shadow...` still feels like a Bible-study conclusion dropped into Noah’s mouth rather than the witness discovering the pattern from the moment.

- **EW-G9 doctrinal concern: “He took the flood” needs guardrails.** `But Christ did more than shelter me — He took the flood.` This is vivid and substitutionary, and the next sentence clarifies: `The judgment my sin had earned broke over Him`. Still, “took the flood” could blur the ark type if heard literally: in Genesis the ark bears Noah through judgment; Christ bears wrath in the sinner’s place. The substitution is named well, but the metaphor should stay precise.

- **Voice slips toward essay-narrator:** `by a light that came long after I was laid in the ground` and `That door was a shadow of a Man to come` sound more like a devotional narrator than Noah’s own first-person recollection. The opening voice is stronger: `I hammered a box of timber on dry ground...`

- **CTA-on-Jesus is close but uses a tired template-adjacent question:** `will you come in?` is better than “will you trust Him?”, and the response is named as `Come to Jesus`. But `Feel the cost` plus `the wrath that fell on Him so it would not fall on you` risks pressure through fear if not anchored more explicitly in grace. It is not a fail, but it is close to the line.

- **First-hearing clarity issue:** `the just for the unjust` is biblical language, but unexplained for a zero-Bible listener. The core meaning is clear from context, but that phrase may land as insider shorthand.

- **Short-form tightness:** It is within range and mostly single-threaded. The spine holds: one witness, one door, one shut-in moment, one Christ reveal. No major sag.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Add proper KJV attribution before `**"and the LORD shut him in"**` and cleanly attribute the Genesis 6:16 fragment.
2. Strengthen Noah’s real wrestling around the one door and judgment, not just personal worthiness.
3. Make the Christ reveal less announced and keep the “Christ took the flood” substitution language doctrinally precise.
