# Independent review — claude (OK, 55s)

Reviewing this as a hostile auditor. Findings, most serious first.

**KJV verbatim + attribution (EW-INV-4) — PASS.** The single quote `**"I am the door: by me if any man enter in, he shall be saved"**` is exact KJV (John 10:9, clean stop before "and shall go in and out"). Attributed: "A Man named Jesus said:" before the quote, plus the `[Jesus]` tag. No alteration, no misattribution. Clean.

**EW-G8 (the wrestling is the real steel-man) — FAIL. This is the biggest hole.** There is NO honest doubt internalized in Noah's voice. Beat 2's "I built the whole ark, but never closed that door. So I could never say I saved myself" is *reflection*, not wrestling — it's already the resolved answer, not a struggle. The genuine strongest objection a zero-faith listener brings to Noah is the **theodicy of the flood**: God drowned the entire world. The piece names it flatly — "the flood that drowned the world" — but never lets Noah wrestle with it. The hundred years of mockery is the other natural doubt ("did I waste my life? is God even real?") and it's used only as a hook flourish, never internalized as a question. The steel-man is absent.

**EW-G7 (reveal earned) — PASS, and it's the piece's strength.** The "one door — not a row, one" detail in Beat 2 is set up from the text Noah actually lived (Gen 6:16, the ark's single door), and the John 10:9 "I am the door" lands on it. The type→Christ turn is shown, not announced. Good.

**Doctrine / substitution (EW-G9) — PASS.** "The wrath my sin had earned did not pass over Him; it broke over Him, the just for the unjust" names the penalty (wrath earned by sin) and what Christ bore (that wrath), echoing 1 Pet 3:18. Typology is held as shadow→fulfillment ("my ark, only its shadow"), no supersession. The ark-through-water salvation echoes 1 Pet 3:20–21 faithfully.

**CTA (EW-INV-2) — PASS.** "Come in to Jesus, and be carried clean through" names Jesus and names the response (come/enter in). Grace-anchored — "nothing to build, nothing to bring." Not the tired "will you trust Him?" template. The "do not come to me — I could not even shut my own door" turn is genuinely fresh and humble. No fear/gain-loss manipulation.

**Pacing / sag (SHORT form) — REVISE.** Beat 3's prose paragraph carries nearly all the theological weight in one dense block (flood→wood→wrath→substitution→"come through alive"). For a punchy ~90s short this is the sag zone — three distinct ideas (ark saved through judgment / Christ did more / substitution) stacked with no breath. Either split it or cut the "But Christ did more" pivot tighter. Word count is in range (~220), so trimming the middle is free.

**Minor — voice (EW-G10) PASS but one vague line.** First person is consistent and characterful ("Long after I was laid in the ground" correctly places Noah as dead before Christ). But "a light fell that I never saw" is poetically vague — a first-hearing listener may not parse it as "the gospel came after my death." Sharpen or cut.

**First-hearing clarity — PASS.** The flood is common knowledge; door=Christ is plain. "the just for the unjust" is mildly churchy but survives.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:

Add the missing wrestling (EW-G8): give Noah a real internalized doubt in his own voice — the flood-theodicy ("a whole world drowned — was that just? and why was I spared?") or the hundred years of mockery as genuine question — and let the door/grace answer it, instead of skipping straight to the resolved "I could never say I saved myself."
2. Break up Beat 3's dense theology block so the middle doesn't sag; keep the substitution but give it room and one clean pivot, not three stacked ideas.
3. Sharpen the vague "a light fell that I never saw" so a Bible-blank listener understands the gospel arrived after Noah's death.
