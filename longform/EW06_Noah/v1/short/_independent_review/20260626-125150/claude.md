The only bracketed quote is `**"I am the door: by me if any man enter in, he shall be saved"**` — exact KJV John 10:9 up to "saved" (legitimate truncation, no doctored words). Attributed first: "A Man named Jesus said:". "the LORD shut him in" (Gen 7:16) is paraphrased in Noah's voice, not in quotes, so no verbatim obligation. No violation.

**CTA-on-Jesus (EW-INV-2):** ✅ Strong. Names Jesus, names the response ("Come in to Jesus, and be carried clean through"), grace-anchored ("nothing to build, nothing to bring"). **Doctrine + substitution (EW-G9):** ✅ Substitution explicitly named: "the wrath my sin had earned did not pass over Him; it broke over Him, the just for the unjust" (1 Pet 3:18). No supersession, no contrarian reading.

**Voice (EW-G10):** ✅ Consistent, characterful first-person Noah; the humility beat ("I could not even shut my own door") is in-character, not essay-narrator.

Now the problems:

1. **"The flood never struck my ark; the wood carried me safe *through* the judgment."** — This is imprecise and borderline self-contradictory in one breath. The flood *did* come upon the ark — the waters bore it up (Gen 7:17), and Scripture frames it as saved *through* water (1 Pet 3:20). "Never struck" then "through the judgment" fights itself. The intended contrast (the ark was carried *above* the judgment, Christ *absorbed* it) is sound, but the wording undersells it. A zero-Bible listener pictures a flood everywhere *except* the boat. Fix the phrasing to "the flood rose, but it could not break my ark" or similar.

2. **No internalized wrestling (EW-G8).** The only objection in the piece is *external* — "everyone laughed." Noah's own honest doubt is never voiced. For the punchy short form (Hook→strange→turn→punch) a full wrestling beat isn't mandated, so this isn't fatal — but the steel-man here is a strawman crowd, not Noah's real question ("a hundred years and no rain — was I a fool?"). One internalized doubt-line would sharpen it without bloating the runtime (it's only ~210 words, room to spare).

3. **"a hundred years"** is inference, not text — Genesis gives 100 years between Gen 5:32 (age 500) and 7:6 (age 600), and Gen 6:3 suggests 120. Defensible as tradition, but a hostile listener could ding it. Minor; consider softening to "for longer than most men live" or keep it knowing it's an inference.

First-hearing clarity is otherwise good; hook grips; one witness, one thread, no swap; tight at ~210 words.

2. Add one line of Noah's *internalized* doubt so the steel-man is his own honest question, not just the crowd's mockery (EW-G8).
