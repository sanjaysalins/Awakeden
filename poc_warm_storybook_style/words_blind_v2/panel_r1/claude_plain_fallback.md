## TITLE (at most 6 words)
The One Thing Not Taken

## WHO / TAKEAWAY / CURIOSITY GAP (one line each)
**Who:** a tired person who keeps everything running and feels nobody sees it.
**Takeaway:** the work gets used up, but time close to Jesus is the one thing that is never taken away, and He offered it to the tired worker too.
**Curiosity gap:** "Does anyone care?" A worn-out woman put that question to Jesus. What did He say back?

## WHAT A NEWCOMER TAKES HOME (one sentence, in a newcomer's own plain words)
"The dinner was gone by night, but being close to Jesus could never be taken away, and He wanted tired Martha to have that too, because He loved her."

## NARRATION (speaker tag before each part: NARRATOR: or JESUS: or MARTHA: ; then the word count)
NARRATOR: You do everything for everyone. Does anyone care? Martha asked Jesus that. She opened her home to Him and got lost in serving. Her sister Mary sat at His feet.
MARTHA: Lord, dost thou not care that my sister hath left me to serve alone?
NARRATOR: His answer? Her name. Twice.
JESUS: Martha, Martha, thou art careful and troubled about many things: But one thing is needful: and Mary hath chosen that good part, which shall not be taken away from her.
NARRATOR: Careful means full of worry. He did not call her work wrong. He named her worry. Her meal would be gone by night. Time close to Him, no one could take away. That place was open to Martha too. Did He care? The Bible answers: "Now Jesus loved Martha."

## WHY THE ENDING IS EARNED AND INSIGHTFUL (2 lines, name the concrete thing from the passage it pays off)
It pays off Jesus's own contrast, "many things" against "one thing… which shall not be taken away." We set that up with the meal she was making, and the payoff is plain: the meal is gone by night, but time with Him lasts.
The "Does anyone care?" hook is answered twice from inside the text: first by her name said twice, then by John 11:5, "Now Jesus loved Martha." The small discovery is that Jesus did not give the tired woman one more job. He offered her the same seat.

## WHAT YOU CUT AND WHY (2 lines)
I cut "bid her therefore that she help me." I also cut the slow bridges ("His first words?", "The one thing needed was not another job. It was Him.", "Mary chose to stay close…"), because Jesus's own words and the meal-versus-time line now do that work.
I cut "Martha, and her sister, and Lazarus," "in her own house," and the repeated "left to serve alone" ending. The quote now stops on the one word that answers the hook: Martha.

## RISKS (2 lines: what could be misread, by a newcomer or by a Bible reader)
A newcomer might hear "Her meal would be gone by night" as "cooking is pointless." The line "He did not call her work wrong" just before it is there to guard against that, so keep it.
A Bible reader may say "That place was open to Martha too" goes past the text. It is a fair reading of "one thing is needful" said to Martha, but it is our reading, not a quote. Also, "Now Jesus loved Martha" cuts John 11:5 mid-verse, so the on-screen reference should still show "John 11:5".
