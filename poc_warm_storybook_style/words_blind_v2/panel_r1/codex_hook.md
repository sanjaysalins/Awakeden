## TITLE

The Guest Who Gave

## WHO / TAKEAWAY / CURIOSITY GAP

WHO: The tired person who keeps everything running and feels unseen.  
TAKEAWAY: Jesus has something to give the person doing all the serving.  
CURIOSITY GAP: Does Jesus care when you are left doing everything?

## WHAT A NEWCOMER TAKES HOME

Jesus cares about the tired person serving Him, and His words are for her, too.

## NARRATION

NARRATOR: You do everything. Does anyone care? Martha asked Jesus that. She had welcomed Him into her home. While she served, her sister Mary sat at His feet, listening.

MARTHA: Lord, dost thou not care that my sister hath left me to serve alone? bid her therefore that she help me.

JESUS: Martha, Martha, thou art careful and troubled about many things: But one thing is needful: and Mary hath chosen that good part, which shall not be taken away from her.

NARRATOR: “Careful” means full of worry. Mary was hearing His words. That was what He protected. Martha had opened her home to Jesus. He was more than another person to look after. He had words to give her, too.

## WHY THE ENDING IS EARNED AND INSIGHTFUL

Martha’s open home sets up Jesus as the guest being served; Mary hearing His words establishes what He gives.  
The ending joins those concrete details: the tired host has welcomed someone who has something for her, too.

## WHAT YOU CUT AND WHY

Removed the John 11 quotation and family scene so the answer grows entirely from this encounter.  
Removed commentary around the repeated name and extra explanations so Jesus’s answer has room to land.

## RISKS

A newcomer could hear another duty to perform; keep the final emphasis on Jesus giving His words.  
A Bible reader could mistake Martha’s final seated pose for a recorded event; it is an imagined illustration, not Luke’s account of her response.
