## TITLE (at most 6 words)
The One Thing Jesus Wouldn't Take

## WHO / TAKEAWAY / CURIOSITY GAP (one line each)
**Who:** a tired person who keeps everything running and feels nobody notices.
**Takeaway:** Martha asked Jesus to pull Mary away from Him. He said no, and that same place close to Him was open to Martha too.
**Curiosity gap:** a worn-out woman asks Jesus "don't you care?" What does He say back?

## WHAT A NEWCOMER TAKES HOME (one sentence, in a newcomer's own plain words)
"Martha wanted Jesus to send her sister back to work, but He wouldn't let anyone pull Mary away from Him, and He wanted the tired one close too."

## NARRATION (speaker tag before each part: NARRATOR: or JESUS: or MARTHA: ; then the word count)
NARRATOR: You do everything for everyone. Does anyone even care? Martha asked Jesus that. She had opened her home to Him, and her sister Mary sat at His feet.
MARTHA: Lord, dost thou not care that my sister hath left me to serve alone? bid her therefore that she help me.
NARRATOR: His first words? Her name. Twice.
JESUS: Martha, Martha, thou art careful and troubled about many things:
NARRATOR: "Careful" means full of worry. He never called her work wrong. He named her worry.
JESUS: But one thing is needful: and Mary hath chosen that good part, which shall not be taken away from her.
NARRATOR: The one thing was Him. Martha asked Him to pull Mary away. He would not. And that place, close to Him, was open to Martha too.

## WHY THE ENDING IS EARNED AND INSIGHTFUL (2 lines, name the concrete thing from the passage it pays off)
It pays off Martha's own request, "bid her therefore that she help me" (asking Jesus to move Mary from His feet), against Jesus' last words, "shall not be taken away from her." He refuses the very thing she asked for.
The small discovery: Jesus says no to Martha's request but not to Martha. He protects Mary's place, and the "one thing is needful" is spoken to Martha, so that place is hers as well.

## WHAT YOU CUT AND WHY (2 lines)
I cut the John 11:5 "Jesus loved Martha" answer and the "right there in her own house" close. They were true, but they were a second ending bolted on. The answer now comes from inside the passage itself.
I also cut "pulled in every direction," "Mary chose to stay close… no one would take that," and the restated "did He care?" bridge, because each one said a line twice.

## RISKS (2 lines: what could be misread, by a newcomer or by a Bible reader)
A newcomer could hear "He would not" as Jesus turning Martha down coldly. The two-name opening and "open to Martha too" need a warm read, and the s12 hand-hold picture helps.
A Bible reader may note the text never says Martha sat down (s15 only shows it, the narration doesn't claim it). "Open to Martha too" is drawn from "one thing is needful" being spoken to her. It's a fair reading, but it is an inference.
