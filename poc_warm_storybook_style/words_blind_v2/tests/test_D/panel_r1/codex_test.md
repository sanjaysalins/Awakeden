**1. NEWCOMER**

WHO: Martha, an overwhelmed woman hosting Jesus, and her sister Mary.

WHAT: Martha asks Jesus to make Mary help; He answers by addressing Martha’s worry and defending Mary’s listening.

WHY: Feeling overwhelmed and unsupported does not mean Jesus does not love you.

Likely first-hearing obstacles:

- “Lord”: Jesus’ title and authority are unexplained.
- “Dost thou,” “hath,” “bid her therefore,” “thou art”: unfamiliar old English. Martha’s actual request for help could be lost.
- “Serve”: here it means doing the work of hosting.
- “At His feet”: the position is understandable; its meaning as a learner’s place may be missed.
- “Careful”: sounds like “cautious” until the explanation arrives.
- “Needful”: means necessary.
- “That good part”: unclear what “part” refers to.
- “Which shall not be taken away from her”: understandable words, uncertain meaning—what exactly is protected?
- “It was Him”: emotionally clear, but vague about what Martha actually needed.
- “Lazarus”: an unexplained new person introduced near the ending.
- “Later, the Bible says”: some viewers may hear a later change in Jesus’ feelings rather than evidence from another passage.

**2. BIBLE READER**

Yes—a small fresh observation: Jesus begins His correction with Martha’s name twice, and the related verse explicitly names Martha as loved.

**3. CURIOSITY GAP**

The opening asks: “Does Jesus care when I am exhausted and nobody helps?”

Mostly paid off: John’s statement answers whether He loves Martha. The film never fully addresses the tension that He does not grant her request for help. **8/10.**

**4. THE 5 QUESTIONS**

- **Who is this for?** Someone carrying everyone else’s needs and feeling unseen. The opening reaches that person immediately.
- **Main takeaway?** Jesus sees Martha’s worry and loves her; listening to Him has value beyond getting everything done.
- **Global audience OK?** Mostly. The narrator is accessible, but the longest, most important exchange uses English many newcomers will struggle to process.
- **Biblically accurate?** All direct quotations match the supplied KJV word for word: Martha’s speech, Jesus’ speech across the interruptions, and John 11:5. “Careful” is explained appropriately. “Tired” is an inference from her burden and distress. “The one thing needed … was Him” is an interpretation; the passage more directly shows Mary hearing His word. “No one would take that from her” expands the wording into a universal claim about other people. Lazarus’ family relationship is not established in the supplied verses. Martha is treated sympathetically, although the final line risks minimizing her complaint: she said she was **serving** alone, and already knew Jesus was present. There is no explicit promise of practical relief.
- **Best version possible—one change?** Replace the final line with an ending that connects Jesus’ love for Martha to the value He places on hearing His word. His physical presence alone does not resolve her stated problem.

**5. ENDING**

“Jesus loved Martha even when she felt left to do all the work.”

That is a real small discovery, supported especially by John’s explicit statement. The last sentence weakens it by treating being in the same house as the answer to unsupported work. **7/10.**

**6. TIGHTNESS**

“Twice” repeats what we have just heard, though it provides a deliberate emotional pause.

“Mary chose to stay close and hear Him, and no one would take that from her” repeats both the setup and Jesus’ words. Some paraphrase helps comprehension, but this section could be shorter.

The story itself is clear. What needs more space is the meaning of Jesus’ answer; the added name Lazarus needs less attention.

**7. OVERALL**

**7/10.** Warm, coherent, and sympathetic to Martha. The care question lands more convincingly than the final explanation of it.

**TOP 3 FIXES**

1. Replace the final physical-presence reassurance with an insight earned from Jesus’ response.
2. Ground “It was Him” more precisely in Mary hearing His word, and keep the promise’s wording close to the passage.
3. Make Martha’s request immediately understandable in plain narration; recover the time by trimming repeated explanation.

**Verdict: FIX.**
