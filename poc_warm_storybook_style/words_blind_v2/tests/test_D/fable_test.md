# Blind test viewer review — test_D (Fable seat)

## 1. NEWCOMER (no Bible knowledge, English as a second language)

- WHO: A tired woman called Martha, who is doing all the work while Jesus is a guest in her home.
- WHAT: She asks Jesus if He even cares that her sister left her to work alone; He answers by saying her name twice, names her worry, and says her sister chose the one thing that is needed.
- WHY: It says the person who feels alone in the work is seen and loved, and that Jesus was with her the whole time.

Words or lines a newcomer would NOT understand on first hearing:

- "dost thou not care" — old English; the meaning is saved only because the narrator asked the same question in plain words just before.
- "hath left me"
- "bid her therefore that she help me" — "bid" is the hardest word in the film and is never explained. A newcomer may miss that Martha is asking Jesus to ORDER Mary to help.
- "thou art careful and troubled" — "careful" is explained after (good). "troubled" is not, but it is guessable.
- "needful"
- "that good part" — part of what? Unclear until the narrator explains.
- "sat at His feet" — may sound strange or literal; "and listened" rescues it.
- "Lazarus" — a new name with no introduction, three lines from the end. "About this family" helps, but a newcomer will still ask "who?".
- "Lord" and "Jesus" themselves are not introduced (fine for this channel, but a true newcomer gets no hint of who He is).
- "Later, the Bible says it plainly" — "later" is vague (later in the story? another book?). Small.

## 2. BIBLE READER

Yes. Two things: the "does He care?" question is answered with John 11:5, where Martha is named FIRST in the list of those Jesus loved; and "He never called Martha's work wrong. He named her worry." is a fair correction of the usual "Martha bad, Mary good" sermon.

## 3. CURIOSITY GAP

- Question opened: "You do everything for everyone. Does anyone even care?" — then sharpened into "did Jesus care about Martha?"
- Paid off: Yes, twice. First softly ("His first words? Her name. Twice."), then plainly with John 11:5, then with the last line.
- One hole: the viewer's real ache is "nobody helps me." Jesus does NOT tell Mary to help. The film never faces that. A worn-out viewer may notice the question got changed from "will anyone help?" to "am I loved?". It is an honest change (the passage does it too), but the film glides past it.
- Score: 8/10.

## 4. THE 5 QUESTIONS

**Who is this for?** The worn-out helper who does everything and feels unseen. The first two lines hit that person directly. Clear.

**Main take-away:** You felt alone in the work, but Jesus was right there with you; He sees your worry and He loves you. The one thing needed is Him, not one more job.

**Global audience OK?** Mostly yes. The narrator's own words are plain and short. The KJV quotes carry the hard words; "careful" is glossed, "bid" is not. "Pulled in every direction" is an idiom but an easy one.

**Biblically accurate?**

Quotes checked word for word against the KJV given:

- Martha, Luke 10:40: "Lord, dost thou not care that my sister hath left me to serve alone? bid her therefore that she help me." — EXACT.
- Jesus, Luke 10:41: "Martha, Martha," + "thou art careful and troubled about many things:" — EXACT. Note: the narrator's "Twice." cuts into the middle of the verse. No word is changed or dropped, but the verse is split by a narrator line. I think it is fair and effective; flagging it because of the word-for-word rule.
- Jesus, Luke 10:42: "But one thing is needful: and Mary hath chosen that good part, which shall not be taken away from her." — EXACT.
- John 11:5: "Now Jesus loved Martha, and her sister, and Lazarus." — EXACT.

Claims checked:

- "A tired woman" — the text says "cumbered about much serving." Tired is an inference, a small and fair one.
- "She had opened her home to Him" — matches v38 ("received him into her house"). Good.
- "pulled in every direction" — fair plain rendering of "cumbered."
- "sat at His feet and listened" — matches v39.
- "His first words? Her name." — true to v41.
- "'Careful' means full of worry." — correct for the KJV sense.
- "The one thing needed was not another job. It was Him." — INTERPRETATION. The text says Mary chose "that good part" (sitting and hearing His word). "It was Him" is a common and sound reading, and the next line ties it back to "stay close and hear Him," so it holds. But it is stated as fact, not shown from the text.
- "no one would take that from her" — fair for "shall not be taken away from her."
- "He never called Martha's work wrong. He named her worry." — true to the text. Good.
- "about this family" — supported by John 11:1 and 11:5.
- "She felt left to serve alone." — her own words, v40. "But Jesus was right there, in her own house." — v38. Earned.

Unfair picture of Martha? No. She is the host, she is tired, her question is treated as a real question. She is not the villain.

Over-promise? No. The film does not promise help, rest, or a fixed life. It only says she was loved and He was there. Honest.

**Best version possible? The ONE change:** Tighten the middle of the last narrator block. Three lines in a row explain ("Careful means... The one thing needed... Mary chose to stay close..."). It turns into a small lesson just when the film should be moving to its ending. Cut or merge one of them so "So, did He care?" arrives sooner.

## 5. ENDING

What a newcomer would say back: "She thought she was alone, but Jesus was in her house the whole time — and the Bible says He loved her."

Real small discovery, not a slogan. It turns Martha's own words ("serve alone") against the plain fact of the scene (He was in her house — she invited Him). It is built from the passage, has no command in it, and no pressure. It also quietly reaches the viewer without saying "you."

Weak spot: the ending has two landings back to back (John 11:5, then "right there in her own house"). Both are good. The John 11:5 line is the proof; the last line is the discovery. It works, but the jump to "Later, the Bible says..." and a new name (Lazarus) takes a little air out just before the final line.

Score: 8/10.

## 6. TIGHTNESS

Drags or repeats:

- "The one thing needed was not another job. It was Him. Mary chose to stay close and hear Him, and no one would take that from her." — this mostly re-says what Jesus just said. The Mary sentence is the weakest; the film is about Martha, and this line pulls the eye to Mary.
- "Her name was Martha." right after "A tired woman once asked Jesus that." — fine, not a drag.
- "Twice." — earns its place. Keep.

Cut too far / unclear:

- Nothing is missing for the story to make sense.
- "bid her" is left unexplained, so the newcomer may not catch what Martha actually asked for.
- The film never says what Jesus did about the help she asked for. Not unclear, but a watchful viewer will feel the gap.

## 7. SCORE

**8 / 10 overall.**

Strong hook, every quote exact, Martha treated fairly, ending earned from the text. Held back by an explain-heavy stretch in the last block and one un-glossed old word ("bid").

## TOP 3 FIXES (most important first)

1. Trim the explanation run in the last narrator block. Merge or cut the Mary sentence so there are two explaining lines, not three, before "So, did He care?"
2. Help the newcomer with Martha's ask. One plain clause before or after her line (for example, that she wanted Jesus to send her sister to help) so "bid her" is not lost.
3. Smooth the John 11:5 hand-off. "Later" is vague and "Lazarus" arrives cold. A tiny cue (for example "their brother") or a clearer "later in the story" keeps the newcomer with you into the last line.

Optional, not blocking: one short honest beat that He did not send Mary to the kitchen — He gave Martha something else. It would close the "nobody helps" hole for the intended viewer.

## VERDICT: LOCK

The fixes above are polish. Nothing is wrong in the text, the doctrine, or the picture of Martha. It can lock as is; fix 1 and 2 would lift it to about 8.5.
