# Blind test viewer review — test_E (Luke 10:38-42)

## 1. NEWCOMER (no Bible knowledge, English as a second language)

- WHO: A tired woman called Martha, her sister Mary, and Jesus, who is a guest in Martha's home.
- WHAT: Martha does all the work alone, asks Jesus if He even cares, and asks Him to make her sister help. He says her name twice, tells her she is full of worry, and says Mary chose the one needed thing: listening to Him.
- WHY: It says Jesus did care about the tired one. He loved her, but gave her something other than the help she asked for.

The newcomer gets who and what. The why is only half there: they hear "He gave her what she needed" but are never told plainly what that was.

Words or lines a newcomer would NOT understand on first hearing:
- "dost thou not care" (old English)
- "hath left me"
- "bid her therefore that she help me" (the hardest line; "bid" and "therefore" together)
- "thou art"
- "careful" (in modern English it means the opposite of the meaning here; the film does explain it, good, but only after the line has passed)
- "troubled about many things" (mild)
- "needful"
- "that good part" (part of what?)
- "which shall not be taken away from her" (what cannot be taken?)
- "sat at His feet" (a newcomer may hear this as odd or low, not as "a student listening")
- "Lord" (who is He to her?)
- "Later, in another story" + "Lazarus" (an unknown name arrives in the second-last beat with no help)
- "He named her worry" (short and clever; a second-language ear may hear "named" as "gave a name to" and miss it)
- "He gave her what she needed" (never said what)

## 2. BIBLE READER

Yes, a little. The link from Martha's "dost thou not care" to John 11:5 "Now Jesus loved Martha", with Martha named first, is a real small gift. "Careful means full of worry" and "He never called her work wrong" are well known to a lifelong reader.

## 3. CURIOSITY GAP

- Question opened: "You do everything for everyone. Does anyone even care?" then, more sharply, "Did Jesus care about Martha?"
- Paid off: Yes, but by a verse from a different story, not from this scene. The answer is told to us ("the Bible says He loved her") more than shown. The mini-gap "His answer? Her name. Twice." is strong and is the best moment in the film, but the film never says why the doubled name matters. That is the proof of care that is inside the passage, and it is left unused.
- Score: 7/10

## 4. THE 5 QUESTIONS

**Who is this for?** A worn-out person who serves everyone and feels unseen. The first two lines hit that person well. The film then leaves "you" and never comes back.

**Main take-away:** Jesus cares about the tired worker; He does not call her work wrong; He names her worry and points her to the one needed thing, hearing Him.

**Global audience OK?** Mostly. Narrator lines are short and plain. The two KJV quotes are heavy for a second-language ear, above all Martha's "bid her therefore that she help me". The gloss for "careful" is good. There is no gloss for "that good part" or "needful" (the narrator's "The one thing needed?" helps with needful). "Lazarus" is an unexplained name.

**Biblically accurate?**
- Martha quote vs Luke 10:40: word for word. PASS.
- Jesus quote vs Luke 10:41-42: word for word, punctuation kept. PASS.
- John 11:5 quote: word for word. PASS. Honestly flagged as "later, in another story". Good.
- "She had opened her home to Him" = "received him into her house". Fair.
- "sat at His feet and listened" = "sat at Jesus' feet, and heard his word". Fair.
- "Martha asked Jesus that": she asked "dost thou not care". Fair.
- "Careful means full of worry": correct sense of the KJV word.
- "The one thing needed? Hearing Him, like Mary." This is a reading, not the text. The text says Mary chose "that good part"; it does not say in words that the one thing is hearing. It is the usual and fair reading, but it is said as flat fact. Small flag.
- "He never called Martha's work wrong. He named her worry." True to the text. Fair to Martha. She is not the villain.
- "He did not give Martha what she asked for." True (He did not send Mary).
- "He gave her what she needed." SOFT OVER-CLAIM. The passage ends at verse 42. It does not say what Martha received or how she took it. The film also never says what "what she needed" was. This is the one line not earned from the text.
- Picture of Martha: fair. No guilt, no pressure.
- Over-promise to the viewer: none direct. But the hook asks the viewer "does anyone care?" and the film answers only for Martha.

**Best version possible? The ONE change:** Make the last line say what He gave her, taken from the scene itself (her name, twice; His full attention; the truth about her worry), and turn it back to "you" once. Right now the ending is a neat shape with nothing inside it.

## 5. ENDING

A newcomer would say back: "Jesus did not give her the help she wanted, He gave her something better that she needed."

It is closer to a slogan than a discovery. The "asked for / needed" pair is a well-worn saying, heard in many places outside the Bible. Asked "what did she need?", the newcomer could not answer. The real discovery (He loved her; He said her name twice; He saw her worry, not just her work) is in the film but the last line does not land on it.

Ending score: 6/10

## 6. TIGHTNESS

- Nothing drags. The film is lean, maybe too lean at the end.
- "So, did He care?" is a good turn and should stay.
- "His answer? Her name. Twice." is set up and then dropped. Cut too far: one more short line would pay it off.
- "Later, in another story, the Bible says" is a little clumsy and "Lazarus" lands with no meaning for a newcomer. The quote must stay whole (KJV word for word), so the lead-in has to do the work, e.g. say first that this was a family He knew.
- "The one thing needed? Hearing Him, like Mary." then "He never called Martha's work wrong. He named her worry." Three very short verdict lines in a row. It starts to sound like a list of rulings. Fine, but close to the edge.
- Three question-and-answer fragments ("His answer?", "The one thing needed?", "So, did He care?") is one too many of the same trick.

## 7. SCORE

7/10 overall.

Clean, honest, accurate on every quote, kind to Martha, strong hook. Held back by an ending that is a known saying with no content, an unused best moment (the name said twice), and a hook about "you" that never returns to "you".

## TOP 3 FIXES

1. **Fill the last line.** Say what He gave her, from this scene: He did not send her help; He gave her His attention, her own name said twice, and the truth about her worry. Do not claim how Martha felt or answered; the text does not say. Then one plain turn back to the tired viewer.
2. **Pay off "Her name. Twice."** One short line on why it matters (He saw her, not only her work). This is the in-passage answer to "does He care?", so John 11:5 becomes the seal, not the only proof.
3. **Help the newcomer with the hard words.** Give a plain line before or after Martha's "bid her therefore that she help me" (she wants Jesus to tell Mary to help), and set up the John verse so "Lazarus" is not a stray name (e.g. this was a family He knew: two sisters and a brother).
