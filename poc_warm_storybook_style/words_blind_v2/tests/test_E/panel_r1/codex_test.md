1. **NEWCOMER**

WHO: Martha, an overwhelmed host, her sister Mary, and their guest Jesus.
WHAT: Martha asks Jesus to make Mary help; He addresses Martha’s worry and defends Mary’s listening.
WHY: It suggests Jesus can care about someone even when His answer is not the help they requested.

First-hearing barriers:

- “Sat at His feet” — the posture of a learner may be unfamiliar.
- “Lord” — Jesus’ identity and Martha’s relationship to Him are unexplained.
- “Dost thou,” “hath,” “bid her therefore,” “thou art,” “needful,” and “shall” — unfamiliar language or grammar.
- “Careful” — initially sounds like praise for being conscientious; the explanation arrives afterward.
- “That good part” — what exactly has Mary chosen?
- “Which shall not be taken away from her” — what could be taken, and by whom?
- “Hearing Him” — does this mean listening, learning, or obeying?
- “Lazarus” — an unexplained person introduced near the ending.
- “He named her worry” — figurative wording.
- “He gave her what she needed” — what did she receive, beyond a correction?

2. **BIBLE READER**

Yes, modestly: connecting Jesus’ correction with John’s explicit statement that He loved Martha gives a familiar passage a useful emphasis.

3. **CURIOSITY GAP**

The opening asks, “Does anyone care about the person doing everything?” It is paid off explicitly through “Jesus loved Martha,” although that answer comes from another passage. Why she receives no practical help remains unresolved. **8/10.**

4. **THE 5 QUESTIONS**

- **Who is this for?** Someone exhausted by caring for others who feels unseen.
- **Main takeaway?** Jesus cares about Martha and addresses her worry; listening to Him matters. The risk is hearing, “You need a better attitude more than you need help.”
- **Global audience OK?** The situation travels well. The uninterrupted KJV speech makes the central exchange difficult for listeners with limited English.
- **Biblically accurate?** Martha’s quotation matches the supplied Luke 10:40 wording exactly. Jesus’ quotation matches Luke 10:41–42 exactly. The John 11:5 quotation is exact. “Careful” as worried is appropriate here. Identifying the needed thing as hearing Jesus is a supported interpretation of verses 39 and 42, rather than an explicit definition. “He never called Martha’s work wrong” fairly describes the recorded words, although the passage does connect her distress with much serving. Martha is treated sympathetically. The final sentence goes further: Luke records Jesus’ answer, but neither Martha’s response nor a demonstrated resolution of her need. There is no explicit promise of relief, but the ending can sound as though her situation has been adequately resolved.
- **ONE change for the best version?** Replace the final sentence with a concrete discovery grounded in the exchange: “Mary was hearing His word. Now His words were for Martha, too.”

5. **ENDING**

A newcomer would probably say, “Jesus loved Martha, but decided she needed His teaching more than help with her work”—a small discovery that the final sentence compresses into a slogan and makes more certain than the passage does. **6/10.**

6. **TIGHTNESS**

“He never called Martha’s work wrong. He named her worry” partly repeats the explanation of “careful,” though it serves the intended viewer.

The John quotation adds evidence of love but also introduces another story and an unexplained name just as the film should land.

The biggest compression is in Jesus’ answer: the difficult phrases receive less explanation than the narrator’s concluding interpretation.

7. **OVERALL: 7/10**

**TOP 3 FIXES**

1. Replace “He gave her what she needed” with an ending that shows what Jesus actually does in this passage, without implying Martha’s burden was resolved.
2. Explain “good part” and “shall not be taken away” plainly enough that a newcomer understands Jesus’ answer.
3. If retaining John 11:5, shorten its introduction to “Elsewhere, the Bible says…” “Later, in another story” adds an unnecessary narrative detour.

**VERDICT: FIX.**
