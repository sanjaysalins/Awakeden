# Test viewer review: Luke 10:38-42 (Martha and Mary)

## 1. NEWCOMER

- **WHO:** A busy woman, Martha, and Jesus, a guest in her home.
- **WHAT:** Martha does all the work while her sister sits and listens. Martha complains to Jesus. He says her name twice and says her sister chose the better thing.
- **WHY:** Jesus cares about how worried Martha is, not only about the work she does.

**Words or lines a newcomer would not get on first hearing:**
- "dost thou not care," "hath left me," "bid her therefore." They will get the feeling, but not the grammar.
- "thou art careful and troubled." "Careful" is explained after the quote, which helps, but the newcomer has already misheard it as "being careful."
- "one thing is needful"
- "that good part"
- "which shall not be taken away from her." The newcomer can't tell who would take what away. The line is never explained.
- "sat at His feet." A newcomer won't know this means being a student. It may sound like sitting on the floor and being lazy.
- "Lazarus." The name is never introduced, so it's just noise.
- "Later, in another story." This is unclear: another story from where?

## 2. BIBLE READER

Yes, a little. Reading "careful" as "full of worry," and seeing that Jesus names her worry and not her work, is useful. The John 11:5 link is familiar to a lifelong reader, but it is well used here.

## 3. CURIOSITY GAP

- **Opens:** "Does anyone even care?" (that is, does God care about me, the one doing everything).
- **Paid off?** Partly. The answer comes from a different book (John 11). It is not earned inside this scene. The scene's own answer ("Her name. Twice.") is set up well, but the film drops it before the payoff.
- **Score: 7/10**

## 4. THE 5 QUESTIONS

- **Who is it for:** The worn-out helper. The opening hooks this person well.
- **Main take-away:** Jesus cares about you, not just about what you do for others. He sees your worry.
- **Global audience OK?** Mostly. The KJV block is heavy, and "careful" is the only word explained. "That good part" and "taken away" get no help.
- **Biblically accurate?**
  - **Quotes:** I checked every quote against the KJV given. Luke 10:40, Luke 10:41-42 and John 11:5 all match word for word. Leaving out "And Jesus answered and said unto her" is a fair speaker frame.
  - **"Martha asked Jesus that":** A slight stretch. She asked "dost *thou* not care," not "does *anyone* care." It's fine as a bridge.
  - **"The one thing needed? Hearing Him":** Interpretation, but it's well grounded in v39 ("heard his word").
  - **"He never called Martha's work wrong":** Fair. The text names her worry, not her serving.
  - **"Later, in another story":** The timing is soft but honest.
  - **"He gave her what she needed":** Vague. The film never says *what* He gave her. Is it the invitation? The name? The narration doesn't say.
  - **Over-promise:** None.
  - **Picture of Martha:** Fair and kind. She is not the villain.
- **Best version possible?** Not yet. **The ONE change:** make the payoff come from this scene. Her name said twice is the proof of care. John 11:5 should only confirm it, not carry it.

## 5. ENDING

- **What a newcomer would say back:** "Jesus loved Martha. He gave her what she needed, not what she wanted."
- **Discovery or slogan?** That's a slogan. "Not what you asked for, but what you needed" is a stock phrase. The real small discovery is "He named her worry, not her work," but it sits in the middle and isn't the last word.
- The film opens on "You…" and never comes back to the viewer.
- **Score: 6/10**

## 6. TIGHTNESS

- **Too many separate turns:** "So, did He care?" then John 11 then the final two lines. That's three moves in about 10 seconds, after the narrator has already given two explanations.
- **Cut too far:** "Mary hath chosen that good part, which shall not be taken away" goes unexplained. A newcomer may feel Martha lost and was told off, which works against the no-guilt rule.
- **Weak on screen:** "in another story" is vague.

## 7. OVERALL: 7/10

## TOP 3 FIXES

1. **Make the ending come from this scene.** "Martha, Martha" is the answer to "do you care." Land on that. Keep John 11:5 as a short confirmation, and make the last line a fresh, concrete line, not "what she needed."
2. **Come back to the viewer once, with grace and no pressure.** Example: "He says your name the same way." The film opens on "you" and has to close that loop.
3. **Clear up the newcomer blocks with one short plain line each.** Explain "sat at His feet" as learning from Him. Explain "not taken away" as Jesus protecting Mary's time with Him, not scolding Martha. Introduce Lazarus as "her brother" or drop the name.

**Verdict: FIX.** The fixes are small. The hook and the middle are strong.
