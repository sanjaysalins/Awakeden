# Blind test viewer review — test_A (Luke 10:38-42)

## 1. NEWCOMER (no Bible knowledge, English as a second language)

- **WHO:** Martha, a woman who has Jesus as a guest in her home, and her sister Mary.
- **WHAT:** Martha does all the work alone, complains to Jesus that He does not care, and asks Him to make Mary help. He answers gently, says her name twice, and does not send Mary away.
- **WHY:** If you carry everything and feel unseen, Jesus sees your worry and loves you. (A newcomer gets "He loved her." They probably do NOT get what "the one thing" is in practice.)

**Words / lines a newcomer would NOT understand on first hearing:**

- "dost thou not care" — old English; meaning is guessable only because the narrator asked "Does anyone care?" just before.
- "hath left me" — old form.
- "bid her therefore that she help me" — hardest line in the film. "Bid" and "therefore" both lose an ESL listener. Never paraphrased.
- "thou art" — old form.
- "careful" — modern English meaning is the opposite-ish ("cautious"). It IS explained afterwards, which is good, but the explanation comes a full sentence late.
- "troubled about many things" — okay, borderline.
- "needful" — not a word in modern use. Narrator later says "the one thing needed," which half-rescues it.
- "that good part" — the key phrase of the passage, never explained. Part of what? A newcomer hears a riddle.
- "which shall not be taken away from her" — long, old syntax, spoken fast in a 60s film.
- "sat at His feet" — heard literally (sitting on the floor). The meaning (listening, learning from Him) is never said. The film cuts "and heard his word," which is the one clause that explains it.
- "Lord" — unexplained title.
- Who Jesus is, and why Him being in the house is a big deal — assumed.
- "The one thing needed was Him" — abstract. Needed how? What do I do?
- "called Martha close too" — a newcomer did not hear Him call her anywhere. Confusing.

## 2. BIBLE READER

**Yes, a little.** Joining "dost thou not care?" to John 11:5 ("Now Jesus loved Martha") as the direct answer is a genuinely nice link, and "She wanted Mary taken... shall not be taken away" is a sharp echo. "Careful = full of worry" is known to most lifelong readers.

## 3. CURIOSITY GAP

- **Question opened:** "You do everything for everyone. Does anyone care?" — then, does Jesus care about Martha (and me)?
- **Paid off?** Yes. The last line answers the exact question in the Bible's own words. Clean loop.
- **Weak spot:** the hook is about "you," then drops "you" entirely. The pay-off is about Martha; the viewer must make the jump alone. Also the question "what is the one thing?" is opened mid-film and only answered with an abstract "Him."
- **Score: 7/10**

## 4. THE 5 QUESTIONS

**Who is this for?** The worn-out helper who feels nobody helps. The first two sentences hit that person well. Clear.

**Main take-away:** He did not tell you off for your work; He saw your worry; He loves you. Clear enough. The "so what do I do" (come and sit, listen to Him) is missing.

**Global audience OK?** Partly. The narrator's own words are plain and short — good. But 2 of the 5 blocks are long unglossed KJV, and the two phrases that carry the meaning ("bid her therefore that she help me," "that good part") are never put in plain words. A second-language listener follows the narrator and loses both speeches.

**Biblically accurate?**

- Martha's line (Luke 10:40): **word for word. Pass.**
- Jesus' line (Luke 10:41-42): **word for word. Pass.**
- Closing quote: "Now Jesus loved Martha, and her sister." — the words are exact, but the verse is **cut short**. John 11:5 reads "...and her sister, **and Lazarus**." The film ends the quote with a full stop as if the verse ends there. Flag: truncated quote presented as whole.
- Same quote: it is from **John 11**, a different book and a different day (Lazarus's sickness). The film gives no signpost, so it sounds like Luke's verdict on this dinner. Honest fix: one or two words ("Later, John wrote:").
- "She had opened her home to Him" — fair for "received him into her house." Pass.
- "She wanted Mary taken from His feet." — **interpretive.** The text says she wanted help. It follows logically, and it sets up "shall not be taken away," but it paints her motive a shade harder than the text. Mild flag; borderline on "Martha is not the villain."
- "His first words? Her name." — true to the text. Pass.
- "'Careful' means full of worry." — accurate. Pass.
- "He never called her work wrong." — true (argument from silence, but fair). Pass.
- "The one thing needed was Him." — **interpretation, not in the text.** The text says Mary's good part was sitting and hearing His word. Defensible, mainstream, but stated as fact.
- "He kept Mary close" — fair for "shall not be taken away."
- "and called Martha close too." — **not in the text.** No invitation is recorded. This is an inference from the doubled name. It is the one real over-reach in the film.
- Over-promise: none. No "your load will lift" claim. Good.
- Picture of Martha: mostly fair and kind. Not a villain.

**Best version possible? The ONE change:** replace "and called Martha close too" (not in the text, and confusing) with a plain line saying what Mary was doing — listening to Him — so "the one thing" and "the good part" finally mean something a newcomer can hold.

## 5. ENDING

**What a newcomer would say back:** "She asked if He cared about her, and the Bible says He loved her."

**Discovery or slogan?** A real small discovery. The answer is not the narrator's opinion; it is a line from the Bible itself, and it answers the question asked in the first five seconds. It is earned by the question, though it is borrowed from another chapter without saying so, and the cut "and Lazarus" slightly weakens the honesty. The lines just before it ("The one thing needed was Him. He kept Mary close, and called Martha close too.") are the slogan-ish part.

**Score: 7/10**

## 6. TIGHTNESS

- **Drags / repeats:** "He kept Mary close, and called Martha close too." — "close... close" is soft and vague; the weakest line. "He never called her work wrong. He named her worry." is good and should stay.
- **Cut too far:**
  - "and heard his word" is missing, so "sat at His feet" has no meaning and "that good part" has no referent.
  - "Martha was cumbered about much serving" is not shown in words — the narrator jumps from "opened her home" to the complaint. We never hear that she is working. The pictures may carry it; the words do not.
  - "You do everything for everyone" never comes back. The viewer is addressed once and dropped.
- "His first words? Her name." — best line in the film. Keep.

## 7. SCORE

**7/10 overall.** Strong hook, kind to Martha, excellent closing loop, quotes accurate. Held back by unglossed KJV at the two points that matter most, one invented claim, and a trimmed, unsignposted John 11:5.

## TOP 3 FIXES

1. **Say what Mary was doing and what "the good part" is, in plain words.** Add that she was listening to Him (the text: "heard his word"). Without it the centre of the passage is a riddle for a newcomer.
2. **Cut or replace "and called Martha close too."** It is not in the text. Let the doubled name and John 11:5 do that work instead.
3. **Fix the last quote honestly.** Either quote John 11:5 whole ("...and her sister, and Lazarus") or do not close the quote as if the verse ends there; and add a tiny signpost that this is said elsewhere/later ("John wrote:"). Also give "bid her therefore that she help me" a plain-word echo ("Tell her to help me").

## VERDICT

**FIX** — small fixes, one more pass. Not far from a lock.
