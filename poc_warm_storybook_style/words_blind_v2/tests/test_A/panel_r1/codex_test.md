1. **NEWCOMER**

WHO: Martha, an exhausted host, her sister Mary, and Jesus, their guest.  
WHAT: Martha asks Jesus to make Mary help; He addresses Martha’s worry and lets Mary stay with Him.  
WHY: Jesus can correct someone’s worry while still loving and caring about them.

Likely first-hearing difficulties:

- “Lord”: what relationship or authority does this title imply?
- “Sat at His feet”: the posture is clear, but its meaning as listening and learning is missing.
- “Dost thou”: do you.
- “Hath”: has.
- “Serve alone”: serving whom, and doing what?
- “Bid her therefore that she help me”: tell her to help me.
- “Thou art”: you are.
- “Careful”: initially sounds like being cautious; the later explanation helps.
- “One thing is needful”: one thing is necessary—but what?
- “That good part”: what has Mary chosen?
- “Shall not be taken away from her”: what could be taken away?
- “The one thing needed was Him”: what does needing a person mean practically here?
- “Called Martha close too”: did He ask her to come or sit down? We never hear that.
- “Now Jesus loved Martha”: “now” might sound as though He began loving her at this moment.

2. **BIBLE READER**

No: the repeated name receives useful emphasis, but the interpretation is mostly familiar, and the invitation to Martha is asserted rather than demonstrated.

3. **CURIOSITY GAP**

The opening asks: “Does Jesus care when I am exhausted and unsupported?” The ending explicitly answers yes through John 11:5. That pays off the emotional question, although the film leaves the practical burden of serving alone unresolved. **8/10.**

4. **THE 5 QUESTIONS**

- **Who is this for?** Someone who feels overworked, unseen, and resentful about carrying responsibilities alone.
- **Main take-away?** Jesus notices your worry and loves you even when His answer differs from the help you requested.
- **Global audience OK?** The emotional situation travels well. The KJV dialogue needs support from clear pictures and plain narration. Omitting that Mary was listening makes the central contrast harder to understand.
- **Biblically accurate?**
  - Martha’s entire quotation matches the supplied Luke 10:40 word for word.
  - Jesus’s entire quotation matches the supplied Luke 10:41–42 word for word.
  - The final John quotation retains the exact opening words but ends early: the supplied sentence continues “, and Lazarus.” It also replaces that continuation with a full stop.
  - Receiving Jesus into her home and Mary sitting at His feet are supported.
  - “His first words? Her name” is accurate.
  - Explaining “careful” as worried fits the passage.
  - “He never called her work wrong” accurately describes this exchange, though “never” can sound broader than this scene.
  - “The one thing needed was Him” is an interpretation; the supplied passage specifically shows Mary hearing His word.
  - “Called Martha close too” is a possible implied invitation, but no such invitation is stated.
  - “She wanted Mary taken from His feet” makes Martha sound intent on depriving Mary. Her stated request is for help with serving.
  - There is no explicit promise that her workload disappears. The suggested invitation nevertheless sounds more explicit than the passage warrants.
- **ONE change that would make it better?** Replace “The one thing needed was Him. He kept Mary close, and called Martha close too” with a concrete explanation: “Mary was listening to Him. Jesus would not take that away from her.”

5. **ENDING**

A newcomer might say, “Jesus still cared about Martha when He corrected her worry”—a real small discovery, although the unsupported invitation weakens how firmly the ending lands. **7/10.**

6. **TIGHTNESS**

“His first words? Her name” earns its space. The sequence “He never called her work wrong. He named her worry” partly repeats the preceding explanation of “careful,” but serves the intended viewer.

“The one thing needed was Him” and “called Martha close too” compress interpretation too far. The consequential omission is that Mary **heard His word**: without that, she can look like someone simply sitting while her sister works.

7. **OVERALL: 7/10**

**TOP 3 FIXES**

1. Ground the ending in Mary listening to Jesus; remove the explicit-sounding invitation that the passage does not record.
2. Replace “She wanted Mary taken from His feet” with wording that fairly represents Martha asking for help.
3. Make the final quotation conform to the exact-quotation rule; the supplied sentence includes “and Lazarus.”

**VERDICT: FIX.**
