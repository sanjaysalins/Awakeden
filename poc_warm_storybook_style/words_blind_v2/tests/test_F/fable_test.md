# Blind test viewer review — test_F (Luke 10:38-42)

## 1. NEWCOMER (no Bible knowledge, English as a second language)

- **WHO:** Martha, a woman who is doing all the work in her house while Jesus visits, and her sister Mary who sits and listens to Him.
- **WHAT:** Martha asks Jesus if He cares that she is left to work alone; He answers by saying her name twice and naming her worry.
- **WHY it matters:** If you carry everything for everyone, Jesus does not scold your work; He sees the worry under it.

**Words or lines a newcomer would NOT understand on first hearing:**

- "dost thou not care" / "hath left me" — old English; the narrator's "Does anyone even care?" just before it saves the meaning, so this survives.
- "bid her therefore that she help me" — "bid" is not known; the sense (tell her to help) is not given anywhere in plain words.
- "careful" — glossed ("full of worry"). Good. But the gloss comes after the whole Jesus quote, so on first hearing it still lands as "you are careful" = a compliment.
- "troubled about many things" — fine.
- "needful" — half-glossed by "the one thing needed".
- "that good part" — never explained. A newcomer does not know what "part" means here (a share? a role?).
- "which shall not be taken away from her" — hard grammar.
- "He would not take that from her." — **unclear who "her" is.** The sentence before is about Mary, the film is about Martha. A newcomer will likely hear "He would not take it from Martha", or not know what "that" is.
- "sat at His feet" — may sound strange or servile; pictures will probably carry it.
- "Lazarus" — a brand-new name, 20 words from the end, never explained. A newcomer asks "who?" at the exact moment the film wants them to feel the answer.
- "Later, the Bible says" — later than what? Newcomer does not know this is another book, another day.
- "the Lord" in Martha's mouth — fine, clear from context it is Jesus.

## 2. BIBLE READER

**Yes, a little.** Two real things: (a) "His answer? Her name. Twice." makes them hear "Martha, Martha" as tenderness, not a telling-off; (b) John 11:5 as the answer to "dost thou not care" is a good link, and a sharp reader will notice Martha is named FIRST in that verse — but the film does not point at it, so most will miss it.

## 3. CURIOSITY GAP

- **Question opened:** "You do everything for everyone — does anyone even care?" then, more exactly, "Did Jesus care about Martha?"
- **Paid off?** Yes. "So, did He care?" is asked out loud and answered with a direct Bible quote, then deepened by "saw her worry".
- One weakness: the gap is answered twice, in two different ways (John 11:5, then "look again at His answer"). The first answer closes the gap; the film then re-opens the earlier quote. It works, but the order feels like a step back.
- **Score: 8/10.**

## 4. THE 5 QUESTIONS

**Who is this for?** The worn-out helper who serves everybody and feels unseen. The first line hits that person hard and fast. Clear.

**Main take-away:** Jesus did not say her work was wrong; He saw the worry under the work — and He loved her.

**Global audience OK?** Mostly. Narrator words are short and plain. Weak spots: "good part" unexplained, "bid her" unexplained, "Lazarus" unexplained, the unclear "her", and the "careful" gloss coming late.

**Biblically accurate?**

- Martha quote vs Luke 10:40 — **word for word. Correct.**
- Jesus quote vs Luke 10:41-42 — **word for word, punctuation included. Correct.**
- "Now Jesus loved Martha, and her sister, and Lazarus." vs John 11:5 — **word for word. Correct.**
- "She had opened her home to Him" — fair paraphrase of "received him into her house".
- "her sister Mary sat at His feet and listened" — fair ("heard his word").
- "Martha asked Jesus that" — fair; her question is narrower (do You care that my sister left me), but the heart is the same.
- "'Careful' means full of worry" — sound.
- **Flag (interpretation stated as fact):** "The one thing needed? To hear Him, like Mary." The text does not say what the one thing is; it only says Mary chose the good part. This is the common reading and I accept it, but it is the film's reading, not a quote.
- **Flag (small shift):** "He would not take that from her." The KJV is passive — "shall not be taken away from her". The film makes Jesus the one who will not take it. Reasonable (He is refusing Martha's request), but it is a step beyond the words, and the pronoun is unclear.
- **Flag (soft):** "Later, the Bible says" — John 11:5 is another Gospel and another event. "Later" is defensible, but it uses a verse about a different day to answer this day's question. Honest enough as written; do not strengthen it further.
- "He never called Martha's work wrong." — true, by silence. Fair.
- "He looked past all her work, and saw her worry." — earned from "careful and troubled about many things". Fair.
- **Picture of Martha:** fair and kind. She is not the villain. One risk: "The one thing needed? To hear Him, like Mary." is the single moment the tired viewer may feel "so I am the wrong sister". The next lines repair it, but the sting is there for about five seconds.
- **Over-promise:** none. It does not promise the viewer help, rest, or a fixed life. Good.

**Best version possible? The ONE change:** rewrite the middle lump ("The one thing needed? To hear Him, like Mary. He would not take that from her.") so that it is clear, short, and does not sound like "be like Mary". It is the only place the film turns teacher, the only unclear pronoun, and the only guilt risk.

## 5. ENDING

**What a newcomer would say back:** "Jesus did not tell her that her work was bad — He saw that she was worried."

**Real discovery or slogan?** A real small discovery. It comes from the passage's own words ("careful and troubled"), and it re-reads an answer the viewer thought was a rebuke. Not a slogan.

Two honest limits:

- The film opens on "**You**" and ends on "**her**". The viewer has to make the last step alone. That is a fair, no-pressure choice, but some newcomers will file it as a nice story about Martha and not about themselves.
- "looked past all her work" could be heard as "ignored her work". "Looked under" or "saw through to" is not the fix either — but the word "past" is slightly cold.

**Ending score: 7/10.**

## 6. TIGHTNESS

- **Drags / repeats:** "work" three times in the last two sentences ("Martha's work wrong... all her work... her worry"). The work/worry echo is nice once; the third "work" is one too many.
- **Double payoff:** "So, did He care?" + John 11:5, then "Look again at His answer." Two endings back to back. Either one alone would be clean; together the middle one loses weight.
- **Teacher voice:** "'Careful' means full of worry." is needed, but it is a dictionary line sitting right after the most tender line in the film.
- **Cut too far:** "Lazarus" arrives with no introduction. "He would not take that from her" is cut so tight it is unclear. Mary's choice ("good part") is never put in plain words.
- **Works well, keep:** "His answer? Her name. Twice." — best line in the film. The first two sentences. The plain set-up.

## 7. SCORE

**7/10 overall.**

Strong hook, all quotes exact, kind to Martha, a real ending. Held back by a muddy middle and an unexplained name at the emotional peak.

## TOP 3 FIXES

1. **Fix the middle lump.** Replace "The one thing needed? To hear Him, like Mary. He would not take that from her." with something clear and without the "be like Mary" sting — name Mary in the last sentence so "her" is not a puzzle (e.g. "The one thing needed? To hear Him. Mary chose that, and He would not take it from Mary."). Check it does not read as a rebuke to the tired viewer.
2. **Handle "Lazarus" and "Later".** A newcomer hits an unknown name at the payoff. Either add three words ("her brother Lazarus" cannot go inside the quote, so set it up before: "Later, when her brother is sick, the Bible says:") or accept the bump. Also consider pointing at the thing a Bible reader would love: Martha is named first.
3. **One ending, not two — and trim the triple "work".** Put the order as: explain His answer -> "He never called her work wrong; He saw her worry" -> "So, did He care?" -> John 11:5 as the last word. Or keep the current order but tighten the last line to one "work". Optional: one soft clause that turns back to "you" without pressure or promise.

## VERDICT: FIX

Small fix, not a rewrite. The bones are right; fix 1 alone would likely move this to LOCK.
