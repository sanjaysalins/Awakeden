**Test viewer review: Luke 10:38-42, "Martha, Martha"**

**1. Newcomer (no Bible knowledge, English as a second language)**
- **Who:** A busy woman, Martha, and Jesus, who is a guest in her home.
- **What:** She complains that her sister will not help. Jesus says her name twice and tells her one thing matters more.
- **Why it matters:** Jesus sees how worried she is, not only how hard she works. He loves her.

Words or lines a newcomer would not get on first hearing:
- "dost thou not care", "hath left me", "bid her therefore": old grammar, and Martha speaks fast.
- "troubled about many things": just about OK.
- "needful": not a common word.
- "that good part": what part? This is the key phrase and it goes by too fast.
- "which shall not be taken away from her": long and passive.
- "sat at His feet": a newcomer sees a sitting position. They will not know it means a student learning from a teacher.
- "He would not take that from her": "her" could mean Mary or Martha. The film is about Martha, so a newcomer may pick the wrong one.
- "Lazarus": a new name with no intro. It costs a beat of confusion.
- "Later, the Bible says": fine, but it jumps to another book with no bridge.
- "Lord": OK.
- "Careful": the gloss "full of worry" works well. Keep it.

**2. Bible reader**
Yes. "Careful" meaning full of worry is a real small find for KJV readers. So is the double name ("Martha, Martha") read as tenderness rather than a telling-off. Putting John 11:5 next to this story is nice, but a lifelong reader already knows it.

**3. Curiosity gap**
- **What it opens:** "Does anyone even care?" This is strong and personal, and it fits the target viewer exactly.
- **Payoff:** "So, did He care?" is answered with John 11:5. The question is paid off, but mostly by a verse from outside the passage. The answer from inside the passage ("Her name. Twice.") comes earlier and is the better payoff. It gets slightly buried.
- **Score: 7/10**

**4. The 5 questions**
- **Who is this for:** Tired people who serve and feel unseen. It is clearly aimed at them.
- **Main take-away:** Jesus cares about you, the worried person, not just about your work.
- **Global audience OK?** Mostly. The KJV dialogue is heavy for a second-language listener, and the narrator only explains one word ("careful"). "That good part" and "needful" get no help.
- **Biblically accurate?**
  - **Quotes:** Martha's line matches Luke 10:40 word for word. Jesus' line matches Luke 10:41-42 word for word. John 11:5 matches exactly. All pass.
  - **"He would not take that from her":** The text says it "shall not be taken away," which is passive. Saying Jesus Himself refused is a fair reading, since He did not send Mary to the kitchen. But it is an inference.
  - **"The one thing needed? To hear Him":** A mainstream reading, and it is backed by verse 39 ("heard his word"). OK.
  - **"He never called Martha's work wrong":** True to the words. But Jesus did decline her request. The film skips over the "no", so it reads a little softer than the text.
  - **"Saw her worry":** An interpretation, and a fair one.
  - **"Later":** Chronology across Luke and John is uncertain. "Later" is safe enough, or use "Elsewhere."
  - **Martha:** Treated fairly, not as a villain. Good.
  - **Over-promise:** None. The film never says "He sees your worry too," which is safe but also leaves the viewer out.
- **Best version possible:** Not yet. The film opens on "you" and never comes back to "you." The tired viewer is left watching Martha, not invited themselves.
- **The ONE change:** Close the loop to the viewer with something earned from the text. For example: the seat Mary chose was not hers alone. It was open to Martha too. Or: "He said your name twice too" style, kept modest.

**5. Ending**
- **What a newcomer would say back:** "Jesus saw she was worried, not just busy, and He loved her."
- **Discovery or slogan?** A real but small discovery. The last line, "He looked past all her work, and saw her worry," is good and earned. It just ends on Martha. The worn-out viewer gets no turn of their own, so it lands as observation, not invitation.
- **Score: 6.5/10**

**6. Tightness**
- The last narrator block is too long: seven sentences after Jesus speaks. It lists define, explain, inference, question, outside verse, "look again", and conclusion, one after another.
- "Look again at His answer" adds little. Cut it and go straight to "He never called…"
- "The one thing needed? To hear Him, like Mary. He would not take that from her." These are three clipped fragments in a row, which is choppy. "Her" is ambiguous here.
- Cut too far: "sat at His feet" needs a two-word hint, like "like a student", or newcomers miss why Mary's choice matters.

**7. Overall: 7/10**

**Top 3 fixes**
1. **Return to the viewer at the end.** Add one closing beat, grounded in the text, that lets the tired person see themselves invited: the "good part" or the seat at His feet was open to Martha too. Do not over-promise. This is the biggest gap.
2. **Tighten the final block.** Cut "Look again at His answer." Merge the choppy "one thing" lines. Fix the "her" in "He would not take that from her": say "He would not take it from Mary."
3. **Help newcomers with two phrases.** Add "like a student" to "sat at His feet". Give one plain hint for "that good part". Optionally drop the name Lazarus with "her brother."

**Verdict: FIX.** Small fixes only. The quotes are clean, Martha is treated fairly, and the hook is strong.
