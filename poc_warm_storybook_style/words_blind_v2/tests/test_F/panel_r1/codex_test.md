**1. NEWCOMER**

WHO: Martha, an overwhelmed host; Mary, her sister; and Jesus, their visiting teacher.  
WHAT: Martha asks Jesus to make Mary help; He names Martha’s worry and defends Mary’s choice to listen.  
WHY: Jesus can address Martha’s worry and still love her.

Likely first-hearing difficulties:

- “Opened her home to Him”: an idiom for welcoming Him.
- “Sat at His feet”: the position of someone learning from a teacher.
- “Lord”: Jesus’ role and this title are unexplained.
- “Dost thou,” “hath,” “bid her therefore,” “thou art,” “needful,” and “shall”: unfamiliar older English.
- “Careful”: sounds like “taking care”; the later explanation helps.
- “Mary hath chosen that good part”: “part” of what? The explanation partly resolves this.
- “Which shall not be taken away from her” / “He would not take that from her”: what exactly is being protected, and for how long?
- “Lazarus”: an unexplained new person near the ending.
- “Looked past all her work”: an idiom that could also sound as though Jesus overlooked her effort.

**2. BIBLE READER**

Yes, modestly: connecting Jesus’ words about Martha’s worry with John’s explicit statement of His love gives the familiar scene a useful emphasis.

**3. CURIOSITY GAP**

The question is: **Does Jesus care when you are doing everything and feel abandoned?** The John quotation answers His love clearly. The passage does not resolve Martha’s practical need for help, so the emotional payoff is stronger than the practical one. **8/10.**

**4. THE 5 QUESTIONS**

- **Who is this for?** Someone exhausted by caring for others, especially someone whose exhaustion has become resentment.
- **Main takeaway?** Jesus noticed Martha’s worry, and His response did not mean He lacked love for her.
- **Global audience OK?** Mostly. The modern narration is accessible; the concentrated KJV language creates a substantial listening hurdle.
- **Biblically accurate?** Martha’s quotation matches Luke 10:40 exactly. Jesus’ quotation matches Luke 10:41–42 exactly. The John 11:5 quotation is exact. The welcome and Mary’s listening faithfully summarize verses 38–39. “Martha asked Jesus that” broadens her specific complaint into the opening’s general question. “The one thing needed? To hear Him” is a supported interpretation, not Jesus’ explicit wording. “He would not take that from her” interprets the passive promise as His personal protection. “He never called Martha’s work wrong” accurately notes what He did not say, but could obscure His correction of her priorities. “He looked past all her work” adds a description the text does not give; “saw her worry” is supported by His words. Martha is treated sympathetically. There is no explicit promise of practical help or an end to exhaustion.
- **One change that would improve it most?** Replace “looked past all her work.” For this viewer, being overlooked is already the wound. End by connecting His recognition of her worry with His love for her.

**5. ENDING**

A newcomer would probably say, “Jesus knew Martha was worried, and He loved her even though He didn’t give her the answer she wanted.” That is a real small discovery, although “looked past” makes the final wording less secure than the insight. **7/10.**

**6. TIGHTNESS**

“He would not take that from her” repeats the immediately preceding quotation without making its meaning much clearer. “Look again at His answer” adds another restart after the John quotation has already delivered the answer. Keep the love quotation; tighten the surrounding explanation.

The story itself remains clear. The main compression problem is asking a newcomer to absorb several archaic phrases in one uninterrupted speech.

**7. OVERALL: 7/10**

**TOP 3 FIXES**

1. Replace “looked past all her work” with an ending that directly joins Martha’s worry and Jesus’ love.
2. Make “that good part” and what cannot be taken away clearer in the plain-English explanation.
3. Cut the repeated protection sentence and the “Look again” transition so the ending lands once.

**VERDICT: FIX.**
