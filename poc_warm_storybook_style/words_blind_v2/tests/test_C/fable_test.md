# Blind test viewer review — test_C (Luke 10:38-42)

## 1. NEWCOMER (no Bible knowledge, English as a second language)

- **WHO:** Martha, a woman who is doing all the work in her house, her sister Mary, and their guest Jesus.
- **WHAT:** Martha complains to Jesus that her sister left her to work alone; He answers her gently, by name, and does not send Mary away.
- **WHY:** If you feel you carry everything and nobody cares, this says Jesus sees your worry and loves you.

A newcomer gets who and what. The why arrives, but only in the last line, and only if they follow the old English.

**Words or lines a newcomer would NOT understand on first hearing:**

- "dost thou not care" (old grammar)
- "hath left me" (old grammar)
- "bid her therefore that she help me" — the hardest line; "bid" is unknown to most learners
- "thou art"
- "careful" — modern meaning is "cautious"; the film does explain it, good
- "troubled about many things" — probably OK, borderline
- "needful" — never explained
- "one thing is needful" — the film never says plainly what the one thing IS
- "that good part" — part of what? Not explained
- "which shall not be taken away from her"
- "sat at His feet" — sounds strange or servile if you do not know it means "sat and listened to Him teach"
- "Lord" — not explained; and the film never says who Jesus is
- "Now Jesus loved Martha" — "Now" as a sentence opener is odd to a learner
- "called Martha close too" — vague even for a native speaker

## 2. BIBLE READER

**Yes, a little.** Answering Martha's "dost thou not care?" in Luke with John 11:5, where Martha is named first, is a real and pleasing link most readers have not made. "Careful = full of worry" and "He named her worry, not her work" are familiar sermon points.

## 3. CURIOSITY GAP

- **Question opened:** "You do everything for everyone. Does anyone care?" — then, does Jesus care about Martha?
- **Paid off?** Yes. The last lines ask "Did He care?" and answer with a Bible verse. The loop closes cleanly on the same word, "care."
- **Weak spot:** the opening question is about "anyone"; the pay-off is about Jesus and Martha. The step back to the viewer is left unsaid. That is tasteful, but a newcomer may not make the jump.
- **Score: 8/10**

## 4. THE 5 QUESTIONS

**Who is this for?** The worn-out helper who does everything and feels unseen. The first two sentences speak straight to them. Clear.

**Main take-away:** Jesus did not scold Martha's work; He saw her worry, and He loved her.

**Global audience OK?** Partly. The narrator lines are plain and short. But both long quotes are heavy KJV, and only one word ("careful") gets a gloss. "Bid her therefore that she help me" and "one thing is needful" carry the story and will be lost on many second-language viewers.

**Biblically accurate?**

Quotes checked word for word against the KJV given:

- Martha line (Luke 10:40): **exact.**
- Jesus line (Luke 10:41-42): **exact.**
- "Now Jesus loved Martha, and her sister." (John 11:5): words are exact as far as they go, but the verse is **cut short** — the KJV goes on ", and Lazarus." The film ends it with a full stop as if it were the whole sentence. Small, but flag it.

Claims not strictly in the text:

- **"He ... called Martha close too."** Not in the passage. Jesus says her name twice and corrects her gently; nothing says He called her to come close. This is the one real over-reach.
- **"She wanted Mary taken from His feet."** Interpretation. Martha asked for help, not for Mary to be removed from Jesus. It is a fair inference and it sets up "shall not be taken away," but it puts a slightly harder motive on Martha than the text does.
- **"The meal would end. Time with Him would last."** Interpretation of "shall not be taken away." Reasonable. Note the text says Mary "heard his word" — the good part is listening to Him, not just "time."
- **John 11:5 used as the answer.** It comes from a different day and a different book. The film does not say so. It is honest in spirit, but a viewer will think this line is in the same scene.

Picture of Martha: **fair.** "He never called her work wrong" protects her well. She is not the villain.

Over-promise: **none.** No pressure, no guilt, no gain/loss framing.

**Best version possible? The ONE change:** replace "He kept Mary close, and called Martha close too" with a plain line that says what the "one thing" is (listening to Him), so the centre of Jesus' answer is actually explained and the unsupported claim goes.

## 5. ENDING

**What a newcomer would say back:** "She asked if He cared, and the answer is yes — He loved her."

**Discovery or slogan?** A real small discovery. The question in her own mouth is answered by a verse that names her first. It is earned from the Bible's own words, not a stock call to action. It loses a little because the film does not signal that the verse comes from elsewhere, and because the line stops mid-verse.

**Score: 8/10**

## 6. TIGHTNESS

- **Drags / repeats:** nothing drags. It is lean.
- **Slightly too clipped:** "His first words? Her name. Twice." works. But three question-and-fragment beats in one short film ("Does anyone care?" / "His first words?" / "Did He care?") start to feel like a pattern.
- **Weakest line:** "He kept Mary close, and called Martha close too." Vague, and half of it is not in the text.
- **Cut too far:** the meaning of "one thing is needful" and "that good part" is never stated. The film explains the easy word ("careful") and skips the hard idea. "The meal would end. Time with Him would last" hints at it but a newcomer will not connect it to "one thing."
- **Missing for a newcomer:** half a sentence on who Jesus is (a teacher people came to hear), which would also explain "sat at His feet."

## 7. SCORE

**7.5 / 10 overall**

Strong hook, strong close, accurate main quotes, kind to Martha. Held back by one unsupported claim, an unexplained centre ("one thing"), and heavy KJV for a global ear.

## TOP 3 FIXES

1. **Cut "called Martha close too."** It is not in the passage. Use that space to say plainly what the one needed thing is: listening to Him.
2. **Help the global ear with the KJV.** Add a short plain set-up before Martha's quote (she asked Him to tell Mary to help) and make "sat at His feet" mean "sat and listened." Then "bid her" and "needful" no longer carry the story alone.
3. **Be honest about the last verse.** Signal it comes from later / elsewhere (for example "Later the Bible says it plainly"), and either quote the full verse or make clear it continues. Do not let it look like the end of this scene.

## VERDICT: FIX

Small, surgical fixes. The shape is right; do not rewrite it.
