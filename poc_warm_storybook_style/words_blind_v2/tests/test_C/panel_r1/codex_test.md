1. NEWCOMER

WHO: Martha, an overwhelmed host, her sister Mary, and their guest Jesus.  
WHAT: Martha asks Jesus to make Mary help; Jesus addresses Martha’s worry and defends Mary’s choice.  
WHY: Someone can care about Martha without granting the help she asks for.

Likely first-hearing obstacles:

- “Lord”: Jesus’ role and the meaning of this title are unexplained.
- “Sat at His feet”: a newcomer may picture sitting on the floor without understanding that Mary is listening and learning.
- “Dost thou not care”: “Don’t you care?”
- “Hath left me to serve alone”: unfamiliar grammar; “serve” also leaves the actual work vague.
- “Bid her therefore that she help me”: “Tell her to help me.”
- “Thou art”: “You are.”
- “Careful”: initially sounds like “taking care.” The later explanation helps.
- “One thing is needful”: “One thing is necessary”—but which thing?
- “Hath chosen that good part”: what has Mary chosen? The narration never says she is hearing Jesus’ words.
- “Which shall not be taken away from her”: the protected thing remains abstract.
- “The meal would end”: this is the first mention of a meal.
- “Time with Him would last”: does this mean the visit, a memory, or something eternal?
- “Called Martha close too”: a newcomer may wonder when Jesus actually invited her over.

2. BIBLE READER

Yes—the repeated name and closing affirmation of Jesus’ love bring attention back to Martha as someone loved, although the invitation to “come close” is inferred.

3. CURIOSITY GAP

The opening asks: **Does Jesus care when I carry everything alone?** The ending answers through His stated love for Martha. It pays off emotionally, while leaving her practical burden unresolved. **8/10.**

4. THE 5 QUESTIONS

- **Who is this for?** An exhausted caregiver or dependable person who feels overlooked.
- **Main takeaway?** Jesus loves Martha even while correcting her, and He protects Mary’s choice to be with Him.
- **Global audience OK?** The situation travels well. The KJV dialogue needs clearer narrative context, especially that Mary is listening to Jesus.
- **Biblically accurate?**
  - Martha’s dialogue matches the supplied Luke 10:40 quotation word for word.
  - Jesus’ dialogue matches the supplied Luke 10:41–42 wording.
  - The explanation of “careful” as worried is sound.
  - The final John quotation is shortened. The supplied sentence is: “Now Jesus loved Martha, and her sister, and Lazarus.” Under the exact-quotation rule, restore it.
  - “She wanted Mary taken from His feet” is a reasonable inference, but makes Martha sound more opposed to Mary’s listening than simply desperate for help.
  - “He never called her work wrong” describes this exchange reasonably; “never” sounds broader than these verses establish.
  - A meal is plausible, but not specified.
  - “Time with Him would last” is an interpretation of the lasting “good part,” expressed as a stronger promise than the passage explicitly makes.
  - “Called Martha close too” is an inferred invitation, presented as an event. Jesus does not explicitly say this.
- **Best version possible—ONE change?** Replace the meal/time/invitation ending with an explanation grounded in Mary hearing His word. That would make the comfort feel earned from Luke itself.

5. ENDING

A newcomer might say, “Jesus loved Martha too, even though He corrected her”—a real small discovery, although the John quotation supplies most of its certainty. **7/10.**

6. TIGHTNESS

“His first words? Her name. Twice.” works aloud. “He never called her work wrong. He named her worry” slightly repeats the preceding explanation, but serves the intended viewer.

The main problem is compression: Mary’s listening has been cut, while several interpretations of the scene remain. “The meal would end. Time with Him would last” sounds polished but requires the listener to supply its meaning.

7. OVERALL

**7/10.** The central emotional question works; the explanation needs firmer grounding.

TOP 3 FIXES

1. Ground the closing comfort in what Jesus actually says; remove or qualify the lasting-time promise and explicit invitation to Martha.
2. Say that Mary sat at His feet **and heard His word**. This makes her choice understandable.
3. Restore the exact John 11:5 sentence, including Lazarus.

**Verdict: FIX.**
