# Blind test viewer review — test_B (Luke 10:38-42)

## 1. NEWCOMER (no Bible knowledge, English as a second language)

- **WHO:** Martha, a woman who lets Jesus into her home and does all the work, while her sister Mary sits and listens to Him.
- **WHAT:** Martha asks Jesus if He even cares that she works alone. He says her name twice, names her worry, and says Mary chose the one thing that lasts.
- **WHY:** If you carry everything for everyone and feel unseen, Jesus sees your worry and loves you; a seat near Him is open to you too.

A newcomer gets all three. The WHY is the weakest: it is carried by two short lines at the very end.

**Words or lines a newcomer would NOT understand on first hearing:**

- "dost thou not care" — old grammar. The narrator's "Does anyone care?" just before it rescues the meaning.
- "hath left me" / "hath chosen" — old verb form, guessable.
- "thou art" — old grammar.
- "careful" — means the opposite of today's sense. The film glosses it ("Careful means full of worry"). Good, but the gloss comes after the long quote, so the first hearing is still a stumble.
- "troubled" — less common word for ESL.
- "needful" — not a living English word. Not glossed.
- "that good part" — part of what? Not glossed directly.
- "which shall not be taken away from her" — long old sentence; the paraphrase after it helps.
- "sat at His feet" — idiom. May sound strange or servile without the picture. "Heard his word" (v39) is dropped, so the newcomer never hears that Mary was LISTENING.
- "got lost in serving" — idiom; ESL may hear "lost" literally.
- "Her meal would be gone by night" — no meal was mentioned before. The newcomer has to guess what Martha was serving.
- "That place was open to Martha too" — "that place" has no clear referent. The line before said "time", not a place.
- "Lord" — fine in context.

## 2. BIBLE READER

**Yes.** Two small things: "His answer? Her name. Twice." makes the reader notice the doubled name as tenderness, and answering Martha's "dost thou not care" with John 11:5 (where Martha is named FIRST) is a fresh link most readers have not made.

Caveat: a careful reader will notice the answer comes from a different book and a later story, and the film does not say so.

## 3. CURIOSITY GAP

- **Question opened:** "You do everything for everyone. Does anyone care?" — then, does JESUS care about the one doing the work?
- **Paid off?** Yes, directly and with Scripture: "Did He care? ... Now Jesus loved Martha." There is also a nice mini-gap in the middle ("His answer? Her name. Twice.").
- **Score: 8/10.** Clean open-and-close. It loses points because the opening question is "does ANYONE care" (people, family) and the answer is only "Jesus does" — fair, but the sister-helping question is quietly dropped, and the film never says Jesus did not send Mary to help.

## 4. THE 5 QUESTIONS

**Who is this for?** A tired carer / over-worker who feels alone and unseen. The first two sentences hit that viewer squarely. Clear.

**Main take-away:** Jesus did not scold Martha's work; He named her worry, and He loved her. Being near Him is the one thing that lasts, and it is open to the busy one too.

**Global audience OK?** Mostly yes. Narrator lines are short and plain. The risk is the 31-word KJV quote from Jesus: four hard items in a row (thou art / careful / needful / good part) and only "careful" is glossed by name. "Needful" and "good part" are explained only indirectly.

**Biblically accurate?** Quotes checked word for word against the KJV given:

- MARTHA: "Lord, dost thou not care that my sister hath left me to serve alone?" — exact match with Luke 10:40. Stops before "bid her therefore that she help me." A fair trim, but note it softens Martha: in the text she also tells Jesus what to do. Not unfair, slightly flattering.
- JESUS: "Martha, Martha, thou art careful and troubled about many things: But one thing is needful: and Mary hath chosen that good part, which shall not be taken away from her." — exact match with Luke 10:41-42.
- "Now Jesus loved Martha." — exact words of John 11:5, but cut mid-verse ("...and her sister, and Lazarus"). Presented as a full quote. The words are true; the cut is honest enough, but it is a truncation and it is from another book and another day. "The Bible answers" is true; it is not Luke answering.

Claims not in the text:

- "Her meal would be gone by night." The text says "much serving", never a meal and never "by night". Reasonable picture, but it is invented detail stated as fact.
- "Time close to Him, no one could take away." The text says "that good part". Verse 39 says Mary "heard his word". The film turns the good part into closeness/time and leaves out hearing His word. Soft drift, not an error.
- "That place was open to Martha too." Not stated in Luke 10. A fair inference from the gentle doubled name and from John 11:5, but it is the film's claim, not the text's.
- "He did not call her work wrong. He named her worry." Accurate reading of v41. Good.
- "Careful means full of worry." Correct.
- "got lost in serving" for "cumbered about much serving" — fair.

Unfair picture of Martha? No. She is treated with respect. Martha is not the villain.

Over-promise? No. No promise of help, rest or results. No guilt, no pressure. Grace-anchored.

**Best version possible? The ONE change:** fix the "That place was open to Martha too" line so it has a clear referent and lands on the viewer — e.g. tie it to the seat at His feet ("That seat at His feet was open to Martha too") — because it is the hinge between the passage and the tired viewer, and right now it is the vaguest line in the film.

## 5. ENDING

**What a newcomer would say back:** "She asked if He cared — and the Bible says He loved her."

**Discovery or slogan?** A real small discovery. It answers the exact question Martha asked with an exact verse, and it names Martha, not Mary. It is earned from Scripture, not a stock call to action. It stops short of a slogan.

Weakness: the answer comes from outside the passage with no signpost, and it answers "did He care" but leaves the viewer's own next step implied only.

**Ending score: 8/10.**

## 6. TIGHTNESS

- No line drags. About 115 words; nothing repeats wastefully.
- "Careful means full of worry. He did not call her work wrong. He named her worry." — "worry" twice in three short sentences. It works as emphasis; borderline.
- Cut too far: "heard his word" is gone, so Mary only sits. A newcomer may think Mary is simply resting or being lazy, which makes Jesus' answer feel less fair.
- Cut too far: "Her meal" arrives with no earlier mention of cooking or a meal.
- Unclear jump: "Time close to Him..." then "That place..." — time becomes place in one step.
- Missing beat: Martha's actual request (make Mary help) and the fact that Jesus does not grant it. The film skips the hardest part of the story. Acceptable for 60 seconds, but an honest viewer who knows the story will notice.

## 7. SCORE

**8/10 overall.**

Strong hook, exact KJV, fair to Martha, grace not pressure, an ending that is earned and fresh. Held back by three small clarity gaps and one unsignposted jump to John.

## TOP 3 FIXES

1. **Fix "That place was open to Martha too."** Give it a clear referent (the seat at His feet) and keep "time close to Him" and "place" as one image, not two. This is the hinge line for the tired viewer.
2. **Put the listening back and set up the meal.** One or two words each: Mary "sat at His feet and listened" (v39 "heard his word"), and Martha serving a meal earlier, so "Her meal would be gone by night" has something to point back to — or soften that line so it does not state an invented detail as fact.
3. **Signpost the John quote.** "The Bible answers" hides that this is a later story in another book. A few plain words ("Later, the Bible says it plainly:") keep it honest for the Bible reader and cost nothing for the newcomer. Optional alongside this: a plain gloss for "needful" / "good part".

## VERDICT: FIX

Small fixes only. The spine, hook, quotes and ending should stay as they are.
