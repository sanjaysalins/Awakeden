1. NEWCOMER

WHO: Martha, an overwhelmed woman hosting Jesus, and her sister Mary.
WHAT: Martha complains that Mary has left her working alone; Jesus addresses her worry and defends Mary’s choice to sit with Him.
WHY IT MATTERS: Jesus’ correction does not mean He has stopped caring about Martha.

Likely first-hearing obstacles:

- “Lord”: a title whose significance is unexplained.
- “Got lost in serving”: an idiom; “serving” could mean preparing food or helping people generally.
- “Sat at His feet”: the location is clear, but its meaning as listening and learning is missing.
- “Dost thou,” “hath,” “thou art,” “needful,” “hath chosen,” “shall not”: unfamiliar older English.
- “Careful”: initially sounds like being sensible or cautious. The later explanation helps.
- “That good part”: the central phrase remains undefined.
- “Which shall not be taken away from her”: what exactly cannot be taken away?
- “Time close to Him”: physical closeness, listening, prayer, or a relationship?
- “That place was open to Martha too”: a literal seat or an invitation to belong?

2. BIBLE READER

No substantial new learning, but a useful fresh emphasis: Jesus repeats Martha’s name, and John explicitly names Martha as someone He loved.

3. CURIOSITY GAP

The opening asks: “Does Jesus care when I am overwhelmed and nobody helps?”

The ending explicitly answers the care question through Jesus’ love for Martha. It leaves the practical complaint about help unresolved, as the passage does. Some viewers may feel their need for help has been translated into a problem with worry.

Score: 8/10.

4. THE 5 QUESTIONS

- Who is this for? Someone exhausted by responsibility who feels unseen, particularly someone who fears that admitting resentment makes them a bad believer.
- Main takeaway? Jesus sees Martha’s worry and loves her; His correction is compatible with that love.
- Global audience OK? Mostly. The modern narration is accessible, but the uninterrupted KJV speech contains the hardest language and the central meaning. Pictures can show the sisters’ activities, but cannot fully explain “that good part.”
- Biblically accurate?
  - Martha’s quotation matches the supplied KJV word for word through “alone?” It omits “bid her therefore that she help me.” This is an accurate excerpt, but removing her request weakens the practical substance of her complaint.
  - Jesus’ quotation matches Luke 10:41–42 word for word.
  - “Now Jesus loved Martha” is an exact excerpt from John 11:5, not the complete verse.
  - Opening her home, being burdened with serving, and Mary sitting at His feet are supported.
  - “Careful means full of worry” is an appropriate explanation here.
  - “He did not call her work wrong” is a fair observation, though His answer also establishes a priority.
  - “Her meal would be gone by night” is invented detail. Neither a meal nor that deadline appears in the supplied text.
  - “Time close to Him, no one could take away” substitutes an interpretation for “that good part.” Luke specifically says Mary heard His word; the film leaves that out.
  - “That place was open to Martha too” is a reasonable inference, not an explicit statement.
  - Martha is treated sympathetically. The main risk is implying that her unmet need for help is adequately answered by reassurance about worry.
- Best version possible—ONE change? Make Mary’s listening to Jesus explicit and build the explanation around it, replacing the unsupported meal-versus-time comparison.

5. ENDING

A newcomer might say: “Jesus loved Martha even when He corrected her worry.”

That is a real small discovery: correction need not mean rejection. The final quotation earns that emotional turn, although it draws its decisive evidence from John rather than Luke.

Score: 8/10.

6. TIGHTNESS

“His answer? Her name. Twice.” creates a strong pause, though it briefly makes His answer sound gentler and simpler than the full correction.

“Her meal would be gone by night. Time close to Him, no one could take away” is the weakest stretch: it adds an unsupported contrast and requires interpretation.

The story cuts too far when it omits that Mary “heard his word.” Without that, a newcomer can hear a story in which sitting is praised while necessary work goes unsupported.

7. OVERALL

7/10.

TOP 3 FIXES

1. Restore Mary’s listening to Jesus and explain “that good part” through that detail.
2. Remove the invented meal deadline and the claim that the enduring gift is simply time spent close to Him.
3. Restore Martha’s request that Mary help her, preserving the full weight of her complaint.

Verdict: FIX.
