# Cold test: Luke 10:38-42 narration (Martha)

## 1. Newcomer (no Bible knowledge, English is a second language)

- **Who:** A tired woman named Martha, and Jesus, who is a guest in her home.
- **What:** She is working alone while her sister sits and listens. She asks Jesus to make her sister help. He says her name twice, and says she is worried about too much when only one thing is needed.
- **Why it matters:** If you feel nobody cares, Jesus cared about the tired one. He loved her by name, and He was right there in her house.

**Words or lines a newcomer would not get on first hearing:**
- "dost thou not care", "hath left me", "bid her therefore". The pictures and the narrator's set-up help, so the meaning still gets through.
- "troubled about many things". "Troubled" is never explained; only "careful" is.
- "Mary hath chosen that good part". "Good part" is never explained. "The one thing… was Him, and His word" only half covers it.
- "which shall not be taken away from her". This goes past with no help.
- "The book of John". Who is John? A newcomer won't know this is another book about the same family.
- "Lazarus". A name with no person behind it.
- "Martha, named first". A newcomer won't see why the order of names matters. The point is too quiet.
- **"Look who was sitting in her house."** This is the biggest risk. The story has just told us that *Mary* sat. So a cold listener can hear "who was sitting" as Mary, the sister who wasn't helping. That flips the ending. The text never says Jesus sat.

## 2. Bible reader

**Yes, a little.** Three things are worth having: "careful" means full of worry; "Martha, Martha" comes before any correction; and Martha is named first in John 11:5. None of it is deep, but it's honest and not commonly noticed.

## 3. Curiosity gap

- **Question opened:** "Does anyone even care?" It's sharp, personal, and hooks the target viewer in 2 seconds.
- **Paid off?** Yes. The film asks "So, did He care?" and answers it with John 11:5 and the "not alone" turn. The payoff is weakened only by the unclear last line.
- **Score: 7/10.**

## 4. The 5 questions

- **Who is this for:** The worn-out person who does everything for everyone. The opening speaks to them directly.
- **Main take-away:** Jesus cares about the tired one. He named her worry, not her work, and she was never alone.
- **Global audience OK?** Mostly. The narrator's lines are plain. The KJV wording is quoted once and partly explained. The loose ends are "good part", "Lazarus" and the John cross-reference.
- **Biblically accurate?**
  - **Quotes:** Martha (10:40), Jesus (10:41-42) and John 11:5 all match the KJV given, word for word. No problem.
  - **"Tired woman" / "the tired one":** These are inferences from "cumbered about much serving". Fair, but not stated in the text.
  - **"It was Him, and His word":** An interpretation, drawn from 10:39 "heard his word". Reasonable, but the film says it as plain fact.
  - **"He never called her work wrong":** True to the words. The contrast with "that good part" is still implied, though, and the film hides nothing, so this is fine.
  - **"Look who was sitting in her house":** The text gives Mary as the one sitting, not Jesus. As a description of Jesus, this line is slightly inaccurate as well as unclear.
  - **Martha and over-promise:** Martha is not made the villain, and there's no guilt. Nothing is over-promised.
- **Best version possible? One change:** Rewrite the last line so it can only mean Jesus. For example: "She said she was alone. Look who was in her house." Or name Him outright: "She said she was alone. Jesus was in her house."

## 5. Overall score: 7.5/10

## Top 3 fixes

1. **Last line.** Drop "sitting". It points the listener to Mary, and the text doesn't say Jesus sat. Make it clear the "who" is Jesus.
2. **John bridge.** Add 3-4 plain words for the newcomer, e.g. "Another book about this same family says it plainly…", so "John" and "Lazarus" aren't left hanging. Also put a little weight on "named first" so the point lands.
3. **"That good part."** Give it one short plain gloss, or let the "one thing… Him, and His word" line say outright that Mary chose to be with Him. Right now the key phrase of the quote goes past unexplained.

**Verdict: FIX.** The fixes are small, and the last line is the one that matters.
