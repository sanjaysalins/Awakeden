**1. NEWCOMER**

WHO: A tired woman named Martha, and Jesus.
WHAT: She welcomes Jesus and works hard while her sister Mary sits and listens. Martha asks him for help. He says her name twice, says she is full of worry, and says Mary chose the one thing that matters. Then we hear that Jesus loved Martha.
WHY: If you do everything and feel no one cares, Jesus sees you and loves you. Your work is still good. What you need most is him.

Words and lines that fail on one hearing:

- "hath left me"
- "bid her therefore that she help me"
- "thou art careful and troubled about many things" ("careful" is only explained after)
- "one thing is needful" ("needful" is only explained after)
- "that good part, which shall not be taken away from her"
- "pulled in every direction"
- "another job" (sounds like paid work, not hosting)
- "the book of John"

- "Martha, named first"
- "Look who was sitting in her house" (who?)

**2. BIBLE READER**

Yes. He does not call her serving wrong. He names her worry. Tying John 11:5 to the woman who asked "dost thou not care" is a real point. It is a different chapter. In John 11:1 Mary is named first, so "named first" is only the word order of the love verse.

**3. CURIOSITY GAP**

The opening asks: you do everything for everyone. Does anyone care? The film turns that into: did Jesus care about Martha? It pays off with her name said twice, then the exact line "Now Jesus loved Martha, and her sister, and Lazarus." The last line blurs the picture. **8/10.**

**4. THE 5 QUESTIONS**

Who for: The worn-out helper. The first line speaks straight to that person.
Main take-away: Jesus cared about Martha. He named her worry. He left her work honored. The one thing was him and his word. She was not left alone.
Global audience: The narrator's English is plain and works. The KJV middle is hard. "Good part" and "bid her" never get a plain gloss. "Lazarus" and "named first" will not land.
Biblically accurate: The three quotes match the KJV you gave, word for word. "Twice" sits between Jesus's words and does not change them. Fair plain readings: tired, opened her home, came for help, careful = worry, needful = needed. "The one thing was Him, and His word" is the sense of the scene, said more firmly than the verse. Soft spots: she said she was left to serve alone, and the film shortens that to "alone." Mary is the one who sat. Jesus is not said to sit. "Named first" is a reading of word order, not a claim the verse makes. Martha stays honored. The film does not pile on guilt. It does not promise that someone else will take her work. The love line is labeled as the book of John. It is a later line, not his answer in the kitchen.
ONE change: Replace "Look who was sitting in her house" with a plain line that names Jesus and stays on her words. She was left to serve alone. Jesus was in her house, and he loved her.

**5. SCORE**

**7/10.**

**TOP 3 FIXES**

1. Fix the last line. "Sitting" is Mary's verb. Heard one way, Jesus is with her. Heard the other way, the ending points at Mary and scolds Martha. Name Jesus.
2. Add one plain sentence at the climax, in the same style as the "careful" and "needful" glosses. "Good part" means staying with him to hear him. "Bid her" means "tell her to help me."
3. Do not spend the landing on "named first." Say who Lazarus is in a few plain words, then leave the exact quote alone. A stranger's name arrives at the moment of comfort.
