# Blind test viewer — Fable seat — Luke 10:38-42 (Martha)

Read once, cold, as a phone viewer. Judged as written.

## 1. NEWCOMER (no Bible knowledge, English as a second language)

- **WHO:** A tired woman called Martha, who does all the work in her house, and Jesus, her guest.
- **WHAT:** She asks Jesus if He cares that her sister left her to do the work alone. He answers by saying her name twice, names her worry, and says only one thing is needed.
- **WHY:** If you are worn out and feel nobody cares, Jesus does care. He loved Martha, and He was right there in her house.

A newcomer gets who / what / why. The why lands, but only just (see the last line, below).

**Words or lines a newcomer would NOT get on first hearing:**

- "dost thou not care" / "hath left me" / "bid her therefore" — old English. The narrator's set-up ("So Martha came to Him for help") carries the meaning, so this survives.
- "thou art careful and troubled" — glossed after, good. But "troubled" is not glossed (minor).
- "that good part" — never explained. What part? Part of what?
- "which shall not be taken away from her" — hard grammar, and no gloss. A newcomer drops this whole clause.
- "sat at His feet" — may sound odd or servile to some cultures. The picture will probably fix it.
- "pulled in every direction" — an idiom. Most will get it; some will not.
- "The book of John" — a newcomer does not know this is another book of the Bible, or that it is a later day.
- "Lazarus" — a new name with no meaning here. It dangles.
- "Martha, named first." — a newcomer cannot tell why being first in a list matters.
- **"Look who was sitting in her house."** — the biggest risk. The only person the film said was *sitting* is Mary ("her sister Mary sat at His feet"). A cold listener can hear the answer as "Mary", which turns the ending upside down.

## 2. BIBLE READER

**Yes.** Two things most readers have not put together: His first word to her complaint was her own name, twice; and John 11:5 names Martha first, which answers her own question "dost thou not care?" with "Jesus loved Martha". That is a real, fresh link.

## 3. CURIOSITY GAP

- **Question opened:** "You do everything for everyone. Does anyone even care?" — then, "Did Jesus care about her?"
- **Paid off?** Yes. "So, did He care?" is asked out loud and answered with an exact verse. The pay-off is in the text, not invented.
- **One hole:** she asked Him to send Mary to help. The film never says He did not. A sharp viewer notices the request is quietly dropped.
- **Score: 8/10.**

## 4. THE 5 QUESTIONS

**Who is this for?**
The worn-out helper who feels unseen. The first line hits that person hard and fast. Clear.

**Main take-away?**
He sees you, He knows your name, and He is closer than you think. Your work is not the problem; your worry is what He names. It comes through. But the film never turns back to "you" after line one, so the viewer has to make the last step alone.

**Global audience OK?**
Mostly yes. Narrator English is plain and short. The two glosses ("careful", "needful") are the right two. Weak spots: "that good part ... shall not be taken away" is left bare, and "the book of John" / "Lazarus" assume Bible knowledge.

**Biblically accurate?**

Quotes checked word for word against the KJV given:

- Martha's line (Luke 10:40) — **exact.**
- "Martha, Martha," + "thou art careful and troubled about many things: But one thing is needful: and Mary hath chosen that good part, which shall not be taken away from her." (Luke 10:41-42) — **exact.** The narrator's "Twice." cuts into the verse but changes no word.
- "Now Jesus loved Martha, and her sister, and Lazarus." (John 11:5) — **exact.**

Claims checked:

- "A tired woman" — the text says "cumbered about much serving", not tired. Fair reading, not a quote. OK.
- "She had opened her home to Him" — matches "received him into her house". OK.
- "His first words? Her name. Twice." — true to v.41.
- "He never called her work wrong. He named her worry." — true. He names "careful and troubled", not the serving.
- "The one thing was not another job. It was Him, and His word." — this is interpretation. The text does not define the one thing. It is the mainstream reading and v.39 ("heard his word") supports it, but it is stated as flat fact. Low risk.
- "Martha, named first. The tired one. The one who asked." — true of John 11:5. But it hints she is first *because* she was tired and asked. The text does not say why. Also John 11:1, in the same pack, names Mary before Martha. Small cherry-pick risk; a hostile commenter could raise it.
- **"She said she was alone."** — not quite. She said her sister left her "to serve alone". She did not say she was alone. Small stretch, and the ending leans on it.
- "Look who was sitting in her house." — the text never says Jesus was sitting. Mary sat. See the newcomer note.
- John 11:5 is from a later event. The film says "The book of John says it plainly", which is honest, but does not say it is another day.

Martha: **fair.** She is not the villain. She is the one who opened her home, the one named first, the one loved. Good.

Grace not pressure: **yes.** No guilt, no "you should". No over-promise, as long as the ending is heard as "Jesus was there" and not "so you are never alone, so stop complaining".

**Best version possible? The ONE change:**
Fix the last line so it cannot point at Mary and does not misquote Martha. For example: "She felt left alone. Look who was in her house." (Drop "sitting". Change "said she was alone" to match the text.)

## 5. SCORE

**8 / 10 overall.**

Strong hook, real pay-off from the text, exact KJV, Martha treated with honour. Held back by an ending that can be misheard, one small misquote of what Martha said, and an unexplained clause.

## TOP 3 FIXES

1. **The last line.** "Sitting" points at Mary, the only one the film showed sitting. And Martha did not say she was alone; she said she was left to serve alone. Reword so the answer can only be Jesus, and so it matches her words.
2. **"that good part, which shall not be taken away from her"** — add one plain line. For example: "What Mary chose, nobody could take from her." Right now a newcomer loses the end of Jesus' answer.
3. **The John bridge.** Make it one step easier and safer: say it is later ("Later, the Bible says it plainly") and soften "named first" so it does not claim a reason the text does not give. Optional but honest: one short beat that He did not send Mary — it closes the dropped request.

## VERDICT

**FIX** — small word fixes only. The structure, hook and pay-off should stay as they are.
