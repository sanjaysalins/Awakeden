1. **NEWCOMER**

WHO: Martha, a woman hosting Jesus, who is overwhelmed while her sister Mary listens to Him.  
WHAT: Martha asks Jesus to make Mary help; He addresses Martha’s worry and defends Mary’s listening.  
WHY: Feeling unsupported does not mean Jesus does not love you, though Martha receives no stated practical help.

First-hearing obstacles:

- **Jesus / Lord:** His identity and authority are assumed.
- **“Opened her home”:** Welcomed Him as a guest.
- **“Pulled in every direction”:** An idiom for having too much to manage.
- **“Sat at His feet”:** The significance of listening as a learner is unstated.
- **“Serve”:** Hosting and household work could be clearer.
- **“Dost thou,” “hath,” “bid her,” “thou art,” “shall”:** Archaic language; the request may be difficult to follow.
- **“Careful” / “needful”:** Explained afterward, but initially confusing.
- **“That good part, which shall not be taken away”:** What “part” means, and what cannot be taken away, remain unclear.
- **“His word”:** Means His teaching.
- **“The book of John” / “Lazarus”:** A new source and an unexplained person arrive near the ending.
- **“Martha, named first”:** The intended significance is unclear.
- **“Look who was sitting in her house”:** Jesus’ presence is clear; how that answers being left with the work is less clear.

2. **BIBLE READER**

Yes, modestly: noticing Martha named first in John’s explicit statement of Jesus’ love gives a familiar passage a useful connection.

3. **CURIOSITY GAP**

The opening asks, “Does Jesus care when I do everything and nobody helps?” John’s statement answers the love question directly. The practical-help question remains unresolved, and the ending partly blurs that distinction. **8/10.**

4. **THE 5 QUESTIONS**

- **Who is this for?** An exhausted caregiver, host, parent, or person carrying everyone else’s responsibilities.
- **Main takeaway?** Jesus sees Martha’s worry and loves her; listening to Him has value that constant serving must not crowd out.
- **Global audience OK?** Mostly. The domestic situation travels well. The KJV dialogue creates a substantial listening barrier; only two difficult words receive explanations.
- **Biblically accurate?** Every spoken Scripture quotation matches the supplied KJV word for word. “Tired” is an inference from her burden and worry. Identifying the “one thing” as Jesus and His word is a supported interpretation, rather than an explicit definition in verse 42. “He never called her work wrong” is fair within this scene; “never” sounds broader than the evidence. John names Martha first, but gives no significance to that order. Jesus sitting is not expressly stated. Martha is treated sympathetically, and no practical rescue is promised. However, she says she is **serving alone**; the ending turns that into being alone generally, which weakens the fairness toward her complaint.
- **Best version possible—one change?** Replace the last two sentences with an ending that preserves her practical burden: **“She was serving alone. She was still loved.”**

5. **OVERALL: 7/10**

**TOP 3 FIXES**

1. Fix the ending’s shift from “serving alone” to “being alone.” His presence does not make her workload complaint disappear.
2. Help listeners understand “that good part,” the central expression in Jesus’ answer. Explaining “needful” as “needed” adds less.
3. Remove the implied significance of “named first.” The explicit statement that Jesus loved Martha already carries the reassurance.

**VERDICT: FIX.**
