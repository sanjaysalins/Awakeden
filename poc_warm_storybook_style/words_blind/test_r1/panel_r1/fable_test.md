# Blind test viewer review — Luke 10:38-42, Film A vs Film B

## FILM A

### 1. NEWCOMER
- WHO: A woman called Martha, who does all the work, her sister Mary, and Jesus, a guest in her house.
- WHAT: Martha is buried in serving, her sister just sits and listens, so Martha asks Jesus "don't you care?" He answers with her name, twice, names her worry, and says only one thing is needed.
- WHY: If you carry everything and feel alone, Jesus sees it, and He is already in the room with you.

Would NOT be understood on first hearing:
- "dost thou not care", "hath left me", "thou art" — old English. The narrator's echo "Don't you care?" rescues the first one. Good.
- "troubled" — not explained (only "careful" is).
- "needful" — never explained. This is the key word of the whole film.
- "sat at Jesus' feet" — a newcomer may not know this means "listening as a learner".
- "Lord" — why does she call Him that?
- "pulled her in every direction" — an idiom; a second-language viewer may picture it literally.
- "went straight to Him" — mild idiom.
- "And her question, don't you care?" — a sentence fragment. By ear it is hard to tell this is being re-asked.
- "Later, John wrote" — who is John? Later than what?
- "Her name comes first." — first in WHAT? The quote stops at "Martha", so the viewer never hears the list. This line cannot land.
- "Look who was sitting in her house." — lovely, but it asks the newcomer to do the last step alone. Some will not.
- The last narrator block carries six ideas in a row (Him / the place was open / work not wrong / worry named / John's line / who was in the house). Too many for one hearing.

### 2. BIBLE READER
Yes. Tying her "dost thou not care" to John 11:5, with her name first, is a real find. "He never called her work wrong. He named her worry" is a fair, fresh reading.

### 3. CURIOSITY GAP
Opens: "You do everything and nobody helps — what did Jesus say to a woman like that?" It is more a feeling than a question. The real question ("does He care?") arrives at the midpoint and is paid off, but softly and by hint. Score: 6.5/10.

### 4. THE 5 QUESTIONS
- **Who is this for:** Exactly the intended viewer. The first two lines are their own inner voice. Best targeting of the two.
- **Main take-away:** You were never alone in the work; the One you need is already in your house, and your place near Him is open.
- **Global audience OK?** Mostly. Weak spots: "needful" unexplained, the idioms, the crowded ending.
- **Biblically accurate:**
  - Martha's line: word for word, cut before "bid her therefore that she help me". Fair cut, but it drops her actual request.
  - "Martha, Martha, thou art careful and troubled about many things:" — word for word.
  - "But one thing is needful:" — word for word.
  - "Now Jesus loved Martha." — the words are exact, but the verse goes on ("and her sister, and Lazarus"). The full stop makes a part-verse sound like a whole sentence.
  - "Jesus came to her village" — text says "a certain village". Fine.
  - "that place was open to Martha too" — NOT in the text. A reasonable inference, but it is stated as fact.
  - "Not one more job. Him." — an interpretation. The text points at what Mary chose (sitting, hearing His word). Sound, but it is a reading, not a quote.
  - "She said she was alone" — she said "left me to SERVE alone". Slight stretch.
  - "who was sitting in her house" — the text never says Jesus sat. Tiny.
  - Leaves out the second half of verse 42 ("that good part, which shall not be taken away from her"). The passage's own closing promise is missing.
  - "Later" — John 11 is a different event; "later" is loose but not wrong.
  - Picture of Martha: fair and kind. The best of the two. No over-promise.
- **Best version possible? ONE change:** Finish the John quote ("...and her sister, and Lazarus") so "Her name comes first" makes sense, and cut "that place was open to Martha too" to make room.

### 5. SCORE: 7/10

## FILM B

### 1. NEWCOMER
- WHO: A tired woman called Martha, her sister Mary, and Jesus, a guest in her home.
- WHAT: Martha serves while Mary listens. Martha asks Jesus to His face if He cares. He answers first with her name, twice, names her worry, says one thing is needed.
- WHY: He did care — He loved her — and He knows the viewer's name too.

Would NOT be understood on first hearing:
- "dost thou not care", "hath left me", "thou art" — old English. Rescued by the opening, which already told us the question.
- "troubled" — not explained.
- "needful" — never explained.
- "sat at His feet" — custom not explained.
- "Lord".
- "pulled in every direction" — idiom.
- "Not a rule." — a newcomer does not know why a rule would be expected.
- "Mary had chosen that, and Jesus said it 'shall not be taken away.'" — "that" and "it" are vague by ear. Chosen what? Taken from whom?
- "Later, John wrote one plain line" — who is John?
- "Her name comes first." — same flaw as A. The list is never heard, so "first" has nothing to be first in.
- Everything else is short and clear. The ending is easy to get in one hearing.

### 2. BIBLE READER
Yes. Same John 11:5 find, plus "the first thing He says is not a rule, it is her name" — a small thing most readers pass over.

### 3. CURIOSITY GAP
Opens: "Does anyone even care? A woman asked Jesus that to His face — what did He say?" A real question, with a bit of danger in it. Re-asked out loud ("So, did He care?") and answered with a verse. Clean open, clean close. Score: 8/10.

### 4. THE 5 QUESTIONS
- **Who is this for:** A tired person who feels unseen. Wider than the brief; it loses the sharp "you do everything for everyone" hit that A has.
- **Main take-away:** He cared. He answered her with her name, and He knows yours.
- **Global audience OK?** Yes, better than A. Shorter lines, one idea each. Same "needful" gap.
- **Biblically accurate:**
  - Martha's line: word for word, same fair cut.
  - "Martha, Martha," / "thou art careful and troubled about many things:" — word for word, split by the narrator's "Twice." Words intact.
  - "But one thing is needful:" — word for word.
  - "shall not be taken away" — exact fragment. Good that the promise is in.
  - "Now Jesus loved Martha." — exact words, part-verse with a full stop (same note as A).
  - "Does anyone even care?" — Martha asked if HE cared, not "anyone". Slight widening.
  - "A tired woman" / "The tired one" — "tired" is not in the text ("cumbered about much serving"). Fair inference, but it is said twice as a label.
  - "Him, sitting in her own house" — interpretation; and "sitting" is not in the text.
  - "He knows yours too." — NOT from this passage. True elsewhere in Scripture, but here it is a step beyond what the film has shown. Mild over-promise; the house rule says the ending must be earned from the passage.
  - Picture of Martha: kind, not a villain. But it never says her work was not wrong, so "Not one more job" can sound like a small telling-off.
- **Best version possible? ONE change:** Add A's line "He never called her work wrong. He named her worry." before the John quote. It protects Martha and the worn-out viewer.

### 5. SCORE: 7.5/10

---

## WHICH FILM WINS

**Film B, narrowly.** It asks one clear question, pays it off out loud, and a second-language viewer can follow every step in one hearing.
Film A has the better first two lines, the kinder picture of Martha, and the more earned last line — but its ending is crowded and leaves the newcomer to work it out.

## TOP 3 FIXES (most important first)

1. **Both: "Her name comes first" does not work.** The quote stops at "Martha", so nobody hears a list. Quote the verse on: "Now Jesus loved Martha, and her sister, and Lazarus." Then the line lands, and the quote is whole.
2. **Both: "needful" is never explained.** It is the key word. Add a short gloss, like the "careful" one: "Needful means needed."
3. **Graft A's best into B.** Open with A's "You do everything for everyone. And nobody helps." Add "He never called her work wrong. He named her worry." Then choose one ending: A's "She said she was alone. Look who was sitting in her house" is earned from the passage; B's "He knows yours too" is not. If B's line stays, tie it to the text (He said her name; He was in her house).
