FILM A

1. NEWCOMER
WHO: Martha, a woman who does all the work, and Jesus, who comes to her house.
WHAT: Her sister Mary sits and listens. Martha asks Jesus if he cares that she serves alone. He says her name twice and says one thing matters.
WHY: She feels alone, but he sees her worry, and he is already in her house.
Hard on a first hear:
- "dost thou," "hath," "thou art"
- "careful" (the next line does explain it)
- "needful"
- "that place was open to Martha too" (what place?)
- "John" (no one says who John is)
- "Her name comes first" (first in what?)
- "And her question, don't you care?" (the sentence does not finish)
- "Look who was sitting" (the "who" is easy to miss)

2. BIBLE READER
Yes. One useful point: he does not call her work wrong. He names her worry. Then John 11:5 puts Martha's name first in the love line. A reader will also notice the film stops before Jesus finishes.

3. CURIOSITY GAP
Opens: I do everything, and nobody helps. Does anyone see me?
Payoff: part way. His double name, "Jesus loved Martha," and "look who was in her house" point to an answer. The film then repeats "don't you care?" and does not say a clear yes.
Score: 6

4. THE 5 QUESTIONS
Who it is for: the tired person who serves everyone and feels unseen. The first line speaks to that person.
Main take-away: your work is not the problem he names. He names the worry. One thing is needed. He was already there, and he loved Martha.
Global audience: the story spine is plain. The old Bible lines, the unfinished question, "John," and "comes first" will lose people.
Biblically accurate: the quoted pieces match the KJV, but they are cut. Missing from her mouth: "bid her therefore that she help me." Missing from his mouth: "and Mary hath chosen that good part, which shall not be taken away from her."
Claims the text does not make:
- "that place was open to Martha too"
- the one thing is "Him" (the text says Mary chose "that good part," which is sitting at his feet and hearing his word)
- "Her name comes first" needs the rest of John 11:5, which is not spoken. John 11:5 also loves her sister and Lazarus. Word order is not a rank.
- "sitting" is a picture. The verse says she received him into her house.
Martha is not made the villain. "He never called her work wrong" is a fair silence. No promise that someone will start helping, and no promise about the viewer's name.
Best single change: say the rest of Luke 10:42 in KJV, then one plain line. Mary chose the good part, at his feet, hearing his word, and it shall not be taken away from her.

5. SCORE: 6

FILM B

1. NEWCOMER
WHO: Martha, a tired woman who opened her home, and Jesus.
WHAT: She asks him, to his face, if he cares. He says her name twice before he talks about her worry. One thing is needed.
WHY: The film answers her question. He loved Martha, the one who asked, and the last line says he knows your name too.
Hard on a first hear:
- "dost thou," "hath," "thou art"
- "careful" (the next line explains it)
- "needful"
- "Him, sitting in her own house. Mary had chosen that" (who chose what?)
- "shall not be taken away" (taken from whom, and what is "it"?)
- "John"
- "Her name comes first"

2. BIBLE READER
Yes, one sharp true point: the first words are her name, twice, before any correction. The same hear teaches a wrong join. Mary chose the good part. She did not choose "him sitting in the house." Those quoted words belong to "that good part," and they include "from her."

3. CURIOSITY GAP
Opens: Does anyone even care?
Payoff: yes, in plain words. "So, did He care?" is answered by the love line and by her name. The answer sits on a later verse, plus a step the passage does not take.
Score: 8

4. THE 5 QUESTIONS
Who it is for: the person who feels uncared for, shown as the tired woman who serves. It fits. It is a little wider than "I do everything and nobody helps."
Main take-away: he cared. He said her name first. The one needed thing is him, and he knows your name too.
Global audience: the best spine of the two. Short words. A clear question and a clear answer. The middle still tangles "that," "it," and the cut quote.
Biblically accurate: "Martha, Martha, thou art careful and troubled about many things" and "But one thing is needful" match the KJV. Her line is exact but cut off before "bid her therefore that she help me."
The real miss: "Jesus said it 'shall not be taken away.'" The KJV says, "Mary hath chosen that good part, which shall not be taken away from her." The film hangs that fragment on "Him, sitting in her own house." That is not what he said. Mary sat at his feet and heard his word. Martha is the one who received him into the house.
Also beyond the text:
- the one thing equals "Him"
- "Her name comes first," while the spoken quote hides "and her sister, and Lazarus"
- "He knows yours too." Love for Martha in John 11:5 is not a statement that he knows the viewer's name. That is an over-promise.
Martha is not mocked. The film never says her work was wrong, and it also never says the work was honored. She stays "the tired one, the one who asked." No guilt. The pressure risk is the personal promise at the end.
Best single change: keep his words on Mary's good part. One plain line: she chose to sit and hear him, and he said that good part shall not be taken away from her.

5. SCORE: 7

WHICH FILM WINS
Film B wins. On one listen, a tired person gets one question and one answer: he said her name, and he loved the woman who asked.
Film A is fairer to Martha's work and adds less, but the ending breaks up, so the same person can miss the answer. B still has a real quote error. It is ahead, and it is not ready.

TOP 3 FIXES
1. Put Luke 10:42 back in full, KJV and plain. Mary chose the good part: she sat at his feet and heard his word, and it shall not be taken away from her. Remove A's "that place was open to Martha." Remove B's join of those words to "him sitting in her house."
2. If you say "her name comes first," speak all of John 11:5: "Now Jesus loved Martha, and her sister, and Lazarus." If you only need that he loved her, drop "comes first."
3. Say her real ask in plain words: she wanted Jesus to send Mary to help. Keep A's mercy line, that he named her worry and did not call the work wrong. Drop B's "He knows yours too."

VERDICT: B WINS, 7 TO 6. DO NOT LOCK UNTIL FIX 1.
