# Panel review: Film A vs Film B (Luke 10:38-42)

## FILM A

**1. Newcomer**
- **Who:** Martha, a busy woman who has Jesus as a guest in her home.
- **What:** She does all the work alone. She complains to Jesus. He answers her kindly, using her name twice.
- **Why it matters:** If you feel alone and overworked, Jesus has not left you. He is already there.

Words or lines a newcomer would not catch on first hearing:
- "dost thou not care," "hath left me," "thou art," "troubled," "needful." "Careful" is explained, which helps.
- "sat at Jesus' feet." A newcomer won't know this was the place a student sat.
- "Not one more job. Him." The listener has to work out that "one thing" means Jesus.
- "Later, John wrote." Who is John? It sounds like a new person in the story.
- "Her name comes first." First in what? The film quotes only "Now Jesus loved Martha," so the list her name heads is never heard. This line is lost on first hearing.
- "And her question, don't you care?" This sentence is never finished. A listener waits for the rest, and it doesn't come.

**2. Bible reader:** Yes. It joins Luke 10 to John 11:5, and it notes that He "named her worry" but never called her work wrong. That framing is fresh and fair.

**3. Curiosity gap:** The opening is more "this is you" than a question. It hooks the target viewer on feeling, not on curiosity. The real question, "Don't you care?", arrives in the middle. The ending answers it with a strong image ("Look who was sitting in her house"), but the fragment before it blurs the answer. **Score: 5/10.**

**4. The 5 questions**
- **Who for:** The worn-out person who does everything. The first line names them directly. Good.
- **Main take-away:** You are not alone. Jesus is in your house, and He loves you by name.
- **Global audience OK?** Mostly. The two blind spots are "Her name comes first" and "John wrote."
- **Biblically accurate:**
  - Luke 10:40 matches the KJV exactly (cut before "bid her therefore…", which is fine).
  - Luke 10:41 and 10:42a match exactly.
  - "Now Jesus loved Martha" is cut short of the full verse, but the film still says "Her name comes first," a claim about a list the viewer never hears. Quote the full verse or drop the claim.
  - "That place was open to Martha too" is not in the text. It is a fair inference, and it protects Martha.
  - "Him" as the one thing is a common reading, not something the text spells out.
  - "She said she was alone" stretches her words a little. She said she was left to *serve* alone.
  - Martha is not made the villain. There is no over-promise.
- **Best version (one change):** Finish or cut the "And her question, don't you care?" fragment so the ending lands on one clear answer.

**5. Overall: 6.5/10**

## FILM B

**1. Newcomer**
- **Who:** Martha, a tired woman serving Jesus in her home.
- **What:** She asks Jesus if He cares. He answers with her name, twice, and points her to Himself. Later we hear plainly that Jesus loved her.
- **Why it matters:** Yes, He cares, and He knows you by name.

Words or lines a newcomer would not catch on first hearing:
- "dost thou," "hath," "thou art," "troubled," "needful."
- "sat at His feet."
- "shall not be taken away." Taken away from whom? It can sound like Mary keeps something good and Martha loses out.
- "Later, John wrote." Same problem as Film A.
- "Her name comes first." Same problem as Film A: the list isn't quoted, so the line doesn't make sense.

**2. Bible reader:** Yes. "Not a rule. Her name" is a good noticing, and the John 11:5 payoff tells them something too.

**3. Curiosity gap:** "Does anyone even care?" is a clear, universal question. "So, did He care?" brings it back on purpose, and John 11:5 answers it. The loop is clean. **Score: 8/10.**

**4. The 5 questions**
- **Who for:** Anyone who feels unseen. That is a little wider than the worn-out helper, but they are still caught by "a tired woman."
- **Main take-away:** Jesus cares, and He knows your name.
- **Global audience OK?** Yes. The short lines and the pause on "Twice." help a second-language listener.
- **Biblically accurate:**
  - All the Luke quotes are word for word; splitting 10:41 across two lines is fine.
  - "shall not be taken away" matches the KJV.
  - John 11:5 has the same cut-short problem as Film A, again with "Her name comes first."
  - "Tired" is a fair reading of "cumbered."
  - Risk: B praises Mary's choice but never says that place was open to Martha too. A careless listener could hear "Mary good, Martha less."
  - "He knows yours too" goes beyond this passage. It is true elsewhere in the Bible, but here it is not earned, and it sounds like a stock line.
- **Best version (one change):** Add A's "that place was open to Martha too" after "shall not be taken away."

**5. Overall: 7.5/10**

## WHICH FILM WINS

**Film B.** Its question opens in line one and is answered by the text itself. That is the cleanest curiosity loop of the two.
Film A has the better last line ("Look who was sitting in her house"), but its unfinished fragment and weak hook cost it.

## TOP 3 FIXES (both films)

1. **Fix "Her name comes first."** Either quote the full verse, "Now Jesus loved Martha, and her sister, and Lazarus," or cut the line. As written, newcomers can't follow it, and the claim rests on words that were cut. Also say who John is in plain words, e.g. "Another book about Jesus says…".
2. **Protect Martha in B.** Put A's "that place was open to Martha too" right after "shall not be taken away," so Mary's good part does not read as Martha's loss.
3. **Swap B's ending.** Replace "He knows yours too" with A's earned ending: "She said she was alone. Look who was sitting in her house." It comes from the passage itself and speaks straight to the tired viewer.
