**Film A**

1. **NEWCOMER**

   **WHO:** Martha, a woman hosting Jesus while her sister Mary listens to Him.  
   **WHAT:** Martha asks why Jesus lets her serve alone; He responds to her worry.  
   **WHY:** Her frustration does not make Jesus stop loving her, although her need for help remains unresolved.

   Likely first-hearing difficulties:

   - **Jesus:** His importance is assumed; a newcomer may not know why listening to Him matters.
   - **“Opened her house to Him”:** Means welcomed Him as a guest.
   - **“Sat at Jesus’ feet”:** The meaning as a learner’s position may be missed.
   - **“Lord,” “dost thou,” “hath,” “thou art,” “needful”:** Unfamiliar religious or older English.
   - **“Careful”:** Initially sounds like praise for being attentive. The explanation helps.
   - **“But one thing is needful … Him”:** Why is Jesus the answer to needing help with serving?
   - **“That place was open to Martha too”:** Could mean an available seat; the invitation to listen is implied.
   - **“Later, John wrote”:** Who is John, and why does his statement settle this?
   - **“Her name comes first”:** First before whom? We hear only Martha’s name in the quotation.
   - **“Look who was sitting in her house”:** The intended reassurance is unclear because Martha’s complaint concerned working alone.

2. **BIBLE READER**

   **No.** It offers a useful emphasis on Jesus naming Martha and her worry, but no substantial new understanding; the significance attached to name order is unproven.

3. **CURIOSITY GAP**

   The opening raises: **What happens when someone doing all the work asks Jesus for help?** The film answers whether He loves her, but leaves the practical complaint hanging. **6/10.**

4. **THE 5 QUESTIONS**

   - **Who is this for?** An overburdened caregiver or host who feels unseen. The opening reaches that person immediately.
   - **Main take-away?** Jesus sees Martha’s worry and loves her; she can listen to Him too.
   - **Global audience OK?** Mostly. The modern narration is accessible, but the archaic quotations and unexplained John reference require effort. The abstract closing lines are harder than the opening.
   - **Biblically accurate?**
     - Martha’s quotation matches Luke 10:40 word for word **as an excerpt**. It omits her request: “bid her therefore that she help me.” That omission softens her demand and obscures the practical help she wanted.
     - Both Jesus quotations match the supplied KJV word for word as excerpts. The film leaves out His explanation of Mary’s choice and its permanence.
     - “Now Jesus loved Martha” is an exact opening excerpt from John 11:5, with the rest of the sentence omitted.
     - “Careful” meaning worried is a sound explanation.
     - Martha feeling exhausted, Jesus seeing “all of it,” and the listening place being open to her are interpretations, not explicit statements. The invitation is reasonable, but should be recognised as an inference.
     - “Not one more job. Him” compresses the passage: Mary specifically hears His word.
     - Jesus does not explicitly condemn her work. However, “He named her worry” gives only part of His answer; He also distinguishes Mary’s choice.
     - Martha’s name does come first in John’s list. The text gives no special meaning to that order.
     - Jesus being seated is inferred, not stated.
     - Martha is treated sympathetically. But the last line risks dismissing her complaint: having company is different from having help with serving.
   - **ONE change to make it better?** Replace the final “alone / sitting in her house” reversal with an ending grounded in Jesus’ full statement about Mary’s choice. The current reversal answers a different meaning of “alone.”

5. **OVERALL: 6/10.**

**Film B**

1. **NEWCOMER**

   **WHO:** Martha, a tired host who questions whether Jesus cares about her.  
   **WHAT:** She complains about serving alone; Jesus names her worry and affirms Mary’s choice to listen.  
   **WHY:** Martha can voice her frustration and still be loved.

   Likely first-hearing difficulties:

   - **Jesus:** His identity and importance remain assumed.
   - **“Opened her home to Him”:** Means welcomed Him as a guest.
   - **“Sat at His feet”:** Its meaning as listening and learning may be missed.
   - **“Lord,” “dost thou,” “hath,” “thou art,” “needful”:** Older or religious English.
   - **“Careful”:** Initially misleading, although immediately explained.
   - **“But one thing is needful … Him”:** The connection between listening to Jesus and Martha’s workload needs interpreting.
   - **“Mary had chosen that”:** “That” could mean Jesus, sitting, listening, or stopping work.
   - **“Shall not be taken away”:** What exactly cannot be taken away, and by whom?
   - **“Later, John wrote”:** John is introduced without explanation.
   - **“Her name comes first”:** No other names are read, so the comparison cannot be understood from the narration.
   - **“He knows yours too”:** The meaning is clear, but the move from Martha to the listener is unexplained.

2. **BIBLE READER**

   **No.** The connection to John 11:5 may refresh a familiar story, but the closing application goes beyond what these supplied verses establish.

3. **CURIOSITY GAP**

   The opening asks: **Does Jesus care about someone overwhelmed and frustrated?** John’s statement supplies a direct answer. The listener’s own situation receives a broader, less supported application. **8/10.**

4. **THE 5 QUESTIONS**

   - **Who is this for?** Someone whose exhaustion has become a question about whether anyone cares.
   - **Main take-away?** Jesus loved Martha even when she challenged Him; the film extends that personal attention to the viewer.
   - **Global audience OK?** Mostly. The opening is easy to grasp. The repeated short fragments and interruptions create emphasis, but also break the flow of Jesus’ answer for a second-language listener.
   - **Biblically accurate?**
     - Martha’s quotation is an exact excerpt from Luke 10:40, again omitting her request that Jesus instruct Mary to help.
     - Jesus’ words from Luke 10:41 remain exact despite the narrator’s interruption.
     - “But one thing is needful” is exact.
     - “Shall not be taken away” is an exact excerpt from Luke 10:42. However, omitting “that good part” and “from her” makes its meaning less clear.
     - “Now Jesus loved Martha” is an exact opening excerpt from John 11:5.
     - “Tired” is a plausible inference. “Does anyone even care?” broadens Martha’s specific complaint into a general question.
     - “Not a rule. Her name” accurately identifies His first words, but the emotional framing can underplay the correction that follows.
     - “Him” simplifies Mary’s choice to hear His word. Jesus’ seated posture is inferred.
     - Name order is factual; its suggested emotional significance is not explained by the text.
     - **“He knows yours too” is not established by these passages.** It may express a wider Christian belief, but this film has not earned that particular assurance from its evidence.
     - Martha is not villainised. The ending nevertheless risks offering personal recognition as a complete answer to an unchanged burden.
   - **ONE change to make it better?** Replace “He knows yours too” with a closing beat drawn from Jesus’ complete statement in Luke 10:42. That would make the ending belong to this encounter.

5. **OVERALL: 7/10.**

**WHICH FILM WINS:** Film B, narrowly: its opening question is clearer, and the statement that Jesus loved Martha directly pays it off.  
Film A protects the value of Martha’s work more explicitly, but its final reversal confuses serving alone with being physically alone.

**TOP 3 FIXES**

1. Restore Jesus’ full Luke 10:42 answer and let Mary’s hearing His word carry the ending.
2. Remove “Her name comes first,” or supply the full John quotation without implying that order proves special affection. Remove the unsupported closing jump to “He knows yours too.”
3. Preserve Martha’s actual complaint about needing help. Especially in A, avoid suggesting that Jesus’ presence makes her unshared workload cease to matter.
