## TITLE

Something for Martha

## WHO / TAKEAWAY / CURIOSITY GAP / WHY IT EARNS ITS PLACE

**WHO:** The exhausted person who keeps everything running and wishes someone would help.  
**TAKEAWAY:** Jesus has something for the person who is always giving.  
**CURIOSITY GAP:** Martha asks Jesus to send help. How will He answer?  
**WHY IT EARNS ITS PLACE:** Her welcome gives the story its turn: the woman receiving Jesus into her house can also receive His words.

## NARRATION

NARRATOR:  
You’re doing everything. Does anyone care?

Martha puts that question to Jesus. She wants Him to send her sister back to help.

Martha has welcomed Jesus into her home.  
Mary, her sister, sits at His feet, hearing His words.  
Martha is serving, pulled in many directions.  
She comes to Him.

MARTHA:  
“Lord, dost thou not care that my sister hath left me to serve alone? bid her therefore that she help me.”

NARRATOR:  
She wants another pair of hands.  
Jesus answers her by name.

JESUS:  
“Martha, Martha,”

NARRATOR:  
He speaks about her worry, all the things troubling her. Then He protects Mary’s listening.

JESUS:  
“Mary hath chosen that good part, which shall not be taken away from her.”

NARRATOR:  
Mary is receiving His words.  
Martha has been giving, making room for Him in her house.  
Jesus speaks to the woman doing the serving.

Here, in the home she opened to Him, His words are for her to receive, too.

## WHY THE OPENING HOLDS A WORN-OUT VIEWER

“You’re doing everything” names the burden immediately; “Does anyone care?” names the hurt underneath it.  
Martha carries that exact question into the story, making Jesus’ response personally consequential.

## WHY THE ENDING IS EARNED

It pays off **Martha welcoming Jesus into her house**, established in the narration and the open doorway.  
Jesus then speaks directly to her: the woman providing the welcome has words to receive within that same home.

## RISKS

A newcomer could hear that needing practical help is wrong; Martha’s service and request should carry dignity throughout.  
A Bible reader could mistake the ending for a recorded invitation to sit; it draws from Jesus addressing Martha, and never shows her taking Mary’s place.
