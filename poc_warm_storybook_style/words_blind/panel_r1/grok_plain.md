## TITLE (at most 6 words)
Do You Not Care

## WHO / TAKEAWAY / CURIOSITY GAP / WHY IT EARNS ITS PLACE (one line each)
WHO: A tired person who keeps the work going and feels left alone in it.
TAKEAWAY: Jesus knows Martha by name and loves her, and he will not take away the good part — his word, heard at his feet.
CURIOSITY GAP: She asks if he cares that she serves alone — will he call Mary away from him to help?
WHY IT EARNS ITS PLACE: She asks him to call Mary away, and his answer is that this good part shall not be taken away.

## NARRATION (speaker tag before each part: NARRATOR: or JESUS: or MARTHA: ; then the word count)

## PICTURES (8-10 pictures in order; each: which words it covers + what we see + what MOVES in it)
1. "She is still serving. Her sister has sat down." — Cream-paper room, honey light. Martha in terracotta, still at the work of the house. Mary small in the background, already seated. Steam rises from a clay cup. Martha's hands keep moving. Mary's hands rest.

2. "The woman who let Jesus into her house asks a sharp question." plus Martha's line — The open doorway she welcomed him through, lamp on the wall. Martha turns toward Jesus and speaks. The small flame leans as she talks. Her hands open, empty, asking.

3. "Her name is Martha. Jesus came into her village. She received him into her house." — A simple village lane, then the same doorway. Jesus steps in. Martha draws the door wider. Dust drifts in the light. The lamp inside brightens as he enters.

4. "Much serving pulled her in many directions. Cumbered means that." — Martha in the middle of the room, body turned one way, then another: cloth, cup, water jar. Nothing labeled as a feast. Her sleeves swing. Steam pulls sideways. She is one person tugged by many small tasks.

5. "Mary sat at Jesus' feet and heard his word." — Low and close. Mary on the floor at his feet, face up toward him, still. Only the lamp flame moves. His hand rests quiet. No writing, no crowd, no words we invent.

6. "Martha asked him to call Mary away, and to help her serve." — Martha comes to Jesus. One hand toward Mary at his feet, asking him to send her to the work. The hem of Mary's garment stirs, as if it might be pulled. It does not leave.

7. "Martha, Martha, thou art careful and troubled about many things:" plus "In these old words, careful means full of worry. He says her name twice: Martha, Martha." — Jesus looks at Martha, not past her. He says her name. Her busy hands slow, but the cup and cloth stay in the room. The flame steadies on her face.

8. "But one thing is needful: and Mary hath chosen that good part, which shall not be taken away from her." plus "The Bible says Jesus loved Martha." — His eyes rest on Martha with the same kindness, then include Mary at his feet. Nothing in the room is cleared away. Steam still rises. He does not send Martha out of the house she opened.

9. "She had asked him to call Mary away. That good part is not taken away." — Wide, quiet hold. Mary remains at his feet. No hand lifts her. Martha remains in the room, still the woman who welcomed him. The lamp burns on. The place at his feet stays.

## WHY THE OPENING HOLDS A WORN-OUT VIEWER (2 lines)
The first words find the one who is still serving while someone else has already sat down. She then asks the question that tired person already carries: do you not care that I am left with this alone?

## WHY THE ENDING IS EARNED (2 lines, name the concrete thing from the passage it pays off)
Early, Martha asks Jesus to call Mary away from his feet so Mary will help her serve. The film ends on his own words from the passage: that good part shall not be taken away.

## RISKS (2 lines: what could be misread, by a newcomer or by a Bible reader)
A newcomer may think Jesus took Mary's side and would not help the tired woman who was serving. A Bible reader may want a sharper correction of Martha, or may think "Jesus loved Martha" is spoken in this scene, though those words are from John.
