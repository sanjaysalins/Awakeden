## TITLE (at most 6 words)
He Said Her Name Twice

## WHO / TAKEAWAY / CURIOSITY GAP / WHY IT EARNS ITS PLACE (one line each)
- **WHO:** A worn-out person who carries the house, the job, the family, the church, and feels nobody sees it or helps.
- **TAKEAWAY:** Jesus saw the tired one, called her by name, and loved her; her work was never called wrong, and she was never alone in that house.
- **CURIOSITY GAP:** A tired woman asks Jesus to His face, "Don't you care?" What does He say back? (Paid off twice: her name, twice; then "Now Jesus loved Martha".)
- **WHY IT EARNS ITS PLACE:** Most tellings side with Mary and scold Martha; this one stays on Martha's side and shows that the Bible itself answers her question with her own name.

## NARRATION (speaker tag before each part: NARRATOR: or JESUS: or MARTHA: ; then the word count)

NARRATOR: You do everything for everyone. And nobody helps.

A woman named Martha knew that feeling.

Jesus came to her village, and she opened her house to Him.

Then came the work: so much serving, it pulled her in every direction.

And her sister Mary? She sat at Jesus' feet and listened.

So Martha went straight to Him.

MARTHA: Lord, dost thou not care that my sister hath left me to serve alone?

NARRATOR: Don't you care? I am alone. She said it to His face.

And He answered with her name. Twice.

JESUS: Martha, Martha, thou art careful and troubled about many things:

NARRATOR: "Careful" here means full of worry. He saw all of it.

JESUS: But one thing is needful:

NARRATOR: Not one more job. Mary had chosen it: to be near Him, and to hear Him.

He never said Martha's work was wrong. He named her worry.

And her question, don't you care? Another Gospel writer, John, answers it:

"Now Jesus loved Martha."

Her name comes first.

She said she was alone. But look who was sitting in her house.

## WHY THE OPENING HOLDS A WORN-OUT VIEWER (2 lines)
The first eight words are the viewer's own private sentence, said out loud with no Bible word in it, so they feel seen before they know it is a Bible story.
Then Martha says to Jesus's face the thing tired people are afraid to pray ("Don't you care?"), and they must stay to hear how He answers her.

## WHY THE ENDING IS EARNED (2 lines, name the concrete thing from the passage it pays off)
It pays off two concrete things from Luke 10: Martha's own word "alone" (v.40) and her own house, which she opened to Him (v.38), so "look who was sitting in her house" is a fact of the text, not a slogan.
Her name is the bridge: Jesus says "Martha, Martha" (v.41), and John 11:5 answers her question "dost thou not care" with her name placed first: "Now Jesus loved Martha".

## RISKS (2 lines: what could be misread, by a newcomer or by a Bible reader)
A newcomer may hear "one thing is needful" and still not know what the one thing is; the film says it only as "to be near Him, and to hear Him", and it may sound as if Mary did nothing wrong and Martha did (we say plainly He never called her work wrong).
A Bible reader may object that John 11:5 is a later, different scene, that we stop the quote before "and her sister, and Lazarus", and that "her name comes first" leans on word order; also that we leave out "that good part, which shall not be taken away from her".
