# Lord, Do You Even Care?

## TITLE (at most 6 words)
Lord, Do You Even Care?

## WHO / TAKEAWAY / CURIOSITY GAP / WHY IT EARNS ITS PLACE (one line each)
- **WHO:** The worn-out one who keeps everything running, late at night, feeling unseen and a little angry.
- **TAKEAWAY:** When Martha asked "Do you not care?", Jesus answered with her name, twice, and the Bible later says plainly: "Jesus loved Martha."
- **CURIOSITY GAP:** A tired woman asks Jesus to His face if He cares. What does He say first?
- **WHY IT EARNS ITS PLACE:** It takes the tired viewer's own question, puts it in Martha's mouth word-for-word, and lets the text itself answer it.

## NARRATION (speaker tag before each part: NARRATOR: or JESUS: or MARTHA: ; then the word count)

NARRATOR: "Does anyone even care?" A tired woman once asked Jesus that. To His face.

Her name was Martha. She opened her home to Him. While she served, her sister Mary sat at His feet and listened. So Martha came to Him:

MARTHA: "Lord, dost thou not care that my sister hath left me to serve alone?"

NARRATOR: Listen to the first thing Jesus says. Not a rule. Her name.

JESUS: "Martha, Martha,"

NARRATOR: Twice. Gently.

JESUS: "thou art careful and troubled about many things:"

NARRATOR: "Careful" here means full of worry. Pulled every way at once.

JESUS: "But one thing is needful:"

NARRATOR: The one thing was not another job. It was Him, sitting in her own house. Mary had chosen that, and Jesus said it "shall not be taken away."

So did He care? Later, John wrote one plain line: "Now Jesus loved Martha."

Martha. The tired one. The one who asked. Her name comes first.

If you are worn out tonight and nobody notices, He knows your name. Twice. He cares.

**Word count: 165**

## WHY THE OPENING HOLDS A WORN-OUT VIEWER (2 lines)
The first words are the viewer's own late-night thought, "Does anyone even care?", spoken before any Bible name.
Then the stake: someone said it to Jesus' face, so the viewer has to stay to hear what He said back.

## WHY THE ENDING IS EARNED (2 lines, name the concrete thing from the passage it pays off)
It pays off Martha's own word "care" and Jesus' double "Martha, Martha": her question is answered by her name, then by John 11:5's plain line with her name first.
"He knows your name. Twice." reuses the exact device the film set up, so it lands without any push or pressure.

## RISKS (2 lines: what could be misread, by a newcomer or by a Bible reader)
A Bible reader may object that "Gently" and "her name comes first" add tone and weight the text does not state; the narrator says them as observation, not as quote, and the name order in John 11:5 is accurate.
Picture 10 (Martha seated near Him) is not told in Luke; a newcomer could take it as fact, so keep it as a soft closing image, or swap to Martha simply standing near Him with the cloth set down.
