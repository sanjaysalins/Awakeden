## 1. WHO IS THIS FOR (one named viewer; who stops, who finishes, who shares; who is NOT served)
Named viewer: Ana, 38, the one who cooks, cleans and carries the family, and feels unseen. Line 1 ("You do everything for everyone. Does anyone even care?") is aimed straight at her; she stops. She finishes because line 3 ("His answer? Her name. Twice.") is a fresh small hook. She shares line 7 with a sister or mother. Church readers finish too, because lines 6-7 defend Martha, which they rarely hear. NOT served: the ESL viewer during lines 2 and 4 (about 45 archaic words at the centre of the story), and the newcomer who does not know who Jesus is or why sitting "at His feet" matters.

## 2. MAIN TAKEAWAY (one sentence a viewer could say back; which line carries it; did the film make it clear?)
"Jesus saw the worry under Martha's work, and He loved her." Line 7 carries it, and the last on-screen words repeat it. Clear at the end. But line 5 pulls sideways: it spends the middle on Mary ("He would not take that from Mary"), so for a moment the film is about who was right, not about being seen.

## 3. GLOBAL AUDIENCE (who / what / why in my own words; where I got lost or misled, quote the line; words that fail in translation)
Who: a tired host, her sister, and Jesus as guest. What: she complains her sister is not helping; He answers gently. Why: to show He cares for the worker, not only the listener.
Lost: line 1 "Martha asked Jesus that" — "that" points back loosely. Line 5 "He would not take that from Mary" — nobody in plain words tried to take anything; the link to "bid her... help me" is never made. Line 6 "Lazarus" is never introduced in speech.
Fail in translation: "dost thou", "hath", "bid her" (line 2); "careful" (false friend, glossed, good), "needful", "that good part" (line 4, not glossed).

## 4. BIBLICALLY ACCURATE (claim by claim: RIGHT / OVERCLAIMED / UNSUPPORTED / WRONG, quote the line; say only what you can support)
- Line 1 "She had opened her home to Him... Mary sat at His feet and listened" — RIGHT (Luke 10:38-39).
- Line 2 — RIGHT, KJV verbatim (Luke 10:40).
- Line 3 "Her name. Twice." — RIGHT.
- Line 4 — RIGHT, KJV verbatim (Luke 10:41-42).
- Line 5 "'Careful' means full of worry" — RIGHT. "The one thing needed was to hear Him" — mild OVERCLAIM: a sound, common reading, but Jesus does not define it; say "Mary had chosen it".
- Line 6 — RIGHT, KJV verbatim (John 11:5). "Later" is a fair inference, not stated.
- Line 7 "He never called Martha's work wrong" — RIGHT (He names worry, not work).
- Pictures 7, 10, 12, 14, 15 (Martha softens, puts down the cloth, kneels, sits at peace) — UNSUPPORTED. Luke stops at Jesus's words. Picture 10 also argues against line 7: it shows the work being dropped as the answer.

## 5. BEST VERSION POSSIBLE (what would the best explainer in the world do differently; the ONE highest-value change; score 0-10 against that bar)
The best explainer would say Jesus's answer once in plain words, aimed at Martha, right after the KJV. ONE change: rewrite line 5 as a plain retelling of the whole of line 4, so the central moment survives translation and stays on Martha. Score: 7/10.

## CURIOSITY GAP (opens at line 1, pays off at line 6; score 0-10; worth the wait?)
Opens line 1, re-asked line 6 ("So, did He care?"), pays off lines 6-7. Score 7/10. Worth the wait: the answer comes from an unexpected verse and Martha is named first. Line 5 is the sag.

## THREE SMALLEST FIXES (line number + exact replacement wording)
1. Line 5: "In plain words: Martha, you are full of worry about many things. Only one thing is needed. Mary chose it, and He would not send her away."
2. Line 1: replace "Martha asked Jesus that." with "A woman named Martha asked Jesus the same thing."
3. Line 6: "So, did He care? Later the Bible says of this family: 'Now Jesus loved Martha, and her sister, and Lazarus.' Her name comes first."
