## 1. WHO IS THIS FOR
**Named viewer:** Grace, 34, a nurse in Manila. She works long shifts, cares for her family, and watches Shorts in English as her second language.
- **Who stops:** Anyone who feels unseen. Line 1, "You do everything for everyone", works in every culture, and so does the on-screen "Does anyone care?"
- **Who finishes:** Women carrying a heavy load, plus churchgoers who know the story.
- **Who shares:** Mothers and carers, sharing it with a sister or friend.
- **Not served:** Viewers in honour-and-hospitality cultures (Middle East, West Africa, South Asia). There, leaving a guest unfed is shameful, so they side with Martha, and nothing in lines 4–5 tells them why sitting was the better choice. Also not served: newcomers who meet "Lazarus" (line 6) with no introduction.

## 2. MAIN TAKEAWAY
"Jesus cared about Martha. He saw the worry under her work, and He loved her."
- Lines 6–7 carry it, and it is clear.
- But the film also makes a second claim: the one thing needed is to listen to Him (line 5). Line 7 drops that claim, so the two halves never quite join.

## 3. GLOBAL AUDIENCE
**Who / what / why:** A busy woman complains that Jesus does not care. He gently says her worry is the real problem and listening to Him matters most. Later the Bible says He loved her.

**Where I got lost:**
- **Line 3, "His answer? Her name. Twice."** "Her" could mean Martha or Mary, because Mary ("my sister") is the last woman named. In machine translation this fragment turns to mush. The on-screen "Martha, Martha," rescues it.
- **Line 5, "He would not take that from Mary."** Non-native readers take this as "Jesus won't stop Mary," which is a refusal of Martha. It does not say whether Martha may have this too. This is the weakest line.
- **Line 6, "Lazarus":** a new name with no role. Picture 13 shows a brother, but nothing said or written names him.

**Words that fail in translation or auto-captions:**
- "dost thou" (line 2): auto-captions will write "does thou" or "dust thou".
- "needful" and "that good part" (line 4): "part" reads as a role in a play or a piece.
- "careful" (line 4): a false friend, since today it means cautious. Line 5 and the on-screen card fix it well. Keep that.
- "opened her home to Him" (line 1): an idiom. Plainer: "welcomed Him into her home."
- "sat at His feet" (line 1): in some cultures this reads as a servant's place, not a student's.

## 4. BIBLICALLY ACCURATE
- **Lines 1–2, 4 (Luke 10:38–42):** RIGHT. The KJV text is quoted word for word.
- **Line 5, "careful = full of worry":** RIGHT.
- **Line 5, "to hear Him, as Mary did":** RIGHT. Verse 39 says she "heard his word".
- **Line 5, "He would not take that":** a slight OVERCLAIM. The text is passive: "shall not be taken away from her". It never names who would take it.
- **Line 6 (John 11:5):** RIGHT, quoted exactly. Linking it to the same Martha is sound (John 11:1, 12:2).
- **Line 7, "He never called Martha's work wrong":** RIGHT for this passage. "Never" across the whole Bible is a little broad, though John 12:2 supports it.
- **Pictures 12, 14, 15:** UNSUPPORTED. They show Martha kneel, change and find peace. Luke's story ends with no answer from her. Viewers from many cultures treat pictures as fact.

## 5. BEST VERSION POSSIBLE
The best explainer would close the line 1 → line 5 → line 7 loop in plain words. It would make the invitation in line 5 open to Martha, and so to the viewer, instead of only protecting Mary.

**ONE change:** rewrite line 5 so the "one thing" is clearly offered to Martha too.

**Score: 7/10.**

## CURIOSITY GAP
- **Opens:** line 1 ("Does anyone even care?").
- **Pays off:** lines 6–7, with the John 11:5 quote.
- **Score: 8/10.** The answer comes from the Bible itself, not from the narrator. Worth the wait.

## THREE SMALLEST FIXES
1. **Line 5:** "'Careful' means full of worry. The one thing needed was to listen to Him, like Mary. Martha could have it too."
2. **Line 3:** "His answer began with her name. Twice."
3. **Line 6:** "So, did He care? Later, the Bible says this about Martha, Mary and their brother: 'Now Jesus loved Martha, and her sister, and Lazarus.'"
