## 1. WHO IS THIS FOR
**Named viewer:** Grace, 38. She has three kids, a job and a church rota, and she scrolls at 11pm.
- **Who stops:** Line 1 ("You do everything for everyone") is aimed straight at tired carers and helpers.
- **Who finishes:** Viewers who know the "Martha bad, Mary good" sermon. Line 7 gently undoes that reading.
- **Who shares:** Women's Bible study groups and mums' WhatsApp groups.
- **Not served:** Men, and the "I'm not busy, I'm lost" viewer. Newcomers also lose the thread at Lazarus (line 6).
- **Church group?** Yes, I'd send it, once line 1 is fixed.

## 2. MAIN TAKEAWAY
"Jesus saw the worry under my busyness, and He loved me." Line 7 carries it, and the film lands it cleanly. Line 5 is a second takeaway ("the one thing is to listen to Him"). It is packed into one fast sentence, so it is the part people forget.

## 3. GLOBAL AUDIENCE
**In my words:** A woman hosts Jesus. Her sister sits and listens instead of helping. She complains. Jesus says her name twice, names her worry, and defends the sister. Later we learn He loved them all.

**Where I got lost:**
- Line 6: "and Lazarus." Lazarus has never been named. Picture 13 shows him, but the words never say "their brother."
- Line 4: "that good part" is unclear to second-language speakers, and nothing explains it.
- Line 4: "needful" is archaic.
- Line 5 explains "careful" well. That is a good fix.
- Line 6: "Later" could mean later in time or later in the Bible. Unclear.

## 4. BIBLICALLY ACCURATE
- **Line 1, "Does anyone even care? Martha asked Jesus that." OVERCLAIMED/misquote.** Luke 10:40 says "Lord, dost thou not care". She asked if *Jesus* cared that her sister left her, not "anyone." The on-screen hook repeats the misquote.
- **Line 1, "You do everything for everyone." UNSUPPORTED** as a picture of Martha. Fine as a hook aimed at the viewer.
- **Line 1, "opened her home to Him." RIGHT.** Luke 10:38, "received him into her house."
- **Line 1, "Mary sat at His feet and listened." RIGHT.** Luke 10:39, "sat at Jesus' feet, and heard his word."
- **Line 2: RIGHT, word for word** (Luke 10:40).
- **Line 3, "Her name. Twice." RIGHT.** Luke 10:41.
- **Line 4: RIGHT, word for word** (Luke 10:41-42). Dropping "And Jesus answered and said unto her" is fine.
- **Line 5, "'Careful' means full of worry." RIGHT.** That is the KJV-era sense, and it matches "troubled."
- **Line 5, "The one thing needed was to hear Him." OVERCLAIMED.** The text never says what the "one thing" is. It is a fair, mainstream reading from Luke 10:39 ("heard his word"), but the line states it as fact.
- **Line 5, "He would not take that from Mary." Mildly OVERCLAIMED.** Luke 10:42 is passive: "shall not be taken away from her." Still defensible, because He does turn down "bid her... help me."
- **Line 6: RIGHT, word for word** (John 11:5). It is the same household (John 11:1). "Later" is fair, but loose.
- **Line 7, "He never called Martha's work wrong." OVERCLAIMED.** He never condemns serving, true. But "many things" against "one thing is needful," and "Mary hath chosen that good part," clearly rank the two. "Never" is an argument from silence.
- **Line 7, "He saw her worry." RIGHT** (Luke 10:41). **"He loved her." RIGHT** (John 11:5).
- **Pictures (not spoken, but noted):** Pictures 7, 10, 12 and 14 show Martha softening, putting down the cloth and kneeling "for the first time." Luke 10 never tells us how she responded. These pictures invent a happy ending the text leaves open.

## 5. BEST VERSION POSSIBLE
The best explainer would make the hook her real question: "Lord, don't You care?" Then the payoff answers *her* question, not a made-up one. They would also introduce Lazarus in four words, and show Martha's response as open, not a scripted conversion.

**ONE highest-value change:** fix line 1 and the on-screen hook to match Luke 10:40. The whole film hangs on that question, so it must be her actual words.
