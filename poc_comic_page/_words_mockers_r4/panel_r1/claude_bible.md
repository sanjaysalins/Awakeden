## 1. WHO IS THIS FOR
A churchgoing teen or adult who half-knows the Cross story and likes "hidden connection" Bible content. They stop at "WHO WROTE THE MOCKERY?" and finish for the twist. They share it with a doubting friend. Not served: a total newcomer, who gets no setup on who David, Matthew or the crowd are (lines 1, 3). Also not served: a skeptic who wants proof the Psalm was not written to fit the story.

## 2. MAIN TAKEAWAY
"The mockers at the cross said almost what the Psalm said a thousand years before, and Jesus stayed and forgave." Lines 5, 9 and 11 carry it. Two ideas compete: prophecy (line 9) and "He stayed" (lines 7, 11). Line 11's CTA follows the second, but line 9 sets up the first. That split makes it unclear.

## 3. GLOBAL AUDIENCE
My version: Jesus is dying. Crowds mock Him. A king wrote almost the same mockery in a song a thousand years earlier. Jesus stayed and forgave. Turn to Him.
- I got lost at line 3: "go back a thousand years" and "He writes it into a song" is ambiguous. Is "he" David or Jesus?
- Line 7: "He could have come down... So He stayed." The step from "no one takes His life" to "stayed" is dense for a newcomer.
- Line 9: "that same song heard the insults" is odd, because a song cannot hear.
- Fail in translation: "mocker", "come down from the cross", "turn unto", "ends of the world".
- The card "WHO WROTE THE MOCKERY?" is never answered outright. The answer is implied (David, Psalm 22).

## 4. BIBLICALLY ACCURATE
- Line 2: Matthew 27:43 quoted "He trusted in God; let him deliver him now". RIGHT. It is verbatim but truncated. It drops "if he will have him: for he said, I am the Son of God." Acceptable.
- Line 3: "In plain words: he trusted God, so let God rescue him." RIGHT as a paraphrase.
- Line 3: "a king named David... a thousand years". RIGHT in substance (roughly 1000 BC). The Psalm's title and authorship are not in the supplied text, so they are UNSUPPORTED by the pack, though traditional. "People laugh at him too... writes it into a song" is a fair reading of 22:7 but it asserts David's own context. It is a plausible, not certain, claim.
- Line 4: Psalm 22:8 quoted "He trusted on the LORD that he would deliver him". RIGHT and verbatim. It stops before "let him deliver him".
- Line 5: "Almost the same words". OVERCLAIMED slightly. The verbs match ("trusted", "deliver") but the prepositions differ (on / in) and "let him deliver him" is shared. It is mostly fair.
- Line 5: "People passing by shout one more thing". Matthew 27:39 says they "reviled... wagging their heads". Shouting is inferred. Minor.
- Line 6: Matthew 27:40 quoted "If thou be the Son of God, come down from the cross". RIGHT and verbatim.
- Line 7: "Jesus had said that no one takes His life from Him. He gives His life Himself." RIGHT (John 10:18). The cited verse is not named on screen. "He could have come down" is supported by Matthew 26:53 but is an inference.
- Line 7: "So He stayed." This is theological inference tied to John 10:18, not stated in Matthew 27. It is defensible but not shown to be His stated reason.
- Line 8: Luke 23:34 quoted "Father, forgive them; for they know not what they do." RIGHT and verbatim. But Luke 23:34 is a different Gospel and a different moment from the Matthew mockery. Saying "from the cross He prayed" is fine, but the sequence "So He stayed. And... prayed" implies a causal link that is not in the text.
- Line 9: "That same song heard the insults a thousand years early." OVERCLAIMED in its framing. It is poetic, since the song did not "hear".
- Line 9: "people from every nation will turn to God." RIGHT (Psalm 22:27, "all the ends of the world... all the kindreds of the nations"). "Also says what comes next" is chronologically supported.
- Line 10: Psalm 22:27 "All the ends of the world shall remember and turn unto the LORD". RIGHT and verbatim.
- Missing: the film never says the Psalm opens with "My God, my God, why hast thou forsaken me?" (22:1), which Jesus quoted. This is the strongest link, and it is absent.

## 5. BEST VERSION POSSIBLE
The best explainer would show both texts side by side and name the Psalm. It would answer the opening card in one sentence ("David wrote it, Psalm 22"). The single highest-value change is to state the answer to the title question explicitly at line 3. Score: 6/10.

## CURIOSITY GAP
Opens at the card (0s). It pays off partly at lines 4–5. Score: 6/10. It is worth the wait, but the payoff is implied, not stated.

## THREE SMALLEST FIXES
1. Line 3: "...go back a thousand years, to King David. People laughed at David too. He wrote it in a song, Psalm 22."
2. Line 9: "That song, Psalm 22, described this scene a thousand years early."
3. Line 7: "So He stayed" → "So He chose to stay."
