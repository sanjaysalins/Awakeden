# JUDGE, NO HAT — The Mockers' Words (Psalm 22:7-8)

## 1. WHO IS THIS FOR
Named viewer: Priya, 24, Mumbai, Hindu home, English as a second language, scrolls Shorts, curious about Jesus, allergic to preaching. She STOPS on the card WHO WROTE THE MOCKERY? and the shouted taunt (line 2). She FINISHES because line 5 ("Almost the same words, a thousand years apart") is a real puzzle paid off fast. SHARERS are already-Christian viewers who want a "prophecy proof" clip to send. NOT served: the skeptic who asks "couldn't Matthew have copied David's song when he wrote his book?" The film never meets that question, so the one viewer most likely to argue gets nothing.

## 2. MAIN TAKEAWAY
"The insults shouted at Jesus on the cross were already written in David's song a thousand years earlier, and that same song says the whole world will turn to God." Carried by line 5 and line 9. Clear? Mostly. But the film has two endings: "He stayed" (line 7) and "the nations turn" (lines 9-10). Line 11 stitches them ("the One who stayed"), yet the viewer is never told WHY His staying leads to nations turning. The two halves sit side by side; they do not lock.

## 3. GLOBAL AUDIENCE
Who: Jesus on a cross; a crowd shouting; King David a thousand years earlier. What: the crowd's taunt matches David's song almost word for word; Jesus could have come down and chose not to. Why: so people everywhere can turn to Him. Where I got lost: line 3 "He writes it into a song" — "it" is vague (the laughing? their words?), and a newcomer may not know a Psalm is a song. Line 9 "That same song heard the insults" — a song cannot hear; this personification translates badly. Line 7 "He gives His life Himself" is stiff and awkward in translation. Words that fail: "ends of the world" (line 10, may be read as apocalypse, not geography); "remember" (line 10, remember what?); "they know not" (line 8, parseable but old). "A thousand years" is said three times (lines 3, 5, 9) — clear but heavy.

## 4. BIBLICALLY ACCURATE
- Line 1, crowd shouting at the dying Jesus: RIGHT (Matt 27:39-44).
- Line 2, Matt 27:43 quote: RIGHT, verbatim (cut before "if he will have him"; acceptable).
- Line 2 voiced as "the crowd": OVERCLAIMED. Matt 27:41 gives these words to the chief priests, scribes and elders. Line 5 then says "People passing by shout one more thing," implying the same group shouted both. The passers-by speak 27:40; the priests speak 27:43. The film blurs them.
- Line 3, David laughed at, writes a song: RIGHT (Ps 22:7).
- Line 4, Ps 22:8: RIGHT, verbatim.
- Line 5, "Almost the same words": RIGHT.
- Line 6, Matt 27:40: RIGHT, verbatim.
- Line 7, "He could have come down": RIGHT (Matt 26:53, John 10:18). "No one takes His life from Him. He gives His life Himself": RIGHT (John 10:18). "So He stayed. And from the cross He prayed": OVERCLAIMED by order — Luke puts the prayer (23:34) at the crucifixion itself, before the mocking (23:35-39). The film implies the prayer answers the taunts. Soft, but real.
- Line 8, Luke 23:34: RIGHT, verbatim.
- Line 9, the song "says what comes next": RIGHT (Ps 22:27 follows the suffering).
- Line 10, Ps 22:27: RIGHT, verbatim first half.
- Line 11, "turn to the One who stayed": Ps 22:27 says "turn unto the LORD." Reading the LORD as Jesus is orthodox but asserted, not shown. UNSUPPORTED from the pack alone; acceptable for a Christian lens, but a newcomer will not see the step.

## 5. BEST VERSION POSSIBLE
The best explainer would make ONE spine lock: taunt predicted -> He stayed on purpose -> the same song says what staying wins -> so you can turn. Right now the "wins" link between line 7 and line 9 is missing. It would also give Jesus' own words (line 8) a voice: David and the mockers each get one, Jesus does not, so the hinge line is the flattest-delivered line in the film. ONE highest-value change: rewrite line 9 so the Psalm's ending is the RESULT of His staying, not a second fact beside it. Score: 6/10.

## CURIOSITY GAP
Opens at the card plus line 1 ("Listen."), sharpened by line 3 ("Now go back a thousand years"), pays off at line 5. Score 7/10 — fast, honest, worth the wait. But it closes around 20 seconds; lines 6-11 ride with no second gap. The second half needs its own question asked out loud ("Why did He not come down?"), not only answered.

## THREE SMALLEST FIXES
1. Line 3: "He writes it into a song." -> "He writes their words into a song."
2. Line 5: "People passing by shout one more thing." -> "The crowd shouts one more thing."
3. Line 9: "That same song heard the insults a thousand years early. It also says what comes next:" -> "That same song had the insults a thousand years early. It also says what His staying wins:"
