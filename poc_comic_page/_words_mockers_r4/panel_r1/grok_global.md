## 1. WHO IS THIS FOR

Mei, 19, Taipei. School English. She knows the name Jesus. She does not know David, a psalm, or KJV. She watches on a phone, often muted.

She stops on the opening card. "MOCKERY" is not her word. She stops again at line 2, on "let him deliver him."

Who finishes: a church teen who already believes this psalm points to Jesus.

Who shares: English-speaking Christians, into their own chat. Mei cannot retell it, so she does not share.

Who is not served: second-language viewers; anyone with sound off (the simple English is only spoken; the balloons are KJV); viewers who do not already accept "Son of God." Line 1 states that title as fact, with no "the Bible says." For many Muslim viewers that first sentence is an insult, not a frame.

## 2. MAIN TAKEAWAY

"People mocked Jesus with words David had already written."

Line 5 is meant to carry it. The film never says it that cleanly. Line 3 says David writes "it" into a song. Line 9 says the song "heard" the insults. Then lines 7–11 add four more points: he could leave, he chose to die, he forgave, every nation will turn, you should turn. One sentence does not survive. Not clear.

## 3. GLOBAL AUDIENCE

Who: Jesus, dying. Then a king, David. What: insults at the cross match an old song. Why: I lost it. The match turns into a sermon, then an invitation.

Lost at line 2. "Let him deliver him now." Which "him"? "Deliver" means a package, or a baby, before it means rescue. The card DELIVER = RESCUE comes later, on line 4, after the damage.

Misled at line 7. "Come down" can mean come downstairs. "He could have come down" sounds like climb off the wood. "He gives His life Himself" is an idiom buried in pronouns.

Misled at line 9. A song does not "hear." "What comes next" sounds like the next line. It is not.

Line 6 on a small balloon: "If thou be" looks like a typo. Line 8: "they know not" looks like broken English, and it can sound like the film says the killers are innocent.

Also fail: "In plain words" and "go back a thousand years" (line 3); "trusted on" versus "trusted in" (lines 4 and 2); LORD in capitals (lines 4 and 10, looks like shouting); "ends of the world" and "turn unto" (line 10); "the One who stayed" (line 11 — stayed where?); [Matthew 27:43] looks like a timestamp. "The Mockers' Words" hides the apostrophe. "WHO WROTE THE MOCKERY?" is a rare noun used as clickbait over a dying man.

## 4. BIBLICALLY ACCURATE

Line 1. RIGHT that the Gospels put him on a cross. "Son of God" is the narrator's claim, spoken before the scene shows it.

Line 1 with line 2. WRONG speaker. Matthew 27:43 is the chief priests, scribes, and elders (27:41–43), not "people." Passers-by say 27:39–40.

Line 2. OVERCLAIMED as a quote. It cuts "if he will have him: for he said, I am the Son of God."

Line 3, the rescue gloss. RIGHT as a taunt. "So" can sound like the narrator agrees.

Line 3, "a thousand years" and "a king named David." OVERCLAIMED. The verses shown never name David. The laugh is Psalm 22:7. The title cites 22:7. The film never says it.

Line 4. OVERCLAIMED by the cut. It stops before "let him deliver him, seeing he delighted in him," the half that most matches Matthew. Line 5, "Almost the same words," is tidy because both quotes were shortened.

Lines 5–6. RIGHT that passers-by say 27:40. OVERCLAIMED as "one more thing" from the same mouths as line 2. Matthew's order is the reverse. The quote drops "save thyself."

Line 7, the plain gloss. RIGHT. "He could have come down." OVERCLAIMED. Matthew 26:53 and John 10:18 do not say he could climb down. "No one takes His life... He gives His life Himself." RIGHT as John 10:18.

Lines 7–8. "So He stayed," with the forgive prayer as the reply. OVERCLAIMED. The words of line 8 are in KJV Luke 23:34 (some ancient copies omit them). Luke does not make that prayer the answer to "come down."

Line 9. OVERCLAIMED. The match is real. Matthew here does not say "this fulfills the psalm." "What comes next" is WRONG. After 22:8 comes birth (22:9), not the nations. The nations are 22:27, later.

Lines 9–10, every nation turning to God. RIGHT as a gloss of 22:27. The quote cuts the rest of the verse.

Line 11. OVERCLAIMED if heard as the psalm. Psalm 22:27 says turn to the LORD. The film aims that at Jesus, and does not say this is the narrator's step.

## 5. BEST VERSION POSSIBLE

The best explainer would show two simple sentences, one under the other, and mark the matching words. She would name rulers, people walking past, and David. She would say once: "David wrote this insult first." She would not add forgiveness, the nations, and "you can turn" in the same minute. Ear or eye must be simple English, not KJV on both. She would show Psalm 22:7, the laugh and the shaken heads. That is the picture, and the title already promises it.

ONE change: one on-screen pair, in simple English, of the full taunt and the full psalm line, plus one spoken line: "Same insult. David wrote it first."

Score: 4/10. The parallel is real. The words, the cut quotes, and the extra endings fail Mei.

## CURIOSITY GAP

Opens on the card before line 1. Pays off weakly at line 5. Line 3 names David early and vaguely. Line 9 then says the song "heard" the insults. The card asks for a person. The film answers with a person, then a song. Score: 4/10. Not worth the wait. After line 5 a second sermon starts.
