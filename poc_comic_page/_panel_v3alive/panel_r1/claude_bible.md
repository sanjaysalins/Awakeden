## 1. WHO IS THIS FOR
A 30-year-old who grew up near church, left, and carries private shame (line 1). They stop on "your record." They finish if line 3 feels like a promise. They share it with a friend who thinks they're too far gone. It does not serve the self-satisfied, or the viewer who has never feared rejection by God. Nor does it serve a seeker who needs to know who Jesus is. He is never introduced.

## 2. MAIN TAKEAWAY
"If you come to Jesus, He will not turn you away." Line 3 carries it, and line 5 repeats it. The film makes it clear.

## 3. GLOBAL AUDIENCE
- **Who/what/why:** a man with a shameful record fears God's rejection. Jesus says anyone who comes is accepted. So come.
- **Where I got lost:** "rehearsing whether you're allowed in" (line 2) is idiom-heavy and abstract. "In no wise" is archaic and gets repeated, so non-native listeners may not parse it. The narration never names Jesus before "Jesus answers it," so a newcomer has to guess who "He" is in line 1. "Fine print" (line 4) is a very English idiom. "Record" could mean a music record, so it depends on the scroll image.

## 4. BIBLICALLY ACCURATE
- Line 3, the quote: **RIGHT**. It is verbatim KJV John 6:37.
- Line 3, "Not maybe. Not if you clean up first": **RIGHT as application**. The verse sets no precondition on the one who comes.
- Line 2, "Jesus answers it before you can ask": **OVERCLAIMED**. John 6:37 was spoken in a different setting, not as an answer to this fear. It's a rhetorical frame.
- Line 4, "too far gone, too late, too much… him that cometh has no fine print": **RIGHT**. "Him that cometh" has no stated exception.
- Line 4, "The only way to be cast out is to never come": **OVERCLAIMED**. The verse promises that the one who comes is not cast out. It does not say that the non-comer is "cast out," or what happens to them. It also skips the verse's first half, where the Father gives and they come. The film turns a promise into a logical rule.
- Line 5, "He already read it, and still said come": **UNSUPPORTED** by this text. The KJV text offered has no "record" that He read, and Jesus does not say "come" in John 6:37. It says "him that cometh."
- Line 5, "The door… was never locked": **UNSUPPORTED**. John 6:37 has no door. It's a metaphor. It also clashes with the image of the closed door (line 1, per the summary).
- Framing "the record": ungrounded in this verse. A grounded version would cite another verse, but none is supplied.

## 5. BEST VERSION POSSIBLE
The best explainer would stay inside the verse. It would name Jesus in the first 3 seconds. It would let one metaphor (the door) pay off visibly, perhaps by showing the door opening or standing open before the narrator says "never locked." Highest-value change: fix line 5 so the door and the record claims match the text, since an unsupported claim sits in the closing line. **Score 6/10.** The quote and the emotional arc are strong. The weaknesses are the unsupported landing and the abstract opening.

## CURIOSITY GAP
It opens at line 1 ("he'd turn you away") and is answered at line 3. It pays off at line 3, then restates twice. **Score 6/10.** Worth the wait, but the payoff comes at about 20 seconds, and lines 4–5 repeat it.

## THREE SMALLEST FIXES
- **Line 4:** replace the last sentence with "The only ones turned away are the ones who never come" → "So no one who comes is ever turned away."
- **Line 5:** replace "He already read it, and still said come" with "He knows all about it, and He promised: I will in no wise cast out."
- **Line 2:** replace "rehearsing whether you're allowed in. Jesus answers it before you can ask" with "afraid to knock. Jesus answers that fear."

## 6. MOST VIEWERS
**Change:** open on the closed door and the line "Are you afraid God will turn you away?" in the first 2 seconds. Name Jesus right away. Hold the line from John 6:37 as large on-screen text, since the quote is the share line. **Keep:** the night-blue and warm-gold palette, the scroll as the record, and the open-armed ending in the archway. **Odds (my guesses, wide error bars):** swipe past in 3 seconds is roughly 55% (range 40–70%). Watch to the end is roughly 35% (20–50%). Share is roughly 2–3% (0.5–5%). Simulated viewers are not real, so test with real seed viewers.
