## 3. GLOBAL AUDIENCE (who / what / why in my own words; where I got lost or misled, quote the line; words that fail in translation)

A man expects rejection because of his past; Jesus promises welcome; the invitation is to approach despite shame (lines 1–5).

“Your record” can suggest criminal history rather than moral guilt (line 1). “Giveth,” “him that cometh,” and “in no wise” require decoding during a fast feed (line 3). “No fine print” assumes familiarity with contracts (line 4). “The door … was never locked” could imply that approaching God requires no response at all, despite “So come” (line 5).

## 4. BIBLICALLY ACCURATE (claim by claim: RIGHT / OVERCLAIMED / UNSUPPORTED / WRONG, quote the line; say only what you can support)

- **RIGHT:** “I will in no wise cast out” accurately quotes John 6:37 KJV (line 3).
- **RIGHT:** “Not if you clean up first” reasonably expresses the absence of a prior moral-cleanup condition in this promise (line 3).
- **OVERCLAIMED:** “The only way to be cast out is to never come” turns a promise about those who come into an exhaustive rule about rejection. The quoted verse does not establish that rule (line 4).
- **UNSUPPORTED by this verse:** “He already read it” and “was never locked” are illustrative additions, not statements contained in John 6:37 (line 5).

## 5. BEST VERSION POSSIBLE (what would the best explainer in the world do differently; the ONE highest-value change; score 0-10 against that bar)

**Highest-value change: deliver the promise earlier.** Line 1 diagnoses a fear; line 2 diagnoses it again before answering. The best version makes rejection feel imminent, then lets Jesus interrupt that expectation.
