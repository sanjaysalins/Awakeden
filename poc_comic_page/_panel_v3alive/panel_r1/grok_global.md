## 1. WHO IS THIS FOR

A 26-year-old in Manila, Lagos, or São Paulo who speaks everyday English, reads the captions, already knows the name Jesus, and feels too ashamed to pray. They can finish, and they may send it to a friend who will not walk into a church.

A cold scroller with no Bible English stops at line 3, on "giveth" and "in no wise." Captions and translation break there.

Not served: a total newcomer, a Muslim or Hindu viewer with no map from "God" to "Jesus" to "the Father," and anyone watching muted. No on-screen words are described, so the pictures never state the promise.

## 2. MAIN TAKEAWAY

"If I come to Jesus, he will not turn me away because of my past."

Line 5 carries it in plain speech. Line 3 is the source, and it is the hardest English in the film. Open arms in the lit arch can show the feeling. The words make it clear only to someone who already knows the verse.

## 3. GLOBAL AUDIENCE

Who: a person afraid to approach God. What: Jesus promises not to reject the one who comes. Why: they think their past blocks them. I get that from lines 1 and 5 and from the closed door.

I get lost at line 3. "All that the Father giveth me shall come to me" can sound like "only people already chosen may come," which fights the later "just come." "Father" is never explained. In many places that word means a family dad, not God.

Words that fail, quoted:

- Line 3, "in no wise," and the title. "Wise" means smart. Translators write "in no way wise." A scroller learns nothing.
- Line 3, "giveth" / "cometh." Captions hear "give it" / "comet."
- Line 3, "clean up first." Sounds like tidy the house.
- Line 1, "record." Police file, sports score, or music disc. The scroll saves it only if it clearly shows a list of deeds.
- Lines 2 and 5, "rehearsing." Means play practice, not "standing there arguing with yourself." "Rehearsing at" the door is muddy even in English.
- Line 4, "too far gone." Sounds like you traveled far. "Too much" has no noun.
- Line 4, "fine print." Often becomes "small letters," not "hidden rule."
- Line 4, "cast out." Line 3 means "I will not throw you out." Line 4 switches it to "you cast yourself out by staying away." Same words, two meanings.

The closed door is universal. The guilt words ("record," "case," "ashamed") are Western-church. Court, contract, and theater in one minute is too many pictures for a second-language ear.

## 4. BIBLICALLY ACCURATE

- Line 3 quote: RIGHT. It matches John 6:37 KJV, and those are Jesus' words.
- Line 3, "Not maybe": RIGHT. The verse is a strong "I will not."
- Line 3, "Not if you clean up first": narrow RIGHT. That clause names no precondition. The same chapter still ties coming to the Father's gift and to belief. This is not "the whole Gospel has no call to turn."
- Line 4, "no fine print": OVERCLAIMED. Fair for this one clause. Not a proof that no other word of Jesus applies.
- Line 4, "The only way to be cast out is to never come": OVERCLAIMED. The verse says he will not cast out the one who comes. It does not say staying away is itself being cast out.
- Line 5, "He already read it, and still said come": UNSUPPORTED by John 6:37. This verse does not say he read your record, and it does not say the command "come."
- Line 5, "never locked": a metaphor, not the verse. Fine as a picture. Misleading if heard as "walk in with no trust."

## 5. BEST VERSION POSSIBLE

Keep the door and the open arms. Say the KJV once. At that second, put one plain line in the voice and on the screen: "If you come to me, I will never turn you away." One metaphor only. Call the Father God.

Highest-value change: make line 3 understandable to a caption reader.

Score: 5/10 for a global viewer. The arc is right. The payoff is in insider English.

## CURIOSITY GAP

Opens at line 1. The intended payoff is line 3. The payoff a global viewer can use is line 5, "was never locked."

Score: 6/10. The fear is clear fast. The answer arrives in words that do not travel, so the wait is only half paid.

## THREE SMALLEST FIXES

1. Line 3: "In no wise. Not maybe. Not if you clean up first." → "That means never. Not maybe. You do not have to fix your life first."
2. Line 4: "But him that cometh has no fine print. The only way to be cast out is to never come." → "But anyone who comes to him, he will not turn away. There is no hidden rule."
3. Line 5: "The door you've been rehearsing at was never locked." → "The door you were afraid to open was never locked."

## 6. MOST VIEWERS

Change the title and the first on-screen words to "He Will Never Turn You Away." "In No Wise Cast Out" will not stop a thumb.

Keep the night-blue door, the scroll in the hand, the ink lines, and the last shot: Jesus in the gold arch, arms open.

Odds on a cold feed, wide error bars, not measured views: still watching at 3 seconds 30% ± 10; watch to the end 20% ± 8 of plays; share 3% ± 2. Line 1 is abstract, and the quote is archaic.

With that title on frame one: still watching at 3 seconds 45% ± 8; watch to the end 32% ± 8; share 6% ± 3. Church chats will share it more than the public feed.

VERDICT: REVISE — the door-and-open-arms story can travel; the payoff words ("in no wise," "giveth," "fine print," "rehearsing") fail a second-language viewer at the exact second that matters.
