## 1. WHO IS THIS FOR
A 25-year-old in Lagos or Manila who half-knows the cross story and clicks a question card. They stop at "WHO SCRIPTED THE MOCKERY?" and finish if the payoff lands. Bible readers share it. Not served: total non-Christians who don't know who "the King" or "the Messiah" is, and anyone who wants the "why" of Psalm 22 beyond mockery.

## 2. MAIN TAKEAWAY
"The people mocking Jesus were unknowingly repeating a 1,000-year-old psalm, and He stayed on the cross anyway." Lines 1 and 9 carry it. It is mostly clear, but lines 11–12 add a second takeaway (turn to Him) that dilutes it.

## 3. GLOBAL AUDIENCE
Who: mockers at the cross. What: they repeated a prophecy. Why: it shows Jesus is the One and stayed for them.
Where I got lost:
- Line 3 packs two quotes, a paraphrase and a cut-off "…" into one beat. A newcomer can't tell where the Psalm ends and Matthew begins.
- Line 5, "And they threw one more taunt at Him: "save thyself." Line 6 then starts mid-sentence. This is a broken split.
- "Messiah", "prophecy", "deliver", "ends of the world" and "the whole song" (what song? Line 8 never says Psalm 22 is a song or poem) all fail in translation.
- "Shoot out the lip" is correctly left out.

## 4. BIBLICALLY ACCURATE
- L1 "reading from a script": RIGHT as rhetoric, but it implies the mockers consciously followed a text. Line 3 fixes this ("they just didn't know it").
- L2 "A thousand years before… the gestures, and the words": RIGHT. David is traditionally dated about 1000 BC, and Ps 22:7 has the head-shaking and the "laugh me to scorn" words. Minor: the gestures are the lip and head, and only the head-wagging recurs in Mt 27:39.
- L3 Ps 22:7–8 quote: RIGHT but spliced. KJV has "saying, He trusted on the LORD that he would deliver him: let him deliver him, seeing he delighted in him." The film drops the end. The "…" is honest.
- L3 "nearly line for line": OVERCLAIMED. The overlap is one clause: "trusted… deliver him" (Mt 27:43 against Ps 22:8). Mt 27:43 adds "if he will have him… I am the Son of God", which has no Psalm counterpart. "Nearly" is stretching it. Fix: "echoing the Psalm".
- L3 "the rulers sneering": RIGHT. Mt 27:41–43, chief priests, scribes and elders. "Passers-by wagging their heads": RIGHT, Mt 27:39.
- L3 "They thought they were inventing the cruelty": UNSUPPORTED. Matthew says nothing about their thinking. It is fair inference, but it is stated as fact.
- L4 "unwilling witnesses that this was the One": OVERCLAIMED. Scripture doesn't say they were unwilling or that they witnessed anything. They mocked. Matthew shows no awareness. "Unwitting" is the accurate word. "Unwilling" is a different claim.
- L5–6 "save thyself… If thou be the Son of God, come down from the cross": RIGHT, Mt 27:40. The Mt 27:40 words come from the passers-by, not the rulers. The film is vague on who said what.
- L6 "But He could have come down": UNSUPPORTED by these texts. It is orthodox (Mt 26:53, not in the pack), but nothing supplied backs it.
- L7 "He showed He was the Son… by staying": an interpretive claim, not a text claim. Fair, but it is presented as plain fact.
- L8 "the whole song was about": OVERCLAIMED for the mockery verses. Psalm 22 is about the sufferer; it is typological and messianic, and the film never shows that the NT applies it (it doesn't cite Mt 27:46).
- L11 "ends of the world turning to Him": RIGHT, Ps 22:27 "remember and turn unto the LORD". Note it says the LORD, and "to Him" quietly equates Jesus with the LORD. That is orthodox but not shown. "A script they never finished" is a good, accurate hook-back.
- L9 "They told the King to come down": RIGHT, Mt 27:42, though the title "King of Israel" is never named.

## 5. BEST VERSION POSSIBLE
The best explainer would show Psalm 22:8 and Mt 27:43 side by side, with the matching words highlighted. That would replace the "nearly line for line" claim with proof the viewer can see. Highest-value change: say the matching clause is the only echo, and show it on screen. Score: 6/10.

## CURIOSITY GAP
Opens at the card and line 1 ("who scripted?"). Pays off at lines 3–4, with David's psalm as the script. The card asks "who", but the answer is a text, so there is a slight mismatch. Score 7/10. Worth the wait, though line 3 is crowded.

## THREE SMALLEST FIXES
- L3: replace "nearly line for line" with "echoing the Psalm's words".
- L4: replace "unwilling witnesses that this was the One" with "unknowing witnesses that this was the One".
- L5–6: merge into one line: "And they said, 'If thou be the Son of God, come down from the cross.'"
