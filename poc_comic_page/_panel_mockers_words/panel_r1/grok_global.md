## 1. WHO IS THIS FOR
Putri, 20, Jakarta. English comes from school and short videos. She knows the cross from pictures. She does not know “Messiah” or “psalm.”

The film serves a church viewer who already likes the Psalm 22 / Matthew 27 parallel and can hear KJV. Putri stops at line 3, when the balloon fills with “trusted on the LORD,” “deliver him,” and “if he will have him,” while three voices trade lines. A church viewer finishes. That viewer shares it to a church chat. Putri cannot retell it, so she does not share it.

Left out: first-time viewers; Muslim viewers, who meet “He showed He was the Son” as a plain fact in line 7; Jewish viewers, who hear David’s voice and then line 8 giving “the whole song” to the man on the cross; anyone who hears “scripted” as “staged.”

## 2. MAIN TAKEAWAY
“People at the cross said insults Psalm 22 had already written, and Jesus stayed on the cross.”

Line 3 carries the match. Line 7 and line 9 carry the staying. Line 10–12 add “save the mockers, then turn.” By line 4 the first point is already sayable. From line 5 a second sermon starts. A viewer leaves with two sentences and no single line to repeat.

## 3. GLOBAL AUDIENCE
Who: Jesus on the cross, a crowd insulting him, and an old writing called Psalm 22. David’s voice speaks the old words. A picture of a king writing sits behind that voice.

What: The crowd’s words match that old writing.

Why, as I can say it: the narrator says this marks him as the promised one (line 4), that staying on the cross shows he is the Son (line 7), and that he stayed so mockers would be won (line 10). Line 12 tells me to turn and come.

Where I got lost:
- The card and line 1 ask **who** scripted the mockery. Line 4 says “prophecy.” Line 11 says the script was unfinished. No person is named.
- On short-video English, “scripted” means the scene was staged. The card, line 1, and line 11 all use that word.
- David speaks in the “I,” then line 8 says the whole song was about the mocked man. I cannot tell who the “I” is.
- Line 6 says “Son of God.” Line 9 suddenly says “the King.”
- Line 10, “win the scorners.” I hear a fight: he beat them.
- Line 11, “all the ends of the world,” next to “never finished.” I hear the world stopping.

Words that fail: “reading from a script” (line 1); “wagging their heads,” “sneering nearly line for line,” “inventing the cruelty” (line 3); “reciting prophecy,” “unwilling witnesses,” “the One” (line 4); “threw one more taunt” (line 5); “save thyself,” “If thou be” (line 5–6); “bearing the scorn He could have silenced, for the very people throwing it” (line 7); “the whole song” (line 8); “stayed under scorn,” “win the scorners” (line 10); “ends of the world” (line 11); “would not come down” (line 12). Only DELIVER = RESCUE is glossed. “Thou / thyself / wagging” get no plain word, and line 3’s two KJV sentences will not fit a 9:16 balloon at speaking speed.

Line 10, “theirs, and ours,” shames a stranger with no reason given. In many places that public shame ends the video. The dawn walk toward a hill makes line 12, “Turn, and come,” look like a walk to that hill.

## 4. BIBLICALLY ACCURATE
Line 1 — OVERCLAIMED. The texts show matching words. They do not show the crowd reading a script.

Line 2 — RIGHT on the shared head gesture (Psalm 22:7; Matthew 27:39) and on the deliver-words (Psalm 22:8; Matthew 27:43). “Gestures” plural reaches past the one shared gesture (“shoot out the lip” stays in the psalm). “A thousand years” and “Messiah” are outside the supplied lines.

Line 3 — RIGHT. The psalm quote fairly shortens 22:8. The Matthew quote is 27:43, including words only Matthew has. Passers-by wagging heads is 27:39. “Rulers” is a loose label for the chief priests, scribes, and elders (27:41). “Nearly line for line” is fair. “They thought they were inventing the cruelty” — UNSUPPORTED.

Line 4 — OVERCLAIMED. Matching words do not state that the crowd was witnessing “this was the One.”

Line 5–6 — RIGHT for Matthew 27:40. “But He could have come down” — UNSUPPORTED in the supplied verses.

Line 7 — OVERCLAIMED. The taunt sets the test. These verses do not say staying proved he was the Son, that he could have silenced the scorn, or that he bore it for the people throwing it.

Line 8 — OVERCLAIMED. Matthew ties this scene to the psalm. “The whole song was about” him runs past 22:7–8, and David’s speaking voice leaves the “I” unclear.

Line 9 — RIGHT for the chapter: Matthew 27:42 has “King of Israel… come down.” That sentence is never spoken in the film. “He never did” belongs to the story around these verses.

Line 10 — UNSUPPORTED. Psalm 22:27 says the nations turn to the LORD. It does not say he stayed “to win the scorners,” and it does not include “ours.”

Line 11 — RIGHT in substance for Psalm 22:27, “turn unto the LORD.” “Ends with” lands a little early. “To Him,” meaning the crucified man, is a further step. “Script they never finished” is the line 1 metaphor again. OVERCLAIMED as an event.

Line 12 is a call. Nothing here is flat WRONG against the KJV. The solid center is the word match in line 3.

## 5. BEST VERSION POSSIBLE
The best explainer would show the two sentences in plain English, one under the other, name David as the writer, use one name for the text, and speak the King line if she uses “King.” She would save “ours” for a later film. She would end on Psalm 22:27 in plain words: people from every country turn to the LORD.

ONE highest-value change: replace “script / scripted,” and answer WHO in one spoken line — David wrote the words; the crowd said them at the cross.

Score: 5/10. The parallel is real. Stacked idioms, KJV, and a second sermon bury it for a worldwide viewer.

## CURIOSITY GAP
Opens at line 1 (card: “WHO SCRIPTED THE MOCKERY?”). Pays off at line 3, when the quotes match. Line 11 repeats the hook and pays the end of the song. The word WHO still has no name.

Score: 5/10. The quote match is worth the few seconds to line 3. The question on the card stays open.

## THREE SMALLEST FIXES
1. Line 1: “The people who insulted Jesus at the cross said words David had written long before. They did not know it.”
2. Line 3, narrator clause: “At the cross, Matthew records the same scene — people passing by shaking their heads, and the leaders saying almost the same words:”
3. Line 10: “He stayed and took their insults, so he could save the people who mocked him.”

VERDICT: NEEDS CHANGES — the Psalm 22:8 / Matthew 27:43 match is sound; the global viewer loses it at “scripted,” the unanswered WHO, KJV line 3, and “win the scorners.”
