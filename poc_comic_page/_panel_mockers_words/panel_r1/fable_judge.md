# Judge seat — The Mockers' Words (Psalm 22:7-8)

## 1. WHO IS THIS FOR
Named viewer: Daniel, 24, Manila, raised Catholic, scrolls Shorts on the bus. He knows Jesus died on a cross; he has never read Psalm 22. The card stops him; line 1 ("they just didn't know it") makes him wait; he finishes because the reveal lands by line 4. Sharer: the church-raised Bible reader who loves prophecy matches (lines 3, 11). NOT served: the true newcomer — "Messiah" (line 2) is never glossed, and the "old king" on screen is never named, so the card's WHO gets no answer. Under-served: the second-language viewer hit with ~60 words of KJV in one breath at line 3.

## 2. MAIN TAKEAWAY
"The people mocking Jesus were quoting a 1,000-year-old song about him without knowing it — and he stayed on the cross to save them." Line 4 carries the proof half ("unwilling witnesses that this was the One"); line 7 the grace half. Clear? Mostly. Two takeaways compete, but line 12 fuses them ("the One who would not come down"). Viewers will repeat the proof half far more easily than the grace half.

## 3. GLOBAL AUDIENCE
In my words: Who — Jesus on a cross, a crowd insulting him, a king who wrote a song 1,000 years earlier. What — the insults match the song. Why — it proves he is the one the song meant; he stayed to save the insulters; so turn to him.
Lost or misled:
- Line 3: two archaic quotes back to back; "if he will have him" reads as possession, means "wants him."
- Line 2: "Psalm" is never linked to "song" until line 8 — a newcomer asks "what song?"
- Line 10: "to win the scorners" — in many languages "win" = defeat. The gospel heart is the line most likely to be mistranslated. "Scorn" appears three times (lines 7, 10); "mock" is the simpler word already in the title.
- Line 11: "all the ends of the world" — a second-language ear hears "end of the world" (apocalypse).
- Card: WHO SCRIPTED THE MOCKERY? — answered with a book (line 2, "Psalm twenty-two recorded"), never a person.

## 4. BIBLICALLY ACCURATE
- Line 1 "reading from a script" — RIGHT as metaphor; line 3's "they thought they were inventing" keeps their guilt intact.
- Line 2 "A thousand years before" — RIGHT. "recorded how the Messiah would be mocked" — RIGHT by NT use (Matthew echoes Ps 22:7-8), mildly OVERCLAIMED in wording: the psalm never says "Messiah"; it is David's first-person prayer. "described" is safer.
- Line 3 both quotes — RIGHT, verbatim KJV (Ps 22:7-8; Matt 27:43). Passers-by wag heads (27:39), rulers say "He trusted" (27:41-43) — attribution RIGHT. "nearly line for line" — RIGHT.
- Lines 5-6 "save thyself. If thou be the Son of God, come down" — RIGHT, verbatim Matt 27:40. Order reversed (27:40 precedes 27:43), but "one more taunt" claims no sequence.
- Line 6 "He could have come down" — RIGHT doctrinally (Matt 26:53; John 10:18), UNSUPPORTED by the supplied text.
- Line 7 "for the very people throwing it" — RIGHT (Luke 23:34; Rom 5:8), outside the pack.
- Line 8 "the One the whole song was about" — mildly OVERCLAIMED: vv9-10 are David's own birth; "whole" is stronger than the NT states.
- Line 9 — RIGHT (Matt 27:42 "King of Israel... come down").
- Line 10 "to win the scorners" — RIGHT (Acts 2:36-41; 6:7 priests believed), outside the pack.
- Line 11 — RIGHT (Ps 22:27). The psalm says "unto the LORD"; reading Him as Jesus is orthodox but taken silently.
- Line 12 "Turn" — RIGHT, earned from 22:27.
Nothing WRONG.

## 5. BEST VERSION POSSIBLE
The best explainer would show the match, not read it: two balloons side by side — "let him deliver him" (psalm) / "let him deliver him now" (crowd) — and let the eye do the proving. Line 3 is ~65 words of 1611 English in ~15 seconds, at the exact payoff moment. The ONE highest-value change: cut line 3 to one matched pair, each fragment under ten words, and move "for he said, I am the Son of God" to just before line 6. This helps the scroller, the second-language reader and the newcomer at once; the Bible reader loses nothing. Score: 7/10 — sound spine, earned CTA, no doctrinal error; loses on quote density, the unnamed WHO, and triple "scorn."

## CURIOSITY GAP
Opens at the card + line 1; pays off at lines 3-4. Second gap: line 6 ("But He could have come down") → line 7. Third: line 11 ("a script they never finished") → line 12. Score 7/10. Worth the wait — the payoff arrives by ~20s, good for retention — but the card's WHO never gets a name, and the proof is heard only by those who parse KJV fast.

## THREE SMALLEST FIXES
1. Line 10 → "He stayed under their mocking — and ours — to rescue the mockers." (Reuses the card's own word RESCUE; kills the win = defeat trap.)
2. Line 11 → "...the song ends with people from every nation on earth turning to Him." (Ps 22:27 "all the kindreds of the nations"; removes the apocalypse misread.)
3. Line 2 → "A thousand years before, a king named David wrote a song — Psalm twenty-two — describing how the Messiah would be mocked: the gestures, and the words." (Answers the card's WHO; links psalm = song for line 8.)
