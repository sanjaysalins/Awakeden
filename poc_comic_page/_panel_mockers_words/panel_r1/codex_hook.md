## 3. GLOBAL AUDIENCE (who / what / why in my own words; where I got lost or misled, quote the line; words that fail in translation)

People ridicule Jesus during his execution; their gestures and words resemble an earlier psalm; the film interprets his refusal to escape as saving love (lines 3–7).

The paired quotations are the strongest evidence, but line 3 demands substantial listening comprehension without a pause. Speech balloons help, yet viewers must process archaic language while comparing two passages.

“He will have him” means something different from ordinary modern “have”; “deliver,” “scorn,” and “the One” also impose comprehension costs (lines 3–4, 10). The rescue card addresses only one.

“They were reading from a script they never finished” implies the mockers’ knowledge or reading history, which the supplied text does not establish (line 11).

- **RIGHT:** “wagging their heads” and “He trusted in God” accurately reflect Matthew; the correspondence with Psalm 22 is strong (line 3).

- **UNSUPPORTED by this pack:** “A thousand years before,” “They thought they were inventing,” and “He could have come down” require evidence beyond the supplied verses (lines 2–3, 6).

- **RIGHT, with qualification:** worldwide turning appears near the psalm’s end, in verse 27; “the song ends with” compresses its location (line 11).
