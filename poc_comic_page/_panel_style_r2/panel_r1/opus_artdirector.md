# Seat: short-video art director (Opus)

## 1. FIRST IMPRESSION
A warm, reverent motion-comic of an old man at a door and a welcoming Jesus. It's clearly a church-friendly devotional, aimed at people who already half-believe. It looks better made than most AI Bible shorts. But in the first second (f_0.3) I'm looking at the back of a hood, a hand and a wall of planks. There's no face, no eyes and nothing moving. The hook text does the stopping, not the picture. I would pause on the text, not on the art.

## 2. SCORES
- **Scroll-stopping in 1 second: 5.** The hook text is strong. The picture is a back view of brown on brown with no eye contact.
- **Reverent and trustworthy: 8.** Calm, warm light and a dignified Jesus. Nothing cheap or gimmicky.
- **Global appeal (any culture, ESL): 6.** The comic look travels well. The KJV verse box ("giveth", "in no wise") is hard for ESL viewers, and the Jesus is a Western type.
- **Consistent shot to shot: 5.** The old man changes from hooded (f_0.3) to short-haired and bare-headed (f_1.8, f_9.0). Jesus changes from a stern, realistic stare (f_16.0) to a soft smile (f_30.0) to a puffy, cartoon-ish grin (f_58.5).
- **Feels alive (motion): 4.** f_44.5, f_46.0 and f_52.0 are almost the same frame, about 8 seconds with nothing changing. f_58.5 to f_61.7 is a small tilt.
- **Readable on a phone: 6.** The big hook cards are great. The caption boxes are fine. The red verse text on beige (f_21.0, f_25.5) is low contrast. Grid panels shrink faces to thumbnail size.

## 3. WEAKEST THREE VISUAL THINGS
1. **Grids shrink the emotion (f_16.0, f_36.5, f_44.5).** Three or four panels on a 9:16 phone make every face about 1/4 of the screen. Worse, the pencil-reveal cells sit EMPTY or half-sketched for seconds: the blank bottom-right in f_16.0 and the empty top of the lower-right cell in f_36.5. On a feed, an empty box reads as "still loading", not as craft.
2. **Text pile-up (f_25.5).** Three text layers fight each other: the "IN NO WISE = NEVER" card, the verse box and a grey pencil sketch behind both. It's the key line of the film, and it's the busiest frame. f_21.0 also puts the "JOHN 6:37" chip on top of the art.
3. **Halftone dot screen over everything, captions included (f_9.0, f_30.0).** On a compressed phone video it turns into moire and mush, especially in the dark blue skies. It also makes the caption boxes look dirty. It cheapens the clean ink you can see in the goldseam proofs.

## 4. VS THE FEED
**Better** than the oil-painting AI slop: it's more graphic, reads faster and looks less uncanny. **Better** than cheap 3D/Pixar Jesus: it's more adult and more sincere. **Worse** than live-action or cinematic realism on the hook, because those open on a human face in pain and this opens on a back. **Worse** than anime or motion-graphics on energy, because they cut every 1 to 2 seconds and this holds 8 seconds. Overall it's different in a good way (an ownable look), but it's paced like a picture book, not a feed video.

## 5. BEST ALTERNATIVE
Keep this family, but move toward **round-2 strip, image 1**: bolder line, a radiant burst behind Jesus and a figure crossing the threshold. It has a single clear action and backlit contrast that reads at thumbnail size. **goldseam_resurrection_heroshot.png** shows the right finish: clean ink, no dot screen and a real rim-light. Avoid the watercolour (strip 1, image 4) and the pencil-only looks (strip 2, images 4 and 5). They're too pale to stop a thumb. This direction isn't wrong. It's under-finished and under-paced.

## 6. ONE BIGGEST CHANGE + SMALL ONES
**BIGGEST: one full-bleed panel at a time, and open on a FACE.** Put the old man's frightened eyes, close-up and looking toward camera, in the first second. Then cut between big single panels every 2 to 3 seconds. Use a grid only as a one-beat "page reveal" flourish (at most twice), never as the resting state. Never show an empty cell. **Costs money** for one new close-up still for the hook (and re-animation). The rest is mostly a re-edit of existing panels, which **costs nothing**.

Small ones:
- **Drop the halftone overlay**, or cut it to about 20% and keep it off caption boxes. *Costs nothing.*
- **One text layer at a time.** Remove the pencil layer behind "= NEVER", show the verse as a separate beat, and make the verse dark ink on light, not red on beige. *Costs nothing.*
- **Lock the faces.** Regenerate f_58.5's cartoon Jesus and the bare-headed old man to match the f_30.0 Jesus and the hooded f_0.3 man. Add small motion (cloth, flame, light sweep) to the 44 to 52 s hold. *Costs money.*

## 7. KEEP
The night-blue vs warm-gold palette, with the gold coming in only when Jesus does. Bold black ink lines. The big white/yellow hook cards (f_0.3, f_46.0 "SO COME."). The door and scroll motifs, with the dropped scroll (f_44.5) as a wordless payoff. The calm, reverent tone with no gimmicks.
