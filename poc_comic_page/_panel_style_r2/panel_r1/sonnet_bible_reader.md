## 1. FIRST IMPRESSION
A night-blue and gold painted comic about a frightened old man at a door, then Jesus welcoming him. It is made for Christian viewers who want comfort, and it is easy to follow without Bible knowledge. I would stop on f_0.3.png: the big hand on the wood and the question "AFRAID GOD WILL TURN YOU AWAY?" are strong. As a churchgoer my gut reaction is mostly warm, but a few frames make me hesitate to share it (see section 3). It feels sincere, not mocking. It is a little "app-made".

## 2. SCORES 0-10
- Scroll-stopping in 1 second: 8. f_0.3.png has a huge hand, a clear emotion and a bold question.
- Reverent and trustworthy: 6. The scenes are respectful, but Jesus is drawn like a handsome model, and the final frame is too cheerful.
- Global appeal (any culture, ESL): 8. The story is visual (door, fear, welcome), and the caption text is short and plain.
- Looks consistent shot to shot: 7. The ink, the colours and the old man hold steady. Jesus's face changes between frames.
- Feels alive (motion): 5. Judging only from the stills, I see pencil-to-ink and layout changes (f_1.8.png), but the panels look like static pictures.
- Readable on a phone: 7. The hook and the closing text are huge. The caption boxes are small, and the halftone dots add noise.

## 3. WEAKEST THREE VISUAL THINGS
1. **f_58.5 / f_61.7 end frames (open-armed Jesus, f_61.7.png).** He has a wide grin and a cartoon-like glowing outline, which looks like a church-bulletin or stock "Jesus" with a salesman smile. It is much less reverent than the earlier frames. His face also looks different from the one in f_21.0.png and f_30.0.png (younger and softer, different hair). This is the frame most likely to make a believer say "not quite right".
2. **f_21.0.png and f_30.0.png (Jesus panels).** He looks like a model posing: handsome, stiff, and standing straight at the camera. In f_30.0.png he is smiling while the scroll panel says "Not maybe. Not if you clean up first." That wording with that smile feels slightly off. The cream paper caption boxes, with the dot overlay on faces and robes, make the picture look dirty and low-resolution.
3. **f_1.8.png and f_9.0.png (split layout and caption box).** The pencil sketch in the bottom panel clashes with the hook text on top of it, and the sketch is muddy. In f_9.0.png the caption sits on top of the old man's art. The caption box font is plain Arial-like, which feels cheap against the painted art. The "John 6:37" label in f_21.0.png is tiny.

## 4. VS THE FEED
Better than most oil-painting AI Bible shorts, which look glossy and samey. Better than 3D/Pixar shorts, which are childish. The bold ink and the limited palette make it more trustworthy than photoreal AI Jesus, which can look creepy. It is worse than a real live-action or a really good illustration, because the halftone overlay looks like a filter. It is different from the feed in a good way: it looks like an actual graphic novel.

## 5. BEST ALTERNATIVE
This look is already close to the best. In the contact strips, the second-round pick with the bold ink line, the warm glowing arch and Jesus welcoming (round 2, first image) is the closest to the film, and it has a cleaner and more reverent Jesus. If I had to name a challenger, it is the watercolour (round 1, fourth). It is gentle and very reverent, and it would be loved by churchgoers, but it would not stop the scroll on a phone as hard. Keep the current look and fix the Jesus face and the overlay.

## 6. CHANGES
**Biggest:** Make Jesus one consistent, calmer, humbler face (a gentle, serious expression, not a grin and not a model pose), and drop the glow outline in f_61.7.png. This costs money (re-draw the Jesus pictures).

Small ones:
- Remove or lighten the halftone dot filter over faces and robes. Costs nothing (editing).
- Make the caption boxes larger, with a better font and without sitting on the faces. Costs nothing.
- Keep the final frame's emotion calm, e.g. the quiet "open hand" from f_52.0.png instead of arms spread wide. Costs money for a new picture, or nothing if you reuse f_52.0 as the ending.
