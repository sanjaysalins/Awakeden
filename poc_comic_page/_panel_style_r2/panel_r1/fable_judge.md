# Fable seat — visual style judge (blind, whole-world audience)

## 1. FIRST IMPRESSION
A painted-ink comic page that slowly comes alive: an old man with a scroll at a locked door, then Jesus at the door. Night blue + warm gold, thick ink. It reads as adult, not kiddie, and the big two-tone text cards (f_0.3, f_25.5, f_46.0, f_58.5) carry the message with no Bible knowledge needed. Would I stop? Probably — for the question on the card, not for the picture. The opening image (f_0.3) is the back of a hood and a hand; no face, no colour clash. Gut: a good, distinctive look that has been slightly spoiled in the edit by a dot-screen filter and some careless crops.

## 2. SCORES
- **Scroll-stop in 1s: 6** — bold question text does the work; the first picture is quiet (no face, no eyes).
- **Reverent / trustworthy: 7** — clean ink is dignified; drops at f_58.5 (sticker glow, arms-wide cartoon pose, eyes looking away).
- **Global / ESL: 7** — comics are a world language; door/lamp/scroll need no words; "IN NO WISE = NEVER" (f_25.5) is exactly right for ESL. The fair-skinned, light-eyed Jesus (f_16.0) is the standard feed Jesus — instantly recognisable, but the one choice a non-Western viewer may read as "not for me".
- **Consistent: 7** — palette and characters hold, but f_0.3/f_1.0 are clean and from f_1.8 on everything wears a halftone dot screen; the Jesus face drifts (stern f_16.0, warm f_21.0, cartoon f_58.5).
- **Alive: 5** — f_44.5, f_46.0, f_52.0 are the same page for ~8s; the pencil-to-ink reveal is the only real "event".
- **Phone readable: 6** — big cards 9/10; small captions 4/10 (dots laid over the box, bottom-edge placement, three text objects at once on f_25.5).

## 3. WEAKEST THREE
1. **Halftone dot screen over everything** (f_5.5, f_9.0, f_44.5): on a 6-inch phone it reads as compression noise, muddies the cream caption boxes, and makes the film look like a "comic filter" app. The chosen proofs (goldseam_cross_final.png, goldseam_resurrection_heroshot.png) have no dots — the film added them.
2. **Jesus's face cropped** — f_30.0 (eyes cut off at the panel top) and f_44.5/f_46.0/f_52.0 (only the beard at the frame edge) while the old man looks up at a headless figure, under "SO COME." The one face the film is about is missing at the turn.
3. **Text clutter and placement** — f_1.8 keeps the hook card up while the first caption has started (two messages at once); f_21.0/f_25.5 put the red verse box on the very bottom edge, inside the TikTok/Reels UI zone; f_25.5 stacks big card + verse box + pencil sketch.

Also: the whole f_44.5–f_52.0 page is one flat yellow; the night-blue that defines the look vanishes for 8s. f_36.5 bottom-right is mostly empty paper.

## 4. VS THE FEED
- vs **oil-painting AI** (most Bible shorts): better — rarer, sharper edges, reads at thumbnail size; but f_58.5 drifts back into the glowing-Jesus cliché.
- vs **live-action / cinematic AI video**: worse on motion and real faces; better on "someone made this" — the ink reveal is the proof of hand.
- vs **3D / Pixar**: better — this reads adult.
- vs **anime / webtoon**: just different; this is Western-comic, which plays weaker in East/South-East Asia than manga would.
- vs **motion graphics**: the big cards already borrow its strength; keep.
Net: distinctive and better than the oil default, if executed clean.

## 5. BEST ALTERNATIVE
No letters in the file names, so I number left-to-right. Nothing in the strips beats the chosen look as a whole. Two worth borrowing, not switching to: **strip 1, panel 2** (dramatic purple-blue sky, backlit Jesus) — more colour range in wide shots; **round 2, panel 4** (black-and-white + one orange flame) — the most scroll-stopping single image shown, and the cleanest on a phone. Use it for the first second only, then let colour flood in with the ink reveal. The watercolour (strip 1, #4) and engraving (round 2, #5) are too soft/old for a feed. Verdict: this one is already the best; fix execution.

## 6. CHANGES
**Biggest: remove the halftone/vignette layer (or cut it to ~20%) so the whole film looks like f_0.3.** Costs nothing (editing).

Small:
1. Reframe every Jesus panel so the head is whole (f_30.0, f_44.5–f_52.0); use the f_21.0 portrait (direct eye contact, best face in the film) behind the final card instead of the arms-wide f_58.5. Costs nothing.
2. Lift all captions out of the bottom 20% and right 10%; never more than two text objects on screen; kill the hook card the moment the first caption lands. Costs nothing.
3. Bring night-blue back into the f_44.5 page (colour grade) and cut its hold to ~4s. Costs nothing. (If re-rendering: a hook still with the old man's face visible, eyes to camera — costs money.)

## 7. KEEP
- Bold ink line + night-blue/gold palette (f_0.3, f_25.5).
- The big white+gold text cards and the "= NEVER" gloss — the strongest ESL asset here.
- The pencil-to-ink reveal (f_1.8, f_16.0 → f_21.0) — the hand-made signal; make it faster, not rarer.
- Red serif for Scripture vs black sans for narration — tells the viewer "these are the Bible's own words" without saying so.
- Wordless props: door, lamp, scroll, nailed scroll (f_36.5).
- Small top-left brand mark.
