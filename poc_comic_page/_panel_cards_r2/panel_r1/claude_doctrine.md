## VERDICT
REVISE. Most plain-word cards are sound. A few swaps are loose or wrong for the verse. Two hooks make claims that Scripture does not directly make.

## LINE-BY-LINE

**2, 3. CHANGE.** `BEHELD = LOOKED AT`
- Numbers 21:9 says "when he beheld the serpent of brass, he lived." The KJV "beheld" means looked with attention and expectation. "Looked at" is too weak and drops the faith-look the type depends on.
- Better: `BEHELD = LOOKED (AND TRUSTED)`. If that is too long, use `BEHELD = LOOKED AT, FIXED`.
- Safest short version: `BEHELD = LOOKED STEADILY AT`.

**4. CHANGE.** `EVEN SO = IN THE SAME WAY; WHOSOEVER = ANYONE WHO`
- "Even so" is fine. In John 3:14–15 it is a comparison, "just like that."
- "Whosoever" is fine. Keep it.
- Problem: two cards on one verse pair may crowd the screen. Show `EVEN SO = JUST LIKE THAT` first, because it points to the serpent comparison and is plainer for ESL viewers than "in the same way."

**5. CHANGE.** `TARRY = WAIT`
- In Matthew 26:38 the instruction is "tarry ye here, and watch with me." "Wait" is right, but it loses the "stay here with me" meaning.
- Better: `TARRY = STAY`.
- Hook `TOO WEAK TO PRAY?` is accurate to "the flesh is weak" (Matt 26:41). However, the verse is a rebuke plus mercy aimed at the disciples. Better: `TOO TIRED TO PRAY?`, which is closer to "their eyes were heavy."

**6. CHANGE.** `RAIMENT = CLOTHES`
- Luke 23:34 says "parted his raiment, and cast lots." In this verse "raiment" means garments. "Clothes" is okay.
- Better: `RAIMENT = CLOTHING`.
- Hook `HE PRAYED FOR HIS KILLERS` is accurate. The hands that crucified him were Roman soldiers, and the verse says "them." Hostile voices will say the "killers" are the Jews. Better: `HE PRAYED FOR THOSE KILLING HIM`. This keeps it tied to the soldiers and the scene.

**7. CHANGE.**
- `REDEEMED = BOUGHT BACK` is true to Psalm 31:5, "thou hast redeemed me." The ransom sense is right. Keep it.
- `COMMEND = ENTRUST` is true to Luke 23:46. Keep it.
- Hook `AFRAID / TO DIE?` implies Jesus was afraid. Luke 23:46 shows him confident and surrendering. That is a doctrinal framing problem.
- Better: `HOW DID JESUS DIE?` or `HIS LAST WORDS, TO WHOM?`

**9. CHANGE.**
- `GAVE UP THE GHOST = DIED` is correct in meaning. But "ghost" here is "spirit," and it points to the voluntary nature of his death (John 10:18).
- Better: `GAVE UP THE GHOST = DIED (BY CHOICE)`. Keep this only if the narration supports it. If not, leave as `DIED`.
- Hook `WHAT WAS / FINISHED?` is fine. Keep it.

**11. CHANGE.**
- `FORSAKEN = ABANDONED` is true to the Hebrew and to the sense in Psalm 22:1.
- Hook `EVER FEEL FORGOTTEN BY GOD?` is a risk. It treats Jesus' cry as comfort for our feelings. It also implies God forgot him. Psalm 22 says God did not hide his face (v.24).
- Better: `WHY DID JESUS CRY THIS?`

**12. CHANGE.**
- `POTSHERD = BROKEN POT PIECE` is right for Psalm 22:15.
- `CLEAVETH = STICKS` is right in meaning, but it is the weakest swap. Better: `CLEAVETH = STICKS TO`.
- Hook `WHY DID JESUS / SAY I THIRST?` is OK. John 19:28 says it was "that the scripture might be fulfilled." Fine.

**14, 15, 16. CHANGE (16 only).**
- `CHRIST WON. / WHAT'S STILL TO COME?` could imply Christ's victory is incomplete. Genesis 3:15 is fulfilled at the cross, with final consummation in Rev 20. This is acceptable only if the narration says it clearly. Better: `CHRIST WON. WHAT'S LEFT?`
- Lines 14 and 15 are OK. In 15, `WHOSE HEAD? / WHOSE HEEL?` is good.

**17. CHANGE.** `JONAS = JONAH`
- Right. Matthew 12:39 says "Jonas," and "Jonah" is the OT form. Keep it.
- Hook `ONE SIGN ONLY. / WHICH ONE?` is OK. Jesus does say "no sign … but the sign of the prophet Jonas." Keep it.

**18. OK.** `SEPULCHRE = TOMB` is correct.

**19. CHANGE.** `VERILY = TRULY`
- Luke 23:43 says "Verily I say unto thee." "Truly" is right. Better for emphasis: `VERILY = I TELL YOU THE TRUTH`.
- Hook `TOO LATE FOR GOD?` is fine. It points to the thief's last-minute faith and Jesus' answer, "Today."

**20. CHANGE.** `BEHOLD = LOOK`
- John 19:26 uses "Behold thy son." Here "behold" carries a sense of "see, receive, take care of." "Look" is too flat.
- Better: `BEHOLD = SEE (AND TAKE CARE OF)`.
- Hook `DOES JESUS SEE YOUR PAIN?` is a pastoral claim the verse does not make directly. It does show Jesus caring for Mary in pain. Better: `JESUS SAW HIS MOTHER'S PAIN`.

## BIGGEST RISK
Line 7's hook, `AFRAID / TO DIE?`, frames Jesus as afraid in a verse where he is calm and trusting. Line 11's hook is the second risk: it turns a doctrinally heavy cry into a feelings prompt and implies God forgot him.

## WHAT IS GOOD (keep)
- `SEPULCHRE = TOMB`, `JONAS = JONAH`, `POTSHERD = BROKEN POT PIECE`, `REDEEMED = BOUGHT BACK`, `COMMEND = ENTRUST`, `FORSAKEN = ABANDONED`.
- Hooks 5, 12, 14, 15 and 17 each open a real question without twisting the text.
- The plan to add no card on pieces that already have a title card is sound.
