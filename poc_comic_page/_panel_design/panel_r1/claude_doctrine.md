## VERDICT: REVISE — 5.5/10

The KJV byte-for-byte gate is good. The rest leaves several doctrinal gaps. Some of them are caused by the design itself.

## BIGGEST RISK

> "Spoken: white fill, ink rule. Thought: grey dashed cloud, italic. Invented lines: max 2 per episode, max 6 words."
> "An invented line may only say what the narration already says. No new claims."

The man's thought is "What if He says no?" The film then has Jesus answer it. The two lines are paired as a question and an answer, and that pairing is itself a claim. It makes the fabricated question the thing John 6:37 answers.

John 6:37 was said to a crowd that had seen signs and still did not believe (6:36). It was not said to an anxious seeker. Dropping the verse into an invented dialogue changes its context. It then reads as an exchange between a particular man and Christ.

The "invented" flag does not fix this. The viewer cannot see the flag. The flag also only catches the man's words. It cannot catch what the pairing implies.

The second problem is the rule "may only say what the narration already says." The narration says "rehearsing whether you're allowed in." The thought reads as a fear of rejection. But "What if He says no?" quietly adds that Jesus might say no, which the narration did not say. Your own rule fails on your own example.

## WHAT TO CUT OR SIMPLIFY

- **Cut the man's thought balloon in the proof episode.** The narration already carries the feeling. You are adding an invented speaker only to get a prettier dialogue beat.
- **Cut "dialogue beats narration" as a design goal.** The recipe wants dialogue up to 30%. That pushes the engine to find speech, so the incentive points toward invention.
- **Drop the "OLD = PLAIN" decode card for theological terms.** "In no wise = never" is safe. For other words ("cometh," "rest," "heavy laden") a one-word swap can flatten what the KJV means. Limit decode cards to archaic idioms, not doctrinal words.
- **Cut the "highlight words as the voice says them" option.** It adds complexity without helping reverence.

## WHAT IS MISSING

- **Verse boundaries.** The gate checks that the text equals KJV byte for byte. It does not check that the balloon is a complete thought. Chained balloons of 8 words or fewer will break verses mid-clause, and fragments like "and him that cometh to me" can be read alone. A fragment of Jesus's speech can be misquoted even when each word is exact.
- **Speaker attribution.** Whose speech counts as Jesus's? Some red-letter material is disputed, for example John 3:16–21. A balloon asserts "Jesus said this." There is no rule saying a balloon is allowed only where the Gospel text attributes the words to Him in direct speech.
- **Context line.** There is no rule about when and to whom the words were spoken. The gate checks only that a verse tag appears.
- **Decorum around the Father and the Spirit.** The design has no rule on how God the Father's speech is shown. A third balloon style would arrive eventually.
- **Reverence QA.** "Calm, low, unhurried" is a direction for the voice actor. There is no gate on an AI Jesus voice doing KJV cadence, and none on the effect of a comic sound or a pop-in on Christ's words.
- **Pop animation.** "Balloon pops, 0.18s, tiny overshoot" applies a bouncy effect to Christ's speech. The SFX rule prohibits sound on sacred beats, but the visual bounce is not covered.
- **Kling hands and mouths.** Jesus's mouth is never animated, yet the tail points at a mouth anchor. If the clip drifts, a closed or distorted mouth under a balloon looks wrong.

## THREE SMALLEST FIXES

1. **Rule:** "Invented human lines are banned on any panel within 10 seconds of a Jesus balloon. The first episode ships with zero invented lines. Any later one needs doctrine-panel sign-off on the pairing, not just the words."
2. **Gate:** "A Jesus balloon requires `kjv_ref`, `speaker_attribution: direct_speech_in_gospel`, and `context_line` (who he spoke to and when, ≤ 12 words). The context line appears on the verse tag or in the description. Chaining must break only at KJV punctuation (`;` `,` `.`), and a chain must be played in full or not at all."
3. **Visual rule:** "Jesus balloons enter with a plain 0.25s fade. No overshoot, no pop, and no tail wobble. Only the ordinary human balloons may pop."

## ODDS

The design is more watchable than a plain Ken Burns comic with a **55–70% chance** (wide error bars, because the sources are AI seats and not real viewers). Most of that lift comes from the hook card, the plain words and the 4-second rule. These are not tied to the balloons. Wet ink and the gutter break are more likely to help a little than to matter. The doctrinal risks here come from the balloon and thought-cloud additions, which add little to retention but carry most of the reverence risk.
