**(a) Narration**

[narrator] King David wrote these words. A crowd at a cross spoke words very like them, a thousand years later. The man on that cross was Jesus, the Son of God.

[narrator] First, David wrote:

[david] "He trusted on the LORD that he would deliver him." [Psalm 22:8]

[narrator] Then the crowd said:

[mocker] "He trusted in God; let him deliver him now." [Matthew 27:43]

[narrator] Two voices. One taunt.

[narrator] They said more to Him.

[mocker] "If thou be the Son of God, come down from the cross." [Matthew 27:40]

[narrator] He could have come down. Jesus had said,

[narrator] "No man taketh it from me." [John 10:18]

[narrator] He gave His life Himself. So He stayed.

[narrator] While they mocked, Jesus prayed for them:

[narrator] "Father, forgive them; for they know not what they do." [Luke 23:34]

[narrator] He stayed for the very people who mocked Him.

[narrator] Now the ending. Psalm 22 does not end with mocking. David ended it with a promise: people from every nation will turn to God.

[david] "Remember and turn unto the LORD." [Psalm 22:27]

[narrator] That means people like you, from any nation. No one is pushing you. The invitation is open. He stayed. Turn to the One who stayed.

**(b) Word count:** about 179 spoken words, including the quotes.

**(c)**
- **Spine:** David wrote the taunt, the crowd spoke a very similar one, Jesus chose to stay (and forgave them), and David's song ends with every nation turning to the LORD.
- **Cut:**
  - I dropped the thieves (Matthew 27:44) and the temple taunt, because they added no new point.
  - I dropped "worm," "shake the head" and the womb verses, because they need explaining and overclaim.
  - I dropped "kindreds" and "ends of the world," because they are rare words or easy to misread.
  - I made no claim about what the crowd thought, and no claim that the whole Psalm is only about Jesus.
