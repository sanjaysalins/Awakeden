## 1. WHO IS THIS FOR
A churchgoing teen or adult who half-knows the crucifixion story and likes "hidden link" Bible facts. They stop at the card "WHO WROTE THE MOCKERY?" and finish for the Psalm 22 / Matthew match. They share it with a friend at church. Not served: a total newcomer who doesn't know who David is (line 3), or a skeptic who wants proof of prophecy.

## 2. MAIN TAKEAWAY
"The mockers at the cross said almost the same words David wrote 1,000 years earlier, and Jesus stayed and forgave anyway, so turn to Him." Lines 5, 7 and 11 carry it. The parallel is clear. The jump from forgiveness to "turn" (lines 9-11) is less clear.

## 3. GLOBAL AUDIENCE
**Who/what/why:** Crowds mocked Jesus. A king wrote nearly the same mockery long before. Jesus could have escaped but didn't, and He forgave them.
**Where I got lost:**
- Line 3: "People laugh at him too, and he writes down what they say" is a fragment. It's unclear who "him" is until David is named.
- Line 5: "shout one more thing" does not say who they are.
- Line 7: "He gives it Himself" is vague. Gives what? To whom?
- Lines 9-10: "people from every nation will turn to God" appears with no setup. Why does it matter here?
- "Turn unto the LORD" (line 10) and "turn to the One who stayed" (line 11) are idioms. They are fine for believers but abstract for a newcomer.
- "Deliver", glossed in line 3, is good.

## 4. BIBLICALLY ACCURATE
- Line 1 "People at the cross shout at Jesus": RIGHT. Matt 27:39-40.
- Line 2 "He trusted in God; let him deliver him now" [Matt 27:43]: RIGHT but truncated. The verse continues "if he will have him". The ellipsis is silent, and the quote ends mid-sentence. A brief note, not an error.
- Line 3 "a thousand years earlier, a king named David": RIGHT. About 1000 BC is the usual date. It is an assumption that Psalm 22 is David's, though the psalm's own heading says so. OVERCLAIMED lightly on "People laugh at him too, and he writes down what they say". Ps 22:7 says "All they that see me laugh me to scorn". The psalm does not say these are people laughing at David in any recorded event, and "writes down what they say" is interpretive but fair.
- Line 4 [Psalm 22:8]: RIGHT, verbatim. Note the KJV reads "He trusted on the LORD that he would deliver him".
- Line 5 "Almost the same words": RIGHT. Compare "trusted on the LORD" with "trusted in God". "Deliver him" is identical in both.
- Line 6 [Matt 27:40]: RIGHT, verbatim, though it starts mid-verse. The film doesn't say it was "passing by" in Matt 27:39. It does say "People passing by", which is RIGHT.
- Line 7 "He could have": UNSUPPORTED by the supplied text alone. Matt 26:53 is supplied but speaks of twelve legions of angels, and the verse supplied (26:53) doesn't say that. The film's wording relies on John 10:18, which is RIGHT: "No man taketh it from me, but I lay it down of myself". The paraphrase "no one takes His life from Him" is RIGHT. "He stayed" is an inference. "And He prayed for them": RIGHT, Luke 23:34.
- Line 8 [Luke 23:34]: RIGHT, verbatim. The pack shows the quote without "Then said Jesus," which is acceptable.
- Line 9 "Near its end, David's song says…every nation will turn to God": RIGHT. Ps 22:27 "All the ends of the world…". "Every nation" is a fair paraphrase.
- Line 10 [Psalm 22:27]: RIGHT. The snippet "shall remember and turn unto the LORD" is verbatim but trimmed from the subject "All the ends of the world".
- Line 11: an application, not a claim.

## 5. BEST VERSION POSSIBLE
The best explainer would anchor each voice with a clear "who": "The crowd said…" and "King David wrote…". It would also earn the bridge from the mockery to the nations (lines 9-10) in one sentence, for example, "That same psalm ends with the whole world turning to God." The highest-value change is to fix lines 3 and 9 so a newcomer knows who is speaking and why the ending matters.
**Score: 6/10.**

## CURIOSITY GAP
It opens at the card "WHO WROTE THE MOCKERY?" and line 1. It pays off at line 4, when David's words match, and again at line 5. Score: 7/10. Worth the wait, though the answer, "David wrote it first", arrives vaguely in line 3.

## THREE SMALLEST FIXES
1. Line 3: replace with "Deliver means rescue. A thousand years earlier, King David wrote a song. In it, crowds laughed at him, too."
2. Line 7: replace "He gives it Himself" with "He gives His life Himself."
3. Line 9: replace with "That same song ends with a promise: people from every nation will turn to God."
