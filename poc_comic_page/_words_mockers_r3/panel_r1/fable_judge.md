# Independent judge — The Mockers' Words (Psalm 22:7-8)

## 1. WHO IS THIS FOR (one named viewer; who stops, who finishes, who shares; who is NOT served)
Named viewer: Grace, 26, Manila, grew up near a church, has never read a Psalm, scrolls Shorts on the bus. She stops only if the first card's word "MOCKERY" lands — it is a hard word for her; "INSULTS" would stop more of her. She finishes because line 5 ("a thousand years apart") hands her a fact she did not have. She shares if she is already Christian, as a "prophecy came true" proof clip.
NOT served: the total newcomer who does not know Jesus is nailed up and dying — the film never says "they are killing him," so "come down from the cross" (line 6) carries no stakes for them. Also NOT served: the skeptic, because line 3 ("he writes down what they say") hands them the objection the film never meets — "Matthew just copied the Psalm."

## 2. MAIN TAKEAWAY (one sentence a viewer could say back; which line carries it; did the film make it clear?)
"The men insulting Jesus were repeating a thousand-year-old song without knowing it — and that same song says people everywhere will turn to God, so I can too." Line 5 carries the first half; line 11 the second. Clear? Half. The film proves the words match (lines 2-5) but never says WHY the match matters to me. It jumps from "nations will turn" (line 9) to "you can turn" (line 11) with the logic left silent.

## 3. GLOBAL AUDIENCE (who / what / why in my own words; where I got lost or misled, quote the line; words that fail in translation)
Who: Jesus on a cross, people insulting him; a king a thousand years earlier insulted the same way. What: the insults were written down long before they were shouted. Why: Jesus chose to stay and die, and the old song says people will turn to God.
Lost at line 9: "David's song" — line 3 said he "writes down what they say"; nobody said it was a song. Lost at line 5: "Almost the same words" — but line 2 said "God" and line 4 said "the LORD"; a newcomer does not know these are the same name. Line 7 "He could have." — a two-word fragment; ESL ears miss what he could have done.
Fails in translation: "mockery" (card). "Deliver" is rescued by the card — good. "Come down from the cross" assumes you know he is fixed to it. "Turn to" (line 11) survives in most languages.

## 4. BIBLICALLY ACCURATE (claim by claim: RIGHT / OVERCLAIMED / UNSUPPORTED / WRONG, quote the line; say only what you can support)
- L1 "People at the cross shout at Jesus" — RIGHT (Matt 27:39-44).
- L2 quote — RIGHT, verbatim. But the speakers are the chief priests, scribes and elders (27:41), not "the crowd" the [mocker] label implies — OVERCLAIMED (minor).
- L3 "Deliver means rescue" — RIGHT. "a thousand years earlier" — RIGHT (round). "a king named David ... writes down" — rests on the Psalm's heading, which the pack does not supply; I cannot verify it from the pack.
- L4 quote — RIGHT, verbatim.
- L5 "Almost the same words" — RIGHT.
- L6 quote — RIGHT, verbatim; "passing by" (L5) is RIGHT (27:39).
- L7 "He could have" / "no one takes His life ... gives it Himself" — RIGHT (John 10:18). "So He stayed" — fair inference, RIGHT. "And He prayed for them" — OVERCLAIMED on sequence: Luke places the prayer at the nailing (23:33-34), before the rulers' derision (23:35), and "them" is not specifically Matthew's mockers. Substance sound.
- L8 quote — RIGHT, verbatim.
- L9 "Near its end ... every nation will turn to God" — RIGHT (22:27).
- L10 quote — RIGHT, verbatim fragment.
- L11 "turn to the One who stayed" — the Psalm says turn to the LORD; the film has only called Jesus "Son of God." Orthodox, but UNSUPPORTED inside the film: the step Jesus = the LORD is never shown.
- Pack note: the text labelled Matthew 26:53 is 26:54's wording. The film does not quote it; no viewer harm.

## 5. BEST VERSION POSSIBLE (what would the best explainer in the world do differently; the ONE highest-value change; score 0-10 against that bar)
The best explainer would say the knot out loud: the song got the insults right a thousand years early, so trust what it says next. It would also give Jesus His own voice at L8 — right now the mockers and David speak, and the only speaker who matters is read by the narrator. ONE highest-value change: rewrite L9 to carry the logic (fix 3 below). Score: 6/10.

## CURIOSITY GAP (opens at line __, pays off at line __; score 0-10; worth the wait?)
Opens on the card + L1 "Listen"; pays off at L3-5 (David wrote the taunt first). Too early — answered by about 20 seconds. The film's real tension (L7 "He could have ... So He stayed") is a second, better gap that pays at L11. Score 6/10. Worth the wait: yes, but the viewer is waiting on the wrong question.

## THREE SMALLEST FIXES (line number + exact replacement wording)
1. L3: "People laugh at him too, and he writes down what they say." → "People laugh at him too. He writes their words into a song."
2. L7: "So He stayed. And He prayed for them." → "So He stayed. And from the cross He prayed:" (then route L8 to a [jesus] voice)
3. L9: "Near its end, David's song says people from every nation will turn to God." → "The same song that heard the insults a thousand years early says what comes next: people from every nation will turn to God."
