## 1. WHO IS THIS FOR
A viewer like "Min-jun", 19, in Seoul, scrolling Shorts, no Bible knowledge. He stops at the card "WHO WROTE THE MOCKERY?" (curious). He may finish because it is short. He is unlikely to share: nothing in lines 1-11 gives a punchy line to pass on. NOT served: people who need to know who Jesus is, why the cross happened, what "Son of God" means (line 1 just states it).

## 2. MAIN TAKEAWAY
"Jesus stayed on the cross, and you can turn to Him." Carried by line 11 and line 7. Not clear. Line 11 "the One who stayed" lands as a surprise; I only half know what "stayed" means (stayed where? why is that good?). I did not learn why His staying matters for me.

## 3. GLOBAL AUDIENCE
Who: Jesus, with people shouting at Him at a cross (line 1). A king, David, long before, was mocked in the same way (line 3). What: the words match (lines 2, 4). Why: unclear. I guess: it proves something, but proves what?
Lost or misled:
- Line 1 "at the cross": I do not know Jesus is being executed. No one says it.
- Line 2 "let him deliver him now": "deliver" confusing; line 3 explains it, but after I already heard it. "Deliver" also means mail or give birth.
- Line 3 "a thousand years earlier... People laugh at him too, and he writes down what they say": too much in one line. "writes down" is odd.
- Line 5 "Almost the same words": the stress on a coincidence, but the film never says that this is the point, or that David is writing about the future.
- Line 6 "come down from the cross": fine. "If thou be" is old English; I cannot parse it.
- Line 7 "no one takes His life from Him. He gives it Himself": hard. Four ideas fast. "Prayed for them": who is "them"?
- Line 8 "forgive them; for they know not what they do": "know not" old English; "forgive" what?
- Line 9 "people from every nation will turn to God": turn where? Turn how? "Turn" as a body motion confuses me.
- Line 10 "shall remember and turn unto the LORD": old English, no context, who is "the LORD"? Is it God or Jesus?
- Line 11 "the One who stayed": I assume Jesus.
Names left unexplained: Matthew, Luke, Psalm (brackets in speech?), Son of God, LORD, David (a "king" only).

## 4. BIBLICALLY ACCURATE
I cannot judge accuracy. Things I was TOLD that I could not follow: why two old texts matching matters (5); how "He gives it Himself" connects to the cross (7); how David's "song" near its end relates to Jesus (9-10); what "turn unto the LORD" asks me to do (10-11); what was forgiven and why (8); how David is "laughed at" and Jesus is the same person or not (3).

## 5. BEST VERSION POSSIBLE
A world-class explainer would say the setup plainly in the first 5 seconds: "Jesus is being killed on a cross. The crowd mocks Him. A thousand years before, a poem predicted these exact words." Then one clear payoff: "He could have escaped. He chose to stay, for them, for you." Fewer quotes, more plain talk.
ONE highest-value change: line 1 should say what is happening (Jesus is dying on a cross) and line 5 should say the point of the match ("A poem written 1000 years before described this moment").
Score: 4/10.

## CURIOSITY GAP
Opens at the on-screen card (WHO WROTE THE MOCKERY?). Pays off at line 5/line 3 (David wrote it first), but the film never states that as an answer, and the real question (why?) is never answered. Score: 4/10. The wait is partly worth it, but the ending moves to a new topic (nations turning, lines 9-11) without closing the first.

## THREE SMALLEST FIXES
1. Line 1: "Jesus is dying on a cross. The crowd shouts at Him. Listen."
2. Line 5: "The same words, written a thousand years before. Long before Jesus was born."
3. Line 11: "He chose to stay on the cross. For them. For you. You can turn to Him."
