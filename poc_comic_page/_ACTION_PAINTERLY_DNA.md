## 1. What makes the genre work (analysis, NOT a prompt)

The dynamic painted Bible-comic category, generically:

- **Kinetic anatomy** — figures caught mid-motion, weight shifted, cloth flying; nothing stands square to camera.
- **Ink + paint together** — loose confident drawing under full painted color; the line gives energy, the paint gives light and depth.
- **Heroic low-angle framing** — the camera looks UP at the moment; diagonals everywhere; foreground elements break toward the viewer.
- **Bold single-source drama light** — one strong key, deep shadow, painted atmosphere (dust, glow, haze).
- **The trade-off:** all that energy naturally drifts toward idealized superhero bodies — exactly what our body gate forbids. Our version must keep the ENERGY and refuse the BODY (§2c).

## 2. Making it OURS

### a. Palette = the existing DNA language, not "rich saturated"

The generic genre says "rich saturated palette." We say OUR palette (DNA §2, unchanged):

| light/color role | our ink |
|---|---|
| Key light + glory | **Gold** `#E9C877` — Christ / glory only |
| Shadow + dread depths | **Storm blue-black** `#2E3A52` — law / judgment beats |
| Blood / heat | **Comic red** `#ED1C24` — SPARING, never decorative |
| Rare cool accent | **Hero blue** `#2E3192` — rare by rule |
| Earthy base | **Desert/robe tan** `#8A7039` + warm black `#231F20` line |

So the painted drama is already theologically coded: warm gold light vs storm-blue dark IS
the gospel arc, frame by frame. No new palette invented.

### b. THE SIGNATURE MOVE — "the Gold Seam" (light eats the line)

One concrete, recognizable, repeatable rule:

- **Wherever the key light strikes a figure's edge, the black ink outline gives way to a
  thin unbroken seam of warm gold rim-light.** The shadow side keeps thick loose black ink;
  the light side's line IS the gold.
- Why it's ours: it fuses our ink style + our gold=glory color code into a line-level rule —
  visible in every frame, readable even at thumbnail size, and no other painted-comic does
  the outline itself in glory-gold.
- Grammar it creates for free:
  - **Glory beats:** the seam widens, radiant.
  - **Judgment beats:** figures sit against storm blue-black, so the seam burns hardest.
  - **Passion beats:** the seam stays — but faint, thin, close to the body. Glory hidden in
    suffering, never a halo, never triumph (that's exactly the failure H's cross render made).
- Every scene therefore has ONE key light with a stated direction — the seam needs it.

### c. The body-gate tension — flagged and resolved

- **The tension, named:** "dynamic action" is the single strongest pull toward heroic
  musculature — the H cross render grew six-pack abs from style pressure alone, and G "ran
  closest to the muscle-gate line." This style courts that failure by design.
- **The resolution — energy lives in the COMPOSITION, never the body:**
  - Motion comes from camera angle, diagonals, flying cloth, light, atmosphere.
  - Bodies stay **lean everyday human figures in simple first-century dress** — that wording
    is baked into the style block itself (§3), not left to scene authors.
  - Passion beats: DNA §5a unchanged and senior — servant register, gaunt, marred, head
    bowed; blood always dried dark and matted; **positive end-state wording only** (never
    "no muscles" — naming a thing, even to forbid it, can draw it).
  - Body-gate Vision check runs on every passion frame, as already locked.

## 5. Three signatures, one grammar

> **The light is His. The camera kneels. We stand in the crowd.**

| # | Signature | Level | Rule in one line |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The Gold Seam** (§2b) | line | Light eats the ink — gold rim replaces the outline on the lit edge |
| 2 | **The Bowed Camera** (§5b) | framing | The hero shot is EARNED at glory only; at suffering the camera stands level, human |
| 3 | **The Witness Edge** (§5c) | composition | Key beats framed past a shadowed foreground witness — we are in the crowd, not in the cape |

### 5b. The Bowed Camera — camera grammar tied to the gospel arc

Generic action grammar looks UP at the hero constantly, even in defeat. We invert it:

| Beat type | Camera | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **GLORY** (resurrection, ascension, the welcome, the gospel pivot) | **Low-angle hero shot — ONLY here.** Camera looks up, sky behind Him, seam radiant | The genre's biggest gun, saved for the one place it's true |
| **ACTION / JUDGMENT** (plagues, storms, battles, the law) | Dynamic diagonals, tilted horizon, mid-angle energy | Full genre energy — on the events, not on bodies |
| **PASSION / SUFFERING** (the cross, the scourging, Gethsemane) | **Level or slightly elevated — a standing witness's eye height, horizon level and calm. Never the hero shot.** | The camera bows when He bows |

**Why this fixes the cross drift:** heroic FRAMING drags heroic ANATOMY with it — a worm's-eye
camera makes the model reach for the worm's-eye body (V-taper, abs). The ab-definition problem
is partly a camera problem. A level, human, grieving-witness camera pulls the body human.
This is the missing lever the wording passes alone couldn't reach.

**Testable:** every scene plan states its beat type; the camera angle is checkable per frame
(deterministic in the plan, Vision-checkable in the render).

### 5c. The Witness Edge — the third signature

- **The rule:** on key beats, the frame includes ONE soft dark foreground witness element at
  the edge — a shoulder, a hand, a head in profile — a simple silhouette in storm blue-black
  or desert-tan shadow, close to camera, softly out of focus. The viewer stands among the
  people watching.
- **Scripturally grounded:** "And the people stood beholding" (Luke 23:35); "all his
  acquaintance... stood afar off, beholding" (Luke 23:49). The crowd was there.
- **Why it compounds instead of being a third unrelated tweak:** the Bowed Camera puts the
  lens at a witness's eye height; the Witness Edge puts a witness's shoulder in the frame —
  together they say the same thing: **you are not the hero of this story, you are one of the
  saved.** And the Gold Seam never falls on the witness silhouette — the light is His alone.
  Three rules, one sentence.
- **It's also the brand:** the house already tells Scripture through eyewitnesses (the
  Awakeden eyewitness format). This is that format, drawn.
- **Guardrails:** silhouette only — no added face (keeps the animation-clean ≤3-faces rule);
  used on KEY beats, not every frame (spread thin, like SFX); gives free 2.5D parallax depth
  for the animation stage later.

### 5d. Why this is the BETTER take, not just a legally-distinct one

- **The genre flattens the story's own climax; we stage it.** Uniform heroic grammar makes
  the cross look like every other fight scene. Our grammar CHANGES REGISTER at the turn —
  the seam faints, the camera bows, the crowd watches — so the inversion (power laid down)
  is visible in the art itself, not just in the words.
- **The constraint IS the content.** The body gate isn't a limit on the style — it's the
  point of the story. A gaunt Christ under a level camera says Isaiah 53; a muscled Christ
  under a hero shot says the opposite of the passage it illustrates.
- **Contrast is drama.** Because the low-angle hero shot is RATIONED, the resurrection hits
  harder than any always-heroic page can — the genre spends its biggest shot every scene;
  we save it, so it lands.
- **The viewer is placed, not flattered.** Genre grammar puts you in the hero's boots. Ours
  puts you in the crowd at Golgotha — which is the CTA's own posture. The style preaches
  the same sermon as the script.
- **One-line pitch:** *the genre draws Jesus like the echoes; we draw the original.*
