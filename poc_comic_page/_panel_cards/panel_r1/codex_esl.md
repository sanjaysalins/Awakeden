## VERDICT (REVISE)

Two glosses need correction. Several hooks rely on idioms, vague references, or interpretation that second-language readers may mistake for the verse’s wording. The pack omits the narration, so exact placement against spoken phrases cannot be verified.

## LINE-BY-LINE

- **5 — CHANGE:** `TARRY = STAY`. In “tarry ye here, and watch with me,” the instruction is to remain there; “wait” loses that emphasis.
- **11 — CHANGE hook:** `EVER FEEL ABANDONED BY GOD?` “God left you” can suggest physical departure; “abandoned” names the experience intended by “why hast thou forsaken me?”
- **12 — CHANGE:** `POTSHERD = PIECE OF A BROKEN POT`. In “My strength is dried up like a potsherd,” a potsherd is a fragment, not an entire broken pot.
- **14 — CHANGE hook:** `WHAT DID GOD PROMISE / BEFORE THEIR PUNISHMENT?` “Their sentence” has competing grammatical and judicial meanings, and “their” lacks an identified referent.
- **15 — CHANGE hook:** `WHAT DOES THE PROMISE / ABOUT THE SERPENT MEAN?` “Who gets bruised? Who gets crushed?” presents an interpretive distinction as though the KJV uses different verbs; its wording is “bruise thy head” and “bruise his heel.”
- **16 — CHANGE hook:** `CHRIST HAS WON— / WHAT IS STILL TO COME?` “Why still future?” omits the subject and leaves readers guessing what remains future.
- **17 — CHANGE hook:** `CAN GOD STILL / FORGIVE YOU?` “Fallen too far” requires readers to decode a metaphor before understanding the question.
- **19 — CHANGE hook:** `TOO LATE TO / TURN TO GOD?` “Too late for God?” leaves the intended action unstated.
- **20 — CHANGE:** `BEHOLD = LOOK AT`. In “Woman, behold thy son,” “look at” makes the relationship to the following object clearer than bare “look.”
- **20 — CHANGE hook:** `DOES JESUS / SEE YOUR PAIN?` “He” has no named referent on the card.

## BIGGEST RISK

**An equals sign makes an explanatory choice look exact.** `POTSHERD = BROKEN POT` teaches the wrong object. Likewise, the bruised/crushed hook risks making interpretation look like a distinction explicitly present in the KJV.

`BRASS = BRONZE` also needs restraint: in “a serpent of brass,” “bronze” is a reasonable modernization of the traditional description, but the card should not be treated as proof of the object’s precise alloy.

## WHAT IS GOOD (keep)

Keep the remaining plain-word cards. Particularly clear choices include:

- `BEHELD = LOOKED AT` — “when he beheld the serpent of brass.”
- `EVEN SO = IN THE SAME WAY` — “even so must the Son of man be lifted up.”
- `COMMEND = ENTRUST` — “into thy hands I commend my spirit.”
- `GAVE UP THE GHOST = DIED` — removes a misleading modern association with ghosts.
- `CLEAVETH = STICKS` — “my tongue cleaveth to my jaws.”
- `SEPULCHRE = TOMB`, `RAIMENT = CLOTHES`, and `JONAS = JONAH` — direct, useful substitutions.
