## VERDICT (REVISE)

Do not air this set as written. Three hooks are the bar: **1**, **12**, **19**. Three are the worst: **17**, **7**, **20**. Most authored hooks stop the thumb by putting the viewer on trial. The verse is then optional.

## BIGGEST RISK

The cross and psalm cards share one stencil: Too weak. Too guilty. Afraid. Left. Too far. Your pain. It stops a scroll and trains people to distrust the next card, including a fair one. Worst case is 17: the sign of Jonah is not a despair test. Next to it, 7 turns a trust saying into death-fear. On 9, `WHAT WAS / FINISHED?` is a fair question, and the only plain card cashes a different clause (`GAVE UP THE GHOST = DIED`). The hook can outrun the card.

## WHAT IS GOOD (keep)

**Best: 1, 12, 19.**
- 1. Two goats, one dead, one sent away, then "why two?" Concrete, fair, a real question.
- 12. "Why did Jesus say I thirst?" Names the speaker, the odd line, and a question the piece can pay.
- 19. "Too late for God?" is the one anxiety question that matches the story: the thief, and "today."

Also keep 3 (the killer lifted up: a real paradox), 9 (the right question on "It is finished"), and 10 (snakebite answered with a snake on a pole: a strange fact, said plain). Those need no guilt hook.
