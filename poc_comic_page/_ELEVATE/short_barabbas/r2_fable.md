# Barabbas — Round 2 — Fable seat (blind, from scratch)

## (a) Script

[narrator] A robber waits in chains to die.
[narrator] Outside, a crowd is shouting his name.
[narrator] Governor Pilate will set one prisoner free.
[pilate] Barabbas, or Jesus which is called Christ?
[narrator] Pilate knows Jesus is innocent. He knows the leaders are envious.
[crowd] Barabbas!
[narrator] The robber walks out free. Jesus is led to the cross.
[narrator] (pause)
[narrator] That is what the cross is: "the just for the unjust."
[narrator] Jesus, in the guilty man's place. Barabbas was the first. He was not the last.

## (b) Word count + runtime

- Words: 77 (voice tags and the pause cue not counted)
- Runtime at 2.4 words/s: 32.1 s + ~1.0 s pause = ~33 s

## (c) KJV fragments used (verbatim)

- Matt 27:17 — "Barabbas, or Jesus which is called Christ?"
- Matt 27:21 — "Barabbas" (the crowd's one answer)
- 1 Peter 3:18 — "the just for the unjust"
- Facts carried in plain words, not quoted: Mark 15:7 (bound in prison), John 18:40 (a robber), Matt 27:18 (Pilate knew it was envy), Matt 27:26 (Barabbas released, Jesus delivered to be crucified), Matt 27:31 (led away to the cross).

## (d) Why the hook and landing work

1. Hook: seven words put a newcomer inside a cell with a guilty man about to die (who / what / why), then "a crowd is shouting his name" opens the gap — why would a crowd shout a condemned robber's name? — before any custom is explained.
2. Reversal is felt, not briefed: one question from Pilate, one shout, then two parallel lines — "The robber walks out free. Jesus is led to the cross." The newcomer sees the swap happen in the same breath; Pilate's procedure is cut to one line, and the envy and innocence are stated so no people group carries the blame.
3. Landing: after the pause, the exact words "the just for the unjust" name what the cross is in plain English; "Barabbas was the first. He was not the last." pays off the opening gap (the shout meant freedom, paid for by another) and opens the door to the viewer by grace alone — no fear, no "you must", no stock closer.
