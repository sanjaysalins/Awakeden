# Seat: Fable (cold viewer, blind) — short_barabbas

Read: candidates.md only. Bible check against KJV Matt 27:15-26 + John 18:40.

---

## Script 1

**1. Who / what / why (newcomer, ESL)**
- WHO: Pilate (some kind of ruler — never told he is the governor), Barabbas (a robber), Jesus, a crowd, "the leaders".
- WHAT: Pilate asks the crowd which of two prisoners to free. The crowd picks the robber and shouts for Jesus to be crucified. Pilate does it.
- WHY it matters: the guilty man goes free for nothing; the innocent man dies in his place. A newcomer gets this — the last line says it plainly and names Jesus.
- Gap: a newcomer does not know WHY a ruler lets a crowd pick a prisoner. The feast custom is never said, so the "Why?" is only half-answered.

**2. Lines I re-read**
- "Pilate had two prisoners." — Who is Pilate? Not introduced.
- "the leaders handed Jesus over" — which leaders? Handed over to whom?
- "Jesus which is called Christ" — "which" for a person is odd to an ESL ear. It is a quote, so acceptable.
- "hath" / "crucified" — old or hard words, but context carries them.

**3. Hook and payoff**
- "A guilty man walked free. Why?" — Answered: the crowd chose him; Jesus took the cross instead. Clear landing, names Jesus.
- Score: **7/10**. Loses points because the mechanism (one prisoner freed at the feast) is missing, so the "why" relies on the viewer guessing.

**4. Bible reader**
- "a robber" — John 18:40. Correct.
- "knew the leaders handed Jesus over out of envy" — Matt 27:18. "They" in the text = chief priests and elders (v.1-2, 12, 20). Fair.
- Dialogue order: "Barabbas" (v.21), then "Why, what evil hath he done?" → "Let him be crucified" (v.23, verbatim). Correct — it skips v.22, which is fine.
- "set Barabbas free, and handed Jesus over to be crucified" — v.26. Fine (scourging omitted, fine).
- "Pilate had two prisoners" — small over-reach. The text says they had "a notable prisoner" and Jesus had been delivered to Pilate; Pilate held others too (the two thieves). Harmless but not exact.
- "Jesus went to the cross in his place" — the text never says Barabbas was due to be crucified. The swap is the SHAPE of the story and the historic Christian reading, not its stated words. Fair as a picture; just know it is an inference.
- Fairness: names "the leaders" as the envious party — good. Does NOT say the leaders persuaded the crowd (v.20), so the crowd still carries the choice alone. Avoids v.25 ("His blood be on us") — good. No group is named as "the Jews" — good.

## Script 2

**1. Who / what / why (newcomer, ESL)**
- WHO: Barabbas (robber), Pilate (again never introduced), the crowd, Jesus. No leaders at all.
- WHAT: at a feast one prisoner is freed and the crowd picks. They pick Barabbas and want Jesus crucified. Pilate knows Jesus is innocent and does it anyway.
- WHY it matters: Barabbas did not earn it; "someone else took the cross." A newcomer CAN work out "someone else" = Jesus from the line before, but has to work for it. The name is missing from the landing.
- Strength: the feast custom is explained in 8 words. The newcomer understands why there is a choice at all.

**2. Lines I re-read**
- "And today, he walks free." — "today"? Is this a modern story? Takes a beat.
- "Pilate knew it was envy." — WHAT was envy? WHOSE? Nothing earlier names anyone envious. Coming right after the crowd shouts, an ESL viewer may think the crowd's shout was the envy.
- "Someone else took the cross." — Who? And "took the cross" can be heard as "carried the cross" (a different man did that). Vague final line.
- "At the feast" — which feast? A party? Minor.

**3. Hook and payoff**
- "Guilty. And today, he walks free. Why?" — strong hook, stronger than Script 1.
- Answered: yes, the crowd chose at the feast. But the payoff hides the name and uses an idiom ("took the cross").
- Score: **6/10**. Best hook, best mechanism, weakest landing.

**4. Bible reader**
- "a robber" — John 18:40. Correct.
- "At the feast, Pilate freed one prisoner. The crowd chose." — Matt 27:15 "whom they would." Correct and useful.
- "Pilate knew it was envy." — v.18, correct, but the subject is dropped so it reads as unsourced.
- "He knew Jesus had done no wrong." — supported by v.23 (the question) and v.24 ("this just person"). Fair.
- Dialogue order — same as Script 1, correct.
- "Then he released Barabbas, and handed Jesus over to be crucified." — v.26. Fine.
- "Someone else took the cross." — same substitution inference as Script 1 (not stated word for word); plus the idiom risk above.
- Fairness: this is the bigger problem. The chief priests and elders are gone entirely. Matt 27:20 says they PERSUADED the multitude. Script 2 puts the whole choice on "the crowd" with no one stirring them, and "it was envy" has no owner. Over a 60-second short that tilts the blame onto the people in the square alone. Not malicious, but less fair than the text, and less fair than Script 1.

---

## 5. Which is better, and the ONE change

**Pick: Script 1.**

Why: it names who was envious (the leaders), and it names Jesus in the landing. Those are the two things a cold viewer and a Bible reader both need. Script 2's hook and its feast line are better, but it has three wobbles (ownerless "it was envy", missing leaders, nameless "someone else") against Script 1's one (missing custom).

**The one change** — put the feast custom into Script 1's second line, and introduce Pilate as the text does ("the governor", v.15). Replace:

> [narrator] Pilate had two prisoners. One was Barabbas, a robber. The other was Jesus.

with:

> [narrator] Each feast, Pilate the governor set one prisoner free. The crowd picked. This time: Barabbas, a robber. Or Jesus.

Same length within a few words. It fixes the only real gap in Script 1 (why does a crowd get to choose?) and tells the newcomer who Pilate is, using the Bible's own word for him.

(If a second change were allowed: "the leaders handed Jesus over out of envy" → "the chief priests handed Jesus over out of envy, and stirred the crowd" — v.18 + v.20, so the crowd is not left holding the whole choice alone.)
