# Barabbas — Round 3 (seat: opus) — "the NAME"

## (a) Script

[narrator] Two prisoners. One goes free. Both are "son of the father."
[narrator] "Barabbas" means son of the father.
[narrator] "Now Barabbas was a robber."
[narrator] Some early copies even call him Jesus Barabbas.
[narrator] The other prisoner called God His Father.
[pilate] "Barabbas, or Jesus which is called Christ?"
[narrator] Pilate knew the leaders acted from envy.
[crowd] "Barabbas."
[narrator] The robber walked out, still wearing that name.
[narrator] The only man who truly owned it went to the cross.
*(pause ~1.0 s)*
[narrator] He owned that name — and came to share it.

## (b) Length

- Words: 78
- Runtime at 2.4 words/s: ~32.5 s, plus a ~1 s pause before the landing = ~33.5 s

## (c) KJV fragments used (verbatim)

- John 18:40 — "Now Barabbas was a robber."
- Matt 27:17 — "Barabbas, or Jesus which is called Christ?"
- Matt 27:21 — "Barabbas." (from "They said, Barabbas.")
- Paraphrased, not quoted: Matt 27:18 (Pilate knew it was envy), Matt 27:26 (Barabbas released; Jesus delivered to be crucified).
- Non-KJV fact, flagged as such on screen and in words: "some early copies" (Matt 27:16-17 in some early Greek/Syriac copies, known to Origen) read "Jesus Barabbas".

## (d) Why it works

- Hook: the first 2.5 s give who (two prisoners), what (one goes free) and the surprise (both are "son of the father"). That opens a gap: how can both have the same name?
- Payoff: the gap closes on irony, not a sermon. The man who only carried the name walked out. The man who truly owned it went to the cross. Every fact is in the text (release, delivery to be crucified, envy). Nothing is claimed about what the crowd knew.
- Landing: it comes from the name itself and rests on grace, with no pressure. The true Son came to share His sonship (John 1:12; John 20:17, "my Father, and your Father"), so even a robber can be called a son. No people group is blamed: it says "the crowd" and "the leaders", and names envy as Pilate saw it.
