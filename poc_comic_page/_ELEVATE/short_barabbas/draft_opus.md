# Barabbas — draft (opus seat)

## (a) Script

[narrator] A guilty man walked free. Here is why.
[narrator] Pilate had two prisoners.
[narrator] "Now Barabbas was a robber."
[narrator] The other was Jesus. Pilate knew the leaders handed him over out of envy.
[pilate] "Barabbas, or Jesus which is called Christ?"
[crowd] "Barabbas."
[pilate] "Why, what evil hath he done?"
[crowd] "Let him be crucified."
[narrator] So Pilate set Barabbas free, and handed Jesus over to be crucified.
*(pause, ~1 s)*
[narrator] Barabbas did nothing to earn it.
[narrator] He walked out free. Jesus went to the cross in his place.

## (b) Count

- Words: 79
- Runtime: 79 / 2.4 = ~32.9 s, plus ~1 s pause = ~34 s

## (c) KJV fragments used (verbatim)

- John 18:40 — "Now Barabbas was a robber."
- Matt 27:17 — "Barabbas, or Jesus which is called Christ?"
- Matt 27:21 — "Barabbas." (They said, Barabbas.)
- Matt 27:23 — "Why, what evil hath he done?"
- Matt 27:22 — "Let him be crucified."
- Matt 27:26 — paraphrased in plain words (released Barabbas / delivered Jesus to be crucified).
- Matt 27:18 — envy, paraphrased ("Pilate knew ... out of envy").

## (d) Why it works

- Hook: the first line says who (a guilty man), what (he walked free) and opens the gap (why?) in under 2.5 s; the last line pays it off.
- Plain words: every archaic phrase is a short quote the story frames; blame sits only on "the leaders" and "the crowd", with Pilate knowing it was envy.
- Landing: earned from the scene itself — the guilty man earns nothing and walks out, the innocent one is delivered; "in his place" rests on 27:26 (one released, the other delivered), no fear, no pressure.
