# Barabbas — Round 2 (opus seat)

## (a) Script

[narrator] A robber named Barabbas sits in chains, waiting for his verdict.
[narrator] Then his cell door opens. He is free. Why?
[narrator] The governor gives the crowd one choice.
[pilate] Barabbas, or Jesus which is called Christ?
[crowd] Barabbas.
[narrator] Pilate knew the chief priests acted from envy.
[pilate] Why, what evil hath he done?
[crowd] Let him be crucified.
[narrator] So the guilty man walks out. The innocent man goes to the cross.
[pause 0.8s]
[narrator] The just for the unjust.
[narrator] That door is open for you too.

## (b) Count

78 words. About 32.5 s at 2.4 words/s (plus the 0.8 s pause = ~33.3 s).

## (c) KJV fragments used

- Matt 27:17 "Barabbas, or Jesus which is called Christ?"
- Matt 27:21 "Barabbas."
- Matt 27:23 "Why, what evil hath he done?"
- Matt 27:22-23 "Let him be crucified."
- 1 Peter 3:18 "the just for the unjust"
- Grounding (paraphrased, not quoted): Mark 15:7 (lay bound), John 18:40 (a robber), Matt 27:18 (envy), Matt 27:26 (released Barabbas; delivered Jesus to be crucified).

## (d) Why it works

- Hook: we start in the cell with one man in chains, then his door opens and we ask "Why?" Who, what and the gap all land in the first lines.
- The middle answers that "Why?" with one choice, one name and one reversal. Pilate only gets his two short lines, and the envy fact puts no blame on any people group.
- Landing: the guilty man walks out and the innocent man walks in. After a pause, "the just for the unjust" names the cross. The open door from the hook comes back as a gift, with no pressure on the viewer.
