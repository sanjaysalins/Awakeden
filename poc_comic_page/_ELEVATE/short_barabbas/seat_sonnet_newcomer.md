# Seat: Sonnet, cold newcomer (English as second language)

## Script 1

1. WHO/WHAT/WHY
- Who: A man named Barabbas (a robber), a man named Jesus, and a leader called Pilate.
- What: Pilate asks a crowd to choose. They pick Barabbas to go free. Jesus is killed on a cross.
- Why it matters: Jesus took the place of a guilty man. "In his place" tells me the point.
- I got this, but I am not sure who Jesus is. "Called Christ" is a strange word to me. I do not know who the "leaders" are or why they had envy.

2. Re-read / not understood
- "Pilate knew the leaders handed Jesus over out of envy." Which leaders? What is envy? I read it twice.
- "Which is called Christ": old English word order. Hard.
- "Why, what evil hath he done?": "hath" is old. I guessed the meaning.
- "Handed Jesus over": over to who?

3. Hook and payoff
- Question: "A guilty man walked free. Why?"
- Answer: Because Jesus went to the cross in his place. Yes, answered, and clearly.
- Score: 8/10. Hook is short and clear. The payoff is plain and direct.

4. As a Bible reader (Matt 27:15-26, John 18:40)
- Accurate: two prisoners, the choice, the crowd's cry, "let him be crucified", release of Barabbas. Matt 27:18 supports Pilate knowing it was envy.
- Barabbas called "a robber": John 18:40 says robber. Matthew calls him a notable prisoner. Fine.
- Mild risk: "the leaders handed Jesus over" is the text and is fair. It blames the leaders, not "the Jews". Good. It does not name the crowd as a whole people.
- "Jesus went to the cross in his place": a theological reading (substitution), not stated in the text itself. It is a fair gospel reading, but it goes a little past these verses. "Barabbas did nothing to earn it" is fine.
- Missing: Pilate's wife, washing hands, and "his blood on us" (Matt 27:24-25). Leaving them out is wise. That verse has been used against Jewish people.

## Script 2

1. WHO/WHAT/WHY
- Same people. Barabbas, a robber, goes free. The crowd chooses. Jesus is sent to the cross.
- Why: "Someone else took the cross." I understand a substitute, but it is less clear who "someone else" is. Jesus is named earlier, so I can connect it.

2. Re-read / not understood
- "And today, he walks free." "Today" confused me. This happened long ago. I re-read it.
- "Pilate knew it was envy." Envy of what? By who? No subject. Confusing.
- "He knew Jesus had done no wrong." Good and clear.
- "Someone else took the cross": a little vague. Took it how?
- Same old words: "hath", "which is called Christ".

3. Hook and payoff
- Question: "Why does he walk free?"
- Answer: "Someone else took the cross." It is answered, but softer and more poetic. I must work it out.
- Score: 7/10. Hook is stronger with "Guilty", but the ending is less direct.

4. As a Bible reader
- "The crowd chose": accurate to Matt 27:17, 21. It stresses the crowd as the ones who chose. No leaders named, so less blame on any group, but also it hides that Matt 27:20 says the chief priests and elders persuaded the crowd. Slightly unfair to the crowd alone.
- "Pilate knew it was envy": true to Matt 27:18, but the "it" has no clear reference.
- "Barabbas! " with an exclamation is an added tone, not in the text. Small.
- "Someone else took the cross" is a gentle reading of substitution. Same level as Script 1. Not stated outright, but reasonable.
- "And today, he walks free" is a little odd. He was released then, not today.

## 5. Which is better, and ONE change

- Better: Script 1. Clearer story order (two prisoners first, then choice), plain payoff ("Jesus went to the cross in his place"), and the leaders are named as the ones who acted from envy.
- ONE change: Say who Jesus is in plain words early, for example "Jesus, the man many called God's Son", and say "the religious leaders" instead of "the leaders". A newcomer needs that to get why Jesus was handed over.
