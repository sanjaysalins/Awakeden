# Seat: Sonnet, total newcomer (English as a second language)

## 1. Who / what / why

Both scripts, same answer:
- WHO: a man called Barabbas (a robber), and Jesus. A ruler called Pilate. A crowd.
- WHAT: Pilate lets the crowd pick one man to free. The crowd picks the robber. Jesus is killed.
- WHY it matters: the robber's name means "son of the father." Jesus called God "Father" (Abba). So the robber, a "son of the father" by name, goes free. The real Son of the Father dies. The last line says Jesus gives that name (child of the Father) to us.

I got the main idea. I did not know who Pilate is (a ruler? a judge?). "Crucified" I half know. "Christ" I half know.

## 2. Lines I re-read or did not get

- "Abba ... the word inside the robber's name": I got it only because the script says "Abba means Father" right before. Then "Bar-abbas" hides "abba." It is clear on the page. Spoken aloud, fast, I might miss it. "Bar" is not explained as "son of." A hearing-only viewer must connect "Bar-abbas" to "son of the father" from line 1. It works, but just barely.
- "Which is called Christ": strange. Christ is a title, not a name. A newcomer will not know.
- "Why, what evil hath he done?": old English. I understand from context.
- "Jesus died to give it to us": give WHAT? "That name" = "son of the father." Takes a second. The ending is the best line, but "it" is a bit vague.
- Script 1 only: "Some old copies of Matthew even call him Jesus Barabbas." I did not understand. Two men named Jesus? Confusing. Why does it matter? It made me stop and re-read.
- Script 2 only: "He knew the leaders were jealous of Jesus." Clear. But who are "the leaders"? Unclear to me.

## 3. Fresh or sermon?

- Script 1: freshness 7, hook-and-payoff 7. The "Jesus Barabbas" twist is fresh but confusing for a newcomer.
- Script 2: freshness 6, hook-and-payoff 8. Smoother. The name idea is still the fresh part. The jealousy line is a familiar sermon note.

The name hook ("son of the father") opens a question. The payoff ("the word inside the robber's name", "born with that name, Jesus gave it to us") answers it. I liked the trade idea: one son goes free, one dies. It feels like a story, not a lecture, until the very last line, which is a little sermon-like but short.

## 4. As a Bible reader

- Matt 27:15-26, John 18:40: story matches. Crowd chooses Barabbas. John 18:40 calls him a robber. Good.
- Mark 14:36 "Abba, Father" and Gal 4:5-6: "Jesus called God Abba" is fine. The last line leans on Gal 4:5-6 (adoption, we cry "Abba, Father"). That is backed by the text. OK.
- Bar-abbas = "son of the father": standard meaning. Fine. A small point: "Abba" inside "Barabbas" is a real wordplay, but the text never says Matthew meant it. It is a nice observation, not a claim of the text. The script states it as fact ("It is the word inside the robber's name"). That is true as language. It does not claim it was planned.
- "Jesus Barabbas" (Script 1): correctly worded as "some old copies." Honest. But it is a minor textual point that may distract. It also makes two "Jesus" men, which could confuse.
- "Crowd" says "Let him be crucified": KJV Matt 27:22-23. Good. Script uses "the crowd," not "the Jews." Fair to all groups. Good.
- Script 2: "He knew the leaders were jealous": from Matt 27:18 ("for envy"). Backed by text. But "the leaders" may be heard as blaming one group. It is the text, but the risk of unfairness is higher here than in Script 1. Suggest "the priests" or leave out.
- "The robber went free. The true Son of the Father did not.": nice. Careful point: the text does not say Barabbas was "saved" or changed, and the script does not claim it. Good.
- "Born with that name. Jesus died to give it to us": a theology claim (adoption), supported by Gal 4:5-6. Reasonable. Slight stretch: Jesus died to make us sons, yes, but "give it" is a poetic shortcut.

## 5. Which is better, and one change

Script 2 is better for a newcomer: shorter, no confusing second "Jesus" line, cleaner path to the payoff.

ONE change: before "Pilate offered to free one man," or in the first lines, say who Pilate is in a few words ("The ruler, Pilate, offered..."). Alternative if only one swap is allowed: in Script 2, replace the "leaders were jealous" line with "He offered to free one man. The crowd could choose." That removes the group-blame risk and keeps the pace.
