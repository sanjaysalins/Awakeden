# Seat: Fable (cold viewer) — r3_test

Read only candidates.md. Blind.

## Script 1

**1. Who / what / why**
- WHO: a robber called Barabbas, and Jesus. Pilate is the judge.
- WHAT: Pilate lets the crowd free one man. They pick the robber. Jesus is crucified.
- WHY: the robber's name means "son of the father." Jesus, the real Son, dies so we get that name.
- Newcomer gets who and what. Gets why, mostly. "Give it to us" = give us the name "son of the Father." Clear with the pause before it.

**2. Lines I had to re-read**
- "Some old copies of Matthew even call him Jesus Barabbas." Re-read twice. A newcomer does not know "Matthew" is a book. "Two men called Jesus?" is a shock with no payoff later in the script. It is a scholar's fact, not a viewer's.
- "Abba ... the word inside the robber's name": clear ON PAPER, because line 1 gave "son of the father" and line 11 gave "Abba = Father." BY EAR it depends on the narrator. English says "buh-RAB-us"; the "Abba" can hide. Needs the name split once ("Bar-ABBA") or shown on screen with ABBA lit.
- "Jesus which is called Christ" and "what evil hath he done": old English, but short. Passable for a second-language listener.

**3. Fresh?**
- "Guilty man goes free, innocent man dies in his place" is a sermon I have heard. The NAME turn (Abba inside Bar-abbas, Mark 14:36, Gal 4) is the fresh part. The landing line is earned and new to me.
- Freshness: 7. Hook-and-payoff: 6. Line 1 is a fact about a stranger. Weak first 2 seconds. Payoff is strong (9); hook is middling (5).

**4. Bible check**
- Bar-abba = son of the father: right.
- Robber: John 18:40. Prisoner: Matt 27:16. Right.
- "Jesus Barabbas" in some early copies of Matt 27:16-17: true, and the wording "some old copies" is honest. Not in KJV. Not wrong, just unexplained.
- Pilate's line, "Why, what evil hath he done?", "Let him be crucified": KJV Matt 27:17, 23, 22. Verbatim fragments. Right.
- "Abba" = Father: Mark 14:36. Right.
- "Jesus died to give it to us": Gal 4:5-6 (adoption, Spirit crying "Abba, Father"). An interpretive step, but grounded. Fair.
- Fairness: it says "crowd," not "the Jews." Good. No group blamed as a whole.
- Nothing claimed beyond the text.

## Script 2

**1. Who / what / why**
- Same as Script 1. Plus one plain-words reason: Pilate "knew the leaders were jealous of Jesus." A newcomer now knows WHY Pilate made the offer. That helps.

**2. Lines I had to re-read**
- None new. "The leaders" is a little vague (which leaders?) but it does not stop me.
- Same "Abba by ear" risk as Script 1.

**3. Fresh?**
- Freshness: 7. Hook-and-payoff: 7. Same hook weakness; the motive line makes the middle hold together better.

**4. Bible check**
- "He knew the leaders were jealous": Matt 27:18 "for he knew that for envy they had delivered him." Right. "Leaders" = chief priests and elders (27:20). Fair to the text, fair to the group.
- Everything else as Script 1. Nothing wrong. Nothing over-claimed.

## 5. Pick and the ONE change

**Pick: Script 2.**
Why: it trades a line a newcomer must re-read and that never pays off ("old copies of Matthew ... Jesus Barabbas") for a line that answers a newcomer's real question ("why did Pilate offer?") in plain words. Same landing, fewer stumbles.

**The ONE change:** make the Abba link land by EAR, not just on paper. Replace
"It is the word inside the robber's name."
with
"Listen. Bar-ABBA. The word is inside the robber's name."
(and light up ABBA inside BARABBAS on screen at that moment).
Without this, a listener who never saw the name written may miss the whole turn.

Still weak after that: the first line is a fact about a stranger. A question would hook harder ("Two men. One name. Only one goes free." or similar), but that is a second change, not my one.
