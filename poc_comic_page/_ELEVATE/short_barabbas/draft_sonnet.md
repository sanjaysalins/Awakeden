# Barabbas - draft (sonnet)

## (a) Script

[narrator] Barabbas was a robber. Guilty. And today, he walks free. Why?
[pilate] "Barabbas, or Jesus which is called Christ?"
[narrator] Pilate knew it was envy. The chief priests wanted Jesus gone.
[crowd] "Barabbas!"
[pilate] "Why, what evil hath he done?"
[crowd] "Let him be crucified."
[narrator] Then Pilate released Barabbas, and handed Jesus over to be crucified.
[narrator] One man went free. The other, who did no wrong, was handed over.
(pause, about 1 s)
[narrator] Barabbas did not earn it. Someone else took the cross.

## (b) Count and runtime

- Words: 74 (spoken words only, voice tags not counted)
- Runtime at 2.4 words/s: about 30.8 s, plus about 1 s pause = about 32 s

## (c) KJV fragments used

- "Barabbas, or Jesus which is called Christ?" (Matt 27:17)
- "Barabbas" (Matt 27:21)
- "Why, what evil hath he done?" (Matt 27:23)
- "Let him be crucified" (Matt 27:22)
- "Then released he Barabbas" and "delivered him to be crucified" (Matt 27:26), in plain words
- "a robber" (John 18:40)
- Envy (Matt 27:18), in narrator voice

## (d) Why it works

- Hook: the first line names who (Barabbas), what (a guilty robber), and sets the gap: why does he walk free? The answer comes at the end.
- Landing: the story itself earns it. Barabbas is freed, Jesus is handed over. No pressure, no stock closer.
- The pause before the last line lets the contrast sink in, and "Someone else took the cross" pays off the "Why?" without claiming more than the text says.
