# Barabbas — Round 3, seat: Fable (blind, from scratch)

## (a) Script

[narrator] Barabbas means "son of the father."
[narrator] A robber, held in prison.
[narrator] Pilate offered to free one man.
[pilate] Barabbas, or Jesus which is called Christ?
[narrator] Pilate knew the chief priests envied Jesus.
[crowd] Barabbas.
[pilate] What shall I do then with Jesus?
[crowd] Let him be crucified.
[narrator] The night before, Jesus had prayed.
[jesus] Abba, Father.
[narrator] Abba — the word inside the robber's name.
[narrator] Barabbas was freed. The true Son of the Father was not.
[narrator] (pause)
[narrator] Barabbas was born with that name. Jesus died to give it to us.

## (b) Count + runtime

- Words: 80 (all voices, pause marker excluded)
- Runtime at 2.4 words/s: 33.3 s, plus ~1 s pause before the landing = ~34 s
- Hook: 6 words = 2.5 s

## (c) KJV fragments used (verbatim)

- Matt 27:17 — "Barabbas, or Jesus which is called Christ?"
- Matt 27:21 — "Barabbas."
- Matt 27:22 — "What shall I do then with Jesus" (opening of the verse)
- Matt 27:22 — "Let him be crucified."
- Mark 14:36 — "Abba, Father." (Gethsemane, the night before the trial)
- Facts carried in narrator voice: John 18:40 (a robber); Matt 27:16 (a prisoner); Matt 27:18 (Pilate knew it was envy, chief priests named, no people group); Matt 27:26 (Barabbas released, Jesus delivered).
- Name fact: Barabbas = Aramaic bar abba, "son of the father". The Aramaic abba is the same word Jesus prays in Mark 14:36.
- Landing grounded in Gal 4:4-6 / Rom 8:15 ("Abba, Father" as the cry of adopted sons) and John 1:12 — stated as purpose ("died to give"), not as an automatic claim.
- Deliberately not used: the "Jesus Barabbas" manuscript variant (would need a hedge in a 34 s short) and 1 Peter 3:18. The words went to a verbatim KJV link instead.

## (d) Why the hook and landing work

1. Hook: the first 6 words hand a newcomer a fact they have never heard — the robber's name means "son of the father" — and leave the gap "why does that matter?" open; who / what / why follow in the next 5 s (a robber, a prisoner, Pilate will free one man).
2. The payoff is not a sermon line but the name itself: Jesus says the exact word "Abba" the night before, so the viewer hears the robber's name and the true Son's prayer collide, and the reversal (freed / not freed) lands in two short sentences, no "in his place", no archaic word.
3. Landing: earned from the name alone — one man had "son of the father" by birth, the other died so it could be given — a gift statement with no fear, no pressure, no call-to-action cliche; it closes the loop the hook opened.
