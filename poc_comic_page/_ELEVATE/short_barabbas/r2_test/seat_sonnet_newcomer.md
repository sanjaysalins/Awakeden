# Seat: Sonnet, cold newcomer (English 2nd language)

Note: Q.md says "r2_testndidates.md" (typo). I read candidates.md.

## Script 1

1. WHO/WHAT/WHY
- Who: a robber, Barabbas, and Jesus.
- What: a crowd picks Barabbas to go free. Jesus is sent to the cross.
- Why it matters: Jesus died in the place of a guilty man. "The just for the unjust" tells me that. I get it, but only at the end.

2. Lines I re-read
- "waiting for his verdict" - what is a verdict? Maybe "waiting to hear if he will die."
- "Why, what evil hath he done?" - "hath" is old. I guessed it meant "has".
- "Jesus which is called Christ" - what is Christ? A newcomer does not know it is a title.
- "Pilate" - who is he? Never said (a ruler / judge).
- "the leaders acted out of envy" - which leaders? Why jealous? Short, so I just accept it.
- "the just for the unjust" - hard words. "Just" and "unjust" are not everyday words for me.

3. Hook and payoff: 6/10
- Hook: "He is free. Why?"
- Answer: the crowd chose him. Then the cross, and Jesus "took" his death. That is a real answer, but a bit late and a bit quiet. The word "took" does the work.

4. Bible reader
- Fair to people: it does not say "the Jews" and does not use Matt 27:25 ("his blood be on us"). Good. But "the crowd" alone is slightly unfair. Matt 27:20 says the chief priests and elders persuaded the crowd. The script blames the crowd for the choice, then the leaders for envy. Acceptable.
- Past the text: "sits in chains, waiting for his verdict" is invented. Matthew says "notable prisoner"; John 18:40 says "robber". "Then his cell door opens" is invented but is a harmless picture. Barabbas is not shown to be "waiting for death" in Matt/John (Mark/Luke add insurrection and murder). "The robber waited for death" is a small stretch.
- "The just for the unjust" is 1 Peter 3:18, not Matthew or John. It is in quote marks with no source. Fine, but unnamed.
- "Pilate knew ... envy" matches Matt 27:18. Good.

5. Better and one change
- Script 1 is the stronger ending. The last line "Jesus took it" is sharp and earned.
- Change: say who Pilate is in the first mention ("the Roman governor, Pilate"), and say "Jesus, the Christ, God's chosen king" once.

## Script 2

1. Same as Script 1. The extra line makes the "so what for me" clear.

2. Re-read: "That door is open for you too." Which door? The cell door? I had to think. I guess it means freedom is offered to me too.

3. Hook and payoff: 6/10, same as Script 1. The echo with the first cell door gives a small bonus. But it explains the point after the point was already made.

4. Bible reader: the same as Script 1. The extra line is an invitation, not a claim about the text. It goes beyond Matt/John but that is normal for a gospel short. No unfairness to any group.

5. Script 1 is better, by a little. Change: cut the last line, or replace it with something from the story itself.

## Does "That door is open for you too" feel earned or stock?

Half earned, mostly stock.
- For earned: the cell door opened in line 2, so "door" does echo. A careful listener feels a loop.
- Against: the first door was a prison door, and Barabbas walked out. Now "door" is a church-style door (heaven / Jesus). The picture switches and the newcomer must work it out. "Open for you too" is a very common closing phrase in this kind of video. It sounds like the thing every short says at the end. It also tells me what to feel after the story has already shown it. "The robber waited for death. Jesus took it." is the real landing. Line 31 weakens it.
- Verdict: stock closer in a nice coat. Score 4/10 for earned. If kept, make the link clear: "Your cell door can open too." That keeps the one picture.
