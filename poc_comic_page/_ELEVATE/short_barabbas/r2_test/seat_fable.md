# Seat: Fable (cold viewer) — Barabbas r2

Read only candidates.md. Scripts are identical except Script 2's last line.

## Q1. Who / what / why (as a newcomer, English as 2nd language)

**Who:** A robber called Barabbas, in prison. A man asking questions (I learn later he is "Pilate" — the script never says who he is). A crowd. Jesus.

**What:** The crowd is given a choice: free the robber, or free Jesus. They shout for the robber and ask for Jesus to be killed on a cross. The robber walks out. Jesus goes to the cross.

**Why it matters:** The script says this IS the cross — the good man dies in the place of the bad man. "The robber waited for death. Jesus took it."

I got all three. The swap is clear and the ending lands.

## Q2. Lines I had to re-read

1. **"Pilate knew the leaders acted out of envy."** — Two unknowns in one line. Who is Pilate? (I guessed: the man asking.) Which leaders? Leaders of what? Envy of whom? I guessed "leaders of the crowd, jealous of Jesus," but I had to guess. This is the one line that drops the newcomer.
2. **"the just for the unjust"** — I know "just" as "only" or "fair." "The just" as a person (= the good man) is new to me. I understood it from the contrast, but it is the hinge line and it made me pause.
3. **"Jesus which is called Christ"** — "which" for a person is odd; "Christ" sounds like a surname to a newcomer. Short, so it passes.
4. **"what evil hath he done"** — "hath" is old, but guessable.

## Q3. Does the opening question get answered?

Opening question: "He is free. Why?"
Answer: yes — the crowd chose him over Jesus. Answered directly. Then the deeper answer: "That is the cross."

- **Script 1: 7/10.** Clean loop (chains → door opens → why → the swap). Hard stop on "Jesus took it." is the strongest possible end. Loses points for the "leaders" line and for "the just" being the hinge word.
- **Script 2: 7/10 as written.** Same loop, plus a door call-back that is nice — but the "you" arrives with no set-up (see below) and it softens the hard stop. Net zero. **8/10 with the fix in Q5.**

## Q4. As a Bible reader (KJV Matt 27:15-26, John 18:40)

- **Verbatim lines are right.** "Barabbas, or Jesus which is called Christ?" (27:17), "Why, what evil hath he done?" (27:23), "Let him be crucified" (27:22-23), "Barabbas!" (27:21). "Robber" = John 18:40.
- **"Pilate knew the leaders acted out of envy"** = Matt 27:18 ("for envy they had delivered him"); "they" = chief priests and elders (27:1-2, 27:20). "Leaders" is a careful, fair word — envy is pinned on the leaders, not on a people. **Fair to every group.** One gap: the script skips 27:20 (the leaders *persuaded* the crowd), so the crowd looks like it chose on its own. Adding "stirred up by their leaders" would be truer AND would introduce "the leaders" before the envy line.
- **Beyond the text, not wrong:**
  - **"waiting for his verdict" / "The robber waited for death."** The cited verses say only "a notable prisoner" and "a robber." No sentence is stated. It is a reasonable inference (a pardon implies a sentence; two others were crucified that day) — but the landing's whole weight rests on it. Acceptable, flag it as inference.
  - **"the just for the unjust"** is 1 Peter 3:18, not from this passage. Sound doctrine. The viewer hears it as the narrator's own phrase, not as Scripture — fine, just know it.
  - Chains, cell door — visual inference, harmless.
- **Doctrine:** substitution, the innocent for the guilty — sound. No fear, no pressure. Good.

## Q5. Which is better, and the ONE change

**Better: Script 2 — but only with the last line fixed.** As written, Script 1 is the tighter film; it ends harder. Script 2 is the one that actually invites the viewer, which is what the piece is for — but the invitation is half-earned (see below).

**The ONE change (last line only):**

> [narrator] The robber waited for death. Jesus took it.
> [narrator] **Not for him only.** That door is open for you too.

Four plain words. They extend the swap from Barabbas to the viewer, so "for you too" lands as a consequence, not an add-on. "Not for him only" echoes 1 John 2:2 KJV ("not for ours only, but also for the sins of the whole world") — grounded, not invented.

(If a second change were allowed: "Pilate knew the leaders acted out of envy" → "Pilate, the judge, knew their leaders had stirred the crowd out of envy." Introduces both unknowns and restores 27:20.)

## Extra question: does "That door is open for you too" feel earned or stock?

**Half-earned.**

**Earned:** The noun is the piece's own. "His cell door opens" is line 2 — the hook image — and the closer brings it back. It is not a borrowed church phrase; it is not in the "come to Jesus / just ask Him / will you" family. The door-opens → door-is-open loop is a real structural rhyme.

**Not earned:**
1. **The viewer was never put in the cell.** No line says "you are where Barabbas was." The script jumps from Barabbas's death-sentence straight to "for you too," and asks the viewer to make the leap alone. A newcomer may ask: "Which door? Why am I in a prison?"
2. **The mould is familiar.** "____ is for you too" is a standard gospel-short closer shape. The specific noun (door) half-rescues it; the shape still shows.
3. **It costs the hard stop.** "Jesus took it." is the best final beat available. Anything after it must earn its place; this line, as written, does not quite.

**Fix:** the 4-word bridge above ("Not for him only."). It puts the viewer in the swap first; then the door line reads as earned.
