## The ONE visible difference that drove the scores

**Lettering style / text covering art.**
A uses short, word-timed KEYWORD cards (3-6 words, caps, one word in red) tucked in a small box low on the frame; a tagged scripture bar for KJV lines; the brand mark top-left. The art stays the hero.
B and C use the SAME full-sentence subtitle pill, centred at mid-frame, on every single frame. It sits on the subject (over the potter's hands, across Judas's chest, over the cross) and reads like auto-captions. One choice, and it moves premium, feel AND share at the same time.

Cut rhythm is the secondary driver — it is what separates B from C (29 cuts / 3.5s max hold vs 16 cuts / 7.2s max hold). The hook card ("500 YEARS EARLY") is what gives B its only clear win over A, at the open.

Honest note outside the four named options: A also simply has MORE DISTINCT PICTURES (~20 different compositions incl. multi-panel grids) versus B/C (~10 pictures re-cropped). That variety is a big part of why A finishes. B's 29 cuts are mostly re-crops of the same picture, so the number flatters it.

## Top 3 reasons

1. **A keeps the art in charge.** Small keyword cards + red emphasis word = comic lettering, not subtitles. B/C's centred full sentences hide the drawing the viewer is meant to feel.
2. **A shows two men; B/C show one.** A has an old prophet (scroll, lamb, scales) AND a young Judas (green, coins, priests) — the 500-year echo is VISIBLE. In B/C Zechariah never appears; Judas is on screen during Zechariah's own line "So they weighed for my price" (~12s), so a newcomer sees one story, not a prophecy and its fulfilment.
3. **A's pictures match the words; B/C drift.** B/C hold an empty desert for ~5 frames (~12s) under "Cast down. In the temple. The priests took it." A cuts coins → scroll → coins-with-blood-trail → scales for the same lines. C then ends "Receive his price, and go free" on nailed FEET and a blood-soaked post base — reverent, but it is not an invitation image; A and B end on the golden open-handed Jesus.

## Per-film notes

### Film A (26 cuts, 5.8s max hold)
+ Multi-panel comic grids (2-up / 3-up inside one frame) — looks like a real comic page, premium.
+ Blood-trail-of-coins across the stones under "BLOOD MONEY BOUGHT THE POTTER'S FIELD" — the one frame I would share.
+ Scales-with-coins against the night sky for "EVERY COIN FOUND ITS MARK" — the thesis in one picture.
+ Lands on golden Jesus with open hand, brand mark present.
- Nail-through-hand arrives at ~15s under "THE LORD TOOK IT PERSONALLY" — before any cross context. Great for stop/feel, costs a point on clarity for a total newcomer.
- Frame 15 ("CAST DOWN. IN") shows coins on top and a large blank/white lower half. Probably a page-slam transition caught mid-flight; if it is a real blank flash in playback, fix it. Check by eye.
- Keyword captions are fragments ("ZECHARIAH ASKED FOR" → "...HIS SHEPHERD'S WAGES"). Fine with audio; an ESL viewer on mute gets less than B/C.

### Film B (29 cuts, 3.5s max hold)
+ "500 YEARS EARLY" big yellow hook card in frame 1 — the best first second of the three.
+ Every spoken line is on screen — strongest for sound-off / ESL reading.
+ Fastest rhythm, so repetition is partly masked.
- Full-sentence pills centred over the subject on every frame — subtitles, not lettering.
- ~10 distinct pictures re-cropped; coins for first ~10s (4 frames), potter 4 frames, desert 5 frames.
- No Zechariah figure; Judas appears under Zechariah's words.
- No brand mark.

### Film C (16 cuts, 7.2s max hold)
+ Cleanest single frames (wider Judas-with-priests shot reads well).
+ Ends firmly on the cross — doctrinally safe.
- No hook card. Coins alone for ~10s with a subtitle pill = weakest open.
- Slowest rhythm by far; 7.2s holds on a repeated picture in a short is a scroll-away risk.
- Same drift as B (desert under "in the temple"), same one-man problem.
- Closes on feet + bloody post base under "go free" — the CTA has no face and no open hand.
