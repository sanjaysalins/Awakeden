## What I see

**A** — Real comic pages: inked gutters, 2-3 panel layouts, then full-bleed splashes. The most distinct pictures by far: coin floor, scroll with a coin on it, Zechariah on a rooftop, scales, nail in the hand, Zechariah with scroll + lamb, Zechariah throwing coins in the temple, potter, Judas clutching the bag, Judas casting coins up, coin trail with blood on stone, empty scales, Jesus with crown of thorns, the cross, and a last frame of Jesus reaching a hand out to the viewer. Lettering is cream comic caption boxes with red key words, plus KJV bars with a red ref tag (ZECHARIAH 11:12b, MATTHEW 27:5a). Words land a few at a time ("THIRTY SILVER / COINS HIT"), like a comic narrator box.
Weak spots: two frames show big empty white/grey panels ("CAST DOWN. IN", "EVERY COIN FOUND") — these read as unfinished pages for a beat. Captions are partial phrases, so a frame paused mid-reveal says odd things ("ZECHARIAH ASKED FOR"). Nail-in-hand at "The LORD took it personally" jumps to the cross very early.

**B** — Opens on a big yellow "500 YEARS EARLY" title over the coin floor: the best first-second hook of the three. Fastest cut rhythm (29 cuts, max hold 3.5s). Ends on two Jesus portraits (thorn-crowned close-up, then a radiant Jesus) — a warm, face-to-face finish.
Weak spots: subtitle-style grey pill captions, full sentences, small — looks like auto-subs, not a comic. Fewer pictures: coins (x4), Judas (x4), potter (x4), empty desert field (x5). Zechariah is never shown — his quoted lines ("So they weighed for my price") sit over Judas with a ZECHARIAH tag, so a newcomer may think Judas is the prophet. Five near-identical desert frames in a row through the "cast down / priests / blood money / nobody / every coin" stretch.

**C** — Same art set and caption style as B, but no hook card, half the cuts (16, holds up to 7.2s), and no Jesus face at the end — it closes on a blood pool at the foot of the cross and a repeated legs-only crop. Same Zechariah-missing confusion as B. Feels like a slideshow; the landing ("Receive his price, and go free") sits on feet, not on a person you could receive anything from.

## Top 3 reasons for the ranking

1. **Variety of pictures** — A gives almost every narration beat its own picture (incl. Zechariah himself and the coin trail); B/C reuse 4-5 images and hold the desert for ~20 s of the story.
2. **Who-what-why** — A shows two different men (old prophet, then Judas) doing the same act; B/C put the prophet's words on Judas, muddying "500 years apart," which is the whole point.
3. **Finish** — A and B both end on Jesus' face/hand; C ends on feet and blood, so the invitation has no one to look at.

(B's hook card is a real plus and the fastest cut rhythm; it nearly beats A on Stop, not on the rest.)

## The ONE visible difference that drove the scores
**Variety of pictures.** Lettering and cut rhythm matter, but A wins because it has more, different, story-specific pictures — and that is also what makes its who-what-why clear.
