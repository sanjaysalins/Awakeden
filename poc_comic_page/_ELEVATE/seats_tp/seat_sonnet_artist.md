## Better of the two best
D over C. D has everything C has (banner lettering, gold keyword emphasis, balloons with tails, split panels, LUKE reference tags) and adds more: a burst balloon for the mocker, a flash-forward to the Paradise hill in the opening seconds, and two new image beats (praying figures at the cross, "REMEMBER ME" balloon) that give the ending its own picture instead of recycling the thief's face. D also cuts most often, with no hold over 3.5s.

## Top 3 reasons D wins
1. Real comic lettering grammar. Balloons have tails pointing at the speaker, the mocker gets a jagged burst, narrator gets a caption box, and the Jesus lines are gold-on-art. The reader always knows who is talking. A, B and G show every line (spoken or narrated) in the same grey pill, so the speakers blur together.
2. Cut rhythm. 28 cuts/min with a longest still of 3.5s is the one place the stills never go dead. B/E/G sit at 5.0s holds and F has a 7.9s hold, which on a static comic page reads as a stall.
3. Panel variety. Split panels (mocker over thief, thief over Jesus) show the "same nails, opposite eternities" contrast in a single frame, which is exactly the story. The other films only say it in text.

## The ONE change that made the biggest difference
Replacing the uniform grey pill with differentiated lettering (balloons with tails and a burst for speech, banners for narration, gold for key words). It lifts clarity, premium look and share-ability all at once, and it costs nothing. Second place goes to the cut rhythm.

## Which visible differences drive scores
- Lettering/balloons: biggest driver. A/B/G (plain pill) score lowest; C/D/E (balloons + banners + gold keyword) score highest. The pill also sits across the faces and bodies in A/B/G, covering the art. Banners sit at top or bottom and balloons point in.
- Cut rhythm: D (28) and C (26) feel alive. E, B, G (17-18 cuts, 5.0s holds) feel slower but more reverent; fine for the tone, weaker for the scroll. A has the most cuts (31) but its frames are repeated close-ups of the thief and the pill gives no relief, so more cuts did not rescue it.
- Split panels: help in C, D, G (contrast beats). In G they are good but the gold tag is small.
- Wild layout: D's burst balloon, the overlapping banner on the "TAUNT" frame and the early Paradise flash give punch. Risk: the ghost balloon fade ("FOR WE RECEIVE...") looks like a half-faded layer and is a small lettering fault. Keep the flash-forward only if the hook still reads.
- F (translucent boxes, Awakeden watermark, "TOO LATE FOR GOD?" title tile, "VERILY = TRULY" tile, empty cream panels in frame 1 and 2, stray words like "HE", "A CONFESSION. A", "THE OTHER" as partial captions): this is the weakest. Text is low contrast and small, some frames show a blank/unfinished panel, and the watermark clutters. The title hook idea is good, but it is executed like a draft. Nobody would call this premium.

## Notes for the artist
- Hook: only A/B/C/E/G open on a quiet wide shot of three crosses. D and F try a stronger first beat (D: mocker face + paradise glimpse; F: a title tile). D's works; F's does not.
- Ending: D/E/G finish on the Paradise hill with a short "Paradise" or "Today." word. D's "Today." as a single big word on the final frame is the best last beat.
- Art quality is the same house art in all films (strong inks, good faces). The differences are layout and lettering, not drawing.
