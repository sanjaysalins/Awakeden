## Who / what / why (one sentence)

The "why" is that a man who asks gets welcomed, no matter what he did. I got this from the balloons and captions, not from any background knowledge.

## Better of the two best
D beats C. D shows more different pictures (the angry man, then the crying man, then Jesus, then the two kneeling at the cross). The balloons also point at who is speaking, so I know who says what. C repeats the same close-ups more.

## Top 3 reasons
1. Speech balloons with a pointer, plus split panels (angry man above, crying man below). I can see who talks and the contrast between the two men with no words needed.
2. More cuts and more different images (D 28, C 26). The picture changes about every 2 seconds, so I do not get bored. B, E and G sit longer on the same face.
3. Lettering with hierarchy: short bold captions, gold on key words (OPPOSITE, INNOCENT, TODAY). My eye knows what matters even with weak English. Plain grey pills (A, B) look all the same.

## The ONE change that mattered most
Moving the Bible words into speech balloons on the picture, instead of a grey caption pill at the bottom. It turns a slideshow with subtitles into a comic where people talk. Cut rhythm is second.

## What drives scores (visible differences)
- Lettering and balloons: biggest effect on clarity and premium look (C, D, E up; A, B down).
- Cut rhythm: A has the most cuts but the same plain look. It is busy, not better. Long holds (B, E 5s, F 7.9s) feel flat.
- Split panels: help the "two men" story (C, D, G a little). Good when used sparingly.
- Wild layout: D's extra scenes (kneeling pair at a cross, sunset hill) help feel and share. F's wild layout hurts. It has empty cream boxes, cut-off text ("A CONFESSION. A", "HE") and logo clutter, so it looks unfinished. Its "TOO LATE FOR GOD?" hook is a real stop-the-scroll idea, though.

## Still weak (all films)
- "Verily" and "thou" are hard for a second-language viewer (F helps with "VERILY = TRULY").
- Opening frame does not say who the middle man is. F's hook is the only film that names a question at second 0.
- Heavy crucifixion imagery may make some people scroll past.
