## Pick of the top two: **D over C**

D wins on the three things a Short lives on: the first frame, the middle argument, the
last image.

1. **Best hook frame of all seven.** D frame 1 is a split: the mocker screaming (top) /
   the old thief weeping (bottom). No words needed; two faces, two fates, full frame.
   Every other film opens on the same wide three-crosses-and-Jerusalem shot, which is
   the expected image and the weakest stopper.
2. **The layout tells the argument, not just the words.** "Opposite eternities" is cut
   as paradise garden (top) / screaming mouth (bottom). The thief's lines sit in a
   balloon above Jesus's face in the same frame, so the exchange is intimate instead of
   a cut between two wide shots. "A verdict: innocent" and "Then one request" each get
   their own frame, so text chunks land with the voice.
3. **A new landing image that literalises the CTA.** D frame 21-22: a cross silhouette
   on the shore, one figure kneeling each side, "WE ARE ALL ONE OF THOSE TWO MEN" and
   then "REMEMBER ME." Nobody else gives the landing its own picture; A/B/C/E/G reuse
   the bald thief's head again. This is the frame that gets shared.

**D's risks (fix before it ships, they are small):**
- Frame 1 has no on-screen text. Add a short top caption so a muted scroller still
  gets "two criminals died beside Jesus".
- Frame 10 shows a half-faded (ghosted) caption. If that is a real fade mid-sentence,
  it reads as a glitch on a contact sheet; check it in motion.
- Frames 8 and 23 carry no caption; fine as breaths, but make sure no spoken line
  goes untexted.
- Last sampled frame reads "TODAY." over the garden. Make sure "PARADISE" is the final
  word on screen, since it is the final word spoken.

**C is the safe pick** if the owner wants less risk: same lettering wins, no textless
frames, 26 cuts, cleanest premium read of the set together with E. Its weakness is the
base art: the tight crop of the old thief's bald head appears in roughly 8 of 24
frames, so it feels samey by the middle.

## The ONE change that made the biggest difference

**Comic lettering: caps captions moved to the TOP of the frame + speech balloons with
tails for spoken lines + one underlined emphasis word per caption.**

Evidence from the matched pairs (same art, same edit, one variable changed):
- B -> E (plain pill captions -> comic lettering + balloons): **+9**
- A -> C (same, on the fast tight-crop edit): **+11**
- B -> G (plain lettering, add split panels only): **+3**
- A -> B (31 cuts tight crops -> 18 cuts wide crops): **+1**
- E -> D (comic lettering -> wild layout + new landing art + text chunking): **+3**

So lettering is worth ~3x what splits alone are worth, and cut rhythm alone is worth
about nothing. The wild layout adds on top of good lettering, not instead of it.

Why lettering matters this much on this set: the plain A/B/G captions sit in a white
pill in the vertical CENTRE of the frame. On the close-ups that pill covers the face
(A frame 4: the caption sits across the mocker's eyes, only the mouth shows; A frames
16-17: it crosses Jesus's brow and crown of thorns). Moving captions to the top frees
the face, and the balloon tail answers "who is talking" without a word of narration.

## Per-film notes

**A** — 31 cuts, 2.8s max hold. Plain lowercase pill captions in frame centre. Tight
crops. Caption covers faces (frames 4, 16-17). Bald thief head in ~8/24 frames. Fastest
edit in the set, but speed does not fix sameness. Luke tags are tiny yellow chips on the
caption, easy to miss.

**B** — 18 cuts, 5.0s. Same lettering as A, wider crops (Jesus bust with nail, thief with
rope and shoulder, mocker with chest). Better framing than A, but the three-crosses wide
holds for frames 9-11, a flat stretch in the middle. Centre captions still.

**C** — 26 cuts, 5.0s. A's tight-crop art + comic lettering (caps, top captions, orange
underline emphasis), balloons for the mocker and thief, Jesus's line as bold open
lettering over his face. Three meaningful splits: "SAME NAILS. SAME HILL." (mocker /
thief), "ASKED SOMEDAY. GOT TODAY." (thief / Jesus), "WITH ME. TODAY." (thief / risen
Jesus). Clean. Only drag is the repeated bald-head crop.

**D** — 28 cuts, 3.5s. The wild one. Textless visual hook (two faces), paradise/scream
split, thief-balloon-over-Jesus frames, text chunked to the voice, bottom-band captions,
new silhouette landing image. Richest storytelling. Risks listed above (textless hook,
ghosted caption frame 10, "TODAY." as the last sampled word).

**E** — 18 cuts, 5.0s. B's wide art + comic lettering + balloons, no splits. The cleanest
"premium comic" read of the set alongside C. Slightly slow and the thief/Jesus exchange
is less intimate without a split. Zero visible defects.

**F** — 21 cuts, 7.9s max hold. Real comic page layouts with white gutters, "AWAKEDEN"
brand tag top-left on every frame, "TOO LATE FOR GOD?" title on frame 1 (a strong text
hook), speaker labels with verse refs ("THE MOCKER - LUKE 23:39b", "JESUS - LUKE
23:43b"), a "VERILY = TRULY" gloss card, word-by-word kinetic captions. Best clarity
for a newcomer. Defects: on-screen text does not match the audio in places ("BOTH
SPOKE TO HIM" vs spoken "Both spoke"; "HE GOT THAT SAME AFTERNOON" vs spoken "Jesus
gave him that same afternoon"); frame 2 shows an empty cream panel; mono font + red
emphasis + dark subtitle boxes cheapen the look; the thief's whole quote holds on one
static page for ~8s.

**G** — 17 cuts, 5.0s. B's wide art + C's three splits, but with the plain centre pill
lettering and no balloons. A mixed idiom: the splits say "comic", the captions say
"subtitle". Slowest edit. Proves splits alone are not the lever.
