## Better of the two best: D (over C)
Top 3 reasons:
1. Frame 1 is the whole story: a screaming man stacked over a weeping man. The contrast ("opposite eternities") is visible before a word is read. C and A open on a static wide shot with a subtitle.
2. Words land as beats, not paragraphs. One short card per line ("A VERDICT: INNOCENT.", "TODAY.") plus balloons with tails, so a newcomer knows WHO says each line. The application line gets its own new image (two men kneeling at the cross) instead of reusing a cross shot.
3. Fastest rhythm without a dead hold (3.5s max), and the image set is the most varied — C/A keep returning to the same bowed-head face.

D's weak spots: busy; the early paradise flash (frame 4) may confuse a cold viewer; one frame shows a ghosted/faded balloon mid-fade ("FOR WE RECEIVE..."), check that transition; mocker face is reused a lot in the first 6 frames.

## The ONE change that made the biggest difference
Comic lettering — speech balloons with tails + caption boxes + gold keyword highlights — replacing the grey subtitle bar. Clean test: A vs C (same images, ~same cuts) and B vs E (same images, same 18 cuts). Lettering alone lifts each by ~1-1.5 points, mainly on premium look and who-is-speaking. It turns "slideshow with subtitles" into "a comic".

## What drives the scores
- Lettering/balloons: biggest single lever (premium + who-what-why). The burst balloon for the mocker's taunt is a strong "who is the bad guy" signal.
- Cut rhythm: helps only with variety. A has the most cuts (31) but still feels flat because it repeats one face and uses subtitles. Cuts without new images = churn.
- Split panels: help most at the open (C, D, G frame 2) — showing both thieves at once IS the premise. G proves splits alone don't rescue grey subtitles.
- Wild layout (D): best stop/finish, slight clarity risk. Worth it.
- F: best hook card ("TOO LATE FOR GOD?") and best newcomer aids (speaker tags, VERILY = TRULY), but looks unfinished — blank pale panels in frames 1-2, tiny low-contrast word-by-word cards that show half-phrases ("THE OTHER", "HE"), and a 7.9s hold. Steal its hook card and the VERILY gloss for D.
