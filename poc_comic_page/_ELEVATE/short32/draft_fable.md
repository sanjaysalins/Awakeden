## Why the hook and the landing work (3 lines)

1. **Hook:** "Jesus promised paradise to a thief" puts the two words that should not sit together — *paradise* and *thief* — in the first 2.5 seconds; a newcomer instantly knows who (Jesus, a thief), what (a promise of paradise) and is left asking why, which the rest of the piece answers.
2. **One point, built from the text's own contrast:** the dying man asks only to be *remembered* "when thou comest" — a memory, later — and Jesus answers with "To day" and "with me": not later but today, not a memory but His company. The pivot is in the structure (asked for less, given more), not in clever vocabulary.
3. **Landing is earned, not stock:** it closes on the verbs the passage itself gives — he *asked* ("he said unto Jesus"), and he was *heard* (Jesus "said unto him") — and the single soft invitation "You can ask too" offers the thief's own action to the viewer with no fear, no reward-framing and no urgency; the grace is already shown in the text (a guilty man, no hands free, only words, answered in full).
