# Newcomer blind seat (Sonnet)

## Script 1
1. Understood: Two men die next to a man called Jesus. One mocks, one asks to be remembered, and Jesus says "today, with me, in paradise." Why: a small ask got a big answer. Odd/unclear: "thou be Christ" (what is Christ?), "paradise" (heaven?), "To day" (two words), "criminals dying beside Jesus" (who is Jesus, why on a cross?), "ask Him the same" (ask for what?).

## Script 2
1. Understood: Jesus promised paradise to a thief, and I want to know why. The thief asked for little and got "today." Odd/unclear: "paradise," "thou be Christ," "And with Him." (broken line), "asked for very little" (what is little?), "ask Him the same" (ask for what?). Also no setup: where are they, why dying?
2. Hook 8. Gap opens 8. Payoff 6 (the "why" is only half answered; cross is never shown). Landing earned 6. Pressure-sell 7. Clear "ask the same" 5.

## Script 3
1. Understood: Two criminals die next to Jesus. One mocks, one defends Jesus and asks to be remembered. Jesus says "with me." Odd/unclear: "amiss" (archaic, but meaning is clear from "defended"), "paradise," "thou be Christ," "ask Him the same."
2. Hook 5. Gap opens 4. Payoff 7. Landing earned 8. Pressure-sell 7. Clear "ask the same" 6 ("with me" helps).

## Rank
1. Script 2 for the hook (but it is the least clear on who/what).
2. Script 3 for clarity and the strongest landing.
3. Script 1 (works, no standout moment).

Best line: "Jesus promised paradise to a thief. Why?" (Script 2)
Worst line: "He asked to be remembered someday. Jesus gave him today. And with Him." (Script 2, the broken "And with Him." fragment)

Fix for top pick (Script 2): add a short setup, e.g. "Both were dying on crosses beside Jesus," and make the ending clear, e.g. "You can ask Him to remember you too."
