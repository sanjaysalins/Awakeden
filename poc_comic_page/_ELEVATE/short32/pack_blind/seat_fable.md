Read: candidates.md only. Checked against Luke 23:39-43 KJV as given.

## Script 1

**1. Newcomer (no Bible, English second language)**
Who/what/why: Jesus is dying next to two criminals. One wants to be saved from dying. The other only asks Jesus to remember him later. Jesus says: not later — today, you will be with me in paradise.
Lines I did not get on first hearing:
- "If thou be Christ, save thyself and us" — old words. The narrator line after it saves me ("One wanted off the cross").
- "off the cross" — I was not told they were ON crosses. First line says "dying beside Jesus", not how.
- "when thou comest into thy kingdom" — old words, but "He asked to be remembered. Someday." tells me what it meant.

**2. Bible reader**
- Thief quote: verbatim v.39. Correct.
- "The other said Jesus had done nothing wrong" = v.41 "done nothing amiss". Correct plain paraphrase.
- Penitent quote: verbatim v.42. Correct.
- Jesus quote: verbatim v.43 minus "Verily I say unto thee". A trim, not a misquote. Fine.
- "Both asked Him for something": the first man "railed" (taunted), he did not politely ask. Mild stretch, but "save thyself and us" IS a demand to be saved, so defensible.
- "Someday" for "when thou comest into thy kingdom": fair plain gloss. Loses the note that the thief believed Jesus had a kingdom coming — but the twist (today vs someday) is Luke's own.
- Nothing wrong. Nothing invented.

## Script 2

**1. Newcomer**
Who/what/why: Jesus promised paradise to a thief. The thief asked for only a small thing (remember me someday). Jesus gave him more — today, and with Him.
Lines I did not get:
- Right after "Jesus promised paradise to a thief", a thief speaks ("save thyself and us"). For a moment I thought THIS was the thief who got paradise. "One man mocked" fixes it, but I was lost for one line.
- The hook asks "Why?" The script never clearly says why. I am left to guess "because he asked". A newcomer may feel the question was not answered.

**2. Bible reader**
- "Thief": Luke says malefactor; Matthew/Mark say thieves. Standard, fine.
- "One man mocked" = "railed". Correct.
- "Asked for very little": interpretive. He asked to be in Christ's kingdom — small in words, huge in faith. Acceptable as a plain gloss.
- All three quotes verbatim (v.43 trimmed as in Script 1).
- Omits v.40-41 entirely: the rebuke, the confession of guilt, "this man hath done nothing amiss". That is the text's own answer to the hook's "Why?" (he owned his guilt and trusted Jesus). Not an error — but it cuts the Bible's reason and then asks the question anyway.
- Nothing wrong. Nothing invented.

## Script 3

**1. Newcomer**
Who/what/why: Two criminals dying next to Jesus. One wants out. The other says Jesus did nothing wrong and asks to be remembered. Jesus says he will be with Him in paradise.
Lines I did not get:
- "This man hath done nothing amiss" — I do not know "amiss". "The other defended Jesus" before it helps, but the word itself is a wall.
- Five old-English lines in about 30 seconds. More than the other two. I spent more effort decoding than feeling.
- Same "off the cross" problem as Script 1.

**2. Bible reader**
- Most complete of the three: includes the defense (v.41) as a real quote, verbatim. Correct.
- "Defended Jesus" = rebuked the other and declared Him innocent. Correct.
- "Asked for very little": same note as Script 2.
- All quotes verbatim (v.43 trimmed).
- Landing drops the today/someday contrast — Luke's sharpest surprise — and re-quotes only "with me". True, but the text's own twist is left on the table.
- Nothing wrong. Nothing invented.

## Rank

1. **Script 1** — sharpest pay-off (someday → today; remembered → with Him), plainest narrator bridges, nothing wrong.
2. **Script 2** — best hook, but it asks "Why?" and does not clearly answer it; also cuts the verse that holds the answer.
3. **Script 3** — most faithful to the full passage, but heaviest in old words ("amiss" unexplained) and the landing throws away the "today" twist.

2 vs 3 is close. 2 wins on hook; 3 wins on Bible completeness. For a 30-second global short, hook and a clear pay-off matter more, so 2 edges it.

**Best line anywhere:** Script 1 — "Not someday. Today. Not just remembered. With Him."
Two contrasts, four short words each, all taken from the quotes we just heard. Earned, plain, global.

**Worst line anywhere:** Script 3 — "He asked to be remembered. Jesus said, 'with me.'"
Flattest landing of the three. Re-quotes two words from a line heard three seconds earlier and skips the text's real surprise (today).

**One-line fix for the top pick (Script 1):**
Change the first line to "Two criminals were dying on crosses beside Jesus. Both asked Him for something."
Gives the newcomer the picture BEFORE "One wanted off the cross" — right now "cross" arrives after it is needed.

**Shared note (all three):** the closer "You can ask Him the same" is gentle and not a pressure sell, but it is the same stock closer on all three and "the same" is slightly vague for a newcomer (ask for what — to be remembered? to be with Him?). Not blocking.
