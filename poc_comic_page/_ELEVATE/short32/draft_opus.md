# Two Thieves, 32s short (Luke 23:39-43), draft (opus seat)

## Script

[narrator] Two criminals were dying beside Jesus. Both asked Him for something.
[thief] "If thou be Christ, save thyself and us."
[narrator] One wanted off the cross. The other defended Jesus.
[penitent] "This man hath done nothing amiss."
[narrator] Then he asked for very little.
[penitent] "Lord, remember me when thou comest into thy kingdom."
[jesus] "To day shalt thou be with me in paradise."
[narrator] He asked to be remembered. Jesus said, "with me." You can ask Him the same.

## Numbers

- Words: 73 (narrator 35, thief 8, penitent 15, jesus 9... narrator 35 + quotes 38 = 73)
- Est. time: 73 / 2.4 = ~30.4s, plus short pauses between voices = ~32s

## Why it works

1. Hook: "Both asked Him for something" opens a gap: the mocker asked too, so the viewer must wait to see what made the two asks different.
2. Point: one ask was "get me down," the other was "remember me." Plain words, the twist is in the structure, not the vocabulary.
3. Landing: earned from the quote itself. He asked only to be "remembered"; Jesus gave him "with me." The close is a soft invite, no fear, no pressure.
