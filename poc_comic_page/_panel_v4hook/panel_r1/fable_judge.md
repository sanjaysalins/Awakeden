## 1. WHO IS THIS FOR
Ana, 34, Sao Paulo, raised religious, drifted, scrolls at 1am thinking she has "done too much to go back." She stops on the question (line 1), finishes because line 3 answers her in Jesus' own words, and shares it to a friend in the same place. NOT served: anyone with no fear of God to begin with (the hook assumes you already want in), and the long-time Bible reader, who gets a famous verse read the standard way with only "in no wise = never" as a new bit.

## 2. MAIN TAKEAWAY
"If I come to Jesus, He will never turn me away, whatever I have done." Carried by line 3 and the IN NO WISE = NEVER card (23.8s). Clear: one verse, one thread, and the end card repeats it.

## 3. GLOBAL AUDIENCE
Who: a frightened man at a shut door, and Jesus. What: Jesus promises never to reject anyone who comes. Why: His own words say so. Where I got lost: the scroll is never named as "your record" on screen, and "record" (lines 1, 5) means a song or a sports result in many languages, so the prop stays a mystery. At 36.5s a scroll is nailed to a wooden post with a comic "THUD!" while the voice says "too far gone" (line 4); no viewer can say what that picture means. The door shows a heavy bolt at 25-30s, then line 5 says it "was never locked"; the picture argues with the words. Words that fail in translation: "cometh" (lines 3, 4), "fine print" (line 4), "rehearsing" (lines 2, 5), "too far gone" (line 4). "In no wise" is rescued by the card; good.

## 4. BIBLICALLY ACCURATE
- Line 3, John 6:37: RIGHT, KJV verbatim. "In no wise = never": RIGHT (Greek ou me, the strongest "never").
- Line 3 "Not if you clean up first": RIGHT; the verse sets no condition on the one who comes.
- Line 4 "him that cometh has no fine print": RIGHT for the verse's second half; the first half ("All that the Father giveth me") is quoted but not explained, which a careful reader will notice.
- Line 4 "The only way to be cast out is to never come": OVERCLAIMED from this verse alone; supported by John 3:18, 36.
- Line 5 "He already read it": RIGHT (John 2:24-25; Hebrews 4:13).
- Line 5 "never locked": RIGHT in spirit (John 10:9), but the film's own bolted door at 25-30s contradicts it.
Nothing WRONG. Grace-anchored, no pressure, lands on Jesus.

## 5. BEST VERSION POSSIBLE
The best explainer lets no picture fight the words and gives the muted viewer the CTA in big text. ONE change: rebuild the payoff page (44-57s). Jesus' head is cropped off by the frame edge during "So come" (line 5), a scroll lies dropped on the floor while the voice says "bring" it, and there is no big text at all from 27s to 58s. Fix: full-figure welcome panel, scroll in the man's hand, big card "SO COME." on line 5. Score: 7/10.

## CURIOSITY GAP
Opens at line 1 (0s), pays off at line 3 (18.6s). Score 5/10: the title and the hook already give away that the answer is "no," so it is a want, not a wonder; the "= NEVER" decode is what earns the wait. Worth it, barely; 18 seconds is long for a guessable answer.

## THREE SMALLEST FIXES
1. Line 1: "He'd look at your record and turn you away" -> "He'd read your record, everything you have done, and turn you away."
2. Line 4: "But him that cometh has no fine print." -> "But 'him that cometh' means anyone who comes. No conditions. No exceptions."
3. Line 5: "The door you've been rehearsing at was never locked." -> "The door you've been standing at was never locked."

## 6. MOST VIEWERS
ONE change: the first frame. At 0.3s the top half is a wide aerial shot with a tiny figure and the bottom half is a pale pencil sketch; only the text is doing the work. Open full-bleed on the inked close-up from 5.5s (hooded face, hand flat on the door) with "AFRAID GOD WILL TURN YOU AWAY?" over it, then cut to the wide. A face at a door stops a thumb; a sketch does not. KEEP: the ink + night-blue + gold palette (distinct in a feed of oil-painting Bible shorts), the pencil-to-ink reveal, the IN NO WISE = NEVER card, the consistent Jesus face across panels, the arms-open archway ending. Drop the "THUD!" burst; it is the one cartoon note in a reverent piece. The comic style travels well worldwide: no uncanny photoreal Jesus, reads at phone size, captions sit in-world. Odds now: swipe in 3s 50% (+/-15), watch to end 25% (+/-10), share 3% (+/-2) of finishers. After the change: 42% / 28% / 4%, same error bars.
