## 1. WHO IS THIS FOR
Named viewer: Dewi, 34, shift worker in Jakarta, English second language, feels ashamed of past mistakes. She stops at the big text "AFRAID GOD WILL TURN YOU AWAY?" (frame 0.3), because the fear is hers (line 1). She finishes if the quiet voice stays kind. She shares with a friend who feels guilty. NOT served: anyone with no fear of God or no idea who "God" or "Jesus" is; a curious viewer who wants a story or facts.

## 2. MAIN TAKEAWAY
"If you come to Jesus, he will never send you away, however bad your record is." Carried by line 3 ("him that cometh to me I will in no wise cast out") and line 5. Mostly clear: the last frame (open arms, "HE WILL NEVER TURN YOU AWAY") says it with no words needed. But I only guess who Jesus is.

## 3. GLOBAL AUDIENCE
Who: an old man at a door at night with a scroll; a kind man, called Jesus, who welcomes him. What: you are afraid you will be rejected; he says no. Why: so you will not stay afraid and will "come".
Lost or misled:
- Line 1 "God" and line 2 "Jesus" are not linked. I guess Jesus is God, or God's helper. Never told. Line 2 "Jesus answers it before you can ask" does not say who he is.
- Line 3 "All that the Father giveth me shall come to me": who is "the Father"? Lost. "cometh" and "in no wise cast out" are old, odd English. The film itself helps with "Not maybe" and the on-screen "IN NO WISE = NEVER" (good).
- Line 4 "no fine print", "the exception", "too far gone": idioms that fail in translation. "Record" (line 1, 5) is guessed as "bad things I did"; the scroll helps but a literal reader may think a police or work record.
- Line 5 "The door you've been rehearsing at": "rehearsing" is hard. "Come" is vague: come where? do what? Pray? Go to church? Never told. That is my biggest question.
- The door is shown closed, then locked-looking, then open. Fine, but the old man seems to be Jesus's visitor, not me.

## 4. BIBLICALLY ACCURATE
I cannot judge accuracy. Things I was TOLD but could not follow: who "the Father" is (line 3); who Jesus is; what "come to me" means in real life (lines 3-5); "He already read it" (line 5, how, when?); the verse name "JOHN 6:37" (a book? a person?).

## 5. BEST VERSION POSSIBLE
The best explainer would say in plain words in the first 10 seconds: "Jesus, the man many call God's Son, said this." Then say what "come" means in one concrete act (e.g. "talk to him, right now, in your own words"). The one highest-value change: define "come" with one simple action. Score: 6/10. Emotion and picture are strong; the instruction is blurry.

## CURIOSITY GAP
Opens at line 1 (title card, "turn you away?"); pays off at line 3 and visually at the last frame (open arms). Score 7/10. Worth the wait, yes, though the payoff is emotional more than informative.

## THREE SMALLEST FIXES
- Line 2: "Jesus answers it before you can ask." -> "Jesus, God's Son, answers that fear before you ask."
- Line 3: "All that the Father giveth me shall come to me;" -> "All that God, my Father, gives me will come to me;" (keep the KJV on screen, change the spoken frame).
- Line 5: "So come." -> "So come to him. Just talk to him, as you are."

## 6. MOST VIEWERS
The ONE change: add a very short first-line identity ("Jesus said:") over the hook so a stranger knows who is speaking; keep the first 3 seconds' question and the open-arms ending. KEEP: the big hook text, the warm gold/night blue look, the final open-arms picture, "IN NO WISE = NEVER".
Odds (rough, simulated, wide error bars): swipe in 3s about 40% (+/-15), watch to end about 45% (+/-15), share about 3% (+/-2). The hook is relevant to guilty viewers but the look is dark and busy; strangers may think it is a religious ad.
