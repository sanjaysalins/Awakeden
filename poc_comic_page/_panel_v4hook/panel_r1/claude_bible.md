## 1. WHO IS THIS FOR
A churchgoing or lapsed-Christian viewer, about 30, who feels unworthy and hasn't prayed in months. They stop on the hook (0–3.6s, "AFRAID GOD WILL TURN YOU AWAY?"), finish because line 5 is the payoff, and share it with a friend who is struggling. Not served: non-believers with no idea who "the Father" is, and anyone who wants the reasoning behind the verse. It is warm reassurance, not explanation.

## 2. MAIN TAKEAWAY
"Jesus promised he will never cast out anyone who comes to him." Line 3 (the quote) and line 5 ("still said come") carry it. The film makes it clear, and the title and the final on-screen text repeat it.

## 3. GLOBAL AUDIENCE
- Who: a man afraid of rejection. What: Jesus says he won't reject him. Why: so he comes.
- "In no wise" (line 3) is archaic. The film does gloss it with "IN NO WISE = NEVER", which is good.
- "Fine print" (line 4) is an idiom that translates poorly.
- "Cast out", "record", "the exception" and "rehearsing" are abstract for ESL viewers.
- "Jesus answers it before you can ask" (line 2) is vague, and the film shows no one called "Jesus" before this line.
- "The Father giveth me" is unexplained, but the quote is short enough to pass.

## 4. BIBLICALLY ACCURATE
- L1 "quiet fear… He'd look at your record and turn you away": UNSUPPORTED by John 6:37. It is a pastoral framing, not a claim from the text. Fine as a hook.
- L2 "Jesus answers it before you can ask": UNSUPPORTED/loose. The verse does not speak to a "fear", and "before you can ask" is rhetorical.
- L3 the quote: RIGHT. It matches the KJV verbatim ("All that the Father giveth me shall come to me; and him that cometh to me I will in no wise cast out"). "Not maybe. Not if you clean up first": the first is a fair gloss of "in no wise". The second is OVERCLAIMED. It is a reasonable inference, but the verse says nothing about cleaning up. Mark it as commentary.
- L4 "him that cometh has no fine print": RIGHT in effect, since the promise is unconditional. "The only way to be cast out is to never come": OVERCLAIMED. The verse says nothing about a way to be "cast out". It promises that the one who comes is not cast out. The line also blurs the first half of the verse ("All that the Father giveth me shall come"), which stresses the Father's giving and the certainty of coming. "Never come" implies that coming is a free choice with no divine initiative, and the film drops that tension. Better: "Everyone who comes is received."
- L4 "too far gone, too late, too much": UNSUPPORTED by this verse alone. It is an application of "him that cometh" (anyone), which is fair.
- L5 "Bring the record you're ashamed of - He already read it, and still said come": OVERCLAIMED. John 6:37 says nothing about Jesus having "read" a record. Omniscience is true elsewhere (John 2:25), but it is not in the supplied text. "Still said come" is not in the verse either. The invitation to "come" is elsewhere (Matt 11:28), not here. Here it is a promise, not a command.
- L5 "The door… was never locked": UNSUPPORTED. The door is a modern metaphor. John 10:9 ("I am the door") is the nearby grounding, but it is not in the supplied KJV. The door and the scroll "record" are not drawn from John 6:37. They are pictures added to the verse, not exegesis of it. A "record" does echo Col 2:14 (a handwriting of ordinances), but that is outside the supplied text.
- On-screen "HE WILL NEVER TURN YOU AWAY": a fair paraphrase of "in no wise cast out". "IN NO WISE = NEVER" is RIGHT.

Doctrinal soundness overall: no heresy. The risks are the unconditional-sounding "no fine print" with no mention of repentance or faith, and treating "come" as the only requirement. "Cometh" itself implies faith, and that is acceptable here.

## 5. BEST VERSION POSSIBLE
The best explainer would show the man actually knocking or stepping through the door, and would keep every claim inside the verse. The highest-value change is to cut or flag the claims that go beyond it ("He already read it, and still said come"; "the only way to be cast out is to never come"). Score: 6.5/10.

## CURIOSITY GAP
Opens at line 1, with the question "will He turn me away?". It pays off at line 3 (the quote) and lands at line 5. Score 7/10. Worth the wait, but the answer arrives at about 18s, and the question is answered in the title before that.

## THREE SMALLEST FIXES
1. L4: change "The only way to be cast out is to never come." to "No one who comes is ever turned away."
2. L5: change "He already read it, and still said come." to "He knows all of it, and His promise still stands."
3. L2: change "Jesus answers it before you can ask." to "Jesus answers that fear in one sentence."

## 6. MOST VIEWERS
Keep: the hook text, the gold-light ending, and "IN NO WISE = NEVER". Change: move the verse to the first 8 seconds, with the line 3 voice-over first. Open on the question, then give the answer straight away. The 18s wait is too long for a Short.

Odds, with wide error bars: swipe in the first 3s about 45% (±15); watch to the end about 40% (±12); share about 2% (±1.5).
