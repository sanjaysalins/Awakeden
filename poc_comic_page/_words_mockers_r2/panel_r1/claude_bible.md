## PER VERSION

**X — 6/10**
- "A king named David wrote these words": RIGHT in attribution (the pack presents Ps 22 as David's).
- "A thousand years later, a crowd at the cross spoke almost the same words": RIGHT in substance. The "thousand years" figure is not in the KJV text supplied, so it is UNSUPPORTED by the pack but historically fair.
- "People walk past and shake their heads. The leaders join in.": RIGHT. Mt 27:39 has "wagging their heads", Ps 22:7 has "shake the head", and Mt 27:41 has the chief priests.
- Ps 22:8 quote, verbatim and unpunctuated: RIGHT. The KJV is "He trusted on the LORD that he would deliver him", and the line stops before "let him deliver him".
- Mt 27:43 quote, "He trusted in God; let him deliver him now": RIGHT. It is truncated before "if he will have him", but the clause is intact.
- "if God wants Him, let God rescue Him": RIGHT. This is a fair paraphrase of "if he will have him".
- "come down from the cross" [Mt 27:40]: RIGHT. It is a verbatim fragment, but it drops "If thou be the Son of God", so the sneer's condition is lost.
- "He had said that no one takes His life, He lays it down Himself": RIGHT. This paraphrases John 10:18, but there is no citation on screen.
- "prayed for them: Father, forgive them" [Luke 23:34]: RIGHT. Luke 23:34 is in the pack, but the line is only a paraphrase and is not cited as a quote.
- "David's song … says people from every nation will remember, and turn to the LORD": RIGHT. It matches Ps 22:27, "All the ends of the world… all the kindreds of the nations".
- Overall: accurate. Weaknesses: the Luke 23:34 prayer and the John 10:18 claim are not given as quotes, and the shout is attributed to "a crowd" without saying the passers-by said it (Mt 27:40).

**Y — 8/10**
- "People are laughing at him, and he writes down what they say": RIGHT for Ps 22:7, "laugh me to scorn… saying". The "writes down" framing is interpretive but fine.
- "The leaders in the crowd call out" [Mt 27:43]: RIGHT. The chief priests, scribes and elders speak at 27:41–43.
- "Almost the same words, a thousand years apart": RIGHT.
- "come down from the cross": RIGHT. It is verbatim, but it is attributed to a "they" who are still "the leaders". In Mt 27:40 it was the passers-by, and the leaders say it again at 27:42, so this is fine.
- "Jesus had said that no one takes His life from Him. He gives it Himself.": RIGHT, a paraphrase of John 10:18.
- Luke 23:34 quote: RIGHT and verbatim: "Father, forgive them; for they know not what they do."
- "shall remember and turn unto the LORD" [Ps 22:27]: RIGHT. It is a verbatim fragment.
- "People from every nation, turning to the LORD": RIGHT.
- "Almost the same words" is slightly OVERCLAIMED: Ps 22:8 reads "that he would deliver him" and Mt 27:43 reads "let him deliver him now". The overlap is real but not exact. "Almost" is acceptable.
- Overall: the best fidelity and the clearest sourcing.

**Z — 7/10**
- "King David wrote": RIGHT.
- "words very like them": RIGHT and more careful than "almost the same".
- Ps 22:8 with a closing period, "…deliver him.": minor. The KJV continues with a colon, "let him deliver him", so the period misrepresents the punctuation but not the meaning.
- Mt 27:43 with a period: the same minor issue.
- "If thou be the Son of God, come down from the cross." [Mt 27:40]: RIGHT. This is full and verbatim, and it keeps the "Son of God" taunt.
- "No man taketh it from me." [John 10:18]: RIGHT and verbatim.
- But it is spoken by the narrator while the labelled voices carry other Scripture, and the line is not tied to its context. John 10:18 says "it", meaning His life, so a newcomer may not know what "it" is. This is an accuracy-of-comprehension problem, not a Bible error.
- Luke 23:34: RIGHT and verbatim.
- "Psalm 22 does not end with mocking. David ended it with people from every nation turning to God.": RIGHT.
- **"Remember and turn unto the LORD." [Ps 22:27]: OVERCLAIMED and not verbatim.** The KJV reads "shall remember and turn unto the LORD", and the quote drops "shall" and capitalizes "Remember", so it turns a prophecy into a command. The quote marks make it a misquote.
- **"That means people like you, from any nation. No one is pushing you. The invitation is open.": OVERCLAIMED.** Ps 22:27 predicts that the nations will turn. It does not say "people like you" or give an open invitation. This is application, not text. It is doctrinally harmless but is presented as what the verse "means".
- "Jesus prayed for them while they mocked": RIGHT. Luke 23:34 is placed at the cross, though the pack does not say the prayer came during the mockery.

## RANKING
1. **Y**: all its quotes are verbatim, the sources are sequenced clearly, and it makes no verse-level overclaims.
2. **Z**: it has the fullest quotes (including Mt 27:40 and John 10:18), but it has one misquote and an overclaiming gloss.
3. **X**: it is accurate but thin on sourcing. The Luke prayer and John 10:18 are only paraphrased, and "Father, forgive them" is clipped.

## WHAT TO TAKE FROM THE OTHERS
- From Z: the full Mt 27:40 "If thou be the Son of God" (it gives the taunt its point) and "words very like them" in place of "almost the same".
- From X: the compact hook.
- From Y: keep "shall remember and turn unto the LORD" verbatim.

## ANY ACCURACY PROBLEM
- **Z:** `"Remember and turn unto the LORD." [Psalm 22:27]` is not verbatim. It drops "shall", which changes a prediction into a command.
- **Z:** "That means people like you, from any nation." This reads the verse as a personal invitation. It is application, not text.
- **X and Y:** "Almost the same words" is acceptable but loose. X also lacks a quote mark on the Luke prayer.
- **All:** "A thousand years later" is not in the KJV text. It is historically defensible, so I'd keep it.
