## PER VERSION

**X (6/10)**
- Who/what/why: A king (David) wrote words. 1000 years later a crowd said almost the same words to Jesus on a cross. Jesus stayed, forgave them. David's song says all nations will turn to God. So: turn to Jesus.
- Could not follow: "trusted on the LORD" (odd grammar) and "deliver him" (explained after, but late). "He had said that no one takes His life, He lays it down Himself" is compressed; I do not know when/who he said it to. "Father, forgive them" is only paraphrased, no full line. The ending claim "It says people from every nation will remember..." has no quoted line, so I must trust it. "The One who stayed" is clear enough from "He stayed". The hook line "Who wrote the mockery" gets answered fast (good).
- Tight and short. Gaps: little emotion, ending is abrupt.

**Y (8/10)**
- Who/what/why: Clearest order. David first (people laugh at him, he writes it down). Then the cross, same words. "Hear it? Almost the same words" tells me what to notice. Jesus could come down but stayed, prayed "Father, forgive them" (full quote). David's song ends with nations turning to the LORD. You can turn to the One who stayed.
- Could not follow: "the LORD" (who is that?). "Deliver" is explained right after the quote (good). "He gives it Himself" is a bit vague. "Psalm" never named, only "David's song" (fine for me).
- Best story shape: setup, parallel, pivot, ending. The quote of the forgiveness line gives the emotional peak.

**Z (6/10)**
- Who/what/why: Same story, but longest and most split up. Two short bridge lines ("They said more to Him." "Jesus had said:") make it choppy on a phone. The John 10:18 quote "No man taketh it from me" alone, with no "of myself" part, is hard: "taketh" is old English, and the half-quote may confuse me. "Deliver" is never explained, so I do not know the mocking meaning. The "If thou be the Son of God" line uses "thou", hard for me. "Remember and turn unto the LORD" is clear. "No one is pushing you. The invitation is open." is warm and clear, but wordy.
- Most direct invitation, but "deliver" unexplained and old words hurt.

## RANKING
1. Y: clearest order, explains "deliver", full forgiveness quote, "Hear it?" cue helps a newcomer see the match.
2. X: short and clear, but compressed; paraphrase leaves key lines unquoted.
3. Z: choppy bridges, unexplained "deliver", old-English half-quote of John 10:18.

(X and Z are close; I give X the edge for plain wording.)

## WHAT TO TAKE FROM THE OTHERS
- From X: the fast opening that names Jesus as Son of God in one breath; "People walk past and shake their heads" is a vivid, simple line.
- From Z: the warm last lines ("No one is pushing you. The invitation is open.") are the kindest, most newcomer-friendly invitation. Keep a short version.
- Add to Y: one half-line on who "the LORD" is (God), and name "Psalm 22" once for Bible readers.

## ANY ACCURACY PROBLEM
- X: "It says people from every nation will remember, and turn to the LORD" is fair to Ps 22:27. "He had said that no one takes His life, He lays it down Himself" fair to John 10:18. X says the crowd shouted "come down from the cross" but cuts "If thou be the Son of God", fine.
- X/Y: the [david] quote "He trusted on the LORD that he would deliver him" is Ps 22:8, but in the psalm those are the mockers' words about David; X/Y say David "wrote these words" and Y says "he writes down what they say" (Y is accurate, X is looser).
- Z: "David ended it with people from every nation turning to God" is fine. Quote "Remember and turn unto the LORD" trims the verse start ("All the ends of the world shall"), acceptable. Z's Matt 27:40 quote merges two phrases ("If thou be the Son of God, come down from the cross") skipping "save thyself"; minor.
- All: "a thousand years later" is roughly right (about 1000 years).
- Y: "shall remember and turn unto the LORD" cut from verse 27 mid-sentence, fine.
