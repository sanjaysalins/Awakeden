## PER VERSION

### X — 5/10
Fails on a small screen and in translation: the title **mockery**; **On that cross hung Jesus** (backwards sentence); **walk past**; **shake their heads** (in some countries a head-shake means yes); **join in**; **trusted on**; **deliver** (also means “hand over a package” or “birth” — the rescue line helps); **He could have.** (could have what?); **lays it down**; **He stayed**; **the One who stayed**.

Served: fluent viewers who already know the cross. Not served: anyone reading captions, or anyone who needs the missing verb. Offense stays low. **They said: if God wants Him** can sound, for a moment, like the film’s own claim that God might refuse Jesus. The crowd is not called “the Jews.” That is the right care.

### Y — 7/10
Fails: title **mockery**; **spoke them** (what is “them”?); fragments **First, David.** and **Now the cross.**; **writes down**; **trusted on**; balloon **deliver** (narrator does add “rescue”); lowercase **come down from the cross**; **know not**; **for they** (“for” = because); **unto**; subjectless **shall remember**; fragment **People from every nation, turning**; **the One who stayed**.

Served: an intermediate second-language viewer who needs short sentences, **laughing**, **hangs there, dying**, and **Hear it?** Not served: a beginner facing **know not** and **unto** with no plain line beside them. Offense stays low. The same “they did not know” line can sound like the film excuses the cruelty. Speakers stay “leaders” and “crowd.”

### Z — 4/10
Fails: title **mockery**; **very like** (“like” is easy to mix up); **trusted on**; **deliver** with no rescue word anywhere; **If thou be**; **No man** (sounds like “no male”); **taketh**; **it** (no noun in the balloon); **know not**; **for they**; **Psalm** (never says it is a song); **unto**; **Remember and turn** (reads as an order); **The invitation is open**; **the One who stayed**.

Served: a Bible reader who wants real lines and can decode old English. The opening (**a crowd**, **a cross**, **The man on that cross was Jesus**) is the clearest of the three, and **That means people like you** plus **No one is pushing you** is the kindest close. Not served: the target viewer. The balloons are the film, and these balloons are old English. A strong misread: **Remember** lands as a command. Offense is the lowest of the three because the push is named and refused.

## RANKING
1. **Y** — only version that keeps short plain speech and still translates the hard word “deliver.”
2. **X** — easier balloons, broken narrator sentences.
3. **Z** — clearest order, and the version a learner is most likely to understand wrong.

## WHAT TO TAKE FROM THE OTHERS
From Y, keep **Deliver means rescue**, **He could have come down**, **Hear it?**, **hangs there, dying**, **no one**, and **song**. From X, keep the plain taunt **if God wants Him, let God rescue Him**, and the easy prayer line **Father, forgive them**. From Z, keep **words very like them**, First / Then, **The man on that cross was Jesus**, **He gave His life**, **That means people like you**, and **No one is pushing you**. Any “thou / taketh / know not / unto” balloon needs those plain words on the same card.

## ANY ACCURACY PROBLEM
Y: **“The crowd at the cross spoke them.”** They spoke words close to David’s, which Y itself later calls **“Almost the same words.”**

Y: **“It ends like this.”** then **“shall remember and turn unto the LORD.”** Psalm 22 does not end there (it goes on to 22:31). The real line has a subject: **“All the ends of the world shall remember and turn unto the LORD.”**

Z: **“David ended it with people from every nation turning to God.”** Same overclaim. The balloon **“Remember and turn unto the LORD.”** cuts **“All the ends of the world shall”** and turns a statement into an order.

All three David balloons stop at **“He trusted on the LORD that he would deliver him.”** The matching half is still in the verse: **“let him deliver him, seeing he delighted in him.”** Matthew’s taunt is that half.

X and Y label **“come down from the cross”** as Matthew 27:40. That verse says, **“If thou be the Son of God, come down from the cross.”** Those words are the passers-by (27:39–40). **“He trusted in God; let him deliver him now”** is the chief priests (27:43). Y’s “leaders call out” fits the second line, then the next shout is cited as the passers-by’s verse.

X: **“It says people from every nation will remember, and turn to the LORD.”** That is a summary. The verse’s words are the “ends of the world” and “all the kindreds of the nations.”

## VERDICT
No version locks. Y is the one to rebuild (7/10). Put Z’s “very like” and “that means” into Y, and put X’s rescue sentence under the deliver balloon. A second-language viewer still cannot read **know not**, **unto**, **thou**, **taketh**, or a cut Psalm 22:27.
