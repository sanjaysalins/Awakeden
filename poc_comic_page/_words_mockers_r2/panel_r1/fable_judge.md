# JUDGE (no hat) — blind seat

## PER VERSION

### X
- **Who for:** a cold newcomer; all three names (David, the crowd, Jesus) land in ¶1.
- **Takeaway:** Jesus could have come down and chose to stay; you can turn to Him.
- **Global audience:** best glosses of the three — "Deliver means rescue" and "if God wants Him, let God rescue Him" (¶5). But ¶2 ("People walk past and shake their heads") puts us at the cross, then ¶3 jumps to David with no signpost — a newcomer hears David speaking AT the cross. ¶7 "He could have." is an ellipsis non-native ears will miss.
- **Biblically accurate:** yes. ¶6 (27:40) placed after ¶4 (27:43) with "Then" reverses Matthew's order. ¶8 paraphrases Ps 22:27 instead of quoting it — the ending is told, not shown.
- **Best version?** No. Safest on runtime (~140 words), but the echo is asserted in ¶1 ("almost the same words") and never heard as a reveal.
- **Score 6/10. Curiosity gap 4/10.**

### Y
- **Who for:** newcomer and Bible reader both. "First, David." / "Now the cross." (¶2, ¶4) is the clearest who/where in the pack.
- **Takeaway:** the crowd quoted David's psalm without knowing it; the psalm ends with nations turning; He stayed, so you can turn.
- **Global audience:** ¶6 "Hear it? Almost the same words, a thousand years apart." is the only line in the three versions that makes the viewer do the comparison — that IS the payoff. Keeps "Deliver means rescue." Nothing load-bearing is archaic.
- **Biblically accurate:** ¶1 "The crowd at the cross spoke them" overclaims; ¶6 then correctly says "almost". ¶4 "The leaders... call out" followed by ¶7 citing 27:40 puts the passers-by's line in the leaders' mouths (their own version is 27:42). ¶10 "It ends like this" — 22:27 is the closing movement, not the last verse; acceptable. ¶9 and ¶11 are KJV-exact.
- **Best version?** Closest. Risk: longest (~165 words) — at 60-70s it needs a quick narrator or a trim.
- **Score 8/10. Curiosity gap 7/10.**

### Z
- **Who for:** a viewer who wants the verses themselves. Most scaffolded ("First... Then... Now the ending").
- **Takeaway:** same, with the fullest Scripture (John 10:18 and the whole of 27:40 in balloons).
- **Global audience:** FAILS the gloss — "deliver" (¶3, ¶5) is never explained; a non-native hears a parcel word. "thou be" (¶7) and "taketh" (¶9) both quoted; ¶10 does paraphrase ¶9, good. Connective tissue is bare ("They said more to Him.", ¶6) — no picture and no payoff beat after ¶5; the viewer must notice the echo alone.
- **Biblically accurate:** ¶13 "Remember and turn unto the LORD." drops KJV "shall" and capitalises — a prophecy becomes a command in David's balloon. That is a mood change, and it sits oddly beside ¶14 "No one is pushing you." ¶7 "If thou be the Son of God, come down from the cross" is the best mocker line of the three — it echoes ¶1's "Son of God" and gives ¶8 its force.
- **Best version?** No. ¶14 is the weakest landing: three stock sentences announcing softness ("No one is pushing you. The invitation is open.") then a bare imperative.
- **Score 6.5/10. Curiosity gap 5/10.**

## RANKING
1. **Y** — the only version that stages the echo (David, then cross, then "Hear it?") and quotes its own ending.
2. **Z** — clearest sequence and fullest quotes, but no "deliver" gloss and a mood-shifted Ps 22:27.
3. **X** — tight and well glossed, but the David/cross intercut (¶2-¶3) confuses a newcomer and the ending is paraphrased.

## WHAT TO TAKE FROM THE OTHERS
- Into Y from Z¶7: the full taunt "If thou be the Son of God, come down from the cross" — it ties to "Son of God" and makes "He could have come down" hit.
- Into Y from Z¶9: put "No man taketh it from me" in a balloon, keep Y¶8's plain gloss right after it.
- Into Y from X¶5: "if God wants Him, let God rescue Him" — best one-line gloss of 27:43.
- Fix Y¶1 to "spoke almost the same words" (X¶1 has it right).
- Trim Y to pay for the above: "hangs there, dying" (¶4) and "And hanging there" (¶8) are carried by the picture.
- All three: the card asks WHO WROTE THE MOCKERY? and sentence one answers "David". The card's gap is dead in three seconds. Open on the mocker balloon (27:43) first, then "A king wrote those words. A thousand years earlier." — reveal, don't announce.
- All three: "Then" / "more" before the 27:40 line claims an order Matthew does not have (v40 comes first). "They also shouted" costs nothing.

## ANY ACCURACY PROBLEM
- **Z¶13:** "Remember and turn unto the LORD." — KJV Ps 22:27 reads "All the ends of the world **shall** remember and turn unto the LORD". Dropping "shall" and capitalising turns a promise into an order. Restore "shall" (as Y¶11 does).
- **Y¶1:** "The crowd at the cross spoke them" — they spoke almost the same words (Matt 27:43 vs Ps 22:8 differ in wording). Y¶6 says it right.
- **X¶5-6 / Y¶4+¶7 / Z¶6-7:** 27:40 (passers-by, v39-40) presented after 27:43 (chief priests, v41-43) with "Then"/"more" — order inverted; Y also attributes the passers-by's words to "the leaders" (their own line is 27:42, not cited). Minor; fix with "also".
- No doctrinal error in any version. John 10:18, Luke 23:34 and Ps 22:27 are each used within their meaning; the CTA in all three is grace-anchored, no pressure framing.
