A (Storybook Bible Strip) · B (Action Painterly) · C (Clean-Line European) · G (Mainstream Ink) · F (Manga Ink). 20 stills, $6.00 spent (NBP nano_banana_pro). Click any image to open full-res.
Two headline findings from this round:

1. The cross scene is MUCH better this time — hardening the blood wording to "dried into dark matted stains, fixed and still" (positive end-state only, no negation) fixed most of the dripping-blood problem from the last round. Small drip marks still show up on B/C/G/F (minor, not the flowing streams seen before) — worth one more wording pass, but this is a real, working fix.

2. Noah + Rainbow scene: the ark is drawn as a curved BOAT/SHIP in ALL 5 styles — a direct violation of the locked canon ("NOT a sailing ship, NOT a boat with a curved or pointed hull") even though the correct box-shaped ark reference image was chained every time. Strange and important: the very next scene (Ark Boarding) gets the box-shape RIGHT in all 5 styles. The richer boarding composition (ramp + door prominent) seems to anchor the geometry; the simpler rainbow composition lets the model's default "storybook ark" bias win. This needs a fix (stronger shape wording, or lead with the ark reference image) before any rainbow-scene content ships.

Overall per-style take across all 4 scenes:
A (Storybook) — most consistent all-rounder; cleanest cross this round, warm throughout, no storm/tone risk.
B (Action Painterly) — most dramatic and cinematic, but its own style tendency risks storm-weather skies on the ONE scene that must never look stormy (the cross).
C (Clean-Line) — calmest and most legible across all four; minor recurring blood-drip at the cross, otherwise very solid.
G (Mainstream Ink) — bold and energetic; keep an eye on body definition at passion scenes, it runs closest to the muscle-gate line.
F (Manga Ink) — defaults to monochrome far more than intended when not explicitly told to use color; technique is clean and dignified but doesn't give the "manga" identity as strongly as hoped.
