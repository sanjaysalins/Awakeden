# Curiosity-gap seat — 'The Mockers' Words' (blind, Fable)

GAP SCORE (0-10): title A = 5 / line 1 = 3 / thumbnail = 7
(Title B = 2, Title C = 3 — both state the twist outright.)

SPOILS: yes.
- Title B spoils fully: "Repeated Psalm 22" IS the reveal. Nothing left to find.
- Title C spoils fully, and overclaims ("Predicted" — see WRONG CLAIMS).
- Line 1 spoils: it says the shout "is also in Psalm 22:8", then asks "Whose words were they first?" — a question the same sentence has just answered. It also repeats the opening card ("WHOSE WORDS ARE THESE?") and Title A ("Whose Words...") — the same question three times, with no new angle.
- Title A half-spoils: the question is good, but "Psalm 22" in the title hands over where the answer lives.
- Thumbnail does NOT spoil. It is the only piece that adds a new angle (the time paradox). Its weakness: "THEY ... THEY" means two different groups, which a non-native viewer may read as a typo, and nothing in the words says who or what is at stake — it leans entirely on the picture.

Three things none of A/B/C fix:
1. None of the three titles contains the word "Jesus". For a global stranger that is the one anchor word; "Cross Mockers" is not natural English and "mockers" is a low-frequency word for non-native speakers.
2. The promise is paid off at roughly 25 seconds ("Almost the same words, a thousand years apart"). The remaining ~50 s (come down / He stayed / forgive them / the Roman officer) is never promised anywhere in the packaging, so a viewer who got what the title sold may swipe at 30 s. The packaging should promise the second turn too.
3. A concrete number (1,000 years) is a stronger gap than a reference (Psalm 22). Strangers feel a number; they feel nothing for "Psalm 22".

(plain words, a real question, "Jesus" in it, "insult" instead of "mockers"; the Psalm 22 tag stays at the end for search only.)

WRONG CLAIMS
1. Title C — "Predicted". The film never says this. It says the words "were already in the Bible before they said them." Psalm 22:8 records what David's enemies said to him; calling it a prediction is a stronger claim than the film makes and invites a fight the film does not have. Use the film's own wording.
2. Share line — "someone who thinks the Bible was written after the fact". The Psalm/Matthew match does not prove that; the standard skeptic reply is that Matthew wrote the scene to echo the psalm. The line picks an argument a 76-second film cannot finish. Drop it.
3. Not wrong, but missing from the read-it line: John 10:18 ("No man taketh it from me, but I lay it down of myself") carries the turn "no one takes His life from Him. He gives it Himself"; Matthew 20:28 / Mark 10:45 ("to give his life a ransom for many") carries "He came to give His life for many." Add both, or the read-it line under-cites the film's own landing.
4. Hair: "shouted" — Matthew 27:41-43 has the chief priests "mocking him ... said"; the passers-by "reviled him ... saying" (27:39-40). "Shouted" is a dramatization, not the text. Fine in narration; do not put it inside the quotation frame.
