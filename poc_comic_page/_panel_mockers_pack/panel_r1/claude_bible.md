SPOILS: Partly.
- Title C ("Psalm 22 Predicted the Mockers") gives away the answer. Title A and line 1 ask the question, which is better. Line 1 names both verses, so the answer is half-visible, but a global viewer still has to watch to learn why it matters.

BEST TITLE: A, but shorter: "Whose Words Did the Cross Mockers Say?" (drop "Psalm 22" from the title and put it in line 1 or the pinned comment.)

WRONG CLAIMS:
1. **"Predicted" (title C).** The film never says "predict." It says "already in the Bible before they said them." That is fine for a Christian reader, but it is a stronger claim than the film proves. Many scholars read Psalm 22 as David's own suffering that Matthew echoes. Title C also does not match the film's "Almost the same words" framing.
2. **Thumbnail "THEY SAID IT / BEFORE THEY SAID IT."** This is confusing. "They" is unclear, and it reads as a time-travel claim. It also overclaims: David wrote *his enemies'* words, not the Roman-era mockers'. The speakers were different people, so "they said it before they said it" is literally false. The accurate version is "Someone wrote it first."
3. **Psalm 22:8 vs Matthew 27:43.** Not an exact match. Psalm 22:8 reads "He trusted on the LORD that he would deliver him: let him deliver him, seeing he delighted in him." Matthew 27:43 reads "He trusted in God; let him deliver him now, if he will have him." The film says "almost the same," which is honest. Line 1 says "is also in Psalm 22:8," which is slightly too strong. Use "is almost word for word in Psalm 22:8."
4. **Read-it range.** "Psalm 22:7-8" is correct for the mocking, but the film quotes only v.8. The film also does not cover the "Eli, Eli" cry (Matthew 27:46) or the other Psalm 22 parallels. That is fine, but do not imply the whole psalm is covered. The film quotes only the Psalm 22:8 text, and its own on-screen citation is "Psalm 22:8". Make the range match: "Psalm 22:8".
5. **Film claim, not packaging, but it affects trust.** "Jesus had said that no one takes His life from Him" is John 10:18 and is not cited. The read-it line omits it. Add John 10:18, or the line looks like it is pulled from nowhere.
6. **"A thousand years."** David lived about 1000 BC, so about 1,000 years before the cross. This is fine.
7. **Share line.** "Someone who thinks the Bible was written after the fact" assumes the viewer will draw a skeptic's conclusion. It is a debate hook. Some viewers will push back (the Gospels could have been shaped around the Psalm). Keep it, but expect that argument in the comments.
8. **Title "Cross Mockers Repeated Psalm 22" (B).** "Repeated" has the order backwards. The mockers echoed it, and David wrote it first. Reject B.

WRITE IT BETTER:
- Thumbnail text:
  **WHOSE WORDS**
  **WERE THESE?**
  (Or, for a stronger hook: **WRITTEN 1,000 YEARS / BEFORE THE CROSS**.)
- Line 1: "The mockers at Jesus' cross shouted words that are almost the same as Psalm 22:8, written about 1,000 years earlier. Who said them first?"
