# Curiosity-gap judge — 'The Mockers' Words' (blind seat)

GAP SCORE: title 6 / line 1 4 / thumbnail 8

SPOILS: yes.
- Line 1 spoils the title. The title asks "who wrote it?" Line 1 answers it in the first sentence: "was already written in Psalm 22:8." The gap is closed before the tap.
- The title half-spoils itself. "Psalm 22" is in the title, so a Bible reader has the answer already. A stranger does not know Psalm 22, so the gap holds for them. Keep Psalm 22 for search; just know that is the price.
- Thumbnail does not spoil. It gives the "how old" without naming the book. Good.
- Bigger gap problem: nothing in the title or thumbnail sells the second half of the film (come down / He stayed / a Roman gets the last word). Only the last five words of line 1 do, and that is the least-read spot.

Repeat or add?
- Title (who wrote it) + thumbnail (1,000 years old) ADD to each other. Good pair.
- Line 1 REPEATS both (Psalm 22:8 + "already written"), then adds the real second hook at the end. Flip it: give line 1 the second gap only.
- "| Bible" in the title and "Bible:" at the start of line 1 are dead words in hook positions. #Bible already covers search.

Would a stranger tap?
- Thumbnail: yes, "an insult 1,000 years old" sounds impossible, and numbers read in any language.
- Title: a Christian taps; a stranger maybe. "Who wrote the words" is a quiz question, not a stake. "Insult" would tell a stranger it is hostile, which is the stake.
- Line 1: adds nothing for the tap as written.
- On the Shorts feed the real hook is the 2.6s card "WHOSE WORDS / ARE THESE?" — that lines up with the title, which is right.

BEST TITLE: keep the shape, two small changes:
Who Wrote the Insult They Shouted at Jesus? | Psalm 22
(The question is 43 characters, so it survives mobile truncation; Psalm 22 at the tail is for search. If you must keep "Words" to match the on-screen card, fine, but drop "| Bible".)

WRITE IT BETTER:
Thumbnail (yours is the strongest piece, keep it):
THEIR INSULT WAS
1,000 YEARS OLD
(Variant if the image shows the shouting faces or the cross: THIS INSULT WAS / 1,000 YEARS OLD.)

Line 1:
They shouted at Jesus: "Come down from the cross." He did not. Watch what He did instead, and who spoke the last word.
(Line 1 now has its own gap. Psalm 22 stays in the title and the Read-it line. "He did not" is not a spoiler: the payoff is WHY He stayed and the Roman's confession, not the fact.)

WRONG CLAIMS: none false. Two precision notes:
1. The film itself says "almost the same words." The title ("Who Wrote the Words") and line 1 ("was already written in Psalm 22:8") drop the "almost." Matthew 27:43 and Psalm 22:8 match in thought and most words, not word for word. Fine for a hook; never let the description or pinned comment say "the exact words."
2. "1,000 years" is a round number (David ~1000 BC to ~AD 30 is nearer 1,030). Acceptable as a headline figure.
