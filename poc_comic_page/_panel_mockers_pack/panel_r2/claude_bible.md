**GAP SCORE:** title 6 / line 1 5 / thumbnail 6

**SPOILS:** Partly. Line 1 gives away the answer ("already written in Psalm 22:8"), so the question the opening card asks is answered before the viewer watches. "Watch what He did not do" is a good hook, but it comes after the spoiler. The title does not spoil. The thumbnail "1,000 YEARS OLD" gives away the time gap but not the Psalm.

**BEST TITLE:** Keep it, with a small change: "Who Wrote the Words They Shouted at Jesus?" is a clear question. "Psalm 22 | Bible" is filler and could go. The title also does not match the film well. The film shows that the words were already in the Bible, which is more than just "who wrote" them.

**WRITE IT BETTER:**
Thumbnail:
THEY SHOUTED IT AT JESUS
IT WAS WRITTEN 1,000 YEARS BEFORE

Line 1: "Soldiers and leaders mocked Jesus on the cross. Their words are in a song from 1,000 years earlier. Read them both."

**WRONG CLAIMS:**
1. **"Their words were already in the Bible before they said them."** This is accurate in the narrow sense. But "already written" in the line and the thumbnail could be read as "predicted." Psalm 22 is David describing his own enemies. The Gospel writer links it to Jesus. The film does not explain that link, and it does not say what a Christian reader would call it (fulfilment). A skeptic will say the Gospel writer shaped the scene to fit the Psalm. It is safer to say "echoes" or "same words," as the narration does ("Almost the same words").
2. **"THEIR INSULT WAS 1,000 YEARS OLD."** The insult was not 1,000 years old. David's enemies said something similar. Say "THIS INSULT IS IN A 1,000-YEAR-OLD SONG." It is also an approximation: David lived around 1000 BC, so "a thousand years" is fine and rounds well.
3. **Quote accuracy.** Psalm 22:8 in KJV reads "He trusted on the LORD that he would deliver him: let him deliver him, seeing he delighted in him." The film uses only the first half. That is fine, but the second half ("let him deliver him") is the part that actually matches Matthew 27:43. The film's quote, cut off at "deliver him," drops the matching phrase. Make sure the on-screen text shows "let him deliver him" from Psalm 22:8, since that is the real match.
4. **Matthew 27:43 wording.** KJV: "He trusted in God; let him deliver him now, if he will have him: for he said, I am the Son of God." The film uses a trimmed version. That is fine, as long as the trim is not shown as a full quote.
5. **Read-it range.** It lists John 10:18 but the narration only paraphrases it ("no one takes His life from Him"). Matthew 27:40 and Luke 23:34 are covered. The range matches the film, but it should add "Psalm 22:7-8" since verse 7 ("all they that see me laugh me to scorn... shoot out the lip, they shake the head") matches the "people passing by" beat in Matthew 27:39, which the film does not use.
6. **"The religious leaders shout at Him"** then "People passing by also shout" is accurate to Matthew 27:39-43. But the film's second quote (27:40, "If thou be the Son of God, come down") is from the passers-by, and the film introduces it as a separate group, which is correct.
7. **"A Roman officer, and the men with him, felt the earthquake."** Matches Matthew 27:54. Fine. This is the second claim in the film that is not in the packaging at all, so the title does not reflect the ending.
8. **Pinned comment** "What do you notice?" is a weak prompt. Ask "Which line in Psalm 22 surprised you?" instead.
