ADVERSARIAL PASS (2026-07-28, hostile re-review of v3)

Verdict: the elevation is real, but 6 things objectively hold it back. Ranked:
  1. Dimmed panels went MUDDY. The new darker grade × the old 0.35 dim factor = non-live panels read as olive murk through most multi-panel stretches (see frame below, 20.0s). Fix: raise the dim floor to ~0.5 now that the grade carries the contrast. Biggest single fix.
  2. The cover taxes the hook. First spoken word now lands at 2.0s, first 0.5s near-silent. Gorgeous, but Shorts viewers swipe in ~1s. Fix: tighten cover to ~1.2s and/or start the narration under it.
  3. Border breaks are timid. The Jesus overflow reads as a whisper, the scroll one is nearly invisible (its art fills the cell anyway). A real break = a limb/object crossing INTO the neighbouring panel with a cast shadow. Half-delivered promise.
  4. The virtual camera beheads a portrait. During the scroll focus (~20s) Jesus' face slides out of the top of frame, leaving a torso panel. Camera weighting should clamp so portrait panels keep their faces in frame.
  5. THUD! sits ON its own subject (covers the scroll's lower curl). A letterer would put it beside the nail, not over the art it's shouting about.
  6. Pages 4–5 are still hue-monotone at the ART level. The regrade fixed values (blacks), not hues — there is no storm-blue still in the back half to grade INTO. Upstream fix for Piece 2: at least one blue-dominant panel per page-pair.
What survived the hostile pass untouched: the pencil→ink-in grammar (unique, on-brand, kills dead paper); the cover as brand object; the hero-splash landing + its ration-then-spend logic; nail/creak/boom all measurably in the mix (nail peaks −1.2dB); full-page density in every frame; the aged paper + grain.
The deeper truth: v3 made the PAGE alive, not yet the PANELS. Interior motion is still "light breathes, dust drifts" everywhere — we've legislated all acting out of the panels to stop AI invention. The two accepted exceptions (p5a embrace, the hero's opening arms) point at the real next step: deliberately DESIGN 2–3 acting panels per piece (motion completes, then holds; prompted on purpose; fail-closed QC) instead of treating real motion as an accident to forgive. And the layout system needs one diagonal/inset page per piece — five rectangle-grid pages in a row is its own monotony. Both belong in the Piece 2 map, where the subject (crowd + cross) can actually use them.

Part 2 — the original findings (2026-07-28 morning, vs the locked cut)

What's already working: the art is genuinely strong. The Gold Seam reads on every Jesus panel. Faces hold across panels. Period detail is clean. Red-letter verse is there. The word-timed slam engine works.
The gap: right now it reads as beautiful stills presented one at a time on paper — a polished slideshow. A real comic page is a crowded, loud, physical OBJECT. Seven ingredients below, biggest first. Almost all are $0 composite work — no new AI renders.

The 7 missing ingredients

1Dead paper — the page is half empty most of the time

After every page turn the screen is mostly blank green paper. This frame (21.4s) is ~75% empty. The hook frame is ~45% empty. The pivot page ~45%. A comic page is DENSE — panels fill the page, edge to edge. Empty paper is the #1 thing killing the "comic book" feel on a phone screen.

Fix ($0): never show bare paper. Two options: (a) the whole page is already there in faint pencil/ink state, and each panel inks itself in when its slam hits — the comic literally draws itself as you read; (b) layouts where the panel set always tiles the full 9:16 (like the best frame below).

↑ The target density — this frame already proves it

10s in: two stacked panels, zero waste, over-the-shoulder shot + wide shot playing against each other. This is the only moment of the film that looks like a real comic page. Every second should feel like this one.

2Nothing ever breaks a panel border

Every panel is a neat rectangle. In real comics the big moment BURSTS the panel: a hand, a shoulder, a scroll crossing the gutter into the page. That border-break is the classic "comic came alive" move — and it never happens once in 62 seconds.

Fix ($0): we already cut subjects out with rembg (parallax-panel skill). Paste the cutout 20–40px past its panel edge on 2–3 key beats — Jesus' shoulder over the gutter on the pivot; the scroll tip over the frame on the nail beat. Two or three per piece, no more.

3The story's action beats have no comic-book voice

This piece has real physical beats: the scroll nailed to the beam (Col 2:14), the barred door, the door swinging open, the embrace. In a comic those beats get drawn SFX lettering — a hand-inked THUD on the nail, a CREAK on the door — plus a burst or speed-lines on the impact frame. Here they pass silently in the same calm caption box as everything else.

Fix ($0): impact-burst + typography skills already exist. One drawn SFX word on the nail beat, one on the door opening, synced to a real sound_library hit (hammer, wood creak). Sparing — 2 per piece max, never on sacred frames.

4No cover — the film never announces "this is a comic book"

It opens straight into a quiet panel + caption. A comic experience starts with a COVER: drawn masthead lettering, issue box, one striking image. We already built the AWAK+EDEN Issue No.1 cover mock for the audience test — it's not in the piece.

Fix ($0–0.30): a 1.5–2s cold-open cover: title lettering slams on, corner box stamps in, then the cover ink-wipes into page 1. Also fixes the slow hook — the first 8 seconds currently sit on one static wide shot.

5The colour collapses into one olive-gold soup

Page 1's blue night vs lamp-gold is lovely. But from mid-film on, everything — panels, paper, captions — is the same tan/olive/gold value. No true blacks, no storm blue-black, and the paper is nearly the same tone as the art, so panels melt into the page. The DNA promises gold vs storm blue-black drama; the back half delivers gold vs slightly-different-gold.

Fix ($0): re-grade: deepen blacks inside panels, keep the storm-blue in shadows, and darken/age the paper (warm newsprint, vignette) so panels POP off it. The red accent stays reserved for red-letter — but make that one red moment count bigger.

6The Bowed Camera saves the hero shot — then never spends it

The grammar rations the low-angle radiant hero shot for GLORY beats so it hits hard when it comes. But the landing — the welcome, THE glory beat of this story — is a level mid-shot of Jesus in the doorway that we've already seen twice (16s, 25s, then 49–61s). The rationed shot never fires, and the last 13 seconds repeat earlier compositions.

Fix (~$0.30, the one render worth buying): ONE new landing still: low-angle, seam at full radiance, door wide open, light flooding past Him. The whole grammar was designed to earn exactly this frame — cash it in.

7Held panels sit computer-still; the page isn't a physical object

Between slams, panels hold with only a gentle drift — clean, but digitally frozen, and the page itself never moves like paper. Three $0 tools we already own are not in this cut: line-boil (hand-inked wobble on held panels), parallax-panel (2.5D depth on 1–2 calm panels), and a subtle whole-page perspective tilt during holds so the page feels like a thing in your hands. Plus print-grade is so gentle it disappears — the halftone should be visible in shadows at phone size.

Fix ($0): line-boil on all held panels; parallax on 2 calm ones; slight page tilt during dwells; one notch stronger halftone + a paper-grain pass. Spread thin per the locked panel-animator rule — these are seasoning, not sauce.

Honest framing: this topic can't carry "action" alone

"In No Wise Cast Out" is one man at a door at night — a QUIET story. Even perfectly executed, it will read as a moody comic, not an action comic. That's fine — the grammar above makes any page feel alive. But the real action test is Piece 2, The Mockers (crowd + cross + Witness Edge): that subject can use diagonals, crowd energy, and the passion register at full power. Recommendation: bake ingredients 1–5 + 7 into the Piece 2 panel map before rendering, and optionally retrofit this piece afterwards ($0 except one landing still).
