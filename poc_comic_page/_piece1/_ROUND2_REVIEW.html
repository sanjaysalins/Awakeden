The hard scene — The Mockers at Golgotha

Eye-audited against every requirement: one cross, arms correctly traced to the beam · exactly 3 mockers with clear faces, mocking gesture not violence · background crowd as massed dark silhouettes only · the foreground witness is a pure silhouette — no face, and critically no gold seam touches them — the light stays His alone · Christ reads gaunt, small at distance, faint seam on his edge · sky is calm deep dusk, no storm. This is the strongest single proof yet that the full grammar (Gold Seam + Bowed Camera + Witness Edge together) survives a real, complex, doctrinally-loaded scene.
My honest palette read: gold still wins, and here's why — the whole piece's visual logic is "cold world outside, warm door in" (the Seeker's fear vs. Christ's welcome). Gold keeps that contrast sharp: warm light is HIS and only His. Moonlight is genuinely the most beautiful of the three on its own, but it cools the WHOLE world, which quietly erases that contrast — everything becomes "atmospheric night" rather than "cold fear vs. warm grace." Ember goes the other way and warms everything, so the doorway stops being special too. Worth keeping Moonlight in mind as a possible palette for a different, non-doorway piece — but for this one, gold is doing real theological work the other two don't.
