## 3. GLOBAL AUDIENCE

Who: Jesus dying on a cross, religious leaders mocking him, a king named David with an older song, a Roman soldier at the end.

What: one insult matches the old song. A second insult, "come down," is new. Jesus stays. He prays for the mockers. The soldier calls him God's Son.

Why: line 7 is clear — he gives his own life, and he stayed. In an honor-shame culture the crowd wins unless staying is heard as a choice. The film does say that. Why the old song matters stays insider. There is no plain "so what."

Lost at the card "WHO WROTE THE INSULTS?" I answered "the leaders" at line 2. Line 3, "He writes their words," sounds like David composed the cruel lines.

Words that fail in translation and captions:

- Line 2, "let him deliver him." Two "him"s. "Deliver" means give birth, or drop off a parcel. Plain: "Let God rescue him now."
- "DELIVER = RESCUE" is the right fix, easy to miss, and English-only.
- Line 4, "trusted on the LORD." "Trusted on" is not living English. Plain: "He trusted the LORD to rescue him."
- Line 6, "If thou be the Son of God." "Thou be" breaks captions. Plain: "If you are God's Son."
- Line 8, "they know not." Old word order, and this quote never gets a plain replay.
- Line 5, "also shout," can mean the same insult. It is a different sentence.
- On-screen "MATT" can look like a man's name. "PSALM" is an insider code.
- Line 1, "the leaders," means politicians in many countries. Here it is the religious leaders.
- "Son of God" (line 1) is insider theology with no gloss.

Universal: public shame, refusing to escape, forgiving the mockers, one witness who changes his mind. Insider: Psalm numbers, David as setup, the cross as a known church symbol.

## 4. BIBLICALLY ACCURATE

- Line 1, dying on a cross while leaders shout: RIGHT for the religious leaders (Matt 27:41-43).
- Line 2: RIGHT as a shortened quote. It cuts "if he will have him: for he said, I am the Son of God."
- Line 3, "let God rescue him": RIGHT gloss. "A thousand years": RIGHT as a round span.
- Line 3, "People laugh at David too. He writes their words into a song": OVERCLAIMED. Psalm 22 contains the mockery. The supplied text does not show people laughing at David while he copies them down.
- Line 4: RIGHT as a shortened Psalm 22:8. It stops before "let him deliver him," so the ear-match with line 2 is thinner than the real verses.
- Line 5, "Almost the same words": RIGHT for this one insult.
- Line 6: RIGHT speaker and RIGHT shortened quote (Matt 27:39-40).
- Line 7, "He could have come down": OVERCLAIMED. John 10:18 says he lays his life down himself. It does not say he could climb down.
- Line 7, "no one takes His life from Him. He gives His life Himself": RIGHT paraphrase, rightly placed before the cross ("had said").
- Line 8: RIGHT words for Luke 23:34. The narrator's voice can hide that Jesus is the one praying. The "JESUS" balloon repairs it for readers.
- Line 9: RIGHT.
- Line 10, the soldier's words: RIGHT (Matt 27:54). "A Roman soldier" is a fair thin stand-in for the centurion. "Watching Him die" as the reason he speaks: OVERCLAIMED. Matthew names the earthquake and the things that were done.
- The card, insults plural, "who wrote": OVERCLAIMED. One shout is the old line. David did not write the shouts at the cross.

## 5. BEST VERSION POSSIBLE

The best explainer would put plain English in the balloons, keep KJV only as a small tag, and make the card true: "THIS INSULT IS 1,000 YEARS OLD." One "so what": those words were already in David's song, and Jesus still stayed and forgave. Quote the same half of both verses so the ear hears the match. If the tag stays Matt 27:54, name the earthquake in line 10. Keep "Son of God," and add six plain words of gloss.

The one highest-value change: rewrite the card and the balloons so a caption, sound off, carries the true plain claim — one insult was already in David's song.
