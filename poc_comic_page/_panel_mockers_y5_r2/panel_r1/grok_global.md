Priya, 21, in Bangalore. English is her school language. She knows the name Jesus, not King David, and not the word "psalm." She watches on a phone, often on mute, with auto-captions.

She stops if the first quote stays in old English. She finishes if the pictures carry the time jump, because 73 seconds is short. She shares only if she can retell the match in her own words. A church friend who already knows Psalm 22 is more likely to share than she is.

Not served: anyone watching muted (the balloons are still old English); viewers who hear "Son of God" as a biological child; Muslim viewers, because line 9 states that he died, which their tradition denies; anyone who does not already know that a cross is an execution.

## 2. MAIN TAKEAWAY (one sentence a viewer could say back; which line carries it; did the film make it clear?)

"People mocked Jesus with words King David had already written down, and Jesus stayed on the cross anyway."

Line 5 carries the word-match. Line 9 carries a second idea: he stayed so he could die for them. The film does not make one takeaway clear. A viewer can leave with either sentence, or with neither, if line 2 never became plain.

## 3. GLOBAL AUDIENCE (who / what / why in my own words; where I got lost or misled, quote the line; words that fail in translation)

Who: Jesus, religious leaders, an old king, people walking past, one Roman soldier. What: they mock a man on a cross, an old song has nearly the same mockery, he forgives them and dies, a soldier calls him God's Son. Why: he chose to stay and give his life.

I got lost at line 2: "let him deliver him now." Two "him"s. Who rescues whom? "Deliver" also means hand over a package, or give birth. The card "DELIVER = RESCUE" is the right help, but it comes late, and the balloon still prints the hard word.

Line 4 fails the same way: "He trusted on the LORD that he would deliver him." "Trusted on" is not modern English. "The LORD" in capitals is a Bible code for God's name. Auto-captions will not explain it.

Line 6 fails on screen and in speech: "If thou be the Son of God." "Thou" and "be" break machine translation. Line 7 then says it in plain words, so the spoken path recovers. The balloon does not.

Line 7 misleads: "no one takes His life from Him." That can sound like "nobody is killing him," which fights line 1 ("is dying") and line 9 ("He died there"). "He gives it Himself" — "it" has no clear noun.

Line 8 is never put in plain words: "for they know not what they do." "For" here means "because." "Know not" is reversed grammar. Captions will copy it as-is.

"Psalm," "MATT," and "elders" are insider words. "Elders" often translates as "old men," not as officials. "Priests and elders" can also land, in some countries, as "the Jews did this," even though those exact words are not said.

The dark sky is never explained. A viewer can invent a reason: God is punishing the crowd. The soldier's line then feels like a twist with no cause.

What is universal: mockery, refusing to save yourself, forgiving the people who insult you. What is insider: David, psalm numbers, "Son of God" as a title, and the idea that an old song can pre-write a later death.

## 4. BIBLICALLY ACCURATE (claim by claim: RIGHT / OVERCLAIMED / UNSUPPORTED / WRONG, quote the line; say only what you can support)

Line 1, "who says He is God's Son" — RIGHT as a report. Matthew 27:43 has the mockers say he said it.

Line 1, "The priests and elders shout at Him" plus line 2 — RIGHT. That sentence is the leaders' mockery in Matthew 27:43, shortened.

Line 3, "a thousand years" — RIGHT as a round figure, not a counted date.

Line 3, "Enemies laughed at him too" and "he wrote down what they said" — RIGHT only if the singer of Psalm 22 is David, which the KJV heading says. The laugh is in Psalm 22:7. It is a song-scene, not a separate history report.

Line 4 — RIGHT as a shortened Psalm 22:8.

Line 5, "Almost the same words" — RIGHT. They are close, not identical.

Line 6 — RIGHT as a shortened Matthew 27:40.

Line 7, "no one takes His life from Him. He gives it Himself" — RIGHT as a short form of John 10:18.

Line 7, "So He stayed" — OVERCLAIMED. John 10:18 says he lays his life down himself. It does not say "therefore he will not come down from the cross."

Line 8 — RIGHT. It matches the supplied Luke 23:34, and line 7 frames it as his prayer.

Line 9, "He did not come down. He died there" — RIGHT.

Line 9, "He stayed to give His life for them" — OVERCLAIMED. Matthew 20:28 says a ransom "for many," not "for these shouters," and it does not say he stayed on the cross for that reason.

Line 10, the soldier's sentence — RIGHT as words (Matthew 27:54). The rank is flattened: he is the centurion, and others spoke too. The film omits why they said it. The verse names the earthquake and what had just happened. A viewer can think he said it only because Jesus would not come down. That cause is not in the verse.

## 5. BEST VERSION POSSIBLE (what would the best explainer in the world do differently; the ONE highest-value change; score 0-10 against that bar)

The best explainer would put one idea on screen: these two insults, in today's English, side by side, a thousand years apart. Same words highlighted. Then one plain sentence: he heard "come down," and he stayed.

The film already glosses two hard lines out loud, then adds a second sermon (chose to die, forgive them, died for them, the soldier). Muted viewers never get the gloss. They get the balloons.

ONE change: make every balloon the plain sentence, and keep the old wording only as a small tag under it. Speak those same plain sentences.

Score: 5. The match is real and the pictures follow it. The words a new viewer must read are still the words most likely to fail.

## CURIOSITY GAP (opens at line __, pays off at line __; score 0-10; worth the wait?)

Opens on the card before line 1, "WHOSE WORDS ARE THESE?" The words arrive in line 2. Pays off at line 5.

Score: 6. The wait is short, so yes, it is worth it. The question is aimed a bit wrong. By line 2 a viewer can already answer "the priests." The real surprise is not who speaks. It is that the insult is old. A sharper question would have been: "Why do these words sound old?"

## THREE SMALLEST FIXES (line number + exact replacement wording)

Line 2: "He trusted God. Let God rescue him now."

Line 6: "If you are God's Son, come down from the cross."

Line 8: "Father, forgive them. They do not know what they are doing."

Say these in the voice and print them in the balloons. Line 7 still needs a later pass: "No one forced him to die. He chose to give his life. So he stayed on the cross."
