## 1. WHO IS THIS FOR

Priya, 26, on a bus in Bengaluru. Kannada first, English second. Captions on, sound low. She knows the cross from films. She does not know "deliver," "thou," or "Psalm."

She stops at line 2 if the caption locks onto "let him deliver him." In her English, deliver is a parcel or a speech. She finishes if she reaches line 3 ("let God rescue him") and line 9. She can share line 9. She cannot share the David echo.

Not served: anyone with no sound. The plain glosses are spoken. The balloons print the hard old English. Also not served: a viewer who needs "God's Son" explained. Line 1 states the claim and moves on.

## 2. MAIN TAKEAWAY

The sentence she can repeat: "They told him to come down. He stayed and died for many."

Line 9 carries it, and it is clear.

The title promises a different point: these insults were already written. That point sits in line 5, and line 5 is muddy. Two takeaways compete. The simple one is the one she keeps.

## 3. GLOBAL AUDIENCE

Who: Jesus is dying on a cross. Leaders mock him. An old king, David, wrote a song long before. A Roman officer speaks at the end.

What: The leaders' line matches the song. Passers-by tell him to come down. He stays, asks forgiveness, and dies. The officer says this was God's Son.

Why she can say: he came to give his life for many (line 9).

Lost at line 5: "Their words were already in the Bible before they said them." Whose words — the leaders, David, or God? "The Bible" sounds like a finished book already on a shelf. Lost again at line 7: "He gives it Himself." Gives what? "Himself" often translates as "to himself."

Words that fail in translation and captions:

- Line 2: "let him deliver him." Two "him"s. Who rescues whom? The card "DELIVER = RESCUE" arrives at ~12 s, after the hard word, and captions still print "deliver."
- Line 4: "trusted on the LORD." "On" looks like broken English. Caps on LORD are an insider code.
- Line 3: "go back a thousand years." Some engines turn this into the viewer traveling in time.
- Line 6: "If thou be the Son of God." "Thou" sticks, or becomes "though."
- Line 8: "for they know not." Here "for" means because. Translators read "for" as "for the sake of." "Know not" is flipped word order. The narrator speaks Jesus's prayer, so a caption may not show that Jesus is the speaker. The balloon "JESUS LUKE 23:34" is the only clue.
- Tag "A MOCKER MATT 27:43" reads as a name: Mocker Matt.

Clear: the opening card "WHOSE WORDS ARE THESE?". The card "DELIVER = RESCUE". Line 9. "A Roman officer" in line 10. The cross, the sneer, the pointing hands, the dark sky.

Insider: Psalm numbers, "religious leaders," and head-shaking as scorn. In much of South Asia a head-shake is not mockery. The sneer and the pointing finger do that job. Universal picture: a dying man, a jeering crowd, a choice to stay, a prayer for that crowd, a soldier who changes his mind.

## 4. BIBLICALLY ACCURATE

- Line 1, dying on a cross: RIGHT. "Who says He is God's Son": RIGHT as the claim in Matt 27:43 ("for he said, I am the Son of God").
- "Shout" in lines 1 and 6: OVERCLAIMED. The verses say they mocked, reviled, and were "saying." Speakers are right: leaders for Matt 27:43, passers-by for Matt 27:40.
- Line 2 quote: RIGHT, shortened. It leaves out "if he will have him."
- Line 3, "let God rescue him": RIGHT for that clause.
- "King David" and "a thousand years" (lines 3–5): UNSUPPORTED by the supplied verses. Psalm 22:7–8 names no writer and no date. Usual tradition, absent from the pack.
- Line 4 quote: RIGHT for the first half of Psalm 22:8. The cut half is "let him deliver him," the clause that most matches Matthew.
- Line 5, "Almost the same words": RIGHT. "Already in the Bible before they said them": OVERCLAIMED. The lines are close. They are not one sentence, and "the Bible" is a later name.
- Line 7: RIGHT as a paraphrase of John 10:18. "Gives" is softer than "I lay it down of myself." No reference appears on screen.
- Line 8: RIGHT. Matches Luke 23:34.
- Line 9, "give His life for many": RIGHT beside Matt 20:28. That verse also says "a ransom," and the film leaves that out.
- Line 10, "Roman officer," "the men with him," and the quote: RIGHT. "Felt the earthquake": OVERCLAIMED. Matt 27:54 says they saw the earthquake and "those things that were done," and that they feared greatly.

## 5. BEST VERSION POSSIBLE

The best explainer would put David's line and the leaders' line on screen together, in modern words, with the matching words marked, and would answer the title in one spoken sentence. Old wording would stay only as a small source line. The voice and the balloon would use the plain sentence, so the caption translates the plain sentence.

ONE change: at line 5, say and show, "David wrote their insult a thousand years earlier."

Score: 6/10. The story spine is strong. The words a translator receives are the weak part.

## CURIOSITY GAP

Opens on the card before line 1 ("WHOSE WORDS ARE THESE?"). Pays off at line 5. Score: 6/10. The wait is short, about twenty seconds. The payoff still does not name David, so "whose" stays open.

## THREE SMALLEST FIXES

1. Line 5 — replace "Their words were already in the Bible before they said them." with "David wrote their insult a thousand years before they said it."
2. Line 6 — replace "If thou be the Son of God, come down from the cross" with "If you are God's Son, come down from the cross."
3. Line 8 — replace "Father, forgive them; for they know not what they do." with "Father, forgive them, because they do not know what they are doing."
