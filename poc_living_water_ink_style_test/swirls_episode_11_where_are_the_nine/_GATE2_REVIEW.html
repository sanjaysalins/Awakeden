"Where Are the Nine" — all 12 stills fixed for consistency, and the back cover completely reimagined. Click any image to zoom.

What you flagged, and what changed: the Samaritan wore a different outfit almost every time he appeared (a bold diagonal sash, sometimes a headband, sometimes a vertical stripe), and "the ten" didn't read as the same group page to page. Root cause: his reference photo was cropped from an already-inconsistent early render, then dropped from some pages to fix a different bug — nothing ever locked. Fix: built one clean, plain reference photo of him from scratch (bare head, a single thin red trim on his mantle's edge, nothing else) and re-rendered all 10 interior pages and the front cover against it. The group-of-ten reference didn't chain reliably at all (even identical ref, different result) — dropped it, relying on the already-precise costume description instead.

Back cover: completely reimagined, not just re-rendered — the close-up feet + pressed-face-in-dust concept is gone. New scene: both figures held at a clear middle distance (matching the front cover's own composition), Jesus standing still with empty hands, the Samaritan already down in a held, dignified stillness.

Total cost across every round so far: $2.96 — still inside the original estimate.

One real trade-off worth knowing about

  • On the two pages where every man's mouth must be covered (F02, F03), chaining the Samaritan's face photo kept overriding the cloth and leaving his mouth bare — so on those two pages specifically, he's identified only by his red mantle-trim and general build, not an exact face match (his face is mostly hidden by the cloth anyway, so this isn't visible to a viewer). Everywhere his face IS visible (F01, F05–F10, front cover isn't a close read either), it's chained to the same clean reference.
  • Exact headcount on the densest group pages (F01, F02, F04) is still genuinely hard to verify by eye — worth your own count too.
