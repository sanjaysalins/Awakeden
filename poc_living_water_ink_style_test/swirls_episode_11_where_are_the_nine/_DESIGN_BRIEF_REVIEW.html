Episode 11 — the picture plan

Fable's full visual design for "Where Are the Nine" (Luke 17:11-19). Nothing rendered yet — this is the paper stage, $0 so far.

Two hard problems this episode poses, and Fable's fix:

1. Jesus isn't on the page when the healing happens. The text says they were cleansed "as they went" — on the road, after Jesus already sent them off. Fix: a single thin blue thread stands in for his WORD, not his body. It leaves his hand on one page, then appears again above the walking men on the very next page — same road, same men, but the ten men's mark of sickness has gone from a wet dark stain to a pale dried ring. The men never touch the thread and the thread never touches them — the healing is felt, never shown happening.

2. Making sure the pictures don't say "the nine got less." All ten really were healed — equally, fully, at the same moment. Only one came back to actually meet Jesus. So the stain (their sickness) clears for all ten together, on one page, before anyone reacts — nobody is left half-marked. The blue thread (standing for Jesus himself) never touches the one man extra — he doesn't get more ink, he just walks back to where the ink already is.

The shape: 10 pages + 2 covers

New characters & places for this episode

The ten men — drawn as a company, not individuals; costume and condition (bound mouths, torn clothes, bare heads — the real Old Testament sign of a leper) carry the group's look.
The Samaritan (the one who returns) — a man like any other: no foreign costume, no different skin, nothing that marks him as an outsider except a narrow red band on his mantle. His only distinction is his own face, drawn plainly and with full dignity.
The village edge & the road — one fixed place: Jesus and the village stand on the left of every page, the road runs away to the right. Every walk away from him goes right; the one man's return comes back from the right.

Two things worth your call

1. Back-cover Bible verse (sits under the final picture, the one people remember):

Ephesians 2:8 — "For by grace are ye saved through faith... not of works" — recommended. This is the exact doctrine this episode's script fought six rounds of review to protect: it was a free gift, not something earned by coming back.
John 6:37 — "him that cometh to me I will in no wise cast out" — the welcome itself, naming the stranger being received.
Luke 17:19 — the episode's own verse ("thy faith hath made thee whole") — simplest, but repeats a line already spoken in the episode rather than pointing outward.

2. Which page is the hero (the one that opens AND closes the finished video)? Fable recommends F09 — the moment Jesus personally says "thy faith hath made thee whole," matching this project's rule that the hero is always the turn toward Christ, not the most dramatic beat. The alternative is F10, the narrator's closing line ("Luke calls it thanks. Jesus calls it faith") — strong, but a summary rather than the moment itself.
