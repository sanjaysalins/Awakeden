What I found

  • Kling 3 Omni is 4x cheaper (10 credits vs 40, about $0.02 vs $0.08 per still) and its woodcut linework actually looks MORE period-authentic than Nano Banana Pro's — denser hatching, more like a real 16th-century print.
  • But it undressed the men. The prompt asked for "coarse rent tunics torn open at the breast" — still substantially clothed, just torn at the chest. Kling drew them almost bare-chested with just a cloth sling, far more exposed than intended for a reverent Bible channel. Nano Banana Pro kept them properly clothed, ragged but decent.
  • Title placement and framing are off. The title was supposed to sit "near the top of the frame" — Kling dropped it into the middle of the hillside instead, and cropped the men's feet at the very bottom edge instead of leaving them fully inside the frame.
  • Nano Banana Pro without any reference images still holds up well — close to the shipped version's look, so most of the quality here is coming from the model itself, not the refs.

Bottom line

Kling 3 Omni is a genuinely cheaper option and wins on raw woodcut texture, but as tested here it is not a safe drop-in replacement — the under-dressed figures alone would fail this project's own reverence bar. It might be worth a second try with a stronger "fully clothed, chest covered" clause and a tighter framing instruction, but I would not switch the whole series to it without that follow-up test passing clean. Nano Banana Pro stays the safer default for now.
