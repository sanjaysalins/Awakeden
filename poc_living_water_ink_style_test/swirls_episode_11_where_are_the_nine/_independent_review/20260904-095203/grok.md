## Doctrinal soundness

No invented miracle, no two-tier “cleansed vs *sozo*” prize, no contested Greek stunt. The scene is Luke 17:12–19 as written.

The leak is the close, not the story.

**“Nine had exactly what they’d asked for, and kept walking.”** They asked for **mercy**, not a product. The line turns mercy into a fulfilled order. That sets up gratitude-as-payment.

**“Luke calls it thanks. Jesus calls it faith.”** Then **“Turn back.”** If Jesus names the return as **faith** (17:19, gift), the last word cannot be the viewer’s **act**. That sequence preaches: the Samaritan did the thing; now you do the thing. Faith’s fruit gets sold as the saving move.

**“Whatever he’s already done for you”** only addresses the already-blessed. A listener who thinks Christ has done nothing for them is outside the analogy. The lost are not in the CTA.

Not heresy. Works-adjacent. The Spirit’s question in the text (**“but where are the nine?”**) is doing the piercing; the script then tells the listener to manufacture the return.

## KJV verbatim

Quoted spans match 1769 KJV. No altered word:

- **“Jesus, Master, have mercy on us.”** (17:13)
- **“Go shew yourselves unto the priests.”** (17:14) — *shew* kept

- **“Arise, go thy way: thy faith hath made thee whole.”** (19)

Nits, not FAIL:

- **“and with a loud voice… and he was a Samaritan.”** splices 17:15b–16, including Luke’s aside **“and he was a Samaritan”** inside quote marks. Words are KJV; the marks make Luke’s comment sound like speech.
- **“To the one still on his face”** is not in the text. 17:16 has the fall; 17:19 does not say he was still down.

## Clarity on first hearing

A stranger can follow the plot. They cannot follow the moral without already knowing it.

Jesus says **go**. The script praises **“They obeyed before they could see any difference.”** Then it treats **“Nine kept going”** as the failure and **“Turn back”** as the obedience. On first hearing that is a contradiction: the nine are still doing the only command they were given. The missing beat is: they were **cleansed on the road**, so the Giver now stands behind them, and glory belongs **to him** (17:18), not that “keep walking” is disobedience.

**“save this stranger”** is never unpacked. **Samaritan** is quoted and then dropped. Dead weight.

**“Luke calls it thanks. Jesus calls it faith.”** is a critic’s couplet, not a first-hear line. It asks the ear to hold two labels and then obey a third verb.

~197 words against a 135–165 / 60s budget. Long KJV at normal pace will overrun or get rushed. That is not “tight.” It is a recap of the pericope.

## Grace-anchored conviction

The pierce is honest until the last two sentences. No hell, no “you’ll lose the healing.” Then:

**“Whatever he’s already done for you, he’s still asking where you are. Turn back.”**

That is duty after benefit. Gain already received → you move. Constitution: the close may turn Jesus’s question onto the viewer only if the **answer** is something God gives. Here the answer is the listener’s U-turn.

**“Turn back”** does not name Jesus. The Person is a pronoun two lines up (**“to him”**), then gone.

## Freshness / one thread

Hook shock: **go before a single one was healed** / **cleansed on the road**.

Middle and CTA: the one who **came back** vs the nine who **kept walking**.

That is a swap. The distinctive textual surprise is spent as setup for the autocomplete take: *only one said thank you; don’t be like the nine.*

**“and he was a Samaritan”** never becomes the thread either. Three candidates, none carried to the last line.

Jaded-scroller test: from “ten men with leprosy” you can predict this ending. You can. **“Turn back.”** is the title-card sermon.

## LANDING: 5/10

Final sentence: **“Turn back.”**

Portability test: could that end a different piece? Yes. Prodigal. Lot’s wife. Any “return to God.” That is the stock-closer cap.

It is also thinner than this scene’s own images, which the body already paid for and then abandoned:

- **fell down on his face at his feet**
- **where are the nine**
- **thy faith hath made thee whole**

**“he’s still asking where you are”** is the one earned echo (17:17). The last word throws it away for a two-word repentance slogan. No Jesus. No feet. No *whole*. CTA to the motion, not to the Giver.

Under 8 on both scores. That alone is REVISE. The grace leak and the thread-swap are why it is not a near-miss.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Kill **“Turn back.”** Rebuild the last sentence from this scene only — the man **at his feet**, **“where are the nine?”**, or **“thy faith hath made thee whole”** as gift — so it cannot close a prodigal short, and so the CTA is to the Giver, not to the listener’s thank-you.
2. Pick one spine and run it hook → CTA. Either **go before they were healed** (and land on trusting the Speaker you cannot see yet) or **return to the Giver** (and open on the missing nine / the face at his feet). Stop using the first as a teaser for the second.
3. Hold **“Only one of them came back”** out of the open, and add one clause that breaks the obey/don’t-obey contradiction: they were **cleansed as they went**, so the nine’s failure is leaving the Giver, not finishing the walk to the priests.
