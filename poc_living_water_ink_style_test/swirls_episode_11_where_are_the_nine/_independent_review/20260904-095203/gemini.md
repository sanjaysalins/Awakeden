# Independent review — gemini (OK, 46s)

Here is an independent, adversarial review of the narration artifact:

**Doctrinal Soundness**
The piece is biblically faithful to Luke 17:11-19. It accurately captures the sequence of the miracle (cleansed on the way) and the theological weight of the one who returned. No invented doctrine or contrarian readings.

**KJV Verbatim**
Almost perfect, but there is a minor capitalization error in the quotation of Luke 17:15-16. 
- Artifact: `...glorified God, and fell down on his face...` 
- KJV: `...glorified God, And fell down on his face...` (Verse 16 begins at "And").

**Clarity on First Hearing**
Very clear. The contrast between the ten who were healed, the nine who kept walking, and the one who returned is easy to track. The narrative flows logically without confusing conceits.

**Grace-Anchored Conviction**
The conviction is grounded in grace: "Whatever he's already done for you, he's still asking where you are." It relies on the goodness of what Christ has *already* provided rather than using fear or loss to manufacture a response. 

**Freshness = Faithful Depth**
The line, "Luke calls it thanks. Jesus calls it faith," is an exceptional, sharp insight. It takes a familiar Sunday School story about "saying thank you" and elevates it to its actual theological weight. 

**One Thread Spine**
The spine is incredibly tight. The physical directions of "going," "kept going," "turned back," and "kept walking" weave seamlessly from the hook to the final CTA. 

**SCORE THE HOOK: 8**
"Jesus told ten men with leprosy to go — before a single one of them was healed." This is a strong, immediate open. It skips the template fluff and drops the listener right into the paradox and tension of the narrative.

**SCORE THE LANDING: 7**
"Whatever he's already done for you, he's still asking where you are. Turn back." 
The setup is fantastic and deeply tied to the text ("where are the nine?"). However, the final imperative—"Turn back"—while literally what the Samaritan did, functions identically to a stock evangelical closer ("Turn to Jesus," "Come back to God"). It barely misses the mark because it leaves the listener with a cliché rather than a final, piercing image unique to this passage (e.g., settling for the gift vs. returning to the Giver).

-----

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Upgrade the final two words ("Turn back"). It is too close to a stock youth-group closer. Anchor the final imperative more specifically in the visual contrast of this piece—don't just tell them to turn back, contrast it with the tragedy of walking away with the healing but leaving the Healer.
2. Fix the KJV capitalization in the verse 15/16 bridge: change "and fell down" to "And fell down" to be strictly verbatim.
3. Smooth the dialogue setup: "naming exactly what nine men had skipped" feels slightly clunky and modern right before the weight of the KJV quote.
