### Doctrinal soundness

**Mostly sound, but two framing choices push past what the text says.**

1. **`"naming exactly what nine men had skipped"`** — Overclaims. Jesus sent all ten to the priests; the nine were still doing what He commanded. The text’s rebuke is not “they skipped obedience” but **they did not return to give glory to God** (explicit in the very quote you use). “Skipped” turns a lament about absent worship into a missed assignment — and it misrepresents the nine.

2. **`"Nine had exactly what they'd asked for, and kept walking."`** — They cried `"have mercy on us,"` not “heal our skin.” Jesus Himself treats their absence as a real failure (`"where are the nine?"` / `"returned to give glory to God"`). Saying nine got **exactly** what they asked for softens the passage’s own sting and can imply the nine were spiritually fine once cleansed. That is an inference, not Luke’s stated point.

3. **`"Luke calls it thanks. Jesus calls it faith."`** — Catchy, but loose. Luke narrates the **action** (giving thanks); Jesus names **faith** as what made him whole. Those are not two interchangeable labels for the same thing. The epigram risks equating gratitude with saving faith without saying so plainly — a familiar pulpit move, not a fresh reading **of this text**.

No heresy, no clickbait contrarianism. But the nine are framed slightly unfairly, and the thanks/faith line is sermon shorthand, not careful exposition.

### Clarity on first hearing

**Strong in the setup; muddy in the moral geometry.**

**Works well:**
- `"before a single one of them was healed"` — clear, surprising.
- `"They obeyed before they could see any difference"` — a zero-Bible-knowledge listener can follow.
- `"Only one of them came back"` — arithmetic is plain.

**Problems:**
- **`"Nine kept going."`** — After `"Go shew yourselves unto the priests,"` keeping going *is* obedience. A stranger can hear: nine bad, one good — without understanding that the nine were not disobeying Jesus; they failed to **return** and **give glory**. The text names that gap; the narration does not, until buried inside a quote.
- **`"Luke calls it thanks. Jesus calls it faith."`** — Two proper names, two abstract nouns, 60 seconds in. A stranger may hear: thanks and faith are the same word in different Bibles. One plain bridge line is missing (“he came back to say thank you — and Jesus said his faith had saved him”).
- **`"Whatever he's already done for you, he's still asking where you are."`** — Assumes every listener has already received a visible mercy. Not everyone has. “Where you are” also blurs Jesus’s literal question (where are the nine **who were healed and absent?**) into a vague spiritual locator.
