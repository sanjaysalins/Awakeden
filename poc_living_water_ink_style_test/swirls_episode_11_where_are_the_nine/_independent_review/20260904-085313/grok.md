### Doctrinal soundness
The cleansed-vs-whole / σῴζω observation is mainstream, not clickbait. The Greek claim itself is not invented doctrine.

The **application** is the problem. Jesus’s own sentence is `"thy faith hath made thee whole."` The script’s closing mechanism is not faith — it is location/posture:

> “The nine got everything they asked for. Cleansed happened on the road. Whole still happens at his feet.”

That reads as: stay on the road → only cleansing; come back to his feet → wholeness. That is a soft **works / performance / gain-loss** frame. Thanksgiving-return becomes the upgrade path. The text’s emphasis is glory given to God and faith; the narration relocates the hinge to the hearer’s movement.

### Clarity on first hearing
Fatal for a zero-Bible listener at the climax:

> “in Luke’s Greek, the same words he gave the woman who washed his feet”

Who is she? Which scene? Why now? A second unexplained episode is dropped into the CTA paragraph. “Luke’s Greek” is a scholarly aside, not a heard image. The spine is ten lepers → nine missing → whole; the landing briefly swaps in Luke 7 linguistics.

Also slightly foggy earlier:

> “the inspection for a leper already healed”

True enough to Lev 14, but dense. The next beat (“They weren’t. Not yet.”) rescues it — unlike the woman line, which never gets rescued.

### Grace-anchored conviction
Fails the spirit of the lens. Not fear-mongering, but clearly **comparative self-interest**:
- “The nine got everything they asked for” = they settled / missed the better thing
- “Whole still happens at his feet” = you can still get the upgrade

Invite should be: the One who saves is here — come to Him. This version is: don’t be the nine who only got skin-deep.

### Freshness / thread spine
Freshness about the text is real: Jesus counting; Luke delaying “Samaritan”; cleansed vs whole. Good entry point.

Thread mostly holds (count the missing → return → whole), then **wobbles at the worst place** by importing the foot-washing woman as the landing’s load-bearing proof.

### Scores
**HOOK: 8** — “Jesus healed ten men at once — then counted how many didn’t come back. Nine.” Not a template open. Specific. Punch lands. (Mild ding: leprosy isn’t named in the first ~5s, so stakes are a beat late.)

**LANDING: 7** — “Whole still happens at his feet” is earned from this piece’s image/verb, not a stock “come to Jesus.” But it does **not** land cleanly in the bones: the Luke 7 detour + works-tinged “nine got everything / whole at his feet” undercut it. Under 8 → mandatory REVISE.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Re-anchor the close in Jesus’s own verb — faith / being saved by Him — not “return to his feet” as the mechanism that upgrades cleansing into wholeness (kill the gain/loss “nine got everything they asked for” frame).
2. Cut the unexplained “woman who washed his feet” insert from the CTA, or make the σῴζω point in plain English without a second unnamed story.
3. Keep cleansed-vs-whole as faithful depth about THIS text only — one spine, one image, one invitation — so the final sentence can hit without a Greek seminar.
