Jesus healed ten men at once — then counted how many didn't come back. Nine.

Ten lepers stood afar off and lifted up their voices: "Jesus, Master, have mercy on us." His answer: "Go shew yourselves unto the priests" — the inspection for a leper already healed. They weren't. Not yet. "And it came to pass, that, as they went, they were cleansed."

Nine kept going. One turned back, "and with a loud voice glorified God, and fell down on his face at his feet, giving him thanks: and he was a Samaritan." Luke saves that for last: a leper, and a foreigner.

Then Jesus counted: "Were there not ten cleansed? but where are the nine? There are not found that returned to give glory to God, save this stranger."

To the one at his feet: "Arise, go thy way: thy faith hath made thee whole."

Whole. Ten were cleansed. One heard that word — in Luke's Greek, the same words he gave the woman who washed his feet: "Thy faith hath saved thee." The nine got everything they asked for. Cleansed happened on the road. Whole still happens at his feet.
