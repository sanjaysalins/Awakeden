# Independent review — claude (OK, 153s)

## Adversarial Review — "Where Are the Nine" (Luke 17:11-19)

**KJV verbatim check** — every quoted clause checked word-for-word against Luke 17:12-19 and Luke 7:50:
- "Jesus, Master, have mercy on us." ✓ exact (v13)
- "Go shew yourselves unto the priests" ✓ exact (v14a)
- "And it came to pass, that, as they went, they were cleansed." ✓ exact (v14b)
- "...with a loud voice glorified God, and fell down on his face at his feet, giving him thanks: and he was a Samaritan." ✓ words exact — merges v15b+v16, lowercase "and" at the join is grammatically necessary and doesn't alter any KJV word.
- "Were there not ten cleansed? but where are the nine? There are not found that returned to give glory to God, save this stranger." ✓ exact (v17-18)
- "Arise, go thy way: thy faith hath made thee whole." ✓ exact (v19)
- "Thy faith hath saved thee." ✓ exact fragment of Luke 7:50.
No alterations found. Clean.

**Doctrinal fact-check** — "the inspection for a leper already healed" is accurate to Leviticus 14 (the priest's role was to *confirm* a healing that had already occurred, not to initiate one) — the ten had to obey before the visible healing, which is the real point of the passage. Not invented.

**The "same Greek words" claim** — verified: Luke 17:19's "thy faith hath made thee whole" and Luke 7:50's "Thy faith hath saved thee" both translate the identical Greek (σέσωκέν σε, sesōken se, perfect of σῴζω). KJV renders the same verb two different ways depending on context. This is a real, checkable original-language fact, not a stretch — it's the piece's best asset.

**Problem 1 — the piece never turns to the listener.** Read start to finish: it is 100% third-person Bible narrative. "Whole still happens at his feet" is a true, earned, piece-specific closing image (passes the "could this end a different piece?" test — it can't, it's Luke 17's own word) but it is an *observation*, not an *invitation*. The project's own model is "CTA-to-Jesus," not the non-preachy Attenborough mode — this piece reads as the latter. It never says or implies "you." That's a structural gap, not a wording problem — swapping in a different closing sentence won't fix it; the piece needs one earned second-person beat.

**Problem 2 — the Luke 7 cross-reference has zero setup.** "The same words he gave the woman who washed his feet" assumes the listener already knows who that is. A true cold listener (the stated bar: "zero Bible knowledge") gets an unexplained pronoun-reference to a story that isn't this story — the payoff of "same Greek word" partially depends on recognizing *her*, and right now she's a floating clause. One clarifying word (e.g., "the sinful woman who wept at his feet") would let the echo land instead of requiring outside knowledge.

**Problem 3 (minor) — compressed exposition.** "The inspection for a leper already healed. They weren't. Not yet." is accurate but dense — three short fragments doing a lot of logical work back-to-back. First-time listeners may need a beat to parse it before "as they went, they were cleansed" resolves the tension. Not disqualifying, just a friction point.

**Grace-anchored check:** passes — "the nine got everything they asked for" is stated as fact about the text, not aimed at the listener as a threat ("don't be like the nine or you'll miss out"). No fear/gain-loss framing found. (Ironically this is downstream of Problem 1 — it's grace-anchored partly *because* it never turns to address anyone.)

**Thread spine:** single and clean — count/absence (hook) → obedience-before-sight → the one who returns → "whole" vs. "cleansed" (landing). No thread-swapping.

**HOOK SCORE: 8/10** — concrete, numeric, genuine curiosity gap ("counted how many didn't come back"), not a template open ("did you know/imagine/what if"). Stops scroll.

**LANDING SCORE: 7/10** — the closing image is earned and piece-specific (not a stock "come to Jesus" phrase, passes that test cleanly), but it never converts into an actual call to the listener — it stays inside the Bible story to the last word. Strong material, incomplete function.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Add one earned second-person turn at the landing so "whole" becomes an invitation to the listener, not just a closing observation about the nine and the one — keep it built from THIS piece's own word ("whole"), don't reach for a stock CTA phrase to do it.
2. Give "the woman who washed his feet" one clarifying clause so a zero-background listener recognizes her and the "same Greek word" echo actually lands instead of referencing an unexplained outside story.
3. Ease the "inspection for a leper already healed... Not yet" beat — accurate but compressed; a first-time listener needs a half-beat more room before the "as they went, they were cleansed" payoff.
