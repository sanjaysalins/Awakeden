This is the Sunday-school retell of the ten lepers, with a spatial metaphor bolted on at the end. The quotes are mostly clean. The spine is not. The close asks the listener to produce a return trip.

---

**HOOK: 7/10**

**“Jesus sent ten men with leprosy away — before a single one of them was healed.”**

Not a template open. The dash is a real reversal. A stranger might pause.

It still does not clear 8. Word one is church-coded. Anyone who knows the story can predict the rest from the title. **“sent … away”** also sounds like dismissal. The text is **“Go shew yourselves unto the priests”** (Luke 17:14) — the Lev 14 path, not a brush-off. The next two lines — **“They were cleansed on the road. Only one of them ever came back.”** — dump the whole plot. The hook spends its surprise in three sentences.

---

**LANDING: 5/10**  (under 8 → REVISE)

**“If he's ever answered you from a distance, you already know the way back to where he's standing.”**

Portability test: that exact sentence could close the centurion, Bartimaeus, or any “you already have what you need, just come closer” piece. It is **“come to Him”** with furniture moved.

It is also the spatial cousin of the banned closer **“He's waiting for you.”** The piece’s load-bearing verbs are **cleansed / glory / faith / whole**. The last sentence uses none of them. **“the way back”** never names the way. The listener is told they already know. That is a dodge, not a landing.

Worse: the line before it — **“Only one closed it for good”** — never says what **it** is. If **it** is the disease, it is false (Jesus: **“Were there not ten cleansed?”**). If **it** is the relationship, you are back in the contested two-tier pulpit inference this draft was supposed to have dropped.

---

**Doctrinal soundness**

No heresy. No clickbait. Not a FAIL.

1. **Two-tier smuggle, still here.** All ten **“were cleansed.”** Then only one **“closed it for good, and heard Jesus call him whole.”** That insinuates: nine got skin, one got the real thing. Jesus’ own contrast in the quoted verse is not saved-vs-healed. It is **“returned to give glory to God.”** Contested pulpit inference used as the turn. Banned move.

2. **Works-shaped close.** Jesus’ last word in the piece is **“thy faith hath made thee whole.”** The script’s last word is **“the way back.”** The Samaritan’s distinctive act is treated as the product the listener must supply. Constitution: the answer must point to something God gives, never something the viewer must produce. This snaps the grace anchor on the final line.

3. **Overclaim.** **“Only one of them ever came back.”** Luke does not give the nine a later biography. **“ever”** is argument from silence.

4. **Misframe in the hook.** **“sent … away”** is not what he said. He sent them *to the priests*. The nine who kept walking were doing the commanded thing. The script never faces that tension; it just calls it **“what nine men had skipped.”**

**KJV verbatim**

Exact inside the marks:

- **“Jesus, Master, have mercy on us.”** (17:13)
- **“Go shew yourselves unto the priests.”** (17:14)
- **“And it came to pass, that, as they went, they were cleansed.”** (17:14)
- **“Were there not ten cleansed? but where are the nine? There are not found that returned to give glory to God, save this stranger.”** (17:17–18, words exact; two verses glued)
- **“Arise, go thy way: thy faith hath made thee whole.”** (17:19)

One altered word: **“and fell down on his face…”** Luke 17:16 begins **“And fell down…”** You lowercased **And** and spliced 15b onto 16 after dropping **“when he saw that he was healed, turned back.”** G1 is punctuation-strict. Split the quotes or keep **And**.

Six substantial quoted spans. The piece is recitation with glue. That is why it feels like a retell, not one idea.

---

**Clarity on first hearing**

**“They obeyed before they could see any difference.”** praises obedience. Then the nine who **“kept walking toward the priests”** — still obeying — become the warning, and the one who turned back becomes the hero. A listener with zero commentary hears the script bless obedience, then punish it.

**“closed it for good”** is a blank pronoun. Closed the sores? the distance? the story? You cannot leave that in the air.

**“Samaritan”** is quoted and never explained. Zero Bible knowledge does not know why **“this stranger”** matters. If the outsider is the point, the script does not say so. If he isn’t, the word is inert.

**“you already know the way back to where he's standing”** is a riddle. Standing where? The way is what — the road, the thanks, the faith, the face at his feet?

---

**Grace-anchored conviction**

No fear. No gain/loss. No “you’ll lose the healing.”

The turn onto **you** is real, and that is the one improvement over a pure plot retell. It still does not pierce. **“If he's ever answered you from a distance”** only addresses people who already got a yes; everyone else is outside the close. Then it pats them: you already know. That is not Hebrews 4:12. It is a nudge to go do the return.

---

**Freshness / one thread**

Jaded-scroller test: from **“ten men with leprosy,”** every line is predictable except the last metaphor. The autocomplete take of this story *is* “ten healed, one came back, don’t be the nine.” That is this draft.

Thread swap, in order:

1. Hook: sent before any healing (word before sight)
2. Middle: missing **glory**, **where are the nine**
3. Close: close the **distance** / **way back**

Three ideas. None runs hook → CTA. The only fresh observation in the first line (he sent them still unclean) is abandoned by sentence four.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Rebuild the last sentence from this piece’s own verb — whole / glory / faith / as they went — as a grace invitation to Jesus. Kill “closed it for good” and “the way back to where he's standing.” If that exact line could end another distance-healing, it is still stock.
2. Pick one spine (sent-before-healed, or return-to-give-glory, or afar-off-to-his-feet) and run it hook → proof → close. Do not open on timing, prove gratitude, and land on geography.
3. Stop the two-tier overclaim. Ten were cleansed. Do not imply only one was finished. Fix “sent away” if the hook stays; resolve the obey-then-punish-obedience collision so a first hearing can follow it.
