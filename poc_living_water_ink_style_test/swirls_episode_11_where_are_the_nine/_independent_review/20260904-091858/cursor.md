### Doctrinal soundness
Mostly orthodox, but one tension worth flagging.

**Problem — nine’s faith gets erased by the “whole” beat.** You correctly show all ten obeyed in faith: *"They obeyed before they could see any difference"* and *"They were cleansed on the road."* Luke gives all ten cleansing on the road. Then the close collapses everything into: *"Only one closed it for good, and heard Jesus call him whole."*

A careful listener can hear: nine got skin; one got salvation. That’s a defensible sermon reading of *"thy faith hath made thee whole"* (σῴζω), but **the text’s explicit rebuke is not missing faith — it’s missing return and glory**: *"returned to give glory to God"* / *"where are the nine?"* The nine believed enough to go; they failed at thanksgiving and presence. Without one plain line naming that gap, *"only one… whole"* risks a two-tier mercy story the passage doesn’t cleanly support and can smell like gratitude-as-the-real-save.

**Missed faithful depth (not heresy):** Jesus calls the grateful leper *"this stranger"* while the quote already says *"he was a Samaritan."* The outsider is the one who returns — one of the passage’s sharpest ironies. You quote both facts but never let a zero-Bible listener feel why *"stranger"* lands like a hammer.

### Clarity on first hearing
**Ambiguous pronoun:** *"Only one closed it for good"* — **closed what?** Distance? The mercy transaction? The gap between cry and face-to-face worship? On audio, *"it"* forces guesswork. The distance motif is set up earlier (*"stood at a distance"*), but *"closed it for good"* is still a logic hop, not a picture.

**Possible contradiction for a stranger:** Para 1: *"They were cleansed on the road. Only one of them ever came back."* That’s clear. Para 5: *"Only one… heard Jesus call him whole"* — a listener who missed the plural *"They were cleansed"* could think only one was healed at all. Tighten the “all ten cleansed / one returned” fact before the *"whole"* line.

**"save this stranger":** Without a beat on Samaritan-as-outsider, *"stranger"* may not connect to *"Samaritan"* for someone with no Bible background.

### Grace-anchored conviction
No fear/gain-loss framing. Tone invites rather than threatens.  

**Soft exclusion risk:** *"If he's ever answered you from a distance, you already know the way back"* assumes prior experience of being answered. Listeners who feel unheard or don’t know what *"the way back"* means get no handhold. Not works-framing, but not fully welcoming either.

### Freshness = faithful depth
**Strength:** *"before a single one of them was healed"* / obedience-before-visible-change is real textual depth, not clickbait.

**Weakness:** The passage’s freshest punch — **nine healed, zero glory; the despised stranger is the only worshipper** — is quoted but not preached. The close pivots to generic return language instead.

---

### One thread spine (hook → middle → CTA)
**Mostly one thread:** distance → cry for mercy → go-before-you-see → cleansing → one returns → Jesus’ lament → *"made thee whole"* → close the distance.

**Slip at the landing:** The middle’s controlling verbs are **glory, thanks, return, whole**. The final sentence runs on **answered / way back / where he's standing** — related imagery, but **glory and thanks disappear** right when Jesus named them as the point. Not full thread-swap, but the CTA drifts off the spine Jesus just spoke aloud.

### Hook score: **8/10**
*"Jesus sent ten men with leprosy away — before a single one of them was healed"* is concrete, paradoxical, and non-template. Stranger scroll-stops. Not a 9–10 — no name, no “ten… nine” count sting in the first breath — but solid.

### Landing score: **5/10**
*"If he's ever answered you from a distance, you already know the way back to where he's standing"* borrows the piece’s distance motif, but:

- It does **not** use this piece’s climax verbs (**glory, thanks, whole**).
- **Could this exact sentence end a different piece?** Yes — prodigal return, distant cry for mercy, any “come back to Jesus” short. Portable invitation, not a locked landing.
- **"you already know"** tells rather than shows; weaker than the Samaritan face-down at Jesus’ feet you just painted.
