# Independent review — codex (OK, 37s)

The quotations are KJV-verbatim; I found no altered words in the quoted material when checked against [Luke 17:12–19 KJV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Luke%2017%3A12-19&version=KJV). The problems are interpretive and rhetorical.

- “Only one closed it for good” is unclear on first hearing. What did he “close”—the distance, the encounter, or his leprosy? More seriously, it implies the Samaritan secured permanent wholeness by returning. Jesus attributes it to faith: “thy faith hath made thee whole.” Gratitude evidences that faith; the narration risks making gratitude the completing work.

- “Only one … heard Jesus call him whole” is textually true, but paired with “closed it for good” it presents a debated inference as certainty: that the nine were merely healed while this man alone received spiritual salvation. That is a defensible evangelical reading, but not the only orthodox reading of *sōzō* here. The script should not quietly build its entire landing upon the strongest version of that contested distinction.

- “Nine kept walking toward the priests” exceeds what Luke reports. The text establishes that they did not return, not where they subsequently went. It is a plausible narrative inference, but stated as witnessed fact.

- “Jesus sent ten men with leprosy away” creates unnecessary harshness. He did not dismiss them; he directed them to the priests in accordance with the prescribed process. The provocative wording helps the hook but distorts the emotional character of the command.

- “If he’s ever answered you from a distance” is spiritually vague. A listener with no Bible knowledge will not know what counts as Jesus “answering” them or why physical distance has become a metaphor for their relationship with him.

- “you already know the way back” weakens grace into presumed human knowledge and action. The passage’s center is Christ’s mercy received through faith, with gratitude flowing from it. Here the closing emphasis becomes: you know the route, so retrace it. That shifts conviction toward the hearer’s performance.

- The spine begins with obedient faith before visible cleansing, moves to gratitude, then lands on overcoming distance. Those ideas relate, but the governing thread changes twice. Choose one: mercy received by faith and answered with worship would most naturally unite the whole piece.

HOOK SCORE: 8/10. The opening has a concrete paradox and immediate narrative motion. “Sent … away” makes it sharper than the text warrants, however.

LANDING SCORE: 6/10. It is customized through the distance/return imagery, so it is not wholly stock. But “closed it for good” is opaque, “where he’s standing” is an invented spatial picture, and the decisive invitation is not clearly anchored in Christ’s mercy or the text’s stated emphasis on faith.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Remove the claim that returning “closed it for good”; distinguish faith from gratitude as its fruit, not a completing work.
2. Build one continuous thread around mercy received through faith and expressed in worship.
3. Replace the vague distance-based CTA with a text-specific, grace-anchored invitation to return and give Christ thanks.
