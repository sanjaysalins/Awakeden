# Independent review — claude (OK, 167s)

Reviewed the artifact against KJV, doctrine, and craft. Findings below.

**KJV verbatim check** — every quoted clause checks out against Luke 17:12-19 word-for-word: "Jesus, Master, have mercy on us" (v.13), "Go shew yourselves unto the priests" / "And it came to pass, that, as they went, they were cleansed" (v.14), "and with a loud voice glorified God, and fell down on his face at his feet, giving him thanks: and he was a Samaritan" (v.15-16, one word's capitalization lowered — "And fell" → "and fell" — to fuse the two verses into one sentence; not a wording change, just a merge convention), "Were there not ten cleansed?...save this stranger" (v.17-18), "Arise, go thy way: thy faith hath made thee whole" (v.19). No alterations found.

**Doctrine** — sound. No invented theology, no fear/gain-loss framing, no works-righteousness slippage — the "faith hath made thee whole" distinction (all ten cleansed, only one made "whole") is textually supported, not manufactured.

**Clarity** — mostly followable cold, but "and he was a Samaritan" is dropped in as a bare fact with zero context. The piece leans on the word "stranger" (from v.18) to carry the outsider point, which partially covers it, but a listener with no Bible background won't know Jews and Samaritans were estranged — the actual reason this detail is the emotional hinge of the story (the outsider was the only grateful one). Worth one clause of context.

**Thread spine** — consistent. Hook's "sent away before healed" tension resolves fast ("cleansed on the road"), then the real spine (gratitude / who returns) carries cleanly to the landing. No thread-swap.

**HOOK SCORE: 8/10** — "Jesus sent ten men with leprosy away — before a single one of them was healed" is a genuine paradox, not a template open ("Did you know…"), and it's answered honestly two lines later rather than milked for clickbait.

**LANDING SCORE: 5/10** — this is the real problem. "If he's ever answered you from a distance, you already know the way back to where he's standing" applies the rubric's own test badly: this exact line could close the Prodigal Son just as easily — arguably better, since "while he was yet a great way off" (Luke 15:20) is THE canonical "distance + return" image in the gospels, more famous than the ten lepers' ceremonial distance. "Distance, then returning to where he's standing" isn't unique to this piece's text; it's generic gospel homecoming imagery grafted onto this episode. Per the rubric's own instruction ("could this exact final sentence end a different piece? If yes... cap at 5"), that's a stock-shape closer even though the *wording* isn't the literal "come to Jesus" family.

Also minor: "where he's standing" invents an unstated posture for Jesus — the text never says he was standing; the leper fell "at his feet," which is the actual textual image available to land on.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Rewrite the landing so it can't be lifted onto the Prodigal Son (or any other "healed/welcomed from a distance" gospel story) — anchor the final line to something unique to THIS text: the nine-vs-one contrast, the word "stranger," or the leprosy-specific ceremonial distance, not generic "distance and return."
2. Give "and he was a Samaritan" one clause of context (Jew/Samaritan estrangement) so the outsider-was-the-grateful-one point lands for a listener with zero background, not just via the word "stranger."
3. Fix "to where he's standing" — the text never puts Jesus standing; use the story's own image (the leper falling at his feet) instead of an invented posture.
