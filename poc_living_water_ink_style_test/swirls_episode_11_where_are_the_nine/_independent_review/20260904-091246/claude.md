# Independent review — claude (OK, 163s)

**KJV verbatim check** — I pulled the actual KJV text for Luke 17:12-19 and Luke 7:50 to verify. Every quoted phrase is word-for-word accurate:
- "Jesus, Master, have mercy on us" (v13) ✓
- "Go shew yourselves unto the priests" / "And it came to pass, that, as they went, they were cleansed" (v14) ✓
- "and with a loud voice glorified God, and fell down on his face at his feet, giving him thanks: and he was a Samaritan" — this splices v15 into v16 across the verse boundary, and lowercases the original "And" to "and." Words are unchanged; only the capitalization of one conjunction shifts to fit mid-sentence flow. Trivial, not a real alteration.
- "Were there not ten cleansed? but where are the nine? There are not found that returned to give glory to God, save this stranger" (v17-18) ✓
- "Arise, go thy way: thy faith hath made thee whole" (v19) ✓
- "Thy faith hath saved thee" (Luke 7:50) ✓

**The Greek-verb claim checks out.** Luke 17:19 and Luke 7:50 do use the same underlying verb (σέσωκέν, sesōken/sōzō), rendered "made thee whole" in one place and "saved thee" in the other. This is a real, well-attested linguistic point — not invented. Genuinely fresh-but-faithful.

**Speculative overreach beyond the text:** "Nine men walked away healed and never once turned to see who had healed them." Scripture only says they didn't *return* to give glory to God — it never says they didn't look back or acknowledge Him at all. Stated flatly as narrative fact, this goes past what's written.

**Clarity risk on first hearing:** "Not their faith. Their glory to God." — two clipped fragments with no verb, dropped immediately after a dense KJV quote. A listener with zero Bible background has to reconstruct "what was missing wasn't their faith, it was their giving glory to God" in real time, on audio, with no visual punctuation to lean on. Risky for a cold listener.

**Minor rhetorical overstatement:** "History remembers the Samaritan who turned around." The text never names him — nobody in this story is named. Forgivable as a figure of speech, but it's asserting a fact ("remembered," implying a known/named figure) that isn't quite true.

**The landing is the real problem.** Scan the whole piece: there is not a single direct address to the listener anywhere — no "you," no imperative, no second person at all. It's third-person narrative start to finish, and the closing line — "Jesus hasn't changed: quick to heal whoever asks, and always glad when someone turns back to say thank you" — is a description of Jesus's character, not an invitation to the person watching. It passes the "stock closer" test (it's not "come to Jesus" boilerplate, it's built from this story's own thanks/turning-back image), but it fails the deeper requirement of this project's own model: "the script invites." This reads as an epilogue/moral, not a CTA. Also note "quick to heal whoever asks" is the generic half of that sentence — it could close almost any healing-miracle piece (blind Bartimaeus, the centurion's servant); only the "turn back to say thank you" half is genuinely this story's own.

**Hook score: 8/10.** "Jesus sent ten men with leprosy away — before a single one of them was healed" is concrete and creates real tension, not a template opener. Would stop a scroll.

**Landing score: 6/10.** Specific, earned imagery (nine unnamed, the Samaritan, giving thanks) — but it never turns to address the listener, so it functions as commentary rather than invitation. Under the 8-point bar.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Rewrite the landing to directly address the listener — right now the piece uses "you" zero times; the CTA needs an actual invitation (built on the story's own "turn back"/"give thanks" language), not a third-person statement about Jesus's character.
2. Cut or rephrase "never once turned to see who had healed them" — the text supports "didn't return to give glory," not "never looked back"; don't assert more than Luke wrote.
3. Give "Not their faith. Their glory to God." an explicit verb/frame so a first-time listener doesn't have to reconstruct the contrast mid-sentence on audio alone.
