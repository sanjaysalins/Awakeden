## Independent adversarial review — Luke 17:11–19 (ten lepers)

### KJV verbatim

All quoted strings match KJV wording. No altered words inside the marks.

- `"Jesus, Master, have mercy on us."` — Luke 17:13 ✓  
- `"Go shew yourselves unto the priests."` — Luke 17:14 ✓  
- `"And it came to pass, that, as they went, they were cleansed."` — Luke 17:14 ✓  
- `"and with a loud voice glorified God, and fell down on his face at his feet, giving him thanks: and he was a Samaritan."` — Luke 17:15–16 (mid-verse join; verse-initial `And` lowercased) ✓  
- `"Were there not ten cleansed? but where are the nine? There are not found that returned to give glory to God, save this stranger."` — Luke 17:17–18 ✓  
- `"Arise, go thy way: thy faith hath made thee whole."` — Luke 17:19 ✓  
- `"Thy faith hath saved thee."` — Luke 7:50 fragment ✓  

**Audio hazard (not a KJV fail, but a clarity fail):** Jesus’s line contains **“save this stranger”** (*except* this stranger). That lands immediately before a paragraph about **“saved thee.”** On first hearing, a zero-Bible listener will hear “saved” twice with two different meanings and think the piece contradicts itself.

---

### Doctrinal soundness

Mostly orthodox. No heresy, no clickbait contrarianism. The Greek link (σῴζω in Luke 17:19 and Luke 7:50) is real.

**Problem:** the piece **imports** the Luke 7 woman and the sozo note but **never earns or resolves it**. Jesus’s actual lament is about **“give glory to God,”** not about a vocabulary lesson. The narration quotes glory, then pivots to a translation aside, then closes on **“say thank you.”** That is a soft downgrade from worship to manners, and it leaves the salvific echo hanging without saying what the listener should do with “saved” vs “whole.” Not invented doctrine — but **over-clever inference left unpaid**, which is how “faithful depth” turns into pulpit fog.

**Minor overreach:** **“quick to heal whoever asks”** generalizes beyond this pericope. Here they asked for mercy and **obeyed while still leprous**; cleansing came **as they went**. The landing flattens that into ask-and-receive, which this text does not teach.

---

### Clarity on first hearing

**Strong:** ten / nine / one, sent before healed, cleansed on the road — a stranger can follow that.

**Weak:**

- **“Nine men walked away healed and never once turned to see who had healed them.”** — False on its face. They opened with **“Jesus, Master, have mercy on us.”** They knew who sent them. The text’s issue is **not returning to give glory/thanks**, not failing to identify the healer. This line trades accuracy for a punchline.

- **“Not their faith. Their glory to God.”** — Compressed to the point of ambiguity. *Whose* faith? The nine’s? Jesus names **missing return to give glory**; he does not run a faith-vs-glory audit on the nine. Worse: two sentences later Jesus says **“thy faith hath made thee whole”** to the one still on his face — so faith **is** load-bearing in the climax. The staccato contrast reads like a logic trick, not exposition.

- **“The same Greek verb Luke had already used — worded differently in English — for a sinful woman who once wept at those same feet”** — Seminar language in a 60s short. **“Those same feet”** is a literary wink (Luke 7 ≠ Luke 17 setting); a first-time listener gets a second story with no payoff. **Why** the verb match matters is never stated in plain English.

---

### Grace-anchored conviction

No hell-threat, no gain/loss lever, no “do this or lose healing.” Tone is observational, not coercive. **But** the close is so soft it barely **invites** at all — it describes Jesus’s disposition in the third person. The Spirit convicts; this script **reports** rather than **addresses** the hearer.

---

### Freshness = faithful depth

**Good:** **“before a single one of them was healed”** and **“They obeyed before they could see any difference”** are faithful, overlooked beats — real text surprise.

**Bad:** The Luke 7 / Greek verb beat is text-deep on paper but **unpaid** in the landing. **“Samaritan”** and **“stranger”** are quoted and dropped — the outsider-only return is one of Luke’s sharpest points and the piece ignores it.

---

### One thread spine (hook → middle → CTA)

| Beat | Thread |
|------|--------|
| Hook | Sent away unhealed; one came back |
| Middle | Jesus names missing **glory to God** |
| Pivot | **Not faith / glory** + Greek **saved/whole** aside |
| Close | **Thank you** + **Jesus hasn’t changed** |

**“Give glory to God”** never lands; it becomes **“say thank you.”** The Greek/faith thread never reaches the close. The hook’s **one came back** survives, but the **indictment’s own words** (glory, stranger, Samaritan) are abandoned. That is partial thread-swap.

---

### HOOK: **8 / 10**

**“Jesus sent ten men with leprosy away — before a single one of them was healed.”**  
Accurate, concrete, not a template open. A stranger should stop — paradox plus number. Not a 9 because **“Only one of them ever came back”** telegraphs the famous Sunday-school headline on the next breath; still strong, not irresistible.

---

### LANDING: **5 / 10**

**“Jesus hasn’t changed: quick to heal whoever asks, and always glad when someone turns back to say thank you.”**

**Portability test: yes.** That sentence could close almost any healing-or-gratitude short (fed 5,000 who leave, any “ten healed / one returned” piece) with minimal edits. It does not use this piece’s load-bearing KJV verbs (**mercy**, **glory**, **whole**, **faith**, **stranger**). **“Jesus hasn’t changed”** is generic Christology filler; **“say thank you”** shrinks **“give glory to God”** into etiquette. Not a stock **“come to Jesus”** closer, but still capped at 5 — and under the 8 bar.

---

VERDICT: REVISE  
TOP FIXES:  
1. Delete or rewrite **“never once turned to see who had healed them”** — they knew Jesus; they failed to **return and give glory**, which is what Jesus actually said was missing.  
2. Rebuild the landing from **this passage’s own words** (glory / whole / faith / stranger), with a **you-facing** invitation — not **“Jesus hasn’t changed… say thank you,”** which could end a different piece and drops the quoted indictment.  
3. Either **pay off or cut** the Luke 7 Greek aside: one plain sentence on why *saved* and *made thee whole* share one word, **or** drop it and let **“give glory to God”** carry the middle; do not leave **“save this stranger”** adjacent to **“saved thee”** without fixing the homonym crash on audio.
