# Independent review — gemini (OK, 37s)

Here is the independent review of the narration artifact:

**HOOK SCORE: 7/10**
The hook ("Jesus sent ten men with leprosy away — before a single one of them was healed") is a solid, narrative-driven open that avoids templates. It creates immediate curiosity about the timeline of the miracle, though it lacks a visceral punch to push it into the 9-10 range.

**LANDING SCORE: 3/10**
The landing ("Jesus hasn't changed... always glad when someone turns back to say thank you") is a massive failure. It completely abandons the deep theological thread you just built (the Greek verb for "saved") and reduces the Gospel of Jesus Christ to a moralistic lesson about basic politeness. It also contains no actual CTA or grace-anchored invitation for the listener. 

**CRITIQUE & FINDINGS:**
- **Thread-swapping / Weak Landing:** You introduce a brilliant, deep connection in the 5th paragraph: the Greek verb *sozo* ("made thee whole" / "saved thee"), linking this leper to the sinful woman weeping at Jesus' feet. But in the very next breath, you throw that profound theological thread away to end on: "always glad when someone turns back to say thank you." This is theological whiplash. You built a bridge to salvation/wholeness and then ended on Sunday School manners. 
- **Clarity / Self-Contradiction:** In paragraph 4, you write: *"Not their faith. Their glory to God."* This is a false dichotomy and immediately contradicts the very next paragraph where Jesus explicitly says, *"thy faith hath made thee whole."* A listener will be confused: was it his faith or not? Don't invent a contradiction just to sound profound.
- **KJV Verbatim:** All KJV quotes are perfectly accurate to Luke 17 and Luke 7. 

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Rewrite the landing to pay off the "saved/made whole" (*sozo*) thread. Do not reduce this to "saying thank you"; build a grace-anchored CTA that invites the listener to fall at Jesus' feet to find true wholeness, not just physical healing.
2. Delete or rewrite the phrase "Not their faith. Their glory to God." It creates a direct contradiction with Jesus' own words in the following paragraph.
3. Remove the academic framing of "The same Greek verb Luke had already used...". Show, don't tell. Connect the leper's wholeness to the sinful woman's salvation seamlessly without giving a grammar lecture.
