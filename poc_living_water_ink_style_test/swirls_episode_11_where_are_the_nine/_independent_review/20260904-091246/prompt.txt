Jesus sent ten men with leprosy away — before a single one of them was healed. They were cleansed on the road. Only one of them ever came back.

They'd stood at a distance, as their disease required, and cried out, "Jesus, Master, have mercy on us." He told them, "Go shew yourselves unto the priests." They obeyed before they could see any difference. "And it came to pass, that, as they went, they were cleansed."

Nine kept walking toward the priests. One turned back, "and with a loud voice glorified God, and fell down on his face at his feet, giving him thanks: and he was a Samaritan."

Jesus said what was missing, out loud: "Were there not ten cleansed? but where are the nine? There are not found that returned to give glory to God, save this stranger." Not their faith. Their glory to God. Nine men walked away healed and never once turned to see who had healed them.

To the one still on his face, Jesus said, "Arise, go thy way: thy faith hath made thee whole." The same Greek verb Luke had already used — worded differently in English — for a sinful woman who once wept at those same feet: "Thy faith hath saved thee."

Nine men are never named again. History remembers the Samaritan who turned around. Jesus hasn't changed: quick to heal whoever asks, and always glad when someone turns back to say thank you.
