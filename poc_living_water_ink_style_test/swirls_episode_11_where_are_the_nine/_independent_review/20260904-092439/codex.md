# Independent review — codex (OK, 40s)

Hook: 8/10. The opening creates immediate tension—“go — before a single one of them was healed”—without resorting to a template.

Landing: 7/10. “Go back and say so” grows from the story’s central action, so it is not a stock closer. But “to his face” is cleverer than it is clear: a new listener may wonder how one literally returns to Jesus’ face. The preceding accusation also weakens the grace-centered invitation.

Specific findings:

- “a foreigner to the very Jews he'd just been healed alongside” invents the other nine men’s ethnicity. Luke identifies this man as a Samaritan and “this stranger,” but never says all nine companions were Jews.
- “in front of everyone” is another unsupported addition. The text records a loud voice and the man falling at Jesus’ feet, but identifies no audience beyond Jesus.
- “That's still the part most people skip” makes an ungrounded claim about most people and shifts from exposition into generalized accusation. It risks manufacturing guilt instead of letting Christ’s question and the contrast convict.
- “Nine kept going” is plausible shorthand, but the text establishes only that they did not return to give glory to God. It does not explicitly say where they went or what they subsequently did.
- “Nine men had exactly what they'd asked for” compresses “have mercy on us” into a request solely for physical healing. That is reasonable in context, but “exactly” overstates what the wording establishes.
- The piece approaches a potentially rich distinction between all ten being “cleansed” and one being “made…whole,” but does not develop it. That may be prudent because interpretations of the latter differ; avoid implying a contested doctrine merely for a sharper contrast.
- All words inside quotation marks match the KJV wording. No altered quoted word is evident.
- The spine is coherent throughout: commanded departure, cleansing while going, one man’s return, and the invitation to “go back.” The problem is not thread-swapping but unsupported embellishment around that thread.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Remove the invented claims that the other nine were Jews and that the Samaritan thanked Jesus “in front of everyone.”
2. Replace “most people skip” with a grace-anchored invitation that lets Jesus’ own question supply the conviction.
3. Clarify “go back and say so, to his face” so the piece-specific return motif remains vivid without sounding literally or spiritually confusing.
