# Independent review — claude (OK, 105s)

Reviewed as an outside adversarial reader — Luke 17:11-19, the ten lepers.

**Doctrinal soundness:** Sound. No invented doctrine, no contrarian reading. "Thy faith hath made thee whole" is quoted straight and not stretched into a claim the text doesn't support.

**KJV verbatim:** Checked every quoted clause against KJV Luke 17:12-19 — all exact, including the archaic "shew." One quotation-boundary quirk worth noting: "One turned back," sits outside the quote marks even though "turned back" is itself KJV wording from v.15 (the verse reads "...turned back, and with a loud voice glorified God..."), while everything from "and with a loud voice" onward is quoted. No word is altered, but the split is inconsistent — either quote the whole clause or none of it.

**Clarity:** Follows cleanly with zero prior Bible knowledge. "As their disease required" (v.12 context) and "a foreigner to the very Jews he'd just been healed alongside" (Samaritan status) are both accurate, unobtrusive context, not invented color.

**Grace-anchored conviction:** Mostly clean — no fear, no gain/loss, no reward promised for returning. One soft spot: "That's still the part most people skip" generalizes the nine's failure onto the listener before the CTA lands. It's not fear-framing, but it's an implicit accusation rather than a pure invitation — worth softening or cutting the generalization and letting the contrast (nine vs. one) do the convicting on its own.

**Thread spine:** Single thread throughout — sent-before-healed → nine keep walking / one turns back → landing built on "turn back." No swapping.

**HOOK — 7/10:** Not a template open, and the "sent before healed" reversal has real curiosity value. But it's expository — three clean factual sentences, no sensory or social weight (what it actually meant to be a leper: untouchable, exiled, can't go home to your own kids) that would make a stranger feel the moment instead of just being told it. The ten/one structure is doing most of the hook's work; that's a fairly common "surprising stat" mechanism, not a felt image.

**LANDING — 8/10:** "Go back and say so, to his face" earns its keep — it isn't a stock "come to Jesus" phrase; "to his face" specifically mirrors "fell down on his face at his feet," and "go back" mirrors "turned back." This wouldn't cleanly close a different piece without those two physical anchors carrying over. Passes the test.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Hook (7/10) is under the bar — open with a felt, sensory detail of what leprosy cost these men (exile, distance, no home) rather than leading with the numeric ten/one structure alone.
2. Cut or soften "That's still the part most people skip" — it generalizes blame onto the listener before the invitation lands; let the nine-vs-one contrast carry the conviction instead of naming the audience's likely failure.
3. Fix the quotation boundary around "turned back" — it's KJV wording from v.15 but currently sits outside the quote marks while the rest of the clause is quoted; make the split consistent.
