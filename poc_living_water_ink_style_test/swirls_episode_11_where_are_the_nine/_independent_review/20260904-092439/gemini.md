# Independent review — gemini (OK, 41s)

Here is the adversarial review of the narration artifact:

**Doctrinal Soundness & Grace-Anchored Conviction**
The script devolves into a moralistic lesson on gratitude rather than a gospel-centered message. The CTA ("Whatever he's already done for you — go back and say so, to his face.") is behavioral instruction. It reduces the interaction with the living God to "remember your manners." The nine received physical healing, but the one received *Jesus* and salvation ("made thee whole"). The script misses the gospel pivot entirely, settling for works/behavioral framing instead of inviting the listener to the Savior.

**One Thread Spine**
There is a glaring thread-swap. The hook sets up a premise about faith before sight: *"Jesus told ten men with leprosy to go — before a single one of them was healed."* But the script immediately abandons this thread. The middle and the landing pivot entirely to a different theme: gratitude and returning to give thanks (*"That's still the part most people skip"*). The hook writes a check that the rest of the script doesn't cash. 

**KJV Verbatim**
Flagged alteration: The script quotes, `"...and with a loud voice glorified God, and fell down on his face..."` This merges Luke 17:15 and 17:16. In the KJV, verse 16 begins with a capital letter: *"And fell down on his face..."* The script alters it to a lowercase "and" to force the grammar of the sentence. 

**Clarity on First Hearing**
The narrative is easy to follow, but the phrase *"a foreigner to the very Jews he'd just been healed alongside"* is slightly clunky and distracts from the pacing of the actual biblical event. 

**SCORE THE HOOK: 7**
It is a strong narrative open that avoids template clichés and creates immediate tension. However, it loses points because it establishes a false premise (healing on the road / faith before sight) that the rest of the script ignores.

**SCORE THE LANDING: 5**
The landing (*"Whatever he's already done for you — go back and say so, to his face."*) is a behavioral command, not a grace-anchored CTA. It could end a secular motivational video about gratitude. It fails to land in the bones because it points the listener to their own actions (saying thanks) rather than pointing them to Christ as the ultimate prize.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Fix the thread-swap: Align the hook and the landing. If the core message is about not settling for the gift over the Giver, rewrite the hook to establish that spine.
2. Rewrite the CTA: Strip out the moralistic "say thank you" framing. Anchor the conviction in grace—point to Jesus as the ultimate wholeness, not just the dispenser of blessings.
3. Correct the KJV verbatim capitalization in the Luke 17:15-16 quote to match the exact biblical text ("And fell down...").
