## Independent adversarial review

### Doctrinal soundness
Mostly sound. The Luke 7 / Luke 17 Greek parallel (`σέσωκέν σε`) is real, not invented. **"That day, healing went out to all ten men equally"** correctly guards against implying the nine were not cleansed.

**Problem:** Tension remains after **"thy faith hath made thee whole"** and **"Only one came back far enough to hear Jesus call him whole."** A careful listener may still hear: nine got healing, one got *wholeness* — and infer the nine were spiritually deficient. You partially fix this with **"Not nine who didn't believe. Nine who didn't come back,"** but the nine *did* act in faith (they went as commanded and were cleansed *as they went*). The piece never names what the nine actually lacked: **return, gratitude, glory to God** — which is what Jesus explicitly rebukes (**"where are the nine?"** / **"returned to give glory to God"**). That leaves a faint works/gratitude-equals-salvation aftertaste.

**Problem:** For a gospel-short whose charter is **CTA-to-Jesus**, this lands on an observation, not an invitation. Jesus is present in the body but absent from the close. No doctrinal heresy — but the landing does not finish *on* Christ.

### Clarity on first hearing
**"The same words — in Luke's own Greek — he had already written for a sinful woman who once wept at those same feet: 'Thy faith hath saved thee.'"**  
On first hearing this sounds self-contradictory: you just said *same words*, then read two *different* English phrases (**"made thee whole"** vs **"saved thee"**). A listener with zero Bible background will not follow unless you spell out: *English differs; Greek is identical.* As written, it is a clever-but-confusing conceit.

**"with a single word"** (hook) — Jesus did not heal with one word. He gave a command: **"Go shew yourselves unto the priests,"** and cleansing happened **"as they went."** Hyperbolic and slightly inaccurate on first hearing.

**"save this stranger"** — KJV-archaic (*except*), embedded in a longer quote. Fine for Bible-literate ears; opaque for a stranger unless glossed.

---

### Grace-anchored conviction
No fear, gain/loss, or manufactured pressure. **"Not nine who didn't believe"** is a good grace guard.

Mild risk: **"turning all the way back to him"** can read as *you must perform gratitude to be complete* rather than *the Spirit invites return*. Invitation is implied, not voiced.

---

### Freshness = faithful depth
Strongest move: the Luke 7 / Luke 17 Greek echo — surprising about the **text**, orthodox in claim.

Weaker: the hook’s 10-vs-1 frame is the passage’s standard sermon point. Freshness lives mainly in the Greek bridge, not the hook.

---

### One thread spine
Single thread holds: **ten healed → one returned → Jesus’s question → faith/wholeness echo → receiving vs returning**.

No thread-swap.

---

### Hook score: **7/10**
**"Jesus healed ten outcasts with a single word. Only one of them turned around to say thank you."**  
Concrete, ratio-driven, not a template open. Loses points for **"single word"** ( inaccurate) and because the 10-lepers “only one returned” angle is widely known in Christian culture — less scroll-stop for that audience.

---

### Landing score: **6/10**
**"Receiving something from him, and turning all the way back to him — those are still two different things."**  
Built from *this* story’s motion (gift vs return). Not a stock **"come to Jesus"** closer.

But: **could this exact sentence end a different piece?** Yes — prodigal partial obedience, healed paralytic who never followed, generic grace-received-vs-responded pieces. It never names Jesus in the final beat and does not land as CTA-to-Jesus. Insight in the head, not in the bones.

**Under 8 on landing → REVISE per rubric.**

---

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Rewrite the Greek beat for first-hearing clarity: state explicitly that KJV renders the same Greek two ways (*saved* / *made whole*), then tie that to what “whole” means here — without implying the nine were unbelievers or only partially healed.
2. Replace **"with a single word"** with what the text actually says (command to go; cleansing **as they went**) so the hook stays punchy but accurate.
3. Rebuild the landing from this piece’s own verbs — *return*, *give glory*, *faith* — and invite the listener toward Christ in one concrete sentence; the current close is transferable to other miracle stories and stops short of a gospel landing.
