## Independent Adversarial Review — "Where Are the Nine" (Luke 17:11-19)

**KJV verbatim — ONE confirmed alteration (high severity).**
The line "**Were** there not ten cleansed? but where are the nine?" changes the KJV. Luke 17:17 reads: "**Are** there not ten cleansed? but where are the nine?" This is Jesus's immediate rhetorical question ("Are" = present-tense astonishment); swapping to past tense ("Were") is a real word change inside quotation marks and must be restored verbatim. Every other quoted clause (vv.13, 14, 15-16, 18, 19, and Luke 7:50) checks out exact, including punctuation-level integration into prose — this is the one miss.

**Doctrinal soundness.** No invented doctrine. The "same Greek word" claim ("The same words — in Luke's own Greek — he had already written for a sinful woman") is accurate: both Luke 17:19 and Luke 7:50 use the identical Greek verb *sesōken* ("hath saved"/"hath made whole") — this is a real, well-attested wordplay, not a fabricated connection. The interpretive move "Not nine who didn't believe. Nine who didn't come back" is a common, defensible evangelical reading (all ten were healed by grace; only one pursued relationship) — but it sits close to a "returning = the real faith" framing. Worth a light touch-up so it can't be misread as gratitude being the thing that completes the healing (works-adjacent), even though as written it stays on the safe side.

**Clarity on first hearing.** Clean. No logic tricks, no self-contradiction. A zero-background listener can follow the ten-lepers-one-returns arc without help.

**Grace-anchored conviction.** No fear, no gain/loss, no self-interest framing. Good.

**Thread spine.** Single thread ("turned back" / "came back") runs hook → proof → point → landing without swapping. Good.

**HOOK SCORE: 8/10.** "Jesus healed ten outcasts with a single word. Only one of them turned around to say thank you." Not a template open (no "Did you know/Imagine/What if"), states a concrete, specific tension that creates a real "why only one?" pull.

**LANDING SCORE: 7/10.** "Receiving something from him, and turning all the way back to him — those are still two different things." The middle sentence ("came back far enough to hear Jesus call him whole") is earned, tied to the KJV's own "whole." But the final sentence leans generic: "**something**" never names what was received (healing/cleansing), and "receiving from him vs. turning back to him" is a reusable gospel-motif shape that could plausibly close a different grace-vs-response piece (prodigal son, rich young ruler) without edit. It also never turns to address the listener directly ("you") — it stays entirely in third person about the ten lepers, so the CTA-to-Jesus is implicit at best, not an actual invitation.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Restore KJV verbatim in Luke 17:17: "**Are** there not ten cleansed? but where are the nine?" — currently reads "Were."
2. Rework the closing sentence to name the concrete thing received (e.g., "healed" / "cleansed") instead of "something," so the landing can't be lifted into a different piece.
3. Give the landing at least a whisper of direct address to the listener (not just third-person recap) so it functions as an actual CTA-to-Jesus, not only a closing observation.
