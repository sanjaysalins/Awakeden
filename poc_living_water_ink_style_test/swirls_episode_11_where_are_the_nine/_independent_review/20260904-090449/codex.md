# Independent review — codex (OK, 53s)

**HOOK: 6/10.** “Jesus healed ten outcasts with a single word” is punchy but factually false: Jesus gave a multiword command—“Go shew yourselves unto the priests.” “With a command” would be accurate. “Outcasts” is understandable but interpretive.

**LANDING: 7/10.** It grows from the piece’s own “turned back” movement, so it is not stock. However, “Only one came back far enough to hear Jesus call him whole” risks making physical return or gratitude the condition for wholeness. Jesus explicitly attributes it to faith: “thy faith hath made thee whole.” The final contrast can therefore sound like gain-loss moralism: nine received only healing because they failed to perform the extra step.

Specific findings:

- “The law required it” overstates the evidence. Leviticus required the ceremonially unclean person to dwell apart and outside the camp; it does not prescribe the precise posture or distance described in Luke. Say the distance was consistent with their legally required separation. [Leviticus 13:45–46 KJV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Leviticus+13%3A45-13%3A46&version=KJV)

- “They went anyway” is unclear on first hearing. “Anyway” normally signals action despite some stated obstacle, but the relevant fact—that they had not yet been cleansed—is separated into the previous sentence and may not register clearly.

- “Not nine who didn’t believe” claims more than Luke tells us. Their obedience suggests trust, but the narrator does not describe the nine’s inner spiritual state. This line also blurs the distinction between enough confidence to obey a healing command and saving faith.

- The Greek connection is the strongest fresh observation: Luke 17:19 and 7:50 use the same underlying formulation, while the KJV renders it “made thee whole” and “saved thee.” But “The same words” needs clearer explanation for listeners who have just heard two visibly different English sentences. [Luke 17:11–19 KJV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Luke+17%3A11-19&version=KJV), [Luke 7:50 KJV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Luke+7%3A50&version=KJV)

- “Only one came back far enough to hear Jesus call him whole” is clever but doctrinally slippery. It implies that returning earned or completed a second-stage blessing, whereas Christ says the man’s faith made him whole. If the intended claim is that his return manifests faith, say that rather than making distance sound causative.

- “Healing went out to all ten men equally” introduces language not found in this account. It is not heretical, but “went out” evokes other healing narratives and needlessly loosens fidelity to this text.

- The quoted KJV wording is substantively correct. Strictly construed as verbatim, however, “and with a loud voice … and fell down” changes the KJV’s verse-initial “And fell” to lowercase and joins verses 15–16 after omitting intervening words. The quotation should preserve capitalization or use separate excerpts. [Luke 17:15–16 KJV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Luke+17%3A15-16&version=KJV)

- The piece keeps one basic thread—receiving healing versus returning—but it shifts late from gratitude to salvation/wholeness without adequately establishing that theological move. The connection is plausible and orthodox, but the narration presents an inference as though it were the passage’s uncontested conclusion.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Remove the implication that returning or gratitude earned wholeness; anchor the distinction explicitly in Christ’s declaration about faith.
2. Correct “with a single word,” qualify “the law required it,” and avoid claiming certainty about the nine men’s belief.
3. Explain the identical Greek formulation clearly and make every quoted KJV excerpt strictly verbatim, including capitalization.
