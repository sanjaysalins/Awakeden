Doctrinal soundness

The Greek observation is real. Luke 17:19 and Luke 7:50 share ἡ πίστις σου σέσωκέν σε. That is not invented.

The **use** of it is. The script turns a possible sense of σῴζω into a two-gift system the passage does not state.

- **“That day, healing went out to all ten men equally. Only one came back far enough to hear Jesus call him whole.”**  
  All ten were **“cleansed.”** Jesus then says to the one who returned, **“thy faith hath made thee whole.”** The script treats those as two different gifts, with extra walking as the condition for the second. σῴζω in miracle scenes is often just “healed” (same KJV line in Mark 5:34, Matt 9:22, Luke 8:48). Linking 17:19 to 7:50 to prove the nine got skin and the one got salvation is a **contested pulpit inference**, not what Luke asserts. Freshness is being mined from that inference.

- **“Not nine who didn’t believe. Nine who didn’t come back.”**  
  Then, immediately: **“thy faith hath made thee whole.”**  
  If the nine had no faith-problem, faith cannot be the distinctive. If not-coming-back **was** the faith-problem, the denial is false. The text does not split “belief” from “return.” Jesus names **“returned to give glory to God.”** The gloss drops the glory and keeps the mileage.

- **“Jesus named exactly what was missing”** is then not what the gloss says. He named missing **glory to God**, not a missing second blessing and not acoustic range.

Not heresy. Overclaim. Do not ship it as the spine.

Clarity on first hearing

A stranger can follow ten / nine / one. They cannot follow the climax, and the open fights the body.

- **“Jesus healed ten outcasts with a single word.”**  
  He did not. He said **“Go shew yourselves unto the priests.”** They were **“still sick.”** Cleansing happened **“as they went.”** First sentence is false, and it hides the one detail that actually surprises.

- **“Only one of them turned around to say thank you.”**  
  Promises a manners story. Jesus’s charge is **“give glory to God.”** Hook and indictment are not the same thing.

- **“The same words — in Luke’s own Greek — he had already written for a sinful woman who once wept at those same feet: ‘Thy faith hath saved thee.’”**  
  You just called **“made thee whole”** and **“saved thee”** the same words. In English they are not. “Luke’s own Greek” is a seminar aside. **“he had already written”** takes **“he said”** (Jesus) and makes Jesus the author of Luke. **“those same feet”** is a different house, a different day. A listener with no Bible hears a contradiction, then a second unnamed story, then a translation note.

- **“save this stranger.”**  
  KJV *save* = *except*. After a paragraph arguing *saved / whole*, a zero-Bible ear will hear “this stranger was saved.” The quote and the claim collide.

- **“came back far enough to hear”**  
  Turns a moral return into walking distance. Cute. Wrong.
