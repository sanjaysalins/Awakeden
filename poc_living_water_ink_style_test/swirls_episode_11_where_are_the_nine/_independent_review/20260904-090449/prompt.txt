Jesus healed ten outcasts with a single word. Only one of them turned around to say thank you.

Ten lepers stood at a distance — the law required it — and cried out, "Jesus, Master, have mercy on us." He told them, "Go shew yourselves unto the priests," while every one of them was still sick. They went anyway. "And it came to pass, that, as they went, they were cleansed."

Nine kept walking toward the priests. One turned back, "and with a loud voice glorified God, and fell down on his face at his feet, giving him thanks: and he was a Samaritan."

Jesus named exactly what was missing: "Were there not ten cleansed? but where are the nine? There are not found that returned to give glory to God, save this stranger." Not nine who didn't believe. Nine who didn't come back.

To the one kneeling at his feet, he said, "Arise, go thy way: thy faith hath made thee whole." The same words — in Luke's own Greek — he had already written for a sinful woman who once wept at those same feet: "Thy faith hath saved thee."

That day, healing went out to all ten men equally. Only one came back far enough to hear Jesus call him whole. Receiving something from him, and turning all the way back to him — those are still two different things.
