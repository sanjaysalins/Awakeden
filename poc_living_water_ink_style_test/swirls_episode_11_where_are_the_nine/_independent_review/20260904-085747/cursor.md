### Doctrinal soundness — **concerns**

**1. Cleansed vs. whole as two tiers of gift (nine vs. one)**  
The payoff line — *"Ten were cleansed the moment they obeyed. One was made whole the moment he believed"* — treats cleansing and wholeness as **different outcomes for different people**. Luke does not say that. All ten were cleansed on the road (v.14). The Samaritan was among them; Luke 17:15 says he turned back **when he saw that he was healed** — after the same cleansing the nine received. Jesus’s *"thy faith hath made thee whole"* commends the one who returned; it is not stated that the nine were denied wholeness/salvation because they kept walking.

Linking 17:19 to 7:50 via Greek *sōzō* is a real textual observation. Using it to imply nine got **only** physical relief while one got something categorically more (salvation / true wholeness) is **commentary**, not stated Scripture — and it edges toward a contested reading used for freshness.

**2. “The nine got everything they asked for”**  
They cried *"have mercy on us."* Mercy in Luke’s gospel is broader than skin. Framing nine as fully satisfied while one alone received the “real” gift **undercuts Jesus’s own complaint** — *"where are the nine?"* / failure to *"return to give glory to God"* — and softens the passage’s actual rebuke into a neat two-tier theology the text does not spell out.

**3. “Cleansing is free for the asking”**  
Cleansing came **as they went** in obedience to *"Go shew yourselves unto the priests."* That is not “free for the asking” in the sense the line suggests; it required active obedience in motion. This minimizes the obedience all ten showed — including the Samaritan before he turned back.

**4. “One was made whole the moment he believed”**  
All ten acted in faith enough to obey. The Samaritan’s distinction is **return, thanks, and face-to-face worship** — not belief versus unbelief. Saying nine didn’t believe misreads the narrative: they believed enough to go; they failed to **return and give glory**.

### Clarity on first hearing — **problems**

**1. Hook misframes the text**  
*"then asked how many hadn't come back"* — Jesus asks *"where are the nine?"* (rhetorical lament), not a head-count quiz. A listener who knows the story will feel the slip; a stranger gets a slightly false picture of the scene.

**2. Luke 7 detour without context**  
*"the same word, in Luke's Greek, he'd already spoken to a sinful woman who once wept at his feet"* — Who? Which scene? Why now? For a listener with zero Bible knowledge, this is an **unexplained second episode** dropped into the CTA paragraph. *"Luke's Greek"* is a scholarly aside, not a heard image.

**3. Timeline contradiction (self-undermining)**  
The Samaritan was cleansed **with the nine** on the road, then returned. The parallel *"Ten… obeyed / One… believed"* reads as two separate tracks, as if the one skipped cleansing. A careful listener asks: wasn’t he already cleansed before he turned back?

**4. Priest gloss is dense**  
*"the priest's job was to confirm a leper already healed. They weren't yet"* — useful for insiders; for a stranger it’s a half-sentence of Levitical procedure mid-quote. Not fatal, but it slows the first pass.

Minor slip: pitting **obedience (nine)** against **belief (one)** can sound like works-vs-faith finger-wagging at the nine, even if that isn’t the intent.

### Freshness = faithful depth

**Strong:** KJV *whole* vs. *saved* for the same Greek in Luke — legitimate text-depth.  
**Weak:** That insight is **borrowed from Luke 7** and displaces the passage’s native center — **gratitude, return, and giving glory to God** — which Jesus actually emphasizes (*"returned to give glory to God"*).

The fresher honest surprise in **this** pericope is: nine received the same miracle and still didn’t come back; the outsider did. The script underplays that in favor of a word-study pivot.
