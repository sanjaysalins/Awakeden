KJV is not the problem. The commentary around it is.

---

Doctrinal soundness

The load-bearing claim is overstated, then sold as the gospel.

> “Ten were cleansed the moment they obeyed. One was made whole the moment he believed”

The passage does not teach two mechanisms. All ten went at His word (that is already faith-in-action). They were cleansed **as they went**, not “the moment they obeyed” as a separate law of works. Jesus then says to the one who returned, **“thy faith hath made thee whole”** — faith, not the walk back. The script splits what the text does not split: obedience bought skin; belief bought “whole.” That is commentary, and it is the contested pulpit two-tier (ritual/physical cleansing vs. a second, salvific gift). σῴζω in miracle scenes often means heal / make well (same KJV line in Mark 5:34, Matt 9:22, Luke 8:48). Linking Luke 17:19 to Luke 7:50 is a real Greek observation. Using it to prove the nine received only skin and the one received salvation is an inference, not what Luke states. Freshness is being mined from a disputed reading.

Worse, the close universalizes it:

> “Cleansing is free for the asking.”

They were not cleansed at the ask. They were cleansed **as they went**. Heard by a stranger, this is a promise that disease-cleansing is on tap if you request it. This episode does not teach that. Scripture does not teach that.

> “The nine got everything they asked for.”

They asked, **“have mercy on us.”** Mercy is wider than a clean skin. Jesus’s own question — **“where are the nine?”** / **“give glory to God”** — treats their absence as a real failure, not as a successful transaction. If “whole” is more than cleansing, they did **not** get everything mercy holds. The line fights the landing.

> “an outsider by birth, the very thing Jesus calls him next”

He calls him **“this stranger”** (ἀλλογενής). That is covenant-outsider language, not a birth certificate. Close, not exact. Small, but it is the same habit: the writer rounds the text toward the hook.

> “the same word, in Luke's Greek, he'd already spoken”

Luke **wrote** both sayings with ἡ πίστις σου σέσωκέν σε. Jesus did not “speak Luke’s Greek” to the woman. The script treats a narrator’s Greek as Jesus’s uttered word.

---

Clarity on first hearing

A zero-Bible listener can follow the plot. They cannot follow the climax.

The last paragraph dumps a second unnamed story plus a translation seminar:

> “the same word, in Luke's Greek, he'd already spoken to a sinful woman who once wept at his feet: ‘Thy faith hath saved thee.’”

They have just heard **“made thee whole.”** Then they are told that is “the same word” as **“saved thee.”** In English those are not the same word. Without one plain beat (“Luke wrote both with the same verb”), it sounds like a contradiction, or like a scholar whispering offstage.

**“Whole”** is never defined. Skin? Soul? Feel-complete? The invitation hangs on a word the listener cannot cash.

Hook vs. text, already in the first sentence:

> “then asked how many hadn't come back. Nine.”

He did not ask how many. He asked **“where are the nine?”** He already knew the number: **“Were there not ten cleansed?”** The punchline is location and glory, not arithmetic. The opening trains the ear on the wrong question.

**“the priest's job was to confirm a leper already healed. They weren't yet.”** — fair Leviticus 14 gloss, dense but rescued by the next quote. Not the fatal beat.

---

Grace-anchored conviction

No hell-threat. No “you’ll lose if you scroll.” Still not grace-clean.

The close is a **gain ladder**:

> “Cleansing is free for the asking. Whole is still there for anyone who turns back believing, the way he did.”

That is: take the cheap gift by asking; collect the better gift by turning back. The Spirit is supposed to convict; this script **sells an upgrade**. The hinge in the last sentence is the hearer’s move (**“turns back believing, the way he did”**), not Christ’s mercy. Imitate the Samaritan’s return, get “whole.” That is works-adjacent even with “believing” glued on.

The last person in the last sentence is **the leper** (`the way he did`), not Jesus. The model is CTA-to-Jesus. This is CTA-to-a-method.

---

Freshness / one thread

The famous fact of this story **is** the hook: ten healed, nine didn’t come back. A jaded scroller can predict it from the title. The attempted depth (cleansed vs. whole / Luke 7 Greek) is about the text, but the **claim** overreaches, which is how freshness goes bad here.

Spine wobble:

| Beat | What it is actually doing |
|---|---|
| Hook | Missing nine / how many didn’t return |
| Middle | Priest-confirm + Samaritan “stranger” |
| Landing | Two-tier gift: obey→cleansed, believe/turn back→whole |

Those are related scenes, not one thread. The outsider beat is raised (**“Luke saves that for last”**) and then dropped; the landing never uses **stranger**. The quoted burden of Jesus’s question — **give glory to God** — never becomes the close. The hook promises the nine. The payoff is a vocabulary ladder.

---

HOOK: **7 / 10**

> “Jesus healed ten men at once — then asked how many hadn't come back. Nine.”

Not a template open (not “Did you know / Imagine / What if”). Concrete number, short punch. A stranger might pause. It is still recap of the Sunday-school headline, and it misstates the question (how many vs. where). Not an 8.

LANDING: **6 / 10**

> “Whole is still there for anyone who turns back believing, the way he did.”

Not a stock “Come to Jesus / look to Jesus.” It borrows this piece’s word (**whole**) and this piece’s motion (**turned back**). Portability test still hurts: swap the noun and this is a generic repentance closer (prodigal, Peter, any “come back” text). It does not land in the bones. It offers a still-available commodity, names no Person, and rests on the contested two-tier. Under 8.

Either score under 8 forces REVISE. The doctrine and the close would force it anyway.

---

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Kill the obedience-vs-belief ladder (“cleansed the moment they obeyed” / “whole the moment he believed” / “Cleansing is free for the asking”). All ten were cleansed as they went; Jesus names faith and glory to God, not a second gift earned by returning.
2. Rewrite the last sentence as a CTA to Jesus the Giver from this scene’s own verbs (mercy, whole, faith, glory) — not “Whole is still there for anyone who turns back believing, the way he did.”
3. One spine from hook to close: “where are the nine?” (gift without the Giver). Cut or demote the Luke 7 Greek aside from the final eight seconds, and stop saying He asked “how many.”
