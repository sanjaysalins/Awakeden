Jesus healed ten men at once — then asked how many hadn't come back. Nine.

Ten lepers stood afar off and lifted up their voices: "Jesus, Master, have mercy on us." His answer: "Go shew yourselves unto the priests" — the priest's job was to confirm a leper already healed. They weren't yet. "And it came to pass, that, as they went, they were cleansed."

Nine kept going. One turned back, "and with a loud voice glorified God, and fell down on his face at his feet, giving him thanks: and he was a Samaritan." Luke saves that for last — an outsider by birth, the very thing Jesus calls him next.

Then Jesus asked it plainly: "Were there not ten cleansed? but where are the nine? There are not found that returned to give glory to God, save this stranger."

To the one at his feet, he said, "Arise, go thy way: thy faith hath made thee whole."

Whole. Ten were cleansed the moment they obeyed. One was made whole the moment he believed — the same word, in Luke's Greek, he'd already spoken to a sinful woman who once wept at his feet: "Thy faith hath saved thee." The nine got everything they asked for. Cleansing is free for the asking. Whole is still there for anyone who turns back believing, the way he did.
