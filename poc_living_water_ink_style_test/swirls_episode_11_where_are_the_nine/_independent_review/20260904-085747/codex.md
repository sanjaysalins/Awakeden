# Independent review — codex (OK, 35s)

The central interpretive move is too confident for what the passage explicitly establishes.

- “Ten were cleansed the moment they obeyed. One was made whole the moment he believed” creates a questionable obedience-versus-faith contrast. The text says all ten acted on Jesus’ command before seeing healing, while Jesus later commends the Samaritan’s faith. It does not say he only began believing when he returned. This framing risks making gratitude a second step that earns spiritual wholeness.

- “The nine got everything they asked for” is not demonstrable. They asked, “have mercy on us,” which is broader than physical cleansing. Jesus’ question about their absence suggests something remained seriously wrong in their response.

- “Cleansing is free for the asking” is awkwardly detached from the narration’s own statement that they were cleansed “as they went.” More importantly, it sounds like a universal promise of physical healing to anyone who asks—something neither this episode nor Scripture teaches.

- “Whole is still there for anyone who turns back believing” likely intends a gospel invitation, but it leaves “whole” undefined. A first-time listener could hear a promise of emotional completeness or physical health. If the claim is salvation, say so carefully and anchor it in Christ’s mercy rather than in the human act of turning back.

- “the priest’s job was to confirm a leper already healed” is broadly helpful but reductive. Priests examined people and administered the prescribed process for declaring them clean; “already healed” may confuse ceremonial cleanness with medical verification.

- “an outsider by birth, the very thing Jesus calls him next” is imprecise. “Stranger” identifies his social-religious status, not literally “the very thing” he was by birth.

- The Greek connection to the sinful woman is genuinely text-based, but “the same word” needs clarification: the parallel is between “made thee whole” and “saved thee,” not between both statements in their entirety. It also introduces a second story late, briefly weakening the ten-to-one spine.

- All words presented as biblical quotations agree with the KJV wording. The lower-case beginnings are excerpt formatting rather than altered words.

Hook score: 7/10. “Jesus healed ten men at once” is immediate and concrete, but “asked how many hadn’t come back. Nine” slightly misstates the form of Jesus’ question and gives the numerical answer before establishing why it matters.

Landing score: 6/10. It carries forward “whole” and “turns back,” so it is not wholly generic, but the final sentence turns the episode into an underqualified formula and does not land with a clear, grace-centered invitation.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Remove the unsupported contrast between obedience and belief, and do not imply that returning gratefully earned a second blessing.
2. Replace “Cleansing is free for the asking” with a claim this passage actually supports, avoiding any promise of automatic physical healing.
3. Define “whole/saved” clearly and make the landing rest on Jesus’ mercy and the Samaritan’s faith rather than a formula of “turning back.”
