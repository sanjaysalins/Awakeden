### Doctrinal soundness
**No invented doctrine, no heresy.** The body stays inside Luke 17: obedience before visible healing, ten cleansed, one returns, Jesus names the missing nine, faith/sozo spoken to the one at his feet. That is standard evangelical exposition.

The **doctrinal fight is not in the body — it is in what the CTA does with v.19**, and the panel’s earlier “two-tier gift” objection was **correct** when prior Camp B drafts implied:

- the nine were spiritually shortchanged, or  
- returning/thanksgiving **earned** wholeness beyond cleansing.

Luke 17:19 attributes wholeness to **faith**, not to the performance of gratitude. Any landing that makes “go back and say thanks” the **mechanism** that unlocks a deeper tier repeats a works-adjacent error — even if unintentionally.

---

### Clarity on first hearing
**Strong.** A listener with zero Bible background can follow:

- ten sick men sent away unhealed → healed on the road → nine keep going, one returns → Jesus asks where the nine are → speaks “whole” to the one on his face.

No logic trick, no self-contradiction. The only mild friction: **“Whatever he's already done for you”** is grammatically clear but **theologically vague** — “he” is Jesus by context, but “done” could mean cleansing, mercy, or salvation without the ear being told which.

---

### Grace-anchored conviction
**Mostly yes in the body; thinner in the landing.**

Good grace signals:

- `"before a single one of them was healed"` — gift precedes visible result  
- `"They obeyed before they could see any difference"` — faith/obedience, not payoff chasing  
- `"Whatever he's already done for you"` — antecedent mercy, not “do this to get healed”

Weak spot:

- `"go back and say so, to his face"` frames the close as a **human speech act** (say thank you) rather than **receiving Christ’s word**. That is not full works-framing — “already done” blocks the worst of it — but it **does** tilt behavioral: the implied success condition is *your* returning speech, not *His* naming you whole.

No fear/gain-loss/manipulation. Clean on that axis.

### Freshness = faithful depth
**Good in the setup, under-used at the close.**

Fresh, text-grounded moves:

- healing **after** obedience, **on the road**  
- Jesus voicing the scandal: `"where are the nine?"`  
- Samaritan/stranger detail preserved

Missed depth at the end: the piece **quotes** `"thy faith hath made thee whole"` then **does not inhabit it** in the CTA. The most surprising textual hinge in the pericope — *sozo* spoken **at his feet**, tied to **faith** — is dropped for a gratitude instruction. That is not contrarian freshness; it is **under-using the text’s own climax**.

### Landing score: **6/10**
`"Whatever he's already done for you — go back and say so, to his face."`

**Text-specific wins:** mirrors `"turned back"`, `"fell down on his face at his feet"`, `"returned to give glory"`. Not a stock “come to Jesus / turn to Him” closer. Camp A’s 7–8 is defensible on those grounds.

**Why it still caps at 6:**

2. The body’s climax is **Christ-centered speech** (`"thy faith hath made thee whole"`). The CTA is **listener-centered speech** (`"say so"`). For a channel whose charter is **CTA-to-Jesus**, this is the wrong final subject even when doctrine is safe.  
3. Camp B’s “moralistic” charge is overstated — but **“go back and say so”** without “hear Him name you whole” **does** make behavior the last image in the ear.

Per your rubric: landing under 8 → **REVISE**.

## Direct answer to the panel question (Camp A vs Camp B vs synthesis)

**This is not a genuine either/or.** Camp B’s failed drafts and Camp A’s doctrinal caution are **both right about different things**:

| Camp | Right about | Wrong if pushed to |
|------|-------------|-------------------|
| **A** | Return/gratitude framing is in the text; “already done” keeps grace; avoid two-tier | Treating thank-you as the **terminal** gospel move |
| **B** | Landing should **weight Christ + wholeness + faith**, not only human gratitude | Contrasting **cleansed vs whole** as two ranked gifts the nine “missed” |

**The escape hatch:** make the CTA about **returning to the feet to receive Christ’s word**, not about **returning to earn a better package than the nine got**.

Narratively safe distinction (not two-tier doctrine):

- All ten were **cleansed on the road** (stated fact).  
- Only one was **at Jesus’ feet** to hear **“thy faith hath made thee whole.”**  
- That is a **proximity / encounter** truth, not proof the nine were damned or spiritually cheated.  
- Attribute wholeness to **faith** (v.19’s own grammar), and treat return as **where faith meets Christ and hears Him** — not as the currency that purchases sozo.

**Do not** write: “They got cleansing; he got wholeness” as a **comparative reward ladder**.  
**Do** write: “He fell at the feet where Jesus **names** faith whole.”

**Recommendation:** **Synthesize — lean Camp B’s Christ/wholeness weight, keep Camp A’s grace/return imagery, explicitly block works framing.** Keep the current ending only if you accept a gratitude-first close; for this project’s stated CTA-to-Jesus charter, **revise the final paragraph only.**

### Synthesized rewrite — **final paragraph only**

Replace the last sentence block with something in this shape (adjust length to fit the cut):

> Nine men had exactly what they'd asked for, and kept walking. One stayed at Jesus's feet until he heard it: "Arise, go thy way: thy faith hath made thee whole." However far he's already brought you — go back to his face. Not to pay him back. To fall where faith hears Christ name you whole.

**Why this thread holds without two-tier:**

- **Christ’s word** is the last weight, not `"say so"`.  
- **Faith**, not gratitude-performance, is the named agent (mirrors v.19).  
- **"Not to pay him back"** blocks works/advance-tier reading.  
- **"At his face / at his feet"** keeps Camp A’s return imagery.  
- Does **not** claim the nine lacked cleansing or were spiritually defrauded — only that the **gospel-shaped encounter** is at **His** feet, hearing **His** sentence.

If you need it shorter for time:

> One fell at his feet and heard what the nine never stopped for: "thy faith hath made thee whole." Whatever mercy he's already shown you — go back to his face. Not to repay him. To hear Christ name faith whole.

Use **“never stopped for”** (missed encounter), not **“never received”** (two-tier payout) — one word of difference, large doctrinal gap.
