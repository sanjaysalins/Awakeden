# Independent review — codex (OK, 38s)

The two camps are not irreconcilable. The solution is to make returning and gratitude the response to mercy, while explicitly denying that they purchase a second-tier gift. Do not build the contrast on *cleansed* versus *whole*: *sōzō* has a broad semantic range, and Luke 17:19 itself assigns the outcome to faith, not gratitude.

Hook: 8/10. “Jesus told ten men with leprosy to go — before a single one of them was healed” creates immediate tension without using a template opening.

Landing: 7/10. “Go back and say so, to his face” is strongly tied to “turned back,” “giving him thanks,” and “fell down on his face.” It is not a stock closer. But its final imperative makes the listener’s act of thanking—not Christ and his mercy—the grammatical climax. “To his face” may also sound literally impossible to a first-time listener unless prayer is intended.

Specific findings:

- All quoted wording is KJV-verbatim. I find no altered word.
- The doctrine is sound overall. “They obeyed before they could see any difference” is a reasonable narration of “as they went,” not an invented mechanism by which obedience earned healing.
- “Nine men had exactly what they’d asked for” is slightly imprecise: they asked for mercy, not specifically cleansing. The sentence also risks making the nine’s physical outcome the story’s complete theological verdict.
- Camp B’s proposed cleansing/wholeness contrast should be rejected if it claims two objectively different gifts. The text does not say gratitude earned what the nine lacked.
- The current CTA is not heretical or plainly works-based, but “go back and say so” can be heard as a behavioral takeaway: *Jesus helped you, so repay him with thanks.* That is weaker than the passage’s movement toward Jesus himself.
- The safest Christ-centered emphasis is not “the thankful man earned more,” but “faith did not merely take the benefit and disappear; it brought the recipient to Jesus’ feet.” Even that should be expressed carefully, since the nine’s spiritual condition is not fully disclosed.

Recommended replacement for only the final paragraph:

> All ten received mercy on the road. The Samaritan did not return to earn a greater gift; he turned back to Jesus, fell at his feet, and heard, “thy faith hath made thee whole.” Do not make your gratitude the price of his mercy. Turn back to the Giver—and at Jesus’s feet, give God the glory.

This keeps the passage’s distinctive verbs and physical image, puts Jesus at the center, preserves gratitude, and explicitly closes the works-adjacent loophole.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Replace the final paragraph with a Christ-centered CTA that explicitly presents gratitude as a response to mercy, never its price.
2. Do not claim that “cleansed” and “whole” establish two different tiers of salvation or blessing.
3. Replace or clarify “exactly what they’d asked for,” since their stated request was for mercy rather than cleansing specifically.
