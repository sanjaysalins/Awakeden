**Direct answer to the question:**

This is not a genuine either/or. The two camps are actually catching two different things, and only one of them is a real doctrinal landmine:

- Camp A's objection to rounds 2-5 was correct and should stand: any version that says *"turn back like he did, and you'll be made whole too"* ties a soteriological outcome (wholeness/sozo) to an act (returning). That's a conditional promise structure — do X, receive Y — and it directly contradicts the text's own attribution: v.19 says "thy **faith** hath made thee whole," not "thy returning." That's not a style problem, it's sola fide vs. works-adjacent, and it's worth protecting even at the cost of four failed rounds.
- Camp B's complaint, stripped of the specific "two-tier gift" mechanism that kept failing, is still legitimate: the current landing's *command* is a virtue (gratitude), not a Person. That's fixable without touching the sozo contrast at all, and separately, the sozo contrast IS fixable — as long as it's stated as a fact about the text (what Jesus called each group) rather than a mechanism offered to the listener (what the listener will get if they do the same act).

The move that resolves both without reopening the works trap: name the cleansed/whole distinction as something Jesus *said*, not something the return *earned*, and then point the CTA at the Person, not at the performance of thanks.

**Synthesized rewrite of the final paragraph only:**

> Nine men had exactly what they'd asked for, and kept walking. One turned back, saw who had done it, and fell at his feet. Jesus didn't just call him cleansed, like the rest — he called him whole. Whatever he's already done for you, don't stop at the gift. Go back and find the one who gave it — to his face.

Why this should survive where rounds 2-5 didn't: it never tells the listener that returning produces wholeness — it reports what Jesus said to the one man, past tense, as a textual fact, and the CTA that follows ("go find the one who gave it") is aimed at the Giver, not offered as a mechanism to acquire an upgrade. It keeps both of Camp A's proven anchors ("fell at his feet," "to his face") and gives Camp B the Christ-directed weight it wanted.
