**The Camp A vs. Camp B Dilemma: A Direct Recommendation**

Camp B is entirely correct in their critique of the current ending. "Whatever he's already done for you — go back and say so, to his face" reduces the climax of the story to simple manners. It is behavioral and moralistic, turning a Gospel encounter into a lesson on etiquette rather than a revelation of Christ. 

However, Camp A is also correct on the doctrine: treating Luke 17:19’s *sozo* (made whole) as a "bonus prize" earned by the *act of returning* introduces works-righteousness. It implies that gratitude is a transactional currency that buys a second tier of salvation.

**The Synthesis:**
You solve this by changing the focus of the CTA. The tragedy of the nine isn't merely that they lacked gratitude; it's that they settled for the gift and completely missed the Giver. The triumph of the Samaritan isn't that his manners earned him a second blessing; it's that he realized Jesus *Himself* was better than the healing. 

By shifting the lens from "saying thank you" (our action) to "finding Christ" (the ultimate prize), you achieve Camp B's Christ-centered gravity without triggering Camp A's two-tier-gift alarm.

**Recommended Rewrite for the Final Paragraph:**
"Nine men had exactly what they'd asked for, and kept walking. Only one turned back, realizing the Healer was better than the cure. Don't just take what He's done for you and walk away—turn back, and fall at His feet."

**Scores:**
- **SCORE THE HOOK: 8.** "Jesus told ten men with leprosy to go — before a single one of them was healed." This is an excellent, text-based curiosity gap that drops the viewer right into the tension.
- **SCORE THE LANDING: 6** (for the *current* draft). "Go back and say so" feels like a behavioral checklist rather than a spiritual conviction. (The recommended rewrite would elevate this to an 8+ by tying the physical act of turning back directly to recognizing Christ's supremacy).

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Replace the moralistic "say so, to his face" CTA with the synthesized approach that contrasts settling for the gift vs. returning to the Giver.
2. Retain the flawless KJV integration and strong hook, which the panel correctly praised.
