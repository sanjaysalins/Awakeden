2 Kings 5: Naaman was a great Syrian general, honored and mighty, and a leper. A servant girl in his own house, taken captive from Israel, told his wife about a prophet who could heal him.

Naaman traveled to Elisha's door with silver, gold, and letters from his king. Elisha did not even come outside. He sent a single instruction through a messenger: "Go and wash in the Jordan seven times, and thy flesh shall come again to thee, and thou shalt be clean."

Naaman was furious. "Are not Abana and Pharpar, rivers of Damascus, better than all the waters of Israel? may I not wash in them, and be clean?" His own servants talked him down: "My father, if the prophet had bid thee do some great thing, wouldest thou not have done it? how much rather then, when he saith to thee, Wash, and be clean?"

He went down. He dipped himself seven times in Jordan. His flesh came again like the flesh of a little child.

Centuries later, Jesus stood up in his own hometown synagogue and told this exact story, naming Naaman by name. His neighbors were so offended they tried to throw him off a cliff.

The best thing Naaman ever did was lose that argument. If the cure God offers you feels too plain, too easy, too small, that is the same argument he almost lost his healing to. It is still worth losing.
