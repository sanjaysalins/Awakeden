Jesus told his hometown one old story — and they tried to throw him off a cliff. This is the story.

Naaman: a great general — and a leper. A servant girl pointed him to a prophet. Elisha didn't even come out. He sent one line: wash in the Jordan, seven times.

Naaman was furious:

"Are not Abana and Pharpar, rivers of Damascus, better than all the waters of Israel? may I not wash in them, and be clean?"

His own servants stopped him:

"My father, if the prophet had bid thee do some great thing, wouldest thou not have done it? how much rather then, when he saith to thee, Wash, and be clean?"

He went down. He washed, seven times. New skin, like a child's.

This is the story Jesus told — centuries later, to his own hometown:

"Many lepers were in Israel in the time of Eliseus the prophet; and none of them was cleansed, saving Naaman the Syrian."

The best thing Naaman ever did was lose an argument. If the cure feels beneath you — too plain, too easy, too small — that's the same argument. It's still worth losing.
