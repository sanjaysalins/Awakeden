# Independent review — grok (OK, 181s)

This kit is not ready to upload. The KJV lines in quotation marks check out. The title and the YouTube body overclaim the history, and they soften what Jesus actually said.

**Title (YouTube Short, YouTube Long, and the same idea on Facebook): "A Queen Crossed a Desert to Test a Rumor. Jesus Noticed."**
- "Crossed a Desert" is not in 1 Kings 10 or Matthew 12:42. The text says she came from Sheba with camels (1 Kings 10:2) and "from the uttermost parts of the earth" (Matthew 12:42). A desert crossing is an added location.
- "to Test a Rumor" aims the test at the wrong thing. 1 Kings 10:1 says she came "to prove him with hard questions" after she heard "the fame of Solomon concerning the name of the LORD." The KJV words are "fame" and "report." "Rumor" is a harsher hook. Option 3 in this same kit ("The Queen Who Asked Solomon Her Hardest Questions") matches the verse. The chosen title does not.
- "Jesus Noticed" is the bait. Matthew 12:42 is not Jesus noticing a traveler. He says the queen of the south will rise in the judgment with that generation, and "behold, a greater than Solomon is here." The in-engine note calls "Jesus Noticed" honest because he "referred to her." That bar is too low. The christological line is already option 4. The description does deliver "a greater than Solomon is here," so this is a soft bait-and-switch, not a different video. It still should not ship as the title.

**YouTube Short and YouTube Long description**
- Same page says both "A queen crossed a desert" and "She crossed a continent to check a king's wisdom." Those cannot both be the text. "Continent" is the worse overclaim. Sheba-to-Jerusalem is not "a continent" on the biblical data. Matthew's phrase is "the uttermost parts of the earth."
- "to find out if Jesus is real" promises a proof the short does not have. She checked a report about Solomon's wisdom. The fair ask, already in the copy, is "Bring Him your hardest question."
- The 1 Kings 10:6-7 quotation matches the KJV words it includes: "It was a true report... the half was not told me." It is cut before "thy wisdom and prosperity exceedeth the fame which I heard" and is still cited as all of 1 Kings 10:6-7. No word is swapped. It needs an ellipsis, or it reads as the whole two verses. Matthew 12:42's "behold, a greater than Solomon is here" is exact KJV. Leave that wording alone.
- TikTok's "the half was not told her" is reported speech, not a marked quotation. Instagram's "a greater than Solomon is here (Matthew 12:42)" matches the KJV phrase.

**Long-form block and tags**
- Format is marked "short," but the YouTube Long title, description, and tags are a clone of the short. The long-form tags still include "bible shorts." That keyword does not belong on a 16:9 upload.
- Other tags are real and on-topic ("queen of sheba", "1 kings 10", "matthew 12 42", "greater than solomon"). "hard questions" is in 1 Kings 10:1, so it is fair, just a weak search term. This is not tag-stuffing.
- YouTube has no hashtag box. The five hashtags sit outside the description, so a straight paste of the description ships with none. TikTok (5), Facebook (3), and Instagram (8) counts are sane. Instagram's extra "#Faith" is generic filler. "#SwirlsOfLife" is the series name, not a search term.

- CTA is present and Christ-pointing: "Bring Him your hardest question" and "Find Jesus in the whole Bible, one panel at a time." Links are intact: `https://youtube.com/@awakeden` and `https://awakeden.com`. Footer is not mangled.
