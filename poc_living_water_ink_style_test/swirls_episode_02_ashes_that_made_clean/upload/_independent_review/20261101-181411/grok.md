## False Hebrews line

YouTube (Short and Long-form), same sentence:

> Hebrews says those ashes could only point forward, to the blood of Christ who can "purge your conscience..."

Hebrews 9:13 says the ashes of the heifer sanctify "to the purifying of the flesh." Hebrews 9:14 says the blood of Christ does the greater work. Hebrews does not say the ashes "could only point forward." The word "only" wipes out the flesh-cleansing the verse affirms.

TikTok, first caption:

> Hebrews 9 says the ashes pointed forward to Christ, who can purge your conscience from dead works.

Instagram, first caption:

> Hebrews 9 says the ashes pointed forward to Christ.

On Instagram that is the only claim tied to Hebrews, and it is the part Hebrews does not say. The real KJV words are missing there.

Facebook is cleaner. It states "The ashes of the red heifer pointed forward" in the narrator's voice, then quotes Hebrews 9:14 for the purge line. Still a typology stated as bare fact. It is not a false citation.

The quoted fragment "purge your conscience from dead works to serve the living God" is exact KJV (Hebrews 9:14). Leave those words alone.

## YouTube opening vs the title

Title (all three title fields):

> Why Touching the Dead Made You Unclean for a Week

That matches Numbers 19:11, seven days. Keep it.

YouTube description, first lines:

> Not sin, just closeness to a grave. By nightfall the law calls him unclean.

Numbers 19:11 and 19:16 say the person who **touches** a dead body, a bone, or a grave is unclean **seven days**. "Closeness" is softer than touch. "By nightfall" is the other rule in the same chapter. "Until the even" is for the priest, the burner, the ash-gatherer, and the sprinkler (Numbers 19:7-10, 19:21). It is not the rule for the person who touched the dead. The title says a week. The hook says nightfall. Same paste, two clocks.

Same description:

> You do not have to have sinned to feel unclean.

Numbers 19 gives a legal status. It does not describe a feeling. Alt title 3, "You Don't Have to Sin to Feel Unclean," is the bait version of this line. Do not switch to it.

## The heifer sentence mis-teaches the rite

YouTube:

> Someone else had to burn a red heifer to ash and mix it with water, and that person became unclean too, just for making it.

Numbers 19:9: the heifer is burned once, and the ashes are **kept** for the congregation. They are mixed with running water later, when someone is sprinkled (19:17). Burning and mixing are not one act, and not one person, and not a new heifer for each man who touched a body. The people who handle the rite are unclean until evening, a shorter uncleanness than the seven days. "Unclean too, just for making it" flattens that.

## "He did not become unclean"

Every platform states this as fact:

> He touched what makes us unclean and did not become unclean, so that we could finally be clean.

The kit's own red-team says this is not cited, then passes it. Mark 1:41 is a leper. Luke 7:14 is the bier. The stronger texts are Mark 5:41 and Luke 8:54, where he takes a dead girl's hand. None of those verses say he "did not become unclean." "Finally" also steps past Hebrews 9:13, where the ashes did purify the flesh. Soften this to a cited Gospel line, or drop "finally."

The question "Ready to let Him make you clean?" is a real Christ invitation. It is only on YouTube. TikTok and Instagram end on "Follow Awakeden."
