# Independent review — codex (OK, 68s)

The kit needs revision. Its “ALL PASS” certification overlooks textual errors and platform problems. Video fidelity remains unverified: the supplied metadata alone cannot establish what the MP4 delivers.

1. **YouTube Short + Long — “Hebrews says those ashes could only point forward” misstates Hebrews.** Hebrews 9:13 acknowledges actual ceremonial purification; verse 14 contrasts that with Christ cleansing the conscience. “Only” erases the ashes’ stated function. [Hebrews 9:13–14, KJV](https://www.bible.com/bible/1/HEB.9.13-14.KJV)

2. **YouTube Short + Long — “Not sin, just closeness to a grave. By nightfall the law calls him unclean.”** This substitutes vague proximity for the chapter’s specified contact and tent situations, and invents a delayed onset. Contact causes uncleanness; nightfall is relevant to particular cleansing provisions. [Numbers 19:11–19, KJV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Numbers+19&version=KJV)

3. **YouTube Short + Long — “Someone else had to burn a red heifer to ash and mix it with water, and that person became unclean too.”** This collapses distinct roles and stages into one person and suggests a fresh sacrifice for each contaminated individual. The chapter provides stored communal ashes, distinguishes burning from gathering, and later describes preparation and sprinkling. [Numbers 19:7–10, 17–19](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Numbers+19&version=KJV)

4. **YouTube Short + Long — “Living close to death can leave a stain water alone cannot reach.”** This risks telling bereaved people that proximity to death spiritually stains them. The surrounding “feel unclean” language merges ceremonial status, emotional distress and guilty conscience. Mark the application as an analogy; do not make grief itself a spiritual contaminant.

5. **YouTube/Facebook titles — “Why Touching the Dead Made You Unclean for a Week.”** “Why” promises an explanation of the rule. The supplied descriptions give the rule and a Christological application without explaining its rationale. Check whether the video actually answers that question. Also, seven days is not an automatic expiry: Numbers 19:12 explicitly conditions cleansing on the prescribed purification. [Numbers 19:11–12](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Numbers+19&version=KJV)

6. **Instagram — eight hashtags, ending “#SwirlsOfLife #BibleStudy #Faith.”** This exceeds Instagram’s announced five-hashtag limit for posts/Reels. “UK-G5 … hashtag counts … OK” is therefore unreliable. [Instagram limit reporting and linked announcement](https://www.storycut.com/blog/instagram-hashtag-limit)

7. **TikTok/Instagram/Facebook — “Find Jesus … Follow Awakeden.”** This directs the requested action toward the account. The explicit Jesus invitation, “Ready to let Him make you clean?”, appears only on YouTube. TikTok and Instagram also spend their opening words on “Numbers 19.” before reaching the hook, weakening the first line.

8. **YouTube Long — “bible shorts” tag under “Long-form 16:9.”** That is mismatched search targeting. The kit identifies only a short asset and provides no separate landscape export. A duplicated metadata block does not establish a long-form deliverable.

9. **Title options — “A Heifer Burned to Ash Could Not Clean Itself.”** This creates a contrived puzzle about a dead animal cleaning itself. It obscures the actual subject: purification of people. “You Don't Have to Sin to Feel Unclean” likewise shifts the promise toward emotional shame rather than the stated Numbers 19 exposition.

The quoted Numbers 19:11 and Hebrews 9:14 excerpt match KJV wording. Footer URLs are visibly intact. However, “UK-G7 … plain ASCII” contradicts the copied “✝”, and sibling-title uniqueness is unsupported by the supplied artifact.
