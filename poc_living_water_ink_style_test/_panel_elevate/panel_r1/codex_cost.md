A. TOP 5 MOVES
1. Release a small, playback-checked test batch now. Thirty unpublished episodes compound decisions with no audience evidence.
2. Cap attempts per page and log active minutes plus spend. Seventy saved rejects signal uncontrolled rework; the ledger cannot price the creator’s time.
3. Test adding titles, numbers and captions in editing. Stop paying image models to repeatedly repair lettering.
4. Approve hard-prop geometry before building dependent pages. A bad reference can spread an expensive defect through the episode.
5. Fix playback and pacing before adding machinery. An unresolved cutoff and a ten-second opening hold deserve attention before another review panel.

B. DRAFT GRADES
1. CHANGE — Separate cheap deterministic checks from visual judgment. No evidence shows reliable automatic detection of these visual defects.
2. CHANGE — Keep approved geometry and an attempt limit. A side-on reference is unproven across camera angles; validate before chaining it everywhere.
3. DROP — A cheaper model’s composition may not transfer. This adds a bridge operation and another approval stage without demonstrated savings.
4. CHANGE — Reuse the motion checks already learned. Pixel changes do not establish semantic correctness, and a push-in may destroy an action beat.
5. CHANGE — Keep the existing narration process and add a lightweight ending comparison. Another mandatory panel has no demonstrated marginal value.
6. KEEP — Seek the owner’s decision to release two or three checked episodes. Treat 48-hour results as initial observations, not a dependable verdict.
7. CHANGE — Queue independent, approved work in small batches. Whole-episode batching can multiply a defective reference before anyone intervenes.

C. WHAT THE DRAFT MISSED
The scarce resource is the creator’s supervised time. The proposal measures neither active minutes nor approval interruptions, then adds gates, drafts and panels. It could reduce render spend while making every episode slower. Define an acceptable page and a stopping rule before automating more attempts.

D. WRONG CLAIMS
- Saved SUPERSEDED files are not comparable failure rates. Older folders may omit rejects, and attempt denominators are missing.
- Rework volume does not establish dollar savings. Ep8 cost more than ep13 despite ep13’s conspicuous reject pile.
- “$0 push-in” means no additional generation charge. Editing, checking and possible storytelling damage remain.
- Automatic checks cannot be assumed to catch garbled captions, disappearing figures or wrong geometry reliably.
- Nothing supplied establishes Fable’s benefit over the existing narration process.
- The deterministic-gate milestone remains unmet if “shipped” means published. Twelve finished pieces do not prove clean delivery.
- Forty-eight hours of data may be too sparse to distinguish episode quality from distribution variation.
