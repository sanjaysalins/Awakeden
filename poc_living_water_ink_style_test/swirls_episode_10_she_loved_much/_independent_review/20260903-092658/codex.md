# Independent review — codex (OK, 64s)

No blocking defects remain.

- Doctrinal soundness: “Forgiven first — love was the receipt, not the price” faithfully captures the parable’s logic: forgiveness produces love; love does not purchase forgiveness. “Thy faith hath saved thee” keeps the saving instrument explicit.
- KJV verbatim: Every quoted word matches Luke 7:37–50 KJV. The ellipses omit material without altering the remaining wording. [Luke 7:36–50 KJV](https://www.bible.com/bible/1/LUK.7.36-50)
- Clarity: “Simon thought it, not out loud” resolves the former contradiction. “What Simon called her — a sinner” now clearly identifies the burden as a condemning identity, not the alabaster box.
- Grace: The narration rejects payment-by-tears, ointment, or love and anchors salvation in faith and freely granted forgiveness.
- Spine: The neglected hospitality, her three acts, the two debts, Simon’s judgment, and the box/peace contrast form one continuous thread.
- Minor polish only: “Simon had seen the sin. Never the woman” is rhetorically absolute—he physically saw her—but its intended meaning, that he saw only her category, is immediately understandable.

HOOK: 8/10. The concrete three-beat opening creates an immediate unanswered question and quickly supplies the social conflict. This is not a lingering defect; substantially exceeding it would likely require a different hook concept, not another line edit.

LANDING: 9/10. “She came in carrying an alabaster box. She left carrying his peace” grows directly from this story’s carrying image and Jesus’ own final command. It could not naturally close an unrelated piece.

VERDICT: PASS
TOP FIXES:
1. No blocking fix; retain the grace-before-love logic.
2. Optional polish: soften “Never the woman” if literal-minded listeners are a concern.
3. Optional performance note: pause after “Forgiven first” and before the final box/peace contrast.
