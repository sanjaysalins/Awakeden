## Independent adversarial review — Luke 7:36-50 narration

### Doctrinal soundness — mostly sound, one structural risk

The piece clearly *intends* orthodox grace: the parable’s “nothing to pay / frankly forgave,” plus **“Forgiven first — nothing to pay. Love came after: the receipt, not the price”** and the faith landing, all push against works-righteousness. That is the right fight for this pericope.

The problem is **order of delivery**. You quote Jesus’s most dangerous line in isolation:

> **“Her sins, which are many, are forgiven; for she loved much.”**

On first hearing, a stranger hears standard English **“for” = because**. For ~3 seconds the piece *sounds* like forgiveness earned by love — exactly the backwards causality the brief warns about. The correction comes *after* the damage. Worse, you omit the verse’s own built-in guardrail:

> **“but to whom little is forgiven, the same loveth little.”** (Luke 7:47, KJV)

That second clause is the text’s own proof that love tracks forgiven-debt size, not the other way around. Leaving it out forces you into a post-quote apology (“receipt, not the price”) instead of letting Scripture carry the logic.

Secondary clarity/doctrine tension: **“Forgiven first… Love came after”** is true *theologically* (parable order), but **false on the story clock** — she weeps, kisses, and anoints *before* Jesus declares forgiveness aloud. A careful listener can hear self-contradiction: “Love came after, but the whole opening was her love acts?” You rescue this only at the very end with **“Thy faith hath saved thee”** — late for a 60s short.

No invented doctrine, no clickbait reading. But the 7:47 handling is a real stumble, not a nit.

Missed freshness: Jesus’s actual pivot question **“Seest thou this woman?”** (Luke 7:44) never appears — you paraphrase its force well, but the line itself is the episode’s title-image (“She Loved Much”) and Simon’s blindness beat. Omitting it is a wasted textual gift.

Stain motif (series brief): **“a sinner”** appears twice via quote; **“Simon had seen the sin”** supports visual handoff without over-explaining. Adequate for pre-lock narration.

But it lands on *her* arc, not the listener’s bones. There is no second-person invitation; the closing image is observational poetry, not gospel pivot. **“Thy faith”** does the doctrinal work, yet the final sentence the ear remembers is the alabaster/peace couplet — beautiful, not convicting. VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. **Reorder Luke 7:47 handling:** State parable logic first (“nothing to pay → forgiven → then love”), then quote **7:47** *with* the second clause **“but to whom little is forgiven, the same loveth little”** so the text itself blocks works-righteousness before the listener mishears “for she loved much” as “because.”
2. **Resolve narrative-time vs grace-time:** One plain bridge line after the parable — e.g. her acts are love *showing* faith/grace already received, not the price paid — so **“Love came after”** doesn’t fight the scene the listener just heard.
3. **Strengthen the landing above observational bookend:** Keep the alabaster/peace image but tie **“Thy faith hath saved thee; go in peace”** to the listener (peace offered on the same terms — faith, not performance) so the final beat invites, not only describes her exit.
