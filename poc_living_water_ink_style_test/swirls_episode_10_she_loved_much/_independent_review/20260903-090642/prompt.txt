The man who invited Jesus to dinner gave him nothing a host owes. No water for his feet. No kiss. No oil. The woman nobody invited brought all three.

"A woman in the city, which was a sinner... stood at his feet behind him weeping, and began to wash his feet with tears, and did wipe them with the hairs of her head, and kissed his feet, and anointed them with the ointment."

Simon said nothing out loud: "This man, if he were a prophet, would have known... for she is a sinner." Jesus answered the sentence Simon never said. Two debtors, five hundred pence and fifty: "when they had nothing to pay, he frankly forgave them both." Frankly — that's freely. "Which of them will love him most?" Simon knew: "he, to whom he forgave most."

Jesus turned to her, and asked Simon: "Seest thou this woman?" Simon had seen the sin. Never the woman. "Her sins, which are many, are forgiven; for she loved much."

Forgiven first — nothing to pay. Love came after: the receipt, not the price. The table wondered: "Who is this that forgiveth sins also?" He didn't answer them. He answered her: "Thy faith hath saved thee; go in peace." Not thy tears. Not thy ointment. Thy faith. She came in carrying an alabaster box. She left carrying his peace.
