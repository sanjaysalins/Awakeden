# Independent review — codex (OK, 127s)

REVISE. The theological center is sound and the quoted wording is lexically KJV, but the draft fails the brief’s structural requirements and both scoring gates.

- Doctrinally, “Love came after: the receipt, not the price” correctly rejects forgiveness earned through love. The debtor parable and “Thy faith hath saved thee” support love as the evidence or fruit of forgiveness.

- However, “Forgiven first” converts theological priority into an asserted chronology. Luke does not narrate when she first received forgiveness; Jesus announces it after her actions. This risks using a contested reconstruction when the safer claim is simply that love did not purchase forgiveness.

- “Receipt” is also an unnecessarily transactional image. It can suggest her conduct supplied proof sufficient to validate the transaction—the very works-shaped implication the paragraph is trying to exclude.

- The locked Stain motif is absent. The script instead cycles through hospitality, debt, sight, receipt/price, and box/carrying. These ideas relate, but no single governing image runs from hook through landing. For a series built around visible dead ink, this episode currently sounds detachable from the series.

- “The woman nobody invited” invents a fact. Luke says that Simon invited Jesus and that the woman entered after learning Jesus was there; it never states that nobody invited her.

- “Brought all three” is literally inaccurate. She brought ointment; her tears supplied the washing and she gave the kiss. A listener unfamiliar with the passage will not necessarily decode this compression.

- “Two debtors, five hundred pence and fifty” omits that these are amounts they “owed” and omits the creditor. Consequently, “he frankly forgave them both” and “Which of them will love him most?” have unclear antecedents on first hearing.

- “Jesus answered the sentence Simon never said” contradicts the text’s wording: Simon “spake within himself, saying.” The intended point is that he never said it aloud.

- “The table wondered” briefly makes the furniture the actor. Luke identifies “they that sat at meat with him”; plain narration should retain the human subject.

- “Simon had seen the sin. Never the woman” is rhetorically effective but logically slippery: Simon plainly saw her in verse 39. Jesus’ “Seest thou this woman?” exposes his failure to perceive her rightly, not literal failure to see her.

- KJV audit: there are no substituted lexical words inside the quotation marks. All retained wording agrees with [KJV Luke 7:36–50](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Luke+7%3A27-50&version=KJV). Several quotations are fragments with omitted surrounding words, but the ellipses generally signal that compression.

- Hook: **7/10.** The hospitality reversal is concrete and non-template, but the first five seconds contain only the weaker claim that the host gave Jesus “nothing a host owes.” The sharper sentence arrives later and rests on the unsupported “nobody invited” plus the imprecise “brought all three.” Under the stated rule, this score requires revision.

- Landing: **6/10.** “She came in carrying an alabaster box. She left carrying his peace” is specific and memorable, so it is not a stock “come to Jesus” closer. But it is not a CTA, does not invite the listener anywhere, does not clear the locked Stain, and introduces another image instead of resolving the hook or “She Loved Much.” Under the stated rule, this also requires revision.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Replace the unproved chronology in “Forgiven first” with an unequivocal grace-first causal claim: her love evidences forgiveness; it does not earn it.
2. Make Stain the single spine from hook through a genuinely invitational, passage-specific landing.
3. Remove unsupported or confusing compression: “nobody invited,” “brought all three,” the missing creditor/owed context, “sentence Simon never said,” and “the table wondered.”
