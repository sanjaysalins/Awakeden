### Doctrinal soundness
**The causality trap is handled, not botched.** After quoting `"for she loved much,"` the script immediately breaks the backwards reading:

> “Forgiven first — nothing to pay. Love came after: the receipt, not the price.”

Then it lands the text’s own instrument: **“Not thy tears. Not thy ointment. Thy faith.”** That is orthodox and answers the brief’s main doctrinal risk.

**Residual risk (not FAIL, still real):** for one beat a zero-Bible ear still hears `"for she loved much"` as cause-before-forgiveness. The repair comes next line — good — but the bare quote is left standing without a pre-emptive frame (“as proof,” “because love showed,” etc.). In a 60s short, that one beat matters.

Skipped `"Thy sins are forgiven"` (7:48) to her. Not false, but it is the cleanest on-text “forgiven first” declaration, and you lean on paraphrase + parable instead.

### Clarity on first hearing
Mostly followable. Two friction points:

1. **“gave him nothing a host owes”** — slightly abstract/stiff for ear; “owes” makes the listener do a half-second parse before the list rescues it.
2. **“Two debtors, five hundred pence and fifty”** — hard cut into the parable with no “Jesus told him…” bridge. A stranger can lose who is speaking and why money appeared.

**Small overclaim:** “The woman nobody invited” — Luke never says she was uninvited; she comes because she knew He was there (7:37). Contrast is useful; the absolute is invention-by-implication.

### Grace / framing
Clean. No fear, no gain-loss, no works-earn-forgiveness. “Nothing to pay” + faith-not-tears is grace-anchored. Pass.

### Freshness + thread spine
One spine holds: **host’s empty courtesy → woman’s costly love → Simon sees only “sinner” → free forgiveness → love as receipt → faith → peace.**

No thread-swap. Freshness is about the text (“frankly” = freely; receipt vs price; “seen the sin / never the woman”), not a contrarian reading. Stain-as-sin/guilt stays attached to her via the text’s own “sinner” / “sins, which are many,” without visual jargon. Good.

### Hook score: **7 / 10**
Not a template open — good. Concrete list + contrast punch works.

It still does not clearly clear 8: “nothing a host owes” is soft for scroll-stop; the first five seconds are setup-contrast, not a gut-level text surprise. Under-8 rule applies.

### Landing score: **7 / 10**
> “She came in carrying an alabaster box. She left carrying his peace.”

Piece-native (alabaster + “go in peace”). Not a stock “come to Jesus.”

But it fails the stronger landing test: **it is a third-person curtain line, not a CTA.** The hearer is never invited. “She left carrying his peace” could almost close any peace-gift story if the alabaster line were dropped; as a gospel-short closer it observes her exit instead of landing on the listener. Under-8 rule applies.
