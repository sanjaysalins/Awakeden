# Independent review — gemini (OK, 45s)

The revisions to the hook are a massive improvement. Dropping the "guest list" anachronism grounds the piece in the text, and the opening contrast (Simon gave nothing, she gave all three) is a sharp, narrative-driven entry point. 

However, the fix to the landing's bridge sentence traded a grammatical error for a logical collision.

**SCORE THE HOOK: 8/10.** It drops the listener right into the tension of the room without relying on a cheap template. It is grounded and immediately compelling.

**SCORE THE LANDING: 7/10.** The imagery is beautifully specific to this piece (alabaster box vs. peace), avoiding stock closers. But the logic trips over itself right at the climax.

Here is the specific problem with the landing's clarity:
Look at the sequence of these three sentences:
> *"Whoever still carries what she carried in gets the same answer. She came in carrying an alabaster box. She left carrying his peace."*

Because the second sentence immediately follows the first, it explicitly defines "what she carried in" as the *alabaster box*. This makes your CTA accidentally imply: *"Whoever still carries an alabaster box gets the same answer."* You are trying to say she carried in a heavy burden of sin/debt, but you only name the physical box. The listener will stumble on this conflation of the physical object and the spiritual weight. 

**Doctrinal Soundness:**
> *"Forgiven first — love was the receipt, not the price."* 
This is exceptional. It perfectly threads the needle on a passage that is frequently (and dangerously) misinterpreted as works-based forgiveness. Highly orthodox and clear.

**KJV Verbatim:**
Clean. (A minor note on your brief: you mentioned restoring Simon's KJV hedge "I suppose", but you actually cut "I suppose" from the KJV quote and put the hedge in the narrator's voice: *"Simon answered carefully"*. This is perfectly fine under the established ellipsis rule, just noting the discrepancy with your brief).

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Fix the logical collision in the landing. Disconnect the listener's spiritual burden from the literal jar of ointment. (e.g., "She came in carrying an alabaster box, and a lifetime of debt. She left carrying his peace. Whoever still carries what she carried...")
2. Preserve the "receipt, not the price" line exactly as it is; it is the doctrinal anchor of the piece.
3. Keep the hook exactly as is.
