# Independent review — codex (OK, 65s)

The revised landing fixes the original grammatical error and the “thy/you” collision. It is now a complete sentence. But two substantive problems remain.

- **KJV-verbatim failure:** `"he, to whom he forgave most"` is not the exact KJV wording. Luke 7:43 reads, “I suppose that he, to whom he forgave most.” The omission is internal, not merely the established omission of introductory words. “Simon answered carefully” does not restore the missing words inside the quotation or preserve Simon’s explicit uncertainty. ([Luke 7:36–50 KJV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Luke+7.36-50&version=KJV))

- **Landing ambiguity:** `"Whoever still carries what she carried in gets the same answer"` is grammatical but unclear on first hearing. “What she carried in” can mean her guilt or the alabaster box—especially because the very next sentence explicitly says she carried the box. More seriously, “whoever…carries” makes possessing guilt sound like the condition for receiving salvation and peace. The text identifies faith, not guilt, as the instrument: `"Thy faith hath saved thee."` The invitation must retain that qualification.

- **Doctrinal phrasing:** `"Forgiven first — love was the receipt, not the price"` gives an orthodox, grace-centered reading of the parable: love evidences forgiveness rather than purchasing it. However, “first” may sound like a chronological claim about an earlier event that Luke does not narrate. “Love was the evidence, not the price” would make the intended logical relationship more exact.

- **Hook score: 7/10.** The contrast between the discourteous host and the uninvited woman is clear and textually grounded. It creates interest, but the first five seconds are mostly setup and lack an unusually arresting question, image, or tension. Under the stated rule, this independently requires revision.

- **Landing score: 7/10.** `"She came in carrying an alabaster box. She left carrying his peace"` is specific to this piece and stronger than a stock closer. But the preceding ambiguous universal promise weakens the theological landing, and the final sentence is a declaration rather than a clear invitation. The tailored image saves it from a lower score but not from revision.

- **Otherwise:** The piece avoids fear, works, and self-interest framing. `"Not thy tears. Not thy ointment. Thy faith"` correctly locates salvation in faith rather than her acts, and the Simon/woman contrast remains coherent through the parable and conclusion.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Restore the exact KJV quotation: “I suppose that he, to whom he forgave most.”
2. Make the landing explicitly connect receiving Christ’s answer with faith, not merely with carrying guilt.
3. Strengthen the first-five-second hook beyond narrative setup so it reaches the required 8/10 threshold.
