# Independent review — codex (OK, 108s)

The prior fixes mostly work, but the opening still imports details Luke does not give, and the hook remains below the required threshold.

- “A woman off his guest list walked in anyway” is invented. Luke says she entered the Pharisee’s house; it never says Simon used a guest list, explicitly excluded her, or that she defied an invitation boundary. That modern framing turns inference into narrated fact.

- “gave him nothing a host owes” overstates the text. Jesus identifies three courtesies Simon withheld, but neither calls them debts nor says every host owed them. “Nothing” also momentarily sounds false because Simon supplied the dinner itself.

- The revised “gave him all three” is now defensible. Jesus himself contrasts Simon’s missing water, kiss, and oil with her tears, kisses, and ointment in verses 44–46. It no longer implies she physically arrived carrying three objects.

- The creditor/debtor antecedent is successfully repaired. “A creditor had two debtors” makes the figures immediately intelligible.

- The restored clause—“but to whom little is forgiven, the same loveth little”—correctly preserves Jesus’ own interpretive guardrail. Together with the parable and “Thy faith hath saved thee,” it supports “love was the receipt, not the price.” “Forgiven first” is orthodox as theological sequence, though “first” should not be pressed as a narrated chronology Luke explicitly supplies.

- KJV audit: no lexical substitution appears in the quotations. However, under a genuinely strict “verbatim” rule, the stitched opening changes the KJV’s verse-opening “And stood” to “and stood,” and “Which” is capitalized where the KJV has “which” following “Tell me therefore,”. These are capitalization/editorial-splicing changes, not altered vocabulary, but they are not character-exact. Compare the [KJV text of Luke 7:36–50](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Luke+7.36-50&version=KJV).

- “still his word over whatever you walked in carrying” supplies the previously missing listener address, but its attachment is muddy on first hearing. Grammatically, “still” could modify “Thy faith,” “go in peace,” or the whole quotation. It also risks sounding like the woman’s assurance is automatically pronounced over every listener, although the preceding “Thy faith” partly guards against that.

- The Stain motif is doctrinally earned: the text explicitly names her sins and their forgiveness. The narration does not turn shame itself into the saving mechanism.

- The spine is basically coherent: withheld hospitality → her answering acts → forgiven debt → love as evidence → peace carried out. The brief “seeing” thread—“Simon had seen the sin. Never the woman.”—is effective but secondary rather than a derailment.

- Hook: **6/10.** “Simon the Pharisee invited Jesus to dinner” is setup, not a strong first-five-seconds interruption. The sharper contrast arrives later, and two of its attention-grabbing claims—“owes” and “off his guest list”—are precisely the least textually secure. Under the supplied rule, this alone requires revision.

- Landing: **9/10.** “She came in carrying an alabaster box. She left carrying his peace” is specific to this episode, bookends an object established in the opening quotation, and transforms the piece’s own verb. It could not naturally close an unrelated Jesus narration. The sentence immediately before it needs clarification, but the final sentence itself lands.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Remove the invented “off his guest list” claim and stop presenting inferred exclusion as Luke’s stated fact.
2. Replace or qualify “nothing a host owes,” which turns omitted courtesies into an unsupported debt claim.
3. Raise the first-five-second hook without relying on those inventions, and clarify what “still his word” specifically refers to.
