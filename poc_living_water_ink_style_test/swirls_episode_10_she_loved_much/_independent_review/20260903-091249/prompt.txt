Simon the Pharisee invited Jesus to dinner and gave him nothing a host owes: no water, no kiss, no oil. A woman off his guest list walked in anyway, and gave him all three.

"A woman in the city, which was a sinner... brought an alabaster box of ointment, and stood at his feet behind him weeping, and began to wash his feet with tears... and kissed his feet, and anointed them with the ointment."

Simon said nothing out loud: "This man, if he were a prophet, would have known... for she is a sinner." Jesus answered anyway. A creditor had two debtors — one owed five hundred pence, one fifty. "When they had nothing to pay, he frankly forgave them both" — frankly, that's freely. "Which of them will love him most?" Simon knew: "he, to whom he forgave most."

Jesus turned to her, and asked Simon: "Seest thou this woman?" Simon had seen the sin. Never the woman. "Her sins, which are many, are forgiven; for she loved much: but to whom little is forgiven, the same loveth little."

Forgiven first — love was the receipt, not the price. He turned from the parable to her: "Thy faith hath saved thee; go in peace." Not thy tears. Not thy ointment. Thy faith — still his word over whatever you walked in carrying. She came in carrying an alabaster box. She left carrying his peace.
