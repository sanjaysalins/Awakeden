### Doctrinal soundness

**Mostly sound, one live hazard.**

The orthodox read is stated clearly: *"Forgiven first — love was the receipt, not the price"* and *"Not thy tears. Not thy ointment. Thy faith."* That pushes against works-righteousness and aligns with the parable (forgiveness → love).

**Problem:** You still read KJV 7:47 straight through first: *"Her sins, which are many, are forgiven; for she loved much."* On first hearing, the natural English causal read is **forgiven because she loved much** — i.e. love as price, not receipt. The guardrail clause helps (*"but to whom little is forgiven, the same loveth little"*), but it comes **after** the damaging half-line, and the corrective gloss comes **after the full quote**. A listener with zero theology may absorb the wrong causality for several seconds. That is not invented doctrine in the script, but it is a **doctrinal clarity failure on audio** unless the inversion lands instantly.

**Minor:** *"Simon knew: 'he, to whom he forgave most.'"* — KJV 7:43 has *"I suppose that he..."* Simon is tentative, not certain. Small, but it subtly upgrades the hostile witness into a confident one.

### Clarity on first hearing (zero Bible knowledge)

**Two friction points.**

1. **Opening hospitality triad:** *"gave him nothing a host owes: no water, no kiss, no oil"* assumes knowledge of ancient host customs. A stranger may not know why that is scandalous or how tear-washing counts as “water.” The later parable does not return to explain the triad; the landing pivots to alabaster/faith/peace instead.

2. **"still his word over whatever you walked in carrying"** — *"still"* is ambiguous (nevertheless? continues to be?). *"his word over"* is abstract. The alabaster bookend helps, but this line is the weakest clarity beat in the close.

**Strong clarity win:** *"Simon had seen the sin. Never the woman."* — concrete, faithful, instantly graspable.
