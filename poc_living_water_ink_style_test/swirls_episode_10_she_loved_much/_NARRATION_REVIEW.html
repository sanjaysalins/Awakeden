Episode 10 — "She Loved Much"

Luke 7:36-50 — the woman who washed Jesus's feet with her tears. Ran through Fable + 4 rounds of the 5-CLI panel. $0 spent so far.

No water for his feet. No kiss. No oil. That's what Simon the Pharisee never gave Jesus at his own table. A woman he hadn't invited walked in anyway, and gave him all three.

"A woman in the city, which was a sinner... brought an alabaster box of ointment, and stood at his feet behind him weeping, and began to wash his feet with tears... and kissed his feet, and anointed them with the ointment."

Simon thought it, not out loud: "This man, if he were a prophet, would have known... for she is a sinner." Jesus answered anyway. A creditor had two debtors — one owed five hundred pence, one fifty. "When they had nothing to pay, he frankly forgave them both" — frankly, that's freely. "Which of them will love him most?" Simon hedged: "I suppose that he, to whom he forgave most."

Jesus turned to her, and asked Simon: "Seest thou this woman?" Simon had seen the sin. Never the woman. "Her sins, which are many, are forgiven; for she loved much: but to whom little is forgiven, the same loveth little."

Forgiven first — love was the receipt, not the price. He turned from the parable to her: "Thy faith hath saved thee; go in peace." Not thy tears. Not thy ointment. Thy faith. That answer holds for whoever still carries what Simon called her — a sinner. She came in carrying an alabaster box. She left carrying his peace.

What actually got fixed along the way

  • A real KJV misquote in my first draft ("that she is a sinner" — should be "for she is a sinner"), caught by Fable.
  • A wrong word — "clean" isn't this episode's word (that belongs to a different Stain sub-case). Fixed to "forgiven," the text's own word.
  • No turn to the viewer — every locked episode in this series ends with a pivot to the listener. My first draft dropped it; now restored, and rewritten twice more until the grammar and the pronouns were both clean.
  • Two small invented facts softened — "brought all three" (she didn't literally carry water/a kiss) became "gave him all three," matching Jesus's own words in the passage.
  • The alabaster box callback — used at the very end but never actually said earlier — now appears in the opening quote so it's earned.

One open call for you

The hook — "No water for his feet. No kiss. No oil..." — scores well (7-8/10, not a stock opener, doctrinally clean) but two reviewers think it could be sharper if the scene opened on the woman's entrance instead, or let Jesus's own rebuke to Simon (verses 44-46) land as a spoken quote later. Getting past this ceiling means a different hook idea, not another editing pass.

My read: this is good enough to lock as-is — every doctrinal, factual, and clarity issue the panel raised across 4 rounds is now fixed, and the landing is strong. I'd ship it. But it's your call whether to spend one more round chasing a sharper hook first.
