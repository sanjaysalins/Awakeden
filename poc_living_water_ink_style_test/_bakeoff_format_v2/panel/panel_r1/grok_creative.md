A. MY OWN BAKE-OFF
One story, strangers, phones, each person sees one film once. Sound-on cohort and muted cohort stay separate.
Arm 1 is the shipped film. Arm 2 is a cold open: no title up front, first frame is the strongest picture, first words are a plain stake a non-Bible viewer feels, last second leads back into that opening question, captions big enough to read under phone chrome.
Arm 3 runs only if Arm 2 holds people longer: the same hook cut to about 30 seconds.
Score is the second they leave on that first watch, plus share still watching at 3 seconds and percent viewed. Crown a winner only if the gap clears a margin set before the test.
AI seats can veto a Bible error or a who/what/why miss. They do not get a swipe vote.

Win rule: CHANGE. Two layers can crown an arm after real people prefer the control.
Bake-off 2: CHANGE. Run it later as a prop check with a quote. An X-rate on 15 stills says nothing about whether anyone stays.

C. BIGGEST FLAW
Control is the only arm in native framing. B, C, and D are stacked edits (title plus page count plus crop plus, on D, captions gone) made from soft crops. Layer 1 then auto-scores those arms for the shorter title and the faster cuts. Layer 1 plus the AI seats can write the skill while real viewers lose the vote. If the soft crops make control win, the rule says stop all format change. The owner walks away "sure" from a test built to be muddy.

D. WRONG OR UNSUPPORTED CLAIMS
The long cover is measured on episodes 11, 12, 13, and Jacob's Ladder. Episode 8 is not on that list. Treating Episode 8 as the title-card patient is unsupported.
"Same words, same voice, same score" cannot also hit a shorter film or a new 6-page rhythm unless pauses are cut or pictures change under the old audio. The pack never says which.
"Hook-first" names a speech problem the audit does not show. Narration already starts at once. The open problem is the card on the picture.
A 3-second cut rate is not a measured retention law. Nobody has watched these films.
A visual rhyme is not a replay loop. The ending sentence has to feed the opening sentence, or the restart just feels like a glitch.
"Beats control on numbers" will happen by construction once the title is shorter. That is not evidence of a better film.
Episode 8 is already about 64 seconds and had 0 rejects. It is the film least able to show why four films run 78 to 106 seconds.
A pixel check for "this cross is an X" is undefined. Two ink beams will look like an X to a naive pixel rule.
The prior panel's "viewers will leave" line is still a guess. Zero films are posted.

E. WHAT TO ADD OR CHANGE
1. One change per arm. Title-only versus control, on a film that actually holds a 3–6 second card. No page-count change in that arm.
2. Add the missing lever: a new first line a stranger understands, same pictures, same length. That is the retention lever this bake-off never touches.
3. Rebuild D from the big illustration at full quality, captions still on screen, phone chrome drawn on the frame before anyone votes.
4. Layer 1 becomes pass/fail only. Primary score is leave-time on the first watch. Muted viewers are different people, not the second pass.
5. Pull the stop-rule out. A null on one recut of Episode 8 must not freeze format tests. A tie means run a larger viewer test.

F. WIN RULE
The 2-of-3 rule is unsound. Layer 1 votes for the recipe, and Layer 2 votes for a model. Together they beat a room of real people.
Better rule: the build check must pass, then the arm must beat control on first-watch 3-second hold by a margin written beforehand, in the human group, with error bars that do not overlap. A who/what/why miss in that human group vetoes it. AI seats can veto facts. They cannot award the win. A tie keeps the shipped format for now and does not ban the next test. One story never rewrites the skill. A second story has to repeat the win. The optional 48-hour post is the only score that can lock a rule.
