Two real pipeline bugs found and fixed today

wherebugfix
PythonProject1/jesus/narration_pipeline.py The round-trip prose-preservation check (compares narration.md word-for-word against the tagged file, to catch a tagger that drops/paraphrases content) predates this project's **[speaker]** header convention and its title/status front-matter — it was counting header tokens and front-matter as "spoken content the tagger dropped," blocking every multi-voice piece written in the current canonical format. Added strip_block_headers() — strips **[speaker]** header lines and the leading front-matter block (everything up to the first ---) from the comparison, mirroring what this project's own parser already does.
pipeline/narration_parse.py _parse_xml_blocks() (parses a rendered narration-tagged.md for this project's own lock/parity gate) only captured text literally inside <speaker> tags — any narrator prose sitting between/around them (the correct, real convention — matches per_turn_synth.py's own turn-parser) was silently dropped from the spoken-text hash. A real multi-voice piece could lock against only its quoted dialogue, missing most of its actual narration. Rewrote _parse_xml_blocks() to capture inter-tag gaps as narrator blocks (mirroring per_turn_synth.py's parse_turns exactly) and strip leading [audio-tag] sequences from every segment. Updated the fail-closed integrity check accordingly.
Why this matters beyond today: any future multi-voice piece written in this project's own canonical narration.md format would have hit the same wall the first time someone actually ran cli_lock.py against it — this was latent, not something I broke.
