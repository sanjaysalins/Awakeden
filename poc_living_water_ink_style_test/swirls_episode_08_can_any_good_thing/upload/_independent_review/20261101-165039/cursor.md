## Hooky but honest / bait-and-switch

- **YouTube (Short) DESCRIPTION, line 1:** *“Before Nathanael ever met Jesus, he doubted Him out loud. **And Jesus did not argue.**”* In John 1:45–51 Jesus is **not** in the Nazareth exchange; **Philip** answers. This reads as Jesus refusing to argue about Nazareth, which the pericope does not support. Worse than the red-team’s Philip pronoun nit — it **names Jesus** as the non-arguer in the wrong scene. The narration correctly has *“Philip did not argue — he invited.”*

- **Title (all platforms using the chosen hook):** *“A Man **Insulted** Jesus **Behind His Back**”* — KJV Nathanael questions **Nazareth** (*“Can there any good thing come out of Nazareth?”*), not a personal insult to Jesus’ face. “Insulted” and “behind his back” are **editorial heat**, not lie-level if the video uses the same framing (it does in `narration.md`), but it **overstates** the tone of the doubt and could feel like clickbait to KJV-literate viewers. Acceptable only if you accept the video’s own rhetorical license; metadata should not pretend it is quotation.

- **YouTube (Short) DESCRIPTION:** *“**That is the ladder Jacob saw, and Jesus says it is Himself.**”* John 1:51 names **“the Son of man”** and angels ascending/descending **upon** him; Jesus does **not** say “I am the ladder” in those words. Typology is fine in commentary; *“Jesus says it is Himself”* is **overclaim / faux-quotation** (red-team flagged; **still in the copy**). The **spoken script** also pushes this (*“the one Jesus said He is”*), so metadata matches the video — but upload copy should not imply a verbatim Christ claim the anchor verse does not contain.

- **Title option 2 (not chosen, but in kit):** *“Can **Anything** Good Come Out of Nazareth? **Jesus Heard That**”* — If ever rotated in, **“Jesus Heard That”** is **likely false** to the text (the slur is to Philip before Jesus sees Nathanael coming). Good that it is not the chosen title; bad that it sits in the kit as an alternate without a warning.
