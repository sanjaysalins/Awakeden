## 1. WHO IS THIS FOR (one named viewer; who stops, who finishes, who shares; who is NOT served)
Named viewer: Ana, 34, who drifted from church after feeling she was in the way. Line 1 ("not now") stops her; line 10 is made for her.
- **Stops:** anyone recently brushed off. Line 1 is a real, universal hurt.
- **Finishes:** most who stop. Lines 2-4 move fast and line 7 ("here is what He did") re-hooks.
- **Shares:** parents, Sunday-school teachers, grandparents. The last picture is the share.
- **NOT served:** the viewer who hears "not now" as *God* delaying a prayer. The film never says who said it. That viewer may think the film promises Jesus never says wait. Also the lifelong reader gets little new beyond line 9.

## 2. MAIN TAKEAWAY (one sentence a viewer could say back; which line carries it; did the film make it clear?)
"People pushed the children away, but Jesus was angry about it and picked them up, and He does not push me away either."
Lines 9-10 carry it. The story half is very clear. The "and me" half is only implied. The bridge from children to the grown-up viewer rests on the last sentence of line 6, which is the vaguest sentence in the film.

## 3. GLOBAL AUDIENCE (who / what / why in my own words; where I got lost or misled, quote the line; words that fail in translation)
Who: Jesus, His followers, parents with small children. What: followers block the children, Jesus is angry, calls them in, holds them. Why: God's kingdom is for people like them.
- **Lost:** line 6, "Anyone who comes in, comes the way a little child does." Comes in where? What way is that? A newcomer cannot answer.
- **Lost:** line 1, "Then stay." Stay where? An ESL viewer may not hear "keep watching." "This story is yours" is an idiom; "for you" is plainer.
- **Translation risks:** "Suffer" (line 5) means pain today, but line 6 fixes it at once. Good. "rebuked" (line 3) and "much displeased" (line 4) are both glossed. Good. "disciples" is never explained.

## 4. BIBLICALLY ACCURATE (claim by claim: RIGHT / OVERCLAIMED / UNSUPPORTED / WRONG, quote the line; say only what you can support)
- Line 2 "that he should touch them": RIGHT (Mark 10:13, verbatim).
- Line 3 "rebuked those that brought them": RIGHT, verbatim. "They sent them away": OVERCLAIMED. The text says rebuked; it does not say anyone left. They *tried* to stop them.
- Line 4 "much displeased" / "Angry": RIGHT (the Greek word means indignant). "Not with the children. With the ones who sent them away": RIGHT in target (He "said unto them"), same small overclaim in "sent away."
- Line 5: RIGHT, verbatim Mark 10:14.
- Line 6 "belongs to people like these children": RIGHT ("of such"). "Anyone who comes in, comes the way a little child does": RIGHT but softened. Verse 15 is a warning (shall not enter). Fair as a positive paraphrase.
- Line 8: RIGHT, verbatim Mark 10:16.
- Line 9 "They asked for a touch. He gave the children His arms": RIGHT, and the best observation in the film.
- Lines 1 and 10 "not now": UNSUPPORTED as wording. No one says "not now" in the text. It is fair application, not Scripture. Keep it in the narrator's voice, as it is.
- Final picture (two girls, one on His shoulders): invented detail, harmless.

## 5. BEST VERSION POSSIBLE (what would the best explainer in the world do differently; the ONE highest-value change; score 0-10 against that bar)
The best version would make the grown-up viewer's way in as clear as the children's. **ONE change: rewrite the last sentence of line 6** so it says plainly that an adult comes the same way. Then line 10 lands on solid ground, not a hint.
Score: **7.5/10**. Strong story, earned landing (line 9), weak bridge.

## CURIOSITY GAP (opens at line 1; pays off at lines 9-10; score 0-10; worth the wait?)
Opens at line 1 ("This story is yours"), re-opens at line 7, pays off at lines 9-10. Score: **7/10**. Worth the wait. The contrast "a touch / His arms" is a real payoff. It loses points because "yours" is never said out loud at the end.
