## 1. WHO IS THIS FOR

Amina, 34, in Jakarta. She learned English at school and at church. She reads the captions. An older woman once told her she could not bring her little girl forward.

She stops on line 5 if "Suffer" is the Jesus line she hears. She finishes if she reaches line 9 and the last picture, because two girls held close matches her child. She shares it with her sister only if the forwarded caption does not still say "Suffer."

The film does not serve a viewer on auto-translate. Line 5 will come out wrong in their language. It does not serve a child watching alone. It also does not serve an adult whose "not now" was about work or money. Line 1 says the story is theirs. The story is about children blocked on their way to Jesus.

## 2. MAIN TAKEAWAY

"They told the children 'not now.' Jesus called them close and held them."

Line 9 carries it. Line 10 says it again to the viewer. By the end, yes, this is clear. The Jesus voice in line 5 does not make it clear.

## 3. GLOBAL AUDIENCE

Who: Jesus, people bringing young children, and his followers. What: the followers pushed the children off. Jesus was very upset with those adults, told them to let the children come, then held the children and blessed them. Why: God's kingdom belongs to people like these children.

I got lost at line 5: "Suffer the little children to come unto me, and forbid them not: for of such is the kingdom of God." Today, "suffer" means pain. Auto-captions and machine translation will teach pain. "Forbid them not" is a double negative, and many languages flip it into "stop them." "Unto" and "of such" do not travel. Line 6 repairs this in the narrator's voice, after the holy voice has already said the bad line. People remember the Jesus sentence.

Other words that fail:

- Line 1: "Then stay" can mean stay in the room. "This story is yours" can mean you own it.
- Line 2: "that he should touch them." "Should" sounds like a duty.
- Line 3: "rebuked" is rare, and captions often garble it.
- Line 4: "much displeased" is rare. "Angry" is clearer, and hotter than the old phrase.
- Line 6: "comes the way a little child does." Which way? Silly, weak, pure, obedient? In many cultures this sounds like "act like a baby." The film never shows the way.
- Line 8: "blessed" means a spell or an ancestor rite in some places.
- Line 9: "He gave the children His arms" can translate as handing over body parts.
- Line 10: "picked up" can mean chose them, or lifted them.

Universal: a child told to wait, then a trusted adult who pulls them close. The last picture reads as welcome in almost every culture. Insider: "disciples," "kingdom of God," and the old Bible sentence. The picture shows only girls. The text says children. A boy who was turned away may not find himself. In some communities a man with a girl on his shoulders must already look like a parent, or the image feels strange. The smile does that work.

## 4. BIBLICALLY ACCURATE

The scene is Mark 10:13–16. Matthew 19 and Luke 18 tell the same moment.

- Line 2: RIGHT. Mark 10:13, quote included.
- Line 3: OVERCLAIMED. They did rebuke the people who brought the children. "They sent them away" states a finished expulsion. Mark shows the rebuke. It does not say the children had already gone.
- Line 4: RIGHT on "much displeased." "Angry" is a strong plain gloss. Some modern Bibles say angry or indignant. "Not with the children. With the ones who sent them away" matches who he corrects.
- Line 5: RIGHT as a King James quote of Mark 10:14. Unsafe in modern speech.
- Line 6: RIGHT for "let them come," "do not stop them," and "people like these children." OVERCLAIMED on "Anyone who comes in, comes the way a little child does." Mark 10:15 is a condition: whoever does not receive the kingdom as a little child shall not enter. The film keeps the warm half.
- Line 8: RIGHT. Mark 10:16.
- Line 9: RIGHT that he took them up in his arms. "They" can sound like the children asked. In line 2 the bringers wanted the touch. The verse also has his hands on them and a blessing, so the touch they wanted is still there.
- Line 10: OVERCLAIMED if "not now" means any adult disappointment. The passage can include adults who come like a child. It does not show Jesus picking up every dismissed person.
- Last picture: the arms are in the text. The smile, the laughter, two girls only, the shoulders, the arch, and the olive tree are UNSUPPORTED as facts. Girls are children, so the picture is not WRONG.

## 5. BEST VERSION POSSIBLE

The best explainer would put plain words in the Jesus voice and the old line in small type as the source. One concrete picture would show "like a child": small, no rank, brought close. "Blessed" would get a few plain words: he spoke God's good over them.

The one highest-value change is line 5. The Jesus voice should say "Let the little children come to me," so the captions cannot teach "suffer."

Score: 6/10. Lines 6, 8, and 9 already speak plainly. The line people will quote is still the line that mistranslates.

## CURIOSITY GAP

Opens at line 1. Pays off at line 10. The real story question opens at line 3 and pays off at line 9. Score: 6/10. Sixty-five seconds is short enough to wait. Line 10 mostly repeats line 9. The embrace and the last picture earn the wait. The wait fails if line 5 has already taught the wrong meaning.

## THREE SMALLEST FIXES

1. Line 5 — JESUS: "Let the little children come to me. Do not stop them. God's kingdom belongs to people like these children."
2. Line 2 — "People were bringing young children to Jesus. They wanted him to touch them."
3. Line 9 — "They wanted a touch. He held the children in his arms."

## VERDICT

REVISE before calling this global-ready. The story and the last picture carry the welcome. The Jesus line can still teach the wrong meaning of "suffer" to a second-language viewer or an auto-caption.
