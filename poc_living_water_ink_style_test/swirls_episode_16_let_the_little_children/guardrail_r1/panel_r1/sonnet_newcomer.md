## 1. WHO IS THIS FOR
A named viewer: Amara, 34, a nurse who was told "not now" at work. She stops at line 1 ("Has someone told you 'not now'?") because it speaks to her. She finishes for the happy last picture and line 9. She shares with a friend who feels pushed away. NOT served: someone with no sense of being rejected, and anyone who needs to know who "disciples" or "the kingdom of God" are (lines 3, 5, 6).

## 2. MAIN TAKEAWAY
"If people push you away, Jesus does not; He holds you close." Lines 1 and 10 carry it, and line 9 ("He gave the children His arms") is the heart. It is mostly clear. But line 6 adds a second idea ("anyone who comes in, comes the way a little child does") that I could not follow, so the message gets muddy in the middle.

## 3. GLOBAL AUDIENCE
Who: Jesus, some children, some parents, and "His own disciples." What: the parents bring the children to be touched, the disciples send them away, Jesus is angry, then He holds them. Why: so I know Jesus welcomes me when others say "not now."
Where I got lost:
- Line 3: "His own disciples" is never explained. I guess they are his helpers or followers, but that is a guess.
- Line 2: "that he should touch them." Why is a touch the one thing they want? I do not know. Is it a blessing? Healing?
- Line 4: "much displeased" is hard English. "Angry" then saves it. Good.
- Line 5: "Suffer the little children to come unto me, and forbid them not" is very hard. "Suffer" sounds like pain to me. "Forbid" and "of such" are also hard. Line 6 helps a little.
- Line 5: "the kingdom of God" is not explained. I guess it is a place or a group. Line 6 does not say what it is. I cannot tell what "God's kingdom belongs to people like these children" means.
- Line 6: "Anyone who comes in, comes the way a little child does." Comes in where? How does a child come? Open? Trusting? Not said.
- Line 8: "put his hands upon them" and "blessed them." I do not know "blessed."
- Line 4: "Not with the children. With the ones who sent them away." Clear and strong.
Words that fail in translation: "suffer," "forbid," "rebuked," "displeased," "blessed," "kingdom."

## 4. BIBLICALLY ACCURATE
I cannot judge accuracy. What I was TOLD but could not follow: "disciples" (3), "the kingdom of God" (5, 6), "blessed" (8), why touch matters (2), "comes in" (6), and who the "Bible" is quoting from (4). I do not know where this happens or when.

## 5. BEST VERSION POSSIBLE
The best explainer would say in one short phrase who the disciples are ("His close followers"), and use one plain word for the child-like way of coming (for example "open hands, no tricks"). It would cut the second idea in line 6 or explain it in 8 words. The ONE highest-value change: fix line 6. It is the only place the film leans on an unexplained idea ("kingdom") and then adds a puzzle on top. Score: 6.5/10. The hook and the ending are strong. The middle assumes I already know the Bible.

## CURIOSITY GAP
Opens at line 1 ("Has someone told you 'not now'? ... This story is yours"). Pays off at lines 9 and 10 (He gave the children His arms; look at who Jesus picked up). Score: 8/10. Worth the wait. The last image of a smiling Jesus holding two laughing girls lands the feeling even where my understanding fell short.

## THREE SMALLEST FIXES
1. Line 3: "But His own disciples" becomes "But His close followers, called disciples," so the word is explained.
2. Line 6: replace with "In plain words: let them come. Do not stop them. God welcomes people who come open and trusting, like these children."
3. Line 8: after the quote, add "Blessed means He asked God to be good to them." Or cut "blessed" in your spoken line and keep "held them close."
