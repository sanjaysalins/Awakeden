## 1. WHO IS THIS FOR
**Named viewer:** a 30-year-old mum in Lagos or Manila who once felt pushed out of church, or had her noisy kid shushed there.
- **Who stops:** anyone who has been told "not now" (line 1). That is a wide, real hook.
- **Who finishes:** parents, and people who feel overlooked.
- **Who shares:** Sunday-school teachers and parents' group chats.
- **Who is NOT served:** a reader who wants the hard edge of v15 ("shall not enter therein"). Line 6 softens it away.

## 2. MAIN TAKEAWAY
"Jesus doesn't send small or pushed-aside people away. He picks them up." Line 9 carries it, and line 10 closes it. Yes, it comes through clearly.

## 3. GLOBAL AUDIENCE
**In my words:** parents brought children to Jesus. His followers tried to stop them. Jesus was angry with the followers, said "let them come", and held and blessed the children.
- Line 5: "Suffer" means *allow*. A newcomer will read it as *pain*. Line 6 saves this ("let them come"). Good.
- Line 3: "rebuked" is a hard word, and the plain gloss given ("sent them away") is not what the text says (see §4).
- Line 9: "They asked for a touch." Who is "they"? It sounds like the children asked. In the text it was the people who brought them.
- Line 1 / line 10: "not now" is a soft idiom, but it is common enough to work.

## 4. BIBLICALLY ACCURATE
- **Line 2** "People were bringing young children… 'that he should touch them.'" **RIGHT.** Mark 10:13, quoted word for word.
- **Line 3** "'rebuked those that brought them.'" **RIGHT.** The quote is word for word (10:13).
- **Line 3** "They sent them away." **OVERCLAIMED.** The text says *rebuked*. "Forbid them not" (10:14) means they were *hindering*. No verse says the children were sent away. The picture also shows they were still there to be held (10:16).
- **Line 4** "Jesus saw it… 'much displeased.'" **RIGHT.** 10:14, word for word.
- **Line 4** "Angry." **RIGHT, if a little strong.** It is a fair gloss of "much displeased" (indignant).
- **Line 4** "Not with the children. With the ones who sent them away." **RIGHT** that it was aimed at the disciples (he "said unto them" right after the rebuke). But it repeats the "sent away" overclaim.
- **Line 5** Jesus's quote. **RIGHT.** 10:14, word for word.
- **Line 6** "God's kingdom belongs to people like these children." **RIGHT.** This is "of such is."
- **Line 6** "Anyone who comes in, comes the way a little child does." **OVERCLAIMED / softened.** Verse 15's verb is *receive* the kingdom, and it carries a warning: "shall not enter therein." The film turns a condition into a description.
- **Line 8** quote. **RIGHT.** 10:16, word for word (it only drops the opening "And").
- **Line 9** "They asked for a touch. He gave… His arms." **RIGHT** in meaning (10:13 vs 10:16). "Asked" is a fair reading of "that he should touch them."
- **Final picture:** a girl "up on His shoulders" is **UNSUPPORTED.** The text says "in his arms." Luke says "infants," and these look like older children. It is minor, but a fact-checker will see it.

Would I send it to my church group? **Yes, once line 3 is fixed.**

## 5. BEST VERSION POSSIBLE
The best explainer would keep the great contrast (touch → arms). They would also let v15 bite: the kingdom is *received* as a gift, like a child receives, not earned. That is the gospel turn, and right now it is blurred.
**ONE highest-value change:** fix line 6's last sentence so it says "receive."
**Score: 7/10.**

## CURIOSITY GAP
Opens at line 1, pays off at lines 9–10. **Score: 6/10.** The payoff is warm, but it is visible from the start. It is worth the wait because of line 9's twist, not because of any real surprise.

## THREE SMALLEST FIXES
1. **Line 3:** "They sent them away." → **"They tried to stop them."** (Line 4 then becomes: "With the ones who tried to stop them.")
2. **Line 6, last sentence:** → **"Anyone who wants God's kingdom must receive it like a little child: as a gift."**
3. **Line 9:** → **"The people asked for a touch. Jesus took the children in His arms."**
