## TITLES: 6 movie titles (1-4 words). Each: title | the question it opens | why it sounds like a real film. Star your top 2.

* **Not Now** | Who was told to wait, and who refused the wait? | Two spoken words. Modern drama titles work this way: short, ordinary, already loaded.
* **They Said Stop** | Who said it, and who was walking anyway? | A three-beat true-story title. It sounds shot, cut, and posted on a one-sheet.
* **The Raised Hand** | What is that open palm holding back? | An object title. One prop becomes the whole film, the way a single image carries a festival poster.
* **Let Them Come** | Come to whom, and who is standing in the lane? | An invitation with a door shut in it. Sounds like a prestige drama built on one spoken line.
* **Open Arms** | If the arms are open, why does the picture feel like a fight? | Two warm words that still leave a clash. Reads as emotion first, plot second.
* **He Saw It** | What did he see that changed his face? | A witness title. The story turns on a look, which is how a short, serious film gets its name.

### THE WALL  (title: They Said Stop)
- THE IDEA in one sentence (the graphic trick): A wall of grown-up backs is the whole poster, and the story is one bright vertical slot cut through it.
- WHAT YOU SEE: Top, a thin band of late-afternoon sky and one olive branch. The rest is backs: linen, wool, rope belts, dusty hems, painted as one heavy surface, faces turned away, calm and firm. On the right, one arm lifts a flat open palm, large and clear. Slightly left of center, a narrow slot of honey light runs almost the full height. Inside it, a mother holds a small girl close. The girl looks up, steady and curious, safe against her mother. The far end of the slot is only gold. No one waits there yet. Clay, dust, and olive for the wall. Rose and a faded red sash only inside the slot. Gouache on the cloth, ink line only on the girl and the edge of the palm.
- LETTERING: THEY SAID STOP in the sky band, condensed, cream, nearly the full width, tall enough to be the second shape after the palm. Tagline: none.
- THE CURIOSITY GAP: What is on the other side of this wall, and why is a kind hand blocking a mother and child? The film answers when his own followers rebuke the people who brought the children, and when he is much displeased with those followers.
- PHONE TEST: A tan block, one bright slit, one pale palm. It reads as a shut gate.
- IMAGE PROMPT: Vertical 9:16 cinematic storybook poster, warm watercolor and gouache, film-poster light. A monumental wall of grown-ups' backs fills a stone village lane, faces turned away, no cruel expressions, linen and wool in clay and dust colors. One follower's arm raises a flat open palm in a clear stop. Between two shoulders a narrow vertical slot of golden late-afternoon light. Inside the slot only, full color: a mother holding a small girl close, the girl calm, curious, looking up, never frightened. The slot ends in pure gold, no figure waiting there. Olive branch at the top. Use film pictures 1 and 3 as character and costume references. No halo, no sunburst, no lettering, no text.

### THE SPLIT  (title: Not Now)
- THE IDEA in one sentence (the graphic trick): A hard vertical split holds the stop on one side and the embrace on the other, with a thin empty gap between them.
- WHAT YOU SEE: Left forty percent: the stone lane in muted dust colors. A flat palm faces forward. Behind it, a father with two small boys and a mother with a toddler, all unhurt, quiet, not crying, followers' faces firm but human. Right sixty percent: Jesus in profile facing left, long dark hair, beard, patched blue robe, rope belt, brow set, mouth serious, never cruel. A laughing girl rests on his hip, eyes bright, joy plain. Late sun rakes both sides. A hairline of bare paper separates the two worlds so they cannot touch. Blue is the only strong color, and it lives on the right.
- LETTERING: NOT NOW sits low on the left half, inside the dusty lane, large cream condensed type, about a third of the poster height. Tagline: none.
- THE CURIOSITY GAP: Why is his displeasure aimed at a hand, while a child laughs in his arm? The film answers it in order: they tried to stop the children, he was angry at the people who stopped them, then he took the children up and blessed them.
- PHONE TEST: Dust on the left, blue on the right, a small bright child, a pale hand. It reads as two sides of one argument.
- IMAGE PROMPT: Vertical 9:16 cinematic storybook poster, warm watercolor and gouache, dramatic side light. A hard vertical split. Left side: stone lane, one raised flat palm stopping a mother with a toddler and a father with two small boys, followers human and firm, not villains, children calm and safe with their parents. Right side: Jesus in profile facing the palm, long dark hair, beard, patched blue robe, rope belt, much displeased but not cruel, a laughing small girl on his hip in joy. Thin blank gap between the sides. Golden late-afternoon light. Use film pictures 3, 4, and 9 as references. No halo, no sunburst, no lettering, no text.

### INSIDE THE ARMS  (title: Let Them Come)
- THE IDEA in one sentence (the graphic trick): Full color exists only inside the silhouette of his open arms. Outside that shape, the crowd is flat brown shadow.
- WHAT YOU SEE: Jesus stands nearly full height as a deep blue-black silhouette, long hair and beard readable, robe hem and rope belt catching a thin gold rim, arms wide. No rays behind his head. Inside the shape of his body and arms, the painting turns on: watercolor children in joy, one girl on his shoulders, one on his hip, both laughing, plus smaller children running in, olive green and warm skin alive only there. Outside the silhouette, a flat umber field of adult backs and one raised palm, no faces, no color, like a shut door drawn around an open one. Stone arch and olive leaves sit in the gold rim light at the edges.
- LETTERING: LET THEM COME centered under his feet, tall cream serif, wide, the brightest type on the dark ground. Tagline: none.
- THE CURIOSITY GAP: Why can the children be seen only inside his arms, and what is the brown crowd outside trying to stop? The film answers when the people come for a touch and he gives the children his arms, after the followers fail to keep them away.
- PHONE TEST: One human shape, arms wide, bright specks inside, a brown mass around it. It reads as a door.
- IMAGE PROMPT: Vertical 9:16 cinematic storybook poster, warm watercolor and gouache, film-poster lighting. Jesus as a full-height deep blue silhouette, long dark hair, beard, patched robe and rope belt edged in gold, arms wide open, smiling posture, no halo and no sunburst. Inside the silhouette only, rich painted color: two laughing girls, one on his shoulders and one on his hip, and small children running toward him, joyful, never frightened. Outside the silhouette, flat umber silhouettes of grown-ups' backs and a single raised open palm, faceless, drained of color. Stone arch and olive leaves in the rim light. Use film pictures 5, 9, 3, and 4 as references. No lettering, no text.

### MY #1 and why (2 lines).
THE WALL. At two centimeters it is still a shut gate with one slit of light, and the film is the act of opening that gate. The block is the picture, so a stranger has to watch to see who gets through.
