## TITLES

- ★ **LET THEM COME** | Who tried to keep them out? | A command with conflict built into it; simple enough to become the film’s signature.
- ★ **NOT NOW** | Who said it—and what did Jesus do? | An everyday rejection becomes a sharp, memorable dramatic title.
- **OPEN ARMS** | What happened before the welcome? | A familiar phrase with a strong physical image and an emotional promise.
- **THE SMALL ONES** | Why do these children matter so much? | Names the overlooked people at the heart of an intimate epic.
- **ROOM FOR THEM** | Who decides who belongs? | Suggests a struggle over belonging without requiring Bible knowledge.
- **ONE TOUCH** | What did they come for—and receive? | A concrete desire that carries the story toward its unexpected abundance.

All three posters below use **LET THEM COME**, forming one release campaign: the barrier, the opening, the embrace.

### THE HAND BETWEEN  (title: LET THEM COME)

- **THE IDEA:** A single enormous stop palm dominates the poster, while Jesus’s displeased face remains visible just beyond its edge.
- **WHAT YOU SEE:** At the top, quiet parchment. Across the middle, a follower’s raised palm fills almost half the frame through close perspective; its attached forearm and earth-colored sleeve establish ownership. Beyond it, Jesus in his patched blue robe looks directly at the follower, serious and controlled. At the bottom right, a mother holds her calm toddler, waiting. Honey-colored stone, dusty ochre, concentrated blue; dry ink edges and rich gouache shadows. Light enters from the side.
- **LETTERING:** Add real type later: three stacked lines across the upper quarter, heavy condensed capitals in deep brown. **TAGLINE:** “His friends said no.”
- **THE CURIOSITY GAP:** Why is Jesus angry with his own friends? The film answers when the followers block the families and Jesus tells them to let the children come.
- **PHONE TEST:** One stop hand. One unmistakable blue figure. One large command. The obstacle reads before any facial detail.
- **IMAGE PROMPT:** Vertical 9:16 theatrical key art, rich painted cinematic storybook, warm watercolor-and-gouache, expressive ink, dramatic film-poster lighting. Use film pictures 3 and 4 as character and costume references. Extreme foreground: one follower’s anatomically natural raised stop palm, attached to a visible earth-colored sleeve, occupying nearly half the composition. Beyond its right edge, clearly visible Jesus with matching long dark hair, beard, patched blue robe and rope belt; his controlled displeasure is directed at the follower. Lower right, a mother securely holds her calm toddler. Simplify the village to warm stone planes. Quiet parchment upper quarter for later typography. Strong blue against ochre, warm lateral light. Follower is ordinary and mistaken, never sinister; child safe and unafraid. No halo or rays behind Jesus’s head, no lettering, no text

### THE OPENING  (title: LET THEM COME)

- **THE IDEA:** Two monumental grown-up backs form a wall, with a narrow opening revealing Jesus’s open arms at a child’s height.
- **WHAT YOU SEE:** Broad clay and olive robes fill the upper corners and both sides, cropped into almost abstract shapes. Between them runs a clean, irregular ribbon of golden daylight. Jesus stands within that opening, blue robe and open arms clearly legible. At the bottom, a small boy holds his mother’s hand; her arm and body remain visible along the right edge. He looks toward Jesus. The adult wall feels solid and ordinary, while the opening feels newly possible.
- **LETTERING:** Add real type later across the broad upper backs: **LET THEM COME**, three substantial cream lines, occupying the upper third. **TAGLINE:** None.
- **THE CURIOSITY GAP:** Will the grown-ups make room—and who will insist on it? The answer comes with Jesus’s command, followed by the children running toward him.
- **PHONE TEST:** A wall, a bright opening, a blue welcome. The large shapes carry the entire story.
- **IMAGE PROMPT:** Vertical 9:16, rich painted cinematic storybook poster, warm watercolor-and-gouache with confident ink and film-poster lighting. Use film pictures 1, 3 and 5 as references. Graphic composition: two close foreground followers seen from behind, their broad earth-colored robes forming a nearly continuous wall across the sides and upper frame. A narrow irregular opening between them reveals sunlit village stone and Jesus, matching the film’s hair, beard, patched blue robe and rope belt, smiling gently with arms open. Bottom foreground, a small boy holds his mother’s hand; her connected arm and partial body are plainly visible. Child is relaxed and accompanied. Preserve a bold readable opening and simple robe shapes. Followers have natural postures, no menace. Golden side light, no halo or head-centered rays, no lettering, no text

### ARMS FULL  (title: LET THEM COME)

- **THE IDEA:** Turn the favorite still into a monumental three-person portrait: the children rise highest, and Jesus’s blue robe becomes the poster’s foundation.
- **WHAT YOU SEE:** A laughing girl on Jesus’s shoulders crowns the upper frame. His smiling face sits below hers; the second girl, held securely on his hip, completes a broad triangular silhouette. Their faces are large and close. Blue fabric fills the lower third. A fragment of stone arch and an olive branch sit off-center against apricot light. Thick painted highlights give the embrace the weight of a major film release.
- **LETTERING:** Add real type later across the lower blue robe: warm ivory capitals, three lines, roughly one quarter of the poster’s height. **TAGLINE:** “Someone said no.”
- **THE CURIOSITY GAP:** Who could object to this—and why did Jesus have to intervene? The blocking scene supplies the conflict; the final embrace supplies the answer.
- **PHONE TEST:** Three joyful faces, one great blue shape, one blunt contradiction in the tagline.
- **IMAGE PROMPT:** Vertical 9:16, rich painted cinematic storybook theatrical poster, warm watercolor-and-gouache, hand-drawn ink, luminous film-poster lighting. Use film picture 9 as the primary exact character, clothing and pose reference; picture 7 for joyful expression. Close monumental portrait of Jesus smiling, one laughing girl securely on his shoulders and another securely supported on his hip. Match every character’s appearance and costume. Three large readable faces form a triangular composition; patched blue robe and rope belt anchor the lower frame. Leave broad quiet blue fabric below for later typography. Minimal off-center stone arch fragment and olive branch, apricot background, warm lateral highlights. Natural anatomy and secure holds. No halo or head-centered sunburst, no lettering, no text

### MY #1 and why

**THE HAND BETWEEN.** The raised palm makes the conflict readable instantly and connects directly to “Why Jesus Was Angry.”  
It leaves the essential question open: what happens when the person saying “stop” is standing in Jesus’s way?
