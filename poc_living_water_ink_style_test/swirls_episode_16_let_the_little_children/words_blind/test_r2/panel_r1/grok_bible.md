## 1. WHO / WHAT / WHY in your own words

People bring young children to Jesus so He will touch them. The disciples shut that down. Jesus is much displeased, says to let the children come, and says the kingdom of God is of such as them. He then takes the children up, lays His hands on them, and blesses them. The film offers that scene to someone who has been told they are in the way.

## 2. FIRST 2 SECONDS: would a pushed-aside viewer stop? score 0-10 and why

**8.** "Has someone told you 'not now'?" names the wound at once. "Then stay. This story is yours." is a clear invite. I would stop. "This story is yours" is a promise the story has not earned yet. The question alone is strong enough.

## 3. ENDING: does it land and feel earned, or like a slogan? score 0-10; quote the line

**6.** "The ones others turned away are the ones Jesus picked up."

The line before it is earned: "They hoped for a touch. He gave the children His arms." That sits on Mark 10:16. The last line turns one scene into a rule. It sounds like a poster.

## 4. WHERE I GOT LOST OR MISLED (quote the words)

"Upset." The text says "much displeased." That is strong indignation. The same word at Mark 10:41 is the ten disciples, angry. "Upset" makes it mild.

"They hoped for one small thing" and "Just one touch." The text states the purpose: "that he should touch them." It never calls the touch small, and it never says they merely hoped.

"They told them no." The quote just named "those that brought them." "Them" blurs the bringers and the children.

"God's kingdom belongs to people like these." That is a fair gloss of "of such is the kingdom of God" only while Mark 10:15 is still in view. That verse is missing. Jesus says whoever will not receive the kingdom as a little child shall not enter it. Without it, "people like these" widens into "anyone who was pushed aside."

## 5. ACCURACY vs the KJV (RIGHT / OVERCLAIMED / WRONG, quote)

**RIGHT:** "that he should touch them." **RIGHT:** "rebuked those that brought them." **RIGHT:** "much displeased." **RIGHT:** the Jesus speech, word for word from Mark 10:14. **RIGHT:** "He took them up in his arms, put his hands upon them, and blessed them." **RIGHT:** "let them come. Do not stop them."

**OVERCLAIMED:** "one small thing" / "Just one touch." **OVERCLAIMED:** "Upset." **OVERCLAIMED:** "The ones others turned away are the ones Jesus picked up." True of these children in this scene. As a standing law, it goes past the text. The summary also keeps the arms and drops the hands and the blessing, which the quoted verse had just included.

**WRONG:** no sentence flatly contradicts the KJV. The miss is silence. Mark 10:15 is not spoken.

## 6. OVERALL score 0-10, and the ONE change that would most improve it (exact wording)

**7.** I would send this to a struggling friend. The quotes are careful, and the welcome is real. I would not call it the whole of Mark 10:13-16.

**ONE change.** Keep the Jesus line, then add His next sentence, still in His voice:

"Verily I say unto you, Whosoever shall not receive the kingdom of God as a little child, he shall not enter therein."

That is Mark 10:15. It ties "of such" to the child before the narrator explains it.

## VERDICT

**REVISE.** Quotes are faithful. Verse 15 is missing, "upset" is soft, and the last line is a slogan. Add the Mark 10:15 sentence, then it is fit to send.
