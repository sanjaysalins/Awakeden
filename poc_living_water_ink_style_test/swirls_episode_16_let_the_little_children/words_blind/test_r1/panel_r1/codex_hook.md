## 1. WHO / WHAT / WHY in your own words (X, Y)

**X:** Jesus welcomes children whom His disciples obstruct. It directly invites a rejected viewer to recognise themselves in that welcome.

**Y:** The same encounter, framed as exclusion followed by a generous reversal: people seek a touch; Jesus gives an embrace.

Both have a clear emotional destination and a strong visual payoff.

## 2. FIRST 2 SECONDS: would a pushed-aside viewer stop? score 0-10 (X, Y) and why

**X: 8/10.** “Has someone told you ‘not now’?” identifies the intended viewer immediately. “Then stay” risks sounding like a retention instruction. The full invitation takes longer than two seconds.

**Y: 7/10.** “They were sent away” creates immediate injustice and curiosity. But “they” offers less personal recognition, and the opening presents an inference as an event.

Subjective stop-probability estimates for the intended viewer encountering this cold: **X 55–70%; Y 45–65%**. These are judgment ranges, not measured results.

## 3. ENDING: does it land and feel earned, or like a slogan? score 0-10 (X, Y); quote the line

**X: 8/10.** “The ones others turned away are the ones Jesus picked up.” Concrete, earned by the quoted action, and aligned with the laughing girls. However, it follows an already excellent ending: “He gave them His arms.”

**Y: 6/10.** “That is how Jesus answers the ones others send away.” Broader and more slogan-like. It shifts from this particular encounter to an implied promise about everyone’s experience.

For viewers who reach the embrace, X has the stronger likely share impulse: it names rejection and answers with a visible action.

## 4. WHERE I GOT LOST OR MISLED (quote the words)

**X:** “In plain words” begins a substantial explanation immediately after Jesus’s speech. Around the middle, momentum slows before “Now remember” restarts it.

**Y:** “It is the only time Mark’s book says that about Jesus” interrupts the emotional conflict with a textual fact. “Calls to the front” introduces a queue or ranking absent from the scene.

**Both:** At 65 seconds, these scripts require brisk delivery. The long KJV speech needs breathing room. Conditional on stopping, my rough completion estimates are **X 40–60%; Y 35–55%**, with considerable uncertainty from delivery and editing.

## 5. ACCURACY vs the KJV (RIGHT / OVERCLAIMED / WRONG, quote)

**X — RIGHT:** “He took them up in his arms…” accurately supplies the payoff.  
**OVERCLAIMED:** “Go away” is an interpretive gloss on rebuking the bringers.

**Y — RIGHT:** “‘Suffer’ means ‘let.’” The “only time…about Jesus” claim is consistent with the supplied evidence; 10:41 concerns disciples.  
**OVERCLAIMED:** “Before they even got close” supplies unstated staging.

**Both:** Omit verse 15, reducing the passage’s emphasis on receiving the kingdom as a child.

## 6. PICK: X or Y, overall score 0-10 each, and the ONE change that would most improve your pick (exact wording)

**Pick X: 8/10. Y: 7/10.**

Replace X’s explanatory paragraph with:

“Let them come. Do not stop them. God’s kingdom belongs to ones like these.”
