## 1. WHO / WHAT / WHY
**X:** Parents bring small children to Jesus, hoping He will just touch them. His disciples scold them. Jesus gets angry at the disciples, says to let the children come, then holds and blesses them. The point is that people others push away are the ones Jesus holds close.
**Y:** It is the same story, and the "why" is the same. Y adds a Bible-reader fact (this is the only time Mark calls Jesus "much displeased") and a word note ("suffer" = "let").

## 2. FIRST 2 SECONDS
**X: 8/10.** "Has someone told you 'not now'?" speaks straight to the target viewer and names their wound in three words. "This story is yours" is a clear promise.
**Y: 5/10.** "They were sent away before they even got close" is moody, but it doesn't say who "they" are. A pushed-aside viewer doesn't see themselves in it yet. It also claims more than the text says (see 5).

## 3. ENDING
**X: 7/10.** "The ones others turned away are the ones Jesus picked up." It is concrete and matches the final picture (girls lifted onto His shoulders). It is earned by the touch → arms payoff. It sits close to a slogan, but it comes from the story's own verb.
**Y: 6/10.** "That is how Jesus answers the ones others send away." This is more abstract. "Answers" is vaguer than "picked up," and it lands on a general idea instead of the picture.

## 4. WHERE I GOT LOST OR MISLED
- Both: "They hoped for a touch. He gave them His arms." The "they" who hoped were the people bringing the children. The ones held were the children. It's a small blur, but a careful reader will notice.
- X: "Not now. Go away." comes right after a KJV quote. A listener may hear it as the disciples' actual words. It is the narrator's paraphrase.
- Y: "With the men in the way" is fine, but "the only time Mark's book says that" will mean nothing to a newcomer. It speaks to Bible readers only.

## 5. ACCURACY vs the KJV
- "that he should touch them" and "rebuked those that brought them" (both): **RIGHT.**
- "much displeased… With the ones who sent them away" (both): **RIGHT** by context (v14 "said unto them").
- "God's kingdom belongs to ones like these" (X): **RIGHT**. That's a fair reading of "of such is."
- "Here, 'suffer' means 'let'" (Y): **RIGHT**, and helpful.
- "It is the only time Mark's book says that about Jesus" (Y): **RIGHT.** In the KJV the phrase appears in Mark only here and at 10:41, where it's used of the ten.
- "He took them up in his arms…" (both): **RIGHT**, word for word.
- "The ones told to stay back, He called close" (X): **OVERCLAIMED.** "Called them unto him" is Luke 18:16. Mark has no calling verb, so this mixes two Gospels without saying so.
- "Jesus calls to the front" (Y): **OVERCLAIMED.** There's no "calls" in Mark, and no "front" anywhere.
- "sent away before they even got close" (Y): **OVERCLAIMED.** Mark says the disciples rebuked the bringers. It doesn't say they were sent away, or how close they got.
- "picked up" (X): **RIGHT.** It's a fair plain-English version of "took them up."

Would I send it to a struggling friend? X, yes. Y, I'd hesitate because its hook doesn't speak to them.

## 6. PICK
**X: 7.5/10. Y: 6/10. Pick: X.**
X has the stronger hook, the more concrete ending, and fewer overclaims. Its one real accuracy slip is a Luke word dropped into Mark.

**One change to X (exact wording):** replace
"The ones told to stay back, He called close."
with
**"The ones told to stay back, He wanted close."**
This keeps the rhythm and the meaning, and every word stays inside Mark 10:13-16.
