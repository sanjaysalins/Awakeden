## 1. WHO / WHAT / WHY in your own words (X, Y)

**X.** Who: parents, their small children, Jesus, His disciples. What: the parents want one touch; the disciples block them; Jesus is angry at the blockers, calls the children in, and lifts them in His arms. Why: if you have been told "not now", Jesus is the one who picks you up. All three are clear, and the "why" is said to me directly.

**Y.** Same who and what, just as clear. Why: Jesus answers pushed-away people with more than they asked. But the film never says it is about me. I have to make that jump myself.

## 2. FIRST 2 SECONDS: would a pushed-aside viewer stop? (X, Y)

**X: 7.** "Has someone told you 'not now'?" names the viewer's own hurt in simple words. "Then stay. This story is yours." is a promise. Weak spot: a question opener is a common shape, and "Then stay" is a little bossy.

**Y: 5.** "They were sent away before they even got close." has some pull, but "They" is nobody yet. It is about strangers, not me. A hurt viewer has no reason to think this is theirs.

## 3. ENDING: does it land and feel earned, or like a slogan? (X, Y)

**X: 8.** "The ones others turned away are the ones Jesus picked up." Earned. "Picked up" is the literal act in verse 16 and matches the last picture. It also closes the loop from "not now". Small cost: it echoes the earlier "The ones told to stay back, He called close", so the same shape is heard twice.

**Y: 6.** "That is how Jesus answers the ones others send away." True, but abstract. "That" points backward and "answers" is a soft verb. The real ending is the line before it: "They came hoping for a touch. He gave them His arms." The last line reads like a summary added on top.

## 4. WHERE I GOT LOST OR MISLED (quote the words)

**X.**
- "Not now. Go away." Sounds like the disciples' words. Mark gives no words. A listener may take it as a quote.
- "God's kingdom belongs to ones like these." "ones like these" is clumsy for second-language ears.
- "Now remember what they hoped for." Teacher voice; tells me what to do.

**Y.**
- "Here, 'suffer' means 'let.'" Helpful, but a grammar note right after Jesus speaks cools the moment. And "for of such is the kingdom of God" is never explained, so the hardest part of the verse stays hard.
- "With the men in the way." Fine, but "men" is a small guess; the text says "disciples".
- "the ones pushed to the back... to the front" - front/back is a picture the text does not have.

## 5. ACCURACY vs the KJV

**X.**
- "that he should touch them", "rebuked those that brought them", "much displeased", verse 16 quote: RIGHT.
- "Not with the children. With the ones who sent them away.": RIGHT (fair reading of v14).
- "He called close": RIGHT (Luke 18:16, "called them unto him").
- "Not now. Go away.": mildly OVERCLAIMED (invented speech).
- "God's kingdom belongs to ones like these": RIGHT (fair plain reading of "of such is").

**Y.**
- All quotes: RIGHT.
- "It is the only time Mark's book says that about Jesus.": RIGHT (the other use, 10:41, is about the ten). Best fresh fact in either version.
- "calls to the front": slightly OVERCLAIMED (image not in text).

Neither is WRONG anywhere.

## 6. PICK

**Pick: X.** Overall **X: 7.5**, **Y: 6.5**.

X speaks to the pushed-aside viewer in second one and ends on the act itself. Y is tighter and has the better fact, but stays about other people.

**The ONE change:** swap X's repeated line "The ones told to stay back, He called close." for Y's fact, placed after "much displeased":

> NARRATOR: Then Jesus saw it. And Mark says He was "much displeased." It is the only time Mark's book says that about Jesus. Not with the children. With the ones who sent them away.

This removes the double ending shape and adds the one detail a Bible reader did not know.
