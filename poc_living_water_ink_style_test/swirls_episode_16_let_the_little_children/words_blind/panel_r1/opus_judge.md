## TITLE
They Asked for a Touch

## WHO / TAKEAWAY / CURIOSITY GAP / WHY IT EARNS ITS PLACE
- **WHO:** Someone who has been pushed to the back and told, in words or without words, "not you, not now."
- **TAKEAWAY:** The ones others send away are the ones Jesus calls closer, and He gives more than they dared to ask.
- **CURIOSITY GAP:** They only hoped for one touch, and were stopped. So what happened to that small hope?
- **WHY IT EARNS ITS PLACE:** The text's own twist (a request for a touch, answered with open arms) carries the whole film, so the ending is a fact from Mark, not a sales line.

## NARRATION
NARRATOR: They were sent away before they even got close.
Mark says people brought young children to Jesus, "that he should touch them."
That was the whole hope. Just one touch.
But his disciples "rebuked those that brought them."
Jesus saw it. And Mark says he was "much displeased." Not with the children. With the men in the way.

JESUS: "Suffer the little children to come unto me, and forbid them not: for of such is the kingdom of God."

NARRATOR: Here, "suffer" means "let." Let them come. Do not stop them. The ones pushed to the back, Jesus calls to the front.
So what happened to that small hope, one touch?
Mark tells us. "He took them up in his arms, put his hands upon them, and blessed them."
They came hoping for a touch.
He gave them his arms.
That is how Jesus answers the ones others send away.

## WHY THE OPENING HOLDS A PUSHED-ASIDE VIEWER
The first line is their own story: stopped before you even reach the door.
It opens a small, clear question (did they get the touch?) that the viewer wants answered.

## WHY THE ENDING IS EARNED
The ending pays off the word "touch" from Mark 10:13, set up three times early ("that he should touch them", "just one touch", "one touch?").
Mark 10:16 answers it in the text's own words: not one touch, but arms, hands and a blessing.

## RISKS
Newcomer: "sent away" and "the men in the way" are my plain paraphrase of "rebuked"; the text does not say how far they were sent or why, and "suffer" must be glossed clearly or it misleads.
Bible reader: "Jesus calls to the front" leans on Luke 18:16 ("called them unto him"), and "not with the children" is a fair reading of "said unto them" but is interpretation, not quoted text.
