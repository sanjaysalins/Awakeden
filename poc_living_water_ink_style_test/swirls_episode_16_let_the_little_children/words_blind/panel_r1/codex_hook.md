## TITLE (at most 6 words)
They Came for a Touch

## WHO / TAKEAWAY / CURIOSITY GAP / WHY IT EARNS ITS PLACE (one line each)
WHO: Someone who has been made to feel in the way.
TAKEAWAY: Jesus’s welcome reaches beyond the people who block the way.
CURIOSITY GAP: When Jesus’s own disciples stop the children, what will Jesus do?
WHY IT EARNS ITS PLACE: The story moves from a requested touch to children held in His arms.

## NARRATION (speaker tag before each part: NARRATOR: or JESUS: ; then the word count)

NARRATOR:
They brought children to Jesus—and got told off.

Maybe you know what it is like to be made to feel in the way.

These children were brought so Jesus would touch them.

But His disciples rebuked the people bringing them.

Then Jesus saw.

And He was much displeased.

The objection came from His disciples. It did not come from Him.

He told them to let the children come.

JESUS:
“forbid them not: for of such is the kingdom of God.”

NARRATOR:
Then Jesus said God’s kingdom must be received like a little child.

Received. A gift does not begin with proving you are important enough.

And Jesus did more than let them through.

They had been brought for a touch.

He took them up in His arms, put His hands on them, and blessed them.

If you have been pushed aside, look at where this story ends.

It ends with Jesus holding the children His disciples tried to keep away.

**Word count: 159**

## PICTURES (7-9 pictures in order; the last is the fixed picture; each: which words it covers + what we see + what MOVES in it)

1. **“They brought children…in the way.”** A child beside an accompanying adult; a disciple stands across their path. **MOVES:** The approaching feet stop; the disciple raises an open palm.

2. **“These children were brought…bringing them.”** A wider view reveals Jesus beyond the interrupted group. **MOVES:** A child leans forward; the accompanying adult pauses.

3. **“Then Jesus saw…much displeased.”** Jesus looks directly toward the interruption, His expression serious. **MOVES:** His head turns; the camera moves closer.

4. **“The objection came…let the children come.”** Jesus addresses His disciples, with the children visible beside them. **MOVES:** He gestures toward the children; the disciples begin stepping aside.

5. **“forbid them not…kingdom of God.”** Jesus speaks with the path now open between Him and the children. **MOVES:** His welcoming hand opens; a child takes a step.

6. **“Then Jesus said…important enough.”** A child stands before Jesus with empty hands. **MOVES:** The small hands open gently; Jesus lowers Himself toward the child.

7. **“And Jesus did more…blessed them.”** Close views of Jesus gathering children near and resting His hands upon them. **MOVES:** His arms gather; His hands settle gently.

8. **“If you have been pushed aside…keep away.”** The fixed picture: smiling Jesus holds two laughing little girls, one hugging His side, one on His shoulders with her hand in His hair; old stone arch, olive tree, warm sunlight. **MOVES:** Their shoulders lift with laughter; her fingers stir His hair; olive leaves sway. Hold through the final words.

## WHY THE OPENING HOLDS A PUSHED-ASIDE VIEWER (2 lines)
The first sentence puts rejection inside an unexpected place: an approach to Jesus.
The viewer’s own experience becomes the reason to stay and discover His response.

## WHY THE ENDING IS EARNED (2 lines, name the concrete thing from the passage it pays off)
The concrete request is a **touch**, established before the disciples interrupt.
Jesus answers with His hands and His **arms**; the closing picture completes that movement.

## RISKS (2 lines: what could be misread, by a newcomer or by a Bible reader)
A newcomer could hear the disciples’ rejection as Jesus’s position; His displeasure and welcome explicitly correct that.
A Bible reader could mistake the girls, laughter, and shoulder ride for recorded details; these belong to the required illustration, not the narration.
