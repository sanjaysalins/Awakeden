## TITLE
They Asked for One Touch

## WHO / TAKEAWAY / CURIOSITY GAP / WHY IT EARNS ITS PLACE
- **WHO:** Someone who has been told "not now" or "you're in the way", and feels too small to matter.
- **TAKEAWAY:** The people others sent away are the ones Jesus took in His arms. He gave them more than they asked for.
- **CURIOSITY GAP:** Mark calls Jesus "much displeased" only once in his whole book. Who was it at, and what did He do next?
- **WHY IT EARNS ITS PLACE:** Most people know "let the little children come." Few notice the twist: the parents asked for a touch, and Jesus gave His arms, His hands and a blessing.

## NARRATION
NARRATOR: Have you ever been sent away before you even reached the door?
Some people once brought little children to Jesus.
They wanted just one small thing:
"that he should touch them."
One touch.
But His own disciples "rebuked those that brought them."
Sent away.
Now watch Jesus.
Only once in Mark's whole book is Jesus called "much displeased."
Not with the children. With the ones who stopped them.
He said, in plain words: let them come. Do not stop them.

JESUS: Suffer the little children to come unto me, and forbid them not: for of such is the kingdom of God.

NARRATOR: God's kingdom belongs to ones like these.
And then He went past what they asked.
They hoped for a touch.
Mark says "he took them up in his arms, put his hands upon them, and blessed them."
Hands. Arms. A blessing.
The ones who were sent away were the ones He held.
They came for one touch. They ended up in His arms.

**Word count: 163** (about 65-70 seconds)

## WHY THE OPENING HOLDS A PUSHED-ASIDE VIEWER
The first line asks about the viewer's own wound: being turned away before you even got in. That is a human stake in two seconds, with no Bible knowledge needed.
Then it shows the people who got turned away were asking for almost nothing, just one touch. Anyone who has been refused a small request feels that.

## WHY THE ENDING IS EARNED
The film sets up "one touch," the parents' own small request in Mark 10:13, and repeats it three times.
The ending pays it off with Mark 10:16: they asked for a touch, and Jesus gave His arms, His hands and a blessing. The people sent away are the people held, and that is the fixed closing picture.

## RISKS
- **Newcomer:** "much displeased" could sound like Jesus is angry at the viewer. The next line points it straight at the people blocking the children, not the children. "Suffer" is explained in plain words before the Jesus voice says it.
- **Bible reader:** "only once in Mark" is true of the wording "much displeased" (Mark 3:5 calls Jesus angry in other words), so the film must never say "the only time Jesus was angry." Also, "came for a touch, ended up in His arms" describes those children, not a promise about every viewer's circumstances.
