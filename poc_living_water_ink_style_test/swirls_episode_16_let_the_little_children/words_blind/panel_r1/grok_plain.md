## TITLE
His Own Friends Said No

## WHO / TAKEAWAY / CURIOSITY GAP / WHY IT EARNS ITS PLACE
WHO: A person who has been stopped, and left feeling in the way.
TAKEAWAY: Jesus was angry at the stop, then took the children up and blessed them.
CURIOSITY GAP: His own friends said no — would Jesus say no too?
WHY IT EARNS ITS PLACE: The touch they came for is given in his arms, as a blessing.

## NARRATION
NARRATOR: Jesus's own friends said no. People had brought young children to him. They wanted him to touch them. The friends stood in the way.

NARRATOR: Jesus saw it. He was much displeased. Angry at what he saw. Not with the children. With the ones who stopped them. He called the children to him, and said,

JESUS: Suffer the little children to come unto me, and forbid them not: for of such is the kingdom of God.

NARRATOR: Suffer means let. Let the little children come to me, and do not forbid them. The kingdom of God, he said, belongs to people like these.

NARRATOR: Receive the kingdom of God as a little child. He said that too. Not a test someone has to pass. He was about to show it with his own arms.

NARRATOR: And he took them up in his arms, put his hands upon them, and blessed them. They had been brought for a touch. What he gave them was his arms, his hands, and a blessing.

## PICTURES
1. "Jesus's own friends said no." Warm pencil-and-watercolour, vertical. A sunny stone path. Adults carry young children toward a bright arch. Two friends of Jesus step across the path, palms up. MOVES: the palms rise, and the walking stops.

2. "People had brought young children to him. They wanted him to touch them. The friends stood in the way." Close and low, at child height. A small open hand reaches through. A friend's sleeve fills the gap. Jesus is only a soft shape of light beyond. MOVES: the small fingers stretch, then stop in mid-air.

3. "Jesus saw it. He was much displeased. Angry at what he saw." Jesus turns. His eyes go to the blocking hands, not to the children. The watercolour on his face cools. MOVES: his brow tightens, and his head turns toward the friends.

4. "Not with the children. With the ones who stopped them. He called the children to him, and said," His face softens as he looks past the friends. One hand reaches through the gap toward the children. MOVES: his hand passes the blocking hands, and those hands begin to lower. A child is turned back toward him.

5. The Jesus line: "Suffer the little children to come unto me, and forbid them not: for of such is the kingdom of God." Under the old stone arch, Jesus speaks. The friends step aside. Small feet come forward. An infant is still carried, but carried toward him. MOVES: his mouth speaks, the friends make room, and olive leaves shift in the warm light.

6. "Suffer means let. Let the little children come to me, and do not forbid them. The kingdom of God, he said, belongs to people like these." Jesus lowers himself to their height. No one stands between. Children gather in close. MOVES: he comes down until his eyes meet theirs, and a carried child leans toward his chest.

7. "Receive the kingdom of God as a little child. He said that too. Not a test someone has to pass. He was about to show it with his own arms." His arms open, still empty. Sunlight warms them. A child is passed toward him. MOVES: both arms open wide, and the space in them waits.

8. "And he took them up in his arms, put his hands upon them, and blessed them. They had been brought for a touch. What he gave them was his arms, his hands, and a blessing." The fixed picture. Jesus smiles. Two little girls laugh: one hugs him from the side, one sits up on his shoulders with her hand in his hair. Old stone arch, olive tree, warm sunlight. MOVES: her fingers curl in his hair, the girl at his side holds on, and his hands rest on them as the light moves through the leaves.

## WHY THE OPENING HOLDS A PUSHED-ASIDE VIEWER
The first words are a no, from Jesus's own friends, to children who were brought only to be touched.
A person who has heard "not now" feels that stop at once, and needs to know if Jesus will agree.

## WHY THE ENDING IS EARNED
The film begins with the touch they were brought for, and with friends standing in the way of it.
It pays that off in his arms: he took them up, put his hands upon them, and blessed them, so the touch is given while he holds them.

## RISKS
A newcomer may fear, for one line, that Jesus is angry at the children, or may think the kingdom word is only about little kids.
A Bible reader may notice "shall not enter therein" is not spoken, and may think the warning was hidden. It is left out so it cannot be heard as a threat. The film also does not know, or say, why the disciples did it.
