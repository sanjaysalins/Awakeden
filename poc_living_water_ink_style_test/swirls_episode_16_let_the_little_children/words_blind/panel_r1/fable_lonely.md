## TITLE (at most 6 words)
They Asked For One Touch

## WHO / TAKEAWAY / CURIOSITY GAP / WHY IT EARNS ITS PLACE (one line each)
- **WHO:** A person scrolling alone who was told "not now" or "you are in the way" today, and has started to believe God says it too.
- **TAKEAWAY:** When people were turned away from Jesus, He was displeased with the ones who turned them away, and He gave far more than was asked: not a touch, His arms.
- **CURIOSITY GAP:** They asked Jesus for one small thing and were told no. What did He do when He saw it, and what did He give in the end?
- **WHY IT EARNS ITS PLACE:** It is the one place in this story where Jesus is displeased, and it is on the side of the people who were pushed aside.

## WHY THE OPENING HOLDS A PUSHED-ASIDE VIEWER (2 lines)
The first three words name the viewer's own day ("not now") before any Bible word is spoken, and the picture is a child stuck behind a wall of backs: they see themselves first.
"Then stay. This story is yours" is the opposite of what they were told today, so the film's first act is to do for the viewer what Jesus does in the story.

## WHY THE ENDING IS EARNED (2 lines, name the concrete thing from the passage it pays off)
The concrete thing is the **touch** in Mark 10:13 ("that he should touch them"), set up early as "one small thing... just a touch" and called back by name before the end.
Mark 10:16 pays it off with more than was asked ("he took them up in his arms"), so the last picture, Jesus's arms full, is the answer to the first request, not an added message.

## RISKS (2 lines: what could be misread, by a newcomer or by a Bible reader)
Newcomer: "the ones Jesus picked up" could be heard as a promise that every pushed-aside person will be lifted out of their trouble; it is a statement of what He did in this story and who He is, not a promise that problems end. "Suffer" is explained at once so it is not heard as "pain".
Bible reader: Mark 10:15 (receive the kingdom "as a little child") is left out, a real cut, made so the verse is never bent into a threat; "He called close" rests on Luke 18:16 ("Jesus called them unto him"), and "told them no" is plain English for "rebuked... forbid", with no motive given for the disciples.
