# Independent review — grok (OK, 370s)

The KJV quotes in the four captions are exact. Do not ship the kit yet. One posted line is false, the Mark 10:15 plain-English line changes Jesus’ words, and the thumbnail can be read the wrong way.

**1. Pinned comment — false**
Phrase: “What did Jesus do when His own followers said that to the children?”
Mark 10:13 says the disciples “rebuked those that brought them.” They did not say “not now.” They spoke to the people who brought the children, not to the children. This line also fights the YouTube and Facebook disclaimer that “not now” is not a Bible quote.
Replacement: “Has anyone ever told you ‘not now’? In Mark 10:13-16, Jesus’ followers rebuked the people who brought children to Him. He was much displeased, and He took the children up in His arms. Read it: Mark 10:13-16.”

**2. Facebook — Mark 10:15 is restated wrongly**
Phrase: “Then He said grown-ups must come the same way, like a little child.”
Mark 10:15 says receive the kingdom as a little child, or not enter it. “Come” is a different verb. Facebook never quotes verse 15, so this wrong gloss is all the reader gets.
YouTube quotes the verse exactly, then restates it as “Grown-ups come to God the same way: like a little child.” That plain line drops “receive the kingdom” and “shall not enter.”
The film’s own line is better: “grown-ups must receive it the same way: like a little child.”
Replacement (Facebook and YouTube): “He also said anyone who does not receive God’s kingdom the way a little child does will not enter it.”

**3. Thumbnail — all platforms — “LOOK WHO JESUS PICKED UP”**
“Picked up” can mean a stranger taking a child, or “the one Jesus chose.” Auto-translate often goes that way. The picture (one girl on His shoulders, her hand in His hair, both laughing) is not Mark 10:16. The verse says He “took them up in his arms.” The film’s on-screen words say “His arms.” None of the four captions say He took them up and blessed them, so the thumbnail’s promise is not in the text beside the video.
Replacement title on the picture: “JESUS TOOK THEM IN HIS ARMS” with “Mark 10:13-16.” Show both children in His arms. Add this sentence to every caption: “He took them up in his arms, put his hands upon them, and blessed them.”

**4. YouTube title — “Why Jesus Got Angry” / Facebook title — “Jesus Was Angry.”**
The KJV word is “much displeased.” YouTube does say that in the description (“In one plain word: angry.”). “One plain word” is too strong. “Much” is gone, and the word is indignation, not a temper clip. The titles still lead with “Angry,” which is the sensational reading.
Replacement YouTube title (62 is fine; this is 47): “They Tried to Stop the Children | Mark 10:13-16”
Replacement Facebook title: “They Tried to Stop People Bringing Children to Jesus”
Keep “angry” only in the body, beside the KJV: “The Bible says ‘much displeased.’ The film uses the plain word ‘angry.’ He was upset with the followers who tried to stop them, not with the children.”

**5. TikTok and Instagram — “‘not now’” can sound like Scripture**
TikTok: “Has someone told you ‘not now’? In Mark 10…”
Instagram: “Pushed away? Told ‘not now’?”
YouTube and Facebook say this is not a Bible quote. TikTok and Instagram do not. “Pushed away” can sound like a shove. The text says “rebuked.”
Replacement first line (both): “Has someone told you ‘not now’? Those are our words, not a Bible quote. In Mark 10, Jesus’ followers tried to stop people bringing children to Him.”

**6. Instagram — “stop children coming to Him”**
TikTok is more exact: “stop people bringing children to Him.” Use the TikTok wording on Instagram too.

**7. Next teaser — “a mother who would not take no for an answer.”**
“Take no for an answer” will fail for many readers and for auto-translate. It can also make Jesus sound like He lost an argument. Do not post this on the Mark 10 film.
Replacement, only when that next film is real: “Next: a mother who kept asking Jesus for help.”

**8. “Old English”**
YouTube: “In old English, ‘suffer’ means ‘let’.” Facebook: “old English for ‘let’.”
KJV English is not Old English. Translators trip on that label.
Replacement: “In the King James Bible, ‘suffer’ means ‘let’.”

**9. Captions file**
“His own close followers” adds “close.” The word in Mark is “disciples.”
The arms quote drops “And” and capitalizes “He.” The rest of that quote is exact. Small, but it is a changed word.
Replacement: “His followers ‘rebuked those that brought them.’” And quote the verse as “And he took them up in his arms, put his hands upon them, and blessed them.”

**What can stay**
These quotes match the KJV word for word: “that he should touch them.” / “rebuked those that brought them.” / “much displeased.” / “Suffer the little children to come unto me, and forbid them not: for of such is the kingdom of God.” / “Whosoever shall not receive the kingdom of God as a little child, he shall not enter therein.”
The shareable fact is fair: “much displeased” is limited to Mark 10:14, and the reason given is that they tried to stop children being brought to Him.
“You can come to Him too” points to Jesus and does not use fear, money, or works. Matthew 19:13-15 and Luke 18:15-17 are the right parallels, and their different wording is not mixed into the Mark quotes.
