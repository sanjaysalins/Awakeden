# Red-team review: upload kit for the Mark 10:13-16 short ("Look who Jesus picked up")

## 1. Bible accuracy (KJV)

**The quotes are exact.** I checked them word for word: "that he should touch them", "rebuked those that brought them", "much displeased", the Mark 10:14 line, the "Whosoever…" line, and "He took them up in his arms…". The opening "And" is dropped, which is fine.

**Overclaims to fix:**
- **Pinned comment:** "What did Jesus do when His own followers said that to the children?" This is wrong on two counts. The followers never said "not now". And they rebuked *"those that brought them"*, not the children.
  → Use: **"What did Jesus do when His own followers tried to stop people bringing children to Him?"**
- **Shareable fact:** "Jesus is 'much displeased' with His own followers." Mark does not say who He was displeased with. That He was displeased with the followers is a fair reading, but the quote marks make it look like the text says so.
  → Use: **"In Mark 10:14 Jesus is 'much displeased' when His own followers try to stop children being brought to Him."**
- **Thumbnail:** a girl "on His shoulders". The text says *"took them up in his arms"*. It is a small addition, but the thumbnail claims to picture Mark 10:16. Arms only is safer.
- **YouTube description:** "Grown-ups come to God the same way." The text says *"Whosoever… receive the kingdom of God"*, which covers everyone, not just adults.
  → Use: **"Everyone must receive God's kingdom the same way: like a little child."**

## 2. Clickbait and misleading text
- **YouTube title:** "Why Jesus Got Angry" is honest. The Greek word means indignant, and the description explains "much displeased". Pass.
- **On-screen label:** "Let the children come to Me." is tagged "Jesus · Mark 10:14", so a paraphrase looks like a citation. That is a film issue, so it is only noted here. In the metadata, never put that wording in quote marks.

## 3. Global / ESL clarity
- **TikTok and Instagram:** both quote "Suffer the little children…" with no gloss. Modern readers and auto-translate will read "suffer" as "feel pain", so it sounds like the children are suffering. "In plain words: let them come" does not fix this.
  → Add **"('Suffer' here means 'let'.)"** to both, as YouTube and Facebook already do.
- **Next teaser:** "would not take no for an answer" is an idiom that translates badly.
  → Use **"Next: a mother who kept asking Jesus for help."** Only post it once that episode really exists.
- **Thumbnail and final line:** "picked up" also translates badly. Many languages turn it into "fetched" or "collected" (see 4).

## 4. Cultural and child-safety sensitivity (the most serious problem)
- **Thumbnail:** "LOOK WHO JESUS PICKED UP" sits over a grown man holding two little girls. In everyday English, "picked up" also means meeting someone for sex or a date ("picked up a girl at a bar"). Bad-faith viewers can screenshot this as a mocking meme. Automated child-safety systems may also flag it, and the drawn hand in His hair adds closeness.
  → Change the thumbnail text to **"LOOK WHO JESUS HELD"** or **"JESUS TOOK THEM IN HIS ARMS"** (the KJV's own wording). Consider showing both children held in His arms, ideally a boy and a girl, so it matches "them" and v.16.
- The film's last line also says "picked up". If the film stays locked, the spoken line is softer in context. The thumbnail is the real risk because it is seen with no context.

## 5. Platform rules
- **YouTube:** title is about 62 characters (OK), 5 hashtags (OK), and the tags are real search phrases with no stuffing.
- **TikTok:** 5 hashtags. **Instagram:** 6. **Facebook:** 3. All OK.
- **YouTube description:** move the hashtags above "Follow:". It is fine as is, but the first 3 hashtags are already the ones that matter.

## 6. AI-sounding wording and disclosure
- The public text has no AI wording, which matches the channel rule. Disclosure depends on the platform AI switches being turned on. Add it to the upload checklist: YouTube "altered/synthetic", TikTok "AI-generated", and Meta "AI info". If a switch is skipped, the disclosure is not honest.
- **YouTube description:** "In plain words" / "In one plain word" appears 3 times. That sounds templated, so cut one.

## 7. Consistency with the film
- Mostly consistent. The "not now" note ("our short way to say it, not a Bible quote") is good, but it only appears on YouTube and Facebook. The TikTok and Instagram captions open with 'not now' and have no such note. Captions are short, so this is acceptable.
- **SRT:** entry 8 is an orphan "them." shown for 0.37 s. Merge it into entry 7.
