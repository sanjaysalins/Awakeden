# Independent review — codex (OK, 349s)

Revise before upload. The main problems are false attribution, an unsupported claim in the narration, and outdated platform guidance.

1. **YouTube/TikTok/Facebook/Instagram — shared pinned comment:** “What did Jesus do when His own followers said that to the children?”

   This turns **“not now” into reported dialogue**, although the YouTube and Facebook descriptions explicitly admit it is invented. Mark says the disciples rebuked **those bringing the children**; it does not record these words or say they addressed the children.

   **Replace with:** “Why was Jesus displeased when His disciples rebuked the people bringing children to Him? Read Mark 10:13-16.”

2. **All four platforms — narration and SRT cue 5:** “They wanted one thing:”

   “One thing” claims exclusive knowledge of their intentions. Mark supplies a purpose without saying it was their only purpose. Your supplied Matthew passage also mentions prayer.

   **Replace with:** “They brought the children to Jesus,” followed by the existing KJV excerpt, “that he should touch them.” Update the narration and SRT together.

3. **All four platforms — on-screen text:** “Let the children come to Me.” Label: “Jesus · Mark 10:14”

   These are **not the KJV words**. The attribution makes an unmarked paraphrase look like a direct Scripture quotation. The spoken KJV quotation and this screen text also differ.

   **Replacement label:** “Mark 10:14 — paraphrase.”

   Alternatively, display the exact KJV wording. The other quoted passages principally use genuine KJV excerpts; shortened quotations are not automatically altered words.

4. **All four platforms — thumbnail:** “one on His shoulders with her hand in His hair”

   Mark describes Jesus taking children **in His arms**, placing His hands on them, and blessing them. The shoulder ride is invented staging and distracts from the specific action promised by “LOOK WHO JESUS PICKED UP.”

   **Replace the staging with:** “Jesus holding children in His arms and blessing them.”

   **Plainer thumbnail wording:** “JESUS HELD THE CHILDREN.”

   This is an accuracy issue. The described affectionate storybook scene does not itself establish a child-safety violation.

5. **Instagram — caption and hashtag block:** “#LetTheChildrenCome #Mark10 #Jesus #Bible #BibleStory #Faith”

   There are **six hashtags**. Instagram announced a five-hashtag limit for posts and Reels; the kit’s “max 30” instruction is outdated. [Instagram announcement coverage](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-implements-new-limits-on-hashtag-use/808309/)

   **Replace both lists with:**  
   `#LetTheChildrenCome #Mark10 #Jesus #Bible #BibleStory`

   **YouTube — internal upload guidance:** “shows the first 3 hashtags, more than 15 voids all”

   Both statements need correction. YouTube can select up to three hashtags from the description; they are not necessarily the first three. Its published threshold for ignoring all hashtags is **more than 60**. The actual five-tag set here is within that limit. [YouTube hashtag rules](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6390658?hl=en)

   **Replacement instruction:** “YouTube may display up to three selected description hashtags. More than 60 causes all hashtags to be ignored.”

6. **Facebook — description:** “Then He said grown-ups must come the same way, like a little child.”

   **YouTube — description:** “Grown-ups come to God the same way: like a little child.”

   These shift Jesus’ statement from **receiving the kingdom** to “coming,” and narrow “Whosoever” to adults. They are imprecise paraphrases, rather than evidence of works-based doctrine.

   **Replace both with:** “Jesus said we must receive the kingdom of God as a little child.”

7. **YouTube/Facebook — explanation:** “In old English, ‘suffer’ means ‘let’” / “‘Suffer’ is old English for ‘let’.”

   Explain the word’s meaning **here**, rather than making a broad claim about English usage.

   **Replace with:** “Here, ‘suffer’ means ‘allow’.”

   **All four platforms — narration and SRT cue 28:** “He gave the children His arms.”

   This poetic construction is awkward for learners and machine translation; it can suggest giving away body parts.

   **Replace with:** “Jesus took the children in His arms and blessed them.”

8. **All four platforms — next teaser:** “Next: a mother who would not take no for an answer.”

   “Would not take no for an answer” is an idiom and frames the coming episode around persistence overcoming refusal. That risks suggesting persistence earns Jesus’ help. The next film is absent, so the promised episode cannot be verified.

   **Replace with:** “Next: a mother asks Jesus to help her child”—**if that accurately describes the scheduled film**. Otherwise, remove the teaser.

9. **All four platforms using this SRT — cues 7–8:** “rebuked those that brought” / “them.”

   Cue 8 displays “them.” for only **0.37 seconds**. Splitting off the final word makes the sentence harder to read.

   **Replace with one cue, 00:00:15,244–00:00:18,215:**

   ```text
   But His own close followers
   "rebuked those that brought them."
   ```

10. **YouTube/Facebook — footer:** “Scripture quoted from the King James Version (KJV), public domain.”

    “Public domain” is not an accurate worldwide statement. Cambridge’s KJV notice identifies continuing UK rights administered for the Crown. [Cambridge KJV rights notice](https://assets.cambridge.org/97805212/28626/copyright/9780521228626_copyright_info.pdf)

    **Replace with:** “Scripture quotations: King James Version (KJV).”

11. **TikTok/Instagram — closing CTA:** “Find Jesus in the whole Bible, one story at a time. Follow Awakeden.”

    This points toward learning about Jesus, but the concrete requested action is following the brand. These captions omit the personal invitation included on YouTube and Facebook.

    **Replace with:** “You can come to Jesus too. Read Mark 10:13-16. Follow Awakeden for more Bible stories.”

12. **YouTube — title:** “Let the Children Come to Me: Why Jesus Got Angry | Bible Story”

    **Lower priority:** it combines two hooks and ends with a generic category label. “Why Jesus Got Angry” is delivered by the film and is a defensible gloss of “much displeased”; the problem is diffuse packaging, not a false promise.

    **Sharper replacement:** “They Tried to Stop the Children. Jesus Took Them in His Arms.”

The 62-character YouTube title and 58-character Facebook title fit their stated limits. The search tags concern the actual passage; there is no clear unrelated keyword stuffing.

The instruction that the platforms’ AI-content switches are enabled provides a legitimate disclosure route. Missing AI wording in the captions is **not itself concealment** when the platform label is applied. The footer URLs are syntactically intact, but their destinations could not be verified. The actual thumbnail and rendered film were not supplied, so crop safety, visual execution, and audio synchronization remain unverified.
