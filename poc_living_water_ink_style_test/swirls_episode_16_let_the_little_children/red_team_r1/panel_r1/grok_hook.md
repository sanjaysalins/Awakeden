## 2. MAIN TAKEAWAY

They can say it back: "Jesus was angry when his friends stopped the children, and he hugged them."

The carrier is 29.6–31.4 plus "let them come" at 38.9. It is only half clear. "This story is for you" at 5.6 is paid at 71.2 by pointing at the children, so the viewer is not in the payoff. Mark 10:15's "he shall not enter therein" is never spoken. They leave with a hug, not the verse's stake.

## 3. GLOBAL AUDIENCE

With sound: Jesus and the children; his followers tried to stop them; he refused and held them, because people like these children belong in God's kingdom, and adults should receive it like a child.

Without sound that story dies. Captions are a side file. From 5.4 to 13.1 the screen has no words. Mute scraps are "Pushed away?", "not now," "a touch," "Angry.", "Let the children come to Me.", "like a little child," "His arms.", and at 71.3 "Look at who Jesus picked up."

Lost at 32.6. The voice says "Suffer the little children to come unto me, and forbid them not: for of such is the kingdom of God." In 1611, "suffer" means "allow." Today, and in machine translation, it means "be in pain." "Forbid them not" is a double negative. "Of such" does not travel. The blue box at 32.6 says "Let the children come to Me" while the voice says "Suffer." Ear and eye fight.

Also fails: "rebuked" at 15.2, "much displeased" at 22.8. "Angry." at 26.5 is clear and too hot. "Close followers" at 15.2 is the plain phrase that works.
