## 1. WHO IS THIS FOR (one named viewer; who stops, who finishes, who shares; who is NOT served)

Maria, a Filipino mother who understands everyday English but has never read an English Bible. She may stop at “Has someone told you ‘not now’?” (line 1), finish because Jesus welcomes children, and share the affectionate ending with family. The film serves parents and viewers who have felt unwelcome. It serves English beginners less well: the central speech uses its hardest English (line 5). People seeking an answer to a specific rejection may find the ending emotionally inviting but inconclusive (line 10).

## 2. MAIN TAKEAWAY (one sentence a viewer could say back; which line carries it; did the film make it clear?)

“Jesus wanted the children to come to Him, and He welcomed and blessed them”: line 8 makes this clear through an action viewers can understand even when some words are unfamiliar.

## 3. GLOBAL AUDIENCE (who / what / why in my own words; where I got lost or misled, quote the line; words that fail in translation)

People bring children to Jesus for His blessing; His followers interfere; Jesus corrects them and embraces the children (lines 2–8). That sequence travels well. In cultures where respected teachers seem difficult to approach, His welcome may be especially striking.

The vocabulary creates avoidable barriers:

- “Keep watching” (line 1) sounds like platform advertising and delays identifying the story.
- “Rebuked” (line 3) is rare. Use “told them off” only cautiously—that is another idiom. Plainest: “angrily told the people to stop.”
- “Much displeased” (line 4) is formal, but the immediate explanation “Angry” helps.
- “Suffer the little children” (line 5) is the largest failure. Modern listeners can hear an instruction about children suffering. “Forbid” and “of such” add difficulty. Plainest: “Let the little children come to me. Do not stop them. God’s kingdom belongs to people like them.”
- “God’s kingdom” (lines 5–6) remains insider language. A newcomer may imagine a country or territory; the film never explains it.
- “His arms” (line 9) can mean weapons in translation. “He held them” preserves the meaning.

The final image communicates affection, although shoulder rides are a culturally specific expression of it.

## 4. BIBLICALLY ACCURATE (claim by claim: RIGHT / OVERCLAIMED / UNSUPPORTED / WRONG, quote the line; say only what you can support)

- **RIGHT:** “That he should touch them” and the followers’ interference (lines 2–3) match the biblical episode.
- **RIGHT:** “Much displeased” (line 4) describes Jesus’s response. “Angry” is a reasonable plain-English rendering.
- **RIGHT:** Jesus’s quoted instruction (line 5) and taking, touching and blessing the children (line 8) follow the account.
- **RIGHT, as explanation:** Receiving the kingdom like a child (line 6) reflects the passage. “Receives a gift” is an explanatory comparison, not quoted wording.
- **OVERCLAIMED:** “They wanted one thing” (line 2) claims exclusive knowledge of their motives.
- **UNSUPPORTED as a personal promise:** “Someone told you ‘not now’” (line 10) assumes the viewer’s experience. The episode does not establish what Jesus will do about every present-day rejection.

## 5. BEST VERSION POSSIBLE (what would the best explainer in the world do differently; the ONE highest-value change; score 0-10 against that bar)

Make Jesus’s speech immediately understandable (line 5), with any historical wording identified separately. The highest-value change is replacing “Suffer” with “Let.” **7/10.**

## CURIOSITY GAP (opens at line __, pays off at line __; score 0-10; worth the wait?)
