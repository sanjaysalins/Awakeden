## 1. WHO IS THIS FOR
**Named viewer:** Grace, 34, scrolling at night. She feels sidelined at church or at home, and she half-believes faith content.
- **Who stops:** people who have felt brushed off. Line 1 asks them directly.
- **Who finishes:** believing parents and people who already like Jesus content. The warm picture of Jesus with children at the end (line 8) rewards them.
- **Who shares:** parents and children's ministry workers. They share it for the image, not the words.
- **Not served:** the newcomer who isn't hurting. Line 1 feels like it's for "someone else," and they leave. Also children: the script talks to adults about children.

## 2. MAIN TAKEAWAY
"Jesus got angry when people blocked the children, and He picked them up."
- Lines 4, 8 and 9 carry it. Line 9 is the best line in the script.
- It is mostly clear. But line 10 says the takeaway is about *you* ("Look at who Jesus picked up"), and the film never really connects an adult to "picked up." Line 6's "grown-ups too" is the only bridge, and it is buried in an explainer.

## 3. GLOBAL AUDIENCE
**In my own words:** parents bring children to Jesus for a blessing. His disciples push them away. Jesus is angry with the disciples, says "let them come," and hugs and blesses the children.
- The glosses work: "Suffer" is explained in line 6, "rebuked" in line 3, "much displeased" in line 4.
- **Misled:** line 1 uses the quote marks "not now," but nobody in the story says it. The disciples rebuked people. That was a firm "no," not a delay. A literal viewer will wait for the words "not now" and never hear them.
- "Of such is" works only because line 6 rescues it.
- "Close followers" (line 3) is fine. "Disciples" would be fine too if shown on screen.

## 4. BIBLICALLY ACCURATE
Source: Mark 10:13–16.
- **Line 2** "that he should touch them": **RIGHT** (v13).
- **Line 3** "rebuked those that brought them": **RIGHT** (v13).
- **Line 4** "much displeased… Angry… with the ones who tried to stop them": **RIGHT**. Indignant at the disciples is the plain reading of v14.
- **Line 5**: **RIGHT**, word for word (v14).
- **Line 6** "belongs to people like these children": **RIGHT**. But "like a little child receives a gift" is **OVERCLAIMED/softened**. Verse 15 is a warning: "shall not enter therein." The "gift" picture is a gloss, and it drops the stakes.
- **Line 8**: **RIGHT**, word for word (v16).
- **Last image**: a child on His shoulders is **UNSUPPORTED**. The text says "in his arms." It's harmless, but the words say arms and the picture says shoulders.

## 5. BEST VERSION POSSIBLE
**Second by second:**
- **0–4 s (line 1):** a generic second-person question plus "Keep watching." That is a retention *request*, not a retention *reason*. This is the main swipe point.
- **4–14 s (lines 2–3):** the setup is fine.
- **14–20 s (line 4):** "Angry" is the real hook. It arrives about 15 s late.
- **24–36 s (line 6):** a dead zone. It is a lecture after the quote.
- **Lines 7–9:** a good re-hook and a good payoff.

**The ONE change:** open on the conflict, not the viewer. Put "Jesus got angry, at His own friends" in the first 2 seconds, over the blocking image. Retention explainers lead with the twist.

## CURIOSITY GAP
- Opens at line 1, but weakly. The real gap opens at line 4: why angry?
- Pays off at lines 8–9.
- **Score: 5/10.** The payoff is worth it, but line 6 makes you wait through a sermon to get it.

## THREE SMALLEST FIXES
1. **Line 1:** "Jesus's own friends sent the children away. Watch what He did next."
2. **Line 6:** "In plain words: let them come. God's kingdom belongs to people like these. Even grown-ups must receive it like a little child."
3. **Line 10:** "They were sent away. Jesus picked them up. Look who He wants close."
