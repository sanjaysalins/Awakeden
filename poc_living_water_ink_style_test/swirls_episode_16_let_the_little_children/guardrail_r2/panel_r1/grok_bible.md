## 1. WHO IS THIS FOR

This is for Ruth, a grandmother who was told the children should wait outside. She will finish, and she may share it with her family. I would not send it to my church group as teaching until Mark 10:15 is put back. It does not serve the person in the disciples' seat, the leader who blocks people. It does not serve a listener who needs the warning. The last picture also misses a viewer who knows Luke 18:15: that verse calls them "infants," and the film shows two laughing girls, one on His shoulders.

## 2. MAIN TAKEAWAY

"When people tried to stop the children, Jesus picked them up." Line 10 carries it, with line 9. That landing is clear. It is smaller than the passage. Mark 10:15 says that whoever will not receive the kingdom as a little child shall not enter it. The film makes that a soft aside.

## 3. GLOBAL AUDIENCE

Who: Jesus. What: people brought young children so He would touch them. His followers tried to stop that. He was much displeased, welcomed the children, took them in His arms, and blessed them. Why: the kingdom of God belongs to people like these children.

Line 6 misled me: "we receive it like a little child receives a gift." Mark 10:15 has no gift. It says "he shall not enter therein."

Line 5 will fail in translation. "Suffer the little children" means "let them come." Today "suffer" means pain. "Forbid them not" is a hard double negative. Line 6 repairs both, one line late. Line 3's "rebuked" is helped by "tried to stop them." Line 4 then turns "much displeased" into "Angry," which is plainer and hotter than the KJV.

## 4. BIBLICALLY ACCURATE

Line 1: OVERCLAIMED. "Not now" is not in the text. Mark 10:13 says "his disciples rebuked those that brought them." A rebuke is a refusal. "This story is for you" is application. "Whosoever" in Mark 10:15 does include the listener.

Line 2: RIGHT. Mark 10:13, "they brought young children to him, that he should touch them." The quoted words are exact. "One thing" matches that one purpose.

Line 3: RIGHT. "rebuked those that brought them" is exact, Mark 10:13. "Close followers" fairly names "his disciples." "Tried to stop them" fits "forbid them not" in Mark 10:14.

Line 4: RIGHT on the quote and the target. Mark 10:14, "when Jesus saw it, he was much displeased," and He corrects the people who were forbidding the children. OVERCLAIMED on "Angry." The KJV says "much displeased."

Line 5: RIGHT. Word for word from Mark 10:14: "Suffer the little children to come unto me, and forbid them not: for of such is the kingdom of God." This is Mark's wording, not Matthew 19:14.

Line 6: RIGHT for "let them come," "Do not stop them," and "people like these children" ("of such is the kingdom of God"). OVERCLAIMED and UNSUPPORTED after that. Mark 10:15 says, "Verily I say unto you, Whosoever shall not receive the kingdom of God as a little child, he shall not enter therein." The film drops "shall not enter therein" and adds a gift the verse does not mention. "Grown-ups too" makes the main saying sound like a side note.

Line 7: no claim. A bridge.

Line 8: RIGHT. Exact Mark 10:16, apart from the opening "And": "he took them up in his arms, put his hands upon them, and blessed them."

Line 9: OVERCLAIMED. They brought the children "that he should touch them." The text does not say they "asked." He laid His hands on them, took them up in His arms, and blessed them. This punch line keeps only the arms.

Line 10: "picked up" fits "took them up in his arms." The picture it points to is UNSUPPORTED. Mark gives no count, no "girls," no laughter, no shoulders, no arch, no olive tree. Luke 18:15 calls the same children "infants." "Not now" repeats line 1.

The KJV phrases in lines 2, 3, 4, 5, and 8 match the text.

## 5. BEST VERSION POSSIBLE

The best explainer would keep these exact quotes, then speak Mark 10:15 whole, in Jesus's voice, with "he shall not enter therein" still in it. The blessing would be the last biblical word. The picture would stay inside arms and hands.

The one change: restore Mark 10:15 whole, and cut the gift sentence.

Score: 6/10. The quotes that are spoken are clean. The missing verse is the one that makes this the way into the kingdom.

## CURIOSITY GAP

Opens at line 1. Pays off at line 10. Score: 6/10. Lines 2 to 8 earn the wait. Line 10 repeats the hook. The arms, the hands, and the blessing have already been said. The ending adds warmth and no new fact from the text.

## THREE SMALLEST FIXES

1. Line 4: Then Jesus saw it. And the Bible says He was "much displeased." Not with the children. With the ones who tried to stop them.
2. Line 6: In plain words: let them come. Do not stop them. God's kingdom belongs to people like these children. Jesus said, "Whosoever shall not receive the kingdom of God as a little child, he shall not enter therein."
3. Line 9: They brought the children for a touch. He gave them His arms, His hands, and His blessing.

## VERDICT

REVISE. The spoken KJV quotes match Mark. Mark 10:15 is the claim that fails, and it is the claim that carries the passage.
