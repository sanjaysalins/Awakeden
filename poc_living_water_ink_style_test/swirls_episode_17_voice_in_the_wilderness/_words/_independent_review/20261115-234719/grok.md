# Independent review — grok (OK, 344s)

Hook **5**. Landing **5**. Both sit under 8, so this is a revise. The five quoted runs do not swap a KJV word. The trouble is what a first hearer can follow, and what the joins around those quotes make John say.

**Hook: 5.** "Is John the promised one?" is a real question, so it is not a "Did you know" open. A stranger still has no person and no stake. Who is John? Promised by whom, to do what? The "no" arrives only on line 3, past the first five seconds.

**Landing: 5.** The last sentence of G1 is "Behold him." That sentence could end a different short. It is the old form of "look to him," and the script's own fallback is "Look to him." Cap is 5. "The voice is John. The Lord is Jesus." is a label key. It drops the Lamb and "the sin of the world," the longest line in the piece. G2 ("Hear the voice. Behold the Lamb: Jesus, the Lord.") stays with this piece's image and still sits under 8. "Behold the Lamb" can close any John 1:29 short.

**Christ is two titles.** Line 1 and line 3 say "the promised one." The quote says "I am not the Christ." Nothing in the spoken line says they are the same. A new listener can hear "Christ" as a last name. Then "I am not the Christ" does not answer the hook. Luke 3:15 says the crowd mused whether he were "the Christ, or not." "The promised one" is a paraphrase, and the priests' line is only "Who art thou?" The hook is the narrator's question wearing the crowd's wonder.

**Line 4 is a riddle.** "They guess two great names. No, twice." John 1:21 is two direct questions, not guesses. The names are unspoken, so "No, twice" is a no to a blank. "They need an answer" does not say an answer to what. This is a clever conceit. It fails on one hearing.

**The cry is the other half of the verse.** Line 5 ends John at "wilderness" and puts a period there. John 1:23 does not stop. The same sentence goes on: "Make straight the way of the Lord, as said the prophet Esaias." Line 6 calls "Prepare ye the way of the LORD" "its cry." Those words are exact Isaiah 40:3, and they are the Matthew, Mark, and Luke form. They are not the words John speaks in this scene. The short hands the voice the clause this Gospel does not give him. "Its" has no clear owner (the voice, or the wilderness).

**"God comes" is doing the doctrine.** The colon in "Isaiah wrote its cry: God comes." makes "God comes" sound like the written cry. The quoted words are "Prepare ye the way of the LORD." The same verse's "highway for our God" is the clause that was not read. "Jesus comes" and "The Lord is Jesus" are an orthodox landing: the Gospels put Isaiah 40 on Jesus, and Jesus is the LORD whose way John prepares. That claim is faithful. A listener cannot check it in the fragment that was read. Two narrator beats carry it.

**The next day is missing from the words.** "Jesus comes" follows the priests at once. John 1:29 is "The next day," and the priests are not the audience of the Lamb line. The ear hears one meeting. A picture note of a new day is not in the narration.

**"Crying" still sounds like weeping.** Line 5 keeps the KJV "crying." Line 6, "Isaiah wrote its cry," locks cry to crying. For many listeners that is tears. A mouth-open picture note does not travel in the audio. "Isaiah" is also a bare name: no "prophet," no "long ago."

**Grace holds.** There is no threat, no prize, no works push. "Prepare ye" is not turned into "get ready or else." Line 7 keeps the whole speech, "Behold the Lamb of God, which taketh away the sin of the world." That is the grace center. "taketh" is hard once, and it is still the right word to leave inside the quotes.

**Spine.** One question runs through: is this man the promised one? He is the voice. Jesus is the Lord. That thread holds. Freshness is in the text (he refuses the title and points away). G1 then flattens it into a chart and leaves the Lamb behind.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. In the spoken lines, make "the Christ" the same person as "the promised one," and replace "They guess two great names. No, twice." with the plain thing that happened.
2. Make the cry match John 1:23 ("Make straight the way of the Lord"), or mark "Prepare ye the way of the LORD" as Isaiah's older line. Keep "God comes" from sounding like the verse, put "the next day" in the words, and stop "its cry" from meaning weeping.
3. Rewrite the last sentence so it cannot close another short. Build it from this piece's voice and Lamb, in words a stranger already knows. "Behold him" stays at the stock-closer cap.
