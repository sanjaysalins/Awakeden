# Independent review — codex (OK, 117s)

**HOOK: 4/10. LANDING: G1 5/10; G2 5/10.**

1. **Line 1 assumes the interest it needs to create.** “Is John the promised one?” gives a newcomer neither John’s identity nor the significance of “the promised one.” The listener needs prior knowledge to feel the question. The supposed irony—someone mistaken for the promised deliverer identifying himself as a voice—only becomes available later. The premise is biblically grounded in [Luke 3:15](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Luke+3%3A15&version=KJV); its opening appeal is weak.

2. **Both endings fail the transferable-final-sentence test.** G1 ends “Behold him.” That exact sentence could close almost any narration about Jesus. Repeating a verb from line 7 does not make the invitation specific to this piece. “The voice is John. The Lord is Jesus” supplies a recap before the generic command. G2 reconnects more directly through “Hear the voice,” but its final sentence—“Behold the Lamb: Jesus, the Lord”—could also close another Lamb-focused piece. Both remain capped at 5 under your rule. The proposed fallback, “Look to him,” has the same problem.

3. **Line 4 sacrifices intelligibility to conceal information.** “They guess two great names. No, twice” asks listeners to follow two rejected possibilities without hearing either possibility. It also misdescribes the text: Elias is a name; “that prophet” is an expected figure’s designation. “They need an answer” adds administrative urgency without explaining what these questions establish. This is avoidable vagueness, not a meaningful curiosity gap. See [John 1:21–22](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John+1%3A21-22&version=KJV).

4. **Line 6 makes the central insight harder to hear than necessary.** “Isaiah wrote its cry” introduces an unidentified person through an awkward abstraction. The listener must resolve “its,” understand that Isaiah wrote a prophecy, and connect that prophecy to John’s speech. An old-scroll picture cannot establish all that in the narration. “God comes” followed by “Jesus comes” expresses an orthodox connection, but much of the explanatory work happens in the author’s notes. The text’s explicit “our God” wording is outside the selected quotation, leaving the narrator’s gloss to carry that connection. See [Isaiah 40:3](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Isaiah+40%3A3&version=KJV).

5. **Line 7 states the Lamb’s work without making the Lamb image intelligible.** “Which taketh away the sin of the world” tells listeners what Jesus does; it does not explain why this arriving man is called a lamb. A newcomer can understand the removal of sin while still missing the sacrificial significance. The climax therefore introduces another unexplained image precisely when it needs to become emotionally clear. The identity thread remains consistent, but the movement through **voice → prepared way → Lamb** is insufficiently explained for the specified audience.

6. **The timing estimate does not establish the intended delivery.** “41.6 s” plus the hold leaves roughly 0.4 seconds within 45 seconds. The directions also require “slow, ringing,” “slow on ‘sin of the world,’” and an “unhurried” landing. The arithmetic is correct; the performance remains unverified. Rushing these particular transitions would aggravate the comprehension problems above.

**Doctrinal and KJV checks:** I found no altered words in the five spoken quotation runs against [John 1:19–29](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John+1%3A19-29&version=KJV) and Isaiah 40:3. Line 5 is an exact excerpt, not John’s complete answer. No invented doctrine, fear appeal, gain-loss bargain, or works-based invitation is evident. The defects are chiefly accessibility and rhetorical execution.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Make the opening question meaningful to someone who knows neither John nor the promised deliverer.
2. Build the final invitation from John’s specific act of directing attention to Jesus; replace the transferable closing sentence.
3. Remove the unnamed guesses and clarify the Isaiah-to-Jesus connection and Lamb image within the narration.
