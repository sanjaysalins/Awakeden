## Independent adversarial review — Swirls Short #17 "Only a Voice" (candidate G)

### Doctrinal soundness
- **Mostly orthodox**: John deflects to Christ; Isaiah → Lamb → Jesus/Lord is standard Johannine/evangelical reading. No contrarian readings, no works/fear CTA.
- **Soft overclaim on Isaiah (line 6)**: *"Isaiah wrote its cry: God comes."* The heard KJV is only *"Prepare ye the way of the LORD."* Isaiah 40:3 does not say “God comes” in those words; that phrase is narrator theology squeezed into the same breath as the quote. A careful listener can hear it as **scripture wording** and think the Bible literally says “God comes” there — slippage between **quoted text** and **narrator gloss**.
- **LORD → Jesus equation (line 8 G1: *"The Lord is Jesus"*)**: Fair in this series’ Christ-centered frame, but it is **one compressed identity step** with no audible bridge except the prior pair *"God comes"* / *"Jesus comes"* (lines 6–7). For listeners who do not already equate “the LORD” with Jesus, that step is asserted, not demonstrated from the verses you actually read (John never says “the Lord is Jesus” in this pericope).

### KJV verbatim
- Quoted runs match KJV as listed; automated check is noted.
- **Not a KJV violation, but a sequence issue**: Line 2 places *"Who art thou?"* at the river with Jerusalem priests (John 1:19). In the full chapter, the Elias/“that prophet” exchange (1:21) sits **between** the first “Who art thou?” block and John’s *voice in the wilderness* answer (1:23). You reorder for compression (lines 3 → 4), which is acceptable for a short **only if** line 4 does not sound like the priests got their final answer in line 3. Line 4 (*"They need an answer"*) partly fixes that — but see clarity below.

### Clarity on first hearing (global / zero Bible knowledge)
- **Hook assumes cast without introduction (line 1)**: *"Is John the promised one?"* — Who is John? Promised by whom, for what? No “Christ,” no “Messiah,” no “people at the Jordan.” The irony the pack wants (Luke 3:15) is **inaudible** unless the viewer already knows the scene.
- **Line 4 is a black box**: *"They guess two great names. No, twice."* You deliberately hide Elijah and “that prophet.” For a newcomer that is **pure fog** — not mystery that pays off in the ear, only on page 4 art (*"letter the two names small"*). On audio-only or fast scroll, line 4 reads as **filler between denials** with no teachable content.
- **Chronology break (line 7)**: *"Jesus comes."* with **no** “next day” (acknowledged cut). After lines 2–5’s priest Q&A at the river, a naive listener will picture **Jesus walking in on that interrogation**, not John 1:29’s **following day**. That is a real **logic/timeline glitch**, not fixed by visuals alone for listeners.
- **Line 5 partial quote**: You stop at *"in the wilderness"* and drop John 1:23’s immediate follow-on (*"Make straight the way of the Lord"*). Line 6 then jumps to Isaiah’s *"Prepare ye the way of the LORD"* — literate hearers may sense the link; newcomers get **two “prepare/make way” ideas** without knowing they echo the same prophecy.
- **“Crying” (line 5)**: KJV-correct; adversarially, the spoken note *"never weeping"* is production-only. Without it, *"voice of one crying"* still defaults to **tears** for many global listeners. Line 6’s *"its cry"* helps only slightly.
- **Line 7 density**: Fifteen words, mostly KJV (*"Behold the Lamb of God, which taketh away the sin of the world."*). For ESL/global ears, **Lamb + taketh + sin of the world** in one line is a lot; meaning still leans on the image, not a single plain gloss word.

### Grace-anchored conviction
- **No gain/loss, no fear, no “come or else.”** Good.
- **G1 landing (*"Behold him"*)** is open invitation, not pressure — aligned with grace-anchored rules.
- **Mild caution**: The atonement line is heavy gospel content without a soft narrator frame (“John is pointing at the one who bears sin”); acceptable for your charter, but not especially *invitational* until line 8.

### Freshness = faithful depth
- **Fresh thread**: “Promised one?” → not Christ → only a **voice** → Lamb/Lord — text-faithful spine, not clickbait.
- **Less fresh**: Priest/deputation + wilderness voice + Behold the Lamb is **well-trodden** sermon territory; freshness lives mainly in **title tie-in** (*"Only a Voice"*) and compression, not a surprising textual detail (e.g. John’s “among you stands one you know not,” unworthy to loose sandal, etc. — all cut).

### One thread spine (hook → middle → CTA)
- **Strong**: wonder about John → denial → voice → prepare way → Lamb → voice/ Lord / behold.
- **Weak joint**: Line 6 *"God comes"* / line 7 *"Jesus comes"* / line 8 *"The Lord is Jesus"* is the doctrinal spine, but **“God” never appears inside quoted scripture** in the script — only narrator voice. That undercuts the pack’s own fix #6 claim that the ear **proves** the bridge from heard words.

### Hook score: **5/10**
