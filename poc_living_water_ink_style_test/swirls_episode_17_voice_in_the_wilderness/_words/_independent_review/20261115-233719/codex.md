# Independent review — codex (OK, 125s)

- **HOOK: 4/10.** Line 1, “Who is this desert man?”, asks a stranger to care about an unidentified person before supplying a reason. Line 2 repeats the identity question—“Who art thou?”—without introducing the piece’s actual surprise: whose arrival John is announcing.

- **LANDING: 4/10.** Line 8, “That Lord is Jesus: God himself, come to take away sin,” ends with explanation, not invitation. It could close another short about Jesus’ arrival or identity, so the stock-closer cap applies. The available invitation verb, “Behold” in line 7, remains inside historical dialogue; the ending never carries that action to the listener. A three-second hold cannot supply the missing CTA.

- **First-hearing obstacle: line 4.** “Elijah, then? The Prophet? No. No.” requires background the narration never provides. A newcomer cannot know why these identities matter or why “The Prophet” means someone particular. Capitalization contributes nothing in audio. This spends eleven words on an unexplained detour.

- **The central textual discovery loses its supporting words.** Line 6 quotes “Prepare ye the way of the LORD”; line 8 concludes “God himself.” That conclusion is orthodox, but the spoken excerpt omits Isaiah’s explicit “for our God,” which would help a novice understand the connection. The capitalization of “LORD” cannot carry the explanation aloud. The strongest textual evidence appears only in the production notes. See [Isaiah 40:3](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Isaiah+40%3A3&version=KJV).

- **The thread weakens at the climax.** Lines 5–6 establish a voice announcing the Lord’s arrival. Line 7 introduces “the Lamb of God,” a second image, without explaining its sacrificial meaning. Line 8 supplies the purpose—taking away sin—but leaves the lamb image unexplained and never completes the voice/path thread. For someone with zero Bible knowledge, the ending stacks titles faster than it connects them.

- **KJV check: no altered words found in the five spoken quotations.** Lines 5–7 use shortened, contiguous excerpts; ending those excerpts early is not a word alteration. Line 4’s “Elijah” is explicitly narrator paraphrase, so its difference from the KJV’s *Elias* is not a quotation violation. Sources: [John 1:19–29](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John+1%3A19-29&version=KJV), [Isaiah 40:3](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Isaiah+40%3A3&version=KJV).

- **Doctrine and grace:** I find no invented doctrine or fear, gain-loss, or works condition to flag. “God himself” expresses Christ’s deity consistently with [John 1:1–14](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John+1%3A1-14&version=KJV). The problem is communicating the biblical connection and inviting a response, rather than merely stating the conclusion.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Build a closing invitation from the voice, way, or “Behold” already established in this piece.
2. Make the hook expose the specific identity tension instead of asking a generic stranger-identification question.
3. Reallocate the unexplained Elijah/Prophet detour to clarify Isaiah’s God-language and the Lamb image.
