# Independent review — claude (OK, 41s)

## Review: "Only a Voice", candidate F

**Doctrine: sound.**
- Line 8 says that the LORD of Isaiah 40:3 is Jesus. That is orthodox, and Matthew 3:3, Mark 1:2-3 and John 1:23 back it.
- "God himself" is fine evangelical shorthand. It could be heard as "the Father came", so "God the Son" or "God, come in person" would be tighter. This is minor and does not block.
- There is no fear, gain-loss or works framing anywhere.

**KJV verbatim: clean.** I checked every quote against the KJV:
- "Who art thou?" (John 1:19): exact.
- "I am not the Christ" (John 1:20): exact.
- "I am the voice of one crying in the wilderness" (John 1:23): exact, cut short.
- "Prepare ye the way of the LORD" (Isaiah 40:3): exact.
- "Behold the Lamb of God" (John 1:29): exact, cut short.
- Line 4 ("Elijah", "No. No.") is narrator paraphrase with no quote marks, so it is allowed. Note that the KJV says "Elias" and John's first answer is "I am not", not "No".

**Clarity on first hearing: two weak spots.**
1. **Line 4 loses a newcomer.** "Elijah, then? The Prophet?" means nothing to someone with no Bible knowledge. Why would anyone think he is Elijah? Who is "the Prophet"? Then "They must have an answer" is ambiguous. It can be heard as "surely they have an answer" instead of "they need an answer to take back". This is the line most likely to make a viewer drift.
2. **"Desert man" (line 1) clashes with "at the river" (line 2).** A first-time listener hears two different places within 3 seconds.
- "Lamb of God" is never linked to sacrifice. Line 8's "take away sin" half-covers it. That is acceptable, but thin.

**Spine: good, but the landing doesn't use it.** The piece is built on an identity question: "Who art thou?", then "not me", then "only a voice", then "behold Him." The natural payoff is to flip the opening question onto Jesus. Line 8 instead switches to a plain statement of doctrine. The "voice" and "way" images from the title and lines 5-6 are dropped right at the end.

**HOOK: 5/10.** "Who is this desert man?" is not a template opener, but it is a bare question with no stake or twist. The real twist is stronger and is already in the text: crowds thought this man might be the Messiah, and he called himself *only a voice*. Lead with that.

**LANDING: 5/10.** "That Lord is Jesus: God himself, come to take away sin." could end any Lamb-of-God or Isaiah piece, so it counts as a stock closer. It is also not an invitation: no CTA exists at all. It closes on a statement of doctrine, not an invitation grown from this piece's own image. Something built from the voice, the way, or the flipped question would land harder. For example: John's question comes back to the viewer, "Who art thou?", then becomes "Behold Him."

**Timing:** 88 words, about 41.6 s plus the 3 s hold. That fits 45 s.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Rebuild line 8 into a real invitation made from this piece's own image. Turn the opening "Who art thou?" around onto Jesus, or use John's "voice / prepare the way" role, so the last sentence could not end any other short. The doctrine can stay, but it must not be the whole closer.
2. Rewrite line 1 around the real irony: people wondered if this man was the Messiah, and he said he was only a voice. Drop "desert man" so it no longer clashes with "at the river" in line 2.
3. Make line 4 work for a newcomer. Either cut the "Elijah / the Prophet" names or gloss them in plain words, e.g. "A great prophet back from the dead? No." Change "They must have an answer" to something clear, like "They need an answer to take back."
