## Version A

**Role 1 - newcomer (7)**
- Who: a man in the desert called John. Then Jesus.
- What: important men from the city ask who he is. He says what he is not. He says he is only "a voice". Next day he points at Jesus.
- Why: Jesus is God come near, and he takes away sin.
- Hard spots: L3 "the Christ", "Elijah", "the Prophet" are three unknown names in one line. I only get "he keeps saying no". L7 "taketh" and "Lamb" are strange, and nobody is named as the speaker or as the one being pointed at. I must wait for L8 to learn the Lamb is Jesus. L6 "Isaiah" is unknown but "long ago ... wrote" tells me enough.
- L1 is the easiest hook of the three. "Desert man" needs no Bible.

**Role 2 - Bible reader (8)**
- L2 says "Priests" only. Text has priests and Levites. Small trim, not an error. "At the river" is fair from John 1:28.
- L3 accurate. Order matches the text.
- L4 is the best handling of v22. "They cannot go home with nothing" is a true plain paraphrase of "that we may give an answer to them that sent us". It is narrator voice, so no invented quote.
- L5 verbatim. L7 verbatim and complete.
- L6 fair summary of Isaiah 40:3.
- L8 sound. "God himself" rests on Isaiah's "way of the LORD ... highway for our God". But the link is only implied. The word "Lord" is never said, so the reader has to make the jump alone.
- Fresh: the men need an answer to carry home. Good small human detail.

**Role 3 - curiosity (7)**
- L1 opens a real question. L5 answers it ("a voice"). L8 turns it to Jesus.
- Gap: the film never says it out loud. John is the voice, Jesus is the one. The payoff is there but the viewer must join it.

**Guardrails**
1. For: newcomers and casual scrollers. Works for Bible readers too.
2. Take-away: John was only a voice. Jesus is the one he pointed to, God come near.
3. Global: mostly yes. L3 name list and "taketh" are the weak points.
4. Accurate: yes.
5. Best possible: no. One change: L8 should close the loop on John (see fixes).

## Version B

**Role 1 - newcomer (5)**
- Who: John. But L1 "Who is John really?" assumes I already know a John. I do not.
- What: men ask, he says no three times, says he is a voice, then sees Jesus.
- Why: Jesus takes away sin.
- Hard spots: L3 "Before any guess" - I do not understand this. L5 "No name." is short but I need a second to get it. L8 "Behold him" - I do not know "behold". The last word of the film is a word I cannot read. L4 switches from "art thou" to "who are you" in the same mouths, which is confusing.

**Role 2 - Bible reader (7)**
- L3 "Before any guess" is half right and half wrong. True for "I am not the Christ" (v20, nobody had asked that). Not true for Elijah and the Prophet. Those WERE their guesses (v21). The line as built covers all three. Factual slip.
- L4 "They pressed harder" is fair. But "Tell us about yourself" sounds like a job interview, and it drops the real reason (they must report back). It is unquoted, so not a false quote, but it is weaker than A/C.
- L5 "No name." is the freshest note in all three versions. He is asked who he is and gives no name.
- L6 "a road made ready for God himself" is the best Isaiah line of the three. Close to "a highway for our God".
- L7 cuts the quote. L8 gives the rest in narrator voice. Fine.
- L8 lands on Jesus but never says Jesus is that God. L6 sets it up and L8 leaves it. Weakest doctrinal close.

**Role 3 - curiosity (6)**
- L1 is a question only for people who know John. L5 pays it well ("No name"). L8 ends on a command, not an answer.

**Guardrails**
1. For: church people. Not newcomers.
2. Take-away: John would not name himself, he pointed to Jesus. Look at Jesus.
3. Global: no. "Before any guess", "Behold him", mixed old/new English.
4. Accurate: mostly. L3 slip.
5. Best possible: no. One change: drop "Before any guess".

## Version C

**Role 1 - newcomer (6)**
- Who: a man who is "baptizing" (I do not know this word). Later named John. Then Jesus.
- What: same story as A.
- Why: Jesus is the Lord, God himself.
- Hard spots: L1 "Who is this man baptizing?" - I hear this as "which person is he baptizing?" It is the wrong question. And "baptizing" is a hard word in the very first second. L6 "Prepare ye" is odd but short. L8 is one long sentence with two commas. "Taking the world's sin" - taking it where? L7 "Lamb" is never explained and L8 drops it.

**Role 2 - Bible reader (8)**
- L2 accurate, and keeps "Levites".
- L4 same good line as A.
- L6 quotes Isaiah directly. This plants the word LORD. Strongest setup of the three.
- L7 "cries" - the text says "saith". Small stretch. It echoes "crying in the wilderness", which is nice, but it is not what v29 says.
- L8 "That Lord is Jesus, God himself" is the clearest and soundest landing. It closes Isaiah's LORD onto Jesus in plain words. "Taking the world's sin" is loose. The text is "taketh away".
- Fresh: the LORD in Isaiah is the man walking up in John 1:29. Said plainly.

**Role 3 - curiosity (6)**
- L1 is broken as a question (see above). The payoff in L8 is the best of the three, but it answers "who is the Lord", not "who is this man".

**Guardrails**
1. For: Bible readers first.
2. Take-away: the Lord Isaiah wrote about is Jesus.
3. Global: body yes, L1 and L8 no.
4. Accurate: yes, bar "cries" and "taking".
5. Best possible: no. One change: replace L1.

## PICK: A

Best hook, cleanest words, nothing wrong in it. It only lacks C's "Lord" link and a close that names John's part.

## TOP 3 FIXES for A

1. **L8** - close the hook. New: "John was only the voice. Jesus is the Lord, God himself, come near."
2. **L6** - plant the word Lord (take C's line). New: "Long ago Isaiah wrote of a voice: 'Prepare ye the way of the LORD.'"
3. **L7** - say who speaks and who he sees. New: "Next day John sees Jesus coming: 'Behold the Lamb of God, which taketh away the sin of the world.'" (If too long for the page, keep the full quote and cut "coming".)

Optional: L2 "Priests and Levites from Jerusalem find John at the river".

## FAIL lines

- **C L1** "Who is this man baptizing?" - FAIL. Reads as "whom is he baptizing?" Wrong question, and a hard word in second one.
- **B L3** "Before any guess" - FAIL. Wrong for Elijah and the Prophet (they were the guesses), and unclear to a newcomer.
- **B L8** "Behold him." - FAIL for global audience. The whole landing leans on an old word a newcomer does not know.

Weak, not FAIL:
- A L7 no speaker named.
- C L7 "cries" (text: "saith").
- C L8 "taking the world's sin" (text: "taketh away").
- B L1 assumes the viewer knows John.
- All versions L3: three unknown names in one line. A newcomer only gets "no, no, no". That is enough, but only just.
- All versions: "Lamb" is never explained.

Simulated viewer, not a real one. Treat scores as plus or minus 1.5.
