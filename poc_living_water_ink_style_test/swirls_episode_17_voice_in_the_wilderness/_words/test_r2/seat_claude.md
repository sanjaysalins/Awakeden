# Seat: claude (blind, round 2)

## Version D

**Role 1 - newcomer. Clarity 6/10**
- Who: a desert man named John, then Jesus. What: priests ask who he is; he says he is not the Christ, only a "voice"; then Jesus comes. Why: Jesus is the Lord the voice got ready for.
- Hard spots:
  - L4 "They guess two names" - which names? I never hear them. Feels like a gap in the story.
  - L5 "crying in the wilderness" - to an ESL ear, "crying" = weeping. "Wilderness" is a hard word. This is the key line, and it is the least clear.
  - "Who art thou?" - old English, but short. Most will get it from context.
  - L8 "The Lord is Jesus, God himself" - clear claim, but the link to "the LORD" in L6 is easy to miss on one listen.

**Role 2 - Bible reader. 8/10**
- Accurate. L4 "What does he call himself?" mirrors John 1:22 "What sayest thou of thyself?" - good.
- L3 "the one God promised" is narrator gloss, fine.
- L7 "Jesus comes" drops "the next day" (John 1:29). Not wrong, but compresses two days into one.
- L8: Isaiah 40:3 "the LORD ... our God" applied to Jesus via Matthew 3:3 - sound, and the fresh point. "God himself" is orthodox; slightly blunt but defensible.
- Fresh: yes - the "LORD" in Isaiah's line is the one who walks up in John 1:29.

**Role 3 - curiosity gap. 6/10**
- L1 "Who is this desert man?" is a mild question. L2 names him "John" right away, so the literal question closes in 5 seconds. The real gap (who is he FOR?) is only implied. Pay-off at L8 is good: "The voice was John" closes the loop neatly.

**Guardrails**
1. For: curious scrollers + Bible readers who know John the Baptist but not the Isaiah link.
2. Take-away: John is only the voice; Jesus is the Lord - God - he made way for.
3. Global: mostly. "crying/wilderness" and the unnamed "two names" hurt.
4. Accurate: yes.
5. Best? No. One change: name the two guesses in L4.

## Version E

**Role 1 - newcomer. Clarity 7/10**
- Who/what/why same as D, a bit easier. "Long ago" in L6 tells me Isaiah is old - helps. "Next day" helps the time order.
- Hard spots: same L4 "two names" (unnamed), same L5 "crying in the wilderness".
- L8 "That Lord is Jesus" - "That" points back to L6, so the link is clearer than D.
- L8 "God, taking away the world's sin" - slightly awkward grammar; reads like a list.

**Role 2 - Bible reader. 8/10**
- Accurate. L4 "They must have an answer" matches John 1:22 "that we may give an answer to them that sent us" - good, real detail.
- L7 "Next day" correct (1:29). Quote cut to "Behold the Lamb of God" - fine; L8 paraphrases the rest ("taking away the world's sin") faithfully.
- L8 sound: Isaiah's LORD = Jesus. "God, taking away" - orthodox (God in Christ reconciling the world), but compressed.
- Lost vs D: D kept the full KJV Lamb line, which is the stronger verbatim moment.

**Role 3 - curiosity gap. 7/10**
- L1 "wild man" is a little more hooky than "desert man". Same early-close problem (L2 names John). L8 "That Lord is Jesus" is a sharper twist than D. But E never restates "the voice was John," so the opening question gets no explicit echo.

**Guardrails**
1. For: same as D.
2. Take-away: the Lord Isaiah said to prepare for is Jesus - God come to take away sin.
3. Global: a bit better than D ("Long ago", "Next day", "That Lord").
4. Accurate: yes.
5. Best? Close. One change: name the two guesses (L4).

## PICK: E

Clearer time order, clearer "that Lord" link, and L4 uses a real text detail. Borrow D's closing echo.

## TOP 3 FIXES (for E)
1. **L4** -> "Elijah, back again? The Prophet? No. No. They must have an answer." (Names the guesses - John 1:21. "Two names" with no names is the biggest newcomer hole.)
2. **L1** -> "Who is this wild man, shouting in the desert?" (Plants the meaning of "crying in the wilderness" before L5, so ESL ears hear shouting, not weeping.)
3. **L8** -> "The voice was John. That Lord is Jesus: God, come to take away the world's sin." (Closes L1's loop like D, and fixes the awkward "God, taking away" grammar.)

## FAIL lines
- No hard FAIL on doctrine or accuracy in either version.
- Near-fail (clarity), both versions: **L4 "two names. No. No."** - the names are never said; a newcomer cannot follow it.
- Watch, both versions: **L5 "crying in the wilderness"** - load-bearing line rests on an archaic phrase ("crying" = weeping to ESL ears). Needs a plain-word setup (fix 2) or a gloss.
