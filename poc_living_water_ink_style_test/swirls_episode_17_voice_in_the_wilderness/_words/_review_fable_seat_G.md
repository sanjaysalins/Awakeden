## (a) Newcomer, English as a second language, one hearing

- **Who / what / why: mostly yes.** A man called John is asked who he is. He says he is not the promised one, only a voice. Then Jesus comes and John points to him. The gap opens in line 1 and pays off in lines 7-8.
- **Line 7 speaker: real risk.** "Jesus comes." followed at once by a quote. Lines 2 and 3 have taught the ear that the person just named is the one who speaks ("Priests ... : Who art thou?", "he says first: I am not the Christ"). So the pattern points to Jesus as the speaker. The voice match with lines 3 and 5 and the picture help, but nothing in the words says John speaks. I put this at about 1 in 4 newcomers (wide error bar, simulated). It is also the most important line in the film.
- **"Behold him": weak for this viewer.** "Behold" is an old word, heard once, inside a KJV line that also has "taketh". It is then the one word the whole invitation leans on. That breaks the project's own rule (old KJV words may be quoted, never load-bearing in the landing). The file's "Still weak" list already admits it.
- **Line 4** gives a newcomer nothing ("two great names" — which names? why?). Harmless, but 11 words spent on fog. "No, twice." is odd by ear.
- **Line 6 "Isaiah wrote its cry"**: "its" is hard to follow by ear, and Isaiah is not introduced. "God comes" is clear and good.
- **"crying"**: the voice cue and "cry" in line 6 are enough. Fine.

## (b) KJV Bible reader

- All five quotes verbatim. Attribution frames stay with the narrator. Good.
- **Hook "Is John the promised one?"** — sound. Luke 3:15 says exactly that the people wondered this. It is the narrator's question, not put in the priests' mouths. No over-reach.
- **Line 3 "he says first"** — fair. John 1:20 has him deny being the Christ before anyone names the Christ.
- **Line 4** — true to John 1:21-22 (two questions, two noes, "that we may give an answer"). Nothing says John is not a prophet. Good.
- **Line 6 "God comes"** — sound. Isaiah 40:3 itself ends "a highway for our God". It is a narrator paraphrase, not a quote; the short stop before the KJV read must keep it from sounding like Isaiah's words.
- **Line 8 "The Lord is Jesus"** — sound. John 1:23 has John apply Isaiah 40:3 to the one he prepares for, and that one is Jesus (1:29-30). The chain God comes -> Jesus comes -> The Lord is Jesus is orthodox (Jesus is God the Son, come in person). No over-reach.
- One loss: "the next day" is gone, so by ear the priests seem to be still there in line 7. Minor; the picture must carry it.

## (c) Landing

- **Grace, no pressure: yes** for both. No gain/loss, no fear, no stock closer.
- **Fresh: G1 yes.** It is built only from this film's words (voice, Lord, Behold) and it answers the hook: "Is John the promised one?" -> "The voice is John. The Lord is Jesus."
- **G2 is weaker.** "Hear the voice" is unclear (whose voice? it sends the viewer back to John, the man who just pointed away from himself). It drops the hook payoff. It still leans on "Behold".
- G1's invitation is thin only because of the old word. In plain words it lands.

## Fixes (max 3)
