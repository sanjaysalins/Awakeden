# Writer pack (blind seat)

## 1. The Scripture (KJV, word for word)


| Ref | Text |
|---|---|
| Isaiah 40:3 | The voice of him that crieth in the wilderness, Prepare ye the way of the LORD, make straight in the desert a highway for our God. |
| Matthew 3:3 | For this is he that was spoken of by the prophet Esaias, saying, The voice of one crying in the wilderness, Prepare ye the way of the Lord, make his paths straight. |
| John 1:19 | And this is the record of John, when the Jews sent priests and Levites from Jerusalem to ask him, Who art thou? |
| John 1:20 | And he confessed, and denied not; but confessed, I am not the Christ. |
| John 1:21 | And they asked him, What then? Art thou Elias? And he saith, I am not. Art thou that prophet? And he answered, No. |
| John 1:22 | Then said they unto him, Who art thou? that we may give an answer to them that sent us. What sayest thou of thyself? |
| John 1:23 | He said, I am the voice of one crying in the wilderness, Make straight the way of the Lord, as said the prophet Esaias. |
| John 1:28 | These things were done in Bethabara beyond Jordan, where John was baptizing. |
| John 1:29 | The next day John seeth Jesus coming unto him, and saith, Behold the Lamb of God, which taketh away the sin of the world. |
| Matthew 3:4 (costume) | And the same John had his raiment of camel's hair, and a leathern girdle about his loins; and his meat was locusts and wild honey. |

**Two things the text does and does not say — every writer and every page must keep them:**

1. **One question, then a denial nobody asked for.** They ask "Who art thou?" (1:19). John answers, unprompted, "I am not the Christ" (1:20). Only then do they guess — twice: Elias, and that prophet (1:21). The KJV does **not** record them asking "Are you the Christ?". So it is **not** "three guesses". It is: one question; three times he says who he is not.
2. **Isaiah's words and John's words are not the same words in the KJV.** Isaiah 40:3: "The voice of **him that crieth** in the wilderness". John 1:23 (and Matthew 3:3): "the voice of **one crying** in the wilderness". John himself says he is quoting Isaiah ("as said the prophet Esaias"). So say: *Isaiah wrote of a voice crying in the wilderness*, or *John took his answer from Isaiah*. **Never say "the same words" or "those exact words".**

## 7. Words brief for the blind writers (step 1)


**Task.** Write the narration for a 45-second Bible short. From scratch. KJV attached (section 1, including its two notes).

**What it is about.** Priests and Levites ask John the Baptist, "Who art thou?" (John 1:19). He tells them who he is **not** — first, unprompted, "I am not the Christ"; then not Elijah; then not the Prophet (1:20-21). Pressed for an answer about himself, he says: "I am the voice of one crying in the wilderness" (John 1:23) — an answer he takes from the prophet Isaiah, who wrote of a voice crying in the wilderness: "Prepare ye the way of the LORD" (Isaiah 40:3). The next day John sees Jesus and says, "Behold the Lamb of God, which taketh away the sin of the world" (John 1:29).

**The film must say these three things** (your own plain words or KJV):
1. John points away from himself to Jesus.
2. Jesus is the Lord whose way is being made ready — God himself coming. Say "God" plainly once.
3. Jesus is the Lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world.

**Shape — exactly 8 lines, in this order:**

| Line | Words | Job |
|---|---|---|
| 1 hook | 4-5 | Open the question. Spoken from the first frame. No "Did you know", no "What if" |
| 2 | 11-14 | Who came, from where, with what one question |
| 3 | 11-14 | He says who he is not. **Voice only one answer** ("I am not the Christ."); report the others in the narrator's words |
| 4 | 11-14 | They press him for an answer about himself |
| 5 the turn | 11-14 | His answer: he is a voice (John 1:23) |
| 6 | 11-14 | He took that from Isaiah, who wrote of that voice long before — and whose way it makes ready |
| 7 | 11-14 | The next day: "Behold the Lamb of God" (John 1:29) |
| 8 landing | 10 or fewer | Land on Jesus, on a word the passage gave you (*voice*, *way*, *behold*, *Lamb*). Then 3 seconds of silence |

**Budget — hard:**
- **80-88 words in total.**
- **At most 5 voiced character quotes in the whole film, and at most 1 on any line.** Each voiced quote adds about 0.4 s of pause.
- Time check: `words / 2.2 + 0.4 x voiced quotes` must come to 38-42 seconds. Show your sum.
- One line may be 10 or 15 words. The total is not flexible.

You may move a beat one line earlier or later if you keep 8 lines and keep the turn on line 5.

**KJV runs that fit.** A quote must be **one unbroken run of the verse's own words: no "...", no dropped word, exact capitals.**

| Verse | Run | Words | Voice / note |
|---|---|---|---|
| John 1:19 | Who art thou? | 3 | questioner |
| John 1:20 | I am not the Christ. | 5 | john. The one voiced answer on line 3 |
| John 1:22 | What sayest thou of thyself? | 5 | questioner. Old wording; the narrator may ask it in plain words instead and save a quote |
| John 1:23 | I am the voice of one crying in the wilderness | 10 | john. **Stop at "wilderness".** The full speech is 22 words and does not fit |
| John 1:23 | Make straight the way of the Lord | 7 | john. Optional, its own run. **Never** "as said the prophet Esaias" |
| Isaiah 40:3 | Prepare ye the way of the LORD | 7 | read by the narrator (not a character quote) |
| John 1:29 | Behold the Lamb of God | 5 | john |
| John 1:29 | Behold the Lamb of God, which taketh away the sin of the world. | 13 | john. The whole speech on line 7, with at most 2 narrator words before it |

For John 1:29 choose one: the whole 13-word speech on line 7; **or** the 5-word run on line 7 and "takes away the sin of the world" said by the narrator in plain words on line 7 or 8. **Do not cut the KJV sentence in two across lines 7 and 8.**

**Rules.**
1. **KJV word for word** for anything a character says, and for the narrator's Isaiah reading. Mark each quote with its reference and speaker (`john`, `questioner`, `narrator`). Jesus has no line.
2. **Do not put a question in the questioners' mouths that the KJV does not record.** They do not ask "Are you the Christ?".
3. **Never say Isaiah wrote "the same words" or "those exact words"** as John. Say Isaiah wrote *of* a voice crying in the wilderness.
4. **The narrator speaks plain modern English.** Say "Elijah" and "Isaiah". A viewer with English as a second language and no Bible knowledge must be able to say who, what and why after one watch.
5. **One thread** from line 1 to line 8. Clever in the story's own twist, never in hard words.
6. **The landing is an invitation, not pressure.** No gain or loss, no fear.
7. **Not these endings:** "Will you come?", "Come and see", "Just ask Him", "Ready to...?", "What about you?".
8. No year counts. No baptism of Jesus here; the priests are not there on "the next day"; do not explain the Elijah question.
9. Words only — no sound, music or picture directions.
10. Each line must stand as one picture for about 6 seconds: name things a viewer could see.
11. Give every character line a short emotion cue for the voice (e.g. quiet, firm, glad).

**Hand back.**
- The 8 lines, numbered, with a word count each, the total, the number of voiced quotes, and your time check.
- Each KJV quote with its reference and speaker.
- Where each of the three must-say truths is said (line number).
- One sentence: the curiosity gap and the line where it pays off.
- A title of 2-3 words and one poster idea in one sentence.

**How drafts are judged:** KJV exact and unbroken · the three truths are all said · no question the KJV does not record · never "the same words" · lands on Jesus · one thread · plain words · hook opens a real question in 4-5 words · the turn on line 5 · every line picturable · ending is fresh · 80-88 words, 5 quotes at most, time check passes.
