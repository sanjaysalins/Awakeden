# Independent review — codex (OK, 76s)

**Hook: 7/10.** “A father begs Jesus: my little girl is dying” establishes immediate stakes, but it is a familiar rescue opening. The distinctive tension—Jesus continuing after the death report—arrives beyond the first five seconds.

**Landing: 5/10.** “If you hear that it is too late, hear Him instead” echoes the hearing motif, but the exact final sentence could close numerous other miracle narrations. It meets your stock-closer cap.

1. **The application implies more than this event promises.** “If you hear that it is too late, hear Him instead” transfers Jesus’ instruction to this particular father into an unrestricted instruction for listeners’ circumstances. Following a restored child, it can sound like sufficient belief guarantees a reversed diagnosis or recovered loss. That implication needs correcting; the narration does not explicitly teach it, but leaves it open.

2. **The ending creates a misleading choice between facts and faith.** “The news had said it was over” followed by “hear Him instead” can suggest that the death report was false or that believers should disregard devastating news. The girl really was dead. The messengers’ mistake was concluding that Jesus could therefore do nothing. Preserve that distinction. ([Mark 5:35–43, KJV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Mark+5%3A35-43&version=KJV%3BNIV))

3. **The invitation risks making belief the listener’s burden.** “Only believe” is authentic Scripture; its inclusion is not the problem. Repeating it as the final instruction, after a successful miracle, can leave a grieving listener asking whether their loss persisted because they believed inadequately. The landing needs to locate confidence explicitly in Jesus’ authority and care, rather than leave belief sounding like the condition for obtaining this outcome.

4. **The translation relationship is unclear on first hearing.** “Talitha cumi” followed by the narrator’s “Damsel, I say unto thee, arise” does not explain that the second phrase interprets the first. A newcomer may hear two separate utterances. “Little girl, get up” then supplies a third version. Mark explicitly identifies the interpretation. ([Mark 5:41, KJV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Mark+5%3A35-43&version=KJV%3BNIV))

5. **KJV wording: no altered words found inside quotation marks.** The death report, both occurrences of Jesus’ reassurance, the Aramaic phrase, and its interpretation match the KJV wording. ([Mark 5:35–43](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Mark+5%3A35-43&version=KJV%3BNIV), [Mark 5:36](https://biblehub.com/kjv/mark/5-36.htm))

The hearing/continuing thread holds through the story. Its weakness is the final move from a specific resurrection to an undefined personal “too late.” The freshness remains mostly rhetorical—“heard it, and gone on”—without developing the textual distinction between actual death and the supposed limit of Jesus’ authority.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Remove the implication that believing guarantees reversal of the listener’s loss.
2. Distinguish the true death report from the false conclusion that Jesus could no longer help.
3. Build a more specific landing from Jesus continuing and taking the child’s hand; clarify that the English command interprets the Aramaic.
