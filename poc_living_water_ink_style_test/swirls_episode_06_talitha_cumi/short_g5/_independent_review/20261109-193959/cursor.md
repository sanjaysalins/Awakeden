## Independent adversarial review (narration)

### Doctrinal soundness
- Core move is sound: Jairus is told the situation is hopeless; Jesus tells him not to fear and to believe; the girl rises. No contrarian reading, no invented theology.
- **Risk (application creep):** The turn *“If you hear that it is too late, hear Him instead”* can be heard as a **general promise that “too late” situations will be reversed**, not only as “trust Christ when the report is hopeless.” The text never limits the analogy (death/resurrection vs. spiritual invitation vs. prayer outcome). A listener with no Bible background may leave thinking Jesus routinely overturns final verdicts in *their* life—a step beyond what this pericope actually guarantees.
- **“only believe”** is faithful as a quote, but **belief’s object is unstated** for the listener. In Mark 5:36 it is trust in Jesus in the face of death news; the script never names that in one plain clause, so the doctrine stays implicit rather than grounded.

### KJV verbatim
Quoted strings checked against Mark 5 (KJV):
- *“Thy daughter is dead: why troublest thou the Master any further?”* — matches Mark 5:35.
- *“Be not afraid, only believe.”* — matches Mark 5:36.
- *“Talitha cumi.”* — matches Mark 5:41.
- *“Damsel, I say unto thee, arise.”* — matches the KJV gloss of Mark 5:41.

**No altered words inside quotation marks.** Paraphrase outside quotes (*“my little girl is dying,”* *“Little girl, get up”*) is not presented as KJV.

### Clarity on first hearing
- **“the Master”** (in the quote only) is KJV-faithful but opaque to a zero-Bible listener unless they already know Jesus is in the scene from the hook (“begs **Jesus**”).
- **Aramaic then English:** `JESUS: "Talitha cumi."` → then narrator gives the English in quotes. For ~1 beat, a stranger does not know what was said until the next line—manageable but a small clarity gap.
- **“The news had said it was over”** is clear emotionally; **“hear Him instead”** assumes “Him” = Jesus from earlier—workable but thin for a total outsider.

### Grace-anchored conviction
- No obvious fear-selling, merit, or gain/loss manipulation. The pressure line is the **messengers’** report, not the narrator shaming the listener. Invitation leans on Jesus’s own words—appropriate.

### Freshness = faithful depth
- Fresh angle is the **hopeless report vs. Jesus continuing** (*“Too late”* / *“did not turn back”* / *“gone on”*), not a gimmick reading. Orthodox throughout.
- **Weakness:** The surprise is familiar to anyone who knows the story; for strangers, freshness depends on the hook landing the paradox (dead vs. arise) faster—see scores.

### One thread (hook → middle → CTA)
- Single spine: dying girl → death report → Jesus does not stop → hand → arise → “too late” vs. *“hear Him instead”* + *“Be not afraid, only believe.”* No thread swap.
- **Mirror repetition:** *“Jesus heard it, and did not turn back”* (opening) and *“Jesus had heard it, and gone on”* (pre-CTA) echo without adding new information—can feel like the middle stalled.

### Voice / delivery (narration craft, not doctrine)
- Resurrection command split: **Jesus** gets only *“Talitha cumi”;* the full KJV English *“Damsel, I say unto thee, arise.”* is **narrator**. In the text, that English is Jesus’s interpreted speech. A hostile ear hears the **raising word in narrator voice**, which dulls the dramatic peak and fights multi-voice logic.

### HOOK SCORE: **7/10**
Concrete scene (*father*, *dying*, messenger line) beats template openers. Not capped at 5. Still **not a scroll-stopper for strangers** in the first ~5 seconds: it reads as “another Jesus miracle story” until *“Too late”* lands; no sharp paradox or question in the very first line.

### LANDING SCORE: **7/10**
Closer is **partly earned** (*too late* / *hear Him instead*) but the **final payload is a portable Jesus one-liner** usable on many faith-in-crisis pieces—not a landing image unique to *hand, arise, walked*. Per your rule, famous closers cap low; this is borderline stock because the last words are entirely *“Be not afraid, only believe.”*
