# Independent review — claude (OK, 52s)

**Review of the Jairus's daughter narration (Mark 5)**

**KJV verbatim: clean.**
- "Thy daughter is dead: why troublest thou the Master any further?" matches Mark 5:35.
- "Be not afraid, only believe" matches 5:36.
- "Talitha cumi" and "Damsel, I say unto thee, arise" match 5:41.
- Everything else is unquoted paraphrase, which is fine.
- In KJV the "Thy daughter is dead" line is spoken by messengers from the ruler's house. The script says "the news comes", which is acceptable and gets no voice, so G9 will flag it as advisory only.

**Doctrine: no invented doctrine, but two real problems.**
1. **The landing overreaches the text.**
   - "If you hear that it is too late, hear Him instead" generalizes a one-time resurrection into a pattern: hear Him, and the "too late" reverses.
   - A grieving listener whose child did die hears either "your faith failed" or "Jesus ignored you."
   - The script never connects this raising to Jesus' own death and resurrection, which is what actually answers "too late."
   - "Only believe" is quoted bare and never defined. A newcomer will hear it as a formula or a condition, which edges into works or prosperity framing.
2. **Jesus is never identified.** The listener has no idea who He is or why His word outweighs the messengers'.
   - The script gives no hint that Jesus is Lord over death.
   - A newcomer therefore cannot tell why He is worth hearing "instead."

**Clarity on first hearing.**
- The "heard / hear" pivot is the best part. The news was heard, Jesus heard it, and now you hear Him.
- "Jesus heard it, and did not turn back" can be read as indifference, as if He ignored the news.
- "Too late" is never explained. The script skips why they were late: Jesus stopped for the woman in the crowd, which is the whole tension.
- "Talitha cumi" arrives untranslated, and then the archaic "Damsel…arise" is spoken before the plain gloss "Little girl, get up." A non-native listener gets the hard word first. Put the plain line first, or have the narrator gloss it in the same breath.

**Thread spine: consistent.** "Too late / heard" runs from hook to CTA with no swapping. It is a good spine.

**Freshness.** The angle is conventional ("don't give up on Jesus"). There is no fresh textual detail. The strongest unused material is "She is not dead, but sleepeth", the mourners' laughter, and the Aramaic being preserved as an eyewitness detail.

**HOOK: 6/10.**
- "A father begs Jesus: my little girl is dying" is clear and human.
- It is a plain story opener, not a pattern interrupt.
- A stranger has no reason to stay for the first five seconds. "Too late." arrives only at second 10 or so.
- The strongest line is buried.

**LANDING: 6/10.**
- It does reuse the piece's own "heard" verb, which earns some credit.
- The last sentence is basically a stock "trust Jesus in hard times" closer. It could end a Lazarus, centurion's-servant or storm piece unchanged.
- It restates the earlier quote instead of landing on something the piece found.

Both scores are under 8.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Rebuild the landing so it doesn't promise a reversal. Anchor it in who Jesus is (the one who has authority over death, and who Himself rose), with a plain statement of what "believe" means (trust Him, not a formula). It should also cover the listener whose "too late" was real.
2. Strengthen the hook. Lead with "Too late." or the messengers' line as the cold open, and set up the "heard" thread in the first two seconds. Fix "did not turn back" so it can't read as indifference.
3. Make "Talitha cumi" globally clear. Give the plain meaning first ("Little girl, get up"), then the exact KJV "Damsel, I say unto thee, arise" or the Aramaic. Add one short line to identify Jesus for a newcomer. Consider explaining the delay so "too late" makes sense.
