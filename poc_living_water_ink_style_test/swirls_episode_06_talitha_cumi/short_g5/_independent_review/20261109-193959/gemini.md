# Independent review — gemini (OK, 74s)

As an independent, adversarial reviewer, here is my evaluation of the narration:

**Doctrinal Soundness & KJV Verbatim:**
The theology is sound and orthodox. All KJV quotes ("Thy daughter is dead...", "Be not afraid...", "Talitha cumi", "Damsel, I say unto thee...") are perfectly verbatim and used in their correct biblical context. 

**Clarity on First Hearing & Pacing:**
The climax is severely bottlenecked by redundant translations. You have stacked the resurrection command three times in a row:
1. Aramaic: `JESUS: "Talitha cumi."`
2. KJV English: `NARRATOR: "Damsel, I say unto thee, arise."`
3. Modern paraphrase: `NARRATOR: Little girl, get up.`
Hitting the listener with the exact same instruction three times in three different registers destroys the pacing and momentum of the miracle. You need to drop the modern paraphrase ("Little girl, get up")—the KJV translation is already perfectly clear to a modern ear.

**Thread Spine:**
The spine is tight. The contrast between the world's voice ("too late / it is over") and Jesus's voice ("Be not afraid") tracks cleanly from the first sentence to the last without swapping threads.

**Hook Score: 7/10**
While it thankfully avoids cliché templates, the opening line ("A father begs Jesus: my little girl is dying") reads like a dry plot summary. It lacks the visceral, immediate desperation needed to stop a scroller in the first 5 seconds. It tells us he's begging rather than making us feel the urgency.

**Landing Score: 8/10**
The CTA ("If you hear that it is too late, hear Him instead: 'Be not afraid, only believe.'") is strong. It successfully pays off the "too late" thread established in the hook and uses the piece's own KJV quote as the anchor, preventing it from being a generic stock closer. 

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Delete the redundant modern paraphrase ("Little girl, get up") to stop stalling the climax; let the KJV translation carry the moment.
2. Rewrite the opening sentence of the hook to be more visceral and gripping rather than a clinical summary of the scene.
3. Smooth the transition into the Aramaic quote so it doesn't feel like an abrupt stop in the audio flow.
