# Independent review — grok (OK, 229s)

The quoted KJV lines match Mark 5. The spine is one thread: the report says she is dead, Jesus hears it and goes on, the close asks the listener to hear Him. Hook and landing both sit under 8, and two listener problems need a fix.

**KJV.** These quotations are exact: "Thy daughter is dead: why troublest thou the Master any further?" (Mark 5:35); "Be not afraid, only believe." (Mark 5:36); "Talitha cumi." and "Damsel, I say unto thee, arise." (Mark 5:41). No word inside the quotes is altered. "my little girl is dying" and "Little girl, get up" sit outside quotes. They are glosses. Leave them unquoted.

**Hook: 7.** "A father begs Jesus: my little girl is dying." A stranger hears a summarized Bible setup. The line that can stop a scroll is the next one, "Thy daughter is dead" and "Too late." That blow comes after the setup.

**Landing: 7.** "If you hear that it is too late, hear Him instead: 'Be not afraid, only believe.'" The stock-closer cap stays off. That full sentence, with Mark 5:36 in it, cannot end another piece without becoming false. It still misses the bones. The picture that lands is "took her by the hand" and "She got up and walked." The last sentence leaves that picture and repeats the quote already spoken in the middle. Spoken to "you," with the girl walking as the only result, it can sound like any "too late" will turn if the listener believes.

**Clarity.** On audio the quotation marks vanish. Jesus says "Talitha cumi." Then the narrator says "Damsel, I say unto thee, arise." In Mark 5:41 that "I" is Jesus, "which is, being interpreted." In the narrator's voice, "I" sounds like the narrator commanding the child. Nothing hands the line back to Him. "Little girl, get up" then says it a third time.

**Doctrine.** The event is sound. He heard the death news and did not turn back. He took her hand. She arose. No invented doctrine, no contrarian reading. The soft spot is the leap. Mark 5:36 is spoken to this father about this girl, and the raising follows. "If you hear that it is too late" speaks to anyone, and the only outcome shown is that she walked. That can be heard as a promise the text does not make: believe, and your dead case will get up too.

**Grace.** The piece invites. It does not threaten, bargain, or make the father earn the miracle. Jesus acts after the news. "Only believe" has to stay His call to trust Him.

**Freshness and spine.** The depth is in the text: the messengers say stop, and He goes on. One thread runs from "Too late" through "gone on" to the close.

Smaller: "with her father and mother" is easy to hear as those two alone. Peter, James, and John went in with them (Mark 5:40). "why troublest thou" is hard on a first hearing. "Too late." carries that beat.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Put "Damsel, I say unto thee, arise" in Jesus' voice, or add "which means," so the narrator never says "I say unto thee."
2. Aim the last line at trusting His word when the report says "too late," so it does not sound like every dead situation will get up and walk.
3. Open on "Thy daughter is dead" / "Too late," and close on this piece's picture (He heard it, took her hand, she arose), so hook and landing can clear 8.
