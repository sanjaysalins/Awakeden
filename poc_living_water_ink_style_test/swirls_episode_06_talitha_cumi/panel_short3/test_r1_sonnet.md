# Blind review R1 (Sonnet) - E1 vs E2

Verdict up front: both are mostly the same script. The father is paid off in both. E2 is more resolved than E1 only if the last two lines land; I think they land for a Bible reader but cost a newcomer a beat.

## E1
1. Newcomer. Who: a father. What: two messages, then his girl is raised. Why: Jesus can be trusted. Unclear: "troublest thou the Master any further" (archaic). "Damsel" is explained. "Why bother Him now?" helps. "Took the parents in" is fine. "That, he could do." is odd English; a second-language ear may not parse the inverted order.
2. Gap: "two messages". Opens strongly. Message one is clear (she is dead). Message two ("give her something to eat") arrives at the very end, so it pays off, but the payoff is small and domestic. 7/10.
3. Ending: clipped. "Her father could only stare. The second message... That, he could do." The father arc is paid off, but it stops on a small chore and the last beat is about the father, not Jesus. 6/10. "That, he could do." lands as quiet humour for a native speaker; confusing for ESL.
4. Hook 8/10 (concrete, a question). Pace comfortable, 8/10. "Mark tells us:" interrupts the flow.
5. Doctrine: sound, no outcome promise, no fear pressure. "Be not afraid, only believe" is the grace anchor. Jesus appears, but there is no explicit pointer beyond the story: it drifts to "feed her" and stops. Quotes: 5:35 is "Thy daughter is dead: why troublest thou the Master any further?" (verbatim). 5:36 "Be not afraid, only believe" (KJV has "Be not afraid, only believe." verbatim). 5:41 "Talitha cumi... Damsel, I say unto thee, arise" (verbatim). Small issue: "At the house, people laughed" compresses (5:40 "laughed him to scorn") but is not false. The script says the second message was "give her something to eat": that is a command by Jesus (5:43), fine, though "message" is a metaphor stretch.
6. REVISE. 

## E2
1. Same as E1. Extra: "First: it's over. Last: she's hungry." Newcomer understands "it's over" = death over, "hungry" = she is alive. Short, but "First/Last" is read as message order, which is good.
2. Same gap. Payoff is sharper because the final contrast (death ended / girl hungry) states the reversal in plain words. 8/10.
3. Ending: more resolved, specific, and it echoes the "two messages" frame. 7/10. Risk: "it's over" could be misread as "the story is over" rather than "death is over", and "she's hungry" is a flat final note. Does it say Jesus did it? Only implicitly.
4. Hook and pace same as E1 (8, 8).
5. Doctrine: same as E1. Still no explicit line saying Jesus conquers death for the listener; it lands on a hungry girl, not on Him. Enough pointer? Marginal. Also note "First: it's over" is the narrator's gloss on message one, but message one ("she is dead") was not "it's over"; this slight reframing could be seen as inaccurate, though harmless.
6. PASS (conditional).

## Rank
1. E2. 2. E1.

## Max 3 fixes
1. End on Jesus, not the chore: add a short line such as "Death had said it was over. Jesus said, arise." before the hunger beat.
2. Replace "That, he could do." (E1) with plain English: "A father could do that."
3. Simplify the first message quote's tail or gloss it ("Why bother Him now?" is good, keep it); cut "Mark tells us:" to save time.
