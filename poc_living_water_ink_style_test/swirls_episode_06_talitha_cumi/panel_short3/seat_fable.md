## 3 lines

1. **The gap:** a father asks Jesus for one thing — a hand on his dying girl — and before Jesus arrives the house sends word: dead, stop bothering Him. The open question is not "can Jesus raise her" but "what happens to the father who just heard *too late*."
2. **Where the father's arc resolves:** three visible steps from Mark's own text — he is not left outside with the laughter but **taken into the room** (5:40); he **watches her stand and walk** (5:42; B adds "Her father could only stare" = "astonished with a great astonishment"); and Jesus **hands her back to him** with "feed her" (5:43).
3. **What the last line does:** "That, her father could do." Jesus did what the father could not; then He gave the father the one ordinary thing he could. The man who could do nothing is given something to do. No invitation, no promise, no pressure — the payoff is a plate of food in a father's hands. In B it also rhymes "could only stare" → "could do."

Notes for the judge:
- Dropped the "any further / went further" turn in both — a 35-40 s budget has no room for a second ending, and the father's payoff is the ending now.
- KJV verbatim kept: 5:35, 5:36, 5:41 (+ Mark's gloss, in NARRATOR). "Damsel" quoted once and glossed "little girl"; "troublest thou" glossed "why bother Him." No claim that Mark keeps only this Aramaic.
- Bleeding woman: zero lines (card allows at most a half-line).
- The mother and the three disciples are not named (POV compression); nothing says the father was alone.
- "Twelve years old" not stated (no room); not contradicted.
