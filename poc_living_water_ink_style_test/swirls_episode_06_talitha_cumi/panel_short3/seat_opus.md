# Seat: opus (blind) - Talitha Cumi short

Structure: TWO MESSAGES to one father. The first comes from his house ("dead").
The second comes from Jesus, in the room ("give her something to eat").
The film opens on "two messages" and only pays the second one off at the very end.

## Version A - 73 spoken words (~35s at 2.1 w/s)

NARRATOR: One father got two messages that day. The first: "Thy daughter is dead: why troublest thou the Master any further?"

JESUS: Be not afraid, only believe.

NARRATOR: People laughed. Jesus took the parents in and held her hand.

JESUS: Talitha cumi.

NARRATOR: Mark tells us: "Damsel, I say unto thee, arise." Little girl, get up. She stood and walked. Her father was amazed. The second message: give her something to eat. First: it's over. Last: she's hungry.

## Version B - 83 spoken words (~40s at 2.1 w/s)

NARRATOR: One father got two messages that day. The first: "Thy daughter is dead: why troublest thou the Master any further?" Why bother Him now?

JESUS: Be not afraid, only believe.

NARRATOR: At the house, people laughed. Jesus took the parents in and held her hand.

JESUS: Talitha cumi.

NARRATOR: Mark tells us: "Damsel, I say unto thee, arise." Little girl, get up. She was twelve. She stood and walked. Her father was amazed. The second message: give her something to eat. First: it's over. Last: she's hungry.

## Notes (3 lines)

- GAP: "One father got two messages that day." We hear the first (dead) at once; the viewer waits for the second.
- FATHER'S ARC RESOLVES: he is in the room (5:40), sees her stand and walk, is amazed (5:42), and gets the second message (5:43) - his "too late" is answered by an ordinary father's job: feed her.
- LAST LINE: "First: it's over. Last: she's hungry." It closes the two-message frame and turns death news into a normal-life need. Specific to this story, no promise, no pressure.

Fact notes: "Talitha cumi" in Jesus's voice; Mark's gloss in narrator's voice, verbatim, plain gloss after it. "Why bother Him now?" (B only) glosses "troublest". "Amazed" plainly glosses 5:42's "great astonishment". 5:43's command was to those in the room (the parents were there) - "the second message" is not claimed as said to him alone. No "only Mark keeps Aramaic" claim. Woman with bleeding not mentioned.
