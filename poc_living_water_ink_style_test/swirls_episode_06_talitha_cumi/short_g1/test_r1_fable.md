# Blind review — short_g1 (Fable seat, round 1)

Read only `narration.md`.

**1. Takeaway.** Half delivered. The story half ("Jesus heard 'too late' and kept going") is clear from line 1 to the end. The "you" half ("Someone told YOU too late") is never spoken. The viewer is not addressed once, so the person whose situation got worse watches a story close, not a door open. That is the flatness the owner feels.

**2. Curiosity gap.** Opens well: "Too late. Why bother Him now? Jesus heard it." — what will He do with that word? Pays off in "He did not turn back" and the hand. Works for a newcomer. Known story for Bible readers, so the gap is modest.

**3. Last line.** Correct echo, but weak. "Jesus heard it too" — the "too" does no work (we already heard it in line 1) and reads oddly for ESL. "He kept going" repeats "He did not turn back" and sounds like a self-help slogan — generic. Ends in past tense, on them.

Alternatives:
- A: `"Too late," they said. Jesus heard it. He took her hand anyway.`
- B: `"Too late," they said. Jesus heard it, and walked on to the house. If someone said "too late" to you — He heard that too.`

B makes "too" earn its place and finishes the takeaway. No outcome promised.

**4. Doctrine.** Sound. Grace-anchored, no promise the viewer's dead will rise, no pressure. 5:35, 5:36, 5:41 all KJV verbatim. "Jesus heard it" is the text's own verb ("As soon as Jesus heard the word," 5:36) — fully fair. "Father and mother" omits Peter, James, John — partial, not false. Lands on Jesus' act, not on who He is; acceptable at 38s.

**5. ESL.** "troublest thou the Master" and "Damsel" are both paraphrased at once — pass. "Heard it too" — confusing. "Kept going" — passable.

Scores: hook 6 · gap 7 · ending 5 · pace 8.

**6. REVISE.**
1. Last line → B above.
2. Line 1: `A father asks Jesus for help.` → `A father begs Jesus: my little girl is dying.` (5:23; sets stakes in 2s, pays off "Little girl, get up").
3. `She stood and walked.` → `She got up and walked.` (mirrors "get up").
