# Seat: Fable — blind draft, Talitha Cumi (Mark 5:21-24, 35-43 KJV)

## Narration

NARRATOR: The Bible keeps two words in the exact sound Jesus spoke them. Why these two?

A father named Jairus fell at Jesus' feet. His little girl was dying. "Come and lay thy hands on her," he begged. And Jesus went with him.

The crowd pressed in. Someone touched Him. He stopped.

While Jairus waited, men came from his house.

"Thy daughter is dead. Why troublest thou the Master any further?"

Too late.

Jesus heard it, and turned to Jairus.

JESUS: Be not afraid, only believe.

NARRATOR: He took only Peter, James, John, and the parents. The mourners laughed at Him. He put them out.

Then He took the girl's hand. She was twelve.

JESUS: Talitha cumi.

NARRATOR: "Damsel, I say unto thee, arise." Little girl, get up. And she got up, and walked. Then: give her something to eat.

Those are the two words. Spoken into a room full of "too late."

If you asked for help, and it got worse while you waited — Jesus heard that too. He did not turn back. He went on in.

## 3 lines on choices

1. Hook = the one thing a newcomer can feel without any Bible knowledge: the Bible kept two words in Jesus' own sound. The payoff is structural, not clever — those two kept words are the ones spoken into the "too late" room, so the hook closes exactly where the story turns.
2. "Too late" is the Fray. It is said once by the messengers, once as a bare beat, and once in the landing, so the viewer who asked for help and watched it get worse is named without pressure; "Be not afraid" stays Jesus' own line, a comfort not a condition.
3. Landing is built only from the piece's own actions — He heard it, did not turn back, went on in — no stock closer, no promise that every dead child is raised today; "damsel" appears once as the KJV gloss and is immediately paraphrased ("Little girl, get up") so no archaic word carries the meaning.
