# Seat: opus (blind writer) - Talitha Cumi

## Narration

NARRATOR: Mark wrote his book in Greek. But one time, he kept the very sound of Jesus's own language. Two words: Talitha cumi. Why keep those two?

A father, Jairus, falls at Jesus's feet. His little girl is dying. Jesus goes with him. But the crowd is slow. Someone touches Him. They stop.

Then men come from the house:
"Thy daughter is dead: why troublest thou the Master any further?"

Too late.

Jesus hears it. He turns to the father.

JESUS: "Be not afraid, only believe."

NARRATOR: At the house, people are crying loudly. Jesus says she is only sleeping. They laugh at Him. He sends them all out. Only the mother, the father, and Peter, James and John go in.

He takes her by the hand.

JESUS: "Talitha cumi."

NARRATOR: Mark tells us what it means: "Damsel, I say unto thee, arise." Little girl, get up.

She is twelve. She gets up and walks. And Jesus says: give her something to eat.

That is why Mark kept the words. The house said, "too late." Jesus took her hand and said, "arise."

## Choices

1. Hook = a real puzzle (Mark keeps Aramaic in a Greek book); the "why?" is paid off in the last two lines: so we hear what Jesus says after "too late."
2. Plain words carry the meaning ("little girl, get up"); "damsel" appears once, only inside the KJV quote; Jesus voices only his two KJV lines; the woman is one half-line.
3. Landing is built from the piece's own words ("too late" vs "arise", "hand"). No promise that every death is undone today, no pressure.
