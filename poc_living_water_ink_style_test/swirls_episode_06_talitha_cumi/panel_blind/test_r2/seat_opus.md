# Seat: opus (newcomer, cold read)

## TEXT P
1. WHO: Jesus and Jairus, a father whose little girl is dying. WHAT: She dies while Jesus is delayed; Jesus goes in anyway, takes her hand, says "Talitha cumi," and she gets up. WHY: Jesus speaks after "too late," so he has the last word.
2. Re-read: "But the crowd is slow. Someone touches Him. They stop." (Who stops? Why? It reads like the crowd stops.) "troublest thou the Master" is old English, hard. "Damsel" is hard, but "Little girl, get up" saves it.
3. Opening "why do these two words matter?" is only answered by suggestion (last line). 5/10. Not quite earned; I had to guess the answer.
4. 6/10.
5. "The crowd is slow" is not quite fair: the text says Jesus stopped because a woman touched him. Otherwise faithful (Mark 5).

## TEXT Q
1. WHO: Same. WHAT: Same, but clearer that Jesus himself stopped and Jairus had to wait. WHY: Jesus goes in even when it is "too late" and the news got worse while waiting.
2. Re-read: "troublest thou the Master." "a room full of 'too late'" is a nice image but slightly abstract. Last line "If it got worse while you waited" took two reads.
3. Yes, it says "Here is why those two words matter." 7/10. Mostly earned; the answer is about "too late," less about the Aramaic sound itself.
4. 7.5/10.
5. Delay line now matches Mark 5. Nothing false.

## Verdict
Best: Q. One fix: make the answer tie back to the language hook, e.g. "Mark kept Jesus's own words because these were the words that beat 'too late'" — so the opening question is answered directly, not sideways.
