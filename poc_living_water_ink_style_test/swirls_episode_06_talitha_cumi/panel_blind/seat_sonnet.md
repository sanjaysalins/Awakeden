# Seat Sonnet - Talitha Cumi (blind draft)

NARRATOR: Two strange words, kept in the Bible exactly as Jesus said them. Talitha cumi. Wait for what they mean.
NARRATOR: Jairus fell at Jesus's feet. His little girl was dying. Jesus went with him, but the crowd was thick, and every step was slow.
NARRATOR: Then men came from his house.
NARRATOR: "Thy daughter is dead: why troublest thou the Master any further?"
NARRATOR: He had asked for help, and it got worse while he waited. Too late. Jesus heard it, and turned to him.
JESUS: "Be not afraid, only believe."
NARRATOR: Jesus took three friends and the parents. Inside, people were wailing. He said she was only sleeping. They laughed at him. He sent them out, went in, and took her hand.
JESUS: "Talitha cumi."
NARRATOR: Mark tells us what it means.
JESUS: "Damsel, I say unto thee, arise."
NARRATOR: Girl, get up. She stood, and she walked. She was twelve. Then Jesus said, give her something to eat.
NARRATOR: Jairus heard "too late." Jesus had the last word. A hand. "Arise." Then bread.

Word count: 164 (spoken text, tags excluded)

## Choices
- Hook is the unexplained Aramaic, with a promise ("Wait for what they mean") that pays off at the translation line, then the landing reuses "arise" and the hand.
- The mourners are cut to wailing and laughing, the bleeding woman is only implied as "the crowd" and delay; Jairus-only, no pressure, no promise about others.
- Landing is built from the piece's own words (last word, hand, arise, bread), with no stock closer; "damsel" is quoted once and glossed as "Girl, get up".
