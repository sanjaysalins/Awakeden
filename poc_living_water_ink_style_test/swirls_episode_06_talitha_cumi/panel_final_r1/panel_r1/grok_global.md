## 1. WHO IS THIS FOR

For Linh, 26, in Ho Chi Minh City. She watches Shorts with auto-captions, has school English, knows the name Jesus, and does not know what "Mark" means.

She finishes, because the pictures tell the story: a father begs, bad news, Jesus keeps walking, a still girl opens her eyes and walks, the family hugs. She stops trusting the words at line 2, when captions hit "why troublest thou the Master." Her mother, reading the boxes, is not served. "Thy," "Talitha cumi," and "Be not afraid" look like broken English. The person who shares is a church friend who already knows this story. A parent with no Bible and no English is not served.

## 2. MAIN TAKEAWAY

"People said the girl was already dead, but Jesus told her father not to be afraid, and she got up."

Line 9 carries the lesson. Line 7 carries the proof. The orange stroke through "Too late." makes that point with the sound off. What to believe is not clear. Line 4, "only believe," has no object.

## 3. GLOBAL AUDIENCE

Who: a father, his dying daughter, and Jesus. What: a message says she is dead; Jesus still goes in, takes her hand, and she gets up and walks. Why, as the film wants it: when you are told it is too late, listen to Jesus. I get that from lines 3 and 9 and from the pictures.

I get lost at line 2. Spoken: "why troublest thou the Master any further?" "Troublest thou" is old word order. Auto-captions and machine translation mangle it. "Master" means teacher here, and it also means slave-owner or boss. The box says "Thy daughter / is dead." "Thy" is not a living word. Plain replacement: "Your daughter is dead. Why bother the Teacher any more?"

Line 6 is the serious miss. For seven seconds the only words on the rising girl are "Talitha cumi." In many places, strange holy words plus a body opening its eyes means a spell. The voice translates it at line 7. The box does not. Plain replacement on that box: "Talitha cumi — Little girl, get up."

Other words that fail:
- Line 4 and line 9: "Be not afraid, only believe." "Be not" translates badly. Plain: "Do not be afraid. Just believe."
- Line 7: "Damsel, I say unto thee, arise." "Damsel" is a fairy-tale word and can come out as a young woman, not a child. "Arise" also means "come into being." The plain line, "Little girl, get up," arrives on screen at 32.3 s, after her eyes are already open.
- Line 8: "The news had said it was over. Jesus had heard it, and gone on." "The news" means TV news. "Over" and "gone on" each have several meanings. The picture is a hug, not a walk, so "gone on" floats. Plain: "The message said it was finished. Jesus heard it and kept going."

Universal: a parent, a dead child, someone who does not quit, the child walking, a family holding food. Insider: gold "Mark" labels, King James grammar, and Aramaic with no meaning on the frame.

## 4. BIBLICALLY ACCURATE

- Line 1: RIGHT. A father begs for his dying little girl (Mark 5:22-23). The film skips his name, Jairus, and his ask that Jesus come and lay hands on her. Not a false claim.
- Line 2: RIGHT. The quote matches Mark 5:35, and the label is the right verse. It is the messenger speaking.
- Line 3: RIGHT as a paraphrase. The Bible does not say "Too late." The messengers say she is dead, so stop. Jesus hears and still goes to the house (Mark 5:36-38).
- Line 4: RIGHT. Exact Mark 5:36.
- Line 5: RIGHT. He goes in with the father and mother and takes her hand (Mark 5:40-41). Peter, James, and John are left out. "With her parents" is still true.
- Line 6: RIGHT. "Talitha cumi," Mark 5:41, label correct.
- Line 7: RIGHT. That is the King James translation of the phrase, and she got up and walked (Mark 5:41-42). "Little girl, get up" is a sound plain rendering. A girl of about twelve matches the text.
- Line 8: RIGHT as a summary. The bowl matches the command to give her something to eat (Mark 5:43). The voice never says that. The text says they were astonished. The tears are the picture's addition.
- Line 9: the quote is RIGHT for that father. The leap is OVERCLAIMED. Crossing "Too late." out of the viewer's life treats one word to one father about one girl as a promise that "too late" is false for everyone watching.

The film never says Jesus called her only asleep (Mark 5:39). That is an omission, not a wrong sentence.

## 5. BEST VERSION POSSIBLE

Keep these pictures. Put a line a child could repeat on the box in the same second it is heard. At the miracle, put the foreign words and their meaning together so nobody learns a chant.

The one highest-value change: on the line 6 card, while her eyes open, print "Talitha cumi — Little girl, get up."

Score: 6/10. The story is visible without English. The climax text and the last card are insider English.

## CURIOSITY GAP

Opens at line 2 (she is dead — why go on?). The stroke at line 3 leaks part of the answer. Pays off at line 7, when she walks. Score 7/10. Worth the wait: yes. The wait is short, and the walk is a real payoff.

## THREE SMALLEST FIXES

1. Line 2, say and show: "Your daughter is dead. Why bother the Teacher any more?"
2. Line 6 box: "Talitha cumi — Little girl, get up."
3. Line 9 box (same words as line 4): "Do not be afraid. Just believe."
