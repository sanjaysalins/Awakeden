# Independent review — claude (OK, 54s)

**Narration review: Jairus's daughter (Mark 5)**

**KJV check**
- Mark 5:35, "Thy daughter is dead: why troublest thou the Master any further?" is exact.
- Mark 5:36, "Be not afraid, only believe." is exact.
- Mark 5:41, "Damsel, I say unto thee, arise." is exact. "Talitha cumi" is correct, though the KJV pairs it with the gloss "which is, being interpreted."
- "People laughed at Him" is a paraphrase of "laughed him to scorn." It is outside the quotation marks, so it is acceptable. It does lose the scorn.
- No altered words inside quotation marks.

**Doctrine**
- Nothing invented, and no contested reading.
- The piece drops "the damsel is not dead, but sleepeth" (Mark 5:39). This is the key interpretive line. Without it, a newcomer doesn't see why the crowd laughed, or that Jesus claims power over death.
- The man is never identified (Jairus, a synagogue ruler). That is fine for brevity, but "the Master" is never tied to who Jesus is.

**Clarity and the "two messages" conceit**
- The conceit contradicts itself. Three things are spoken to the father: the messengers' message, "Be not afraid, only believe," and "give her something to eat."
- The narration calls "feed her" the "second message." That label belongs more naturally to "Be not afraid, only believe," which is Jesus's actual answer to the first message.
- A first-time listener has to work out why "only believe" doesn't count as the second.
- Mark 5:43 puts "give her something to eat" after the raising, as a command to the household. It is not a message "about his daughter" delivered to the father in parallel with the first. The framing bends the text to fit the device.
- "The first said it was over. The second said: feed her. Only the living eat." is a neat line. It treats the feeding command as proof of life, which is an inference. Luke 8:55 says the same thing about the same command, so the inference is sound. The piece presents it as the point without saying so.
- Archaic words: "troublest thou," "Master," "Damsel." Each is glossed or short, so these are acceptable.
- At about 90 words, the piece is far short of a 60-second short. It plays as roughly 35 seconds, with no room for Beats 3 and 4 (proof and conviction).

**Grace and thread**
- No fear framing and no works framing.
- The thread of two messages, over versus alive, is consistent, but it drops out of the closing line.

**Hook: 6/10.**
- "One father got two messages about his daughter that day" is not a template open and does open a gap.
- It has no stake in the first words (no death, no stranger-level jolt).
- "Why bother Him now?" is a soft second beat.

**Landing: 5/10.**
- The final line is built from this piece's own image, and "Only the living eat" does land.
- But the piece never addresses the listener and never lands on Jesus as the one who raises the dead.
- It ends on an observation about eating, with no invitation. The project's contract requires a CTA to Jesus, so this misses the spine.
- The line "The first said it was over" gives the piece its only turn. It doesn't carry the gospel weight of death undone.

**Both scores are under 8, so the verdict is REVISE.**

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Add a real landing on Jesus as the one who undoes death, with a CTA built from the piece's own "over" versus "alive" image. Do not end on the feeding aphorism alone. The line should invite the listener, with no fear or works framing.
2. Repair the "two messages" conceit. Either make Jesus's reply, "Be not afraid, only believe," the second message and use the feeding command as the proof of life, or drop the numbering. Include "not dead, but sleepeth" (Mark 5:39) so the laughter makes sense.
3. Sharpen the hook with a concrete stake in the first sentence, such as the girl being dead before Jesus arrives. Lengthen the narration to a full ~59-second five-beat shape, since it is currently only about 90 words.
