# Independent review — codex (OK, 82s)

**Hook: 6/10. Landing: 5/10.**

1. **The hook delays the actual stakes.** “One father got two messages about his daughter that day” supplies a counting puzzle before giving a stranger a reason to care. The death announcement creates urgency, but it arrives after the opening sentence.

2. **The two-message framing strains the sequence.** “Then the second message, from Jesus” follows two earlier statements from Jesus: “Be not afraid, only believe” and “Talitha cumi.” A listener can reasonably wonder why those do not count. The comparison between a death report and a feeding instruction is understandable, but its numbering creates unnecessary friction.

3. **The laughter lacks its cause.** “At the house, people laughed at Him” omits Jesus’ preceding statement about the girl sleeping. With no explanation, a newcomer hears people laughing during a child’s death scene without understanding what prompted it. That causal connection is explicit in [Mark 5:39–40, KJV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Mark+5%3A35-43&version=KJV).

4. **The translated command sounds like another utterance.** Placing “Talitha cumi” under JESUS and “Damsel, I say unto thee, arise” under NARRATOR, without identifying the latter as its translation, obscures their relationship. “Little girl, get up” then supplies a third formulation. On first hearing, this can sound like successive commands.

5. **The landing supplies a deduction, not a CTA.** “Only the living eat” connects to the feeding image, but gives the listener no invitation or response. It could also close another resurrection narration involving food, so it fails your portability test. Its logical neatness displaces the personal weight of Jesus restoring this particular daughter.

6. **Doctrinal and quotation checks:** No altered words occur in the four quoted passages; they match the KJV wording. “Give her something to eat” is an unquoted paraphrase, so it does not violate the quotation rule. The narration introduces no evident invented doctrine, works requirement, or fear-based appeal. However, the feeding detail remains an observation about restored life; the ending does not develop it into a grace-anchored invitation. [Mark 5:35–43, KJV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Mark+5%3A35-43&version=KJV)

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Build a listener-facing invitation from Jesus’ restoring and feeding the girl; replace the closing deduction.
2. Resolve the two-message counting problem so the framing survives first hearing.
3. Explain the cause of the laughter and identify the English command as the translation of “Talitha cumi.”
