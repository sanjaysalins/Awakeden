# Independent review — grok (OK, 177s)

The food line is doing the job that belongs to "arise." That swap is the problem.

**Doctrinal soundness.** The story is Mark 5, and the miracle is real: she was dead, Jesus raised her, she walked, he told them to feed her. That part holds. The frame does not. "The second message, from Jesus: give her something to eat" and "The first said it was over. The second said: feed her" make a household command the answer to "Thy daughter is dead." In the text, the answer to the death report is "Be not afraid, only believe" and "Damsel, I say unto thee, arise." Eating comes after, to show the life is bodily and the child is well. Treating "feed her" as the counter-message to death is a freshness move. It is not what Mark claims.

**KJV.** Every word inside quotes matches the KJV. "Thy daughter is dead: why troublest thou the Master any further?" "Be not afraid, only believe." "Talitha cumi." "Damsel, I say unto thee, arise." No altered word. "give her something to eat" is outside quotes. It paraphrases "commanded that something should be given her to eat" (Mark 5:43). A listener can still hear it as a third Jesus quote. It is not marked as one.

**Clarity on first hearing.** The piece promises two messages, then speaks four times.
1. "Thy daughter is dead..."
2. "Be not afraid, only believe."
3. "Talitha cumi" / "arise."
4. "the second message... feed her."

A newcomer cannot tell which speech is "the second." "Why bother Him now?" is a fair plain gloss. "Little girl, get up" is clear. "people laughed at Him" is not. They laughed because he said the girl was not dead but sleeping (Mark 5:39). That line is missing, so the laugh has no cause. "Only the living eat" is easy to hear as a proverb and easy to miss as "this child is really alive."

**Grace.** No threat, no bargain, no works pitch. "Be not afraid, only believe" is left hanging. Nothing after it invites the hearer. The close states a fact about the girl.

**Freshness.** The food detail is in the text, and it is worth noticing. The script promotes it into the spine. The surprising word that answers death is "arise." "She stood and walked" already paid that off. The ending then steps past it.

**Spine.** Hook: two messages, death vs something else. Middle: faith, a laugh, a resurrection. Close: back to message-counting, and the "something else" is food. "only believe" starts a second thread and never returns. One story, two spines.

**Hook: 7.** "One father got two messages about his daughter that day" spends the first seconds on a writer's frame. "Thy daughter is dead" arrives at the edge of five seconds. A stranger gets a setup, then a stake. Stronger than a template open. A scroll still survives it.

**Landing: 6.** "Only the living eat" comes from this piece's own image, so it is not a stock "Come to Jesus." It still fails the bones test. It is a proverb about her, not an invitation to the hearer. The same sentence could close another "they ate, so they were alive" story. And it answers "it was over" with "feed her," after the script already showed the real reversal: "She stood and walked."

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Make "arise" the answer to "Thy daughter is dead." Keep "feed her" as the proof that she is really alive, after she is up.
2. Drop the "two messages" count. It fights the lines Jesus already speaks. Add why they laughed, in plain words, or cut the laugh.
3. Replace "Only the living eat" with a last line that uses this story's own verb and invites the hearer. That sentence is a proverb about the girl.
