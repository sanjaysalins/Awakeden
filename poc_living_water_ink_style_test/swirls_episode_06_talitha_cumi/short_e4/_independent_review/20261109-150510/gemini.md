Here is the independent, adversarial review of the narration artifact.

**Doctrinal soundness:** The retelling of Jairus's daughter (Mark 5) is biblically accurate, but it completely lacks a gospel application. It functions as a historical recap rather than an evangelical message because it never turns the truth of the text toward the listener. 

**KJV verbatim:** PASS. All quoted text exactly matches Mark 5:35, 36, and 41. 

**Clarity on first hearing:** The framing conceit is slightly strained. The narrator claims the father got "two messages about his daughter." The first ("Thy daughter is dead") is indeed a message. But the second ("give her something to eat") is a direct command to the parents, not a "message about his daughter." A listener will follow the story, but the structural logic is a bit forced.

**Grace-anchored conviction:** FAIL. There is absolutely no conviction and no invitation. The script simply stops. It observes a fact about the story but makes zero attempt to invite the listener to Jesus.

**Freshness = faithful depth:** "Only the living eat" is a genuinely fresh and sharp observation about the text. However, the depth is wasted because it never bridges to the orthodox claim of what Jesus does for *us* today. 

**One thread spine:** The "two messages" thread holds the piece together from start to finish, but it abruptly drives off a cliff at the end instead of landing the plane.

**SCORE THE LANDING: 2.** "The first said it was over. The second said: feed her. Only the living eat." There is NO Call To Action (CTA). I cannot evaluate if the CTA is built from the piece's own image because the writer forgot to include one. A 60-second short in this engine must land on Jesus and invite the viewer. This just ends on a poetic cliffhanger.
