# Blind test R1 (Sonnet) - C1, C2, C3

## C1 (30s)
**Newcomer:** Who: a father (Jairus), Jesus, a dead girl. What: Jesus takes her hand, says two words. Why: ends "Told it is too late? ... only believe" - clear.
**Unclear lines:** "Damsel ... arise" (but "Little girl, get up" rescues it). "troublest thou the Master" is heavy; "Why bother Him now?" helps. Who "the Master" is: guessable. It never says she lived.
**Gap open/pay off:** 7. Opens strongly; meaning given. But no visible result (she gets up?) - payoff is the translation only.
**Hook (3s):** 8. **Earned ending:** 6 - "Told it is too late?" is a stock-ish turn, but the repeated line is earned. **Rushed:** 6 (dense for 30s; "He took her hand" is a bare beat).
**Bible reader:** No error. "Be not afraid, only believe" (Mk 5:36) is verbatim. Mk 5:35 "Thy daughter is dead: why troublest thou the Master any further?" verbatim. Mk 5:41 "Damsel, I say unto thee, arise" verbatim. Slight over-promise: "He says to you" applies a specific miracle to all hearers (a common sermon move, not an error). Omits the sleeping/laughter, the 12 years.
**Overall: 7.**

## C2 (40s)
**Newcomer:** Same, plus clearer sequence: delay on the way, mockers, she walked.
**Unclear:** "someone touched Him. He stopped." - a newcomer does not know why this matters (the woman with the issue of blood is not named); it feels like a detour. "He said she was sleeping" - confusing right after "dead" with no explanation. "feed her" is abrupt.
**Gap:** 8 - the resurrection payoff ("She walked") is shown. **Hook:** 8. **Ending:** 7. **Rushed:** 5 (many events in 40s, the touch detour eats time).
**Bible reader:** Accurate. Mk 5:39 "not dead, but sleepeth" is not quoted, but paraphrased fine. No over-promise. The touch detour is faithful.
**Overall: 7.**

## C3 (40s)
**Newcomer:** Hook "Before Jesus said them, people laughed. Why?" is odd - a newcomer does not know who laughed or when. The promise is a laughter question, not a meaning question; "Talitha cumi" meaning is paid off ("Little girl, get up"), the laughter is answered ("they laughed" because he said sleeping) but only implicitly.
**Unclear:** "The crowd pushed. Someone touched Him." Same detour confusion. "She was twelve" is nice but unexplained why it matters. "give her food" fine.
**Gap:** 6 - the gap asked (why laugh) is answered weakly; the "dead" fact is not stated up front, so the hook is vague. **Hook:** 6. **Ending:** 7. **Rushed:** 5.
**Bible reader:** Accurate (Mk 5:42 age twelve; 5:43 "something should be given her to eat"). Minor: "laughed" before the words is correct.
**Overall: 6.**

## Ranking
1. C1 (cleanest, strongest hook, best pace for its length)
2. C2 (best payoff, a bit crowded)
3. C3 (weakest hook, unclear gap)

## Top 3 convergent fixes
1. Add a short reason for "sleeping": say plainly "To Him, death was only sleep" or similar, so newcomers do not think it contradicts "dead". (C2, C3)
2. Cut or explain the "touched Him / He stopped" detour (name it: a sick woman). Otherwise cut it. (C2, C3)
3. In C1, add one beat that shows the result ("She stood up.") before the closing line, so the miracle pays off and not only the translation.
