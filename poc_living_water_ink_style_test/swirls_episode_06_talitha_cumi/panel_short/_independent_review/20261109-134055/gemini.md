# Independent review — gemini (OK, 70s)

Here is the independent review of the narration artifact:

**Doctrinal Soundness & Grace-Anchored Conviction**
The script flattens a profound biblical miracle into generic, therapeutic encouragement. By landing on "Told it is too late? He says to you: 'Be not afraid, only believe,'" it takes Christ's specific authority over physical death and applies it as a blanket comfort for a viewer's bad day. It lacks a true gospel pivot—there is no mention of sin, the cross, or ultimate resurrection, just a sentiment to "believe" when things look bleak. 

**KJV Verbatim**
The quotes are accurate to Mark 5:35-41. No altered words found in the quotation marks.

**Freshness & Depth**
There is zero freshness here. The script is simply a rushed, linear summary of the biblical narrative. It completely fails the "surprising about the TEXT" requirement. There is no overlooked detail, historical context, or deeper theological thread being pulled—just an abridged retelling.

**One Thread Spine**
The thread of "too late" is present (introduced by the messengers, resolved in the CTA), but it is incredibly thin and does not carry enough weight to sustain a compelling 60-second video.

**Hook Score: 5/10**
"Talitha cumi. Two words Jesus said to a dead girl. What do they mean?" This is a vocabulary lesson, not a viral hook. It promises a translation rather than a revelation. It feels like the opening to a children's Sunday school lesson and will not stop a secular scroller.

**Landing Score: 6/10**
"Told it is too late? He says to you: 'Be not afraid, only believe.'" While it avoids the banned stock phrases ("Come to Jesus") and reuses the piece's own KJV verse, it fails to land in the bones because it lacks the weight of the gospel. It is purely motivational. 

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Elevate the landing from generic life-encouragement to a true gospel CTA anchored in Christ's ultimate victory over death.
2. Rewrite the hook to introduce a compelling tension, paradox, or overlooked detail, rather than a simple translation quiz.
3. Inject actual "faithful depth" into the body of the script—find a specific, surprising angle in the Mark 5 text rather than just summarizing the plot.
