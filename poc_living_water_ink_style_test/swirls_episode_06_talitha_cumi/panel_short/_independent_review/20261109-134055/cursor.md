## Independent adversarial review

### KJV verbatim
Quoted lines match Mark’s account in the KJV (5:35–36, 5:41 interpretation): messenger line, both `"Be not afraid, only believe."`, `"Talitha cumi."`, and `"Damsel, I say unto thee, arise."` No altered words inside quotation marks.

**Nit:** Jesus’s spoken KJV line in Mark 5:41 is Aramaic plus the gloss *“which is, being interpreted, Damsel…”* The script splits that (Jesus = Aramaic only; narrator = English gloss). That’s a dramaturgy choice, not a misquote—but a strict “everything Jesus said in KJV” auditor might ask whether the English belongs in narrator voice only (as here) or should stay tied to the same beat as in the text.

### Doctrinal soundness
Orthodox reading of Mark 5:21–43: Jesus overrules “too late,” raises the child, faith/command to Jairus. No contrarian reading, no invented theology.

**Pressure point:** *“Told it is too late? He says to you:”* + `"Be not afraid, only believe."` jumps from Jairus (a father whose daughter just died) to the listener without a one-line guard (e.g. that this is faith in **Christ**, not a promise that every “too late” situation gets the same **physical** outcome). Evangelical pulpits do this application often; for a **global, zero–Bible-knowledge** audience it can still sound like “believe and your dead situation reverses,” which is easy to mishear as formula.

### Clarity on first hearing
Strengths: *“Little girl, get up”* after the KJV gloss helps non-KJV listeners.

**Gap vs hook:** Opening asks *“What do they mean?”* The body delivers the KJV English and a plain paraphrase but never briefly names the Aramaic (*roughly “little girl, get up”*) before or beside *“Talitha cumi.”* A stranger may think the question was dropped.

**Logic:** *“Why bother Him now?”* after the messenger quote is clear paraphrase; fine. The arc *too late → hand → arise → to you* is followable.

### Grace-anchored conviction
No gain/loss, works, or scare tactics. *“Be not afraid, only believe”* invites; it does not threaten. OK on this axis.

### Freshness = faithful depth
Entry via two Aramaic words and the messengers’ brutal practicality is text-faithful and not clickbait. The surprise is in the scene (hand on a dead child, command to rise), not in a novel doctrine.

### One thread (hook → middle → CTA)
Single spine: **meaning of Jesus’s words → “too late” → touch → arise → same word to “you.”** No thread swap.

**Weakness:** The **rise/arise** image does the heavy lifting in the middle, but the **close does not land on “arise” / “get up”**—it lands on **believe/fear** from an earlier beat (Jairus before the miracle). That’s a small internal pivot: resurrection verb in the body, faith verb at the end.

### Hook score: **8/10**
*“Talitha cumi.” Two words Jesus said to a dead girl. What do they mean?* — concrete, visceral, not a template opener. Loses a point or two because the promised “meaning” payoff is delayed/implicit rather than paid in the next breath.

### Landing score: **6/10**
*"Told it is too late? He says to you:"* then **`"Be not afraid, only believe."`** again.

- Built partly from **this** pericope (same chapter as Jairus), not a generic “come to Jesus.”
- But **`"Be not afraid, only believe."` already appeared once**; the ending doesn’t escalate—it **repeats**.
- The piece’s distinctive hook/body image is **Talitha / arise**; the final sentence could still close **many** “just believe / don’t fear” shorts without the child or the command to rise. Not a pure stock closer (cap 5), but not “in the bones” of *this* image either—**under 8** per your rule.

### Other flags
- **Runtime/density:** Very compressed; almost no beat between *“She stood up”* and application—conviction may feel like a leap.
- **Multi-voice:** Jesus lines are routed; narrator carries story—appropriate for the scene.
