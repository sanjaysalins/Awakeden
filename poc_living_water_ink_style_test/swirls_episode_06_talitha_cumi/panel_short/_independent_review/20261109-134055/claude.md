# Independent review — claude (OK, 50s)

**Narration review: Talitha Cumi (Mark 5:35–43)**

**KJV check**
- The quoted lines are exact KJV: "Thy daughter is dead: why troublest thou the Master any further?" (5:35), "Be not afraid, only believe" (5:36) and "Damsel, I say unto thee, arise" (5:41).
- "come, she is dying" sits after a colon in narrator voice, where it reads like a quote. Mark 5:23 is "my little daughter lieth at the point of death… come and lay thy hands on her", so it is a paraphrase. Use a clean indirect form ("begged Him to come") or the real KJV words.
- "Little girl, get up" and "Why bother Him now?" are fine as plain paraphrase, because they are not in quote marks.

**Doctrine**
- Nothing is invented, and the story is told accurately.
- **The landing leaves out the gospel link.** The piece stops at "believe" and never says who this Jesus is. A listener with no Bible knowledge gets a coaching line, not the Christ who conquers death. The whole-Bible-through-Jesus spine requires the cross and resurrection link here.
- **"He says to you: Be not afraid, only believe"** applies a promise made to Jairus directly to the listener. With no resurrection anchor it can sound like "believe hard enough and your situation reverses". It also drifts toward faith as a formula or effort. The grace framing is thin, because nothing shows what the believing rests on.
- "Told it is too late?" is not what the text says. The messenger said she was dead. The paraphrase is fair but loose.

**Clarity**
- A newcomer is never told that "Talitha cumi" is Aramaic, or that Jairus is a synagogue ruler.
- "Damsel" is archaic. It is rescued by "Little girl, get up", which is good.
- "Told it is too late?" is a fragment that does not parse cleanly aloud.
- Mark has the mourners laughing Him to scorn, and the girl is twelve and told to be given food. The piece leaves these out, and they are the details that would make it fresh.

**Hook: 6/10**
- "'Talitha cumi.' Two words Jesus said to a dead girl." is a strong, odd opening.
- "What do they mean?" is a quiz-style question, and the answer arrives within about 20 seconds as the plain translation. The gap is small and closes cheaply, so nothing is left to pay off.
- The hook asks about the translation, but the thread the piece actually follows is "be not afraid, only believe". The hook and the middle are about two different things.

**Landing: 6/10**
- The refrain bookend is a real structure.
- But "Be not afraid, only believe" could end the Peter-on-the-water, Gethsemane or storm-calming stories unchanged. That makes it a stock closer, capped at 5–6.
- The piece's own image is the taken hand and the command "arise". The landing does not use either, and does not use the resurrection echo.

**Thread**
- One thread (death, then "believe") runs through the piece, which is good.
- The hook's "meaning of two words" thread is dropped. It is answered in passing and never developed.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Rebuild the landing from the piece's own image (the taken hand, "arise") and tie it to Jesus' own resurrection. For example: the one who took a dead girl's hand is the one who was later raised Himself. This replaces the generic "only believe" closer and fixes the missing gospel anchor, the recycled-closer problem and the faith-as-formula risk.
2. Fix the hook so its question matches the thread that pays off. Either lean on "arise" and resurrection ("He spoke to death like it was sleeping") or open on the messenger's "too late". Put in a real curiosity gap instead of "What do they mean?", which is answered immediately.
3. Clean the quote and paraphrase handling. Replace the "come, she is dying" pseudo-quote, say that "Talitha cumi" is Aramaic, and fix the fragment "Told it is too late?". Consider adding one of the text's concrete details (the mourners' laughter, or "give her meat" / the twelve-year-old) so the retelling feels fresh and not a summary.
