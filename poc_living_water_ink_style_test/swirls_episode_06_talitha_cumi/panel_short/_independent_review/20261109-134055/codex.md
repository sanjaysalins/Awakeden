# Independent review — codex (OK, 76s)

**Hook: 7/10. Landing: 4/10.**

1. **The application exceeds what this episode promises.** “Told it is too late? He says to you:” transfers Jesus’ specific words to Jairus directly to any listener’s unspecified situation. Following “She stood up,” this can imply that believing will reverse whatever loss the listener faces. Mark 5 records a particular resurrection; it does not promise every listener a comparable reversal.

2. **The closing risks making belief sound like the condition for getting your desired outcome.** The final “Be not afraid, only believe” is biblical, but the narration’s bridge gives it a potentially transactional meaning: your situation looks hopeless; believe and it will change. The problem is the framing, not Jesus’ command. The landing needs to anchor confidence in Jesus’ character and authority without implying a guaranteed immediate outcome.

3. **The ending abandons the hook’s distinctive words.** “Talitha cumi” and “arise” establish the central image, but the CTA switches to a general encouragement about fear. “Be not afraid, only believe” could close numerous other miracle narrations unchanged. Under your portability test, it is a stock closer despite being Scripture.

4. **The promised discovery is mostly a translation.** “What do they mean?” creates curiosity, but “Little girl, get up” supplies a definition without developing the passage’s depth: Jesus addresses a dead child with a direct command, and she rises. The script reports that event but rushes past its significance into personal reassurance. No contrarian reading is present; the weakness is underdeveloped textual depth.

5. **First-hearing clarity slips at two transitions.** “Her father, Jairus, begged Jesus: come, she is dying” jumps backward from the dead girl without explicitly marking the earlier moment. Later, “Told it is too late?” leaves “it” undefined, inviting listeners to substitute anything from bereavement to an ambition.

**KJV check:** Every quoted word matches Mark 5:35–41; there are no altered words. The period after “Talitha cumi” replaces the verse’s semicolon, which is a punctuation difference, not a wording error. “Little girl, get up” is unquoted explanatory paraphrase. [KJV text, Mark 5:35–43](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Mark+5%3A35-43&version=KJV).

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Remove the unsupported transfer from Jairus’s miracle to a promised reversal of the listener’s circumstances.
2. Anchor the invitation in Jesus’ authority and grace so belief does not sound like a mechanism for securing an outcome.
3. Build the landing from “Talitha cumi,” the taken hand, or “arise,” carrying the hook’s thread through the close.
