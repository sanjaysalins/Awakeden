# Independent review — grok (OK, 189s)

The KJV lines check out word for word. The story order is right. The piece still fails as a narration, because the close hands Jairus’s line to the listener as if it were theirs.

**Hook: 6/10.**  
“Talitha cumi.” Two words Jesus said to a dead girl. What do they mean?  
“Dead girl” can slow a thumb. “What do they mean?” turns it into a word quiz. That is a definition open. A stranger is not stopped cold.

**Landing: 4/10.**  
Last line: “Be not afraid, only believe.”  
That sentence could end a storm piece, a fear piece, or any “just believe” short. It is a stock closer. Cap is 5. It also ignores this piece’s own picture: the hand, “arise,” the girl who stood up.

**Doctrinal.**  
“Told it is too late? He says to you: ‘Be not afraid, only believe.’”  
In Mark 5:36 Jesus said that to Jairus, about this girl, before he took her hand. The script moves that sentence onto the listener and pairs it with the girl standing up. Heard once, it promises this: if you are told it is too late, believe, and the dead thing gets up. Mark does not say that. The messengers’ line — “why troublest thou the Master any further?” — is a word to a father in a house, not a slogan for every loss.

**Grace.**  
The close is fear plus a lever. “Told it is too late?” is a loss frame. “Only believe” puts the weight on the hearer. There is no gift in the last beat. He came, he took her hand, she rose — that is the grace. The script skips it and gives a command instead.

**Clarity.**  
“Told it is too late?” has no person and no object. Believe what? Believe whom? A first-time hearer just watched a dead girl stand up, then gets a chopped question and a command. The leap is easy to mishear as “your case will turn if you believe.”

**Spine.**  
The hook asks what the two words mean. The middle answers: “Damsel, I say unto thee, arise.” Little girl, get up. She stood up. Then the close drops “arise” and rewinds to the fear line already used on Jairus. Two threads. The ending takes the second one.

**Freshness.**  
The text’s own surprise is tender and hard: he takes a dead girl’s hand and tells her to get up, and she does. The script spends that on a transferable pep line. The claim is softer than the passage.

Both scores are under 8.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Cut the transfer “He says to you: ‘Be not afraid, only believe.’” That line was to Jairus about this girl. Do not make it a promise that the hearer’s “too late” reverses if they believe.
2. Keep one thread. The hook is “Talitha cumi” / get up. Close on his hand and “arise,” not on a fear command that any other short could use.
3. Drop the loss frame “Told it is too late?” Let the scene invite: he took her hand, he said get up, she stood. Do not make “only believe” the lever.
