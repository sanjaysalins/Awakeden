# Seat: Opus (blind) - Talitha Cumi short

## Version A (~30 s) - 75 words

Structure: opens AT the bed on the two words (no meaning given), jumps back to the father, returns to the words, then pays off the meaning.

NARRATOR: "Talitha cumi." Two words Jesus said to a dead girl. What do they mean?
Her father, Jairus, begged: come, she is dying. The news came:
"Thy daughter is dead: why troublest thou the Master any further?" Why bother Him now?

JESUS: Be not afraid, only believe.

NARRATOR: He took her hand.

JESUS: Talitha cumi.

NARRATOR: "Damsel, I say unto thee, arise." Little girl, get up. She rose.
Told it is too late? He says to you: be not afraid.

Word count: 75 (quotes included).

## Version B (~40 s) - 99 words

Structure: opens on the laughter (a second gap: why laugh at a healer?), then the father, the delay, the news, the laugh explained, then the words and their meaning.

NARRATOR: Two words: "Talitha cumi." Before Jesus said them, people laughed. Why?
A father, Jairus, fell at His feet: my little girl is dying, come. The crowd pushed. Someone touched Him. Word came:
"Thy daughter is dead: why troublest thou the Master any further?" Why bother Him?

JESUS: Be not afraid, only believe.

NARRATOR: At the house He said she was sleeping. They laughed. He took her hand.

JESUS: Talitha cumi.

NARRATOR: "Damsel, I say unto thee, arise." Little girl, get up. She was twelve. She walked. He said: give her food.
Told it is too late? He says to you: be not afraid.

Word count: 99 (quotes included).

## Notes

- Cut: A drops the crowd, the laughter, Peter/James/John, age 12 and the food; B drops only the three disciples and the parents in the room. Neither says the words are Aramaic, so there is no claim about what language Mark keeps.
- A newcomer might miss: that "Talitha cumi" is a foreign language (only hinted, never named), who "Master" is (glossed only as "Him"), and in A why "too late" matters, since the delay is not shown.
