# Seat: Fable (blind) — Talitha Cumi, short versions

## Version A — ~30 s

NARRATOR: "Talitha cumi." Two words a father never forgot.
Jairus begged Jesus: come, my little girl is dying.
Then a messenger: "Thy daughter is dead: why troublest thou the Master any further?"
Too late. Why bother Him now?
Jesus heard.

JESUS: Be not afraid, only believe.

NARRATOR: He went in, took her hand.

JESUS: Talitha cumi.

NARRATOR: "Damsel, I say unto thee, arise." The girl got up.
When you hear "too late," He still says:

JESUS: Be not afraid, only believe.

**Word count A: 75** (spoken words, quotes included)

## Version B — ~40 s

NARRATOR: "Talitha cumi." Two words a father never forgot.
Jairus begged Jesus: come, my little girl is dying.
On the way, someone touched Him. He stopped.
Then a messenger: "Thy daughter is dead: why troublest thou the Master any further?"
Too late. Why bother Him now?
Jesus heard.

JESUS: Be not afraid, only believe.

NARRATOR: At the house, He said she was sleeping. They laughed.
He sent them out, went in, took her hand.

JESUS: Talitha cumi.

NARRATOR: "Damsel, I say unto thee, arise." The girl got up. He said: feed her.
When you hear "too late," He still says:

JESUS: Be not afraid, only believe.

**Word count B: 100** (spoken words, quotes included)

## Notes

- Cut: her age (12), Peter/James/John, the secrecy charge; in A also the delay, the laughing mourners, and "feed her" — A keeps only the spine: ask, "too late," "Be not afraid," hand, arise.
- Fear for a newcomer: they may not catch that "Damsel, I say unto thee, arise" IS the meaning of "Talitha cumi" (it is Mark's own translation, read by the narrator, not a new line), and in A they may not realise the girl was already dead when He took her hand — the messenger's one line has to carry that alone.
