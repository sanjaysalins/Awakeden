# Independent review — codex (OK, 62s)

- **Instagram — implied promise of a personal reversal.** “Send this to someone who has been told it is too late” applies a resurrection account to any apparently hopeless situation. Alongside “And she did,” this risks implying that believing will reverse the recipient’s circumstances. YouTube and Facebook include “Jesus did not promise that every story ends like this one”; Instagram and TikTok omit that qualification. The film’s closing application makes this ambiguity more consequential.

- **Instagram — seven hashtags exceed the announced five-hashtag cap.** “#JairusDaughter #TalithaCumi #Jesus #Bible #Faith #KJV #Hope” contains seven, in both the caption and the separate hashtag field. Reduce both versions to five. [Current limit and announcement](https://postpeg.com/blog/instagram-hashtag-limit)

- **YouTube and Facebook titles — an ambiguous hook creates the wrong possible expectation.** “She Was Dead. Jesus Kept Walking” can suggest Jesus walked past or abandoned her. The intended meaning is that He continued toward her house. That continuation is a reasonable narrative inference, but the title withholds its direction to manufacture ambiguity. This is a misleading-reading risk, not a demonstrable historical lie.

- **YouTube and Facebook — the KJV attribution does not clearly distinguish quotation from paraphrase.** YouTube’s quoted “Little girl, get up” and Facebook’s “which means ‘Little girl, get up’” are modern glosses. The KJV interpretation is “Damsel, I say unto thee, arise.” Because both descriptions end with “Scripture quoted from the King James Version,” explicitly identify the modern wording as a paraphrase. The actual quoted KJV excerpts otherwise show no altered words; this is an attribution problem, not an altered-verse finding. [Mark 5:41, KJV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Mark+5%3A41&version=KJV)

- **YouTube tags — “mark 5 explained” promises more than the film delivers.** This is a 54-second retelling of selected verses, with a brief application. It neither explains the chapter nor addresses its other episodes. That keyword targets a broader explanatory intent than the supplied film satisfies.

- **TikTok — the opening spends its strongest position on generic setup.** “A father begs Jesus to save his dying girl” delays the distinctive conflict until “the news comes: she is dead.” The caption also never names Jairus in ordinary text; his identity appears only in a hashtag. That weakens both the opening’s specificity and its natural search wording.

- **YouTube and Facebook footers — redundant follow instructions.** “Subscribe to Awakeden” / “Follow Awakeden” is immediately followed by another “Follow:” block. Facebook then supplies only a YouTube destination, leaving the second instruction’s platform unclear. The URLs are intact as written, but the footer needs one unambiguous action and destination.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Remove the implied promise that a viewer’s hopeless circumstances will reverse through belief.
2. Reduce Instagram to five hashtags and clearly label modern Scripture paraphrases.
3. Clarify the titles’ direction of travel and remove “mark 5 explained.”
