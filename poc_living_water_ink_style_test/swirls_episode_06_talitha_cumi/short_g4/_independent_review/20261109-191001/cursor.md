## Independent adversarial review

### Doctrinal soundness
Orthodox on the core move: human “too late” is not Jesus’s stopping point; he continues and raises the child. No contrarian reading, no invented theology.

**Skepticism:** The application line **“If you have been told it is too late, He has heard that too”** does not carry **“only believe”** or **“arise”** into the hearer’s lane. A listener can hear outcome comfort (“he’s still in the story when others quit”) without faith in Christ—the same weakness the messengers had until Jesus spoke. That is not heresy, but it under-delivers the series spine (“lands on Jesus”) and edges toward **situation comfort** rather than **Christ-anchored invitation**.

You also compress out Mark’s **“the damsel is not dead, but sleepeth”** (5:39) and the mourners’ scorn. For this short that may be intentional, but anyone who knows the passage may feel you flattened “dead” vs “sleepeth”; for a novice, “dead” in the quote and “get up” is still coherent.

**Voice/attribution flag (not a word change):** In KJV, **“Damsel, I say unto thee, arise”** is Jesus’s speech (interpretation of *Talitha cumi*). Here **JESUS** only gets the Aramaic; the English command is **NARRATOR** in quotation marks. Words are KJV; **routing is wrong** for multi-voice discipline and blurs who is speaking the resurrection word.

Non-quoted lines (**“Little girl, get up.”**, **“Too late. Why bother Him now?”**) are gloss/paraphrase—appropriate.

### Clarity on first hearing
Strengths: plain setup (**“my little girl is dying”**), blunt messenger line, gloss after Aramaic.

**Problems:**
- **“Jesus heard it.”** appears twice without saying *what* he did with it the first time (only the second pass pays off with **“kept walking”**). First use feels like a teaser that repeats instead of advancing.
- **“He did not turn back”** is dramatic but not in the quoted KJV chunk; a careful listener may wonder if that is scripture or embellishment (it is faithful inference, but audio-only it lands as authorial certainty).

### Grace-anchored conviction
**“Be not afraid, only believe”** is scripture’s own grace-shaped word—good.

No overt fear-selling or works. Mild risk: **“If you have been told it is too late…”** can be taken as **“your bad news doesn’t disqualify you from a miracle”** (hope framed as circumstance reversal) unless you tie it to **believe**, not to guaranteed reversal. The father’s arc is belief amid report, not “try harder.”

### Freshness = faithful depth
The hinge—messengers declare finality; Jesus has already heard and does not adopt their verdict—is text-grounded (5:35–36, onward). Not clickbait.

Freshness is somewhat **safe** because you skip the sharper textual irony (**sleepeth** vs wailers). Still faithful.

### One thread spine
Single thread holds: **bad news → Jesus hears → does not stop → hand → arise → “over” vs “kept walking” → your “too late.”** No thread swap.

### Hook score: **8/10**
Immediate stakes, direct speech, no template **“Did you know / Imagine / What if.”** A stranger can grasp the scene in the first seconds. Not a 9–10 only because it opens generic (**“A father”**) without Jairus/synagogue color that could sharpen curiosity.

### Landing score: **5/10**
**“If you have been told it is too late, He has heard that too”** is warm and on-theme, but:
- It could close **many** crisis-short narrations (illness, debt, broken relationship)—not locked to **arise**, **believe**, **hand**, or **Talitha**.
- It does not complete the CTA arc your engine expects: invite toward **Christ/belief**, not only **“He heard your bad report too.”**
- It never echoes Jesus’s own landing verb from *this* piece (**believe** / **arise**).

Per your rubric: **under 8 on landing ⇒ REVISE.**

### Additional findings
- **Prose voice for KJV Jesus line** (see multi-voice above).
- **No passage anchor** in the script (e.g. Mark 5)—optional for shorts, but zero-Bible listeners get no hook to “look it up.”
- Very **compressed** for ~60s; rhythm may feel rushed unless audio pauses carry the weight after **“arise.”**

TOP FIXES:  
1. Rebuild the landing from **this** story’s verbs—e.g. braid **“only believe”** and/or **“arise”** into the final invitation so the closer cannot fit arbitrary “too late” pieces and actually lands on Christ-facing faith.  
2. Move **“Damsel, I say unto thee, arise.”** to **JESUS** (keep **“Little girl, get up”** as narrator gloss if needed).  
3. Pay off **“Jesus heard it”** once with a clear beat (heard the word → spoke **“Be not afraid, only believe”** → kept going); cut the duplicate or make the second line clearly climactic (**heard your ‘too late’ and kept walking toward you**), and avoid implying guaranteed reversal without **believe**.
