# Independent review — claude (OK, 35s)

**Review: Jairus's daughter narration (Mark 5)**

**KJV verbatim.** I found no altered words inside quotation marks.
- "Thy daughter is dead: why troublest thou the Master any further?" matches Mark 5:35.
- "Be not afraid, only believe." matches Mark 5:36.
- "Talitha cumi" and "Damsel, I say unto thee, arise" match Mark 5:41. The piece drops the "which is, being interpreted" link, and its plain-English gloss "Little girl, get up" replaces it. That is acceptable.

**Doctrine.** The retelling is faithful and I found no invented doctrine. It leaves out the mourners being put out and the three disciples, which is acceptable compression. The weak point is the final line: "If you have been told it is too late, He has heard that too."
- It turns the story into a general promise. A grieving viewer whose child did not get up could hear "He heard, so He'll reverse it."
- The piece never says what Jesus's authority over death points to. The whole-Bible-through-Jesus spine needs the cross or the resurrection, and that is missing. The landing is comfort only.

**Clarity.** It is easy to follow. Two things to tighten:
- "Jesus heard it" appears three times. The first use is slightly ambiguous: a newcomer may think Jesus simply overheard.
- "Kept walking" in the last paragraph is an image the text barely supports. The earlier line "He did not turn back" is the stronger one.

**Grace-anchored.** There is no fear or gain-loss framing. "Be not afraid" is used well. The landing is gentle but it is not an invitation. It is a statement with no verb for the viewer to act on, so the script neither convicts nor invites.

**Thread spine.** "Heard it / too late" runs cleanly from hook to close. That is a real strength.

**Hook: 6/10.** "A father begs Jesus: my little girl is dying" is a story-open with real stakes, but it is not a scroll-stopper. The best line is "Too late. Why bother Him now?", and it comes after the opening.

**Landing: 7/10.** It is built from the piece's own thread, "heard it / too late". But the shape "If you have been told X, He has Y" could end many Jesus pieces, and it never reaches the cross or the resurrection. The "heard that too" payoff is also thin, because Jesus *acting* is what the story turns on, not Jesus hearing. Both scores are under 8.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Rebuild the landing around the piece's own image. Jesus heard "too late" and answered with a hand and a word, so the close should land on Him doing that. Tie it to the cross or the resurrection ("the One who raised her was later raised Himself") so it lands on Jesus, not just comfort. Add a verb that invites the viewer, not a bare statement.
2. Open on the sharpest line. Start with "Too late." or with the messenger's "Thy daughter is dead" as the cold open, then show the father. This moves the hook from about 6 to 8 or higher.
3. Remove the pastoral risk in "He has heard that too." Make clear it is Jesus's presence and authority being offered, not a promise that every "too late" is undone. Cut the extra "Jesus heard it" repeats, and replace "kept walking" with the text-backed "He did not turn back."
