Here is the adversarial review of the narration artifact based on your criteria.

**Doctrinal soundness:** 
Pass. The narrative faithfully tracks the account of Jairus's daughter in Mark 5 without inventing doctrine or twisting the text for clickbait.

**KJV verbatim:** 
Pass. All quotes are perfectly exact to Mark 5:35, 36, and 41 ("Thy daughter is dead: why troublest thou the Master any further?", "Be not afraid, only believe", "Talitha cumi", "Damsel, I say unto thee, arise"). 

**Clarity on first hearing:** 
Mostly clear, but the script stumbles over a redundant double-translation. Right after the narrator reads the KJV translation ("Damsel, I say unto thee, arise"), it immediately adds, "Little girl, get up." This is repetitive, breaks the pacing, and dilutes the punch of the KJV line.

**Grace-anchored conviction:** 
Fails on structure. While the tone is gracious, there is no actual conviction or invitation here—just a comforting observation ("He has heard that too"). 

**Freshness:** 
Pass. The thematic focus on "Jesus heard it [the bad news], and kept walking" is a powerful, fresh distillation of the passage's underlying truth. 

**One thread spine:** 
Pass. The thread is incredibly tight: the news of "too late" -> Jesus ignoring the "too late" -> walking into the room -> the application to the listener's own "too late". 

**SCORE THE HOOK: 6** 
"A father begs Jesus: my little girl is dying" is a flat, summary-style opening. It feels like a Sunday-school recap and wastes the crucial first 3 seconds. To stop a scroller, you need to open directly on the friction—the interruption, the devastating news, or the concept of being "too late"—before explaining who the father is.

**SCORE THE LANDING: 5** 
There is no Call to Action (CTA). "If you have been told it is too late, He has heard that too" is an empathetic cliffhanger, not an invitation. A true CTA must invite the viewer to turn to Christ. It needs to pivot from this observation into a verb rooted in the piece's own imagery (e.g., "Don't tell Him it's over—let Him step into the room"). 

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Write a real CTA for the landing. "He has heard that too" is an observation; you must add an inviting verb that connects the "walking/room" imagery to the viewer trusting Jesus with their own "too late."
2. Rewrite the hook to be immediate and arresting. Drop the "A father begs Jesus..." summary and start right at the arrival of the devastating news.
3. Delete the redundant modern paraphrase ("Little girl, get up") so the KJV spoken word ("Damsel, I say unto thee, arise") hits with full impact.
