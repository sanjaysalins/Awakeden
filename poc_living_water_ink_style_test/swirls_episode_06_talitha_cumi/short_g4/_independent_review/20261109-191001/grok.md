# Independent review — grok (OK, 196s)

Hook: 8. Landing: 5. This is a revise. The quoted lines match Mark 5. The close lets the story go.

**Hook: 8.** "A father begs Jesus: my little girl is dying." A stranger gets a dying child in the first breath. Concrete stake, before the old wording "troublest thou" arrives.

**Landing: 5.** "If you have been told it is too late, He has heard that too." That exact sentence could end Lazarus, or any "bad report" short. The girl, the hand, and "arise" are gone. Stock closer. Cap is 5.

**KJV.** These match Mark 5 word for word: "Thy daughter is dead: why troublest thou the Master any further?" / "Be not afraid, only believe." / "Talitha cumi." / "Damsel, I say unto thee, arise." No altered word. "my little girl is dying" is outside the quotes. On audio it still sounds like the father's line. It is a fair gloss of "at the point of death," not a fake verse.

**Doctrine.** The room, the father and mother, the hand, and "She got up and walked" match Mark 5. The close shrinks the text. In this story, "Jesus heard it" means He refused the death-news and said arise. "He has heard that too" only says the listener has been noticed. Set next to "only believe," a first hearer can also take a second message home: believe, and your own dead case will get up. The line does not say that. It leaves the equation in the room.

**Clarity.** "She got up and walked. ... Jesus heard it, and kept walking." She is already up. His walk was on the road, before the room. The story rewinds with no signal. Two people "walk" in adjacent lines. Easy to mix.

"Talitha cumi." then "Damsel, I say unto thee, arise." then "Little girl, get up." Three speeches, no "that means." A newcomer can miss that the last two are one translation.

"Too late. Why bother Him now?" is a clear gloss. Without it, "troublest thou" would be fog. That part holds.

**Grace.** The close comforts. It does not use fear, loss, or a works ladder. "Be not afraid, only believe" stays Jesus's words to the father. The invite never picks up this story's own verb.

**Spine.** Hook, road, and room stay on one thread: the news said stop, He did not stop. The last sentence drops "arise" and "kept walking," and ends on "heard." Same topic, smaller claim.

**Freshness.** "why troublest thou the Master any further?" is a real Mark 5 surprise, and the reading is orthodox. The landing lets go of that surprise.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Rewrite the last sentence from this story's own act — the death-news He would not obey, and "arise" / "Little girl, get up." Cut the portable "He has heard that too," and do not promise the hearer the same miracle.
2. Put "kept walking" on the road, before the room, and split it from "She got up and walked" so the two walks and the rewind do not collide.
3. After "Talitha cumi," say in one step that it means "Little girl, get up." Do not stack Aramaic, KJV, and plain English as three speeches.
