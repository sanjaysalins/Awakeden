# Seat: Opus (blind) - Talitha Cumi short

Structure: the messenger's question is asked twice. First it means "give up."
At the end, the same question comes back, and the answer is what Jesus did.
"Talitha cumi" is the climax. The last line is made from 5:35's own word "further."

## Version A (~30s)

NARRATOR: A father begged Jesus: save my dying girl. Then word came:
"Thy daughter is dead: why troublest thou the Master any further?"
Too late. Why bother Him?
What do you say to a father who hears that?
Jesus answered the father first.

JESUS: Be not afraid, only believe.

NARRATOR: He took the girl's hand.

JESUS: Talitha cumi.

NARRATOR: "Damsel, I say unto thee, arise." She stood.
Why bother Him any further? He went further.

Word count: 70 (~33s at 2.1 w/s)

## Version B (~40s)

NARRATOR: A father fell at Jesus's feet: my little girl is dying. Then word came:
"Thy daughter is dead: why troublest thou the Master any further?"
Too late. Why bother Him?
What do you say to a father who hears that?
Jesus heard. He answered the father first.

JESUS: Be not afraid, only believe.

NARRATOR: At the house, they laughed at Him. He took the girl by the hand.

JESUS: Talitha cumi.

NARRATOR: "Damsel, I say unto thee, arise." She was twelve. She stood and walked.
Why bother Him any further? He went further.

Word count: 89 (~42s at 2.1 w/s)

## Gap and payoff

- Gap: what do you say to a father who has just been told "too late, why bother Him any further?"
- Payoff: Jesus speaks to the father first ("Be not afraid"), then answers the question with an act; the last line turns the messenger's own word: "He went further."

Notes: "troublest" is glossed as "Why bother Him?" right after the quote. "Damsel" is quoted once only, never the load-bearing word. No outcome promise, no pressure. Facts used: 5:22-23, 35-36, 40-42.
