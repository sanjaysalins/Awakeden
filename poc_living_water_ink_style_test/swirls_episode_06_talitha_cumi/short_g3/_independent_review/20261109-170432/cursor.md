## Independent adversarial review (Mark 5:35–43 / Jairus’s daughter)

### Doctrinal soundness
- **Mostly orthodox**, but the **modern bridge overreaches the text**: *"If someone has told you 'too late,' He heard that too"* turns a **specific raising of a dead child** into a general promise that Jesus overrides every “too late” verdict. That is inviting, but it **silently blurs categories** (timing of healing vs. final death, repentance vs. miraculous reversal). A grieving listener whose loss was not reversed may hear **implied guarantee**, not invitation.
- **Compressed narrative skips Jesus’s own framing** in Mark (damsel *“not dead, but sleepeth”*; putting out the mourners). Not heresy, but you lose the text’s **correction of “dead”**—which is exactly your “too late” theme. Freshness is borrowed from the moment; **depth from the passage is left on the table**.

### KJV verbatim (quoted strings)
Checked against KJV Mark 5:35–36, 5:41:
- *"Thy daughter is dead: why troublest thou the Master any further?"* — **exact**
- *"Be not afraid, only believe."* — **exact**
- *"Talitha cumi."* — **exact** (partial quote; KJV continues with the English interpretation)
- *"Damsel, I say unto thee, arise."* — **exact**

No altered words inside quotation marks.

### Clarity on first hearing
- **Timeline whiplash** after the miracle: *"She got up and walked. 'Too late,' they said. Jesus heard it, and kept walking."* On a cold listen, **“they said” sounds like it happens after she walks**, while *"kept walking"* sounds like **en route**, not beside a raised child. You mean **rhetorical callback** to the earlier messengers; a stranger likely hears **contradiction or scrambled order**.
- **"Jesus heard it"** appears twice without anchoring *who* “it” is the second time—is it the messengers, the implied “too late” over the listener’s life, or both? Clever for insiders; **fuzzy for zero Bible knowledge**.
- **Voice/routing clarity**: *"Damsel, I say unto thee, arise."* is **Jesus’s KJV speech** but sits on **NARRATOR**. Same words, wrong speaker in a multi-voice piece—listeners may think the narrator is commanding the girl.

### Grace-anchored conviction
- **No obvious fear/gain-loss/works bait.** *"Be not afraid, only believe"* is **invitational**, aligned with the scene. Good on this lens.

### Freshness = faithful depth
- **Refrain** (*"Jesus heard it"*) is text-adjacent (Mark 5:36: He heard the word spoken) but **under-developed**: you never show *what belief looked like for Jairus* between “dead” and “arise.” Surprise is **structural echo**, not an overlooked textual detail (sleep/death language, ridicule, hand-taking, etc.).

### One thread spine (hook → middle → CTA)
- Spine is **“too late” → Jesus does not stop → believe**. Mostly coherent.
- **Fracture**: post-resurrection *"kept walking"* drifts from **“did not turn back”** (journey) into **allegory** without a clean stitch, so the metaphor **almost swaps from narrative to direct address** mid-sentence.

### Hook score: **7 / 10**
- Immediate stakes (dying child, brutal quote) — **not** a capped template opener.
- **Not a 8+ stop-scroll**: no sharp hook line; opens as **familiar Bible anecdote cadence**; *"Too late. Why bother Him now?"* does the real grab but **after** the long quote.

### Landing score: **6 / 10**
- The **earned landing for this draft** is likely *"If someone has told you 'too late,' He heard that too"* — but the piece **ends on a repeated Jesus line**, not that sentence.
- *"Be not afraid, only believe."* is **this story’s KJV**, but **could close many faith shorts** (storm, healing, fear passages). **Not** “Come to Jesus” stock, still **portable**—not “in the bones” at **8+**.
- **Bookend repeat** without a final synthesizing line feels **thin** after the listener pivot.

VERDICT: REVISE  
TOP FIXES:  
1. **Fix post-resurrection chronology**: keep *"too late"* clearly **before** the raising (or say explicitly you’re echoing the earlier report) so *"She got up... 'Too late,' they said. Jesus heard it, and kept walking"* doesn’t read as nonsense on first hearing.  
2. **Land on this piece’s verb/image**: close by tying **“too late” + heard + believe** in one plain sentence (or let the last spoken line be the application), instead of **only** re-quoting *"Be not afraid, only believe."*  
3. **Route KJV speech correctly** (*"Damsel, I say unto thee, arise."* → **JESUS**) and **tighten pastoral scope** so “too late” invites faith in Christ without sounding like a **blanket reversal** of every irreversible “too late” in the listener’s life—optionally lean on Mark’s **“not dead, but sleepeth”** to stay text-faithful.
