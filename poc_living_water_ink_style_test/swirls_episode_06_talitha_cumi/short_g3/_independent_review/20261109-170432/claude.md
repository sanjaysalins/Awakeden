# Independent review — claude (OK, 39s)

**Quote check**
- "Thy daughter is dead: why troublest thou the Master any further?" is exact KJV (Mark 5:35).
- "Be not afraid, only believe." is exact KJV (Mark 5:36).
- "Talitha cumi." and "Damsel, I say unto thee, arise." are exact KJV (Mark 5:41).
- **Problem:** `"Too late," they said.` sits in quotation marks, but those words are not in the KJV. The messengers say "Thy daughter is dead." The line puts a paraphrase in quotes right after verbatim quotes, so a listener will take it as scripture. Drop the quotation marks or say "They told him it was too late."
- **Problem:** `If someone has told you "too late,"` uses the same non-KJV phrase in quotes.

**Accuracy**
- "He went into the room with her father and mother" leaves out Peter, James and John (Mark 5:37, 40). This is a harmless omission, but it is selective.
- "kept walking" and "He did not turn back" are fair readings of Mark 5:36–37.
- "Jesus heard it" matches "as soon as Jesus heard the word" (Mark 5:36). It is a good thread.

**Doctrine and grace**
- There is no fear or gain-loss framing, and no invented doctrine.
- **Risk:** "If someone has told you 'too late,' He heard that too" can be heard as "Jesus will fix your too-late situation, as he did hers." That is a prosperity-style promise the text doesn't make. The piece never ties the girl's rising to Christ's own resurrection or his authority over death. Without that tie, it is a comforting moral about hard situations, not a gospel landing.
- Ending on "only believe" as a bare imperative, with no object, leans toward faith-as-condition. It is not a clear invitation.

**Clarity**
- The messenger's "Thy daughter is dead" has no speaker. A newcomer may not know who says it.
- "Too late. Why bother Him now?" is narrator paraphrase, but it reads as if it could be Jesus or the father.
- The word "Damsel" is explained right away by "Little girl, get up," which works. "Talitha cumi" appears first with no gloss, but the next line covers it.

**Thread spine**
- The thread "Jesus heard it" runs through the hook, the middle and the CTA, and it is clean. This is the strongest part.

**Hook: 6/10**
- "A father begs Jesus: my little girl is dying." is a clear stake, but it is a generic story-open.
- The surprising turn, "Jesus heard it" (he overheard "too late" and ignored it), comes only at the end of the first paragraph. It is the actual hook and is buried.
- Moving "Too late." or "Jesus heard it" to the front would lift it.

**Landing: 7/10**
- "He heard that too" and "kept walking" come from this piece's own thread, which is good.
- The closing repeat of "Be not afraid, only believe" is the weaker part. It is an echo, not a new landing, and it does not reach Jesus' own death and resurrection.
- Could the final sentence end a different piece? Partly yes, since it is a stock comfort verse. The "He heard that too" line before it is the unique one. Ending on that line, or on a line that ties to "arise," would be stronger.

Both scores are under 8.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Remove the quotation marks around the non-KJV "Too late" in both places (`"Too late," they said.` and `told you "too late,"`). Make them plain narrator paraphrase so every quoted word is exact KJV.
2. Lead with the surprise to lift the hook above 8. For example, open on "Too late," he was told. Jesus heard it, and kept walking, then fill in the father's plea. Also name the speakers ("messengers from the house").
3. Rebuild the landing so it ties to Christ's authority over death and does not promise a fix for every "too late." For example, finish on "Arise," the word that will one day be spoken over everyone who sleeps in him, or end on "He heard that too" without repeating the verse. Do not leave the bare "only believe" echo as the closer.
