# Independent review — grok (OK, 205s)

The scripture lines that are actually Scripture check out. The landing does not. A stranger can follow the story, and the grace line is Jesus's own comfort. The close still promises too much, and it can be lifted onto another film.

**Hook: 8.** "A father begs Jesus: my little girl is dying." A child is dying in the first breath. That will stop a thumb. No "Did you know" template.

**Landing: 6.** Under 8, so this is a revise. The last line is `JESUS: "Be not afraid, only believe."` Those words are this story's words, and they matter more the second time, after she walks. They can still end a storm film, a healing film, or a fear film. The line that belongs only to this piece is the one before it: "If someone has told you 'too late,' He heard that too." The closer should be that image. "Heard." "Kept walking." "Too late."

**The parallel is too tight.** The film's shape is: they said too late, Jesus heard, the girl got up, so if someone told you too late, He heard that too, only believe. A parent whose child has died can hear a promise the text does not make: believe, and yours will get up and walk. Mark 5 shows Jesus heard one death-sentence and raised one girl. It does not make "only believe" a lever that replays the miracle. "Be not afraid, only believe" was spoken to that father about that girl.

**The clock restarts after the miracle.** "She got up and walked. 'Too late,' they said. Jesus heard it, and kept walking." On a first hearing, "kept walking" sounds like a new event after she is alive. The listener has to rewind to the road. "He did not turn back" already did this job, before the room.

**"Too late" is put in their mouths.** The messengers' real line is already quoted, and it matches the KJV: "Thy daughter is dead: why troublest thou the Master any further?" Later the script says `"Too late," they said.` They did not say those words. Inside quotation marks, that reads as a line from the text. It is the narrator's gloss. "Talitha cumi," "Damsel, I say unto thee, arise," and "Be not afraid, only believe" match the KJV wording.

**What holds.** One spine runs the whole way: too late, He heard it, He kept going, she arose, your "too late." No fear pitch, no works, no "you will lose this." "Little girl, get up" makes "Talitha cumi" plain for a first-time listener. The fresh point is a real one in the text: Jesus heard the "stop bothering Him" sentence and went on in.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Break the 1:1 promise. "He heard that too" plus "only believe" currently implies the listener's dead situation will stand up and walk. Land on Jesus hearing the "finished" sentence, and trust in Him, without cloning the miracle.
2. Replace the last line. "Be not afraid, only believe" can end a different film. Close on this film's own words: too late, He heard it, He kept walking.
3. Move "Jesus heard it, and kept walking" back before she rises, and drop the false quote "Too late," they said. Keep "too late" as the narrator's plain gloss of the KJV line already spoken.
