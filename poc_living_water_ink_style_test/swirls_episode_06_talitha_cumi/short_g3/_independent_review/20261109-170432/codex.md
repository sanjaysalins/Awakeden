# Independent review — codex (OK, 54s)

**Hook: 7/10.** “A father begs Jesus: my little girl is dying” establishes immediate stakes, but opens as a familiar Bible retelling. The sharper tension—whether death makes approaching Jesus pointless—arrives later.

**Landing: 5/10.** “Be not afraid, only believe” belongs to this passage, but the exact sentence could close many other pieces. It abandons the narration’s distinctive hand/walking imagery and leaves a newcomer unclear about what believing means.

1. **Invented quotation breaks the KJV requirement.** “‘Too late,’ they said” attributes words to the messengers that Mark does not record. The later quoted “too late” also fails your literal requirement that every quoted word be exact KJV. The actual biblical quotations otherwise match KJV wording; no altered words found. [Mark 5:35–43, KJV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Mark+5%3A35-43&version=KJV)

2. **The application suggests more than the passage promises.** “If someone has told you ‘too late,’ He heard that too,” immediately followed by “only believe,” can sound like assurance that Jesus will reverse the listener’s particular loss if they believe. Jesus’ awareness is not the problem; the implied transfer of this miracle’s outcome to any listener’s circumstances is. Anchor the invitation in His character and authority without implying that faith secures the desired reversal.

3. **The ending risks making faith sound like the mechanism.** “Only believe” is faithfully quoted, but its placement as the final instruction can leave the burden on the listener’s believing. The preceding miracle displays Jesus acting and speaking with authority. The application needs to preserve that emphasis rather than leave an apparent condition for obtaining rescue.

4. **The spine weakens at the personal turn.** “Jesus heard it, and kept walking” develops a coherent thread: the death announcement does not stop Him. “He heard that too” reduces that thread to being heard, without explaining what His continued approach means for the listener. The final command then introduces another emphasis.

5. **Faithful depth is underdeveloped.** “Took her by the hand” and “Little girl, get up” contain the piece’s strongest material: Jesus’ tenderness alongside His authority over death. The repeated “too late” slogan occupies the space where that textual depth could shape the landing.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Remove invented quoted speech and enforce exact KJV wording throughout quotation marks.
2. Clarify the application so believing does not imply a guaranteed reversal of the listener’s loss.
3. Build the closing invitation from Jesus taking her hand or continuing toward her, with His action carrying the emphasis.
