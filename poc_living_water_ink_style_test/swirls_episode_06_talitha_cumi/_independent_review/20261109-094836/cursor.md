## Independent adversarial review — Mark 5 (Jairus / Talitha cumi)

### KJV verbatim (quoted speech only)

- **"Thy daughter is dead: why troublest thou the Master any further?"** — Matches Mark 5:35 KJV.
- **"Be not afraid, only believe."** — Matches Mark 5:36 KJV.
- **"Talitha cumi."** — Matches Mark 5:41 (transliteration; correct to leave un-Anglicized).
- **"Damsel, I say unto thee, arise."** — Matches Mark 5:41 KJV gloss.

No altered words inside quotation marks.  
**Note:** *"Jesus says she is only sleeping"* is a paraphrase of Mark 5:39 (*"The damsel is not dead, but sleepeth"*), not in quotes — acceptable, but if you ever quote that beat, the KJV wording must be used.

---

### Doctrinal soundness

- The piece stays inside the narrative: death announced, Jesus speaks, child rises, meal — no invented history or contrarian reading.
- **Risk:** **"Jesus still had the last word"** generalizes a **single narrative miracle** into a universal slogan. A skeptical listener can hear *“Jesus wins every time I’m in crisis”* without the text’s actual claim (Messiah’s authority over death in this case) or the broader gospel (resurrection hope through Christ). That is not heresy, but it is **under-explained** and slightly **prosperity-of-outcome flavored** if taken as a life principle.
- **"Be not afraid, only believe"** is faithful as Jesus’s word to Jairus; the script does not twist it into works-righteousness, but it also does not **anchor “believe”** in who Jesus is — so “believe” floats as bare volition.

---

### Clarity on first hearing

- **Strong:** Aramaic → meaning arc; Jairus, delay, house, hand, arise, meal are followable with no Bible background.
- **Weak beat:** **"Too late."** alone sounds like the **narrator’s verdict**, not the messengers’. A first-time listener may think the story is over or that Scripture agrees death has the final say until the next line rescues it. One word, high ambiguity.
- **Middle density:** Touch-of-the-hem pause is necessary to Mark’s structure but is a **lot of plot** before paying off “two words”; zero-knowledge listeners may forget the hook question (**"What do they say?"**) before **"Talitha cumi."**

---

### Grace-anchored conviction

- No explicit fear–punishment salvation pitch, no gain/loss manipulation, no “do better” works landing.
- **"Too late."** plus twice **"worst news"** is dramatic, not a terror CTA — acceptable for storytelling.
- Jesus’s line is invitation-shaped (**"only believe"**), but the **closing does not invite** — it **summarizes**. Spirit-convicts / script-invites is only half delivered.

---

### Freshness = faithful depth

- **Fresh and text-grounded:** **"Mark, who wrote this book in Greek, kept them in Jesus's own language: Talitha cumi"** — legitimate detail from Mark’s editorial habit, not gimmick exegesis.
- **"A hand. 'Arise.' Then a meal."** — Concrete, from the text (hand, arise, eat), not a generic devotional moral.

---

### One thread (hook → middle → CTA)

- **Spine:** death’s word vs Jesus’s word; hook promises the two Aramaic words; middle delivers them; tagline returns to **"worst news"** vs Jesus.
- **Fray:** Hook asks **"What do they say?"** and Jesus commands **"only believe,"** but the landing keys on **"the last word,"** not *believe*, not *arise*, not the Aramaic. Same story, slightly **different closing verb** than the mid-piece command that is supposed to carry conviction.

---

### Hook score: **8/10**

Opens on death and a mystery (two words, foreign phrase, **"What do they say?"**). Not a template “Did you know / imagine” opener. Loses points because the Greek/Mark line is light homework in the first breath before the emotional punch lands.

---

### Landing score: **6/10**

**"Jesus still had the last word"** is tied to Jairus’s arc, but it could close **any** “bad news then Jesus acts” short (Lazarus, storm, cross, empty tomb) without changing much — not a stock *"come to Jesus"* closer, but still **exchangeable**.  
For this project’s **CTA-to-Jesus** bar: there is **no second-person invitation** and no landing built from **this piece’s** commanded verb (**believe** / **arise**). Poetic yes; “in the bones” and non-portable — **no**. Under 8 → **REVISE** per your rubric.

---

### Additional nits

- **"In plain words: your daughter is dead. Why bother the Teacher now?"** — Fine for accessibility; slightly redundantly deflates the power of the already-plain KJV quote you just read.
- **"give her something to eat"** — Unquoted; aligns with Mark 5:43 sense (KJV: *"commanded that something should be given her to eat"*). Not a violation; cite KJV if you quote it later.
