# Independent review — codex (OK, 100s)

**Hook: 7/10. Landing: 4/10.**

1. **The ending supplies no CTA.** “Jesus still had the last word” concludes the story without inviting any response. That exact sentence could end pieces about Lazarus, the storm, or the resurrection. It therefore meets your stock-closer test and falls below the required score.

2. **The hook promises a language discovery; the payoff provides a translation.** “Mark … kept them in Jesus’s own language: Talitha cumi. What do they say?” makes the preserved words the central curiosity. “Little girl, get up” answers that question, but the narration never develops why those ordinary words matter in this extraordinary scene. The ingredients for faithful depth—His command, her walking, His provision of food—are listed rather than connected.

3. **The chronology reverses without an audible signal.** “His little girl is dead” is followed by “His little girl is dying.” A knowledgeable listener recognizes the rewind; a newcomer must infer it. This creates avoidable confusion immediately after the hook.

4. **Two physical details are supplied beyond Mark’s account.** “He turns to the father” adds a movement where Mark records Jesus speaking to him. “Then a meal” turns a command to provide food into an apparently completed event. Neither invents doctrine, but both exceed what the passage explicitly narrates. [Mark 5:35–43, KJV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Mark+5%3A35-43&version=KJV)

5. **“Too late” has no identified speaker or viewpoint.** As a standalone narrator declaration, it briefly asserts the conclusion the miracle overturns. The intended meaning—too late according to the messengers—is inferable, but should not require inference in a first-hearing script.

**KJV check:** No altered words in the quotations. The isolated “Arise” capitalizes the KJV’s lowercase verb; that is a capitalization difference, not a substituted word. [Mark 5:35–41, KJV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Mark+5%3A35-41&version=KJV)

**Doctrine and grace:** No substantive doctrinal error or coercive fear, gain-loss, or works framing identified. “Only believe” remains addressed to Jairus; the script does not explicitly turn it into a universal promise of immediate healing. The missing element is a grace-anchored invitation built from this particular scene.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Build a specific invitation from the scene’s command, hand, or provision; replace the portable closing slogan.
2. Connect the preserved words to the miracle’s meaning so the hook delivers faithful depth.
3. Signal the chronological rewind and remove unsupported actions and ambiguous narrator assertions.
