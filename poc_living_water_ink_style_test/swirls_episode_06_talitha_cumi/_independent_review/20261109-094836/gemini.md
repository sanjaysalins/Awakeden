# Independent review — gemini (OK, 95s)

Here is my critical review of the narration:

- **Doctrinal soundness**: The historical events are biblically accurate, but the piece structurally fails its evangelical purpose. It operates strictly as a story recap and completely lacks a Gospel application directed at the listener. 
- **KJV verbatim**: PASS. "Thy daughter is dead: why troublest thou the Master any further?", "Be not afraid, only believe.", "Talitha cumi", and "Damsel, I say unto thee, arise" all match the KJV text precisely.
- **Clarity on first hearing**: FAIL. The narrative timeline is disjointed and confusing. In the very first line, you state: "his little girl is dead." In the second paragraph, you jump backward in time: "His little girl is dying." By paragraph three, she dies again: "Thy daughter is dead". A zero-knowledge listener will be immediately lost. Furthermore, the hook asks a direct question ("What do they say?") and then abandons it for five paragraphs to summarize unrelated backstory.
- **Grace-anchored conviction**: FAIL. There is no conviction and no invitation in this script. It asks nothing of the viewer.
- **Freshness = faithful depth**: Highlighting that Mark preserved the original Aramaic ("Talitha cumi") is a great textual detail, but it remains mere biblical trivia because there is no listener-directed payoff connecting the intimacy of Christ's original language to the viewer.
- **One thread spine from hook -> middle -> CTA**: The spine is completely broken in paragraph two. You establish a thread about the "two words," but then derail the script to mention the bleeding woman ("A woman touches His clothes... Jairus has to wait") which serves no ultimate purpose to your hook or your landing. 
- **SCORE THE HOOK 1-10**: 5. "A father hears the worst news" is an active narrative open, but teasing "What do they say?" and immediately refusing to answer it to tell clunky, out-of-order backstory completely destroys the hook's momentum.
- **SCORE THE LANDING 1-10**: 2. There is no CTA. "Jairus had heard the worst news. Jesus still had the last word" is a neat poetic recap of the history, but it is not a Call to Action. It invites the listener to zero personal inward reflection and points them to no encounter with Christ for themselves.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Write a real listener-directed CTA-to-Jesus. Connect the intimacy and authority of Jesus's Aramaic words to the listener's own life/death state, replacing the current "story summary" ending.
2. Fix the fractured timeline. Do not declare the girl dead in the hook, rewind to her "dying" in paragraph two, and then kill her again in paragraph three. 
3. Delete the bleeding woman subplot ("A crowd is pushing close. A woman touches His clothes..."). It breaks the spine and wastes precious word-count that should be spent on the CTA.
