# Independent review — grok (OK, 204s)

The quoted KJV lines check out. The piece still leaves a first-time listener unsure whether the girl was dead, and it closes on a line that could end a different story.

**Hook: 8.** “A father hears the worst news: his little girl is dead.” A stranger can stop for that. It is not a “Did you know / Imagine / What if” open.

**Landing: 5.** The cap applies. The last sentence is “Jesus still had the last word.” That same sentence could end Lazarus, the storm, or the widow of Nain. The images that belong only to this story — the hand, “Arise,” the meal — are a list in front of a stock closer. They do not land in the bones.

**Dead, then dying.** The hook says the girl is dead. The next beat says, “His little girl is dying.” Then she is dead again. On one hearing, the clock runs backward. “This book” also has no name. A listener who does not know Mark does not know which book.

**“The girl is dead. Jesus says she is only sleeping, and they laugh at Him.”** The narrator states both and explains neither. In Mark 5:39 he says she “sleepeth.” Luke 8:55 says her spirit came again, so she was dead. The plain evangelical reading is that Jesus calls death sleep because he is about to wake her. As written, a newcomer can leave thinking the mourners were wrong and she was only asleep. Then “She gets up and walks” is not a raising. That fights the hook, the line “Too late,” and the whole point of “Talitha cumi.”

**“Too late.”** This is the narrator’s own voice, not the men from the house. For a moment the story itself declares the case closed.

**The woman is a dropped thread.** “A woman touches His clothes, and Jesus stops. Jairus has to wait.” Nothing says who she is or what happened to her. The spine is the girl and the two words. She opens a second story and vanishes.

**The meal is the depth, and it is wasted.** “And Jesus says: give her something to eat” is the text’s proof she is really alive. A body that eats is not a ghost and not a nap. “Then a meal” names it and does not say that.

**Grace.** No fear pitch, no “believe and you will get this too,” no works. “Be not afraid, only believe” stays Jesus’s line to Jairus. Keep it that way.

**KJV.** These match: “Thy daughter is dead: why troublest thou the Master any further?” “Be not afraid, only believe.” “Talitha cumi.” “Damsel, I say unto thee, arise.” The later “Arise” is that same KJV word, lifted out and capitalized. The glosses sit outside the quotes. That part is clean.

**Spine.** Hook, wait, death news, and “Little girl, get up” are one thread. The last sentence leaves it for a slogan.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. After “only sleeping,” say in plain words that she really is dead and Jesus calls it sleep because he is about to wake her — then say he raised her.
2. Cut “Jesus still had the last word.” End on his hand, “Arise,” and the meal: she is alive enough to eat. That last line must belong only to this story.
3. Fix the clock: do not say she is dead and then that she is dying. Name the book. Give the woman one plain result, or cut her.
