# Independent review — claude (OK, 49s)

**Review of the Jairus / Talitha cumi narration**

**KJV and doctrine**
- Every quoted phrase is exact KJV. I checked Mark 5:35 ("Thy daughter is dead: why troublest thou the Master any further?"), 5:36 ("Be not afraid, only believe"), and 5:41 ("Damsel, I say unto thee, arise").
- The story details match Mark 5:
  - He puts the mourners out.
  - Only Peter, James, John and the parents go in.
  - She is twelve.
  - "Give her something to eat."
- I found no invented doctrine, no contested reading, and no fear or gain-loss framing.
- The "Talitha cumi" line is voiced by Jesus, which suits the multi-voice rule.
- "Mark... kept them in Jesus's own language" never names Aramaic. That is clear enough for a newcomer, but vague.

**Problems**
1. **The curiosity gap pays off weakly.** The hook asks "What do they say?", and the answer is "Little girl, get up." That is plain and low-stakes. The narration never shows why it matters that Mark kept the original words.
   - Possible payoff: they are a tender, everyday command, said while holding her hand.
   - The piece names this hook thread and then drops it. The middle is a plot retelling, and the landing moves to a different idea ("last word"). The thread is not carried from hook to landing.
2. **The landing is a statement, not a CTA.**
   - "Jesus still had the last word" does not invite the listener to anything. That conflicts with the project's CTA-to-Jesus model.
   - It also could close almost any resurrection or healing story. It is not built from this piece's own image, which is the hand, the two words and the meal.
   - It is factually loose too. The last thing Jesus says in the scene is "give her something to eat," not "arise."
3. **"A hand. 'Arise.' Then a meal." is the best line, and it is unused.** The meal is a strong, fresh detail: He cares that she is really alive and hungry. Nothing in the piece explains it or builds on it.
4. **The woman who touched His clothes is an unexplained detour.** It only sets up "Jairus has to wait." The piece never pays this off with anything like "Jesus is never too late."
5. **Minor points.**
   - "Too late." works well as a stand-alone beat.
   - "Jesus hears it. He turns to the father." skips the nuance of "as soon as Jesus heard," which is fine.
   - The piece has no Christ-lens link outside the story itself.

**Scores**
- **Hook: 6/10.** The opening image ("his little girl is dead") is strong, but the question it ends on ("What do they say?") is a translation puzzle with a mild answer.
- **Landing: 6/10.** It is on theme but generic, with no invitation and nothing tied to the hand or the meal.

Both scores are under 8, so the verdict is REVISE.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Rebuild the landing from the piece's own image, the hand and the meal. For example, name what Jesus did: He took her hand, spoke two ordinary words, then thought of her hunger. Then invite the listener to the same Jesus. Do not use "last word," which is stock, and the scene's last words are not "arise" anyway.
2. Make the hook's question pay off. Say why Mark kept the Aramaic (an eyewitness remembered the exact sound, a tender command said to a child), so "Little girl, get up" feels like a reveal. Then tie the landing back to those words so the thread runs hook → middle → close.
3. Trim or justify the woman-who-touched-Him detour. Either connect "Jairus has to wait" to "Too late" as a theme (Jesus is not late) or cut it, so the middle stays on one spine.
