# Independent review — codex (OK, 75s)

**Hook: 5/10.** “A man named Mark wrote a book about Jesus, in Greek” spends the opening seconds on background information. The emotional stakes arrive later. “Why do these two matter?” asks a stranger to care about unfamiliar words before giving them a reason.

**Landing: 6/10.** “If it got worse while you waited, Jesus heard ‘too late’ and still went in” recalls this story’s language, so it avoids a wholly stock closer. But it supplies reassurance without a clear invitation, and its application reaches beyond what the passage establishes.

1. **The ending risks promising a reversal of the listener’s circumstances.** “If it got worse while you waited” transfers Jairus’s experience directly onto any listener’s worsening situation. Following a resurrection and a meal, “still went in” can sound like assurance that Jesus will similarly intervene and restore what they lost. That implication needs correction. The problem is an unsupported promise, not an explicit statement of false doctrine.

2. **The hook’s question is never actually answered.** “Here is why those two words matter. They were spoken into a room full of ‘too late’” explains the significance of Jesus’s command in this situation. It does **not** explain why Mark’s preservation of the original-language words matters—the specific curiosity established by “in Greek. But here, he kept the sound.” The spine shifts from preserved language to apparent lateness. The same explanation would work if the opening never mentioned Greek.

3. **The quotation marks violate your literal KJV requirement.** Every occurrence of **“too late”** is invented summary language, not wording from this passage. Putting it in quotation marks breaches the stated rule and makes “Jairus heard ‘too late’” resemble reported dialogue. The quotations beginning “Thy daughter is dead,” “Be not afraid,” “Talitha cumi,” and “Damsel” contain the exact KJV words. “Arise” preserves the word but changes its capitalization. [KJV Mark 5:35–43](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Mark+5%3A35-43&version=KJV)

4. **“Jesus says she is only sleeping” leaves a novice-facing ambiguity unresolved.** A listener could understand the recovery as proof that the death report was mistaken. The narration needs to make clear that Jesus raises a dead child; the sleeping language expresses His authority over death. Otherwise the central miracle can disappear on first hearing.

5. **The closing motivation rests on hoped-for improvement.** There is no explicit works requirement or fear pressure. However, “If it got worse while you waited” makes the listener’s desired outcome the point of connection. The invitation needs to arise from Jesus’s demonstrated authority and tender care without making restored circumstances the reason to trust Him.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Correct the ending’s implied promise of personal reversal and give it a clear, text-specific invitation.
2. Resolve the mismatch between the preserved-language hook and the “too late” payoff.
3. Remove quotation marks from invented wording and clarify that the girl was truly dead.
