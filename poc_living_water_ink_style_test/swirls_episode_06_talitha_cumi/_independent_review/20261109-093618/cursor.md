## Independent adversarial review — Talitha cumi (Mark 5) narration

### Doctrinal soundness
- **Mostly grounded in Mark 5:22–43.** Aramaic preserved in Greek Mark (`Talitha cumi` + interpretation) matches the text; no contrarian reading.
- **Application risk:** *"If it got worse while you waited, Jesus heard 'too late' and still went in."* Sounds like a **general promise** that Jesus will still “go in” when your situation worsened during a divine delay. The story is **one narrative resolution** (raising the girl), not a blanket guarantee for every “too late” listener. That edge is **implied prosperity / outcome assurance**, not full-orbed gospel (cross, sin, resurrection hope beyond this miracle).
- **"Jesus had the last word"** over death in this room is fine for the episode; don’t let the closing line widen that into “your dead situation gets fixed too” without the text’s limits.

### Clarity on first hearing
- **Large skip:** *"Someone touches Jesus, and Jesus stops. Jairus has to wait."* never names **why** the stop matters (the woman with the issue of blood, **12 years** vs the girl’s **12 years** is invisible). A stranger hears “wait,” then death news, but **not** that the wait was because Jesus was healing someone else while Jairus’s crisis clock ran.
- **Payoff depends on missing setup:** *"If it got worse while you waited"* only lands for people who **already know** the sandwiched miracle. On first hearing it can feel like **imported knowledge** or a vague “life got worse” line, not this pericope.
- **"Too late."** (standalone) is **narrator compression**, not Scripture. Clear emotionally, but it **blurs** who said what: messengers said *dead*; “too late” is your label—fine as rhetoric, weak for zero-Bible listeners tracing facts.
- **"They laugh at Him"** (v. 39–40) is accurate in gist; no doctrinal confusion there.

### Grace-anchored conviction
- No obvious fear/gain-loss sell. *"Be not afraid, only believe"* is **Jesus’s** line, not narrator guilt.
- **Gap:** The scripture **commands belief**; the **close never invites belief**—only that Jesus *"still went in."* Conviction is **implied comfort**, not the text’s explicit invite (*believe*), so it’s soft, not manipulative, but **under-powered** for a gospel-short CTA model.

### Freshness = faithful depth
- **Fresh hook angle:** Mark’s Greek narrative **keeping** Jesus’s Aramaic—is legitimate textual color, not clickbait.
- **"Too late" → hand → arise → meal** is a **faithful** narrative braid; meal detail (v. 43) is good, underused in the close.

### One thread spine (hook → middle → CTA)
- **Spine A (chosen):** Talitha / language / “too late” / Jesus’s word / arise / meal—strong in *"Here is why those two words matter…"*
- **Spine B (dropped):** *"Be not afraid, only believe"* is the **turn** after bad news (v. 36) but **vanishes** before the landing.
- **Close verb mismatch:** Middle crystallizes *"A hand. 'Arise.' Then a meal."* Landing pivots to **"still went in"** (motion into the house), not **arise**, **believe**, or **meal**—so the CTA **does not cash the image** the recap just minted. That’s **thread slack**, not full swap.

### Hook score: **5 / 10**
- **"A man named Mark wrote a book about Jesus, in Greek."** reads **classroom**, not scroll-stop; stranger may bounce before *"Two words: Talitha cumi."*
- Not a capped template (*Did you know / Imagine*), but **low tension, no stakes** in the first ~5 seconds—name, language, craft. **Caps ~5** on your rubric for weak open velocity.

### Landing score: **6 / 10**
- **Not** a stock *"Come to Jesus / turn to Him"* closer—**somewhat** tied to “too late” and waiting.
- Still **could almost fit** other “Jesus shows up late / don’t give up” pieces without Talitha, arise, or meal.
