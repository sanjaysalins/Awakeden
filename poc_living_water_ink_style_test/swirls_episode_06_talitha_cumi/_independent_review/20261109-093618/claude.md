# Independent review — claude (OK, 44s)

**Hook: 5/10.** "Two words: Talitha cumi. Why do these two matter?" is a rhetorical-question template. It's also a bare linguistic hook ("Mark kept the Aramaic") that a stranger has no stake in. "In Greek" and "Jesus's own language" never name Aramaic, so a newcomer can't picture it. The curiosity gap opens on Mark's translation habits, but the payoff is about "too late". The hook and the payoff are about two different things.

**Landing: 5/10.** "If it got worse while you waited, Jesus heard 'too late' and still went in." has three problems:
- It is a conditional comfort line, not a landing. There is no invitation, no verb for the listener, and no "arise" or "hand" echo, even though the piece sets up that exact image. Hand, arise and meal were the piece's own material and went unused.
- It could end a different piece about waiting or delay. It does not need Jairus's girl or Talitha cumi.
- "Jesus heard 'too late' and still went in" slides toward a promise that Jesus will always act in time or reverse the situation. That is a mild doctrinal overreach (prosperity-adjacent), and the story only supports that Jesus came to this house.

**KJV check:** The quoted strings are exact KJV: "Thy daughter is dead: why troublest thou the Master any further?", "Be not afraid, only believe." and "Damsel, I say unto thee, arise." Nothing is altered. One minor issue: "Talitha cumi" appears as a bare quote and is then glossed correctly.

**Clarity:**
- "Someone touches Jesus, and Jesus stops. Jairus has to wait." The woman with the bleeding is cut out entirely. A newcomer gets a mystery toucher and no reason the delay matters. Either name her in one clause or cut the beat.
- "Too late" is not in the text. The phrase is the narrator's frame, and it later takes on a life of its own. "Jairus heard 'too late.'" reads like a quote. Make it clearly the narrator's summary, or tie it to the KJV words "is dead".
- "Jesus hears it. He turns to the father." is fine. "Jesus had the last word" is a stock phrase, and it sits in a paragraph that also says Jairus heard "too late".
- "Jairus heard 'too late.' Jesus had the last word. A hand. 'Arise.' Then a meal." is staccato and gets close to mannered. The meal beat is lovely but has no explanation (it shows she is really alive and cared for). The piece never says that, so a newcomer won't catch it.
- Beat 2 runs seven short sentences before the stakes land. It is flat on a first listen.

**Thread spine:** The Aramaic hook is dropped by the middle and replaced by "too late". Pick one thread. The "too late" thread is the stronger one, and the piece can open on it.

**Grace and conviction:** The piece avoids fear and pressure. It has no conviction beat and no gospel pivot. Jesus's own death and resurrection is never touched. "The whole Bible through Jesus" is satisfied only thinly (the girl's rising is not tied to Christ as the one who conquers death). The landing speaks only to someone who is "waiting", which is a narrow audience.

**Freshness:** The "too late" reframe is good, since the men's verdict is the one thing Jesus overrides. The Aramaic detail is true and fresh, but the piece does not use it for anything.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Rebuild the landing from the piece's own images (hand, "arise", the meal, or "too late" overruled by "only believe"). Drop the vague conditional. Make it a clear invitation that does not promise Jesus will always reverse the situation.
2. Replace the hook with a concrete stake that matches the payoff. Open on the verdict itself ("Your daughter is dead. Too late.") or on the father. Move the Aramaic detail inside the story, where "Talitha cumi" is Jesus's real voice, and name it once as Aramaic.
3. Fix the clarity gaps. Explain or cut the "someone touches Jesus" beat. Mark "too late" as the narrator's frame, not a quote. Say why the meal matters. Keep one thread (too late, not Aramaic) from hook to landing.
