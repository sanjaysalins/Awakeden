## 1. WHO IS THIS FOR
**Named viewer:** Dana, 30, raised in church but now distant. Dana hides a private failure and expects to be dropped if anyone finds out.
- **Who stops:** anyone who has felt the fear in the 0.0 hook. It is universal.
- **Who finishes:** viewers who want the answer to the 19.7 question.
- **Who shares:** church people who send it to a struggling friend.
- **Not served:** total newcomers. Judas and Peter appear only as name labels (11.3). Peter's denial is set up and never paid off. That is a loose thread for everyone.

## 2. MAIN TAKEAWAY
"Jesus knows the worst about me, and He doesn't leave. He serves." The line that carries it is 66.5, "He was the one holding the towel." It is clear, and the text box at 73.6 locks it in.

## 3. GLOBAL AUDIENCE
**In my words:** At His last supper, Jesus knew one friend would betray Him and another would deny Him. He washed their feet anyway. The traitor walked out into the dark. The one who knew everything stayed. So you are not too known to be loved.

**Where I got lost or misled:**
- **36.6, "Know ye what I have done to you?"** This is archaic. The film never answers the question, so it reads as a riddle left hanging.
- **19.7, "friends like that"** is an idiom.
- **66.5, "the worst about them"** is abstract.
- **39.1, "unto the end"** is hard for second-language viewers. The narrator does gloss it, which helps.
- **0.0, the text box** puts a question mark on a statement ("…they would leave?"). Non-native readers will stumble on it.

## 4. BIBLICALLY ACCURATE
- **5.8, "sat at supper with His closest friends":** RIGHT (13:1 "his own", 13:2 "supper").
- **5.8, "And He knew them":** RIGHT (13:11, 13:18 "I know whom I have chosen").
- **11.3, "Judas, would betray Him":** RIGHT (13:2, 13:11, 13:21–26).
- **11.3, "Peter, would deny Him three times before morning":** RIGHT in substance (13:38, "cock shall not crow"). But John puts this prediction after the washing and after Judas leaves, so the film's order blurs the sequence a little.
- **11–19.6, Judas holding the money bag:** RIGHT (13:29).
- **22.7, rises, towel, water, washes feet:** RIGHT (13:4–5). The laid-aside robe in the picture is also right (13:4).
- **22.7, "A servant's work":** a fair inference from 13:13–16. It is not stated in the text.
- **33.9, "Judas is still in the room":** RIGHT (13:10–11, 13:30). The film wisely does not claim Jesus washed Judas's own feet.
- **36.6, the quote:** RIGHT (13:12). The picture is WRONG: Jesus is shown still washing, but He said this "after he had washed their feet, and had taken his garments, and was set down again."
- **39.1, the quote:** RIGHT (13:1).
- **39.1, "the end was the cross":** OVERCLAIMED. "Unto the end" can also mean "to the uttermost." The cross reading is defensible from 13:1 ("his hour was come… depart out of this world"), but it is stated as the only meaning. "The next day" is right by a modern clock (John 19).
- **48.5, "Then Judas walks out":** right that he left (13:30), but "Then" compresses verses 13:12–29, which include the sop. "And it was night" really is four words in the KJV (13:30). RIGHT.
- **63.4, "Jesus knows. All of it":** supported by 13:3, 13:11 and 13:18. Applying it to the viewer is a sound inference (John 2:24–25).
- **66.3–79.7, final image:** WRONG. Jesus kneels with the wet towel, watching Judas's open door. By 13:30 the washing was over; He was dressed and back at the table (13:12).
- **Is the ending faithful?** Yes, in substance. The knower stayed and served; the one who was known left. Read as "look at that room again" (looking back), the towel is a fair emblem. Only the staging contradicts 13:12.

## 5. BEST VERSION POSSIBLE
The best explainer would get the order right on screen and pay off every thread it opens: Peter, and "Know ye…?"

**The one highest-value change:** make the ending clearly a look back. Change "Then" at 48.5 to "Later". Then 66.5 reads as remembering the towel, not as Jesus still kneeling while Judas leaves. That way the emotional thesis no longer rests on a staging that 13:12 contradicts.

**Score: 7/10.**

## CURIOSITY GAP
It opens at 0.0 and is sharpened at 19.7. It pays off at 66.5 and 73.6. **Score: 8/10.** It is worth the wait. The reversal (Judas leaves, the one who knows stays) is real and grows out of the text itself. It is not a manufactured twist.

## THREE SMALLEST FIXES
1. **48.5:** "Later that night, Judas walks out. And the story adds four small words: 'and it was night.'"
2. **39.1:** "The story says it in one line: 'he loved them unto the end.' All the way. And the next day, that meant the cross."
3. **0.0, text box:** "If people really knew me, / would they leave?" (put the question mark on a real question).
