# Independent review — codex (OK, 34s)

**REVISE.** The main problem is the move from Jesus washing His betrayer’s feet to an implied promise that He will never leave anyone who feels rejected.

- **YouTube title: “He Knew Who Would Leave.” Facebook title: “He Knew Who Would Leave. He Picked Up a Towel.”** These blur Judas’s betrayal and Peter’s denial into the same action: leaving. The film distinguishes them. “Betray” would make the hook more precise and retain its force.

- **YouTube description: “If you are sure nobody would stay if they really knew you: Jesus knows.” Instagram: “Send this to someone who is sure nobody would stay if they knew.”** Alongside “the one holding the towel,” these invite an unconditional reassurance that the passage does not establish for every reader. The supplied text explicitly distinguishes service from spiritual cleansing: “ye are clean, but not all” (13:10), and “If I wash thee not, thou hast no part with me” (13:8). Preserve the invitation to Jesus without implying that receiving His kindness establishes saving fellowship.

- **YouTube and Facebook: “‘Having loved his own which were in the world, he loved them unto the end.’” TikTok and Instagram: “‘He loved them unto the end.’”** Under the requested strict verbatim standard, the opening capitals differ from the supplied KJV’s **“having”** and **“he.”** These are capitalization changes, not substituted words; there is no lexical alteration in these excerpts. The quotation “For he knew who should betray him; therefore said he, Ye are not all clean” matches.

- **YouTube tags: “john 13 explained” and “jesus washing feet meaning.”** These promise more exposition than the film supplies. It offers a devotional application but omits Jesus’s own explanation about cleansing and His example for the disciples. “john 13 explained” is especially broad for an 81-second treatment of selected moments.

- **Facebook opening: “John 13: on the night before the cross…”** This spends the opening on a reference and scene-setting. The stronger tension—Jesus knowing about the betrayal—arrives later. TikTok’s “Jesus knew Judas would betray Him” and Instagram’s opening question establish that tension immediately.

The titles deliver the towel scene; there is no obvious scene-level bait-and-switch. Hashtag counts are restrained, the supplied footer and links are intact, and “Find Jesus in the whole Bible” supplies a Christ-directed brand CTA. There is no explicit works, financial-gain, or threatened-loss framing.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Qualify the universal “He would stay” implication; distinguish Jesus’s kindness from saving fellowship.
2. Make the titles precise about betrayal and remove the overbroad “john 13 explained” tag.
3. Match KJV quotation capitalization exactly and strengthen Facebook’s opening line.
