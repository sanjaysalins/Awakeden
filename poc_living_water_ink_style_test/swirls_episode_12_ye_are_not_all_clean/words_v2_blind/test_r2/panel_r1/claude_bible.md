## 1. WHO / WHAT / WHY in your own words
This is for someone alone at night who believes being truly known means being left. The night before He died, Jesus knew the worst about His friends. One would betray Him and one would deny Him. He still knelt and washed their feet, with the betrayer in the room. The point is that the One who knows everything about you is the one who stays and serves.

## 2. FIRST 2 SECONDS: would a lonely viewer stop? **8/10**
"Do you ever think: if people really knew me, they would leave?" It names a real, private fear in plain words with no jargon, so it works for a global audience. It loses a little because it is a question format that many feeds already use.

## 3. ENDING: earned or slogan? **8/10**
"The one who knew the worst about them was not the one who left. He was the one holding the towel."
This is earned. It turns the hook's fear around using the story's own picture (knower vs leaver, towel vs door). No slogan, and no pressure.

## 4. WHERE I GOT LOST OR MISLED
- "John gives the answer in one line: 'he loved them unto the end.'" Right after Jesus asks "Know ye what I have done to you?", this sounds like John is reporting Jesus's reply. He isn't. Jesus's own answer (13:14-15) is "an example … wash one another's feet." 13:1 comes *before* the washing.
- "Maybe it is night where you are" comes straight after Judas walks into the night. A struggling viewer may hear "you are like Judas." I think it works as "your darkness," but it is a risk.

## 5. ACCURACY vs the KJV
- **RIGHT:** Jesus knew of the betrayal ("he knew who should betray him," 13:11). The towel, water and washing (13:4-5). "Know ye what I have done to you?" is verbatim (13:12). Judas was present at the washing (13:10-11, leaves at 13:30). "and it was night" is verbatim and is four words.
- **RIGHT (gloss):** "three times before morning" fairly renders "the cock shall not crow, till thou hast denied me thrice" (13:38).
- **OVERCLAIMED (mild):** The text says Jesus foretold Peter's denial *after* the washing (13:38), but the film puts that knowledge *before* it. It is fine theologically (13:3, 13:19), but it reorders the text.
- **OVERCLAIMED (mild):** "And the end was a cross." 13:1 doesn't say this. It is a fair reading (John 19), but it is interpretation. "The next day" is fine for a modern viewer.
- **OVERCLAIMED:** calling 13:1 "the answer" to 13:12, as above.
- **WRONG:** none.

## 6. OVERALL **8/10**
I would send this to a struggling friend. The smallest fix is to stop framing 13:1 as the reply to Jesus's question:

Change "John gives the answer in one line:" → **"John had already told us why, in one line:"**
