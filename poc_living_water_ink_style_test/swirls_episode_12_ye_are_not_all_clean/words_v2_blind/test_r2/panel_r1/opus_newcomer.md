# Newcomer review (opus seat)

## 1. WHO / WHAT / WHY in your own words
Jesus eats dinner with his best friends. He already knows two of them will hurt him: Judas will betray him, Peter will say he does not know him. He still washes their feet, which is a servant's job, and Judas is still in the room. Then Judas leaves into the dark. The film's point is that Jesus knows the worst about you and does not leave. He stays and serves. I got who, what and why. The "why" lands at the end, not before.

## 2. FIRST 2 SECONDS: would a lonely viewer stop? 8/10
"if people really knew me, they would leave?" This is exactly my fear after a breakup. Simple words, it feels personal. I would stop. It is not a 10 only because "Do you ever think:" is a slow start. "If people really knew me..." alone would hit faster.

## 3. ENDING: earned or slogan? 8/10
"He was the one holding the towel." Earned. The towel was set up earlier, so I can picture it. The contrast (Judas left, Jesus stayed) is clear even to me. Small gap: "Jesus knows. All of it." Then what? The film trusts me to feel that knowing did not make him leave. Most viewers will get it. A few may hear "knows all of it" as a threat for a second.

## 4. WHERE I GOT LOST OR MISLED
- "John gives the answer in one line" - Who is John? Nobody told me. Is he at the table? A writer? I lost a beat here.
- "Know ye what I have done to you?" - old English. I understood it, but slowly. And "to you" sounds a bit like he did something bad.
- "he loved them unto the end" - "unto the end" could mean "until he died" or "completely". The next line helps ("the end was a cross"), but I do not know what a cross means here, except that it was death.
- "deny Him" - harder word for second-language speakers. "Say he never knew Him" is easier.
- "four small words" - I counted them; fine, a nice touch.

## 5. ACCURACY vs the KJV
- RIGHT: towel, water, bowl, washing feet (13:4-5); Judas still present (13:2, 13:11, 13:26-30); "and it was night" (13:30); Peter denies thrice before the cock crows (13:38); "he loved them unto the end" (13:1).
- OVERCLAIMED: "John gives the answer" - presents 13:1 as the answer to Jesus's question in 13:12. In the text, Jesus answers it Himself (13:14-15: an example to follow). Soft framing, not false, but stretched.
- OVERCLAIMED (mild): "before morning" - the text says before the cock crows; fair paraphrase.
- Outside John 13: "a cross, the next day" - true per John 19, as the pack says.

## 6. OVERALL 7.5/10
Strong hook, clear story, earned last line. Main snag: "John" appears with no introduction, and it slightly misframes v1 as the answer.

Smallest change:
"John gives the answer in one line:" -> "The story says it in one line:"
