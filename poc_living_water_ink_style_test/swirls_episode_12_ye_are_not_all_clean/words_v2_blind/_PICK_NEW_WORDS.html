Episode 12: new words

For the person alone at night who thinks: if they really knew me, they would leave.

My pick

Do you ever think: if people really knew me, they would leave?

One night, Jesus sat at supper with His closest friends. And He knew them.

He knew one of them, Judas, would betray Him. He knew another, Peter, would deny Him three times before morning.

So what does He do with friends like that?

He gets up from the table. He ties a towel around Himself. He pours water into a bowl, and He washes their feet. A servant's work. And Judas is still in the room.

JESUS: "Know ye what I have done to you?"

The story says it in one line: "he loved them unto the end." And the end was a cross, the next day.

Then Judas walks out. And the story adds four small words: "and it was night."

Maybe it is night where you are. Maybe you are sure nobody would stay, if they knew.

Jesus knows. All of it.

And look at that room again. The one who knew the worst about them was not the one who left. He was the one holding the towel.

181 words, about 70 s. KJV quotes: John 13:12, 13:1, 13:30.

How we got here

6 writers wrote from scratch, blind (Fable, Opus, Sonnet, Claude, Codex, and a second Claude seat that stood in for Grok, which timed out).
All 6 found the same heart on their own: being fully known, and Jesus not leaving. The towel. "And it was night."
Then 2 versions were tested blind. 4 of 4 readers picked this one.

4 questions

Who: someone alone at night who fears being known means being left.
Take-away: Jesus knew the worst about His friends, and He was the one who stayed, holding the towel.
Curiosity gap: "What does He do with friends like that?" Paid off by the washing, and by who left.
Global: plain words; the newcomer readers understood who, what and why.

Still a little weak

- "Know ye what I have done to you?" is old English (kept: it is Jesus's own voice).
- Peter's denial is said at the start; in John it is told later in the chapter (fair, not wrong).
- "Maybe it is night where you are" right after Judas leaves: one reader worried it sounds like "you are Judas".
