## TITLE (at most 6 words)
He Knew, and He Stayed

## WHO / TAKEAWAY / CURIOSITY GAP / WHY IT EARNS ITS PLACE (one line each)
- **WHO:** A person alone at night who is sure that anyone who really knew them would leave.
- **TAKEAWAY:** Jesus knew the worst about the men at his table, and he was not the one who left; he was the one holding the towel.
- **CURIOSITY GAP:** He knows one friend will betray him and one will deny him, so what does he do with friends like that? (Paid off twice: he washes their feet, and the one who walks out is not him.)
- **WHY IT EARNS ITS PLACE:** It answers the lonely viewer's real fear (being fully known) with the chapter's own facts: "he knew", the towel, "unto the end", "and it was night".

## NARRATION (speaker tag before each part: NARRATOR: or JESUS: ; then the word count)

NARRATOR: Do you ever think: if people really knew me, they would leave?

One night, Jesus sat at supper with his closest friends. And he knew them.

He knew one of them would betray him that same night.

He knew another would deny him three times before the rooster crowed.

So what does he do with friends like that?

He gets up from the table. He takes a towel. He pours water into a bowl. And he begins to wash their feet. The work of a servant. And Judas is still in the room.

Then he asks them:

JESUS: "Know ye what I have done to you?"

NARRATOR: The story says it in one line: "he loved them unto the end."

Soon after, Judas walks out. And the Bible adds four small words: "and it was night."

Maybe it is night where you are. Maybe you are sure nobody would stay, if they knew.

Jesus knows.

And look at that room again. The one who knew everything was not the one who left.

He was the one holding the towel.

## WHY THE OPENING HOLDS A LONELY VIEWER (2 lines)
The first line is not about the Bible at all; it says out loud the private thought the lonely viewer already has ("if people really knew me, they would leave"), so they feel seen before they know what the film is.
Then it puts that exact fear into a real room: a man who does know his friends' worst, and a question ("what does he do with friends like that?") they need answered for themselves.

## WHY THE ENDING IS EARNED (2 lines, name the concrete thing from John 13 it pays off)
It pays off the towel (John 13:4-5) and the word "knew" (13:1, 13:11): both are set up in the middle as plain facts, then turned at the end, with "and it was night" (13:30) as the bridge to the viewer's own night.
The last line gives no instruction; it only shows who left the room and who stayed, so the comfort comes from the story's own twist, not from a slogan.

## RISKS (2 lines: what could be misread, by a newcomer or by a Bible reader)
Newcomer: might hear "Jesus knows" as a promise that the loneliness ends tonight, or might feel they are being compared to Judas; the film only claims that being fully known did not make Jesus leave.
Bible reader: John 13 never says in so many words that Jesus washed Judas's feet (it says he washed "the disciples' feet" and Judas leaves later, 13:30), so the script says only "Judas is still in the room"; also "unto the end" is quoted from verse 1, before the washing, and "deny" compresses 13:38.
