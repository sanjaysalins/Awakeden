## TITLE
The Feet That Walked Into Night

## WHO / TAKEAWAY / CURIOSITY GAP / WHY IT EARNS ITS PLACE
- **WHO:** Someone alone at night who feels outside, unwanted, or too far gone.
- **TAKEAWAY:** Jesus knew who would walk out into the dark, and He still knelt and washed his feet. His love goes into the night.
- **CURIOSITY GAP:** "Jesus knew which one would walk out. Watch what He does first." The answer: He washes that man's feet too.
- **WHY IT EARNS ITS PLACE:** It is built on a detail most people skip. The towel touched every pair of feet, even the pair that walked out at "it was night" (13:30).

## NARRATION
NARRATOR: Twelve men at one table. Before morning, one of them would walk out alone into the dark. Jesus knew which one. Watch what He does first.

NARRATOR: He gets up from supper, ties a towel around Himself, pours water into a bowl, and kneels. Dusty feet, one pair after another.

NARRATOR: Peter pulls back.

JESUS: "What I do thou knowest not now."

NARRATOR: Jesus keeps going, all the way round. He knows exactly who will leave.

JESUS: "Ye are not all clean."

NARRATOR: He says it out loud. And still, He kneels at every pair of feet.

NARRATOR: Then Judas takes bread from Jesus' own hand, and goes out. "And it was night."

NARRATOR: So the feet that walked into that darkness had just been washed and dried with Jesus' own towel.

NARRATOR: If you feel like the one outside tonight, the one who left or the one left behind: Jesus did not wait for anyone to deserve the towel. John says He "loved them unto the end."

NARRATOR: Your night is not a place His love has never been. He has knelt there already, towel in hand.

## WHY THE OPENING HOLDS A LONELY VIEWER
The first line is a person walking out alone into the dark, which is the viewer's own feeling, before any Bible name appears.
"Jesus knew which one. Watch what He does first" opens a question the viewer has to stay to answer.

## WHY THE ENDING IS EARNED
It pays off two concrete things from John 13: the towel (13:4–5) and the night (13:30). The towel was set up in picture 2 and touched the leaving man's feet in picture 6.
"Loved them unto the end" (13:1) is the chapter's own summary, so the closing line is a picture the film already showed, not a slogan.

## RISKS
**Newcomer:** "the one who left" could feel like we are calling the viewer Judas or a betrayer. We are not; the line also names "the one left behind" to keep it wide. "He has knelt there already" is a picture of His love, not a promise that the night ends tonight.
**Bible reader:** John never says outright "He washed Judas's feet." We infer it from 13:5, 10–12 ("washed their feet", "not all clean" said during the washing). That is a widely held reading, but the film should not claim more than the text. Washed feet do not mean Judas was "clean" (13:10–11), and the film never says he was.
