## TITLE
The Towel and the Night

## WHO / TAKEAWAY / CURIOSITY GAP / WHY IT EARNS ITS PLACE
WHO: A lonely person awake tonight who feels no one would stoop for them.
TAKEAWAY: Jesus did not wait for his friends to be worthy; he knelt with the towel before they failed, and that is where he is found.
CURIOSITY GAP: Whose feet will he wash, and what happens to the one who walks out into the night?
WHY IT EARNS ITS PLACE: John 13 is the one scene where Jesus kneels to the very people who will betray and deny him, so it answers "would anyone choose me?" with a towel, not a slogan.

## NARRATION
NARRATOR: Alone tonight? Sure nobody would kneel for you? Watch one table, one night. (12)

NARRATOR: It is the last supper. Jesus knows one friend will hand him over. He knows another will say, I do not know him. (23)

NARRATOR: Then he stands. He ties a towel round his waist. He pours water into a bowl. He kneels, washes their feet one by one, and dries them with the towel. (30)

NARRATOR: Peter says no. "Thou shalt never wash my feet." (11)

JESUS: If I wash thee not, thou hast no part with me. (11)

NARRATOR: The bowl goes on to Judas too. Jesus knows, and kneels anyway. Soon Judas walks out. The Bible says only, "And it was night." (24)

NARRATOR: But look at his feet. Washed. Peter will soon deny him. His feet were washed too. Jesus did not wait for worthy. The Bible says he loved them "unto the end." (32)

NARRATOR: So if you are in the dark tonight, here is where he is. Low on the floor, towel in hand, beside the ones who fail him. Near enough for you. (30)

TOTAL: about 173 words.

## WHY THE OPENING HOLDS A LONELY VIEWER
It speaks straight to the private feeling ("nobody would kneel for you") before any Bible fact. The word "kneel" plants the curiosity gap that the film pays off.

## WHY THE ENDING IS EARNED
It pays off the towel and the washed feet set up in the third part, including Judas's and Peter's feet and the "it was night" line. The closing picture is the same towel, not an instruction.

## RISKS
A newcomer may not know Judas or Peter; the lines name what each will do, but quickly. A Bible reader may say the film leaves out the "not all clean" teaching and that Judas's feet being washed is inferred from the disciples being at table; the closing claim is about where Jesus is, not that everyone is saved.
