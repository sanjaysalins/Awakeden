## TITLE
The Towel Came First

## WHO / TAKEAWAY / CURIOSITY GAP / WHY IT EARNS ITS PLACE
- **WHO:** A lonely person scrolling at night who feels like the one on the outside.
- **TAKEAWAY:** Jesus came close to people even though He knew everything about them. His love went first.
- **CURIOSITY GAP:** A man walks out alone into the dark. What happened in that room just before he left?
- **WHY IT EARNS ITS PLACE:** The chapter tells its story in an order: the towel, then the bread, then the night. That order is the good news, and the film shows it.

## NARRATION
NARRATOR: One man stood up from dinner and walked out alone. The Bible says it in three words: "it was night."

NARRATOR: But look what happened just before.

NARRATOR: Jesus knew this man would turn against Him. And still, Jesus took off his robe, picked up a towel and a bowl of water, bent low, and washed his friends' dusty feet. All of them.

NARRATOR: Peter said no: "Thou shalt never wash my feet."

JESUS: "If I wash thee not, thou hast no part with me."

NARRATOR: Then Jesus dipped a piece of bread and handed it to the man who would leave. From His own hand. He took it, and went out into the dark.

NARRATOR: That same night Jesus told Peter he would deny Him three times. He also promised him:

JESUS: "thou shalt follow me afterwards."

NARRATOR: John tells us why, in one line: "he loved them unto the end."

NARRATOR: So if tonight you feel like the one outside, alone in the dark, look at the order of that night. Before anyone walked out, Jesus was already at their feet. The towel came first.

## WHY THE OPENING HOLDS A LONELY VIEWER
The film opens on the exact feeling the viewer has: one person walking out alone into the night.
Then it turns: "look what happened just before." That is a gap the viewer wants closed.

## WHY THE ENDING IS EARNED
It pays off the **towel** and **"it was night"** (13:4–5 and 13:30). We see the towel before the night, so the last line just says what the film has already shown.
It also leans on John's own frame line, "he loved them unto the end" (13:1). The ending rests on the plain word "towel", not on the old word "unto".

## RISKS
- **Newcomer:** "you feel like the one outside" could sound like we are comparing the viewer to the betrayer, or saying the betrayer was saved. The film only says Jesus loved and served before anyone left. It never says how Judas's story ended.
- **Bible reader:** "All of them", which includes Judas, is a reading of verses 5, 11 and 12 (washing "their feet" while Judas was there). It is not a separate line in the text. "Bent low" fits the act of washing, but the text does not say it. Verse 36 (the promise) comes before verse 38 (the denial), so the narration says "also promised" and does not claim one came after the other.
