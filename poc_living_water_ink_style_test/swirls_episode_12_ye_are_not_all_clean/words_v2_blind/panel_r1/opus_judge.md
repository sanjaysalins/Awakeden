## TITLE (at most 6 words)
The Towel Got There First

## WHO / TAKEAWAY / CURIOSITY GAP / WHY IT EARNS ITS PLACE (one line each)
- **WHO:** A lonely person scrolling at night who feels like the one outside the door.
- **TAKEAWAY:** Jesus knew the worst about the man at his table and still knelt at his feet; his love reaches people before their night does.
- **CURIOSITY GAP:** A man walks out alone into the night. Who washed his feet first, and why would anyone do that for him?
- **WHY IT EARNS ITS PLACE:** It turns the chapter's darkest line ("and it was night") around: before the door closed, the towel had already been there.

NARRATOR: It was Jesus. And Jesus knew this man would betray him.

NARRATOR: John says Jesus knew the Father "had given all things into his hands." What did he do with them? He "took a towel."

NARRATOR: Then Jesus said:

NARRATOR: He knew who it was. And he kept washing feet anyway. Then Judas took the bread from Jesus' own hand, and "went immediately out: and it was night."

NARRATOR: Maybe tonight you feel like the one outside the door. Look what happened first. Before Judas left, Jesus had been down at his feet.

NARRATOR: John says he loved his own "unto the end." To the very end.

NARRATOR: Your night is real. But his love was there before it. The towel got there first.

1. **"A man walked out into the night, alone."** A doorway seen from inside: a lone figure's back, stepping into deep blue-black night. MOVES: the night air pulls the edge of his cloak; the lamp-glow on the threshold trembles behind him.

## WHY THE OPENING HOLDS A LONELY VIEWER (2 lines)
The first words are a lonely act anyone recognises: walking out into the night, alone. The second line breaks the pattern: someone had just knelt and washed this man's feet, which makes the viewer ask who, and why.

## WHY THE ENDING IS EARNED (2 lines, name the concrete thing from John 13 it pays off)
It pays off the **towel** (13:4-5), set up at "He took a towel," and the line **"and it was night"** (13:30), set up in the very first line. The film shows the order of events, washing first and night after, so "the towel got there first" is something the viewer has watched, not a slogan.

## RISKS (2 lines: what could be misread, by a newcomer or by a Bible reader)
- **Bible reader:** John never says outright "Jesus washed Judas's feet." It is strongly implied (13:5 "the disciples' feet," 13:10-11 said during the washing, Judas still at the table in 13:26), but "Jesus had been down at his feet" should be checked by the doctrine panel. "Knelt" is also a fair reading of foot-washing, not a word in the text.
- **Newcomer:** A lonely viewer might hear themselves in Judas, or read "his love was there before it" as a promise that Judas's story ended well. The ending is kept about love arriving first, not about how anyone's night ends, but the panel should test whether it lands as comfort without suggesting Judas's betrayal did not matter.
