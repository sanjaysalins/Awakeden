## 1. WHO / WHAT / WHY

**X:** Jesus knows Judas will betray Him and washes his feet anyway. Then Judas walks out into the night. The point: if you feel like the one outside, His love reached you before you left.

**Y:** Jesus knows the worst about His friends: one will betray Him and one will deny Him. He still kneels and washes their feet, Judas's too. The point: being fully known does not make Him leave.

## 2. FIRST 2 SECONDS

**X: 6/10.** "A man walked out into the night, alone" sets a nice mood. But it is about someone else, so a lonely viewer has to wait to see why it matters to them.

**Y: 8/10.** "If people really knew me, they would leave?" names the exact fear the target viewer has. It is direct and personal, and it is the right person to hook.

## 3. ENDING

**X: 6/10.** "Your night is real. But His love got there before it. The towel got there first." That is three closers in a row. The towel line is earned by the hands/towel set-up, but stacking it makes it feel like a slogan.

**Y: 8/10.** "The one who knew everything was not the one who left. He was the one holding the towel." It answers the hook and the contrast is clean. One weak point: "Jesus knows." on its own line can scare someone whose fear *is* being known. The comfort only comes one line later, by implication.

## 4. WHERE I GOT LOST OR MISLED

- **X:** "Maybe tonight you feel like the one outside the door." This casts the lonely viewer as Judas, the betrayer. That is a risky match for someone who feels unwanted rather than guilty.
- **X:** "Before that door closed." The text mentions no door.
- **Y:** "Know ye what I have done to you?" This is old-fashioned English and the film never answers it. A second-language viewer may stall here.
- **Y:** "Jesus knows." Knows what? It only works if the next line comes fast.

## 5. ACCURACY vs the KJV

**RIGHT (both):** "given all things into his hands" (v3), "took a towel" (v4), "unto the end" (v1), "went immediately out: and it was night" (v30). Y's "four small words" is a true count. Y's "Judas is still in the room" is right: he leaves at v30, after the washing. Y's "deny Him three times" matches v38. X's "bread from Jesus' own hand" matches the sop in v26.

**OVERCLAIMED:**
- Both say "knelt" or "kneels". The text says "began to wash", so kneeling is a fair guess but not written.
- X: "all the way round" is implied by v10–12, not stated.
- X: "Peter pulled back" comes *before* the v7 quote, but Peter's refusal is in v8, *after* it. The order is slightly off.
- X uses "loved His own unto the end" to cover the man outside. Whether "his own" includes Judas is disputed, so don't build the comfort on it.
- Y: "that same night" and "before morning" come from v38 and John 18, not from the washing scene. Fair, but it pulls in later verses.
- Y: "knew everything" is backed by v3, v11 and v18, and by John 21:17 elsewhere. Defensible.

**WRONG:** none.

I would send **Y** to a friend who is struggling. I would hesitate with X because of the "you are Judas" framing.

## 6. PICK: **Y**

**Y 8/10 · X 6.5/10**

**One change:** replace "Jesus knows." with this line:
> "Jesus knows. All of it. And knowing all of it, He picked up the towel."

It removes the scary pause and keeps strictly to the text (v3 to v4: knowing, He rose and took the towel). It also sets up the last line instead of leaving it to inference.
