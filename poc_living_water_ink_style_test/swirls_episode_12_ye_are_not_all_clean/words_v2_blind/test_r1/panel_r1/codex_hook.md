## 1. WHO / WHAT / WHY in your own words (X, Y)

**X:** Jesus washes the feet of the man who will betray Him. His care precedes Judas’s departure, offering a lonely viewer an image of love already present.

**Y:** Jesus knows His friends’ coming failures and still serves them. The viewer’s fear of being known and abandoned becomes the reason to revisit the towel.

## 2. FIRST 2 SECONDS: would a lonely viewer stop? score 0-10 (X, Y) and why

**Y: 7/10.** “Do you ever think…” sounds familiar and potentially generic. The completed question is stronger: it names a private fear precisely. That payoff needs more than two seconds.

## 3. ENDING: does it land and feel earned, or like a slogan? score 0-10 (X, Y); quote the line

**X: 7/10.** “The towel got there first.” Memorable and visually prepared. “His love got there before it” feels more manufactured: the metaphor stretches beyond the event.

**Y: 8/10.** “He was the one holding the towel.” The opening fear receives a concrete answer. The preceding contrast with the person who left supplies the emotional force, though its application needs care.

## 4. WHERE I GOT LOST OR MISLED (quote the words)

**X:** “Peter pulled back” opens a second character’s story, followed by an unexplained archaic quotation. Around the middle, that interrupts the Judas thread. “Outside the door” then associates an unwanted viewer with someone leaving to betray Jesus.

**Y:** “So what does He do with friends like that? Watch.” briefly delays an already compelling action. “Jesus knows” shifts from the narrated event to a personal assurance without explaining that transition.

**Both:** These are dense scripts for 70 seconds. Rapid delivery could sacrifice the pauses that make the final image work.

## 5. ACCURACY vs the KJV (RIGHT / OVERCLAIMED / WRONG, quote)

**X — RIGHT:** “had given all things into his hands”; “took a towel.”

**X — OVERCLAIMED:** “all the way round” makes an inferred exhaustive sequence explicit. Judas’s washing is strongly implied, not individually narrated.

**Y — RIGHT:** The betrayal, predicted threefold denial, washing, and departure follow John 13.

**Y — OVERCLAIMED:** “knew everything” exceeds this chapter’s specific statements.

**Both:** “Loved…unto the end” is accurate quotation, but applying “his own” specifically to Judas is not established here.

## 6. PICK: X or Y, overall score 0-10 each, and the ONE change that would most improve your pick (exact wording)

**Y wins: X 7/10; Y 8/10.** Its opening fear and closing image connect more directly.
