# Fable judge — test r1 (lens: NO HAT)

## 1. WHO / WHAT / WHY in your own words (X, Y)
**X.** Who: Jesus, and a man who leaves (named Judas only in line 7). What: Jesus washes His friends' feet, including the betrayer's, and the betrayer walks out into the night. Why: if you feel shut outside tonight, His love reached you before your night did.
**Y.** Who: Jesus and His closest friends, one who will betray Him and one who will deny Him. What: knowing all that, He kneels and washes their feet. Why: you fear people would leave if they knew you; the One who knows did not leave.
Y is easier to retell after one hearing.

## 2. FIRST 2 SECONDS: would a lonely viewer stop? score 0-10 (X, Y) and why
**X: 6.** "A man walked out into the night, alone." A good picture, with some mystery. But it is about a stranger, and the viewer has to wait to learn why it matters to them.
**Y: 8.** "Do you ever think: if people really knew me, they would leave?" It names the viewer's own private fear in plain words. A question opener is a common form, but this one is specific enough to stop a thumb.

## 3. ENDING: does it land and feel earned, or like a slogan? score 0-10 (X, Y); quote the line
**X: 6.** "Your night is real. But His love got there before it. The towel got there first." "Your night is real" is kind and honest. "The towel got there first" is close to a slogan and a little cryptic (first before what?). It also sits on a problem: the man it "got to first" still walked out.
**Y: 8.** "The one who knew everything was not the one who left. He was the one holding the towel." It answers the first line exactly and it is built from the story's own picture. Earned. It stops just short of telling the viewer "He stays with you".

## 4. WHERE I GOT LOST OR MISLED (quote the words)
**X.**
- "A man… someone had knelt… his feet… betray Him": two unnamed people in the first lines, so the pronouns are hard to follow by ear.
- "Peter pulled back." then "What I do thou knowest not now; but thou shalt know hereafter.": a side road. A newcomer does not know who Peter is or what the line means, and it is the only time Jesus speaks.
- "Maybe tonight you feel like the one outside the door.": this puts the lonely viewer in Judas's shoes. Judas was not shut out; he chose to go, to betray. It could be heard as "you are the traitor", or as "love came first and it still did not help".

**Y.**
- "he loved them unto the end": the end of what? A newcomer is not told.
- "Know ye what I have done to you?": archaic, and never directly answered, though the next line half-answers it.

## 5. ACCURACY vs the KJV (RIGHT / OVERCLAIMED / WRONG, quote)
**X.**
- RIGHT: "had given all things into his hands" (13:3), "took a towel" (13:4), the 13:7 quote, "went immediately out: and it was night" (13:30), "unto the end" (13:1).
- OVERCLAIMED (mild): "someone had knelt on the floor and washed his feet" and "all the way round". The KJV never says outright that Judas's feet were washed. It is a strong inference from 13:5, 13:10-11 and 13:30, but X states it as fact.
- OVERCLAIMED (small): "Before that door closed" (no door in the text); "took bread" for the "sop" is fair.

**Y.**
- RIGHT: betrayal known (13:11, 13:21); "deny Him three times before morning" (13:38); "Judas is still in the room" (true until 13:30, and it avoids X's overclaim); "Know ye what I have done to you?" (13:12); "he loved them unto the end" (13:1); "four small words" (correct count in the KJV).
- OVERCLAIMED (mild): "knew everything". John 13 says He knew His hour, the betrayer and the denial; "everything" goes a little further than this chapter.

Nothing WRONG in either version.

## 6. PICK: X or Y, overall score 0-10 each, and the ONE change that would most improve your pick (exact wording)
**PICK: Y.** X = 6, Y = 8.

One change: say what "the end" was, so a newcomer meets the cross. Replace line 7 with:

> NARRATOR: John says it in one line: "he loved them unto the end." And the end was a cross, the very next day.
