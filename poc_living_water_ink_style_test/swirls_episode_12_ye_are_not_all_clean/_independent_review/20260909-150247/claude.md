## Independent Review — Narration: "The Towel" (Judas / John 13)

**Hook:** "Judas walked out into the night on feet Jesus had washed." — concrete, ironic, no template opener ("Did you know/Imagine/What if"). **Score: 8/10.**

**KJV verbatim check** (line by line against John 13 KJV):
- "put into the heart of Judas Iscariot, Simon's son, to betray him" (13:2) — exact.
- "ye are clean, but not all" (13:10) — exact.
- "He that eateth bread with me hath lifted up his heel against me" (13:18) — exact.
- "That thou doest, do quickly" (13:27) — exact.
- "went immediately out: and it was night" (13:30) — exact.

No altered words, no elisions. This is clean — better than the Isaiah 53/Acts 8:35 miss noted in project history.

**Doctrine/sequence:** Order matches John's own sequence (devil→footwashing→"not all clean"→prophecy quote→sop→"do quickly"→misunderstanding→exit). No invented doctrine, no contrarian reading. "Jesus did not wash those feet because Judas earned it... that is what His love does when it already knows the worst" is sound and grace-anchored — no fear/gain-loss framing anywhere.

**Problem 1 — "the old word for betrayal, from Scripture" underplays what's actually happening.** John 13:18 is Jesus explicitly quoting Psalm 41:9 as fulfilled prophecy ("that the scripture may be fulfilled"). Flattening that into "the old word for betrayal" loses the sharpest thread-spine opportunity in the piece (an OT-echo NT-confirms moment) and is vague enough that a first-time listener may not register it as a direct quotation of an older Scripture at all — it reads more like a euphemism than a citation.

**Problem 2 — "hath lifted up his heel against me" is left unglossed.** The archaic idiom is quoted once (fine per the global-audience rule), but its meaning (a treacherous kick/rejection from someone at your own table) is never translated into plain language before or after. A non-native or first-time listener has only "the old word for betrayal" as a hint — that's not enough to parse the image itself on a single hearing.

**Problem 3 — the landing splits into a strong half and a generic half.** "He has not put the towel down" is excellent — a concrete, unique prop from this exact account, not swappable into another piece. But "bring Him your worst" is structurally identical to the stock-closer family this rubric explicitly flags ("come to Jesus," "turn to Him," "come as you are"): a short generic imperative that could close almost any redemption-themed short with zero changes. The piece earns a genuinely fresh landing clause and then reverts to boilerplate for the actual call-to-action verb. **Landing score: 7/10** — the towel clause alone would be an 8-9; the tacked-on "bring Him your worst" pulls it down.

**Minor:** "Some at the table thought he had gone to buy food" is accurate (13:29) but flat filler right before the darkest line in the piece ("went immediately out: and it was night") — it slightly deflates momentum at the worst possible spot in a 60-second arc.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Rewrite the CTA's second clause so it stays inside the towel/washing image instead of pivoting to a generic imperative — the fix is a verb/image from THIS piece (washing, dirt, feet, towel), not "bring your worst."
2. Make the Psalm 41:9 citation explicit as fulfilled Scripture, not "the old word for betrayal" — and add a one-clause plain-English gloss for "lifted up his heel" so it lands on first hearing.
3. Cut or tighten "Some at the table thought he had gone to buy food" to protect momentum going into "and it was night."
