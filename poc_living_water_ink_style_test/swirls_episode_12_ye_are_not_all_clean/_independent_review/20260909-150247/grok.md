**Doctrinal soundness — under-teaches the verse it quotes**

`"ye are clean, but not all."` is not color. It is John 13:10–11. Outer wash ≠ inner clean. Judas got the water. He is the `"not all."` The script never says so.

Then it sells the opposite:

- `"Jesus did not wash those feet because Judas earned it. He washed them knowing Judas would betray Him anyway."`
- `"That is what His love does when it already knows the worst."`
- `"He has not put the towel down — bring Him your worst."`

John keeps three things apart: the towel, the wash, and being **clean**. This narration melts them into one maxim: knowing love keeps washing, so bring your worst. That is not a hot-take heresy. It is a false shape of this scene.

Judas is the **counterexample in the room**. He received the wash. He ate the bread. He still `"went immediately out: and it was night."` Using *that* man as the picture of “come as you are and He will finish” skips the line the episode is built on.

`"He has not put the towel down"` also fights the next verse. After the wash He “had taken his garments, and was set down again” (13:12). In the story the towel-work on those feet is over. Judas still walks into night. The close pretends the towel is still the open gospel.

`"He knows your heart, every corner of it, like that"` points `"like that"` at Judas’s heart — the heart where the devil had already `"put"` betrayal. John never invites the listener to wear that heart as comfort. He marks Judas so the rest will believe (13:18–19). Mapping the viewer onto the betrayer, then offering the towel as the cure, is the wrong analogue.

Not invented doctrine in the clickbait sense. Real grounding error by omission and by the landing.

**Clarity on first hearing**

A listener with no Bible cannot follow the hinge.

1. `"ye are clean, but not all."` — Feet were just washed. Then “not all” are clean. Who? The script never ties it to Judas. First hear: washed → clean → not clean. Unresolved contradiction.

2. `"He that eateth bread with me hath lifted up his heel against me."` — `"the old word for betrayal"` says the *topic*, not the *sense*. A stranger can hear a literal heel. Load-bearing archaic idiom, no plain paraphrase. Global-audience fail.

3. `"That thou doest, do quickly."` then `"Some at the table thought he had gone to buy food."` Restages the disciples’ mix-up. On one pass Jesus is sending a man on an errand, not releasing a traitor. Betrayal was named once, early. This paragraph buries it.

4. Double exit: `"Judas walked out into the night"` in line 1, then `"went immediately out: and it was night"` again. No “that was the moment.” Sounds like he left twice.

5. `"like that"` — like the devil in the heart? like the wash? like the night? Fuzzy on one pass.

`"sop — a piece of dipped bread"` is the one gloss that works.

**Grace-anchored conviction**

No fear pitch. No gain/loss. `"did not wash those feet because Judas earned it"` is anti-works. Good.

`"bring Him your worst"` is invitation, not a threat. Watch it: “bring” can sound like the listener’s disclosure does the cleansing. John does not teach that. Judas could have “brought” nothing; he was already washed, and still not clean. The Spirit convicts; this close asks for a vulnerability performance the scene does not earn.
