**Clarity on first hearing:** 
The imagery is striking, but there is a slight chronological hiccup. The hook states, "Judas walked out into the night...", placing us at the end of the scene. The script then flashes back to the washing and the meal, only to have Judas walk out *again* in paragraph 4 ("Judas 'went immediately out...'"). A listener might briefly wonder if he left twice. 
Additionally, the phrase "quoted the old word for betrayal" is slightly clunky; "lifting the heel" is a prophecy or an image, not a single "word."

**Grace-anchored conviction:** 
Excellent. "He washed them knowing Judas would betray Him anyway. That is what His love does when it already knows the worst." This is a pure grace framing with zero reliance on fear, works, or self-interest. 

**Freshness = faithful depth:** 
The opening image—Judas walking into the dark *on feet Jesus had just washed*—is a fantastic, fresh observation of the text that immediately grounds the listener in the physical reality of grace.

**One thread spine:** 
The thread holds perfectly. It begins with the washed feet, moves through the tension of the betrayer's dirty heart despite the washed feet, and lands beautifully on the image of the towel.
