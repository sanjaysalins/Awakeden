### Doctrinal soundness
Mostly orthodox, but the piece **quotes John’s sharpest distinction and then flattens it**.

- **"ye are clean, but not all."** is John 13:10–11’s hinge: outer washing ≠ inner cleanness. Judas got the towel; he was still the unclean one. The narration never states that.
- **"That is what His love does when it already knows the worst."** turns a specific act toward a known betrayer into a sentimental maxim. John’s scene also carries judgment, fulfillment of Scripture, and departure into darkness — not just “persistent love.”
- The landing invites the listener without acknowledging Judas as the **counterexample in the same room**: full knowledge, full washing, full offer — and **"went immediately out: and it was night."** That is not invented doctrine, but it is **doctrinal compression** the text itself refuses.

Not heresy. It **under-teaches** what John is actually distinguishing.

---

### Clarity on first hearing
Several lines stall a zero-Bible listener:

1. **"ye are clean, but not all."** — Who is “not all”? Judas was named two sentences earlier but is never linked to this line. First hear: feet washed → “clean” → “not all” → **unresolved contradiction**.

2. **"He that eateth bread with me hath lifted up his heel against me."** — Archaic idiom, zero gloss. **"And He quoted the old word for betrayal, from Scripture"** tells us it’s *about* betrayal but not *what it means*. A stranger may picture a literal heel, not treachery.

3. **Sop paragraph** — **"He handed Judas the sop — a piece of dipped bread. 'That thou doest, do quickly.' Some at the table thought he had gone to buy food."** Restages plot the devil/betrayal line already established. On one pass it reads like Jesus sending a disciple on an errand, not releasing a traitor.

4. **"like that"** in **"He knows your heart, every corner of it, like that."** — Like *what*? Judas’s known betrayal? The foot-washing? Both? The antecedent is fuzzy on first hearing.

### Grace-anchored conviction
**Pass**, with one watch-point.

- **"Jesus did not wash those feet because Judas earned it."** — Explicit anti-works. Good.
- **"bring Him your worst"** — Invitation, not fear or gain-loss. Acceptable.

Watch-point: **"bring Him your worst"** can drift toward performance (“do the vulnerability work”) unless the piece keeps grace as *His* initiative — which the washing scene supports, but the close doesn’t quite nail.

### Freshness = faithful depth
**Pass on the entry point.** **"Judas walked out into the night on feet Jesus had washed."** is a real John 13 irony — feet washed before departure, devil already in the heart before the basin. Faithful, not clickbait.

**Fail on follow-through.** The episode’s freshest textual hook — clean / not clean — is quoted and abandoned. That’s depth left on the table.

Middle paragraph 5 (sop / grocery errand) is **plot detour**, not landing fuel. The heel quote is **ornament**, not argument. Thread coherence is weakened, not broken — but the unused hinge is a real structural flaw.

**"Judas walked out into the night on feet Jesus had washed."** — Concrete, ironic, non-template. A stranger may not know Judas, but “walked into the night on feet someone had just washed” still stops the scroll. Not a 9–10 (name recognition helps; no spoken question/hook cadence).

### LANDING SCORE: **6/10**
**"He knows your heart, every corner of it, like that. He has not put the towel down — bring Him your worst."**

- **"He knows your heart, every corner of it"** — portable; could close many “God sees you” pieces unchanged.
- **"bring Him your worst"** — ties to **"knows the worst"** one sentence earlier. Better than a stock “come to Jesus.”
- **"He has not put the towel down"** — best line; John 13-specific.

**Why it caps at 6:** Judas already got the full wash; Jesus *did* put the towel down on those feet. The close asks the listener to respond to an offer Judas fully received and still rejected — without one plain sentence separating **love offered** from **heart received**. Under the rubric (<8 = REVISE).

### Additional findings

- **"He washed them knowing Judas would betray Him anyway."** — Supported by 13:11 (Jesus knew who would betray him). Not invented motive. Safer than an earlier “hoping he’d change” draft.
- **Chronology** — Correct: betrayal in the heart (13:2) → washing → departure (13:30).
- **Global-audience rule** — **"lifted up his heel"** and **"ye are clean, but not all"** are load-bearing but not paraphrased in plain English. Accessibility fail on the episode’s own hinge word.

---

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Resolve **"ye are clean, but not all."** in one plain sentence: Judas was washed and still not clean — outer act ≠ inner heart — so the title thread actually lands (13:11’s **"Ye are not all clean"** is the natural payline).
2. Repair the close against the Judas counterexample: keep **"He has not put the towel down"** but clarify that Judas received the wash and still walked out — the invitation is to *receive Him*, not merely to be touched by an act that did not save the betrayer.
3. Cut or gloss the middle clutter: either drop the sop/grocery beat or tie **"That thou doest, do quickly"** to release into **"and it was night"**; add one plain word before/after **"lifted up his heel"** so a first-time listener hears “betrayed,” not a literal foot.
