# Independent review — codex (OK, 45s)

The narration has a strong visual spine, but its central application overreaches the text and creates doctrinal ambiguity.

- **Hook: 8/10.** “Judas walked out into the night on feet Jesus had washed” is vivid, concise, and rooted in John 13. It creates immediate tension without using a template opening.

- **KJV accuracy:** The quoted wording is verbatim KJV. However, “Then He told them, ‘ye are clean, but not all’” silently omits the preceding KJV word “and.” The words inside the quotation are unchanged, so it technically satisfies the stated quotation rule, but the presentation implies a complete quotation when it is only an excerpt.

- **Imprecise explanation:** “He quoted the old word for betrayal” is unclear and potentially misleading. “Lifted up his heel” is not an old vocabulary word meaning betrayal; it is an image from Psalm 41:9 that Jesus identifies with Scripture being fulfilled. A first-time listener will not understand what “the old word” means.

- **Unsupported generalization:** “That is what His love does when it already knows the worst” moves too quickly from a unique action in the passion narrative to a universal definition of Christ’s love. The foot-washing displays humble, knowing love, but the same chapter also distinguishes Judas from the clean disciples: “ye are clean, but not all.” The narration notices that distinction and then effectively dissolves it.

- **Grace becomes ambiguous:** “He has not put the towel down” suggests that Jesus’ present response to sin is simply continued washing regardless of repentance or faith. John 13 does not use the towel as an open-ended promise that Christ keeps cleansing an unrepentant betrayer. Judas received the outward washing yet was explicitly “not” clean. The script needs to preserve that crucial distinction.

- **The invitation lacks the gospel mechanism:** “bring Him your worst” is emotionally attractive but undefined. Bring it how—through confession, repentance, and faith in Christ? Without that anchor, the line can imply that disclosure itself produces cleansing. Grace should invite the sinner to Christ without implying automatic cleansing apart from faith.

- **Landing: 7/10.** The closing does grow from this piece’s towel image, so it is not a stock “come to Jesus” ending. Nevertheless, “He has not put the towel down” lands on a claim the passage does not establish and confuses offered grace with received cleansing. Because the landing is under 8, the required verdict is REVISE.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Replace “He has not put the towel down” with an invitation grounded in Christ’s cleansing through repentance and faith, while preserving “but not all.”
2. Correct the misleading explanation “the old word for betrayal” by identifying the lifted heel as the scriptural image Jesus says is fulfilled.
3. Qualify “That is what His love does” so Judas’s outward foot-washing is not treated as proof of automatic spiritual cleansing.
