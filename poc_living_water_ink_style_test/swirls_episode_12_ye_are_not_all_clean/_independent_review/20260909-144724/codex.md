# Independent review — codex (OK, 42s)

HOOK: 9/10. “Judas walked out into the night on feet Jesus had washed” is immediate, vivid, and text-rooted.

LANDING: 6/10. “He has not put the towel down” echoes the hook’s image, but it is not actually a CTA. It also leaves the application dangerously undefined: does the towel signify common grace, cleansing, forgiveness, or salvation? Applied directly to “your heart,” it can sound like unconditional assurance without repentance or faith.

Specific findings:

- All words inside quotation marks match the KJV wording. The quotations are excerpted from John 13:2, 10, 18, 27, and 30; no altered word is apparent.
- “He washed them knowing he wouldn’t” is unclear on first hearing. “Them” means Judas’s feet, while “he” means Judas, forcing the listener to untangle two antecedents. More importantly, “wouldn’t change” is the script’s interpretation, not John’s wording.
- “That is what His love does when it already knows the worst” makes a broad doctrinal principle from Jesus washing Judas’s feet. Christ’s love toward His enemy is biblical, but this formulation can blur the distinction between loving service and saving, cleansing grace.
- “He knows your heart, every corner of it, like that” abruptly swaps the thread from Judas and enemy-love to the listener’s hidden sin. The connection is understandable, but undeveloped.
- “He has not put the towel down” risks implying that Judas received—or the unrepentant listener presently receives—the cleansing symbolized elsewhere in the passage. Jesus explicitly says, “ye are clean, but not all,” so the narration must preserve that distinction.
- “He that eateth bread with me hath lifted up his heel against me” will be opaque to a listener with no Bible knowledge. The script explains “sop” but not the more important heel imagery: intimate fellowship answered with betrayal.
- The close offers comfort without a gospel-shaped invitation. It neither names repentance and faith nor explains how Christ’s knowing love leads the listener to Him. Because the ending is declarative rather than invitational, it does not fulfill the requested CTA function.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Clarify the towel image so loving service is not confused with saving cleansing or unconditional assurance.
2. Replace the vague “wouldn’t change” claim and explain the lifted-heel betrayal imagery.
3. Build an actual grace-anchored CTA from the washed-feet/night image while preserving the passage’s “not all” distinction.
