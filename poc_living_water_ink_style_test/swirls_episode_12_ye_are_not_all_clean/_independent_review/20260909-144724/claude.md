## Independent Adversarial Review — Narration ("Judas / Feet Jesus Had Washed")

**KJV verbatim check** — all five quoted fragments checked word-for-word against John 13:
- "put into the heart of Judas Iscariot, Simon's son, to betray him" — matches v2. ✓
- "ye are clean, but not all." — matches v10 (fragment, fine). ✓
- "He that eateth bread with me hath lifted up his heel against me." — matches v18. ✓
- "That thou doest, do quickly." — matches v27. ✓
- "went immediately out: and it was night." — matches v30. ✓

No altered words found. This is clean sourcing — better than most drafts I see.

**Doctrinal soundness — one real overreach.** The line *"He washed them knowing he wouldn't [change]"* goes past what John 13 itself states. The text supports Jesus foreknowing the **act** of betrayal ("the devil having now put into the heart of Judas... to betray him," v2). It does not, in this pericope, state Jesus had settled that Judas's heart was permanently unchangeable — that's an editorial leap the narration makes on its own, not something in quotation marks but stated as narrated fact. It's defensible by cross-reference (John 6:70-71, "one of you is a devil"; John 17:12, "son of perdition") but those aren't cited here, so as written it reads like an assertion the text doesn't quite carry standing alone. Not invented doctrine, not contrarian — just a precision gap between "foreknew the betrayal" and "foreknew he'd never change."

**Grace-anchored conviction** — clean. No fear, no gain/loss, no self-interest framing anywhere.

**Clarity on first hearing** — passes. A newcomer gets "Judas," "devil," "betray" established within the first two lines; the foot-washing custom is shown, not assumed.

**Thread spine** — single thread, no swap: washed feet → foreknown betrayal → prophecy fulfilled → departure into the dark → applied back to "you" via the same towel image. Well held.

**Freshness lever** — genuinely an overlooked-detail angle (the literal feet Jesus washed are the feet that walk out to betray Him), not a contrarian or clickbait reading. Legitimate.

**HOOK SCORE: 8/10.** "Judas walked out into the night on feet Jesus had washed" is not a template opener, it's a concrete, visual, ironic image that only makes sense once you keep watching — real scroll-stopping tension.

**LANDING SCORE: 8/10, but flagging a completeness gap.** "He has not put the towel down" is genuinely earned — piece-specific, could not close a different narration, passes the swap-test cleanly. But it's an *affirmation*, not an *invitation*. Per this project's own stated model (CTA-to-Jesus, not the non-preachy Attenborough model), the viewer is told a true thing about Jesus and never told what to do with it — no "let Him," "kneel," "ask Him," nothing that completes the CTA arc using the towel image itself. It reads as a beautiful stopping point, not quite a call.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Rework "He washed them knowing he wouldn't [change]" — either ground it explicitly in the text's own claim (Jesus foreknew the *act* of betrayal, per v2/v18) rather than asserting an unchangeable heart, or if keeping the stronger claim, anchor it with a supporting cross-reference the writer can defend under panel questioning.
2. Extend the landing one clause so it actually invites a response using the towel image (e.g., something that turns "He has not put the towel down" into an active offer to the listener), not just a statement of fact about Jesus.
3. Sanity-check pacing: five separate KJV quotes strung through one ~60s piece is dense — confirm at synth that they don't rush together and lose the weight of "and it was night."
