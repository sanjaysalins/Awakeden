### Landing — 6
Exact last sentence: **“He has not put the towel down.”**

Not a banned **“Come to Jesus.”** The towel is from this room. That is why this is not capped at 5.

It still does not land in the bones.

- It could close any other foot-washing / servant-king short unchanged. Portability test fails at the *episode* level even if it is not a stock closer.
- It restates paragraph 2 (**“dried their feet with the towel”**). It is not a last reveal.
- It is a statement about Jesus, not an invitation. No receive / stay / be made clean / have part with Him.
- John 13:12: after the washing He **takes His garments and sits down again**. In the scene, the towel *is* put down. The closer fights the next beat of the text it just used.
- **“like that”** in **“He knows your heart, every corner of it, like that”** points at Judas — the man the script just said **“wouldn't”** change, who then **“went immediately out: and it was night.”** Mapping the listener onto *that* heart, then offering the towel as comfort, is the wrong analogue. Judas had the towel and still walked into night.

The cross never arrives. The towel is left to do the gospel’s job. John 13:8 is sitting unused: **“If I wash thee not, thou hast no part with me.”** That is the chapter’s actual CTA image. This landing will not use it.

### Doctrine — orthodox instinct, wrong mechanism
Jesus knowing the betrayal is in the text: **“For he knew who should betray him”** (John 13:11). Enemy-love is orthodox (Romans 5:8). This is not clickbait Judas-revisionism.

**“Jesus did not wash those feet because Judas was going to change. He washed them knowing he wouldn't.”**  
That is invented motive. John gives Jesus’ own reasons: the bath/feet split and **“ye are clean, but not all”** (13:10–11), **“If I wash thee not, thou hast no part with me”** (13:8), and **“I have given you an example”** (13:15). The script replaces those with a controlling claim John never makes: He washed *because* Judas would not turn.

**“Wouldn't”** is also the wrong verb. Judas later **“repented himself”** (Matthew 27:3) — remorse, not saving faith — then died. The text warrants **would betray**, not an undefined **wouldn't change**.

Then the close treats that same love as if it were already the listener’s cleansing. John refuses that collapse: **“ye are clean, but not all.”** Love offered, wet feet, and being clean are three different things in this chapter. The landing fuses them. A listener can hear: He knows the worst and the towel is still in His hand — so I am already in. The verse the script quoted will not let that stand.

### Clarity on first hearing
**“ye are clean, but not all.”** Raised, never unpacked. Who is not clean? How, if the feet were just washed? A stranger hears a contradiction the script never resolves. John 13:11 is the explainer — **“therefore said he, Ye are not all clean”** — and it is missing. This is the episode’s title line. It is treated as garnish.

**“lifted up his heel against me.”** Unexplained KJV kick-idiom. Quoted, then abandoned. A cold ear may picture a foot, not betrayal. It does no work the next line cannot do without it. Global-audience rule: archaic KJV may pass once; it must not sit load-bearing and unglossed. Here it sits dead in the middle.

**“He handed Judas the sop — a piece of dipped bread. ‘That thou doest, do quickly.’”**  
The sop gloss is the right patch. **“Do quickly”** is still the problem. On first hearing it sounds like Jesus ordering the betrayal. 13:27’s **“And after the sop Satan entered into him”** is the beat that blocks that reading. It is missing. **“Then said Jesus unto him”** is also missing, so the quote has no speaker tag in the prose.

**“Some at the table thought he had gone to buy food.”** True compression of 13:29. It does not serve the towel thread. It burns time.

Cold-open on the night walk, flashback, then he walks out again. The hook already spent the night. The second exit is a repeat, not a turn.

### Grace / freshness / spine
No fear, no gain/loss, no **“you’ll be Judas if you don’t.”** **“He did not wash those feet because Judas was going to change”** correctly blocks works. That part holds. The Spirit is not replaced with a threat. The script still does not invite. It only informs: He knows you; towel still in hand.

Freshness: **washed feet + night** is the real textual edge. **“Love that already knows the worst”** is worn Maundy Thursday homiletics. The unused edge is still 13:8 / 13:10: **part with me**, bath then feet, **clean** vs **not all**.

Spine starts as: wet feet, then out into the night.  
Middle opens a **clean / not all** puzzle and a heel-quote, then drops both.  
Close is: He knows you like Judas; towel still in hand.  
That is a partial thread-swap. Judas’s story is the one who walks *out*. The CTA is a leftover prop talking to the empty chair. The title thread (**not all clean**) never reaches the last sentence.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Rebuild the last two sentences off John 13’s real split (love offered / wet feet / **“not all”** clean). Do not map the listener onto Judas-who-left as if **“He has not put the towel down”** were already salvation; name what must be received (cleanness / part with Him), and make the line un-portable.
2. Kill **“He washed them knowing he wouldn't.”** John says He knew who would *betray* Him, not that He washed because Judas would never change. Do not invent the motive.
3. Make the first hearing work: who Judas is in the first breath; drop or gloss **“lifted up his heel”**; unpack or cut **“ye are clean, but not all”** (use 13:11 if you keep it); do not let **“do quickly”** sound like a commission.
