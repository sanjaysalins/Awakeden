**Doctrinal soundness — orthodox claim, sloppy use**

The claim “Judas too” had his feet washed is a fair reading of John 13:5 + 13:30 (he is still at the table). “Ye are not all clean” because of the betrayer is the text.

The problem is how the two wash-sayings sit next to each other with no plain distinction:

- To Peter: “If I wash thee not, thou hast no part with me.”
- Then: “All of them had their feet washed that night. Judas too.”
- Then: “Judas had his feet washed, and was never clean every whit.”

On first hearing that is a contradiction. Jesus just tied the wash to “part with me.” Judas got the wash. Judas is still not clean. The text itself splits the *bath* (“He that is washed… is clean every whit”) from the *feet*. This script never says that in living speech. It quotes 13:10 and hopes the ear will parse “needeth not save to wash his feet.” It will not.

“Never clean every whit” as “Judas was never His” is a standard evangelical reading (13:10–11; 17:12). Not invented. Not clickbait. It is just not *shown*.

**Clarity on first hearing — fails**

1. **Pronoun crash.** Last words of the wash beat: “Judas too.” Next line: “He was ‘troubled in spirit.’” In John 13:21 that “he” is Jesus. Here it attaches to Judas.
2. **Quote dump, not a point.** Paragraph 2 is almost all citation. “clean every whit,” “needeth not save,” “sop,” “lifted up his heel” — zero-Bible ear cannot hold this.
3. **Thread split.** Hook and close are *washed feet / not clean*. The middle is a second story: sop, Satan entered, “give something to the poor,” “The sop was how He marked him.” That is chapter recap, not one spine.
4. **Logic slip at the CTA.** Everyone in the room had feet washed, including the eleven who *were* “clean.” Judas’s mark is not the basin. It is that he was “not all clean.” “If your feet have been washed too, and nothing more” treats the shared outer wash as the Judas-condition. That is the wrong lever.
5. **Length.** ~260 words against a 135–165 budget. This is a chapter walk-through with a punchline taped on.

**Grace-anchored conviction — borderline**

No hell threat, no “you’ll lose this if.” Still: the close analogizes the listener to Judas. “If your feet have been washed too, and nothing more” is “you might be the fake in the room.” That is pressure by identification, then a vague “ask.” The Spirit can convict through Judas. The script should invite to *Christ’s* wash, not leave the hearer as Judas-with-a-homework.

**Freshness**

“Jesus washed Judas’s feet” is a pulpit commonplace, not a new cut in the text. The actual unused edge is Jesus’s own split: bathed vs. feet vs. “not all.” The script quotes that edge and never turns it.

**One thread**

Promised: night + washed feet.  
Delivered: full Upper Room recap (Peter, sop, Satan, the bag, the poor).  
Closed: basin / clean every whit.  
The sop-and-night material does no work in the CTA. Cut it or the spine is two pieces glued together.

**Gospel landing**

Charter: every short ends pointing to Jesus, a Person, not a program. Last sentence does not say Jesus, cross, “part with me,” or “I wash thee.” “Ask for what Judas never had” could be prayer, ritual, or self-repair. That is not a CTA-to-Jesus.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Rewrite the last line so it names the thing Judas never had in this scene’s words — “part with me” / being “clean every whit” — and puts that wash in Jesus’s hands. No riddle. No unnamed “ask.”
2. Keep one spine: outer feet vs. the bath that makes a person His. Say that split in one plain sentence so “If I wash thee not” and “Judas had his feet washed” do not collide. Cut the sop/Satan/poor recap.
3. Fix the hook for a stranger (betrayer, not just “Judas”), fix “He was troubled in spirit” so it cannot mean Judas, and cut to the 135–165 word budget.
