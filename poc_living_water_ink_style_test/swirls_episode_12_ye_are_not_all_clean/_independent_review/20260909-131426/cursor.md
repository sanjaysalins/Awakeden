## Independent adversarial review — narration (John 13 / Judas foot-washing)

---

### Doctrinal soundness

**Mostly orthodox; the application still wobbles.**

The body stays inside John 13: betrayal foreknown, foot-washing, *“Ye are not all clean,”* the sop, Judas sent out into night. No contrarian Judas rehabilitation, no invented gospel.

**The Judas paradox is still under-explained.** The hook locks in: washed by Jesus, still unclean, still left. You now quote 13:10 — *“He that is washed needeth not save to wash his feet, but is clean every whit”* — which is the right verse. But you never **narrate** what Jesus means by *“washed”* there (prior full bathing) vs. what Judas actually received (disciples’ **feet** washed). On a cold ear, that sounds like Jesus contradicting Himself: *“he that is washed is clean every whit”* → *“Judas had his feet washed, and was never clean every whit.”* The exegesis is defensible; the **plain-English bridge is missing**.

**Peter’s strongest line is imported and dropped.** *“If I wash thee not, thou hast no part with me”* is the chapter’s clearest “belonging to Christ” language. The close never ties *“part with me”* to *“ask for what Judas never had.”* Two metaphors (feet / cleanness / part-with-Christ) run in parallel without merging.

**Minor overlay:** *“He knew, and served him anyway”* and *“The sop was how He marked him”* are fair readings, but *“marked”* is narrator vocabulary, not the text’s — acceptable, not doctrinally wrong.

### Clarity on first hearing

**Two hard stumbles; one structural.**

1. **Pronoun crash after paragraph 2.** Paragraph 2 ends: *“Judas too.”* Paragraph 3 opens: *“He was ‘troubled in spirit.’”* On audio, **He** attaches to Judas for a beat. The next line — *“He dips a piece of bread — a sop — and gives it to Judas”* — only clears the confusion late. That is a first-hearing logic trip, not a clever conceit.

2. **Two Satans, no bridge.** *“the devil had already ‘put into the heart…’”* then *“And after the sop Satan entered into him.”* A zero-Bible listener hears contradiction, not progressive hardening.

3. ***“That thou doest, do quickly”*** without frame sounds like Jesus **ordering** betrayal, not releasing a man already committed.

4. **Landing noun is undefined.** *“ask for what Judas never had”* — what? Full bathing? *“Part with me”*? Regeneration? The middle quotes the answer’s vocabulary but never translates *“clean every whit”* into one plain thing Judas lacked.

5. **Hook assumes literacy.** *“Judas”* is never identified; foot-washing’s humiliation/servant meaning is never stated — the irony is pre-loaded, not built.

### Grace-anchored conviction

**Pass.** No fear, no gain/loss, no works ladder. *“ask for”* keeps agency on invitation, not scrubbing. *“He knew, and served him anyway”* is grace-shaped, not condemnation-first.

**Caution:** *“If your feet have been washed too, and nothing more”* can feel like a Judas-comparison to churchgoers — borderline sharp — but it stops at *“ask,”* not *“or else.”*

### Freshness = faithful depth

**Pass on freshness; partial fail on payoff.**

*“Judas walked out into the night on feet Jesus had washed”* and *“The basin was still in the room”* are text-native, surprising about the **scene**, orthodox in claim. Good.

The freshness dies slightly at the close because the piece **names the paradox** (*feet washed / never clean every whit*) but **does not cash it** in plain speech after quoting 13:10 — the listener is told to *“ask”* without being told **what** or **of whom**.

### One thread spine (hook → middle → CTA)

**Mostly one thread:** washed feet → not all clean → sop → night exit → basin still there → you.

**Frays at the landing:** Hook asks *why washing didn’t save Judas.* Middle supplies the text’s answer (13:10–11) but doesn’t explain it. CTA pivots to *“ask for what Judas never had”* without naming the difference Judas proves. Partial swap from **tragedy explained** to **generic petition**.

### HOOK SCORE: **7/10**

*“Judas walked out into the night on feet Jesus had washed.”* — Concrete, non-template, scroll-worthy irony. Not 8+ because: (a) **Judas** assumes cultural knowledge; (b) John never explicitly says Jesus washed **Judas’s** feet — strong inference from *“the disciples’ feet”* while Judas is still present, but sold as cinematic certainty; (c) foot-washing’s weight is unexplained for cold ears.

### LANDING SCORE: **6/10**

*“The basin was still in the room. Judas had his feet washed, and was never clean every whit. If your feet have been washed too, and nothing more, ask for what Judas never had.”*

**Earned:** basin, *“clean every whit,”* Judas counterexample, feet motif.

**Why it caps at 6:**
- **What to ask for is unnamed** — the close’s verb is *“ask”* but the object is a blank.
- **No Christward pointer** — Jesus disappears from the final beat; *“ask”* has no *“of Him”* / *“for His washing”* anchor.
- **Could this close another piece?** Partially yes — any “outer religion vs. inner cleanness” short could end on *“if you only have the ritual, ask for what you still lack.”* Not a cap-5 stock *“Come to Jesus,”* but not bone-deep to **this** hook’s tragedy either.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. After quoting 13:10, add one plain narrator sentence distinguishing Jesus’s **“washed”** (already clean every whit) from **feet** washed at the basin — so *“Judas had his feet washed, and was never clean every whit”* reads as faithfulness, not contradiction.
2. Rebuild the final line from the text’s own verbs: name what Judas lacked (*“part with me”* / the full washing Peter asked for / cleanness every whit) and **who** to ask — so *“ask for what Judas never had”* is not a blank invitation.
3. Fix the audio pronoun crash: paragraph 3 must open with **Jesus** (not *“He”* right after *“Judas too”*), and add one cold-ear frame so *“That thou doest, do quickly”* reads as release, not command to betray.
