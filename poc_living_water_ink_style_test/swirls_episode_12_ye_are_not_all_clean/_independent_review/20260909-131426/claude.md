## Independent Adversarial Review — "Ye Are Not All Clean" (John 13, foot-washing / Judas)

**KJV verbatim check** — I traced every quoted fragment against John 13 word-for-word. All check out as exact substrings (elisions only, no substitutions): v.2, v.4, v.5, v.8, v.10 (full verse, exact), v.11, v.18, v.21, v.27 (both clauses), v.28, v.29, v.30. No KJV violation found — this is a clean pass on the hardest gate.

**Doctrinal soundness** — Sound. The core claim (physical washing ≠ inward cleanness; Judas received the ritual, not the reality) is directly supported by v.10-11 ("ye are clean, but not all" / "he knew who should betray him"). Not a contrarian or invented reading. No fear/gain-loss framing in the CTA — "ask for what Judas never had" is an invitation, not a threat.

**Real defect — pronoun ambiguity breaks first-hearing clarity:**
> "All of them had their feet washed that night. Judas too. **He was** 'troubled in spirit.'"

The nearest antecedent to "He" is *Judas* (the immediately preceding subject), but the scripture being quoted (John 13:21) is about **Jesus** being troubled in spirit at the prospect of betrayal — not Judas's inner state. A listener with zero Bible background will naturally read this as "Judas was troubled in spirit," which quietly swaps the doctrinal point (Jesus's grieved foreknowledge) for a different one (Judas's guilt/anxiety). This is exactly the "logic trick on first hearing" the brief asks me to catch — it's not a matter of taste, it changes who the sentence is about.

This same pronoun-bouncing recurs (more mildly) two sentences later: "Some thought **he** had gone to 'give something to the poor.' **He** knew, and served **him** anyway" — three pronouns, two different referents (Judas, Jesus, Judas), in one breath. Individually recoverable from context, but stacked on top of the v.21 misattribution it compounds the load.

**Landing / CTA:**
> "If your feet have been washed too, and nothing more, ask for what Judas never had."

This is genuinely built from the piece's own image (basin, feet washed, "clean every whit") — not a swappable stock closer ("come to Jesus" could not be pasted in here and mean the same thing). That's a real strength. But it never names who to ask — a zero-background listener has to infer, unaided, that "ask" = pray to Jesus specifically for salvation/regeneration. Given the piece already avoids template CTA language (correctly, per the freshness rule), the fix is a half-clause naming Jesus, not a return to a generic phrase.

**SCORE THE HOOK: 8/10** — "Judas walked out into the night on feet Jesus had washed" is a real, specific, ironic image, not a template open. Earns the stop.

**SCORE THE LANDING: 7/10** — piece-specific imagery, not a stock closer, but the unnamed "ask [whom]" leaves the final ask underspecified for a cold listener. Under 8.
