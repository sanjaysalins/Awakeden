Judas walked out into the night on feet Jesus had washed.

John says the devil had already "put into the heart of Judas Iscariot, Simon's son, to betray him." Jesus knew. He "riseth from supper, and laid aside his garments; and took a towel, and girded himself," and "began to wash the disciples' feet." Peter refuses. Jesus tells him, "If I wash thee not, thou hast no part with me," then explains: "He that is washed needeth not save to wash his feet, but is clean every whit: and ye are clean, but not all." John says why: "he knew who should betray him; therefore said he, Ye are not all clean." All of them had their feet washed that night. Judas too.

He was "troubled in spirit." There is a scripture for it: "He that eateth bread with me hath lifted up his heel against me." Betrayal, plain as that. He dips a piece of bread — a sop — and gives it to Judas. "And after the sop Satan entered into him." Jesus says, "That thou doest, do quickly."

John says "no man at the table knew" what those words meant. Some thought he had gone to "give something to the poor." He knew, and served him anyway. The sop was how He marked him. Judas "went immediately out: and it was night."

The basin was still in the room. Judas had his feet washed, and was never clean every whit. If your feet have been washed too, and nothing more, ask for what Judas never had.
