## Independent Adversarial Review — "Ye Are Not All Clean" narration

**Doctrinal soundness:** The washing/clean distinction ("A washing is something Jesus does to you. Clean is something only He can make true in you.") is a legitimate exegetical read of John 13:10-11 — not invented or contrarian. No fear/gain framing detected.

**KJV verbatim check** — I checked all four quoted fragments against KJV John 13:
- "put into the heart of Judas Iscariot, Simon's son, to betray him" — matches John 13:2 exactly. ✅
- "ye are clean, but not all" — matches John 13:10 exactly. ✅
- "That thou doest, do quickly" — matches John 13:27 exactly. ✅
- "went immediately out: and it was night" — matches John 13:30 exactly. ✅

**Factual/narrative error (real problem):** "He gave Judas the bread that told the others who it was, and let him go." This directly contradicts the text it's drawn from. John 13:28-29 (KJV) states plainly: **"Now no man at the table knew for what intent he spake this unto him."** The sop-sign was revealed privately to "the disciple whom Jesus loved" (v.23-26) — the other disciples did NOT know, and even guessed a totally different reason (Judas going to buy feast supplies or give to the poor, v.29). Saying the bread "told the others who it was" is not a minor compression, it inverts a detail the Gospel goes out of its way to state. This needs a rewrite — something like "He gave Judas the bread that marked him — quietly, to one friend alone — and let him go."

**Clarity on first hearing:** The washing-vs-clean turn is explained well enough for a cold listener (the two-sentence gloss carries it). No logic tricks or self-contradiction otherwise.

**Thread spine:** Hook → middle → landing stays on one image (washed feet / basin / clean) throughout. No thread-swapping.

**HOOK SCORE: 8/10** — "Judas walked out into the night on feet Jesus had washed" is concrete, specific to this text, and not a template opener (no "Did you know/Imagine/What if"). Real tension in the juxtaposition.

**LANDING SCORE: 5/10** — "The basin was still in the room. Whatever is only washed on the outside, Jesus can still make clean, all the way through." is a strong, earned callback. But it's undercut by the final two words: **"Ask Him."** Apply the test in the brief directly: could this exact final sentence end a different piece? Yes — "Ask Him" is functionally identical to "come to Jesus" / "turn to Him" / "look to Jesus." It's a two-word stock tag bolted onto an otherwise-earned image, not a CTA built from this piece's own verb or noun (the piece's own vocabulary is "wash"/"clean" — the landing should call the listener to be made CLEAN, not to generically "ask"). This also matches an open item already logged in project memory for this exact episode (landing-line/CTA exemption undecided) — worth flagging that this draft has NOT resolved that outstanding question.

VERDICT: REVISE

1. Fix the factual error: Judas's identity was NOT revealed to "the others" at the table — John 13:28 says explicitly no one at the table understood. Rewrite that line so it doesn't contradict the passage.
