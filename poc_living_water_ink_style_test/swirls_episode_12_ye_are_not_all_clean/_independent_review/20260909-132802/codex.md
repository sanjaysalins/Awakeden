# Independent review — codex (OK, 44s)

The piece has a strong image, but it needs revision because the landing is generic and one claim misstates the scene.

- Doctrinal soundness: “A washing is something Jesus does to you. Clean is something only He can make true in you” creates an unclear distinction. Both washing and cleansing are presented as Christ’s actions, so the contrast sounds profound without clearly explaining the difference between outward washing and inward spiritual cleansing. It also risks making “washing” sound like a distinct saving rite.

- Textual accuracy: “He gave Judas the bread that told the others who it was” is inaccurate. Jesus gave Judas the sop as the sign in answer to the beloved disciple’s question, but John 13:28 says the wider group did not understand why Jesus spoke to Judas. “The others” therefore overstates who received or understood the identification. Use “the sign identifying him” or specify the beloved disciple.

- KJV verbatim: The quoted words themselves match the KJV. “ye are clean, but not all” is an exact excerpt, though the full clause begins “and ye are clean, but not all.” The other quotations are also exact excerpts. Punctuation and capitalization have been adapted, but no quoted biblical word has been altered.

- Clarity: “Judas had one. Not the other” depends on the preceding abstract distinction and may puzzle a first-time listener: he had a washing but not “clean,” even though ordinary washing is precisely what makes something clean. Name the distinction plainly—washed feet versus a heart not cleansed by Christ.

- Grace-anchored conviction: “Whatever is only washed on the outside, Jesus can still make clean” is gracious rather than threatening, but “whatever” treats the person as an object. “Anyone” would be clearer and more personal.

- Thread spine: The basin/washing/cleansing thread remains consistent from the hook through the close. The Judas-identification paragraph briefly diverts into the sop, however, without developing how that action advances the central washing/cleansing contrast.

- Hook score: 9/10. “Judas walked out into the night on feet Jesus had washed” is immediate, concrete, text-rooted, and arresting.

- Landing score: 5/10. “The basin was still in the room” powerfully recalls the central image, but the exact final sentence—“Ask Him.”—could end almost any devotional piece. It abandons the basin, washing, night, and cleansing imagery instead of completing it. Under the stated rule, that stock final sentence caps the landing at 5.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Replace “the bread that told the others” with a textually accurate account of whom the sop identified Judas to.
2. Clarify the outward foot-washing versus inward cleansing distinction without implying a separate saving rite.
3. Replace “Ask Him” with a closing invitation built explicitly from this piece’s basin, washing, or night imagery.
