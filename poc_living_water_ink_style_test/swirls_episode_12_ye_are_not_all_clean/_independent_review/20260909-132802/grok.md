### Landing — 5
Exact last sentence: **“Ask Him.”**

Could that sentence end a different piece? Yes. Almost any invitation piece. Same family as “Come to Jesus / turn to Him / look to Jesus.” Rubric says cap at 5.

The lines before it try to earn it — **“The basin was still in the room. Whatever is only washed on the outside, Jesus can still make clean, all the way through.”** — but:

- **“Ask Him.”** is not a verb this piece’s KJV ever uses.
- **“all the way through”** is narrator gloss. The chapter’s own phrase is **“clean every whit”** (John 13:10), sitting unused next to the quote you did take.
- **“washed on the outside… make clean”** could close a whitewashed-tomb piece as easily as this one.

The machine `STOCK_CLOSERS` list will not catch **“Ask Him.”** That is why this panel exists.

### Doctrinal soundness — orthodox destination, false mechanism

The claim *Judas was near Jesus and still not clean* is evangelical and in the text (John 13:10–11). Not clickbait. Not a contested “hot take.”

The **explanation** is.

**“A washing is something Jesus does to you. Clean is something only He can make true in you. Judas had one. Not the other.”**

John 13:8 will not carry that split: **“If I wash thee not, thou hast no part with me.”** In the scene, the washing Jesus does *is* the part-with-Him act, not an outer rinse that failed to reach the heart. 13:10 then splits **already bathed / only the feet / ye are clean, but not all** — the eleven vs Judas — not “ritual Jesus performs on your skin” vs “inner upgrade He performs if you ask.”

This piece takes Judas’s wet feet, labels that **“a washing,”** and teaches it as insufficient outer religion. That flattens the verse and quietly contradicts 13:8. Homiletically familiar. Exegetically wrong. Not heresy. Not lockable as stated.

**“Then He said what the water alone could not fix”** makes the basin sound like a failed repair attempt. Jesus was not trying to water-fix Judas. He loved them **“unto the end”** (13:1) and named the uncleanness.

### Clarity on first hearing — the conceit is doing the work

Six-word test for the middle: *outer wash ≠ inner clean.* That only exists because the script **lectures** it. **“A washing is something… Clean is something…”** is G8 lecture-voice (“this teaches us”) without those words. Shown would be: wet feet, then **“not all,”** then the door.

**“Judas had one. Not the other.”** forces a tired stranger to track a two-term private glossary in real time. Miss a clause and the line is noise.

**“That thou doest, do quickly”** is framed only as **“let him go.”** On first hearing, Jesus sounds like He is ordering the betrayal. 13:27’s **“after the sop Satan entered into him”** is the beat that stops that reading. It is missing.

Zero-knowledge gaps the hook never fills: who Judas is, why feet, why night, what “clean” means if he was just washed. The script then uses **“clean”** as the load-bearing word.

### Grace-anchored conviction — invitation is thin, and it fights the scene

No hell threat. No gain/loss. No “try harder or else.”

Still not clean:

- **“Whatever is only washed on the outside”** maps the listener onto Judas without saying what **clean** is, or what to ask *for*. That can land as “you might be the fake” rather than Spirit-conviction through Jesus’s own sentence.
- **“Ask Him.”** puts the last verb on the viewer. The chapter’s verb is Jesus’s: He washes, He speaks, He dismisses.
- Hook → night exit is refused grace. Landing → **“the basin was still in the room… Jesus can still make clean”** reopens the story the text just closed. G8.6: the landing must resolve the thread, not reverse it. Grace for the listener is right. Pretending Judas’s basin is still mid-offer is not what 13:30 says.

### Freshness — pulpit-common, and the sharp text is unused

**Jesus washed Judas too** is not a discovery. It is a well-worn evangelical observation. The actual overlooked edge in the passage is still sitting there: **“He that is washed needeth not save to wash his feet, but is clean every whit”** / **“If I wash thee not, thou hast no part with me.”** Quoted **“ye are clean, but not all,”** then narrated away into a simpler outside/inside binary the verse does not teach.

Surprising about a sermon outline. Not surprising about the text.

### Thread spine — holds, then leaks

Promised: washed feet, not clean, night.

Middle mostly keeps it. Then a sop/identify/dismiss beat appears, does false work, and is dropped. The close returns to the basin only. Two objects (basin, bread), one unused. The CTA does not finish **“it was night.”** It undoes it.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Kill stock **“Ask Him.”** Close on this scene’s own verb/image — basin, **“clean,”** **“not all,”** **“it was night”** — so the last sentence cannot end a different piece.
2. Cut **“the bread that told the others who it was.”** John 13:28–29 says the others did not know. Do not invent a reveal the chapter denies.
3. Drop the lecture split **“A washing is something Jesus does to you / Clean is something only He can make true in you.”** It fights John 13:8 and confuses a first hearer. Say plainly, in the open, who Judas is and that wet feet were not the same as being clean — using the chapter’s own **“clean every whit” / “no part with me”** instead of an outside/inside glossary.
