### Doctrinal soundness — orthodox claim, sloppy application

The core insight — outward contact with Jesus ≠ inward cleanness — tracks John 13:10–11 and is not clickbait.

Problems:

1. **"He gave Judas the bread that told the others who it was"** — Overstates John. The sop marks the betrayer for the disciple whom Jesus loved (13:26–28); **"no man at the table knew for what intent he spake this unto him"** (13:28). Others guessed shopping or alms (13:29). This is not a small elision; it misstates who learned what.

2. **"A washing is something Jesus does to you… Judas had one. Not the other."** — Collapses John 13:10’s own split: prior **bath** (*already clean every whit*) vs **foot-washing** (*needeth not save to wash his feet*). In the text, foot-washing for the eleven is what the **already clean** still receive — not failed “outside-only” religion. Judas’s problem is **"not all" clean**, not “got the basin but missed the heart upgrade.” The gloss sounds faithful; it flattens the verse’s mechanics.

3. **"The basin was still in the room. Whatever is only washed on the outside, Jesus can still make clean"** — Repurposes the basin as an open offer to finish what ritual started. Judas had the basin, the word, and the sop — then **"went immediately out: and it was night."** The CTA reads like unfinished business; the scene reads like refused grace. That tension is never named, so the close can sound like “try harder / ask again” rather than the text’s warning.

No heresy — but the middle asserts a paradox the script never teaches from the text’s own vocabulary.

### Clarity on first hearing — **main wound**

1. **Unearned Judas foot-wash.** **"Jesus knew — and washed his feet anyway. Every one of them."** John has Jesus wash **the disciples’** feet while Judas is still at table — fair inference — but the text never singles out Judas’s feet. A cold ear may not connect “every one of them” to Judas specifically.

2. **"That thou doest, do quickly"** with only **"let him go"** as frame sounds, on first hearing, like Jesus **ordering** the betrayal — not releasing a man already decided. John’s irony needs one plain narrator beat.

3. **Washing vs clean without translation.** You quote **"ye are clean, but not all"** then jump to **"Judas had one. Not the other."** Without one plain sentence on bath vs feet (or **"part with me"** from 13:8, which you dropped entirely), a stranger hears: washed → should be clean → wasn’t → contradiction.

4. **Sop thread dead on arrival.** Paragraph 2 introduces bread/sop/exit; paragraph 3 never uses it. The close runs on basin only — minor thread drop.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Fix or cut **"the bread that told the others who it was"** — John says the beloved disciple knew; the table did not. A factual error in a 115-word piece is fatal weight.
2. Rebuild the close from this chapter’s own verbs — **"clean every whit"**, **"not all"**, and/or **"part with me"** — and replace stock **"Ask Him."** with a final sentence that could not close Naaman, a leper story, or another wash piece.
3. After **"ye are clean, but not all,"** add one plain spoken sentence that resolves the bath/feet split so **"Judas had one. Not the other"** is John’s logic, not a narrator trick — and frame **"do quickly"** as release, not commission.
