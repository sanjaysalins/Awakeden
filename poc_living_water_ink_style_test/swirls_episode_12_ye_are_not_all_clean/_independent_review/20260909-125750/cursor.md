### Doctrinal soundness — one serious misread in the middle

The **foot-washing** thread is orthodox: Jesus serves knowing betrayal; *"ye are clean, but not all"* preserves John’s distinction (13:10–11). Good.

The problem is **the sop**, framed as grace parallel to the basin:

> *"He washed those feet anyway. He put the bread in that hand anyway."*

In John 13:26–30 the sop is **not** a second act of tender persistence. Jesus dips it **to mark the betrayer**, Satan enters Judas **after** the sop, and Jesus sends him out: *"That thou doest, do quickly."* Equating foot-washing (servant-love) with sop-giving (handoff to betrayal) **flattens two opposite moves into one sentimental “He kept reaching anyway.”** That is invented emotional doctrine, not text.

The landing then universalizes without resolving Judas’s outcome:

> *"Whatever is in yours, He already knows — and the basin is still out for you too."*

Judas **had** the basin used on him and was explicitly **not clean**. He walked out into night anyway. *"For you too"* without saying what Judas refused — or what *clean* means — invites the wrong inference: *Jesus already served me at the table, so I’m covered*, which is exactly what this passage denies for Judas.

Also dropped without explanation: *"If I wash thee not, thou hast no part with me"* — on first hearing that sounds like conditional exclusion, not invitation, unless Peter’s “not my feet only” beat is supplied.

No contrarian hot take; but **the sop mis-framing is a real fidelity error**, not poetic license.

### Clarity on first hearing — two gaps

1. **"Judas"** is never defined. Zero-Bible listener: *Who? Why should I care that his feet were washed?* The hook assumes cultural literacy.

2. **"Simon's son"** (in the John 13:2 quote) can collide with Simon Peter in a listener’s ear — Judas’s father Simon is not identified.

3. **Basin as metaphor:** *"The basin was still in the room. He hadn't put it away."* → *"the basin is still out for you too."* The jump from historical object to present-tense offer is never bridged in speech. Clever, but the ear must finish the sermon alone.

4. **"sop"** is helpfully glossed (*"a piece of bread — a sop"*). Good.

Edge: *"If I wash thee not, thou hast no part with me"* hangs in the air without the grace pivot Peter gets (13:9–10). Not fear-preaching, but the hardest line in the piece is left uninterpreted.

### Freshness = faithful depth — mixed

**Fresh:** *"Judas walked out into the night on feet Jesus had washed"* and *"The basin was still in the room"* are vivid, text-rooted, non-template.

**Not faithful:** sop-as-grace (above). Also: *"Judas too"* and the hook’s *"feet Jesus had washed"* treat an **inference** (Judas was present at a general foot-washing; John never names his feet) as **cinematic fact** that carries the whole spine. Defensible exegesis; still constructed certainty presented as witnessed detail.

---

### One thread spine — coherent, with one internal contradiction

Hook → washing despite knowledge → hidden betrayal at table → basin still present → personal application. No thread-swap.

But the spine **pulls against itself**: body establishes *washed ≠ clean* and Judas’s exit into night; landing implies the basin offer still fully applies *"for you too"* without saying what Judas lacked or what the listener should do differently. That’s thread tension left unresolved, not a second thread.

### HOOK SCORE: **8/10**

*"Judas walked out into the night on feet Jesus had washed."* — Concrete, ironic, not a template opener. Would stop many scrollers. Not 9–10: requires prior knowledge of Judas, and the washed-feet detail is inference sold as fact.

---

### LANDING SCORE: **6/10**

*"Whatever is in yours, He already knows — and the basin is still out for you too."*

**Pros:** Built from this piece’s basin/towel imagery; not a stock *"come to Jesus"* closer.

**Cons:** Passive — no text-rooted verb of response (Peter’s *"Lord, not my feet only..."*, Jesus’s *"He that is washed... is clean every whit"*). Judas had the basin and left unclean; the close never names that. A different “Jesus knows your hidden sin” piece could end on *"He already knows — and the door is still open"* with minimal change. Doesn’t land in the bones because the passage’s own tension (*not all clean*) is unresolved in the application.

**Under 8 → REVISE required.**

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Stop pairing the sop with foot-washing as twin grace gestures — in John it marks and sends the betrayer out; rewrite *"He put the bread in that hand anyway"* or the middle collapses into sentimental misreading.
2. Repair KJV quotation marks: add **For** (13:11, 13:15), **Now** (13:28), correct **Give** / full 13:29 fragment; don’t present truncated verses as exact quotes.
3. Rebuild the landing from the text’s own *clean / not all clean* distinction — name what Judas had (feet washed) vs what he lacked (clean), and give the listener Peter’s beat or Jesus’s *"clean every whit"* verb so *"basin still out"* means something actionable, not a passive metaphor Judas already received and rejected.
