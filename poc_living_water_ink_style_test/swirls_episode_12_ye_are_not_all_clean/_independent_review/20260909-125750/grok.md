**Doctrine — the close preaches a different text than the hook**

Hook: washed feet walk into the night. Body quotes the title-verse: `"ye are clean, but not all"` / `"Ye are not all clean."` That is John’s own split: the Eleven are clean; the betrayer is not (13:10–11). Then the CTA treats the basin as an open, unused offer: `"the basin is still out for you too."`

Judas, on this script’s own telling, already had the basin. He still went out. `"And after the sop Satan entered into him."` Then `"it was night."` Offering leftover furniture after that beat tells a first-hearer the problem is they have not yet sat down at the bowl. Judas’s problem in this chapter is not an empty basin.

`"Then, to the whole room: 'ye are clean, but not all.'"`  
John 13:10: `"Jesus saith to him"` — to Peter. `"ye"` is plural. The speech-act is not a public turn to the table. Staging it as a room-wide verdict is a false scene.

`"He put the bread in that hand anyway."`  
Stacked with `"He washed those feet anyway"` so the sop reads as a second mercy. 13:26 is how Jesus *marks* the betrayer. 13:27 follows at once. Last-offer hospitality is a homily. It is not what the quoted sentence is doing.

`"The basin was still in the room. He hadn't put it away."`  
Invented prop. 13:12: after the washing He has `"taken his garments, and was set down again."` The piece then builds the entire CTA on a bowl John has already put down.

The last image is the basin, not the Person. The charter wants a CTA to Jesus. This lands on furniture.
