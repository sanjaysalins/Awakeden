## Doctrinal soundness — real grounding error in the close

The body teaches one thing. The CTA teaches the opposite.

- Hook: Judas leaves `"on feet Jesus had washed."`
- Body: `"All of them. Judas too."` Then `"ye are clean, but not all"` / `"Ye are not all clean."`
- Close: `"Let Him wash your feet too."`

In John 13:8, `"If I wash thee not"` is spoken to **Peter**, who is **refusing** the wash. Judas is the man who (on this script’s own telling) **received** it and still went out. Jesus then distinguishes the two in 13:10–11: the eleven are clean; the betrayer is not. Foot-water is not the same as being clean. This draft quotes that distinction, then throws it away.

`"was never only for Peter"` is homiletical overreach. The line **was** to Peter, about Peter’s refusal. Turning it into the Judas-episode CTA tells a first-hearer: the problem is you haven’t sat down at the basin yet. Judas’s problem in this text is not an empty basin. It is a heart the devil had already entered, still sitting in a washed body.

That is not a fresh reading. It is the wrong verse doing the last job.

## Thread spine — swap at the last eight seconds

Spine through the middle: washed feet → known betrayal → unnoticed exit → night.

Then the landing jumps to Peter’s plot: sit, be washed, get your “part.” `"Ye are not all clean"` is used as plot furniture and never paid off. One-thread rule fails at the CTA.

Leftover from an older close: `"The basin was still in the room. He hasn't put it away."` John never locates the basin after 13:12 (`taken his garments, and was set down again`). The line is invented staging, and this new ending does not even cash it. Orphan prop.

Flag these:

- `"no man at the table knew."` — John 13:28 is `"Now no man at the table knew for what intent he spake this unto him."` The period inside the quotes turns a truncated clause into a complete claim. - `"I speak not of you all."` — 13:18 has a **colon**, not a period; the sentence continues.
- `"I have given you an example."` / `"he knew who should betray him; therefore said he, Ye are not all clean."` — lead word `"For"` dropped. Substring, but the second one is framed as `"John says why:"` as if it were the full explanation.

## Clarity — first hearing still trips

- `"The famous line"` is meta-talk. It tells us the line is famous instead of making us feel it.
- `"serving, washed"` is broken English and broken roles. Judas was washed; Jesus served. A zero-Bible ear cannot tell who did what.
- `"He could read it. He could then."` — then *when*? Incomplete on the ear.
- `"A sop is dipped bread"` is a loose gloss (bread dipped in the dish).
- `"girded himself"` is never helped.
- Order smear: `"All of them. Judas too. Peter refuses"` states the whole room as finished, then Peter’s protest. In John, Peter is the first named approach (13:6), during the washing.

## Freshness that is still a logic trick

`"That old word for treachery. Jesus had knelt at that very foot minutes before."`  
Then: `"He washed that heel anyway."`

Naming the idiom (`"old word for treachery"`) is honest. Gluing it to `"that very foot"` / `"that heel"` is the same conceit as before: Psalm 41 / John 13:18’s **heel** is betrayal-speech, not the ankle Jesus towelled. `"knelt"` is not in the chapter. Surprise is coming from wordplay, not from the text.

Same slant: `"He washed that heel anyway. He put the bread in that hand anyway."` Two `"anyway"`s make the sop sound like a second mercy-gesture. In 13:26–27 the sop **marks** the betrayer; `"And after the sop Satan entered into him."` Parallel mercy is a contested soft-read, not what the quoted sentence says.

## Grace-anchored conviction — invitation with a loss-hinge

No hell-threat, no prosperity bait. `"Maybe you have sat at this table for years too"` is better than a flat accusation.

But the close is: quote `"thou hast no part with me"` → `"Sit down. Let Him wash your feet too."` That is loss-condition plus two commands. The Spirit’s own warning to Peter is being used as a last-second squeeze on the listener. After a Judas story, “sit and be washed or you have no part” also sounds like a rite you perform, not a heart Christ must make clean.

The conviction beat still aims only at the long-time table-sitter. An unchurched scroller is not in `"for years too."` Fine if that is the one person — but then the CTA cannot be “come get washed like Peter.” It has to be: you can be in the room, washed, even serving, and still be `"not all clean."`
