## Doctrinal soundness — orthodox frame, one imprecise move, one missed center

No contrarian reading, no invented theology, no clickbait twist. The arc (Jesus serves the betrayer he already knows) is biblically grounded.

**Imprecise / potentially misleading:** `"He that eateth bread with me hath lifted up his heel against me." That old word for treachery. Jesus had knelt at that very foot minutes before."` and later `"He washed that heel anyway."`  
"Lifted up his heel" is a **treacherous-turn idiom** (Psalm 41:9), not a reference to the physical heel Jesus had just towelled. The piece treats the idiom as if it were about the washed foot itself. That is wordplay, not what the text means — and a zero-Bible listener can walk away with the wrong idea.

**Missed doctrinal center:** The piece quotes `"ye are clean, but not all"` / `"Ye are not all clean"` — John’s point that **external washing ≠ inward cleanness** (Judas was washed and still unclean). The landing never touches *clean*; it pivots to Peter’s `"no part with me"` instead. For an episode keyed to "ye are not all clean," that is a substantive miss, not a stylistic one.

**Landing tension with the story just told:** Judas’s feet **were** washed, then he walked out anyway. Closing with `"Let Him wash your feet too"` without acknowledging the clean/not-clean distinction can sound like the narrative contradicts itself on first hearing — as if more foot-washing is the answer to what Judas already received.

## Clarity on first hearing — REVISE-level friction at the pivot

**Works:** `"A sop is dipped bread."` — good zero-knowledge gloss.

**Cryptic:** `"He could read it. He could then."`  
What is *it*? Betrayal? The heart? And *He could then* — could read then, or could wash then? Two telegraphic fragments; a first-time listener will not parse this cleanly.

**Ambiguous "you" pivot:** `"Maybe you have sat at this table for years too — serving, washed, and already, quietly, turning toward the door."`  
- If *you* = the listener: who **served**? The Last Supper scene has Jesus serving; the disciples were served.  
- If *you* ≈ Judas: Judas did not serve or wash others; he was washed.  
The analogy requires insider decoding; it is not transparent for the stated audience.

**Vague referent:** `"No one there could read what was underneath."`  
Underneath what — the table fellowship? Judas’s outward piety? The phrase never names it.

**Past → present jump:** `"The basin was still in the room."` → `"He hasn't put it away."`  
Valid theological move (eternal present Christ), but with no bridge it can sound like Jesus is still physically at the table after Judas left.

## Grace-anchored conviction — mostly PASS, one caution

No fear sells, no gain/loss bargain, no stock "accept or else." `"Sit down. Let Him wash your feet too"` invites rather than threatens.

**Caution:** `"If I wash thee not, thou hast no part with me"` is quoted twice but never unpacked. A naive listener may hear **participation/containment** ("don’t walk out") more than **received cleansing** — especially when the piece has already shown someone who was washed and still walked out.

## Freshness = faithful depth — mixed

**Fresh and faithful:** `"Judas walked out into the night on feet Jesus had washed."` — concrete, text-rooted, non-template.

**Clever but unfaithful:** `"He washed that heel anyway."` — surprise comes from **pun on an idiom**, not from what John 13 actually teaches about cleanness, betrayal, and grace.

Spine holds: washed feet → betrayal at table → basin still present → invitation to be washed.

**Wobble:** Hook and middle build **clean / not all clean**; landing swaps to **wash / part with me** and never closes the cleanness loop. That is thread drift at the most important beat.

**Earned elements:** basin, wash, sit-down echo of `"He sits down"`, Peter-quote callback.
