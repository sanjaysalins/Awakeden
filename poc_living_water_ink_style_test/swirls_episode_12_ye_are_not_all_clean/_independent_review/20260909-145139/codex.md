# Independent review — codex (OK, 42s)

HOOK: 9/10. “Judas walked out into the night on feet Jesus had washed” is immediate, vivid, and text-rooted. One qualification: John 13 strongly implies Judas was present for the washing, but never explicitly says Jesus washed Judas’s feet.

LANDING: 6/10. “He has not put the towel down — let Him finish” reuses the towel image, but its meaning is unclear and potentially misleading. Christ’s atoning work is finished; sanctification is ongoing. Moreover, the narration never establishes what the listener is supposedly preventing Him from “finishing.” The resistance to washing belonged to Peter, not Judas, so the ending quietly switches biblical threads.

Specific findings:

- “That is what His love does when it already knows the worst” overgeneralizes the scene. Jesus knowingly served Judas, but the passage also stresses judgment, betrayal, and Judas’s uncleanness: “ye are clean, but not all.” The narration turns a complex act into a broad maxim without explaining what kind of love is meant.
- “He washed them knowing exactly what Judas would do” is sound. “Jesus did not wash those feet because Judas would change his mind” is plausible, but it attributes a specific rejected motive that Scripture never discusses.
- “He knows your heart, every corner of it, like that” moves too quickly from Christ’s knowledge of Judas to the listener. Because Judas is the controlling comparison, a first-time listener may hear threat or implied identification with the betrayer rather than grace-anchored invitation.
- “Let Him finish” risks making Christ’s work depend upon the listener granting permission. It also conflicts rhetorically with “It is finished” unless explicitly limited to His ongoing sanctifying work.
- The spine changes at the end: the piece develops Jesus serving a knowing betrayer, but the CTA becomes allowing Jesus to complete a washing. That second idea belongs more naturally to Jesus’ exchange with Peter: “If I wash thee not, thou hast no part with me.”
- The quoted wording is KJV-exact as excerpted. However, “John says the devil had already ‘put into the heart…’” blends paraphrase with quotation; KJV reads, “the devil having now put into the heart….” It is not a misquotation inside the quotation marks, but the attribution should not suggest the full wording is verbatim.
- The landing is not stock, so it escapes the stock-closer cap, but doctrinal ambiguity and the Peter/Judas thread-swap keep it below 8.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Replace “let Him finish” with a CTA that preserves Christ’s finished work and grows directly from His knowing, undeserved service.
2. Clarify the theological meaning of Jesus washing Judas without turning the act into an undefined universal maxim about love.
3. Remove the abrupt comparison between the listener’s heart and Judas, or explicitly establish a grace-centered bridge that does not manipulate through fear.
