**Doctrine / the text’s own split**  
John 13:10–11 keeps three things apart: the towel, the wash, and being **clean**. Judas gets the first two. He is the `"not all."` He still `"went immediately out: and it was night."`

This script collapses that. After stating `"Jesus did not wash those feet because Judas would change his mind"` and `"He washed them knowing exactly what Judas would do,"` it closes: `"He has not put the towel down — let Him finish."`

That is not in the scene. In the scene the towel-work on Judas **did** finish. He left anyway. Treating the towel as the still-running saving act is a homiletic swap the pericope contradicts. It also brushes unfinished-atonement: evangelical hearing of `"let Him finish"` is “the washing is incomplete until you cooperate.” The resistance-to-the-wash beat in this chapter is **Peter** (`If I wash thee not, thou hast no part with me`, 13:8) — not Judas. Judas did not refuse the basin. The close quietly changes characters.

No cross. No 13:8. Constitution requires the landing at the gospel, not at a servant-process. `"let Him finish"` is application-as-chore, not the cross.

**Clarity on first hearing**  
- `"ye are clean, but not all."` is never tied to Judas. A listener with zero Bible does not hear who the `"not all"` is.  
- `"lifted up his heel against me"` is an unexplained idiom. It does no work the betrayal line has not already done. On one pass it is noise.  
- `"That thou doest, do quickly."` then `"Some at the table thought he had gone to buy food"` **helps** the errand misread. Betrayal was named once, early; this paragraph restages the disciples’ confusion instead of resolving it.  
- `"He knows your heart, every corner of it, like that."` Like **what** — Judas’s heart, into which the devil had already put betrayal? The listener is mapped onto the man who was washed and still walked into the night, with no warning that feet-wet ≠ clean.

**Self-contradiction (logic, not style)**  
Hook + body: wash completed, Judas still goes.  
CTA: the problem is that the wash is not finished, so `"let Him finish."`  
Judas **did** let the feet-washing finish. It did not make him clean. The close asks the listener to do the thing the antagonist already “did,” as if that were the gospel.

**Grace vs works**  
`"That is what His love does when it already knows the worst"` is grace. `"let Him finish"` is not. It is a listener-task tacked onto a story whose point was that the wash did not depend on Judas changing. Mild works frame on the last three words.

`"ye are clean, but not all"` is quoted and then orphaned — the episode’s actual textual hinge does no landing work.  
Last clause swaps threads: from **undeserved finished service** to **incomplete process you must allow.** That is thread-swap, not a turn.
