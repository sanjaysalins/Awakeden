## Independent Review — "Judas walked out into the night..." (John 13)

**KJV verbatim check** — I traced every quoted phrase against John 13 from memory and each is exact, word-for-word:
- "put into the heart of Judas Iscariot, Simon's son, to betray him" — John 13:2 ✓
- "ye are clean, but not all" — John 13:10 ✓
- "He that eateth bread with me hath lifted up his heel against me" — John 13:18 ✓
- "That thou doest, do quickly" — John 13:27 ✓
- "went immediately out: and it was night" — John 13:30 ✓

No altered words found. Good discipline.

**Doctrine** — Sound. The piece stays inside what the text states plainly (Jesus "knew who should betray him," John 13:11) and doesn't speculate into predestination/foreknowledge debates. The pivot from Judas to the listener ("He knows your heart... let Him finish") is grace-anchored, not fear-based — it's "He loved knowing the worst," not "avoid Judas's fate or else." No gain/loss framing.

**One weak spot worth flagging**: using Judas — someone who received the washing and still walked into darkness — as the bridge to "let Him finish" carries an unstated tension. Judas is the one person in this story who did NOT let Him finish. The CTA doesn't acknowledge that the same knowledge that didn't stop Jesus's love also didn't guarantee Judas's response. It's not a doctrinal error, but a sharper piece would let that tension surface rather than glide past it — right now the implicit warning ("don't be Judas — this time, let Him") is there but never made explicit enough for a first-time listener to catch on one hearing.

**Minor pacing note**: "Some at the table thought he had gone to buy food" (paraphrase of John 13:29, not a quote — accurate enough, though it also drops the passage's alternate reading "give something to the poor") is the one line that doesn't carry the thread. It's texture, not spine — it slightly softens momentum right before the piece's most weighted phrase ("and it was night"). Cutting it would tighten without losing anything load-bearing.

**Thread**: Washing → knowing → betrayal foretold → sop given → exit → towel-as-CTA. Single spine, no swap. Good.

**HOOK SCORE: 8/10** — "Judas walked out into the night on feet Jesus had washed" is not a template opener; it's a concrete, ironic image that requires the listener to lean in to resolve the tension (why does the washing matter?). Doesn't over-explain itself, which is a strength for a cold open.

**LANDING SCORE: 8/10** — "He has not put the towel down — let Him finish" is built from this piece's own object (the towel from vv. 4-5), not a swappable stock phrase like "come to Jesus" — it fails the swap test in the right direction (it could NOT cleanly cap an unrelated piece). Docked one point because the metaphorical leap ("finish" = complete His work in you, not literally wash your feet) asks the listener to do inference work in real time, and because the Judas-tension above is left unresolved rather than used to sharpen the invitation.

VERDICT: PASS
TOP FIXES:
1. Make the Judas-contrast explicit in the landing — he had his feet washed and still walked out; the listener's invitation is the chance to answer differently. One clause would close this gap without adding length.
2. Cut "Some at the table thought he had gone to buy food" — it's accurate but dilutes momentum right before the most weighted line ("and it was night"); the thread doesn't need it.
3. Consider trimming "Iscariot, Simon's son" on first read-through for a zero-context listener — it's KJV-exact and fine to keep, but flag it as the one phrase most likely to cause a half-second parse delay on first hearing.
