## Independent adversarial review

### KJV verbatim

Most quoted strings match KJV substrings in John 13:

- `"put into the heart of Judas Iscariot, Simon's son, to betray him."` — exact fragment of 13:2 ✓  
- `"He that eateth bread with me hath lifted up his heel against me."` — exact 13:18 ✓  
- `"That thou doest, do quickly."` — exact 13:27 ✓  
- `"went immediately out: and it was night."` — exact fragment of 13:30 ✓  

**Problem:** `"ye are clean, but not all."`

Standard KJV John 13:10 reads: *"…and ye are clean, but not all **clean**."* The artifact drops the final **clean**. That may match some verse-boundary splits, but it is not the full, familiar KJV phrase and will fail a strict verbatim gate. John 13:11’s clearer line — *"Ye are not all clean"* — is never used, even though it is the episode’s title thread.

---

### Doctrinal soundness

No contrarian reading. The core claim — Jesus served Judas knowing betrayal was coming — is supportable from John 13:2–3, 13:11, 13:18.

**Unresolved tension:** Judas received that love and still *"went immediately out: and it was night."* The landing never names that outcome. So **"He has not put the towel down"** can sound like proximity to Jesus’s service guarantees hope — but the text’s counterexample (Judas) says otherwise. That is not heresy, but it is an unearned leap: persistent love is shown; Judas’s end is not integrated.

The piece also never engages John 13:8 (*"If I wash thee not, thou hast no part with me"*). Not required, but without it **"He has not put the towel down"** reads as sentimental reassurance rather than gospel invitation.

---

### Clarity on first hearing

**Strong:** Hook and night-exit line are vivid.

**Weak for a zero-Bible listener:**

1. **Who is Judas?** Never plain-labeled as *the betrayer*. Only `"to betray him"` and the Psalm heel line — both opaque on one pass.

2. **"ye are clean, but not all."** Raised, never unpacked. Who is not clean? What does *clean* mean if his feet were just washed? A stranger hears a contradiction the script never resolves.

3. **"He knew as He held that foot"** — singular, intimate. The hook’s feet belong to Judas, but Judas is not named in the middle. **"like that"** in the close assumes the listener already tied foot → Judas → betrayal.

4. **Logic gap:** Physical washing happened → Jesus says not all are clean → Judas leaves → **"That is what His love does when it already knows the worst."** A cold ear hears: *love that knows the worst still didn’t stop the worst.* The application sentence does not close that loop.

---

### Grace-anchored conviction

Mostly grace-shaped: **"Jesus did not wash those feet because Judas was going to change"** blocks works framing well.

**Caution:** Judas is the load-bearing comparison figure. **"He knows your heart, every corner of it, like that"** quietly invites self-comparison to the betrayer before offering comfort. Not full fear/gain-loss framing, but the Spirit-convicts-through-image line walks close to *“Am I Judas?”* without naming faith, reception, or “part with me.”

---

### Freshness / faithful depth

**Fresh:** *"Judas walked out into the night on feet Jesus had washed"* — real textual irony, not a template hook.

**Worn:** *"Jesus washed knowing he wouldn’t change"* / *"love that knows the worst"* — standard Maundy-Thursday homiletics, not a deep cut from the text.

**Unused textual edge still sitting there:** the bath-vs-feet / *"clean every whit"* / *"not all clean"* distinction John actually argues — quoted in passing, then abandoned for a gentler love-knows-you frame.

---

### One thread spine (hook → middle → CTA)

**Hook:** washed feet → night exit (physical wash, spiritual darkness).

**Middle:** devil → knowing → *"ye are clean, but not all"* → betrayal prophecy → sop → night.

**Close:** love knowing the worst → knows your heart → towel still down.

**Thread fracture:** The middle opens a **clean / not clean** puzzle the title promises; the close pivots to **knowing love + towel** and never answers the cleanness question. That is a partial thread swap — from *“why washed but not clean?”* to *“Jesus still knows and serves.”*

---

### Hook score: **8/10**

*"Judas walked out into the night on feet Jesus had washed."* — Concrete, ironic, not a template open. Loses points because *Judas* assumes name recognition and the stakes are not yet plain.

---

### Landing score: **7/10**

**"He has not put the towel down."** — Built from this scene’s towel image; not a stock *“come to Jesus”* closer.

But it could end another foot-washing / servant-king piece. More importantly: it does not land the **clean / not clean** thread the middle raises, and Judas’s exit undercuts the implied promise. Affirmational, not invitational — no clear *receive this* beat. Under the **&lt;8 = REVISE** rule.

VERDICT: REVISE  
TOP FIXES:  
1. Resolve or drop the **"ye are clean, but not all"** thread — either one plain sentence on who is not clean (and fix KJV to **"Ye are not all clean"** or full **"but not all clean"**), or cut the line so hook → knowing love → towel stays one spine.  
2. Bridge Judas’s outcome before the close: Jesus’s towel did not *save* Judas from walking into night — name what the listener is invited to *receive* (not just be known), so **"He has not put the towel down"** is gospel, not sentiment.  
3. One audible line for strangers: who Judas is (*the betrayer*) and what **"lifted up his heel against me"** means, so **"like that"** lands without Upper Room homework.
