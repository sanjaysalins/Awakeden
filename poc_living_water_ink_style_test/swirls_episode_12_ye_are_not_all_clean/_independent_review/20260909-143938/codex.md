# Independent review — codex (OK, 106s)

**Hook: 8/10.** “Judas walked out into the night on feet Jesus had washed” is vivid, immediate, and genuinely arresting. However, its power depends on presenting a strong inference as an explicit fact.

**Landing: 6/10.** “He has not put the towel down” grows from the piece’s central image, so it is not a generic “come to Jesus” closer. But its meaning is doctrinally unclear: Is Jesus still serving, offering cleansing, forgiving, or washing every heart regardless of repentance? The image cannot safely carry all those possibilities.

Specific findings:

- **The central premise is not explicitly stated in John.** John says Jesus “began to wash the disciples’ feet” and indicates Judas remained until later, so Jesus washing Judas’s feet is a reasonable and traditional inference. Nevertheless, the text never individually identifies Judas as having his feet washed. “Judas walked out…on feet Jesus had washed” and “He knew as He held that foot” turn inference into narrated eyewitness detail. “Held that foot” is especially invented.

- **The script invents Jesus’ motive.** “Jesus did not wash those feet because Judas was going to change. He washed them knowing he wouldn’t” claims why Jesus washed Judas and frames the act around Judas’s future response. John gives Jesus’ stated meanings: cleansing, lordly service, and an example for the disciples. The narration substitutes a different controlling interpretation—love shown to someone certain not to change—without textual warrant.

- **“He wouldn’t change” is vague and potentially inaccurate on first hearing.** Judas later “repented himself” in the KJV wording of Matthew 27:3, though that remorse is not presented as saving repentance. The narration needs to specify that Judas would still betray Christ or would not turn to Him in faith, rather than making an undefined absolute claim.

- **The landing blurs the distinction John emphasizes.** “He knows your heart, every corner of it, like that. He has not put the towel down” applies Jesus’ treatment of Judas directly to the listener, but John deliberately says, “ye are clean, but not all.” The closer risks implying unconditional spiritual cleansing merely because Christ knows and loves someone. It needs to preserve the difference between love offered, outward foot-washing, and being spiritually clean.

- **The gospel connection is missing.** The piece moves from Jesus’ kindness to Judas directly into personal reassurance. It never anchors that love in Christ’s approaching cross or invites faith and repentance. Grace is present sentimentally, but not explained evangelically.

- **The heel quotation is opaque for a new listener.** “Hath lifted up his heel against me” is quoted and immediately abandoned. Someone with no Bible knowledge may picture a literal gesture. Either explain it briefly as betrayal or omit it; currently it interrupts the towel thread without deepening the landing.

- **“He handed Judas the bread” is imprecise.** John 13:26–27 specifies a dipped “sop,” not simply bread. That matters because the narration has just quoted “He that eateth bread with me,” making the next sentence sound like a deliberate symbolic enactment that the text does not describe in those terms.

- **KJV quotation check: all words actually inside quotation marks are verbatim KJV.** The first construction is easy to misread: “had already” is outside the quotation; the quoted words beginning “put into the heart…” are an exact KJV substring. The quotations from John 13:10, 13:18, 13:27, and 13:30 are also exact. [KJV John 13:2](https://www.biblehub.com/kjv/john/13-2.htm), [KJV John 13:10](https://biblehub.com/kjv/john/13-10.htm), [KJV John 13:18](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John+13%3A18&version=KJV).

- **Thread spine:** Mostly coherent. Feet, washing, and towel run from hook to CTA. The unused heel quotation briefly diverts that spine, while the final move from Judas to “your heart” arrives without enough theological bridge.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Stop presenting Jesus washing and physically holding Judas’s individual foot as explicit fact; identify it as an inference or stay with John’s stated details.
2. Replace the invented motive and ambiguous “He wouldn’t change” claim with what the text establishes: Jesus knowingly served in the presence of His betrayer while distinguishing outward washing from true cleanness.
3. Rebuild “He has not put the towel down” so the landing connects the towel to Christ’s cross and offered cleansing without implying that knowledge or love automatically makes an unrepentant person clean.
