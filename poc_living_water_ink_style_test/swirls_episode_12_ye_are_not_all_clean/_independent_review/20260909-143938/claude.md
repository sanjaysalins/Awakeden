## Independent Adversarial Review — Narration

**Doctrinal soundness:** Sound. No invented doctrine. The core claim — Jesus washed Judas's feet knowing full well what was coming — is textually supported (John 13:11, "For he knew who should betray him") and lands on standard grace theology (love preceding worthiness, cf. Romans 5:8) without contrivance.

**KJV verbatim check (line by line):**
- "put into the heart of Judas Iscariot, Simon's son, to betray him" — exact match to John 13:2. ✓
- "ye are clean, but not all" — exact match to John 13:10. ✓ (minor: presented as a stand-alone capitalized-sentence-feeling quote but rendered lowercase "ye," which is technically correct verbatim since it's KJV's own mid-sentence casing — not a real defect, just worth a glance.)
- "He that eateth bread with me hath lifted up his heel against me" — exact match to John 13:18. ✓
- "That thou doest, do quickly" — exact match to John 13:27. ✓
- "went immediately out: and it was night" — exact match to John 13:30. ✓
All quotes verified clean. No alterations found.

**Accuracy nit (not a quote, but a factual flattening):** "He handed Judas the bread" compresses John 13:26's actual detail — Jesus dips a **sop** and gives it as a *secret sign to identify the betrayer to John alone* ("he it is, to whom I shall give a sop, when I have dipped it"). Calling it generic "bread" loses the deliberate, coded nature of the gesture and makes it read like an ordinary meal action rather than the moment the text presents it as.

**Clarity:** Passes. A listener with no Bible background can follow the sequence (wash → statement → quotes → bread → command → exit) without needing outside context.

**Grace-anchored conviction:** Passes. No fear, no gain/loss, no works-framing. "That is what His love does when it already knows the worst" is invitational, not coercive.

**Thread spine:** Solid — the towel/washing image opens the piece and closes it ("He has not put the towel down"). No thread-swapping.

**Freshness:** Genuinely fresh angle (Judas was included in the foot-washing) without touching doctrine or the plain sense of the text.

**HOOK SCORE: 7/10.** "Judas walked out into the night on feet Jesus had washed" is not a template open, and it has real irony — but that irony is borrowed entirely from the listener already knowing Judas is "the betrayer." For a true first-time listener with zero context, the line is just an observation until the piece explains why it matters 20 seconds later. It's evocative, not viscerally arresting in the first 5 seconds.

**LANDING SCORE: 8/10.** "He has not put the towel down" is genuinely built from this piece's own image — it could not cleanly close a different episode. It's not a stock closer. Its one softness: it's a statement of ongoing love/knowledge rather than an active invitation to respond — closer to comfort than call.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Sharpen the hook so the tension lands without relying on the audience already knowing Judas is the betrayer — give the irony inside the first sentence itself, not two beats later.
2. Turn the landing from description into invitation — "He has not put the towel down" is strong, but pair it with (or replace the last beat with) an active verb aimed at the listener, not just a statement about Jesus.
3. Restore the "sop" detail (a dipped morsel given as a secret identifying sign) instead of generic "bread" — the text's own point is that this was a deliberate, coded gesture, not a casual hand-off.
