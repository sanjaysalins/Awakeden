# Independent review — codex (OK, 41s)

HOOK SCORE: 9/10. “Judas walked out into the night on feet Jesus had washed” is immediate, vivid, text-rooted, and arresting.

LANDING SCORE: 8/10. “He has not put the towel down” grows directly from the piece’s central image and is not interchangeable with a generic Christian closer. However, its meaning is doctrinally hazy: ongoing cleansing, patient invitation, or continuing service?

Specific findings:

- “Jesus did not wash those feet hoping Judas would change” invents Christ’s internal posture. John tells us Jesus knew Judas would betray Him; it does not say Jesus lacked any desire for Judas to repent. Certainty about Judas’s action does not justify a claim about what Jesus did or did not “hope.”
- “That is what His love does when it already knows the worst” turns a specific act toward Judas into a broad principle without explaining what the act means. It risks sentimentalizing the scene and overlooking Jesus’s explicit distinction: “ye are clean, but not all.”
- “He knows your heart… He has not put the towel down” offers reassurance but suppresses the passage’s warning. Judas received washed feet and bread yet still departed into darkness. Physical foot-washing did not mean Judas was spiritually clean.
- The narration therefore blurs two truths that John deliberately holds together: Christ’s astonishing humility toward His betrayer and Judas’s uncleanness and rejection of Christ.
- The quotations are verbatim KJV excerpts. No altered word is apparent. “went immediately out: and it was night” is an exact continuous excerpt from John 13:30, though the omitted opening clause should not be mistaken for the complete verse.
- The central thread—washed feet, betrayal, towel—remains coherent from hook to landing.
- The piece is clear on first hearing, and its conviction is invitational rather than fear-based. Its main weakness is theological overreach, not tone or structure.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Remove the unsupported claim that Jesus washed Judas’s feet without “hoping Judas would change.”
2. Preserve John’s distinction between outwardly washed feet and being spiritually “clean.”
3. Clarify what “He has not put the towel down” promises so it does not imply unconditional cleansing apart from faith and repentance.
