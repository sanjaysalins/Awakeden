## Independent Review — Narration Artifact

**Doctrinal soundness:** No invented doctrine. Every claim traces to John 13. Good.

**KJV verbatim check** — I checked all four quoted fragments against John 13 KJV:
- "put into the heart of Judas Iscariot, Simon's son, to betray him" (v.2) — exact. ✓
- "ye are clean, but not all" (v.10) — exact. ✓
- "He that eateth bread with me hath lifted up his heel against me" (v.18) — exact. ✓
- "That thou doest, do quickly" (v.27) — exact. ✓
- "went immediately out: and it was night" (v.30) — exact. ✓

No elisions, no altered words. Chronological order of the four quotes also matches the actual Gospel sequence (wash → "not all clean" → heel saying → sop → exit). This is clean, careful work.

**Real problem #1 — an asserted certainty the text doesn't support.** "Jesus did not wash those feet hoping Judas would change." John never tells us what Jesus was or wasn't hoping. The text shows Jesus *knew* the outcome and washed anyway — that's powerful enough on its own. Flatly denying that Jesus held any hope for Judas is an interpretive claim stated as fact, not something in the passage. It's a small overreach, but it's exactly the kind of "reading in" the doctrinal-soundness bar exists to catch — soften it to what's actually textual (knowledge + love), not a claim about what He wasn't hoping for.

**Real problem #2 — landing is descriptive, not an invitation.** "He knows your heart, every corner of it, like that. He has not put the towel down." This is theologically fine and image-specific (passes the "could this end a different piece" test — no, it's tied to the towel). But it never turns toward the listener with an actual invitation or action. It states a fact about Jesus and stops. The model this series is built on is explicitly "CTA-to-**Jesus**" — every piece must land on an invitation, not just a true statement. Right now a listener finishes with "that's beautiful" but no clear sense of what to do with it. This is the single biggest fix needed.

**Clarity concern — "lifted up his heel against me."** Per the project's own global-audience rule, archaic KJV idioms are fine to quote in passing but shouldn't carry unexplained meaning for a first-time, non-native listener. Here the phrase is dropped in with zero unpacking ("And He quoted Scripture: '...'") — a listener with no Bible background has no way to know this means betrayal. One clause of narrator paraphrase before or after the quote would fix it without touching the quote itself.

**Hook:** "Judas walked out into the night on feet Jesus had washed." Specific, ironic, visual, not a template opener (no "Did you know/Imagine/What if"). **Score: 9/10.**

**Landing:** Image is genuinely this piece's own (the towel callback is well-built), but it stops short of inviting a response — it's reflection, not a CTA. **Score: 7/10.**

**Thread spine:** Consistent throughout — towel/washing/foreknowledge/love, no swapping. Good.

**Grace-anchored:** No fear or gain-loss framing. Good.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Give the landing an actual invitation, not just a statement — something the listener can do with "He has not put the towel down" (e.g., an explicit turn toward Him), so it functions as a CTA, not a closing reflection.
2. Soften "Jesus did not wash those feet hoping Judas would change" — the text supports "He knew, and washed him anyway," not a flat denial of any hope on Jesus's part. Don't assert what isn't written.
3. Unpack "lifted up his heel against me" in plain narrator language before or after the quote — a first-time global listener won't parse the idiom as "betrayed" on their own.
