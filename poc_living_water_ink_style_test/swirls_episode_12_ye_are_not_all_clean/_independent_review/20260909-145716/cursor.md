# Independent adversarial review — narration

## Doctrinal soundness

**Mostly sound, one overreach.**

The core claim — Jesus washed Judas’s feet while already knowing betrayal was coming — is grounded in John 13 (devil put betrayal in Judas’s heart; Jesus knew “not all” were clean; Judas received the sop and left). That is orthodox and not clickbait.

**Problem:** This line asserts inner motive the text does not state:

> *"Jesus did not wash those feet hoping Judas would change."*

John 13 proves Jesus **knew**; it does not prove Jesus had **no hope** of change. Many faithful readings treat the foot-washing and the sop as final pleading love, not love with hope already ruled out. Stating *“did not … hoping”* as fact is invented psychology, not Scripture. Safer: *knowing* betrayal was coming — which you already say in the next sentence.

No contrarian doctrine, no thread-to-Jesus swap (this is a Christ-character piece; landing stays on Jesus). Judas’s outcome is not mishandled.

## Clarity on first hearing

**Strong middle; two friction points.**

1. **Cold open, then rewind:**  
   *"Judas walked out into the night on feet Jesus had washed."*  
   Then you jump back to the devil, the washing, the sop. Works for literate listeners; a zero-Bible listener may not know who Judas is or why “night” matters until halfway through. Not fatal, but the first ~5 seconds assume prior knowledge.

2. **Deictic landing:**  
   *"He knows your heart, every corner of it, **like that**."*  
   “Like that” points back to *knowing the worst while still serving*. On one pass, a stranger may hear exposure without immediately catching the referent. The towel line saves it, but the link is implicit, not spoken.

**Good:** *"sop — a piece of dipped bread"* gloss helps. Narrative spine is followable.

## Grace-anchored conviction

**Mostly invites; slight exposure tilt.**

No gain/loss, no works, no “turn or else.” The Spirit convicts; the script observes Christ’s love.

**Minor flag:** *"He knows your heart, every corner of it, like that"* lands on **known sin** before **continued love**. Grace is there (*"He has not put the towel down"*), but the beat order is know-your-worst → then towel. For a betrayer-shaped listener, one more beat of “and still serves” before “every corner” would balance better. Not fear-framed, but thin on reassurance.
