**Doctrine — this is the real break**

`"Washed is not the same as clean. Judas had one and never asked for the other."`

That slogan collides with the verse sitting in the same paragraph the piece already entered. John 13:10 KJV, spoken to Peter, never quoted here:

> He that is washed needeth not save to wash his feet, but is clean every whit: and ye are clean, but not all.

In this chapter, **washed is how you become clean**. `"He that is washed … is clean every whit."` Judas is the exception because he is **not** in that “washed” number — `"Ye are not all clean"` *because* `"he knew who should betray him"` (13:11), not because Judas collected foot-water and forgot to file a second request.

The script uses `"washed"` for the basin-job Judas is *assumed* to have received, and `"clean"` for a separate inner upgrade he `"never asked"` for. That inverts Jesus’s own lexicon in the same scene. It is a preacher’s contrast, not John’s. Freshness from a contested reading.

Two invented claims ride on it:

1. `"Judas had one and never asked for the other."` Judas does not ask. Peter does (13:8–9). Judas’s sin here is betrayal already in the heart (13:2), then `"after the sop Satan entered into him"` (13:27), then the door. Not a missed prayer.
2. `"Then, to the whole room: 'ye are clean, but not all.'"` John 13:10: `"Jesus saith to him"` — to Peter. The `"ye"` is plural, but this is not a public turn away from Peter to the table.

`"He put the bread in that hand anyway"` loads the sop with mercy. In 13:26 the sop is how Jesus *marks the betrayer*. Hospitality-as-last-offer is a later homily, not what John says the gesture is doing.

`"All of them. Judas too."` Reasonable inference from `"began to wash the disciples' feet"` while Judas is still in the room. The whole hook, the `"anyway,"` and the landing treat it as filmed fact. John never names Judas’s feet.

**Clarity on first hearing**

The body never *teaches* two different washings. It shows a basin, then `"ye are clean, but not all,"` then a sop, then a door. Then the last three sentences spring `"Washed is not the same as clean"` as if the distinction had been built. A listener with no Bible has no way to know `"washed"` just changed meaning.

`"Maybe you have been at this table for years too — washed, serving alongside Him"`  
Judas is not serving alongside Him. Jesus is the one with the towel. The line needs the listener to decode “church member / volunteer” from a supper where the betrayer sat and was served. That is insider code.

`"The famous line"` is a lecturer’s elbow. It tells you the line is famous instead of making it land.

`"He sits down"` after the `"not all clean"` beat smears the order. 13:10–11 is still in the Peter exchange. He is `"set down again"` at 13:12, after the washing, before the example-speech. Read aloud, it sounds like the washing of everyone (including Judas) finished, *then* Peter refused.

`"The basin was still in the room. He hasn't put it away."` After `"He sits down"` and after Judas has left, a zero-knowledge ear can hear a literal leftover bowl, not Christ still offering cleansing. The metaphor is not spoken. 13:12 already has Him done, garments back on, seated.

**Grace-anchored conviction**

No gain/loss deal. No `"or you burn."` The `"anyway"` lines are the actual grace in the piece.

Then the close puts the verb on the listener: `"ask. Ask to be made clean, not just washed."` In this scene, Jesus washes, Jesus knows, Jesus declares clean. The Eleven do not produce cleanness by requesting it. `"Ask"` is a portable religious imperative, not this text’s invitation.

`"turning toward the door, and nobody around you can tell either. He could read Judas then. He can read you now."` That is exposure as the turn: secret apostasy, then omniscience aimed at *you*. Conviction can do that. Fear-framing starts when the threat is the being-seen, and the basin is an afterthought. Here the being-seen sits longer than the offer.

The listener mapped onto Judas is also the wrong patient for 13:10 if they are already His. The Eleven were `"clean"` and only needed their feet. Judas was not clean. The script never says which person it is talking to. A struggling believer can hear `"you were never clean."` That is not what Jesus said to the room.

**Freshness / thread**

The hook’s irony is faithful depth: night, washed feet, the door. Good entry.

Then the piece quotes `"I have given you an example"` — the servant-leadership moral of 13:15 — and drops it. Dead thread. The spine you actually want is washed-and-still-not-clean, or knowing-and-still-serving. Do not tour the example-speech if the CTA is cleanness.

`"I speak not of you all"` plus the heel-verse plus the sop plus Satan-entered plus the poor-guess plus the door plus the basin plus washed≠clean. That is one chapter’s worth of beads, not one thread pulled tight. Sibling Swirls pieces land one image. This one annotates John 13.
