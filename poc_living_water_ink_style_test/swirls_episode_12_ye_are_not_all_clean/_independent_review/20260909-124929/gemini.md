# Independent review — gemini (OK, 53s)

**Doctrinal Soundness & KJV Check**
- **Textual/Doctrinal Collision:** "Washed is not the same as clean." This directly contradicts the English KJV text of John 13:10, where Jesus explicitly says, "He that is washed needeth not save to wash his feet, but is clean every whit." The script uses "washed" to mean the foot-washing, but because it preaches from a passage where Jesus uses "washed" to mean full salvation/cleanness, it creates a contradiction.

**Grace-Anchored Conviction**
- **Fear Framing:** "He could read Judas then. He can read you now." This leans into exposure/threat framing (God sees your secret betrayal). The prompt forbids fear framing. The power of the passage is that He knows our hearts and *washes our feet anyway*. The omniscience should frame His grace, not act as a warning.

**Clarity on First Hearing**
- **Narrative Stutter:** "A sop is dipped bread. He gives one to Judas." Stopping the narrative climax to insert a dictionary definition breaks the tension. It needs to be integrated naturally (e.g., "He dips the bread—the sop—and hands it to Judas"). 

**Scores**
- **SCORE THE HOOK: 9.** "Judas walked out into the night on feet Jesus had washed." Exceptional. No templates, immediately introduces the exact tension of the text in one sentence.
- **SCORE THE LANDING: 8.** "The basin was still in the room. He hasn't put it away... Ask to be made clean." Highly specific to the piece's own metaphor, perfectly avoiding stock Christian phrases. It loses a point only due to the textual collision on the word "washed" mentioned above. 

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Fix the John 13:10 collision: change "Washed is not the same as clean" to something like "Having your feet washed is not the same as being clean" or "Water on your skin is not the same as being made clean."
2. Remove the fear-framing of "He can read you now." Pivot directly to the grace: He knows your heart is turning, and the basin is still waiting for you anyway.
3. Integrate the explanation of the "sop" so it doesn't interrupt the dramatic flow.
