## Independent Adversarial Review — "Ye Are Not All Clean" (John 13, Judas)

**KJV verbatim check** — went quote by quote against John 13 (KJV): "put into the heart of Judas Iscariot, Simon's son, to betray him" (v.2), "He riseth from supper, and laid aside his garments; and took a towel, and girded himself" (v.4), "began to wash the disciples' feet" (v.5), "If I wash thee not, thou hast no part with me" (v.8), "ye are clean, but not all" / "he knew who should betray him; therefore said he, Ye are not all clean" (vv.10-11), "I have given you an example" (v.15), "I speak not of you all" / "He that eateth bread with me hath lifted up his heel against me" (v.18), "troubled in spirit" (v.21), "And after the sop Satan entered into him" / "That thou doest, do quickly" (v.27), "no man at the table knew" / "give something to the poor" (vv.28-29), "went immediately out: and it was night" (v.30). **Every quoted string checks out exact.** No altered words found. This is unusually clean sourcing.

**Doctrine** — the "washed ≠ clean" contrast tracks the text's own vocabulary (v.10-11 literally distinguishes the two), not an invented reading. Judas alone is implicated, per v.11 — the piece doesn't smear it across the other disciples. No fear/gain-loss framing; the CTA verb is "ask" (receptive, grace-consistent), not a work.

**One real doctrinal-adjacent wobble**: "He could read Judas then. He can read you now." This edges toward exposure/surveillance rather than invitation — "he can read you" lands closer to being watched than being loved. It should be anchored explicitly to the *grace* half of the pattern already sitting one sentence later ("He washed those feet anyway") so the emphasis is foreknowledge-plus-love, not foreknowledge-as-scrutiny.

**Craft nit**: "The famous line:" is meta-commentary that breaks the otherwise unbroken concrete/image-driven prose (basin, sop, towel, night) — the only line in the piece that steps outside the scene to comment on the scene.

**Thread spine**: solid single thread — basin/washing opens it, "the basin was still in the room" mid-piece, "not just washed" closes it. No thread-swapping.

**HOOK — 7/10.** "Judas walked out into the night on feet Jesus had washed." Not a templated opener ("Did you know/Imagine") so it clears the auto-cap, and the irony is real once you know who Judas is. But it's a literary/descriptive opening, not a curiosity-gap or stated tension — it leans on the listener already carrying "Judas = betrayer" as cultural knowledge rather than building the hook's tension explicitly in the sentence itself. A genuine cold scroller has to wait a beat to get the payoff.

**LANDING — 8/10.** "Ask to be made clean, not just washed." Passes the "could this end a different piece" test — the word "washed" only pays off because this specific piece staged a literal foot-washing scene; it's not swappable with "come to Jesus" family stock closers. Earned, image-specific, grace-anchored.

Per the stated rule, hook under 8 is enough on its own to trigger REVISE — the doctrine, quotes, and landing are in strong shape; this comes down to tightening the first sentence's scroll-stop power and one exposure-flavored line.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Sharpen the hook so the tension is stated, not assumed — don't rely on the listener already knowing "Judas" means betrayer; make the irony (washed by the man he's about to betray) land inside the first sentence itself.
2. Cut or replace "The famous line:" — it's the one place the prose steps outside the scene to narrate about itself instead of staying inside the image.
3. Reframe "He could read Judas then. He can read you now." toward grace, not exposure — pair it tighter with "he washed those feet anyway" so foreknowledge reads as love, not surveillance.
