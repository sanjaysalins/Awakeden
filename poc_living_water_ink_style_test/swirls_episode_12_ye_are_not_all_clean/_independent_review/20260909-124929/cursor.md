### Doctrinal soundness

**Mostly sound, one real slippage.**

What works: The piece correctly preserves John’s distinction — foot-washing happened in the room, yet *“ye are clean, but not all”* / *“Ye are not all clean”* (13:10–11). It does **not** claim Judas was spiritually clean. That’s orthodox and text-grounded.

**Problem — the application blurs two different spiritual situations:**

*"Maybe you have been at this table for years too — washed, serving alongside Him, and already, quietly, turning toward the door..."*

That maps the listener onto **Judas’s arc** (false table-fellowship, heart already given to betrayal). That’s a valid warning. But the same line also sounds like it could describe a **true believer drifting into sin** — and for that person, John’s logic is different. The eleven were **clean**; Judas was not. A regenerate Christian who is backsliding is not “washed but never clean”; they need repentance/restoration, not initial cleansing. The piece never signals which listener it means. On first hearing, a struggling saint could hear: *I was never actually clean* — which is not what John 13 teaches about the washed feet / clean heart distinction.

**Secondary inference overstated as fact:** *“Judas walked out into the night on feet Jesus had washed”* and *“Judas too”* — Judas was present when Jesus washed the disciples’ feet (13:2–5), so inclusion is reasonable. But John never names Judas’s feet individually. The entire hook, middle, and CTA rest on that inference read as cinematic certainty. Defensible exegesis; still a constructed certainty, not narrated fact.

### Clarity on first hearing

**Two concrete logic problems:**

1. **Timeline smear:** *“He ‘began to wash the disciples' feet.’ All of them. Judas too. **Peter refuses**...”*  
   In John 13, Peter’s protest comes **during** the washing (13:6–9), not after a completed round that includes Judas. Read aloud, this order says: everyone finished → then Peter objected. A zero-Bible listener gets the wrong sequence and may miss that Peter’s exchange is **part of** the washing, not a postscript.

2. **Abrupt metaphor jump:** *“The basin was still in the room. He hasn't put it away.”*  
   Who is *“He”*? (Jesus? God? Christ now?) What is *“it”* for a listener who just heard a historical scene — basin as literal object, or basin as ongoing offer? The intended meaning (grace still available) is preachable, but the bridge isn’t spoken. That’s a clever conceit that asks the ear to finish the sermon.

**Smaller clarity hit:** *“washed, serving alongside Him”* — Judas at this supper is not “serving alongside Him” in any plain sense; the devil has already put betrayal in his heart (13:2). The phrase mixes **visible church participation** with **literal foot-washing** without labeling the slide.

**Why it still caps at 7:**

- *“ask”* is thin — ask **whom**, for **what**, by **what means**? A first-time listener knows something is wrong with “washed but not clean” but not what to do next beyond a generic verb.
