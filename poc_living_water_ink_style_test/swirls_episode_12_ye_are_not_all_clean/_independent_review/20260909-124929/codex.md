# Independent review — codex (OK, 108s)

Hook score: 9/10. “Judas walked out into the night on feet Jesus had washed” is immediate, vivid, and text-rooted.

Landing score: 6/10. It reuses the feet/door/washing imagery, but its theology is too muddled to land cleanly.

Specific findings:

- The central distinction is not accurately grounded: “Washed is not the same as clean.” John 13:10 actually says, “He that is washed … is clean every whit,” before distinguishing that complete washing from washing the feet. The narration uses “washed” for the footwashing and “clean” for inward cleansing without explaining the shift. On first hearing, it sounds as though it contradicts Jesus. [John 13:10–11 KJV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John+13%3A10-11&version=KJV)

- “Judas had one and never asked for the other” is invented. John never says Judas lacked cleansing because he failed to ask, nor does the passage show the other disciples asking for inward cleansing. Peter asks for additional bodily washing. The line turns an evocative contrast into an unsupported doctrine of how cleansing is received.

- “He hasn’t put it away” transfers the historical basin into a present offer without defining what it represents. In this passage, Jesus explicitly makes the footwashing an example of humble service; the narration instead makes the basin function like an instrument of saving cleansing. That risks ritual confusion and obscures cleansing through Christ himself.

- “Ask to be made clean” is an incomplete gospel invitation. Cleanliness here distinguishes Judas from true disciples, but the CTA never names repentance, faith, Christ’s saving work, or grace. It risks making the decisive act a request for an undefined inner cleansing.

- “He can read you now” followed by comparing the listener to Judas is fear-driven conviction. It sounds like divine surveillance and suspicion: perhaps you are secretly apostatizing and nobody else knows. The Spirit’s conviction should expose sin while directing the hearer to Christ’s mercy, not primarily make the hearer fear being an undetected Judas.

- “If your feet are already turning toward that same door” overidentifies ordinary struggling listeners with the uniquely treacherous Judas. The narration gives no category for temptation, doubt, backsliding, or repentance; movement away from Christ is rhetorically collapsed into betrayal.

- “Then, quieter” invents Jesus’ delivery. John supplies no volume or tone. It is cinematic embroidery presented as fact.

- “He put the bread in that hand anyway” implies that the sop was straightforwardly another gracious overture. The text presents it directly as the sign identifying the betrayer, immediately followed by Satan entering Judas. Treating it as uncomplicated mercy is an interpretive claim requiring support.

- For a novice, the sequence “the devil had already” acted and later “Satan entered into him” sounds contradictory unless the distinction is explained. Likewise, “That thou doest, do quickly” may sound as though Jesus commands the betrayal rather than knowingly dismissing Judas to carry out his chosen course.

- The quoted wording is verbally accurate KJV throughout; I found no altered word inside quotation marks. Several quotations are shortened fragments, but the included words match the KJV. [John 13 KJV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John+13&version=KJV)

- The thread itself is consistent—washed feet, Judas’s departure, the door, and cleansing—but the ending forces the image beyond what the passage establishes.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Remove the unsupported claim that Judas lacked cleansing because he “never asked,” and ground the spiritual distinction explicitly in the text and gospel.
2. Replace the fear-heavy identification of the listener with Judas with grace-anchored conviction that distinguishes temptation from settled betrayal.
3. Clarify “washed” versus “clean” so the CTA does not appear to contradict John 13:10 or turn the basin into a saving instrument.
