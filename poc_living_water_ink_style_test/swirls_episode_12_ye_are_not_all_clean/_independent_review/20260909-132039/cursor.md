## Independent adversarial review — narration (John 13 / Judas foot-washing)

### Doctrinal soundness
**Mostly orthodox; one line overclaims without support.**

The core claim — outward service received, inward cleanness withheld — tracks John 13:10–11 and standard evangelical reading. No contrarian Judas rehabilitation, no invented theology.

**Problem:** *“Jesus knew, and served him anyway.”*  
“Served him anyway” is narrator gloss, not John’s wording. John shows Jesus washing **all** the disciples’ feet, betrayer included — but the text does not frame that as mercy **despite** betrayal in those terms. It risks sounding like sentimental moralizing layered onto a scene whose point is **discernment** (*“Ye are not all clean”*), not “serve your enemies.” Defensible as inference; not earned in the lines you actually quote.

**Problem:** *“The sop was how He marked him.”*  
John 13:26–27: Jesus **answered** (“He it is, to whom I shall give a sop…”) and Satan entered **after** the sop. “Marked” compresses identification + possession into one beat and slightly rewrites the scene’s mechanics.

No FAIL-level doctrine — but the middle paragraph **asserts** the Judas paradox without **teaching** the bath/feet distinction the text uses to resolve it (see Clarity).

### Clarity on first hearing (zero Bible knowledge)
**This is the piece’s main wound.**

**1. Apparent self-contradiction (unresolved).**  
You quote: *“He that is washed needeth not save to wash his feet, but is clean every whit.”*  
Then you write: *“Judas had his feet washed, and was never clean every whit.”*  

A cold listener hears: Jesus said the washed are clean every whit → Judas was washed → therefore contradiction. John 13:10 distinguishes **prior full bathing** (*louō* — already clean) from **foot-washing** (*niptō* — dust off the road). You **quote** 13:10 but never **translate** it. Archaic *“needeth not save to”* (= “except to”) will not parse in audio.

**2. Peter’s strongest line is imported and abandoned.**  
*“If I wash thee not, thou hast no part with me”* is the chapter’s clearest **belonging** language. The close never ties *“part with me”* to *“clean every whit”* or to what Judas lacked. Two metaphors (feet / cleanness / part-with-Christ) run parallel without merging.

**3. Two Satans, no bridge.**  
*“the devil had already ‘put into the heart…’”* then *“And after the sop Satan entered into him.”* Zero-Bible ear hears duplicate causation or contradiction, not progressive hardening (13:2 vs 13:27).

**4. Modern metaphor never defined.**  
*“If your feet have been washed too, and nothing more”* — washed how? Church attendance? baptism? blessing? ministry? The Judas parallel requires the listener to infer “proximity without regeneration” with no one plain sentence naming that.

**5. Quote density in paragraph 2.**  
*“clean every whit,”* *“needeth not save,”* *“Simon's son,”* sop/Satan/poor — cold ear cannot hold the through-line; it becomes a recitation, not a point.

**6. “There is a scripture for it”** — slightly writerly; costs a beat without helping the listener.
