**Doctrinal soundness — orthodox claim, inverted application**

“Ye are not all clean” because of the betrayer is the text (John 13:10–11). Judas at the basin is a fair reading of “the disciples’ feet” while he is still at the table. Not clickbait. Not invented.

The script then quotes two wash-sayings and never splits them in living speech:

- To Peter: “If I wash thee not, thou hast no part with me.”
- Then: “He that is washed needeth not save to wash his feet, but is clean every whit.”
- Then: “All of them had their feet washed that night. Judas too.”
- Then: “Judas had his feet washed, and was never clean every whit.”

On first hearing that is a contradiction. Jesus tied the wash to “part with me.” Judas got the wash. Judas is still not His. The text’s own split is *bathed* (“washed… clean every whit”) vs *feet*. The eleven already had the bath; they only needed the feet. Judas had the feet and never the bath. The script quotes 13:10 and hopes the ear will parse “needeth not save to wash his feet.” It will not.

The CTA then treats the *feet* as Judas’s empty ritual: “If your feet have been washed too, and nothing more.” In the verse, feet-washing is what the *already-clean* still need. Judas’s mark is not the basin. It is that he was “not all clean.” That is the wrong lever.

**Clarity on first hearing — fails**

1. **False glue.** “Jesus was ‘troubled in spirit.’ There is a scripture for it: ‘He that eateth bread with me hath lifted up his heel against me.’” The heel line (13:18 / Psalm 41:9) is the scripture for *betrayal*, not for being troubled (13:21). “There is a scripture for it” is a lecture tag, and it attaches the verse to the wrong “it.”
2. **Two Satans, no bridge.** “the devil had already ‘put into the heart…’” then “And after the sop Satan entered into him.” A zero-Bible ear hears a contradiction, not a hardening.
3. **Mark vs nobody knew.** “no man at the table knew” sits next to “The sop was how He marked him.” Marked for whom, if no man knew? (13:26 is private to the disciple Jesus loved. This script never says that.)
4. **“That thou doest, do quickly”** with no frame sounds like Jesus ordering the betrayal.
5. **Quote dump.** Twelve quoted chips, including “needeth not save,” “sop,” “lifted up his heel.” A cold ear cannot hold this. ~270 words against a 135–165 budget. This is a chapter walk-through with a punchline taped on.

**Grace-anchored conviction — pass, with a bruise**

No hell threat. No “you’ll lose this if.” “ask Jesus to make you clean” keeps the work in His hands.

The bruise: the close analogizes the listener to Judas. “If your feet have been washed too, and nothing more” is “you might be the fake in the room.” That is pressure by identification. The Spirit can convict through Judas. The script should invite to *Christ’s* wash — “part with me” — not leave the hearer as Judas-with-a-second-wash.

**Freshness**

“Jesus washed Judas’s feet” is a pulpit commonplace, not a new cut in the text. The unused edge is Jesus’s own split: bathed vs feet vs “not all.” The script quotes that edge and never turns it.

**One thread**

Promised: night + washed feet.  
Delivered: full Upper Room recap (Peter, sop, Satan, the bag, the poor).  
Closed: basin / every whit.

“Jesus knew, and served him anyway” is a second grace-thread (the sop as service). The CTA never pays it. The sop/night material does no work in the last line. Two spines glued together.

**Gospel landing**

Charter: end on the Person, from *this* scene’s verb. Last sentence names Jesus. Good. It still asks for a generic cleansing, not the belonging already in the mouth of the text: “part with me.” “The basin was still in the room” is the earned image; the script explains it away.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. After 13:10, say in one plain sentence: the bath makes a person His; the feet are not that bath — so “Judas had his feet washed, and was never clean every whit” is the text, not a contradiction. Rebuild the last line from “part with me” / “clean every whit” in Jesus’s hands. Drop “If your feet have been washed too.”
2. Keep one spine. Cut the sop / Satan / poor / “marked him” recap, or cut the Peter-wash recap. Both will not fit, and the CTA only uses the basin.
3. Fix the first-hearing traps: name the betrayal in the hook; do not call the heel verse “a scripture for” being troubled; frame “do quickly” as release, not an order. Cut to the word budget.
