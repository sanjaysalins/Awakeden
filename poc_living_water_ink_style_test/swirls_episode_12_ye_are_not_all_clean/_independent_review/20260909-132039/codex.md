# Independent review — codex (OK, 91s)

**Hook: 9/10.** “Judas walked out into the night on feet Jesus had washed” is immediate, concrete, and rooted in the passage. The only caution is that Judas’s washing is a strong contextual inference rather than an individually narrated act.

**Landing: 6/10.** It reuses “feet,” “washed,” and “clean every whit,” so it is not stock. But “If your feet have been washed too, and nothing more” is too cryptic for a first-time listener. It could mean literal washing, baptism, outward religion, receiving Christian kindness, or something else.

Specific findings:

- **No altered KJV wording found.** Every quotation is an exact contiguous portion of the KJV text, even when introductory words such as “And,” “For,” or “Now” are omitted. The quoted wording matches [John 13 KJV](https://www.kingjamesbibleonline.org/John-Chapter-13/).

- **The central application is doctrinally underexplained.** “Ask Jesus to make you clean, every whit” gestures toward salvation but never identifies sin, repentance, faith, Christ’s death, or grace. “Clean” remains an unexplained metaphor. A zero-knowledge listener cannot know what Jesus does to cleanse a person or how this differs from the physical washing.

- **The closing risks fear-based uncertainty.** “If your feet have been washed too, and nothing more” invites the listener to compare himself with Judas without giving any biblical ground of assurance. The effect may be “Perhaps I am secretly Judas” rather than Spirit-led conviction anchored in Christ’s saving work.

- **“Jesus knew, and served him anyway” has an unclear antecedent.** Knew what—the meaning of His command, Judas’s plan, or Judas’s spiritual condition? “Served him” presumably refers to washing his feet, but it follows the discussion of the sop and giving to the poor. On first hearing, the logic blurs.

- **“The sop was how He marked him” needs precision.** Jesus gave it as the identifying sign promised in response to a disciple’s question; the whole table did not understand the disclosure. “Marked him” could instead sound as though Jesus publicly branded Judas or somehow initiated his guilt.

- **“There is a scripture for it” is weak and ambiguous.** “It” could refer to Jesus being troubled or to betrayal. More importantly, John presents the verse as Scripture being fulfilled, not merely as a fitting description. The narration passes over the passage’s deeper textual claim.

- **“All of them had their feet washed that night. Judas too” overstates an inference slightly.** John says Jesus “began to wash the disciples’ feet” and immediately explains “ye are clean, but not all,” strongly supporting Judas’s inclusion. Nevertheless, the narrative never separately states, “Jesus washed Judas’s feet.” The claim is sound but should not masquerade as an explicit sentence from the text.

- **The thread is consistent.** The washed-feet/unclean-heart contrast runs from hook through landing. The failure is not thread-swapping; it is that the final spiritual meaning remains undefined.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Make the final distinction between outward washing and Christ’s saving cleansing explicit, grace-grounded, and understandable without prior Bible knowledge.
2. Remove the Judas-comparison anxiety by grounding conviction and assurance in Christ’s work rather than asking listeners to diagnose whether they received “nothing more.”
3. Clarify “Jesus knew, and served him anyway,” “marked him,” and “There is a scripture for it” so each states the precise action and textual claim.
