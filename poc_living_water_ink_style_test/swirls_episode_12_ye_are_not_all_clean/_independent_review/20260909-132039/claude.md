# Independent review — claude (OK, 309s)

## Independent adversarial review — "Ye Are Not All Clean" (John 13, Judas)

**KJV verbatim — clean.** I traced every quoted fragment against John 13 myself: v.2 ("put into the heart..."), v.4 ("riseth from supper..."), v.5 ("began to wash..."), v.8 ("If I wash thee not..."), v.10 (full sentence, exact), v.11 ("he knew who should betray him..."), v.18 ("He that eateth bread..."), v.21 ("troubled in spirit"), v.27 (both clauses), v.28 ("no man at the table knew"), v.29 ("give something to the poor"), v.30 ("went immediately out: and it was night"). All are exact contiguous KJV substrings — no substituted or altered words. This passes the hardest gate cleanly.

**Real, unflagged problem — the script is roughly double a 60-second length.** I counted it: ~255 words. At a natural narration pace (~150 wpm, the range this project's own shorts run at), that's close to 100 seconds of read time, not 60 — even at a brisk 170 wpm it's still ~90s. Every quote here is load-bearing (can't be trimmed, must stay verbatim), which means the actual fix has to come out of the narrator scaffolding around them: "Jesus knew" is stated twice (once flatly, once as "Jesus knew, and served him anyway"), "John says" is used as an attribution tag three times, and the third paragraph (heel/scripture/sop/Satan/"do quickly") largely re-narrates betrayal mechanics that don't advance the washing/cleanness thread the piece is actually about. This needs a real cut, not a trim.

**Doctrinal core is sound but under-explained, and the landing leans on an unexplained metaphor.** The claim — outward washing received, inward cleanness withheld — is squarely supported by v.10-11. But the piece quotes "clean every whit" (the bath/already-clean vs. feet-only-need-washing distinction Jesus is drawing) without ever unpacking it in plain words. A listener with zero Bible background hears archaic syntax ("needeth not save to wash his feet, but is clean every whit") and then is asked to carry that exact unexplained phrase into the landing ("ask Jesus to make you clean, every whit"). The metaphor is quoted, never translated — that's a real first-hearing clarity gap, not a taste issue.

**The landing risks the fear framing the project explicitly bans.** "If your feet have been washed too, and nothing more" invites the listener to measure themselves against Judas — a named betrayer, traditionally understood as lost. Even though the sentence that follows is an invitation ("ask Jesus to make you clean"), the setup asks the listener to first wonder "am I a fake disciple like him?" before being offered grace. That's self-comparison-to-a-damned-figure doing the convicting work, which sits close to the fear/self-interest framing the brief rules out, rather than the Spirit doing it through a positive image.

**"The sop was how He marked him"** compresses a step the narration never gave: Jesus's answer was to a specific disciple's question ("Lord, who is it?") — without that, "marked him" reads ambiguous (marked for whom? branded how?).

**Thread spine:** genuinely coherent start to finish — washed feet/not clean every whit runs from the opening line to the close. No thread-swapping.

**Freshness:** strong. The Judas-was-washed-too angle is a real, surprising-about-the-text entry point, not a contrarian reading.

**SCORE THE HOOK: 8/10.** "Judas walked out into the night on feet Jesus had washed" — concrete, ironic, not a template open. It works before the listener understands why it matters, which is the one thing holding it back from a 9.

**SCORE THE LANDING: 6/10.** It's built from the piece's own image (basin, "clean every whit"), not a swappable stock phrase — but "ask Jesus to make you clean" alone is generic enough that other pieces could absorb it with a word-swap, and the metaphor it leans on was never explained in plain speech.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Cut the script to fit an actual 60-second read (~150-170 words) — it currently runs ~255 words, close to double. Trim the narrator scaffolding (duplicate "Jesus knew," triple "John says," and most of paragraph 3's betrayal recap), not the KJV quotes.
2. Add one plain-language sentence unpacking what "clean every whit" actually means (full cleansing vs. surface washing) before the landing reuses that exact phrase — right now the metaphor is quoted, not translated, so the CTA lands on an unexplained word.
3. Rework the landing so it doesn't require the listener to first measure themselves against Judas ("and nothing more") before being invited — lead with the invitation to be made clean, not the self-comparison to the betrayer.
