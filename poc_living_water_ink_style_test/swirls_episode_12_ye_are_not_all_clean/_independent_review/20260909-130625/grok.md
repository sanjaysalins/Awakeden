**HOOK: 8/10**  
`Judas walked out into the night on feet Jesus had washed.` Not a template. Concrete. A stranger who already treats “Judas” as betrayer stops. Not a 9: John never names Judas’s feet. The irony is an inference from `began to wash the disciples' feet` while Judas is still in the room, sold as filmed fact. A listener with zero Bible knowledge also has to already know the name.

**LANDING: 6/10**  
Last sentence: `Let Him make it clean.`  
Not `Come to Jesus.` Still fails the test. That exact sentence ends Naaman, the ten lepers, the ashes, Psalm 51 — any uncleanness piece. `clean` is this chapter’s word; the sentence does not need this chapter. Under 8 on the landing. That alone is REVISE.

**Doctrine — the close preaches a different text than the hook**

Hook: washed feet walk into the night. Body quotes the title-verse: `Ye are not all clean.` That is John’s own split. Then the CTA treats the bowl as an unfinished offer: `The basin was still in the room. He is not finished with it.`

Judas, on this script’s own telling, already had the basin. He still went out. `And after the sop Satan entered into him.` Then `it was night.` Offering leftover furniture after that beat tells a first-hearer the problem is they have not yet sat down at the bowl. Judas’s problem in this chapter is not an empty basin.

John 13:10 is the verse this piece will not quote: `He that is washed needeth not save to wash his feet, but is clean every whit.` In this scene, “clean” is the prior bath, not the foot-bowl. Foot-washing is for those already His. `Ye are not all clean` names the betrayer, not a rinse cycle Jesus forgot to finish. `Let Him make it clean` via that basin flattens 13:10–11 into a ritual the text itself refuses.

`But He washes them all anyway. Judas too. John says why: "he knew who should betray him; therefore said he, Ye are not all clean."`  
13:11’s `therefore` explains why Jesus *said* the line, not why He washed Judas. The script makes the title-verse the reason for the wash. Backwards.

`The basin was still in the room. He is not finished with it.`  
Invented prop, then a false claim. 13:12: after the washing He has `taken his garments, and was set down again.` He is finished with the bowl. The whole CTA sits on a basin John has already put down.

`If I wash thee not, thou hast no part with me` is left hanging. No 13:9–10. On first hearing it is exclusion, not the bath/feet split. Then the close offers the same wash as the cure.

The last image is the basin, not the Person. The charter wants a CTA to Jesus. This lands on furniture, then a slogan.

**KJV verbatim**

Words inside the marks match John 13 as substrings. No swapped vocabulary found.

The cuts still change sense:

- `I have given you an example.` / `I speak not of you all.` — both mid-sentence in KJV (`For I have given you an example, that…` / `I speak not of you all:`). Periods inside the marks turn fragments into complete claims.

Outside the marks: `the devil had already` vs KJV `having now put.` Narrator, not a quote break.

`the devil had already put into the heart` and later `Satan entered into him` still sound like a contradiction unless the two moments are distinguished. They are not.

`That thou doest, do quickly.` With no frame, a cold ear hears Jesus commanding the betrayal.

`Then, quieter` invents a delivery John does not give.

`There is a scripture for it` is a lecturer’s elbow. `Betrayal, plain as that` flattens the heel-verse into a label and never uses it.

`girded himself` is never helped.

The body is a walk through 13:2–30: devil, towel, Peter, not-all-clean, example-speech, heel-verse, sop, Satan, the poor-guess, the door, the basin. A listener with no Bible gets beads, not one turn.

The logic trick at the end: if Jesus already washed Judas and Judas still was not clean, the solution cannot be `Let Him make it clean` with the same bowl. The hook’s irony and the CTA cancel.

**Grace-anchored conviction**

No hell-deal. No `what you get if.` The `anyway` lines are the grace that is actually in the piece. `Let Him` puts the verb on Him, not on a request Judas never made. That part is cleaner than the last draft.

Then the last breath maps the listener onto Judas (`your heart` against `Ye are not all clean`) without saying which John 13 person they are: the man already washed and not clean, or the disciple who still needs His hands. A struggling saint can hear they were never His. An outsider can hear they need a ritual bowl. After Satan and night, `does not have to be true of your heart` also ducks the scene’s own finality.

**Freshness / one thread**

The hook’s irony is the one fresh, text-true image: night, washed feet, the door.

Then the piece quotes `I have given you an example` — the servant-leadership moral of 13:15 — and drops it. Dead thread.

The spine the close wants is unfinished-basin / still-offered. The spine the hook promised is washed-and-still-gone. `Ye are not all clean` is the title and it is paid off as a reversible slogan, not as John’s split. That is a swap in the last eight seconds, not one thread.

The sop line is now honest (`The sop was how He marked him`). It does not save the arc. Marking the betrayer is not the same act as washing his feet, and the close still pretends the bowl is the open door.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Rebuild the last sentence from this piece’s actual claim (washed feet into night / `Ye are not all clean`), on Him, not leftover furniture. Kill `Let Him make it clean` — portable — and kill `He is not finished with it` after 13:12 has Him dressed and seated.
2. Stop using 13:11 as the reason He washed Judas. Carry 13:10 (`He that is washed… is clean every whit`) so washed ≠ clean is the spine, not an unfinished bowl.
3. Cut the dead `I have given you an example` / `Then, quieter` tour. One thread from the opening image to the close. Do not map the listener onto Judas and then offer the same wash that did not make him clean.
