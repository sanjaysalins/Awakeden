# Independent review — codex (OK, 96s)

The piece has a strong hook but an unstable theological landing.

- **Hook: 8/10.** “Judas walked out into the night on feet Jesus had washed” is vivid, immediate, and central to the passage. However, John never explicitly says, “Jesus washed Judas’s feet.” The narrative strongly implies it—Jesus finishes washing “their feet” before Judas departs—but the hook presents an inference as an explicit fact. Because that inference is occasionally contested, it needs careful framing rather than absolute assertion. [John 13:2–30 KJV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John+13%3A1-30&version=KJV)

- **Landing: 4/10.** “The basin was still in the room. He is not finished with it” invents both a physical detail John does not report and a continuing meaning Jesus does not assign to the basin. More seriously, it clashes with the piece’s own observation: Judas apparently received the physical washing yet remained “not…clean.” The basin therefore cannot carry the final appeal to spiritual cleansing without explanation.

- “Let Him make it clean” leaves the means of cleansing undefined. In evangelical terms, the listener needs to be directed toward Christ’s saving work and received grace—not toward an ambiguous continuation of the foot-washing. As written, the image risks sounding sacramental or magical even if that was not intended.

- The narration swaps threads. The hook establishes **Jesus knowingly serving His betrayer**. The middle then emphasizes **how Judas was identified**, while the ending suddenly becomes **an appeal for personal cleansing**. Those are related themes, but the script has not demonstrated the transition. The distinctive hook is about Christ’s love toward an enemy; the landing is about the listener’s unclean heart.

- “Peter refuses. Jesus tells him… But He washes them all anyway” obscures the sequence. Peter initially refuses, but after Jesus’ warning he asks for even more washing. Omitting that reversal makes “anyway” sound as though Jesus simply overrode Peter’s refusal.

- “Then, quieter” is invented staging. John records no change in volume or manner. It adds cinematic atmosphere without textual support.

- “The sop was how He marked him” is potentially misleading. Jesus gives the sop as the identifying sign in answer to the beloved disciple’s question; it does not spiritually “mark” Judas or make him the betrayer. The wording is especially risky because it immediately precedes “Satan entered into him.”

- **KJV verification:** All words placed inside quotation marks are exact consecutive wording from the KJV of John 13. Some are shortened fragments, but no quoted word has been altered. The capitalization and punctuation surrounding excerpts do not create a verbal mismatch. [John 13:2–30 KJV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John+13%3A1-30&version=KJV)

- The conviction is invitational rather than threatening, which is appropriate, but it is not adequately grace-anchored. The final appeal needs to distinguish Christ’s outward service to Judas from the inward cleansing Judas lacked and ground that cleansing in Christ’s saving work.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Replace the basin-based landing; it wrongly blurs physical foot-washing with the spiritual cleansing Judas demonstrably lacked.
2. Choose one spine—preferably Jesus knowingly loving and serving His betrayer—and build the middle and CTA from that same truth.
3. Remove invented or overstated details such as “Then, quieter,” qualify the Judas-footwashing inference, and clarify Peter’s reversal.
