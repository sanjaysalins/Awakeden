### Doctrinal soundness — **problems**

**1. John 13:10 is misrepresented as a headcount.**  
*"Some, He said, were already 'clean every whit.' Judas was not."*

In KJV, *"clean every whit"* is not a roll call of disciples. Jesus gives Peter the bathing metaphor (*he that is washed… is clean every whit*), then says to the group *"ye are clean, but not all."* The artifact turns that into *some disciples were fully clean, Judas wasn't* — which is not what the verse says and blurs the foot-washing vs. full-bath distinction John is making.

**2. The Judas arc and the landing pull in opposite directions.**  
The piece builds a betrayer who was explicitly *not* clean, who received the sop, was dismissed (*"do quickly"*), and walked into *"night."* Judas does not become clean. The close — *"Whatever is only washed in you, Jesus can still make clean — every whit"* — offers full cleansing without explaining why Judas’s story ends in darkness anyway. That reads like hope grafted onto a cautionary tale, not earned from the text.

**3. Foot-washing ≠ automatic inward cleansing — but the piece blurs them.**  
Jesus washed Judas’s feet *anyway*. Judas still left unclean. The landing can sound like outward contact with Jesus (washing, bread, proximity) still guarantees *"every whit"* cleansing. John’s point cuts the other way for Judas: proximity and even service from Christ did not save him. The script never names faith/reception; it only names washing.

**4. Weak gospel landing.**  
*"Jesus can still make clean"* names Christ as agent, but not as the one to trust, receive, or come to. For a CTA-to-Jesus short, the close stays at abstract cleansing language and never completes the turn onto Christ as Lord/Savior — especially after a Judas hook that demands that turn.

### Clarity on first hearing — **problems**

**1. *"Whatever is only washed in you"* is opaque.**  
A listener with zero Bible background cannot parse this. Washed *in you* how? Outwardly? Partially repentant? Self-reformed? The line is clever on paper and confusing in the ear.

**2. *"The bread that marked him"* is unexplained.**  
No stranger knows why bread *marked* Judas. That’s insider vocabulary without a half-second of orienting context.

**3. The John 13 logic is assumed, not carried.**  
Foot-washing, *clean every whit*, sop, *and it was night* — all dense Johannine symbolism. The script stacks them without one plain bridge sentence, so the middle reads like a highlight reel for people who already know the Upper Room scene.

**4. *"The basin was still in the room"* lands as mood, not meaning.**  
Evocative, but a first-time listener won’t know what to do with it. What remained? An offer? A ritual? Judas’s empty chair? The image doesn’t resolve.

### Grace-anchored conviction — **mostly OK, one flag**

No fear, gain/loss, or prosperity framing. Good.

Minor flag: *"Whatever is only washed in you"* can sound like inner moral tidying (*what’s been cleaned up inside you*) rather than grace received from Christ — slightly self-referential where the piece should stay Christ-referential.

### Freshness = faithful depth — **mixed**

**Strength:** *"Judas walked out into the night on feet Jesus had washed"* is a real, surprising textual irony — faithful and fresh.

**Weakness:** The landing doesn’t deepen the text; it genericizes it into a soft universal-cleanse offer that could attach to almost any washing metaphor, and it sidesteps Judas’s actual outcome.

---

### One thread spine — **fraying at the close**

Hook → middle stays on washed feet / unclean heart / bread / night. Good spine.

The CTA swaps threads: from *Judas walked out unclean despite Jesus’s acts of love* to *you can still be made clean every whit* without answering the obvious question the hook raises: **Why didn’t Judas? What must the hearer do that he didn’t?** That gap is a logic break, not a turn.
