# Independent review — codex (OK, 73s)

**Hook: 9/10.** “Judas walked out into the night on feet Jesus had washed” is immediate, vivid, and text-specific.

**Landing: 6/10.** It echoes the basin and “clean every whit,” so it is not stock. But “Whatever is only washed in you” is difficult to parse, and the sentence is a theological assertion rather than a CTA or clear invitation.

Specific findings:

- The central theological leap is insufficiently established. “The basin was still in the room” turns Judas’s physical foot-washing into a symbol of merely outward cleansing, but John does not explicitly assign the basin that meaning. Jesus distinguishes one who is “washed” and clean from the washing of his feet; the narration needs to explain that distinction rather than reversing it into the vague category “only washed.”
- “Whatever is only washed in you” is conceptually muddled. A body part may be washed; something “in you” would ordinarily be cleansed. A first-time listener will not know whether this means behavior, baptism, religious appearance, conscience, or the heart.
- “Jesus can still make clean” is orthodox in isolation but incomplete here. The piece never connects cleansing to Christ’s saving work or invites faith and repentance. It risks implying that proximity to Jesus—or receiving an act of service from Him—is one stage short of inward cleansing.
- “Some, He said, were already ‘clean every whit’” weakens the actual contrast. Jesus says, “ye are clean, but not all,” meaning the group is clean with a specific exception. “Some” makes the division sound unspecified.
- “The bread that marked him” needs an object: marked him as what? John presents the sop as identifying the betrayer in response to a question, although the disciples generally did not understand Jesus’s subsequent instruction. A Bible-new listener cannot recover that context.
- “Let him go” is potentially misleading. Jesus knowingly permitted and commanded the immediate departure, but “let” can make the moment sound like reluctant helplessness.
- The quotations themselves are verbatim KJV fragments from John 13:2, 10, 27, and 30. No word inside quotation marks is altered. The surrounding “had already” paraphrases the KJV’s “having now,” but it is outside the quotation. [John 13 KJV](https://biblehub.com/kjv/john/13.htm)
- The thread remains consistent—washing, uncleanness, departure, basin—but the final interpretive leap is not clear enough to carry the otherwise strong image.
- There is no fear, self-interest, or works-based pressure. The problem is not harshness; it is that the grace offered remains undefined.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Replace “Whatever is only washed in you” with a biblically grounded distinction between outward washing and the cleansing only Christ gives.
2. Make the ending a genuine, basin-specific invitation that names faith or receiving Christ’s cleansing.
3. Clarify that the sop identified Judas as the betrayer and replace the vague “Some” with Jesus’s actual group-versus-exception contrast.
