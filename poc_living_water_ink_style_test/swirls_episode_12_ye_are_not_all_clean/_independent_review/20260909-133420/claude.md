## Independent Review — "Judas walked out into the night..." (narration)

**Doctrinal soundness:** Sound. No invented doctrine — the "outward washing without inward cleansing" reading of Judas is the standard, uncontested reading of John 13:10-11, not a contrarian angle.

**KJV verbatim check (word-for-word against John 13):**
- "put into the heart of Judas Iscariot, Simon's son, to betray him" — matches John 13:2 exactly.
- "clean every whit" — matches John 13:10 exactly.
- "Ye are clean, but not all." — matches John 13:10 exactly (correctly sourced from v10's word order, not conflated with v11's "Ye are not all clean").
- "That thou doest, do quickly." — matches John 13:27 exactly.
- "went immediately out: and it was night." — matches John 13:30 exactly.
All five quotes are clean. No elisions or altered words found.

**Clarity — the real problem is here:** the closing sentence — *"Whatever is only washed in you, Jesus can still make clean — every whit."* — is grammatically strained ("washed in you" isn't natural English) and asks a first-time listener to make an unstated theological leap: that Judas's feet were washed but his heart wasn't, and that "you" might be in the same position. That contrast is never spelled out in plain words before the sentence lands on it. This is a comprehension risk on a single hearing, not just a style nitpick.

Secondary nit: *"Some, He said, were already 'clean every whit.' Judas was not."* — "Some" is vague. The text's own contrast is "all but one" (Peter and the rest vs. Judas), not an unspecified subgroup. As written it could read as if there's a distinction among the eleven, which isn't the point.

**Grace-anchored:** Yes — no fear, no gain/loss, no works framing. The invitation is quiet and Spirit-shaped.

**Freshness / thread spine:** One clean thread (basin → washed feet → clean every whit → basin still in the room) carried hook to landing, no swap. The entry point (Judas specifically walking out on feet Jesus had just washed) is a genuinely fresh detail, not a stock angle.

**Hook — 8/10.** "Judas walked out into the night on feet Jesus had washed" is concrete, image-driven, and creates real tension (why does this detail matter?) without a template opener ("Did you know / Imagine / What if").

**Landing — 6/10.** The imagery (basin, "every whit") is earned from the piece's own quote, not a generic CTA phrase — that part is right. But the payoff sentence's syntax is confusing enough that it undercuts the landing's punch on first hearing. Could a different final sentence work interchangeably for another piece? No — "every whit" ties it to this quote specifically. The failure here is clarity, not genericness.

Since the landing scores below 8, this needs a revision pass on that closing line before lock.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Rewrite the final sentence so the surface-washed/heart-unwashed contrast is stated plainly — "Whatever is only washed in you" doesn't parse cleanly and skips the theological step a zero-background listener needs.
2. Fix "Some, He said, were already 'clean every whit.' Judas was not." — replace "Some" with something that matches the text's actual all-but-one contrast, so it doesn't read as a distinction among the eleven.
3. Consider anchoring the hook's premise (Jesus washed Judas's own feet) with a light textual nod earlier in the piece, since John 13 never names Judas individually in the washing scene — it's a sound inference but the hook states it as flat fact before any support is given.
