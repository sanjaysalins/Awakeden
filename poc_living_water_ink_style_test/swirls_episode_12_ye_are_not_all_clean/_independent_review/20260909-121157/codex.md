# Independent review — codex (OK, 90s)

Hook: 9/10. “Judas walked out into the night on feet Jesus had washed” is immediate, vivid, text-specific, and arresting.

Landing: 6/10. It reuses the feet/basin imagery, so it is not wholly stock. But “His hands are still open” is generic, and “before you take one step” implies an unsupported last-chance boundary—as though grace ceases once someone steps through the door.

Specific findings:

- The KJV quotations are verbally accurate. The excerpted wording matches John 13:2–30, including “put into the heart,” “If I wash thee not,” and “went immediately out: and it was night.” Some are shortened fragments, but no quoted word is altered. [John 13:4–16 KJV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John+13%3A4-5%2CJohn+13%3A12-16&version=KJV%3BNKJV), [John 13:18–30 KJV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John+13%3A18-30&version=KJV%3BNKJV%3BNASB%3BNIV)

- “All of them. Judas too” is a reasonable inference, but not an explicit statement. John says Jesus washed “the disciples’ feet” and Judas remained present; he never separately states that Jesus washed Judas. The hook treats an inference as narrated fact.

- “The heel. He had just dried it” turns biblical metaphor into literal anatomy. “Lifted up his heel against me” means treacherous opposition; John does not identify Judas’s freshly dried heel as the referent. This is clever, but exegetically showy and potentially confusing on first hearing.

- The script blurs physical washing and spiritual cleansing. It places “If I wash thee not, thou hast no part with me” beside “Judas too,” yet Jesus immediately says, “ye are clean, but not all.” Judas’s physically washed feet did not make him spiritually clean. That distinction is crucial and currently unstated.

- “John says ‘no man at the table knew.’ … Nobody can tell” changes the object of their ignorance. John says no one knew “for what intent” Jesus spoke to Judas—not that nobody could tell Judas was drifting, hypocritical, or “half out of the door.” The application claims more than the verse establishes.

- “They think you went to do something good” overstates and compresses the account. Only “some” thought Judas might give to the poor; the other stated possibility was buying necessities for the feast. Turning “some” into “they” strengthens the dramatic irony by trimming inconvenient textual detail.

- “The basin was still in the room. He hasn’t put it away” is invented staging. John says Jesus had already taken his garments and sat down again. The basin’s location is never reported, and “He hasn’t put it away” converts an imagined prop into a theological promise.

- “If your feet are already turned toward the door” is understandable as a call to repentance, but “before you take one step” frames grace as a rapidly closing opportunity. Scripture does not make crossing Judas’s threshold the general limit of Christ’s mercy; Peter also sins grievously that night and is restored.

- The spine begins as Christ’s knowing service toward Judas, shifts into exposing concealed apostasy—“You have sat at this table for years”—and then becomes a last-second invitation. Those ideas are related, but the landing does not clearly resolve the central tension: Jesus washed Judas, yet Judas remained “not clean.”

- The conviction leans toward fear and exposure: “you are already half out of the door. Nobody can tell… He can tell.” The gracious element arrives afterward, but it rests on invented basin imagery rather than plainly grounding the invitation in Christ’s knowledge, love, and call to repentance.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Distinguish the literal foot-washing from the spiritual cleansing Judas lacked; do not imply that receiving the former meant receiving the latter.
2. Remove or qualify invented and overextended claims: the specifically dried heel, the basin remaining, “Nobody can tell,” and “They think.”
3. Replace the arbitrary “before you take one step” deadline with a text-grounded invitation built around Christ’s full knowledge and Judas’s refusal to remain with Him.
