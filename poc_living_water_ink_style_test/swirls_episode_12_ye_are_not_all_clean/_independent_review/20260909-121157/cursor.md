## Doctrinal soundness — mostly sound, one soft mislead

No contrarian reading, no invented theology, no clickbait twist. The foot-washing → betrayal arc is orthodox.

**Soft problem:** `"The heel. He had just dried it."`  
"He lifted up his heel against me" is a **treacherous-turn idiom**, not a physical heel Jesus had just towelled. The line is poetically clever but can teach the wrong idea to a listener with zero Bible background — that the prophecy was about the washed foot itself. Freshness here risks **misreading the text**, not faithful depth in it.

**Timeline blur:** The piece quotes `"And after the sop Satan entered into him"` then pivots to `"If your feet are already turned toward the door, His hands are still open before you take one step."` Judas’s exit is **after** Satan enters; the open-handed grace moment in the narrative is **earlier** (foot-washing / sop). The application collapses two different moments. That is not heresy, but it weakens biblical fidelity for careful listeners.

## Clarity on first hearing — REVISE-level confusion at the pivot

**Strong:** `"A sop is dipped bread."` — good zero-knowledge gloss.

**Weak — the "you" pivot:**

> `"You have sat at this table for years. Served. Washed. And you are already half out of the door."`

On first hearing, a stranger cannot tell:
- Who is "you"? (Judas? any drifting believer? the listener-as-hypocrite?)
- Who **served** and **washed**? At the Last Supper Jesus washed; the disciples were washed. Read literally, **Judas did not wash** — he was washed. If "you" is the modern listener, "Served. Washed." is three unexplained fragments with no subject.

> `"Nobody can tell. They think you went to do something good."`

That maps cleanly onto Judas (v.29) but **not cleanly onto "you"** until the listener reverse-engineers the analogy. The logic is there for insiders; it is not transparent for the stated audience.

**Tense jump:**

> `"The basin was still in the room."` → `"He hasn't put it away."`

Past scene → present eternal Christ is a valid move, but without a bridge it can sound like Jesus is still physically at the table after Judas left.

## Grace-anchored conviction — PASS with a caution

No fear sells, no gain/loss, no "accept Jesus or else." `"His hands are still open"` invites; it does not threaten.

**Caution:** Peter’s `"If I wash thee not, thou hast no part with me"` is quoted but never unpacked. A naive listener could hear **participation/containment** ("stay at the table / don’t walk out") rather than **received cleansing**. The closing leans behavioral (don’t leave) more than gospel (let Him wash you / be clean).
