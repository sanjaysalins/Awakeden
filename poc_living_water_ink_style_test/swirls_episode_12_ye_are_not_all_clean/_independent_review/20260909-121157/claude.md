# Independent review — claude (OK, 108s)

Reviewed as an adversarial outside reader — findings below, not a rewrite.

## KJV verbatim check
Traced every quoted phrase against John 13 (and the Psalm 41:9 citation via John 13:18):
- "put into the heart of Judas Iscariot, Simon's son, to betray him" — exact (13:2).
- "riseth from supper, and laid aside his garments; and took a towel, and girded himself" — exact (13:4).
- "began to wash the disciples' feet" — exact (13:5).
- "If I wash thee not, thou hast no part with me" — exact (13:8).
- "ye are clean, but not all" / "he knew who should betray him; therefore said he, Ye are not all clean" — exact (13:10-11).
- "I have given you an example" / "I speak not of you all" — exact (13:15, 13:18).
- "He that eateth bread with me hath lifted up his heel against me" — exact (13:18, quoting Ps 41:9).
- "troubled in spirit" — exact (13:21).
- "And after the sop Satan entered into him" / "That thou doest, do quickly" — exact (13:27).
- "no man at the table knew" / "give something to the poor" — exact (13:28-29).
- "went immediately out: and it was night" — exact (13:30).

No elisions, substitutions, or reordering that change meaning. This is a genuinely clean pass — every quote checks out.

## Doctrine / thread / conviction
No invented doctrine, no contrarian reading. The claim — Jesus knowingly served the man about to betray him, right up to the last bite — is textually supported, not stretched. Conviction is grace-anchored: no threat, no gain/loss framing; "He washed that heel anyway. He put the bread in that hand anyway" states grace as fact before the CTA turns to the listener. One thread (the basin/feet/hands) runs hook to landing without swapping.

## One soft spot
"You have sat at this table for years. Served. Washed. And you are already half out of the door." This asserts the listener's spiritual state as fact rather than posing it as a live possibility — a presumptive move, not a doctrinal error, but it's the one place the script tells the listener what they are instead of inviting them to check. Minor.

## Scores
- **Hook: 8/10.** "Judas walked out into the night on feet Jesus had washed" is a concrete, ironic image, not a template opener — it earns curiosity rather than asking a stock question.
- **Landing: 8/10.** "If your feet are already turned toward the door, His hands are still open before you take one step" is built from this piece's own objects (feet, basin, door) — it wouldn't drop cleanly into an unrelated piece, so it clears the stock-closer test.

VERDICT: PASS
TOP FIXES:
1. Soften "you are already half out of the door" from an assertion of the listener's state to something that leaves room for "if" — keeps the conviction Spirit-led rather than accusatory.
2. "A sop is dipped bread" is a slightly loose gloss (a sop is bread dipped in the dish, not bread that is inherently "dipped") — tighten for precision, not a doctrinal issue.
3. The jump from third-person narrative ("He sits down...") to second-person address ("You have sat at this table...") is abrupt — a one-clause bridge would smooth the turn without slowing the piece.
