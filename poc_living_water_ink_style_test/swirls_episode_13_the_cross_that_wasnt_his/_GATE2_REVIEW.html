Episode 13 — GATE 2 (round 3, the real cross)

"The Cross That Wasn't His" (Simon of Cyrene, Mark 15 / Luke 23 / Hebrews 13) — all 9 story pages redone with the traditional cross shape. Covers are unchanged (they never showed the object, so no re-render needed there). Click any picture to make it big.

What changed, in one line: you said the "correct" crossbeam still just looked like a man carrying a plank — a beam with no upright can never look like "the cross" to an ordinary viewer, no matter how well-made the wood is. By your own decision, Simon now carries the traditional full cross (upright + crossbeam), not just the historically-precise crossbeam alone. Same real wood research as before (rough-hewn, dark, weathered, one plain peg joint) — only the shape changed.

Good news: this was a MUCH easier fix than the last round. Leaning into the shape people already expect, instead of fighting it, meant most pages came out right on the very first try.

Things worth your own eyes

  • The cross shape itself varies page to page — some pages (F03, F06, F07, F09) drew a proper "tall-and-short" cross; others (F01, F02, F04, F08) came out closer to an even X-shape (both arms similar length). Every single page is still unmistakably a cross either way — nobody would mistake it for a plank now. Getting the "tall upright, short crossbeam" look consistent on every page turned out to be a much harder, more stubborn ask than just getting a recognisable cross at all, so this was accepted rather than spending many more redo's chasing it.
  • F08 (the close-up on the wood): the joint shows two small dark peg-like marks instead of one — subtle, wood-toned (not shiny metal), easy to miss. Took two tries to get rid of an earlier version that looked more like metal rivets.
  • F05: the three small top panels came out as two-large-plus-one-below instead of the usual row of three. Tried twice to fix; content in each is correct either way, just a layout quirk on this specific page.
  • F07: this one page draws a thin black box around the big picture that the other 8 pages don't have. Minor, one-off.
