`"The man who carried the cross of Jesus was walking the other way."`  
Not a template open. Concrete. A stranger can stop.

**LANDING: 6/10** — this is the block.  
Last sentence: `"After Jesus."`  
Run the portability test. That exact sentence can close a prodigal piece, a rich-young-ruler piece, any “follow Him” short. It is in the `"Come to Jesus" / "turn to Him" / "look to Jesus"` family: a two-word follow-Christ closer.  
`"Freely, this time"` needs Simon’s compulsion, so the line before it is earned. Then the script throws the specificity away and ends on the transferable fragment.  
The real CTA in the quote is `"go forth therefore unto him without the camp, bearing his reproach."` A listener with zero Bible knowledge cannot parse `"without"` (hears *lacking*) or `"reproach"` (never glossed). So the earned image dies in the ear, and what survives is stock: `"You are invited… After Jesus."`

Also: the gospel gift sits in the middle (`"sanctify the people with his own blood"`) and the close lands on *our* going and *our* bearing. That is application, not the cross. Constitution: land at the gospel, not at the homework.

**Clarity on first hearing — fails in the quotes.**  
- `"the father of Alexander and Rufus"` is exact KJV with zero payoff. Two extra names, one hearing, no reason to keep them.

- `"Why outside the city? Hebrews gives the reason."` is teacher-voice. It announces a lesson. The inbound/outbound fact should *be* the reason, not cue a second sermon.

**Thread — one road, then a splice.**  
Hook = Simon inbound, Jesus outbound, compelled turn.  
Middle = Hebrews sin-offering geography.  
Close = go out to him, freely.  

The spine *can* be “out after Jesus.” The Hebrews block is introduced as a new question (`"Why outside the city?"`), so it plays as thread two: first Simon’s bad luck, then a typology lecture, then an altar call. `"Wrong road, wrong minute"` makes it worse — chance, not the road Hebrews is talking about.

**Grace — not a FAIL, still a lean.**
