### Doctrinal soundness
**Mostly sound and orthodox.** The Hebrews 13:11–13 typology (sin offering outside the camp → Jesus outside the gate → “go forth… bearing his reproach”) is **the letter’s own argument**, not invented doctrine.

**Skeptical flags:**
1. **Simon is not in Hebrews.** The piece implicitly types Simon as a compelled forerunner of the Hebrews 13:13 call. That’s homiletical glue, not Scripture’s explicit claim. Acceptable in a short if the listener can follow the jump; it’s still a **constructed bridge**, not stated in-text.
2. **Cross-bearing ≠ reproach-bearing** — Luke: Simon *“bear[s]… his cross after Jesus.”* Hebrews: *“bearing his reproach.”* The piece never names that shift. A careful listener may wonder whether the landing changed the object (cross → reproach) without warning.
3. *“likely just arriving for Passover”* — **inference**, not Mark/Luke. Crucifixion was Passover week; Simon’s motive isn’t given. *“Likely”* hedges, but it’s still editorial color presented as scene-setting.

No contrarian reading, no works-salvation framing, no clickbait theology detected.
