The man who carried the cross of Jesus was walking the other way.

Mark says the soldiers "led him out to crucify him. And they compel one Simon a Cyrenian, who passed by, coming out of the country, the father of Alexander and Rufus, to bear his cross." Jesus, led out of the city. Simon, coming in from the country, likely just arriving for Passover. Wrong road, wrong minute. They grabbed him, and Simon was turned around. Luke says, "on him they laid the cross, that he might bear it after Jesus."

Why outside the city? Hebrews gives the reason. Under the law, the sin offering was burned "without the camp." Not inside, where the people lived. Then the letter turns to Jesus: "Wherefore Jesus also, that he might sanctify the people with his own blood, suffered without the gate."

Outside is where the sin offering went. Outside is where Jesus went, for the people. Simon was compelled out behind him. You are not compelled. You are invited, and Hebrews puts the invitation in its own words, pointing straight at Jesus: "Let us go forth therefore unto him without the camp, bearing his reproach." Freely, this time. After Jesus.
