## Independent adversarial review

### Doctrinal soundness
Mostly sound and orthodox. The Simon → “without the camp” → Jesus “without the gate” → “bearing his reproach” chain is legitimate typology from Hebrews 13, not a contrarian reading.

**Problem — command before clarification:** The piece tells the listener to **“Turn on your own road, and carry his shame after him”** before it distinguishes disciple-bearing from atonement. On one hearing, that order can sound like *your* cross-bearing is the point of the gospel, with the guardrail (**“Only the shame… the atoning, the dying, the finishing, that was his”**) arriving too late. Not heresy, but the rhetorical sequence works against grace-anchored conviction.

**Problem — homiletical bridge presented as natural:** **“Bearing it is carrying it, as Simon carried the wood”** stitches Simon (Synoptic narrative) to Hebrews 13 (no mention of Simon). The link is preacher logic, not text logic. A skeptical listener could ask: “Did Simon ‘bear reproach,’ or was he just forced to carry timber?” The piece doesn’t acknowledge that leap.

**Minor — typology compressed:** **“The sin offering… had its blood carried into the sanctuary… Its body went out, burned ‘without the camp.’”** That matches Hebrews 13:11 / Leviticus 16 broadly, but “sin offering” covers several Levitical cases. Fine for a short; still a gloss, not a quoted scene.

### Clarity on first hearing
**Problem — hook geography:** **“The man who would carry the cross of Jesus was walking the other way.”** Mark/Luke say Simon was *coming out of the country* while Jesus was being *led out* — same road, opposite travel directions. **“The other way”** on audio can sound moral/spiritual (“walking away from Jesus”) before the next lines clarify geography. One logic beat lost.

**Problem — KJV vocabulary stop:** **“In the King James Bible, outside is ‘without.’”** Clever for readers; for a zero-knowledge *listener* it is a meta detour mid-story. They must hold Simon, sin offering, Jesus, and an archaic English synonym at once before the payoff.

**Problem — rapid typology stack:** Simon → sin offering → sanctuary blood → body burned outside → Jesus outside the gate → Hebrews imperative lands in ~40 seconds. A stranger can follow the *mood* but may not follow the *logic* on one pass.

**Minor — gospel harmonization blur:** After **“Mark says…”**, the line **“They put the cross on Simon’s back”** is narratorial glue; the laying-on detail is Luke’s. Not wrong, but attribution wobbles for a careful listener.

### Grace-anchored conviction
**Borderline FAIL on framing.**

- **“Turn on your own road, and carry his shame after him, freely this time”** is imperative-first discipleship. **“Simon was forced out. You are asked”** is the only grace note, and it frames freedom as *better compulsion*, not as *response to what Christ finished*.
- No fear/gain-loss stock phrases — good.
- But the emotional weight falls on **shame** and **carrying**, not on **“unto him”** (the Person). That tilts toward duty before gift.

### Freshness = faithful depth
**Strong.** Opposite-direction Simon, the KJV **“without”** thread, and shame-vs-atonement distinction are surprising about the *text*, not clickbait. This is the piece’s best asset.

### One thread spine
**Mostly holds:** forced cross-bearer → outside-the-camp pattern → Christ’s unique expiation → our reproach-bearing.

**Slip:** The biblical CTA verb — **“go forth… unto him”** — is quoted in the middle, then dropped. The close pivots to theological summary instead of returning to **him**. That weakens spine integrity at the landing.

**“The man who would carry the cross of Jesus was walking the other way.”** Not a template open. Concrete, tense, scroll-stopping. Loses points because **“the other way”** is ambiguous on first hearing and the hook doesn’t yet name Simon or the road collision.

### Landing score: **6/10**
Final line: **“Only the shame. He went out through that gate first, and the atoning, the dying, the finishing, that was his, with his own blood.”**

- **Not** a banned stock closer (“come to Jesus,” etc.).
- **But** it is a portable doctrinal coda — it could close several crucifixion/discipleship pieces, not only this Simon/Hebrews thread.
- It **does not** land on the piece’s own best invitation verb (**“go forth… unto him”**). It explains Christ’s work more than it invites the listener to *him*. For this project’s CTA-to-Jesus model, that is a weak landing.
- Under 8 → **REVISE** per your rubric.

---

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Rebuild the landing on the piece’s own verb — return to **“Let us go forth therefore unto him”** / **“without the camp”** and close by inviting the listener *to Jesus outside the gate*, not only clarifying that atonement was his alone.
2. Move the atonement guardrail (**“Only the shame… that was his, with his own blood”**) *before* or *inside* the imperative, so **“carry his shame”** cannot be heard as salvific contribution on one pass.
3. Clarify the hook geography in one plain line (Simon coming *in*, Jesus going *out*, same road) so **“walking the other way”** reads as direction, not rejection — and trim or defer the KJV **“without”** vocabulary lesson until the Hebrews quote makes it pay off.
