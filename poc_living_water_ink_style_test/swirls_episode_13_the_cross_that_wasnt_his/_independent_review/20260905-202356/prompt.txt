The man who would carry the cross of Jesus was walking the other way.

Mark says the soldiers "led him out to crucify him. And they compel one Simon a Cyrenian, who passed by, coming out of the country." Jesus, out of the city. Simon, in from the country. Same road. They put the cross on Simon's back. Nobody asked him. Luke says, "on him they laid the cross, that he might bear it after Jesus." Simon turns, and walks out too.

Now he is through the gate. In the King James Bible, outside is "without." The sin offering, an animal killed for the people's sin, had its blood carried into the sanctuary, God's holy tent. Its body went out, burned "without the camp." Then it says of Jesus: "Wherefore Jesus also, that he might sanctify the people with his own blood, suffered without the gate." Out there, where Simon is walking.

Hebrews turns to you: "Let us go forth therefore unto him without the camp, bearing his reproach." Reproach is shame. Bearing it is carrying it, as Simon carried the wood. Simon was forced out. You are asked. Turn on your own road, and carry his shame after him, freely this time. Only the shame. He went out through that gate first, and the atoning, the dying, the finishing, that was his, with his own blood.
