**Doctrine — orthodox claim, homiletical join.**  
Hebrews 13:11–13 really does map bodies burned “without the camp” → Jesus “without the gate” → “go forth… unto him.” That is not a hot take.

The stretch is Simon. Mark/Luke never mention the camp, the sin offering, or reproach. Hebrews never mentions Simon. “Out there, where Simon is walking” makes the Golgotha road and the Levitical burning-ground sound like one GPS pin. Hebrews’ link is theological, not “Simon is walking through the old camp.” Fine as illustration. Not what the text says Simon *is*.

“The sin offering, an animal killed for the people’s sin, had its blood carried into the sanctuary” over-smooths Hebrews 13:11. That verse is not every sin offering. It is the beasts *whose blood the high priest brings into the sanctuary*. Ordinary sin offerings were eaten. Precision slip, not a new gospel.

“The finishing” imports a John 19:30 beat this pericope never earns.

**Clarity — this is the fail point.**  
A listener with zero Bible knowledge can follow Simon on the road. They will not follow paragraph 3.

- “In the King James Bible, outside is ‘without.’” is a language lesson. It is the “notice the word” move. Then the ear must re-translate *without the camp* and *without the gate* as if camp and gate were the same place.
- “God’s holy tent” drops a tabernacle into a city-gate story with no map. Tent, camp, gate, road: four spaces, one blur.
- “Then it says of Jesus:” — *it* who? The sin-offering paraphrase was never named as Hebrews. A cold ear hears Leviticus say “Wherefore Jesus also…”. Attribution only arrives on the next verse: “Hebrews turns to you.”
- “Sanctify” is never glossed. “Reproach is shame” is. The piece knows to define hard words and skips the one at the proof hinge.
- Logic snag: “Bearing it is carrying it, as Simon carried the wood.” Then: “Only the shame.” Simon’s wood *is* the atoning cross. The script uses that wood as the shame-picture, then yanks the atonement off it. Clever on paper. A first hearing cannot hold both.

Hebrews 13:13’s destination is a Person: “unto him.” The paraphrase drops the Person and keeps the load: “carry his shame after him, freely this time.” “Freely this time” also has no first time for the listener. It sounds like a second attempt.
