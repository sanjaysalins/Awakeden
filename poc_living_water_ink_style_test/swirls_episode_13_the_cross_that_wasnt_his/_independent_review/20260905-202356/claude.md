## Independent Review — Narration (Simon of Cyrene / "without the gate")

**Doctrinal soundness:** Sound. The piece cites Mark 15:21, Luke 23:26, and Hebrews 13:11-13 to build a genuine typological argument (sin-offering blood carried into the sanctuary / body burned "without the camp" → Christ "suffered without the gate" → the church's call to "go forth... bearing his reproach"). No invented doctrine. Crucially, the closing line explicitly fences off the atonement from the listener's response — *"Only the shame. He went out through that gate first, and the atoning, the dying, the finishing, that was his, with his own blood"* — which correctly keeps salvation as Christ's alone and the CTA as identification/reproach-bearing, not works. This is a real doctrinal safeguard, not an accident.

**KJV verbatim:** Clean. Checked all four quotes word-for-word against KJV:
- Mark 15:20-21 excerpt — exact.
- Luke 23:26 excerpt ("on him they laid the cross, that he might bear it after Jesus") — exact.
- Hebrews 13:11 partial ("burned...without the camp") — exact.
- Hebrews 13:12 ("Wherefore Jesus also...suffered without the gate") — exact.
- Hebrews 13:13 ("Let us go forth therefore unto him without the camp, bearing his reproach") — exact.
No altered words found.

**Clarity on first hearing:** Mostly holds, with one soft spot. *"Now he is through the gate"* — the referent for "he" is technically resolvable (Simon, last-named subject) but arrives right before the passage pivots to talk about Jesus's own suffering "without the gate," so for a split second a listener could misassign the pronoun. Cheap fix: "Now **Simon** is through the gate." Separately, the jump into Levitical sin-offering mechanics ("blood carried into the sanctuary... body burned without the camp") is the single densest concept-introduction in the script — it's explained, but it's also the one place a zero-background listener has to do real work in a couple of seconds. Not disqualifying, but the tightest spot in the piece.

**Grace-anchored conviction:** Clean — no fear, no gain/loss, no self-interest framing. *"Simon was forced out. You are asked."* is an invitation, not a threat, and the piece is careful to say the listener carries "only the shame," not the atonement.

**Freshness = faithful depth:** Genuinely fresh entry point — most treatments of Simon of Cyrene stop at "he was forced to carry the cross"; this one ties it to the Hebrews 13 "outside the camp" typology, which is real, textual, and under-used. Orthodox in claim and landing.

**One thread spine:** Holds. Simon's literal walk out the gate → Christ's fulfillment of the sin-offering type "without the gate" → the listener's own call to "go forth... without the camp." No thread-swap.

**HOOK SCORE: 8** — *"The man who would carry the cross of Jesus was walking the other way"* is a genuine narrative hook (irony/curiosity: a cross-bearer walking the wrong direction), not a template opener ("Did you know/Imagine/What if"). Earns full credit.

**LANDING SCORE: 8** — *"He went out through that gate first, and the atoning, the dying, the finishing, that was his, with his own blood"* is built from this piece's own images (the gate, the atoning/dying/finishing triad, his own blood callback to the Hebrews 13:12 quote). It could not cleanly close a different piece — it's load-bearing on the specific typology just built. Not a stock closer.

VERDICT: PASS
