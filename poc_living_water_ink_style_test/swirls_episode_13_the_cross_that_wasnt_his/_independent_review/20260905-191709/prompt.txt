The man who carried the cross of Jesus did not volunteer. He was grabbed.

Mark gives him one line: "And they compel one Simon a Cyrenian, who passed by, coming out of the country, the father of Alexander and Rufus, to bear his cross."

Compel. That is the word for pressing a man into service: a soldier could stop anyone on the road and make him carry a load. Simon was passing by, just in from the country, likely for Passover. Wrong road, wrong minute. They put the cross on his back, and he walked behind Jesus to the place of a skull.

Then Mark names the sons. Alexander and Rufus. No explanation. His readers must have known them. Years later, in a letter to the church in Rome, Paul writes: "Salute Rufus chosen in the Lord, and his mother and mine." Hard not to read that as the same Rufus. Simon's boy, chosen. Simon's wife, a mother to Paul.

Simon never chose that cross. It was forced on him, and it became the door his whole family walked through. The cross that wasn't his became his family's whole story. Whatever got laid on your back that you never asked for, look who is walking in front of you.
