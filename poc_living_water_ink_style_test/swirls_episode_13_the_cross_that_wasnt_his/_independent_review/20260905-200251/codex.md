# Independent review — codex (OK, 34s)

Hook score: 8/10  
Landing score: 7/10

The Scripture inside quotation marks is verbatim KJV. The Mark quotation joins Mark 15:20–21 but does not alter the quoted words. The Luke and Hebrews quotations are also exact.

Specific findings:

- “The man who carried the cross of Jesus was walking the other way” overstates an inference. Simon was “coming out of the country,” but Scripture does not explicitly say he was travelling in the precise opposite direction or that he physically “turns around.” Because the hook depends on that detail, qualify it rather than present it as textual fact.

- “Hebrews explains why outside matters” risks implying that Hebrews is explaining Simon’s experience. Hebrews 13 explains the theological significance of Christ suffering outside the gate; it never identifies Simon as an illustration of “bearing his reproach.” The connection is legitimate application, but it must be marked as analogy rather than exegesis.

- “The blood of the sin offering went in, into the sanctuary” is broadly faithful but imprecise. Hebrews speaks of the blood being brought into the sanctuary “by the high priest for sin,” while the bodies were burned outside. Retaining those details would make the contrast clearer for listeners unfamiliar with the sacrificial system.

- “Bearing it is carrying it, as Simon carried the wood” collapses two different objects: Hebrews commands believers to bear Christ’s reproach, not His cross or its wood. Simon’s physical burden can picture discipleship, but it does not define what “bearing his reproach” means.

- “Simon was forced out. You are asked” is a sharp contrast, but Hebrews 13:13 addresses believers already sanctified by Jesus’ blood. The narration moves too quickly from Christ’s completed, grace-giving work to the listener’s voluntary action.

- “Turn around on your own road, go out through that gate, and carry his shame after him” stacks imperatives and makes human resolve the emotional climax. “Carry his shame” is also potentially misleading: Christ is not personally shameful; believers bear reproach because they identify with the rejected Christ.

- “Freely this time” is memorable and specific to the Simon image, so the landing is not stock. Nevertheless, it can suggest that freely chosen suffering improves upon Simon’s compelled service. The invitation should arise explicitly from “his own blood” having sanctified His people, with willing identification as the response to grace.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Distinguish clearly between what the biblical texts state and the proposed Simon–Hebrews analogy.
2. Anchor the final invitation in Christ’s completed sanctifying work before calling the listener to bear reproach.
3. Qualify the inferred “walking the other way” reversal and replace the ambiguous phrase “carry his shame.”
