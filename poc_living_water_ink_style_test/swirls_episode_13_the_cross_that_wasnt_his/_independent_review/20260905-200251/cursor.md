## Independent adversarial review

### KJV verbatim — FAIL on one quoted line

**Hebrews 13:13 (altered word order):** The script quotes *"Let us go forth therefore unto him without the camp, bearing his reproach."* Standard KJV is *"Let us **therefore go forth** unto him without the camp, bearing his reproach."* `therefore` and `go forth` are swapped. In a narration engine that treats quoted text as binding, that is not a minor elision — it is a misquote.

**Passing quotes:**
- Luke: *"on him they laid the cross, that he might bear it after Jesus."* — exact.
- Hebrews 13:12: *"Wherefore Jesus also, that he might sanctify the people with his own blood, suffered without the gate."* — exact.
- Mark splice: *"led him out to crucify him. And they compel one Simon a Cyrenian, who passed by, coming out of the country."* — each word is KJV; content is elided, not altered.
- *"without the camp"* — exact.

---

### Doctrinal soundness — mostly sound, one soft spot

The Simon ↔ Hebrews “outside the camp” link is standard evangelical typology, not contrarian. Blood in / body out follows Leviticus–Hebrews logic correctly.

**Soft spot:** *"Bearing it is carrying it, as Simon carried the wood."* Mark/Luke present compelled cross-bearing as historical fact, not shame-bearing. Hebrews 13:13 says *"bearing his reproach,"* not bearing the cross. The bridge is preached typology, not Synoptic wording — defensible in a sermon, but the script presents it as if Simon’s scene *is* the reproach image. A careful listener could hear cross = shame as imported, not drawn from the Gospel text itself.

**Sanctification underused:** You quote *"that he might sanctify the people with his own blood"* but the landing never rests there. The close is action (*turn, go out, carry*) without tying invitation back to *his blood* already did the sanctifying work.

---

### Clarity on first hearing — good hook, dense middle

**Hook:** *"The man who carried the cross of Jesus was walking the other way"* — a zero-Bible listener may briefly think “Jesus?” before Mark names Simon. Fixable once the story starts, but not instant.

**Middle density:** *"Hebrews explains why outside matters. Its old word for outside is 'without.'"* assumes book familiarity and stacks two abstractions (outside / without / camp / gate) in quick succession. A stranger can follow Simon; the Levitical detour is where you may lose them.

**Narrator gloss:** *"Reproach is shame"* is helpful plain English.

---

### Grace-anchored conviction — mostly clean, slight works tilt at the close

No fear, gain-loss, or self-interest framing. *"Simon was forced out. You are asked"* and *"freely this time"* are strong grace contrasts.

**Risk at landing:** Three imperatives — *"Turn around… go out… carry his shame"* — with no rest in gospel indicatives (what Christ has already done for you). For this channel’s bar, that edges toward *cost-of-discipleship exhortation* rather than *Spirit-convicted invitation*. Not heresy, but the emotional weight is on what *you* carry, not on *him* who sanctified you with his blood one sentence earlier.

---

### Freshness = faithful depth — PASS with caveat

Simon inbound / Jesus outbound on the same road is a real textual detail, not clickbait. The Hebrews “without” wordplay is orthodox depth, not a contested reading.

**Caveat:** The Simon→reproach→wood chain is creative synthesis across corpora. Fresh, but the Synoptic accounts do not invite that read on their own — flag as authorial typology, not as if Mark/Luke said it.
