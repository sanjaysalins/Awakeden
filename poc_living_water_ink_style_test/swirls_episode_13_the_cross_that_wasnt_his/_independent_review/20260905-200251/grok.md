**Doctrinal soundness — orthodox spine, one oversold join, one inverted close.**

Hebrews 13:11–13 is the letter’s own argument: bodies burned *without the camp* → Jesus *suffered without the gate* to *sanctify the people with his own blood* → *Let us go forth therefore unto him*. Not a hot take. Not clickbait.

Simon-as-illustration is *your* glue. Hebrews never names him. *“Out there, where Simon is walking”* plus *“Hebrews turns to you”* sells the join as if 13:12–13 is commenting on Simon’s road, then pivoting to a scroll-stopping stranger. *“Hebrews turns to you”* also mis-audiences 13:13. *“Let us go forth”* is a corporate word to believers already under that blood, to leave the safe camp and bear his shame. Using it as the stranger’s altar call blurs conversion and costly followership. Not heresy. Application stretch.

The close then inverts the verse’s own order. You quoted the gospel: *“that he might sanctify the people with his own blood.”* You never return to it. 13:13’s destination is *unto him*. Your destination is *carry his shame*. Person becomes homework.

*“The blood of the sin offering went in, into the sanctuary. The body went out, burned ‘without the camp.’”* — fair 60s compression of 13:11 (the beasts *whose blood is brought into the sanctuary… for sin*). It is not every sin offering. Do not let *the sin offering* harden into a class claim.
