## Independent adversarial review

### KJV verbatim
All **quoted** strings match KJV wording (elisions only, no altered words inside quotation marks):

- Mark splice: *"led him out to crucify him. And they compel one Simon a Cyrenian, who passed by, coming out of the country."* — matches Mark 15:20–21.
- Luke: *"on him they laid the cross, that he might bear it after Jesus."* — matches Luke 23:26.
- Hebrews 13:12–13 — exact.

**Outside quotes**, narration voice uses *"carried"* (hook) and *"carry his shame"* where KJV has *"bear"* / *"bearing his reproach"*. That is allowed in prose, but it matters for clarity (below).

---

### Doctrinal soundness
**Mostly sound.** The Levitical sin-offering → Jesus *"without the gate"* → Hebrews 13:13 chain is **text-backed** (Hebrews 13:11–13 makes the typology explicitly).

**Problems:**

1. **Simon → believer typology is homiletical, not textual.** Scripture never links Simon of Cyrene to Hebrews 13. The line *"Bearing it is carrying it, as Simon carried the wood"* treats a narrative detail as intentional typology. Preachable, but adversarially: that is **inference dressed as exegesis**. A hostile listener can hear “the Bible says you replay Simon’s role.”

2. ***"Only the shame"* risks an unbalanced discipleship picture.** Believers share reproach; they also share resurrection hope (Rom 8:17). *"Only the shame"* is stark and could be misheard as “Christianity = voluntary humiliation,” especially with no faith/rest beat before the imperative.

3. ***"Simon, in from the country"* vs Mark’s *"coming out of the country."*** Mark says Simon was leaving the countryside toward the city area; the script **reverses the KJV phrase’s surface grammar** into directional shorthand. Not heresy, but it presents reconstruction as clean fact.

---

### Clarity on first hearing
**This is the biggest failure.**

1. **Hook contradicts the body on first pass.**  
   *"The man who carried the cross of Jesus was walking the other way."*  
   Past tense **“carried”** implies the cross is **already on him** while he walks the wrong way. Three sentences later: *"They put the cross on Simon's back."*  
   A listener must **unhear** the opening. That is a logic bump, not clever irony.

2. **“The man” is anonymous for several seconds.** Many hearers will picture **Jesus** first, not Simon.

3. **Middle pivot is seminary-speed.**  
   *"In the King James Bible, outside is 'without.'"* → sin offering blood/body split → Jesus *"without the gate"* → *"Hebrews turns to you"*  
   A zero-Bible listener may lose the thread between Simon’s gate and Levitical ritual before the CTA arrives.

4. ***"The rest, he finished, with his own blood"* — “the rest” of what?**  
   Atonement is clear to you; on one hearing it is **undefined**. The grace note lands weakly because the referent is missing.

5. ***"Simon turns, and walks out too"* — “turns” is inferred**, not quoted. Minor, but presented as story fact.

---

### Grace-anchored conviction
**Mixed.**

- Good: *"Simon was forced out. You are asked"* and *"freely this time"* — voluntary following, not compulsion-as-gospel.
- Good: *"The rest, he finished, with his own blood"* — Christ did the atoning work; believer does not “finish” salvation.

- Weak: The CTA is **behavior-first**: *"Turn on your own road, and carry his shame after him."* No faith, no “unto him,” no invitation to **person** before duty. Hebrews 13:13 is *"Let us go forth therefore unto him"* — the script **drops the Christward “unto him”** and collapses the exhortation to shame-bearing. That skews toward **performance framing** even if unintended.

No overt fear/gain-loss, but the closing **invites a task more than a Person**.

---

### Freshness = faithful depth
**Strong entry point.** Opposite-direction collision on the same road is a **real, overlooked detail** in Mark/Luke — good faithful freshness.

**Weaker at the bridge:** Simon-to-reproach mapping is **imaginative parallel**, not a fresh reading *in* the text. Freshness there comes from rhetoric, not exegesis.

---

### One thread spine (hook → middle → CTA)
**Mostly holds** on *outside / out / without / bearing after Jesus*.

**Frays at the hinge:** Hook sells **directional collision** (*"walking the other way"*); middle sells **ritual geography** (*"without the camp"*); CTA sells **voluntary compulsion contrast** (*"freely this time"*). All related, but the hook’s “wrong-way walker **already carrying**” image is **not the same thread** the body actually tells (empty-handed passerby → cross imposed → then outbound). That is **micro thread-swap inside the opening beat**.

---

### Hook score: **7/10**
Non-template, visual, scroll-stopping potential — but capped because:
- **“carried” vs “they put the cross on Simon’s back”** fights the payoff;
- **“the man”** delays Simon;
- **“the other way”** exceeds explicit Mark wording.

---

### Landing score: **8/10**
*"...freely this time. Only the shame. He went out through that gate first. The rest, he finished, with his own blood."* is **piece-specific**, not a stock *“come to Jesus”* closer. Christ’s finished work is the right landing note.

Held below 9 because **“the rest”** is vague on first hearing and **“Only the shame”** is emotionally narrow without the Hebrews *“unto him”* anchor.

---

**Under the stated rule (either score &lt; 8 → REVISE), Hook at 7 forces REVISE.**

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. **Rewrite the hook so tense and image match the body** — Simon was coming *in* (or passing *toward* the city) **before** the cross was laid on him; do not say he “carried” it while “walking the other way.”
2. **Restore the Hebrews landing shape: “go forth unto him” before “bearing reproach/shame”** — invite the Person, then the cost; clarify what *“the rest”* means in one plain phrase (atonement/death/blood).
3. **Slow or simplify the “without” pivot for cold listeners** — one bridge sentence tying Simon outside the gate to Hebrews’ “without the camp,” and **qualify** the Simon→believer parallel so it reads as illustration, not “the text says Simon was your model.”
