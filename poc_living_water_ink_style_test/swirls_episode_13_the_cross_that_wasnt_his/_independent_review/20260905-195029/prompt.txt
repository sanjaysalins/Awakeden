The man who carried the cross of Jesus was walking the other way.

Mark says the soldiers "led him out to crucify him. And they compel one Simon a Cyrenian, who passed by, coming out of the country." They put the cross on his back. Jesus, led out of the city. Simon, coming in from the country. He never chose it. Luke says, "on him they laid the cross, that he might bear it after Jesus."

Hebrews explains what "outside" means. The offerings whose blood was carried into the sanctuary for sin were burned outside the camp, never brought back to where the people lived. Then the letter says the same of Jesus: "Wherefore Jesus also, that he might sanctify the people with his own blood, suffered without the gate."

Outside is where the atonement was made. Simon carried the wood out there, forced. You are not forced. Hebrews puts the invitation in its own words: "Let us go forth therefore unto him without the camp, bearing his reproach." Go out to him, the one who already carried the shame for you. Freely, this time. After Jesus.
