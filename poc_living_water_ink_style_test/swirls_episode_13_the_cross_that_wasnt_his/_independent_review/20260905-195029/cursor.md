### Doctrinal soundness
**Mostly orthodox.** Hebrews 13:11–13’s outside-the-camp → outside-the-gate → go-forth logic is **the letter’s own typology**, not invented doctrine. No contrarian reading, no works-salvation pitch.

**Skeptical flags:**

1. **Simon is not in Hebrews.** The piece braids two passages by theme (“outside,” compelled vs. free). That’s legitimate homiletics, but the line *“Hebrews explains what ‘outside’ means”* presents the braid as if Hebrews is **directly explaining Simon’s scene**. It isn’t. That’s a rhetorical sleight-of-hand a careful listener may feel.

2. **Hebrews 13:13 is mis-audienced as generic “invitation.”** The line *“Hebrews puts the invitation in its own words: ‘Let us go forth therefore unto him without the camp, bearing his reproach.’”* — in context, that is a **corporate exhortation to believers** to identify with Christ’s shame and leave the religious center. Calling it *“the invitation”* and pairing it with *“You are not forced”* reframes costly discipleship as evangelistic free-will language. Not heresy, but **application stretch** — conversion, voluntary response, and costly followership get blurred.

3. *“Outside is where the atonement was made.”* — Imprecise. Atonement is in **Christ’s blood and suffering** (stated correctly in the Hebrews quote); “outside” is the **typological location**, not the causal engine of atonement. A seminary-trained ear will twitch.

4. **Silent object swap:** Luke has Simon **bear his cross after Jesus**; Hebrews has **bearing his reproach**; the landing has Jesus **carry the shame for you**. Cross → reproach → shame is preachable, but the script **never names the shift**. A listener can wonder if the piece changed what Simon/Jesus/the listener are each bearing without warning.

No self-contradiction, but the **Simon human story and the Hebrews theology do not hand off cleanly** to a stranger’s ear. The middle is seminary-assumed.

### One thread spine (hook → middle → CTA)
**Mostly one thread: “outside / out / forth.”**

- Hook: wrong-direction Simon, compelled cross-bearing  
- Middle: outside = sacrifice pattern; outside = Jesus’s suffering; Simon forced out there  
- CTA: go **out** to him, **freely**, **after Jesus**

TOP FIXES:
1. **Onboard “outside” in plain speech before Hebrews** — one beat that Jesus was led *out* of the city to die, away from the crowd; *then* let Hebrews’ camp/gate language land (and optionally unify “camp” and “gate” as the same “outside” idea).
