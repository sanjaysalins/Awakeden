## Independent Adversarial Review — Narration ("The man who carried the cross...")

**KJV verbatim check** — all four quoted spans checked word-for-word against KJV:
- Mark 15:20b–21a: "led him out to crucify him. And they compel one Simon a Cyrenian, who passed by, coming out of the country." — exact, but silently truncated (see Fix #2 below).
- Luke 23:26b: "on him they laid the cross, that he might bear it after Jesus." — exact.
- Hebrews 13:12: "Wherefore Jesus also, that he might sanctify the people with his own blood, suffered without the gate." — exact.
- Hebrews 13:13: "Let us go forth therefore unto him without the camp, bearing his reproach." — exact.

No altered words found. Good.

**Doctrinal soundness / clarity — real problem here.** The line "Outside is where the atonement was made" directly follows the sentence "The offerings whose blood was carried **into** the sanctuary for sin were burned **outside** the camp." A first-time listener has just been told atonement-blood goes *in*; two sentences later they're told atonement happens *outside*. Nothing in the script explains that this is the Hebrews irony (Christ's once-for-all atonement breaking the old in/out pattern) — it just asserts the opposite of what was said seconds earlier. That's the "logic trick / self-contradiction on first hearing" the brief warns against. It's fixable (one clause distinguishing "the old system" from "the greater sacrifice"), but as written it's a real comprehension trap, not just a compression.

**Citation fidelity.** The Mark quote ends "...coming out of the country." with a hard period and no ellipsis, but the actual verse continues "...the father of Alexander and Rufus, **to bear his cross**." That omitted clause is the single most on-topic phrase in the whole verse for a piece about cross-carrying — dropping it silently (rather than including it or marking the elision) undersells the strongest textual support for the hook's own claim.

**Freshness / thread**: genuine, textually-grounded overlooked detail (Simon's direction of travel vs. Jesus's), not a contrarian reading. The outside/without-the-camp/without-the-gate thread runs consistently from hook to landing — no thread-swap.

**Grace-anchored conviction**: clean. "You are not forced" / "Freely, this time" — invitation, not pressure or fear framing.

**HOOK SCORE: 8/10** — "The man who carried the cross of Jesus was walking the other way" is not a template opener, creates real curiosity from an accurate, specific detail (Simon was heading into the city while Jesus was led out).

**LANDING SCORE: 8/10** — "Go out to him... Freely, this time. After Jesus" is built from this piece's own Luke/Hebrews vocabulary (forced vs. free, "after Jesus" as both physical position and CTA) — not a swappable stock closer.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Fix the "blood carried in... atonement made outside" sequence — as written it reads as self-contradictory to a listener with no background; add one clause distinguishing the old sacrificial pattern from Christ's fulfillment before asserting "outside is where the atonement was made."
2. Either extend the Mark quote to include "...to bear his cross" or mark the elision — silently cutting the verse right before its most relevant clause weakens the piece's own textual grounding.
3. Simplify or slow the Hebrews 13:11 sacrificial-system sentence — it's the densest, least-familiar content in the piece and currently gets one compressed sentence with no room to land before moving on.
