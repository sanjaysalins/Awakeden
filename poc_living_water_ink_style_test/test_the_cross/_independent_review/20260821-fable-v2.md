# Independent creative review — Fable (v2 pass)

Reviewed: SWIRLS_OF_LIFE_SERIES_PLAN_V2.md
Method: fresh agent (Fable model), read the plan + NORTH_STAR_PROMPT.md, given the user's own design brief, asked to be genuinely critical.

## 1. Does it deliver "never seen that before"? Split verdict.

Genuinely fresh: Numbers 19 (the standout), Melchizedek, Simon of Cyrene ("none" in the motif column is the boldest creative decision), Nathanael + Jacob's Ladder pairing.

Safe and expected: Peter on the water, Talitha Cumi, She Loved Much, ten lepers, foot-washing, Martha — "Sunday-school A-list." The restart verified novelty against the project's own catalog, but the brief's bar is the *viewer's* memory, not internal non-duplication. For ~6 of 14 shorts the only new thing is the ink grammar.

"Every lens — people, location, event" is under-delivered: all 15 picks are the same lens (third-person narrative encounter). No location-as-lens, no object's-eye view, no psalm/prophet's own voice. Simon of Cyrene is the only perspective experiment.

## 2. Season shape

The plant/payoff device (Nazareth citing Naaman + Zarephath) is genuine, not a trick — Jesus makes both connections in his own recorded words. But shorts have no viewing order, so the "aha" mostly won't land unless made diegetic (e.g. the cited pages physically pinned into the Nazareth long-form's own margins).

Real hole: Nazareth is the one story where Jesus is present and rejected. The plan's dead-ink thesis ("clears when Jesus is present") doesn't say what happens on that page. No motif column for the long-forms at all.

Middle sags: episodes 9-13 are five consecutive standalone encounters with no structural function.

Finale insight: episode 2 (red heifer, corpse-uncleanness) and the finale (a corpse that leaves the tomb alive) are secretly the same thread — worth saying explicitly.

## 3. Weakest link: Martha (Careful and Troubled)

Fray requires fear/doubt; Martha's text is anxiety about serving, neither. Fatal: Luke records no resolution to her anxiety — the series' signature hard-cut device would have to be invented, violating the plan's own "nothing from thin air" rule. Every other Fray episode has an attested cut (the caught hand, "be not afraid" + a raised child); Martha's does not exist in the text.

## 4. Proposed fix

Rebuild the foot-washing episode around John 13:10-11 — "ye are clean, but not all." Judas, washed, not clean. Gives the season its one unresolved Stain, which retroactively makes every cleared stain mean more (mercy, not mechanism). Textually verbatim, no new verification needed.

## Bottom line

The dead-ink system itself is genuinely original. The slate is only half so — OT picks and Simon deliver the brief; the NT middle is standard canon with new ink on top.
