## The new standing gate — apply to every episode below

Before any episode in this list is finalized (not just narrated — at
candidate-selection time):

1. **Who is this for? Who NEEDS to hear this?** Name ONE real kind of
   person — not "everyone." (This restates the main engine's own G8
   question — the constitution's #4 — now pulled forward to apply at
   backlog-selection time too, not only at narration-draft time.)
2. **What is their takeaway?** Not a fact — a CHANGE: one true thing about
   Christ that reorders how they see Him. Information alone fails this.
3. **What is our intention with this episode?** The specific strategic
   reason THIS story earns its place THIS season — a motif the system
   hasn't proven, an OT→NT rhyme, a real gap in what's been covered, a
   named risk worth taking. "It's a good story" is not an answer.

## The dead-ink system (unchanged from v4)

Swirl (Christ's presence, Stage 0→3, OT-capped at 1-2 unless fulfilment is
on-page) · Stain (moral sin/guilt OR ceremonial/Levitical uncleanness, never
a third case) · Fray (fear/doubt ONLY, earned by the text's own vocabulary)
· high-tide exclusion (`stainDose + swirlStage <= 4`) · swirl-only when
forcing a diagnosis would be invention.

### OPEN QUESTION for the panel — a possible fourth grammar

Fable's review surfaced a real gap: **hardness/refusal has no mark.** The
Nazareth crowd's wrath (#14), the Pharisee's self-trust, Jonah's anger,
Peter's "thou shalt never" — none use fear/doubt vocabulary (not Fray), none
use sin/unclean vocabulary about themselves (not Stain — they don't confess
anything). Proposed fix, NOT yet adopted: hardness reads as the paper
**not taking** the swirl where that person stands — a dry patch on a page
otherwise at Stage 3, like wax resist. Diagnosis-free (states nothing about
what's specifically wrong with them), and it's load-bearing for #14 before
that long-form builds. **This is a request FOR the panel, not a decision**:
does "wax resist" count as a fourth dead-ink category (violating the
"never a third case" discipline that makes the system teachable), or is it
categorically different — an absence-of-mark, not a new mark — and
therefore fine?

## Part 1 — Reassessing the 5 remaining v4 items

### #6 Talitha Cumi (Mark 5:21-24, 35-43) — REDESIGN before build, not next

- Problem found: the sandwich structure puts two diagnoses on one page.
  Jairus is Fray ("Be not afraid, only believe," 5:36 — Jesus's own words).
  The bleeding woman is Stain/uncleanness — but that would be the
  **fifth** ceremonial-uncleanness Stain this season (heifer, Naaman, Nain,
  the nine already shipped). That category is proven; a fifth doesn't earn
  its place.
- **Fix:** Jairus-POV only. The woman stays undiagnosed on the page — one
  panel, a blur at the hem, camera stays on Jairus's face.
- Who: the one who asked, and it got worse while they waited — watching
  Jesus stop for someone else while their own crisis burns.
- Takeaway: Jesus isn't troubled by "too late." He stopped on purpose, then
  turned to Jairus, not the crowd. *Talitha cumi* — Mark's own preserved
  Aramaic — is the hook.
- Intention: the "too late" person hasn't been named yet this season.

### #9 The Woman at the Border (Mark 7:24-30) — RE-AIM, keep swirl-only

- Problem found: correctly swirl-only (the daughter's affliction is
  demonic, not Levitical — forcing a Stain would misdiagnose it), but the
  "outsider/border" framing is the season's most over-used person (Sheba,
  Naaman, the Samaritan leper, Simon of Cyrene, Nathanael — 6 of 15 already
  lean outsider).
- **Fix — retitle:** *"Yet the Dogs"* or *"The Crumb."* Drop "border" from
  the frame entirely.
- Who: the one who's asked and got what felt like silence or a no, and is
  about to stop asking.
- Takeaway: His hard word was a door held open, not shut — she treats a
  crumb as enough, and He calls that "great faith" (Matt 15:28).
- Intention: the only episode where a human presses Jesus and He visibly
  yields the exchange — a structural beat the season has never used.

### #12 Ye Are Not All Clean (John 13:1-30) — GREENLIT, build next

- Strongest of the five, unchanged design, ready to build now — no plan
  overhead. See "Immediate recommendation" below.
- Who: the insider who's been at the table for years, already served,
  already washed, and is quietly already gone.
- Takeaway: Jesus washed the feet of the man He knew would betray Him.
  Grace wasn't withheld — it was refused, sop still in hand. The basin is
  still there.
- Intention: fills the moral-sin Stain gap (4 of 5 shipped Stains are
  ceremonial uncleanness, only 1 — She Loved Much — is moral sin). "Ye are
  not all clean" is Jesus's own clean/unclean vocabulary, moral not
  ceremonial — the tag is earned to the letter. `stainDose + swirlStage <=
  4` caps Judas at dose 1 while the room runs Stage 3 — a small dark dot
  walking out of a page alive with light. That's correct theology, not a
  constraint to work around. Landing on "and it was night" (13:30) — no
  CTA — is the sharpest available fix for the landing-fatigue problem
  already logged in memory.

### #14 This Day Is This Scripture Fulfilled (Luke 4:16-30, long-form) — KEYSTONE, sequence after #4+#5 are live

- Luke 4:25-27: Jesus cites the widow of Zarephath (shipped ep 5) and
  Naaman (shipped ep 4) by name — the season's own earlier pages, read back
  to it by Christ Himself.
- Who: the one for whom Jesus is too familiar to be Lord. "Is not this
  Joseph's son?"
- Takeaway: the most explicit "this day" in the Gospels, met with a cliff.
  Familiarity is its own unbelief. He closes the book mid-verse (stops
  before "the day of vengeance," 4:20) and walks through the crowd's
  middle.
- Intention: **use the format's own native device — the sketchbook turns
  back to pages 4 and 5.** No other format can do this. This is the reason
  the season is a season, not a playlist. Depends on the wax-resist
  decision above for the crowd's wrath.

### #15 Road to Emmaus (Luke 24:13-35, long-form, finale) — UNCHANGED, keep last

Fully earns its place as designed in v4. Luke 24:27 — "beginning at Moses
and all the prophets… the things concerning himself" — is the series'
thesis statement in Christ's own mouth; if it isn't already the series
tagline, it should become one.

## Part 3 — Season-level gaps (Fable's review, unedited findings)

- **Stain sub-type is lopsided:** 4 of 5 shipped Stains are ceremonial
  uncleanness, only 1 is moral sin. **Rule going forward: no more
  uncleanness Stains this season** — every new Stain (Judas, Genesis 3, the
  publican, Isaiah 6) is moral, rebalancing to roughly even.
- **"Outsider" is worn out:** ~6 of 15 planned episodes lean on "the
  unexpected outsider welcomed." The under-served person is the
  **insider** — the one who's been in it for years and is hard, resentful,
  performing, or leaving (Judas, the Nazareth crowd, Jonah, the Pharisee,
  the disciples in the boat). New picks should skew here.
- **Healing miracles dominate:** 6 of 10 shipped episodes are a body healed
  or raised. Nothing yet from a parable, a prophet-as-person, a Psalm, Acts,
  an epistle, or Genesis 1-27/Exodus/David/Daniel/Jonah. The candidate list
  above adds Genesis x2, 2 Samuel, Isaiah, Ezekiel, Daniel, Jonah, Acts,
  and the season's first parable.
- **OT/NT is drifting NT:** all 5 remaining v4 items are NT. Left alone the
  season ends 5 OT / 10 NT for a series whose spine is the whole canon.
  Insert OT builds between the remaining NT ones (see season map below).
- **Persons of the Trinity:** the Spirit has never appeared on a page
  (Ezekiel 37 fixes this); the Father is never who Jesus turns to on-page
  (Gethsemane fixes this, doctrine-gated).
- **Landing fatigue** (already logged in memory
  `feedback_swirls_landing_fatigue`): #12's "and it was night" and Jonah
  4's unanswered question are real FORM fixes, not just wording fixes —
  both close on a question or a withheld resolution instead of a CTA
  phrase.
