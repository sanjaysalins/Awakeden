ArkAIology skills — what Awakeden's sketch storytelling should take

Independent review, 2026-07-28. Lens: fast-paced REENACTMENT storytelling (stories and events brought alive, every piece landing on Jesus) in the proven editorial-sketch style. Verdicts: ADOPT (as-is) · ADAPT (take the idea, refit) · SKIP.
ADOPTcast-bible — the crown jewel, already proven here today.
  • Canon sheet per figure (KJV constraints → deliberate inventions → ONE signature garment) + a COMMITTED anchor portrait + verbatim canon text in every prompt + name-what-drifted re-rolls.
  • Today's evidence: Seeker + Jesus held their faces across all 12 spreads and the taste piece. This is the backbone of reenactment — a witness the audience recognizes episode after episode.
  • Adaptation already made: one anchor PER STYLE FAMILY (canon text is shared; anchor PNGs don't cross styles).
ADAPTvox-motion — a documentary data engine; we take its five best organs, not the body.
  • VerseQuoteCard grammar — word-by-word KJV reveal, gold marker sweeping the key phrase, reference stamp. THIS is the fix for "the captions could be better": the verse should arrive word-timed with the narration, not sit as static caps.
  • Paper-native transitions — paperRip / inkSwipe / halftone-dissolve between spreads. Faster, more physical cuts than my crossfades; exactly the fast-pace lever.
  • MarkerCircle + ArrowCallout — hand-drawn wobble ellipse and arrows that point AT details in the art. The sketch style's native "look HERE" gesture for storytelling beats.
  • The grain-boil layer — deterministic paper-grain re-seat so a held frame never reads frozen. Free life on every quiet spread.
  • The motion-director governor — plan beats against the REAL stills, hard caps (1 beat per spread, ~6 per piece), rationale required. The "spread thin" discipline as code, not vibes.
  • Leave behind: charts/donuts/bars (data language, not story language — except a rare CountUp for a "40 days" beat), the Bangers font, navy rules.
ADAPTmixed-media-explainer — wrong look, RIGHT battle scars.
  • Multi-stage hard-cut sequences — the single most important port for reenactment: never ask one clip to morph a state change (walls falling, water rising, a building going up). Stage stills, image-chained; camera-only motion per stage; the CUT carries the event. This is how Jericho, the Red Sea, and the flood get told fast.
  • The stuck-shot rule — two real content failures after rewording → switch models; never silently fall back to a static push; a stuck shot is a USER decision.
  • Period-lock by naming the correct alternative — converges with our own locked rule (independent confirmation from their Corinthian-columns saga).
  • Leave behind: the flat die-cut style itself, kling2_6 default (rejected in this repo — our tiers stay Seedance 1.5 calm / Kling 3.0 action).
ADAPTsound-design — two jewels for the series' EARS.
  • "The score counts, the Text speaks" — a recurring series MOTIF whose cues map instruments to CONTENT beats (not mood soup); mid-episode cues end unresolved, the motif resolves only where the episode lands (for us: always on Jesus). Our cold-to-warm turn is a primitive version — this is its grown form.
  • Score near-silence under quoted Scripture — one low reverent sustained tone while the Text speaks. Instant reverence, and it makes the red-letter voice land harder.
  • The 4-rule reader-voice test (verbatim + spoken-speech + ≥5 words + lands-harder) sharpens our existing multi-voice rule into a checklist.
ADAPT$0 reveal primitives (visual_bakeoff, not a skill but too good to leave)
  • text_mask_reveal — Scripture physically BECOMES the scene (the verse's letterforms as the reveal mask). A stunning verse-pivot device.
  • radial_iris / vertical_peel — artifact-becomes-the-scene reveals; hunt_and_lock — the camera hunts across a spread then LOCKS onto the detail. Fast-pace visual grammar, all local ffmpeg.
SKIPhiggsfield-video-explainer — generic pipeline, paid server-side assembler, generic styles. ArkAIology's own skills tell you not to use it for house work; nothing here that mixed-media doesn't carry better.
