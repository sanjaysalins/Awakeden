# Independent viewer review: Film C and Film D (John 21:1-17)

## Film C — "The Second Fire" (ends on the meal)

**1. Newcomer**
- **Who:** Jesus and His friend Peter, who let Him down.
- **What:** Peter said "I don't know Him" three times. Jesus came back from the dead and cooked him breakfast on a beach. Then He asked Peter three times, "Do you love me?"
- **Why it matters:** Jesus fed the man who failed Him and gave him a job. He did not send him away.

**Words or lines a newcomer would not get on first hearing:**
- **"Peter is hurt."** Someone with English as a second language may hear "injured," not "sad." This is the biggest risk.
- **"It is the Lord."** Nobody is named as the speaker. A newcomer may not know that "the Lord" means Jesus.
- **"Peter jumps into the sea."** No reason is given. It sounds odd without "to get to Jesus faster."
- **"a fire of coals."** Most people will get it, but "coals" is not a common word.
- **"Lovest thou me?" / "thou knowest"** are old English. They are explained straight after, so this is fine.
- **"Feed my sheep" → "my people."** The jump from sheep to people works because the narrator explains it.
- **"went back to fishing."** It assumes you know Peter used to be a fisherman. "Back" carries it, just barely.

**2. Bible reader:** Yes. The two-fires link is known but not common. "Jesus fed Peter before He asked Peter to feed anyone" is new to most readers, and it's true to the order of the text: verse 13, then "when they had dined" in verse 15.

**3. Curiosity gap:**
- The opening asks: "Is there one night you keep replaying?" That raises a bigger question: is my worst failure the end?
- It pays off for Peter. It never comes back to the viewer, so the "you" opened in the first line is never closed.
- **Score: 7/10.**

**4. The five questions**
- **Who it's for:** Someone carrying one failure they can't put down. It fits the target viewer well.
- **Take-away:** Jesus doesn't start with your failure. He feeds you first, then trusts you again.
- **Global audience:** Mostly OK. Fix "Peter is hurt" and the unnamed "It is the Lord."
- **Biblically accurate:** Mostly yes.
  - Every quote matches the KJV word for word: "I am not" (18:17/25), "It is the Lord" (21:7), "a fire of coals" (18:18, 21:9), "Come and dine" (21:12), "Lovest thou me?" (21:16-17), "Lord, thou knowest all things; thou knowest that I love thee" (21:17), and "Feed my sheep" (21:16-17).
  - "Three times, no / three times, yes" is plain counting from the text. It makes no claim about what Jesus meant by it, so it's honest.
  - Claim to check: "those exact words appear only twice in the whole Bible." I believe this is true of the KJV, but it is the one fact the film stands on. Run a concordance search before it locks.
  - Peter is shown fairly. Nothing is over-promised.
- **One change:** Add a short, gentle line that brings the opening "you" back. Example: "He did that for the man who said no three times." It's an invitation, not pressure.

**5. Overall: 8/10.**

## Film D — "The Second Fire" (ends on the fire)

**1. Newcomer**
- **Who:** Jesus and Peter.
- **What:** Same story: three denials by a fire, then breakfast by another fire, three questions, and a job.
- **Why it matters:** Jesus met Peter in a place like the one where he failed.

**Words a newcomer would not get:** The same list as Film C. Also, "He gave him work" is clear, but it makes the calling sound like a chore.

**2. Bible reader:** Yes, but only the fire link. That link is already given in the middle of the film, so the ending teaches nothing new.

**3. Curiosity gap:**
- Same opening question.
- The ending goes back to the fire, which the film already explained at "only twice." So the last line repeats rather than lands, and the viewer's "you" is again left open.
- **Score: 6/10.**

**4. The five questions**
- **Who it's for:** The same viewer.
- **Take-away:** Jesus met Peter at a second fire.
- **Global audience:** Same issues as Film C.
- **Biblically accurate:** The quotes are the same and correct, with the same "only twice" check. "Peter's worst night" is a fair reading, given that Luke 22:62 says he "wept bitterly." No over-promise.
- **One change:** End on "Jesus fed him first" with something stronger after it. "Then He gave him work" plus a repeat of the fire is two weak lines after the best idea.

**5. Overall: 7/10.**

## Which film wins

**Film C.** Its last line is a new, earned idea taken from the order of the text (fed, then "feed"), not a repeat of the fire link already given. The count of "three no, three yes" is short and easy to remember, even in a second language.

## Top 3 fixes (for both films)

1. **"Peter is hurt" → "This hurt Peter" or "Peter was sad."** A second-language listener may picture an injury.
2. **Check "only twice in the whole Bible" with a real KJV concordance search before locking.** If it doesn't hold exactly, say "only twice in the story of Jesus."
3. **Name who says "It is the Lord" and give the reason Peter jumps.** For example: "His friend cries, 'It is the Lord.' Peter jumps into the sea to reach Him." This makes the moment clear and shows Peter running *toward* Jesus, which is the whole hope of the film.
