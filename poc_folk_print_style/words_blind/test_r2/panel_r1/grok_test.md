Film C — "The Second Fire" (ends on the meal)

1. NEWCOMER
WHO: Peter, a friend of Jesus, who said he was not with him.
WHAT: Jesus dies and lives again. Peter fishes all night and catches nothing. At sunrise Jesus feeds him, asks three times if he loves him, and tells him to care for his people.
WHY: The friend who failed is fed and welcomed.

Unclear on one hearing:
- "keep replaying"
- "a fire of coals"
- "In the whole Bible, those exact words appear only twice."
- "It is the Lord." (who speaks, and what "Lord" means)
- Cloudy, then cleared by the next line: "Come and dine." "Lovest thou me?" "thou knowest / thee." "Feed my sheep."
- "Three times, yes" (yes to the love question, after an earlier no to "are you one of his followers?")

2. BIBLE READER
Yes. "A fire of coals" ties the denial night to this breakfast, and the meal comes before "Feed my sheep."

3. CURIOSITY GAP
Opens: is there a night you cannot stop replaying?
Pays off in the second fire, the three answers, and the meal. The film stays on Peter, so it makes no promise to the viewer. The feeling still lands.
Score: 8

4. THE 5 QUESTIONS
Who it is for: someone who failed badly and thinks they cannot go back.
Main take-away: three nos at the first fire, three words of love at the second, and Jesus fed Peter before he asked Peter to feed anyone.
Global audience: mostly yes. Short sentences. One insider line ("whole Bible" / "exact words").
Biblically accurate: the quoted lines match the KJV — "I am not." "It is the Lord." "a fire of coals." "Come and dine." "Lovest thou me?" "Lord, thou knowest all things; thou knowest that I love thee." "Feed my sheep."
Flags: the first ask is "lovest thou me more than these?" The beloved disciple says "It is the Lord," then Peter jumps. "Went back to fishing" adds "back." The meal is for the whole group. "Three times, yes" is the film's summary. Peter's words are "thou knowest that I love thee." "Hurt" matches "grieved." Peter is not shamed. No promise of a matching miracle for the viewer.
One change: replace the "whole Bible / exact words" line with "The night he said no, he stood by a fire. This fire has fish and bread."

5. SCORE
8

Film D — "The Second Fire" (ends on the fire)

1. NEWCOMER
WHO: Peter and Jesus. Same as C.
WHAT: Same story through the meal and "take care of my people." Then Jesus feeds him, gives him work, and meets him at a second fire.
WHY: A bad night by a fire is not the last meeting.

Same hard lines as C. Also:
- "worst night" is clear, and heavy.
- "gave him work" is clear, and sounds like a job.
The last fire picture is easy to see.

2. BIBLE READER
Yes, for the same two-fire link and the meal before the call. "Worst night" is not in the text. Luke says Peter wept. It does not rank the night.

3. CURIOSITY GAP
Same opening question. The last line answers it with the second fire. "Worst" makes the answer heavier than the question.
Score: 8

4. THE 5 QUESTIONS
Who it is for: the same person, the one stuck on a night they cannot undo.
Main take-away: Jesus fed Peter first, then gave him work, and met him at another fire.
Global audience: the ending is plain English. "Worst" and "work" need no Bible knowledge. They also press harder.
Biblically accurate: same quotes and the same small flags as C. Extra flags: "Peter's worst night" is an added feeling. "Work" turns "Feed my sheep" into a job. The text is care of lambs and sheep. No promise that Jesus will meet the viewer at their own fire.
One change: end with "Peter said no by a fire. Jesus fed him at another one."

5. SCORE
7

WHICH FILM WINS
Film C wins. Its close stays inside the passage: three nos, three words of love, and a meal before any task. That is the lift for someone who thinks they cannot go back.
Film D answers the opening picture more directly. "Worst night" and "work" add weight the passage does not ask for.

TOP 3 FIXES
1. In both, drop "In the whole Bible, those exact words appear only twice." Show the two fires in picture words. That line is where a new viewer is lost, and the script is already full for 75 seconds.
2. Keep Peter's answer as "You know I love you." In C, use that for the three-times line. In D, drop "worst night" and "work."
3. Say another disciple says "It is the Lord," and then Peter jumps. Say "Peter went fishing."

VERDICT
Winner: Film C
Film C: 8/10
Film D: 7/10
C lifts. D presses.
