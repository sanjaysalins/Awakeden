## TITLE
The Fire on the Shore

## WHO / TAKEAWAY / CURIOSITY GAP / WHY IT EARNS ITS PLACE
WHO: Someone who failed badly and quietly thinks they can never go back.
TAKEAWAY: Jesus meets failure with a meal and renewed trust — He does not send the failed one away.
CURIOSITY GAP: Why does the same kind of fire that heard Peter’s “I am not” appear again on the shore?
WHY IT EARNS: The “fire of coals” is the story’s own hinge; denial and restoration share one object, paid off at the end.

## NARRATION
NARRATOR: You failed someone. Three times. And you think you can never go back.

NARRATOR: Peter did. The night they arrested Jesus, he stood by a fire of coals. Three times they asked if he was with Him. Three times Peter said, “I am not.”

NARRATOR: After Jesus rose, Peter went fishing. All night — nothing. At morning a man called from shore.

JESUS: Children, have ye any meat?

NARRATOR: Have you any food? No. Cast the net on the right side. The net filled. “It is the Lord!” Peter swam to Him.

NARRATOR: On the shore: a fire of coals — fish and bread. Jesus said,

JESUS: Come and dine.

NARRATOR: Come and eat. He gave them bread and fish. Then three times He asked if Peter loved Him.

JESUS: Lovest thou me?

PETER: Yea, Lord; thou knowest that I love thee.

JESUS: Feed my sheep.

NARRATOR: Three denials by a fire of coals. Three questions by a fire of coals. Jesus did not send him away. He fed him — then trusted him with His sheep.

(Word count: 172)

## PICTURES
1. “You failed someone. Three times…” — Close on a lone figure at night, back to us, hands empty; breath moves in cold air.
2. “…fire of coals… ‘I am not.’” — Courtyard fire of coals; Peter’s face half in shadow as he shakes his head; sparks rise.
3. “Peter went fishing. All night — nothing.” — Small boat on dark water; empty net hauled up; water drips off the mesh.
4. “Children, have ye any meat?” — Sunrise shore; a figure stands small against big light; boat turns toward Him.
5. “Cast the net… The net filled.” — Net plunges right side; fish surge silver under the surface; boat tips with weight.
6. “‘It is the Lord!’ Peter swam…” — Peter in the water, coat tied, arms pulling hard toward shore; others drag the net behind.
7. “a fire of coals — fish and bread… Come and dine.” — Same orange coal-glow as the denial fire; bread and fish waiting; Jesus breaks bread and holds it out.
8. “Lovest thou me? … I love thee.” — Jesus and Peter face to face by the fire; Peter’s mouth forms the answer; steam rises from the meal.
9. “Feed my sheep.” — Wide shot: Jesus points toward distant flock/path; Peter stands fed, upright, ready to walk.
10. “He fed him — then trusted him…” — Fire of coals still burning low as dawn fully opens; two figures walk inland; smoke drifts up and away.

## WHY THE OPENING HOLDS SOMEONE WHO FEELS DISQUALIFIED
It names their private math in two seconds: failed someone, three times, never go back — their own night, not a Bible class.
Peter is not introduced as a hero; he is introduced as the one who said “I am not,” so the viewer is already inside the story.

## WHY THE ENDING IS EARNED
It pays off the fire of coals set in the denial scene and seen again on the shore — the passage’s own repeated object.
Three denials and three questions sit beside that same fire; the last lines do not invent a new moral — they name what the story already placed there: meal first, then trust.

## RISKS
A newcomer might hear “fire of coals” as magic symbolism; the film must keep it concrete — a real fire, twice — or the hinge feels like a trick.
A Bible reader might want the Greek love-words or a claim that John “explains” the three-for-three link; this version shows the pattern without saying the writer labels it.
