## TITLE (at most 6 words)
The Second Fire

## WHO / TAKEAWAY / CURIOSITY GAP / WHY IT EARNS ITS PLACE (one line each)
- **WHO:** Someone who failed a person or God, replays that one night, and quietly thinks "after what I did, I can't go back."
- **TAKEAWAY:** Jesus knew everything about Peter's worst night, and still fed him and trusted him with His people.
- **CURIOSITY GAP:** Why does John name the fire at Peter's worst night so exactly ("a fire of coals"), and where does that fire show up again?
- **WHY IT EARNS ITS PLACE:** The twist is in the text itself: the same four words appear only twice in the New Testament, once at the failure and once at the breakfast.

## NARRATION (speaker tag before each part: NARRATOR: or JESUS: or PETER: ; then the word count)

NARRATOR: Is there one night you keep replaying? Peter had one. He stood warming himself at a fire. People asked: are you with Jesus?

PETER: "I am not."

NARRATOR: Three times he denied it. John calls that fire "a fire of coals." Then Jesus rose from the dead. Peter went fishing. All night, nothing. At sunrise, a man on the shore calls out. The net fills. A friend says, "It is the Lord." Peter jumps into the sea. On the beach he sees "a fire of coals." Those words: only twice in the New Testament. This one has fish on it, and bread.

JESUS: "Come and dine."

NARRATOR: Come and eat. John records no word of blame. After the meal, Jesus asks him three times:

JESUS: "Lovest thou me?"

NARRATOR: Do you love me? The third time, it hurts. Peter answers:

PETER: "Lord, thou knowest all things; thou knowest that I love thee."

NARRATOR: And each time, Jesus trusts him with His people:

JESUS: "Feed my sheep."

NARRATOR: One fire where he said no. One where he was fed. Jesus knew all of it. And the fire was already burning.

## PICTURES (8-10 pictures in order; each: which words it covers + what we see + what MOVES in it)

1. **"Is there one night you keep replaying? Peter had one."** Deep teal night. Peter alone, seen from behind, small in the frame, a faint orange glow on his patterned robe. MOVES: the orange glow pulses slowly on his back; slow push in.
2. **"He stood warming himself at a fire... 'I am not.'"** A low bed of orange coals in a dark courtyard; Peter's open hands held over it; shapes of other people around, faces turned to him. MOVES: coals breathe bright and dim; Peter's hands pull back from the heat on "I am not."
3. **"Three times he denied it. John calls that fire 'a fire of coals.'"** Close on the coals only, burnt orange on teal. The words *a fire of coals* lettered in the print. MOVES: three small sparks rise, one after another, and go out.
4. **"Then Jesus rose from the dead. Peter went fishing. All night, nothing."** The Sea of Galilee at night, one small boat, an empty net hanging over the side, palms on the far shore. MOVES: the boat rocks; the empty net sways and drips.
5. **"At sunrise, a man on the shore calls out. The net fills."** A huge orange sunrise breaks over teal water. A small figure stands on the shore. The net over the right side goes heavy with fish. MOVES: the sun lifts; the net bulges and the boat tips toward it.
6. **"A friend says, 'It is the Lord.' Peter jumps into the sea."** Peter, coat tied on, mid-leap off the boat, arms wide, toward the shore. MOVES: the splash blooms outward in flat white print shapes; ripples run to the beach.
7. **"On the beach he sees 'a fire of coals.' Those words: only twice... fish on it, and bread."** The same coal bed as picture 3, same shape, same framing, but now in full morning light on sand, with fish and bread on it. The words *a fire of coals* appear again. MOVES: the dark courtyard version dissolves into the bright beach version; thin smoke rises.
8. **"'Come and dine.' Come and eat. John records no word of blame."** Jesus by the fire, hand open toward the food, bread in the other hand. Peter, wet, stands at the edge of the light. MOVES: Jesus holds the bread out; Peter takes one step into the warm light.
9. **"Jesus asks him three times: 'Lovest thou me?'... 'thou knowest that I love thee.'"** Jesus and Peter sitting face to face across the coals, sunrise behind them. MOVES: the coals glow three times, once per question; Peter's head lowers on the third, then lifts as he answers.
10. **"'Feed my sheep.' One fire where he said no. One where he was fed... the fire was already burning."** Wide shot: the shore, the small fire, the two figures, boats, palms, the whole sky gone orange and gold. A few sheep on the far green hill. MOVES: slow pull back and up; the sun keeps rising; the small fire stays lit in the centre of the frame to the last frame.

## WHY THE OPENING HOLDS SOMEONE WHO FEELS DISQUALIFIED (2 lines)
It asks about their own replayed night before any Bible name is said, so they feel seen, not preached at.
Then "Peter had one" puts them beside him, not under a finger: no shame, just "you are not the only one."

## WHY THE ENDING IS EARNED (2 lines, name the concrete thing from the passage it pays off)
It pays off the **"fire of coals"** (John 18:18 and John 21:9): named at the failure, seen again at the breakfast, and it is the last image.
"The fire was already burning" is straight from the text (they came to land and "saw a fire of coals there"), so the lift rests on Jesus, not on a slogan.

## RISKS (2 lines: what could be misread, by a newcomer or by a Bible reader)
Newcomer: "the fire was already burning" could sound like a promise that failure costs nothing; the film shows the third question hurt Peter, and promises only what the text shows (fed, and trusted with work).
Bible reader: "John records no word of blame" is an argument from silence, and the fire link and the three-and-three link are shown as what the story shows, not as something John states; also "I am not" is spoken in two of John's three denials, so the film says "three times he denied it," not "three times he said it."
