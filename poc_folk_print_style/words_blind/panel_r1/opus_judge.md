## TITLE (at most 6 words)
The Second Fire

## WHO / TAKEAWAY / CURIOSITY GAP / WHY IT EARNS ITS PLACE (one line each)
- **WHO:** Someone who failed badly and thinks, "after what I did, I can't go back."
- **TAKEAWAY:** Jesus came back to Peter after his worst failure. He fed him, asked him three times, and gave him work to do.
- **CURIOSITY GAP:** Peter's worst moment happened beside a fire. Why is there a fire waiting for him on the beach?
- **WHY IT EARNS ITS PLACE:** "A fire of coals" appears only twice in the New Testament. The story itself puts Peter back at the same kind of fire, and Jesus is standing beside it this time.

## NARRATION (speaker tag before each part: NARRATOR: or JESUS: or PETER: ; then the word count)

NARRATOR: Peter once stood warming himself by a fire. Three times that night, he denied being a follower of Jesus.

PETER: "I am not."

NARRATOR: Then Jesus was crucified, and rose again. Now Peter is back in a fishing boat. All night, they catch nothing. At sunrise, a man on the shore calls out:

JESUS: "Cast the net on the right side of the ship, and ye shall find."

NARRATOR: The net fills with fish. "It is the Lord." Peter jumps into the sea and swims. On the beach, waiting: a fire of coals, with fish and bread. In the New Testament, those exact words appear in only one other place. The fire where Peter said, "I am not."

JESUS: "Come and dine."

NARRATOR: Come and eat. After breakfast, Jesus asks Peter three times:

JESUS: "Lovest thou me?"

NARRATOR: Do you love me?

PETER: "Yea, Lord; thou knowest that I love thee."

NARRATOR: Each time, Jesus gives him work:

JESUS: "Feed my sheep."

NARRATOR: Take care of my people. Three times, no. Three times, yes. Peter's story did not end at the first fire. Jesus met him at the second.

## WHY THE OPENING HOLDS SOMEONE WHO FEELS DISQUALIFIED (2 lines)
The first line is a man's worst failure, said plainly: he denied his friend three times, by a fire. Anyone who replays one failure sees themselves in it before they know it is a Bible story.

## WHY THE ENDING IS EARNED (2 lines, name the concrete thing from the passage it pays off)
It pays off "a fire of coals": set up in line one (the courtyard fire), named in the middle as a phrase that appears only twice, and closed as "the first fire" and "the second." It also pays off "I am not": three noes, then three yeses.

## RISKS (2 lines: what could be misread, by a newcomer or by a Bible reader)
Newcomer: "Lovest thou me" and "Come and dine" sound old, so each one is glossed right after ("Do you love me?", "Come and eat"). "Three times, no. Three times, yes" shows the link the story points to but does not claim John states it.
Bible reader: John 18:17's first denial was at the door, not the fire, so the opening says "by a fire... that night" and gives only the fire-side "I am not" (18:25). The film never says who lit the fire, only that it was "waiting."
