## TITLE (at most 6 words)

Breakfast for the Man Who Failed

## WHO / TAKEAWAY / CURIOSITY GAP / WHY IT EARNS ITS PLACE (one line each)

WHO: Someone who believes their failure has cost them their place with God.  
TAKEAWAY: Jesus meets Peter’s failure with food and renewed trust.  
CURIOSITY GAP: What will Jesus give the man who denied him?  
WHY IT EARNS ITS PLACE: Breakfast makes Jesus’s welcome visible.

## NARRATION (speaker tag before each part: NARRATOR: or JESUS: or PETER: ; then the word count)

NARRATOR: You failed someone. Now you think there is no place for you.

When Jesus was arrested, Peter denied knowing him three times. He stood warming himself at a fire of coals.

After Jesus rose from the dead, Peter and the others went fishing. All night. Nothing.

Then Jesus stood on the shore. He told them where to cast their net. It filled with fish.

Peter jumped into the water.

On shore were bread, fish, and a fire of coals.

JESUS: “Come and dine.”

NARRATOR: He invited them to eat, and gave them bread and fish.

Only after breakfast did Jesus ask Peter whether he loved him. Three times. The third question hurt.

PETER: “Lord, thou knowest all things; thou knowest that I love thee.”

NARRATOR: Peter said Jesus knew he loved him.

JESUS: “Feed my sheep.”

NARRATOR: Jesus was trusting him to care for his people.

Peter had failed him. Jesus still had a place for him.

And before Jesus asked Peter to feed anyone, Jesus fed Peter.

Word count: 162

## WHY THE OPENING HOLDS SOMEONE WHO FEELS DISQUALIFIED (2 lines)

It names the fear beneath failure: someone you hurt may have no place for you anymore.  
Peter’s denial makes that fear concrete, leaving the viewer waiting for Jesus’s response.

## WHY THE ENDING IS EARNED (2 lines, name the concrete thing from the passage it pays off)

The concrete thing is breakfast: Jesus gives bread and fish before questioning Peter.  
“Feed my sheep” brings feeding back, and the ending reveals the order: Jesus gives before he entrusts.

## RISKS (2 lines: what could be misread, by a newcomer or by a Bible reader)

Newcomer: “dine,” Peter’s older wording, and “sheep” could obscure the meaning; the narration explains each in plain English.  
Bible reader: The repeated fires and three questions invite connections with Peter’s denials; John does not explicitly explain those connections.
