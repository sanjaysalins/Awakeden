# Test viewer review: John 21, Films A and B

## FILM A: "The Second Fire"

**1. Newcomer**
- **Who:** Peter, a friend of Jesus, and Jesus.
- **What:** Peter lets Jesus down three times by a fire. Later Jesus meets him by another fire, feeds him, and asks three times if Peter loves Him.
- **Why it matters:** Jesus does not throw Peter away after he fails.

Words or lines a newcomer would not understand on first hearing:
- "denied Him". This is the key word for the failure, and it is never put in plain words.
- No reason is given for why people asked "are you with Jesus?" or why saying no was so bad. The arrest is missing, so the danger is missing.
- "Then Jesus rose from the dead". Nobody said He died.
- "a fire of coals"
- "New Testament"
- "Those exact words appear only twice". This works for a Bible reader. A newcomer may hear only "some words repeat."
- "It is the Lord". Who is "the Lord"?
- "Come and dine" is glossed. Fine.
- "Lovest thou me" is glossed. Fine.
- "thou knowest all things; thou knowest that I love thee" is not glossed.
- "Three times, no, by one fire. Three times, yes, by another." This is clever, but it goes by fast, and Peter never literally said "no" or "yes".

**2. Bible reader:** Yes. The "fire of coals" link between the two scenes (one Greek word, used only in John 18 and John 21) is a real find for most readers.

**3. Curiosity gap:** The opening asks: "Is there one night you keep replaying? Can you come back from it?" The second fire pays that off cleanly, with one spine from start to end. **Score: 8/10.**

**4. The 5 questions**
- **Who is it for:** Someone stuck replaying a failure.
- **Main take-away:** Jesus met Peter at the scene of his failure and did not send him away.
- **Global audience OK?** Mostly. "Denied", the missing arrest and the unglossed "thou knowest" hurt.
- **Biblically accurate?**
  - Every quote matches the KJV word for word: "I am not", "Come and dine", "Lovest thou me?", "Lord, thou knowest all things; thou knowest that I love thee", "Feed my sheep". "Lovest thou me?" is cut down from verse 15's "more than these?" and that is acceptable.
  - "The third time, it hurts" fairly matches "Peter was grieved".
  - Flag: "only twice in the New Testament" is true, but it is a claim from outside the text pack. Soften it to "John uses these words only twice."
  - Flag: "with fish and bread on it". The KJV says fish laid on the fire, "and bread". Minor.
  - Flag: "A friend cries" adds a little. The KJV says "saith".
  - Flag: "Three times... by one fire" simplifies the scene. The first denial (John 18:17) is at the door. Minor.
  - Flag: "went back to fishing" is a fair inference, not a quote.
  - Peter is treated fairly. There is no over-promise.
- **Best version (one change):** Add "the night Jesus was arrested," and swap "denied Him" for plain words, such as "said he was not one of Jesus' followers."

**5. Overall score: 7.5/10**

## FILM B: "Breakfast First"

**1. Newcomer**
- **Who:** Peter and Jesus.
- **What:** Peter fails Jesus the night He is arrested. After Jesus rises, He cooks breakfast for Peter, then asks three times if Peter loves Him, and gives him work.
- **Why it matters:** Jesus feeds you before He asks anything of you. Failing does not end your place.

Words or lines a newcomer would not understand on first hearing:
- "Children, have ye any meat?" is glossed. Good.
- "It is the Lord!" Who is "the Lord"?
- "a fire of coals" and "the same words John used". Who is John? A newcomer may think he is one of the people on the beach.
- "thou knowest all things; thou knowest that I love thee" is not glossed.
- "Feed my sheep" is glossed. Good.
- "rose" is clear enough here, because the arrest was named.

**2. Bible reader:** Yes, but less. "Fed before asked" (verse 13 comes before verse 15) is a real and quiet point. Many readers have heard the fire link before, and here it is only a side note.

**3. Curiosity gap:** The opening asks: "Is there still a place for me after I failed?" The film pays it off with "still had work for him" and the fine closing line, "Jesus fed Peter." But the fire clue opens a second thread and then drops it, so there are two half-spines. **Score: 7/10.**

**4. The 5 questions**
- **Who is it for:** The same viewer, but the opening "You failed someone you love. Now you think…" tells the viewer what they feel. Some will think "that's not me" and scroll away. Film A's question is gentler.
- **Main take-away:** Grace comes first, and then the calling.
- **Global audience OK?** Yes, better than A. The arrest is named and "meat" is glossed.
- **Biblically accurate?**
  - The quotes are exact: "I am not", "Children, have ye any meat?", "Come and dine", "Lovest thou me?", Peter's answer, "Feed my sheep".
  - "Jesus hands them bread and fish" matches verse 13. "Only after the meal" matches "when they had dined". Good.
  - Flag: "said he did not know Him" is Luke's wording (Luke 22:57, not in this pack). It sits next to John's quote "I am not", which means "not one of his disciples." That is a small mismatch.
  - Flag: Peter's answer is the third-time answer ("all things"), but the film drops the third-time grief, so the answer comes with no weight behind it.
  - "It is the Lord!" adds an exclamation mark. Minor.
  - Peter is treated fairly. There is no over-promise.
- **Best version (one change):** Put back "The third time, Peter was hurt" before his answer. It costs about two seconds and gives the scene its heart.

**5. Overall score: 7/10**

---

## WHICH FILM WINS

**Film A.** It has one clear spine (two fires), a stronger and gentler hook, and a Bible-reader payoff that is truly earned.

Film B is clearer for newcomers, and its last line ("Jesus fed Peter") is the best single line across both films. It should be grafted into A only if it does not break A's spine.

## TOP 3 FIXES (most important first)

1. **Film A:** Name the arrest and drop "denied". For example: "The night Jesus was arrested, Peter stood by a fire. Three times he said he was not one of Jesus' followers."
2. **Both films:** Gloss "thou knowest all things" with "Lord, you know everything; you know I love you." Both films should also keep the third-time grief beat; B currently drops it.
3. **Accuracy polish:**
   - A: change "only twice in the New Testament" to "John uses these words only twice."
   - B: change "did not know Him" to match John's "I am not" ("said he was not one of His friends").
   - B: soften the opening into a question so the viewer is not told what they feel.
