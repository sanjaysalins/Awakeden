# Cold test — Film A "The Second Fire" vs Film B "Breakfast First" (John 21:1-17)

## FILM A — "The Second Fire"

### 1. Newcomer
- WHO: A man called Peter, and Jesus, his teacher, who came back from the dead.
- WHAT: Peter said three times that he was not with Jesus. Later Jesus makes him breakfast by a fire and asks three times, "Do you love me?"
- WHY: Jesus did not throw away the man who failed Him. He came to meet him.

Would NOT be understood on first hearing:
- "are you with Jesus?" — no reason given why this is a dangerous question. The arrest is never mentioned. A newcomer cannot tell why Peter lies.
- "denied Him" — "denied" is a hard word for a second-language listener. "Said no" is plainer.
- "John calls that fire..." — who is John? Never introduced. A newcomer may think John is a person in the scene.
- "Then Jesus rose from the dead." — the film never said He died. Big jump.
- "a man on the shore calls out. The net fills." — the cause is skipped (He tells them where to cast). Reads as two unlinked events.
- "A friend cries, 'It is the Lord.'" — "the Lord" is never tied to Jesus or to the man on the shore. A newcomer has to guess all three are one person.
- "Those exact words appear only twice in the New Testament." — "New Testament" is an insider term.
- "Lovest thou me?", "thou knowest", "Come and dine" — old English, but each is glossed at once. Works.
- "Feed my sheep" — glossed ("Take care of my people"). Works.
- "The third time, it hurts." — who does it hurt? Peter is not named in the line.

### 2. Bible reader
Yes. Most lifelong readers have never noticed that "a fire of coals" marks both the denial and the breakfast. That is a real find.

### 3. Curiosity gap
Opening asks: "Is there one night you keep replaying?" — the question it opens is "what can be done about my worst night?" A second, sharper gap opens mid-film: why does John name that fire?
Paid off: yes. "Three times, no, by one fire. Three times, yes, by another... He met him at the second fire." The pay-off answers the fire question fully and the personal question by picture, not by statement. The viewer is left to apply it — which fits "grace not pressure" but the lift is implied, not said.
Score: 8/10.

### 4. The 5 questions
- Who is this for: someone replaying a failure. The first line names them well, as a question, with no blame.
- Main take-away: Jesus goes back to the place of your failure and meets you there.
- Global audience OK: mostly. Fix "denied", "John", "New Testament", and the missing arrest/death.
- Biblically accurate:
  - "I am not." — exact (John 18:17, 18:25).
  - "a fire of coals" — exact (John 18:18, 21:9).
  - "It is the Lord." — exact (21:7). "A friend cries" is fair for "that disciple whom Jesus loved saith"; "cries" is a little stronger than "saith".
  - "Come and dine." — exact (21:12).
  - "Lovest thou me?" — exact for asks two and three (21:16, 21:17). The first ask is "lovest thou me more than these?" Acceptable as a shared phrase.
  - "Lord, thou knowest all things; thou knowest that I love thee." — exact (21:17).
  - "Feed my sheep." — exact (21:16, 21:17). First reply is "Feed my lambs"; the film does not claim otherwise.
  - "The third time, it hurts." — fair: "Peter was grieved" (21:17).
  - FLAG 1: "Those exact words appear only twice in the New Testament." This is not in the evidence pack. It is true to my knowledge (the Greek word is used only in John 18:18 and John 21:9, and the KJV phrase "fire of coals" likewise), but it is an outside claim and must be checked by a concordance before lock. "John uses these words only twice" is the safer wording.
  - FLAG 2: "Three times, no, by one fire." In John, the first "I am not" is at the door to the damsel (18:17); the fire is named after it (18:18), and the second and third denials are at the fire (18:25-27). "By one fire" for all three is a small stretch on John's own text.
  - FLAG 3: "Three times, yes, by another." The text says "when they had dined"; it does not say the talk took place at the fire. Reasonable, but it is an inference.
  - "Peter went back to fishing" — fine as fact (21:3). It must not be played as "he gave up"; the text gives no motive.
  - Picture of Peter: fair. No over-promise. No guilt. Ending is earned from the passage.
- Best version possible — the ONE change: add the stakes in one short clause — "The night Jesus was arrested..." — so the newcomer knows why Peter said no and how Jesus came to die.

### 5. Score: 8/10

## FILM B — "Breakfast First"

### 1. Newcomer
- WHO: Peter, who failed Jesus, and Jesus, risen.
- WHAT: Peter said three times he did not know Jesus. Later Jesus cooks breakfast, feeds him, and then gives him a job.
- WHY: Failing did not end Peter's place. Jesus fed him first, then trusted him with work.

Would NOT be understood on first hearing:
- PETER: "I am not." — this is the big one. No question comes before it. The narrator has just said Peter "said he did not know Him", then the voice says "I am not." Not what? It lands as nonsense.
- "After Jesus rose" — rose from what? Arrest is given, death is not.
- "Children, have ye any meat?" — "Children" said to grown fishermen is strange; "meat" for food is misleading. The gloss "Any food? No." saves "meat" but "No" has no speaker.
- "'It is the Lord!'" — no speaker named, and "the Lord" is not tied to Jesus.
- "the same words John used" — who is John?
- "a fire of coals" — said once here, so the "same words" point has nothing for the ear to compare. The listener must take it on trust.
- "Lovest thou me?", "thou knowest", "Come and dine" — glossed. Fine.
- "Feed my sheep" / "Care for my people" — fine.

### 2. Bible reader
Yes. "Before Jesus asked Peter to feed anyone, Jesus fed Peter" is a fresh, text-true turn on "Feed my sheep" (21:13 before 21:15). The fire echo is there too, but thrown away.

### 3. Curiosity gap
Opening states: "You failed someone you love. Now you think there is no place for you." The question it opens is "is there still a place for me?"
Paid off: yes, and directly — "Jesus still had work for him." Then the last line gives a second, warmer pay-off.
But the opening is a statement about the viewer, not a question. It tells them what they did and what they think. Viewers it does not fit will swipe; viewers it fits may feel accused. Less pull than A's opener, and there is no mid-film puzzle to hold attention.
Score: 6.5/10.

### 4. The 5 questions
- Who is this for: the stated viewer, named very directly. Perhaps too directly.
- Main take-away: Jesus feeds you before He asks anything of you — and He still has work for you.
- Global audience OK: yes in vocabulary; plainer than A. But the dangling "I am not" and the unnamed speakers hurt a first-time listener.
- Biblically accurate:
  - "I am not." — exact (John 18:17, 18:25).
  - "Children, have ye any meat?" — exact (21:5).
  - "It is the Lord" — exact words (21:7); the "!" is added.
  - "a fire of coals" — exact (21:9, 18:18).
  - "Come and dine." — exact (21:12).
  - "Jesus hands them bread and fish" — true to 21:13.
  - "Only after the meal does He ask" — true: "So when they had dined" (21:15).
  - "Lovest thou me?" — exact for asks two and three; first ask has "more than these".
  - Peter's line — exact (21:17). "Feed my sheep." — exact (21:16, 21:17).
  - FLAG 1: "three times said he did not know Him." John's words are "I am not" (not a disciple). "I know him not" is Luke's wording, not in the pack. The narrator's summary and the quoted voice do not match each other.
  - FLAG 2: "Peter knew that kind of night", right after "you think there is no place for you", puts a thought in Peter's head. The text gives his tears (Luke 22:62), not that belief. Mild invented feeling.
  - FLAG 3: "Jesus fed Peter" — 21:13 says He "giveth them". Peter is among them, so it holds, but it is "them", not Peter alone.
  - "went back to fishing" — same note as A; no motive in the text.
  - Picture of Peter: fair. "Jesus still had work for him" is earned by "Feed my sheep" and is not an over-promise. No pressure in the ending. The opening carries a little guilt.
- Best version possible — the ONE change: put the question before Peter's line ("They asked him: are you one of His? — I am not.") so the first quote makes sense.

### 5. Score: 7/10

## WHICH FILM WINS

Film A, narrowly. It has the stronger hook, a real mid-film puzzle, and a pay-off a viewer will repeat to a friend ("the second fire").
Film B has the better last line and plainer words, but its first quote makes no sense on one hearing and its opener tells the viewer who they are.

## TOP 3 FIXES (most important first)

1. Give the newcomer the missing story in a few words, in both films: Jesus was arrested (why Peter lied), He died (so "rose" means something), and the man on the shore is Jesus / "the Lord". In B, put the question before "I am not." Without this, who/what/why is shaky for someone with no Bible.
2. Make the two fire claims safe. Check "only twice in the New Testament" against a concordance and say "John uses these words only twice" (drop "New Testament", and say "John, who wrote this down"). Soften "Three times, no, by one fire" — in John the first denial is at the door, before the fire is named.
3. Bring B's best idea into A if the runtime allows: one short line that Jesus fed Peter before He asked him anything. It gives the hurting viewer the direct lift that A only implies. Also change B's opener to a question, and swap "denied" for "said no" in A.
