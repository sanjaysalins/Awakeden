Film A — "The Second Fire"

1. NEWCOMER
WHO: Peter, a man who said he was not with Jesus.
WHAT: After Jesus is alive again, Peter fishes, then meets him at a second fire and is told to care for his people.
WHY: Saying no three times did not make Jesus send him away.

Hard on one hearing:
- "keep replaying"
- "denied"
- "a fire of coals"
- "New Testament"
- "It is the Lord"
- "Come and dine" (the next line, "Come and eat," saves it)
- "Lovest thou me" (the next line saves it)
- "The third time, it hurts" (who is hurt?)
- "thou knowest all things; thou knowest that I love thee" (never said in plain words)
- "Feed my sheep" (the next line saves it)
- "Three times, yes" (Peter never says yes)

2. BIBLE READER
Yes. The same phrase, "a fire of coals," sits on the night he failed and on this breakfast, and that link is easy to miss.

3. CURIOSITY GAP
Opens: "Is there one night you keep replaying?" Pays off for Peter at the second fire. The film never comes back to "you." The line about the New Testament pauses the story. Score: 7.

4. THE 5 QUESTIONS
Who it is for: someone stuck on one bad night. It fits.
Main take-away: Jesus met Peter at a second fire and did not send him away.
Global audience: partly. The story is followable. "New Testament," "denied," and Peter's old English are the breaks.
Biblically accurate: the real quotes match the KJV ("I am not," "It is the Lord," "Come and dine," "Lovest thou me," the long Peter line, "Feed my sheep," "a fire of coals"). The "only twice" claim is true of that phrase. Problems:
- "Peter had one" night he keeps replaying. The text never says that. Luke says he wept bitterly.
- "Are you with Jesus?" The text says, "Art not thou also one of this man's disciples?"
- "Went back to fishing." The text says he went fishing. It gives no motive.
- "A man on the shore calls out. The net fills." It skips "Children, have ye any meat?" and "Cast the net on the right side."
- "A friend." The text says "that disciple whom Jesus loved."
- "Asks him three times: Lovest thou me?" The first question is "lovest thou me more than these?" Only the third answer has "thou knowest all things." The first commission is "Feed my lambs."
- "The third time, it hurts." Peter "was grieved." The film does not say who hurt, or why.
- "Three times, yes, by another." They are at that beach after the meal. The text does not say he answered with yes.
No unfair smear of Peter. No promise that the viewer's life will be fixed.
Best version, one change: cut "Those exact words appear only twice in the New Testament," and use that time to show Jesus handing them bread and fish before he asks anything.

5. SCORE
7.

Film B — "Breakfast First"

1. NEWCOMER
WHO: Peter, who said "I am not" on the night Jesus was arrested.
WHAT: Jesus has breakfast ready on the beach, eats with them, then asks Peter to care for his people.
WHY: Failure still left Peter work, and Jesus fed him before that ask.

Hard on one hearing:
- "rose" (rose from where?)
- "said he did not know Him" sitting next to "I am not"
- "Children, have ye any meat?" (sounds like children; "meat" is saved by "Any food?")
- "John"
- "a fire of coals"
- "Come and dine" (the next line saves it)
- "Lovest thou me" (the next line saves it)
- "thou knowest all things; thou knowest that I love thee" (never said in plain words)
- "Feed my sheep" (the next line saves it)

2. BIBLE READER
Yes. The meal is the point: Jesus gives bread and fish, and only after they have eaten does he say "Feed my sheep."

3. CURIOSITY GAP
Opens on the fear: "you think there is no place for you." Pays off: Jesus still had work for Peter, and fed him first. The last line is a real turn. The first sentence tells the theme before the story earns it. Score: 8.

4. THE 5 QUESTIONS
Who it is for: the person who failed someone and thinks there is no place left. It fits this viewer more tightly.
Main take-away: before Jesus asked Peter to feed anyone, Jesus fed Peter.
Global audience: clearer than A. Plain words carry the plot: arrested, food, bread, fish, work. Breaks are "rose," "Children," "John," and Peter's unglossed answer.
Biblically accurate: "Children, have ye any meat?", "Come and dine," "It is the Lord," the Peter line, and "Feed my sheep" match. "The same words John used" matches John 18:18 and John 21:9. "Only after the meal" matches "when they had dined." "Jesus hands them bread and fish" matches "taketh bread, and giveth them, and fish likewise." Problems:
- "Peter knew that kind of night." A feeling the text does not state.
- "Three times said he did not know Him." In John he says "I am not."
- "After Jesus rose." True, and unclear. The text says "risen from the dead."
- "Went back to fishing." Same added motive as A.
- Same flatten: three questions become one "Lovest thou me?", and the third answer stands in for all three. "Feed my lambs" is missing.
- "Jesus fed Peter." He gave food to the group. Peter was one of them.
- "Jesus still had work for him" could sound like a job. The last line puts the meal first, so the grace holds.
"You failed someone you love" points at the viewer. It is the doorway, and it is one step toward guilt. No false promise.
Best version, one change: say Peter's line again in plain words: "Lord, you know everything. You know that I love you."

5. SCORE
8.

WHICH FILM WINS
Film B. On one hearing, a newcomer can say who, what, and why, and the lift is in the passage: the meal comes before the task. Film A's two fires are a strong picture, and the "only twice" line is a true aside that a first-time viewer cannot use.

TOP 3 FIXES
1. Keep the three questions apart. The first is "Simon, son of Jonas, lovest thou me more than these?" Only the third answer is "Lord, thou knowest all things; thou knowest that I love thee." The first charge is "Feed my lambs."
2. Repeat every KJV line in plain English in the next breath. Do that above all for Peter's answer. In B, say "rose from the dead." In A, drop "New Testament."
3. Put both true endings in one film: the second "fire of coals," and Jesus giving bread and fish before he asks Peter to feed anyone. Cut feelings the text does not state.

VERDICT: FILM B WINS
