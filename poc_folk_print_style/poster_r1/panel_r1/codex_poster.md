## 1. TITLES

- ★ **The Second Fire** | What happened at the first fire? | A concrete image with a buried history: classic drama territory.
- ★ **Still Welcome** | Is Peter welcome after saying no? | Two plain words hold the stakes of an intimate film.
- **Three Times** | Why does Jesus keep asking? | Spare, rhythmic, memorable; repetition becomes the mystery.
- **Come and Eat** | Why offer him a meal? | An ordinary invitation carries unexpected emotional weight.
- **The Way Back** | How can Peter return after denying Jesus? | Suggests a journey whose hardest distance is personal.
- **The Empty Net** | What comes after a night of nothing? | A visible object becomes a compact symbol of defeat.
- **Bread at Sunrise** | What changes over this breakfast? | Specific, sensory, quietly cinematic.
- **After That Night** | What happens when Peter meets Jesus again? | Establishes an unseen wound and promises its aftermath.

## 2. POSTERS

### BREAD OVER EMBERS — *The Second Fire*

- **IMAGE:** Built from picture **17**. Make the round bread enormous: the central shape between Jesus’s giving hands and Peter’s receiving hands. Three glowing coals sit directly below. Peter’s tearful, smiling face occupies the upper right. Cream morning light, rust sleeves, deep teal shadows. One unmistakable action: receiving.
- **TITLE TREATMENT:** Upper left, stacked **THE / SECOND / FIRE**. “THE” small; the other words broad and heavy. Cream letters with softly chipped, carved edges. Title occupies roughly a quarter of the cover.
- **TAGLINE:** “After the night he said no.”
- **EXTRAS:** A narrow mustard border gives it the feel of a collected cinema print. For 16:9, place the hands and face right, title and tagline left.
- **WHY IT STOPS A THUMB:** The title promises fire; the giant offered bread makes the viewer wonder what kind of encounter this is.

### THE SPACE BETWEEN THEM — *Still Welcome*

- **IMAGE:** **NEW art.** Peter stands on the left, wet and heavy shouldered, looking down at bread Jesus offers from the right. Peter’s open palm reaches into the central gap. A small beach fire sits below their hands. Jesus’s expression is calm; Peter looks uncertain. Dark teal sea behind Peter, warm cream sky behind Jesus.
- **TITLE TREATMENT:** **STILL WELCOME**, two large lines across the upper quarter. Condensed, hand-carved capitals in deep teal; “WELCOME” slightly wider, like an open doorway.
- **TAGLINE:** “He said no three times.”
- **EXTRAS:** A thin coral frame. Keep both figures within the central square so the 16:9 version can place title left and the encounter right.
- **WHY IT STOPS A THUMB:** The offered bread and the admission in the tagline create an immediate human question: why welcome him?
- **IMAGE PROMPT:** “Vertical 9:16 movie-poster illustration in flat folk-art woodblock print style, grainy paper, burnt orange, mustard, coral, deep teal and cream. On a sunrise beach, Peter, broad fisherman about forty, short curly dark hair, short beard with grey flecks, wet rust-brown tunic, stands left with lowered shoulders, extending one open palm. Jesus stands right, long dark hair, beard, cream tunic, teal and orange patterned mantle, gently offering round bread. Their hands and bread form the central focal point above a small charcoal cooking fire. Peter looks uncertain; Jesus looks calm. Scalloped sea lines, restrained sun rays away from heads. Clear upper quarter for later typography. No halos, no lettering, no symbols resembling letters.”

### THE QUESTION — *Three Times*

- **IMAGE:** Built from picture **13**. Crop close around Jesus and Peter sitting face to face. Their eyes share one horizontal line. Peter leans slightly inward; Jesus speaks gently. Three cold coals make a simple, visible row beneath them. Warm orange firelight against a deep teal sea.
- **TITLE TREATMENT:** **THREE TIMES** across the top in two lines of heavy mustard slab capitals. Slight print misregistration adds character without weakening the letters.
- **TAGLINE:** “He knows the answer.”
- **EXTRAS:** None. For 16:9, keep the faces central and move the title into the left third.
- **WHY IT STOPS A THUMB:** Two familiar human expressions, one repeated question, and the unsettling possibility that the answer is already known.

## 3. RISKS

- **Fire can suggest punishment or an action thriller.** Keep it low and domestic; bread, open hands and gentle faces establish the film’s actual promise.
- **Poster detail can collapse into noise on a phone.** Give each cover one dominant action, one large title and one short tagline. Check the composition at actual thumbnail size.
