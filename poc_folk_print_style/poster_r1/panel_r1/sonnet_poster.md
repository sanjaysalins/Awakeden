## 1. TITLES

1. **THE SECOND FIRE** | Why a second fire? What was the first? | Short, mythic, two-beat like "The Second Coming" or "Catching Fire". Already the working title. *(star)*
2. **THREE COALS** | Why three? What happens to them? | Concrete object + number, like "Three Billboards". The film shows them go out, then light. *(star)*
3. **COME AND DINE** | Who invites, and to what? | Warm, odd, like "Eat Pray Love". Jesus's first line at the beach.
4. **THE MORNING AFTER** | After what? | Sounds like a drama; true to the story (after the denial and the cross).
5. **NO, THEN YES** | No to what? Yes to what? | Tiny and punchy like "Yes Man". Three nos, three yeses.
6. **THE NIGHT PETER** | What did Peter do? | Looks like a thriller, "The Night Of".
7. **FIRE ON THE SHORE** | Whose fire? | Epic adventure feel, like "Fire in the Sky".
8. **PETER** | Who is he, what did he do? | One name, biopic style, like "Lincoln".

Fires on the vertical centre line. No people needed. Optionally add Peter's face from picture 14 as a small cutout in the lower corner.
- TITLE TREATMENT: Large, stacked, across the middle on the woven band. "THE" small, "SECOND FIRE" huge. Chunky woodcut-style capitals with slight rough edges, cream with a deep teal outline.

## 3. RISKS

1. **Too busy for a phone.** The film's pictures are full of tiny people, palm trees and flowers. At thumbnail size they turn to noise. Fix: crop hard to one focal point (two fires, three coals, two hands), keep the title above 15 percent of frame height, and test at 120 px wide.
2. **A movie-poster title implies a feature film or a false hook.** Titles like "The Night Peter" might promise a crime story, and a "coming soon" frame would lie, because the film is 88 seconds long. Fix: use taglines that state what is shown ("He said no. Then...") and "A SHORT FILM" instead of release dates; never show anything the film does not (no arrests, no cross).
