## 1. TITLES

| # | Title | Question it opens | Why it sounds like a film |
|---|---|---|---|
| ★1 | **The Second Fire** | What was the first fire, and what happened at the second? | Simple and a little ominous, like "The Second Coming" or "The Last Duel". The word "second" makes you ask about the first. |
| ★2 | **Two Fires** | Why two fires? What links them? | Two plain words anyone can read. It sounds like a prestige drama ("Two Popes", "Two Lovers"). Easy in a second language. |
| 3 | **Three Times No** | Who said no? To whom? Did they ever say yes? | Has the rhythm of a thriller title. The film answers it with "Three times, yes." |
| 4 | **Fire of Coals** | Why would coals matter? | Old and strange words, like a literary adaptation. It is the exact KJV phrase the film turns on. |
| 5 | **Come and Dine** | Who is inviting, and why is that surprising? | Warm and odd, like an indie festival film. |
| 6 | **Before the Rooster** | What happens before it crows? | Sounds like a quiet suspense film. The rooster is in the film (picture 4). |
| 7 | **The Shore at Dawn** | Who is waiting on the shore? | A classic, beautiful drama title. Safe, but weaker on curiosity. |
| 8 | **Do You Love Me?** | Who asks this, and why three times? | Raw and emotional, like a romance or family drama. The plain English of "Lovest thou me", so ESL viewers get it. |

## 2. POSTERS

### A. "THE TWO FIRES". Pairs with *The Second Fire*
- **Image:** Built from film picture 11. Top half is the teal night courtyard fire, with Peter small and turned away. Bottom half is the orange sunrise beach fire, with Jesus and Peter seated. Crop tight so each fire is big. Make the sparks from the bottom fire rising into the top fire brighter, so they are the only line linking the halves.
- **Title:** On the woven band in the middle, full width. Big, heavy condensed letters with slightly rough edges, like a woodblock print, in cream. "THE" is small above. "SECOND FIRE" is huge.
- **Tagline:** None in the art. If one is wanted: "One night. One sunrise."
- **Extras:** A thin cream border, like a printed one-sheet. No credit block, because it is noise at phone size.
- **Why it stops a thumb:** Cold teal against hot orange is the strongest colour clash in the film, and the split makes you ask "what changed?"

- **Image:** Built from film picture 17. Jesus's hands put round bread into Peter's open hands. The three lit coals glow underneath. Crop so the hands and bread sit in the upper-middle, with the coals glowing at the bottom.
- **Title:** At the top, three stacked lines in big woodcut capitals: "THREE" / "TIMES" / "NO", in cream. Strike through "NO" with one orange hand-cut line, like a print mark.
- **Tagline:** Under the coals, small: "Then came the second fire."
- **Extras:** A dark grain border and a mustard sun-ray fan coming from the bread, not from any head.
- **Why it stops a thumb:** A clear, kind gesture (one person feeding another) against a harsh title makes a tension you want to resolve.
- **Honesty check:** Striking through "NO" promises a turn to yes, and the film delivers it on line 11.

## 3. RISKS

1. **Mush at phone size.** Split images and folk-art doodles turn to noise at 2 cm wide.
   - Fix: test at 120 px tall.
   - Allow one focal point only, and kill the flowers, gulls and doodles in the poster crop.
   - The title must fill at least 25% of the width, in one or two words.
2. **Accidental halo or false promise.** Sunburst skies behind Jesus's head read as a halo. A tagline like "The fire that changed everything" over-promises.
   - Fix: always put the sunburst behind the bread, the fire or the horizon, never behind a head.
   - Only use taglines that repeat what the film literally shows: no, three times, a fire, bread.

My pick: Poster A with **The Second Fire**. It is the film's own title and its clearest picture.
