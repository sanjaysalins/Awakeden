1. TITLES

★ **The Second Fire** | Why is there a second fire? | A number plus one plain thing, the way a real drama is named (*The Third Man*).
★ **Cold Coals** | Why are the coals cold at sunrise? | A hard object-title. The picture can be the title before anyone reads it.
**Three Times, Yes** | Yes to what, three times? | Sounds like a film named for a choice the last scene earns.
**Two Fires** | What joins the night fire to the beach fire? | Two easy words. The poster can be the title.
**Eat First** | Eat before what? | A short command, like *Leave No Trace*. The film's real turn: Jesus feeds Peter before He asks Peter to feed anyone.
**I Am Not** | Not whose friend? | Peter's own sentence. Sounds like a confession film.
**After the Rooster** | What is left when that night ends? | A clock-title, like *After the Storm*. The rooster is in the film. The story goes on.
**Said No** | No to what, and does morning change it? | Two words, built to be stamped huge. The night half is the title. The beach half is the answer.

3. RISKS

**Mud at phone size.** Picture 11 already holds two scenes, many men, sparks, and a band. Add a long tagline, a border, and credits, and it becomes noise in a Shorts grid. Keep one hero shape, a title that fills a wide band, and a tagline of four words or fewer. Drop the credit block.

**The wrong film.** A dark worried face, a rooster, or a verse line reads as horror or a church slide. A halo or a cross promises a holy icon the pictures never show. Keep food, coals, or the split fires as the biggest shape, put warm sunrise in the frame, and leave every word off the artwork until real type is added later.
