## 1. WHO IS THIS FOR
A phone viewer, say "Amara", who knows only the name Jesus. She stops for line 1 ("Is there one night you can't stop thinking about?"), because it is a personal question. She finishes if she feels the story is about guilt and forgiveness. She shares with someone who feels they failed a friend. NOT served: people who need the reason Peter matters, and people who need the Bible words explained. Line 3 is a very fast summary, so a fully new viewer risks dropping off there.

## 2. MAIN TAKEAWAY
"A man who denied his friend three times was welcomed back, with food, and given a second chance." Carried by line 11 ("Jesus fed Peter") and pictures 12 and 17. Mostly clear. It is stronger in feeling than in words, because "denied" is never said. I only learn he "said no" to being a follower (line 1, 3).

## 3. GLOBAL AUDIENCE
Who: Peter, a friend of Jesus. What: he said he did not know Jesus, three times. Later Jesus meets him at a fire and asks three times if he loves him. Why: Jesus forgives him and gives him a job.
Lost or misled:
- Line 3 packs many events into one breath: "Jesus died. He rose from the dead." I am told, but not shown why it matters. I do not know why a dead man is standing on a shore.
- "A fire of coals" and "In the whole Bible, those exact words appear only twice." I do not know what a Bible word count proves. It feels like a clue, but I cannot use it. The three coals pictured help me more than the sentence.
- Line 3 "A friend cries, 'It is the Lord.'" Who is "the Lord"? I guess Jesus, but nobody told me.
- Who are "followers", "the net", "the shore"? I guess fishermen.
- Line 4/5 "Come and dine... Come and eat" and 6/7 "Lovest thou me? / Do you love me?" and 8/9 are repeated in plain English. This helps me a lot. But "dine", "lovest", "thou knowest" are old forms and may fail in translation. The repeat saves them.
- "Feed my sheep" (10) and "Take care of my people" (11): I do not know if sheep means people until line 11 says it. Good fix, but late.
- Line 11 "the second fire": I only remember the first fire if I connect it.

## 4. BIBLICALLY ACCURATE
I cannot judge accuracy. What I was TOLD but could not follow: why "a fire of coals" appearing twice matters; "the Lord" (who); why Peter "is sad" the third time; why Jesus, who died, is alive and nobody is shocked; why Peter had to "reach Him" by jumping; what "denied" meant for the friendship.

## 5. BEST VERSION POSSIBLE
The best explainer would tell me, in one short plain line before the second fire, why Peter's three "no"s hurt: he promised to stand by Jesus. Without that, the forgiveness has less weight. Also make the "only twice in the Bible" line shorter, or show it with the three coals on screen as it is said. ONE highest-value change: add one setup line in 1 or 3 saying Peter had promised to never leave Jesus. Score: 7/10. The pictures, the matching three coals, and the echoing second voice are strong. Line 3 is too crowded.

## CURIOSITY GAP
Opens at line 1 (the night he cannot stop thinking about) and again at line 3 ("only twice"). Pays off at line 11 ("Three times, no... Three times, yes... Jesus fed Peter"). Score 8/10. Worth the wait. The payoff works through feeling even if I miss details.

## THREE SMALLEST FIXES
1. Line 1: replace "People asked if he was one of His followers." with "People asked, 'Are you with Jesus?'"
2. Line 3: replace "A friend cries, 'It is the Lord.'" with "A friend cries, 'It is Jesus, our Lord!'"
3. Line 3: replace "In the whole Bible, those exact words appear only twice. Here, and the night Peter said no." with "These words are used only twice in the whole Bible. Here, and at the fire where Peter said no."
