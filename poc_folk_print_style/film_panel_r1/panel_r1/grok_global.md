## 1. WHO IS THIS FOR

Linh, 22, in Ho Chi Minh City, watching on her phone with Vietnamese auto-captions. She knows the name Jesus. She has never met Peter.

She stops when line 6, "Lovest thou me?", hits her translator as noise. She finishes if she can count the coals with almost no English: three lights die, three lights come back. She shares only if line 11 still means something in Vietnamese. It will not. That line is an English pun.

Left out: a captions-first viewer; a city viewer who has never kept sheep; anyone for whom "the Bible" is not one book with frozen sentences.

## 2. MAIN TAKEAWAY

"Jesus gave Peter a meal and a second chance, and only then asked him to care for people."

Line 11 is the line that should say this. It half does. Lines 5, 7, and 9 already turn the old quotes into plain speech, and that helps. Line 11 then uses "feed" once for bread and once for a life's work. Picture 17 (bread placed in Peter's hands) locks the meal meaning. The three no's and three yes's are clearer in the coals than in the ear: we hear one "I am not" (line 2) and one love sentence (line 8).

## 3. GLOBAL AUDIENCE

Who: Peter, a friend of Jesus. What: on the night Jesus was arrested, Peter said he was not a follower; later, at a morning fire, Jesus gives him bread and Peter says he loves him. Why: the morning fire answers the night fire, and Jesus cares for Peter before he asks Peter to care for others.

Lost, with the words that broke:

- Line 3, "All night, nothing." Nothing of what? Picture 6 (empty net) supplied "no fish." The sentence does not.
- Line 3, "He rose from the dead." "Rose" also means stood up, or a flower. Picture 5 (open tomb, first light) did the teaching.
- Line 3, "A friend cries, 'It is the Lord.'" "Cries" means shouts and means weeps. I heard tears. "The Lord" sounded like a new person. The film had been saying "Jesus."
- Line 3, "a fire of coals" and "those exact words appear only twice. Here, and the night Peter said no." Old word order. In a voice-over, "Here" points at nothing. I could not tell which words were the rare ones.
- Line 6, "Lovest thou me?" and line 8, "thou knowest ... I love thee." Auto-captions often turn "lovest thou" into "love is now" or "lowest now." Lines 7 and 9 repair this for a listener. A bad caption stays broken.
- Line 10, "Feed my sheep," while picture 15 shows real sheep. Line 11 then says "my people." I believed the animals. Online, "sheep" is also an insult for a person who cannot think.
- Line 4, "Come and dine," is old, and line 5 answers it at once: "Come and eat." That is the right method. The last line never gets the same help.
- Line 1 asks about my night and never comes back. Picture 14 (two men, hand on chest, "I love you") reads as romance in many places. The denial is what makes it loyalty, and that context is easy to miss in translation.

Plain wording that travels: "All night, they caught no fish." "Jesus died. Later He lived again." "A friend shouts, 'It is Jesus!'" "On the beach is a charcoal fire. This same fire appears two times: this morning, and the night Peter said no." Jesus says, "Do you love me?" Peter says, "You know everything. You know I love you." "Look after my people."

## 4. BIBLICALLY ACCURATE

- Line 1: arrest night, Peter by a fire, asked if he belonged to Jesus. RIGHT (John 18).
- Line 2: "I am not." RIGHT for John 18:17. John's third denial has no matching sentence. The film speaks one denial and narrates the count.
- Line 3: "Three times, he said no." RIGHT.
- Line 3: "Jesus died. He rose from the dead." RIGHT. The cross is offscreen. The claim still matches the story.
- Line 3: fishing again, a night with no catch, sunrise, Jesus on the shore, a full net. RIGHT (John 21:3–6).
- Line 3: "A friend cries, 'It is the Lord.'" The spoken quote matches John 21:7. RIGHT. "Cries" is the film's verb. The text is "said."
- Line 3: Peter jumps into the sea. RIGHT (John 21:7).
- Line 3: "a fire of coals" on the beach and on the denial night. RIGHT for the KJV phrase, and for the Greek word used in John 18:18 and John 21:9.
- Line 3: "In the whole Bible, those exact words appear only twice." OVERCLAIMED. True of that KJV string. The NIV already tells the night fire with different words. A translated track that says "exact words" becomes false in that language.
- Line 4: "Come and dine." RIGHT as KJV John 21:12. Line 5, "Come and eat," is a fair gloss.
- Line 5: he gives bread and fish. RIGHT (John 21:13).
- Line 6: three asks, "Lovest thou me?" RIGHT as KJV's surface line. The Greek verbs shift across the three asks. The film makes no claim about that.
- Line 7: the third time, Peter is sad. RIGHT (John 21:17, grieved). The text's reason is that Jesus asked again. The film leaves the cause unspoken.
- Line 8: Peter's sentence. RIGHT, KJV John 21:17.
- Line 10: "Feed my sheep." RIGHT as the last command. The first KJV command is "Feed my lambs." One sentence stands in for three.
- Line 11: "Three times, no... Three times, yes." RIGHT as a summary. Peter's word is love.
- Line 11: the meal comes before the command. RIGHT (John 21:13, then 21:15). "Feed anyone" is the usual reading of the sheep, and it matches this film's own gloss, "my people."

## 5. BEST VERSION POSSIBLE

Keep the coals, the two fires, and the plain sentence after a hard quote. Put today's English in Jesus's mouth and Peter's mouth, because captions quote the character, and a narrator's repeat can sound like a second event. Show people on the hill when the words mean people.

The one change that matters most: land line 11 in words a translator cannot collapse. "Jesus gave Peter food first. Only then did He ask Peter to take care of people."

Score: 6/10. The coal count and the glosses (lines 5, 7, 9) are ahead of most Bible shorts. Archaic speech treated as normal English, real sheep during "my people," and a pun as the last sentence hold it there.

## CURIOSITY GAP

Opens at line 1 (a night you cannot leave). That question never returns. The real puzzle opens at line 3 ("only twice") and pays off at line 11. For this viewer, "exact words" in "the whole Bible" is a locked door. The coals are the wait that works. Score: 5/10. Bread in the hands is worth staying for. The phrase-riddle is not.

## THREE SMALLEST FIXES

1. Line 3: "A friend shouts, 'It is Jesus!'"
2. Line 3: "This same charcoal fire appears two times: this beach, and the night Peter said no."
3. Line 11: "Jesus gave Peter food first. Only then did He ask Peter to take care of people."
