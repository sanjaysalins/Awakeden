## 1. WHO IS THIS FOR
**Named viewer:** Grace, 45. She leads a small group and knows John 21 but has never noticed that the same kind of fire appears twice.
- **Who stops:** churchgoers. Line 1 ("one night you can't stop thinking about") is too general to stop someone who doesn't already care about the story.
- **Who finishes:** people who feel guilt or failure. Peter's arc is easy to follow.
- **Who shares:** pastors and small-group leaders, because of the two-fires detail and the last line.
- **Who is NOT served:** viewers who know nothing about the Bible. Their only reason to keep watching is the general hook, and the specific mystery doesn't arrive until about 37s.

## 2. MAIN TAKEAWAY
"Peter said no three times, Jesus let him say yes three times, and Jesus fed him before giving him a job." Line 11 carries this and makes it clear. "Jesus fed Peter" is an earned landing.

## 3. GLOBAL AUDIENCE
**Who / what / why in my own words:** Peter denied Jesus at a fire. After Jesus rose, He met Peter at a second fire, fed him, and gave him back his place. That comes through.

**Where it fails:**
- Line 10's gloss, "Take care of my people," drops the word **"feed"**. The landing in line 11, "before Jesus asked Peter to *feed* anyone," leans on that word. A viewer listening in their second language heard "take care," not "feed," so the closing wordplay half-breaks.
- "A fire of coals" (line 3): "coals" may be unclear to some viewers. The coal doodles rescue it.
- The other glosses are good: "Come and eat," "Do you love me?", "You know everything." They fix the archaic KJV words.

## 4. BIBLICALLY ACCURATE
- L1 "His friend Peter stood by a fire": **RIGHT**. John 18:18, "Peter stood with them, and warmed himself."
- L1 "People asked if he was one of His followers": **RIGHT**. John 18:17 and 18:25. In 18:26 the question is about the garden, but it's still asking the same thing.
- L2 "I am not": **RIGHT**, word-for-word. John 18:17 and 18:25.
- L3 "Three times, he said no": **RIGHT**. John 18:17, 25, 27 ("denied again").
- L3 "Peter went back to fishing": **slightly OVERCLAIMED**. The verse says "I go a fishing" (21:3). "Back" adds the motive of giving up, which preachers often add but the text doesn't state.
- L3 "All night, nothing": **RIGHT**. John 21:3, "that night they caught nothing."
- L3 "At sunrise, Jesus stands on the shore": **RIGHT**. John 21:4.
- L3 "tells them where to throw the net. It fills with fish": **RIGHT**. John 21:6.
- L3 "A friend cries, 'It is the Lord'": the quote is word-for-word and **RIGHT**. "Cries" is **OVERCLAIMED**: the verse says "saith" (21:7).
- L3 "Peter jumps into the sea": **RIGHT**. John 21:7, "did cast himself into the sea."
- L3 "'a fire of coals'": **RIGHT**, word-for-word. John 18:18 and 21:9.
- L3 "In the whole Bible, those exact words appear only twice": **UNSUPPORTED by the pack.** I believe it holds in the KJV, and the Greek word *anthrakia* really does appear only in these two places. But "coals of fire" turns up many times elsewhere (Psalms, Proverbs, Romans 12:20). The first skeptic who searches "coals" will call this out. It is the film's biggest claim, so it must be airtight.
- L4 "Come and dine": **RIGHT**. John 21:12.
- L5 "hands them bread and fish": **RIGHT**. John 21:13.
- L5 "After the meal… three times": **RIGHT**. John 21:15–17, "when they had dined."
- L6 "Lovest thou me?": **RIGHT**. Word-for-word in 21:16–17. 21:15 adds "more than these."
- L7 "The third time, Peter is sad": **RIGHT**. John 21:17, "Peter was grieved."
- L8 Peter's answer: **RIGHT**, word-for-word. John 21:17.
- L10 "Feed my sheep": **RIGHT**. John 21:16–17. Leaving out "Feed my lambs" (21:15) is fine.
- L11 "Three times, yes, at the second fire": **mostly RIGHT**. The yeses are in 21:15–17. That the talk happened by the fire is a fair inference; the text doesn't say it.
- L11 "before Jesus asked Peter to feed anyone, Jesus fed Peter": **RIGHT**. John 21:12–13 come before 21:15–17.

There is no agape/phileo overclaim, which is good. The doctrine is sound and grace-anchored.

**Would I send it to my church group?** Yes, once the "whole Bible" line is fixed.

## 5. BEST VERSION POSSIBLE
The best explainer would put the strange fact in the first two seconds. For example: "This phrase is in the Bible twice. Once when Peter betrayed Jesus. Once when Jesus forgave him."

At the moment the real curiosity gap only appears at about 37s, after 30 seconds of recap. Saying every quote twice (KJV, then the plain gloss) also eats about 10s.

**Highest-value change:** move the two-fires fact into line 1, and word it so it can be checked.

**Score: 7/10.**

## CURIOSITY GAP
- **Opens:** weakly at line 1, really at line 3 ("only twice").
- **Pays off:** line 11.
- **Score: 6/10.** The payoff is worth the wait, but it opens too late.

## THREE SMALLEST FIXES
1. **Line 3:** "In the whole Bible, those exact words appear only twice." → **"John uses those exact words only twice in his whole book."**
2. **Line 11 (gloss):** "Take care of my people." → **"Feed my people. Care for them."**
3. **Line 3:** "A friend cries, 'It is the Lord.'" → **"A friend says, 'It is the Lord.'"**
