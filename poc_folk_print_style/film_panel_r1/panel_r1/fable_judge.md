## 1. WHO IS THIS FOR (one named viewer; who stops, who finishes, who shares; who is NOT served)

Named viewer: Ana, 34, went to church as a child, carries one thing she is ashamed of. Line 1 ("one night you can't stop thinking about") is aimed straight at her.

- **Stops:** people with a regret; people who like the folk-print look.
- **Finishes:** Bible readers (the "only twice" promise in line 3 holds them) and anyone hooked by line 1 who gets past 16-37s.
- **Shares:** church people, on line 11 ("Jesus fed Peter"). That line is quotable.
- **NOT served:** the pure scroller. Seconds 16-37 (tomb, empty net, full net, jump) are plot with no stake stated. Also the total newcomer at the end: the film never says in plain words that Peter was forgiven.

## 2. MAIN TAKEAWAY (one sentence a viewer could say back; which line carries it; did the film make it clear?)

"Peter said no to Jesus three times at a fire, and Jesus gave him three chances to say yes at another fire."

Line 11 carries it ("Three times, no, that night. Three times, yes, at the second fire"). Clear for a Bible reader. Half clear for a newcomer: the pattern is shown, the meaning (Jesus took him back) is left to guess. Line 11 then adds a second idea (fed first), so two payoffs compete in the last 8 seconds.

## 3. GLOBAL AUDIENCE (who / what / why in my own words; where I got lost or misled, quote the line; words that fail in translation)

- **Who:** Peter, a friend of Jesus. **What:** he denied knowing Jesus three times; later Jesus cooked him breakfast and asked "do you love me" three times. **Why:** to undo the three no's. That "why" is my guess; no line says it.
- **Lost:** line 1 asks about MY night, then I am never spoken to again. The question is opened and dropped.
- **Lost:** line 7, "The third time, Peter is sad." Why sad? Not said.
- **Misled:** line 3, "A friend cries." Second-language readers hear "weeps".
- **Slips:** line 10-11. "Feed my sheep" is explained as "Take care of my people", then the punchline turns on "feed". The key word was just translated away.
- **Works well:** the plain echo lines 5, 7, 9 after each KJV line. Keep them.

## 4. BIBLICALLY ACCURATE (claim by claim: RIGHT / OVERCLAIMED / UNSUPPORTED / WRONG, quote the line; say only what you can support)

- Line 1-2, fire, question, "I am not": RIGHT (John 18:17-18, 25).
- Line 3, "Three times, he said no": RIGHT (John 18:17, 25, 27).
- Line 3, "Peter went back to fishing. All night, nothing": RIGHT (John 21:3). Skips the John 20 meetings; fair cut.
- Line 3, sunrise, net, "It is the Lord", Peter jumps: RIGHT (John 21:4-7).
- Line 3, "In the whole Bible, those exact words appear only twice": OVERCLAIMED. True of the Greek word in the New Testament (John 18:18; 21:9), and I believe of the KJV phrase. "Whole Bible" plus "exact words" is an English-translation claim I cannot fully prove here.
- Lines 4, 6, 8, 10: RIGHT, verbatim KJV (John 21:12, 16-17).
- Line 5, bread and fish: RIGHT (21:13). Line 5, "After the meal": RIGHT (21:15).
- Line 7, "Peter is sad": RIGHT ("grieved", 21:17).
- Line 11, three no / three yes: the text gives both counts; the link is a reading, widely held. Fair as stated.
- Line 11, "Jesus fed Peter" first: RIGHT by order (21:13 before 21:15).
- Picture 3: in John the girl asks at the door, before the fire. Minor.

## 5. BEST VERSION POSSIBLE (what would the best explainer in the world do differently; the ONE highest-value change; score 0-10 against that bar)

The best version would close the loop it opens. It asks about my night in line 1, so it must hand that night back at the end, in plain words, with no pressure. It would also cut 16-37s by about 5 seconds.

**ONE change:** add one plain closing sentence to line 11 that says the meaning and returns to line 1 (see fix 3).

Score: **7.5 / 10**.

Opens at line 1, re-opens at line 3 ("only twice"), pays off at line 11. Score **7 / 10**. Worth the wait for the fire pay-off. The personal question from line 1 is not paid.
