# Sound plan: "The Second Fire" (Sonnet seat)

## 1. SCORE IDEA
One small plucked folk tune that is cold and minor at the first fire, and the SAME notes turn warm and major at the second fire.
Instruments: a solo oud (the voice of the score), a low cello drone, a soft frame drum (daf) that only appears at sunrise, a few warm strings, and one high wordless female voice at the very end. Key: D.
Motif ("the three notes"): D - F - A in the night (rising, unanswered, a question). At sunrise it becomes D - F# - A (the same shape, now bright, and it finishes on a fourth note that lands home). Three notes = three denials = three yes's.

## 2. WHY IT BEATS a sparse solo felt piano
- It has a name-able tune. A listener can hum it. The motif is the story: same notes, minor then major.
- Real place and color: oud and daf say Galilee shore, not a generic piano bed. Folk-art picture + folk instrument.
- It has an arc with a body (drone, then pulse, then strings, then voice), not one flat texture. Still quiet: nothing epic, no big drums, no trailer swell.

## 4. SPLIT
Two parts. Split at 23.5 s, on the hard cut from teal night to sunrise. The mood change is the biggest in the film, so a fresh generation lands it on time. Both parts: same key (D), same oud, same cello. Part A is 26 s long (crossfade 23.0-24.0). Part B is about 68 s.

## 5. MUSIC PROMPTS
PART A:
"Instrumental only. Solo oud and a low sustained cello drone, key of D minor, very sparse and intimate, folk, cold night feeling. Around 2 seconds in, the oud plays a slow three-note rising figure D, F, A and stops without resolving. Around 9 seconds in, repeat it thinner with a held dissonant note. Around 15 seconds in, almost silence, only one soft low string note. Around 20 seconds in, the drone slowly sinks and one note hangs unresolved. Quiet, no drums, no choir, not epic, not cinematic trailer, no piano. Ends with a single held note, 26 seconds."
PART B:
"Instrumental only. Continues in D, now major and warm, sunrise. Solo oud, low cello drone, soft frame drum (daf) at a slow heartbeat pace, warm soft strings. Starts with a bright open fifth D and A. Around 1 second in, the oud plays a three-note rising figure D, F sharp, A, then D, hopeful. Around 11 seconds in, the pulse stays and the oud plays gentle arpeggios. Around 17 seconds in, the oud drops out for 7 seconds, only the drone and one high string. Around 24 seconds in, oud and soft strings together, simple and warm, no drums. Around 36 seconds in, a little shade of minor, thinner strings. Around 47 seconds in, strings open into major, the cello plays the three-note figure low. Around 57 seconds in, very slow oud, last note held back. Around 62 seconds in, the held note resolves, strings bloom softly, one high wordless female voice, then fades out gently by 68 seconds. Intimate folk, gentle, never epic, no big drums, no brass, no piano."

## 7. MIX NOTES
- Music about 12 dB under voice, ducked a further 4 dB while anyone speaks. SFX about 15 dB under voice. Fire is the loudest SFX.
- Lifts: 24 s (sunrise), 71-80 s. Drops out: 15-19 s, 40-47 s, and the oud goes quiet under "Jesus fed Peter."
- Hold (84.7-91): music blooms to its fullest (still soft), voice-hum at 86, fire crackle under everything. No new sounds. The fire is last to go.
- Fire is the through-line: same crackle at 3, 37, and 81. Night version is low-passed and thin; morning version is full.

## 8. RISKS
1. Music ignores timing cues and the major turn misses 23.5 s. Check: find the first bright chord in Part B by ear and with a spectrogram; shift the split point or trim to fit.
2. Parts clash in key or sound. Check: play the crossfade alone; compare both parts' tuning and instrument by ear. Regenerate Part B with Part A's last 5 s described in the prompt if clash.
3. Music or SFX masks the voice, or the SFX feel fake or too busy. Check: measure music and SFX level against voice in each spoken line (loudness meter); listen on phone speaker. Cut any NEW SFX that sounds cartoon (the splash, the net) and fall back to library sounds.
