## 1. SCORE IDEA
A quiet folk ballad for a fisherman: one plucked oud and a wooden ney flute carry a three-note "question" motif. At the end the motif finally gets its fourth, answering note.

- **Instruments:** solo oud (plucked, close). Ney or wooden flute. A low cello drone. Soft hand frame drum, felt not heard. Warm low strings come in only after sunrise.
- **Motif:** three rising notes that hang unresolved, like "do you... love... me?" At night they are low and minor. At sunrise the same three notes go to major. In the questions they are played three times, each a little fuller. At the end, a fourth note resolves home to D and holds.

## 2. WHY IT BEATS a sparse solo felt piano
- **It has a tune you can remember.** The 3-note question becomes the film's own sound. It pays off when the fourth note lands on "Jesus fed Peter."
- **It belongs to the place.** Oud and ney feel like a Galilee shore and match the folk-print look. Piano feels like a modern room.
- **It has a real arc.** Night is minor and alone. Dawn is major with strings. Most important, the music steps out of the way for the fire.

## 4. SPLIT
**Two parts. The split is at 23.5 s**, with a 0.8 s crossfade, so the major-key ney lands right on "At sunrise."
- **Part A:** 0–24 s, D minor.
- **Part B:** 23–88 s, D major.

Same tonic, same oud and ney, so the two parts join cleanly.

## 5. MUSIC PROMPTS
**Part A (25 s):**
"Instrumental, intimate Middle-Eastern folk. Solo plucked oud, close and warm, over a low soft cello drone on D. Key D minor, slow, about 60 bpm. The oud plays a simple three-note rising phrase that hangs unresolved, like a question, repeated gently. Around 13 seconds in the oud stops and only the quiet drone remains. Around 16 seconds in, a slow, tired oud pulse returns. Night, lonely, calm, never dramatic. No percussion hits, no choir, no epic build. Ends sustained on the drone, no fade to silence."

**Part B (65 s):**
"Instrumental, warm Galilean folk. Wooden ney flute and plucked oud in D major, about 66 bpm, with a soft low cello and gentle warm strings. A very light hand frame drum, felt more than heard. From the first second, the ney plays a three-note rising phrase in major, like a question, over a soft string swell: sunrise. Around 11 seconds in, a small gentle lift. Around 13 seconds in, drop almost to silence for 3 seconds, one held soft drone only. Around 17 seconds in, the oud returns quietly. Around 30 seconds in, the ney plays the same three-note phrase three times, each a little fuller: strings join the second time, cello the third. Around 48 seconds in, warm steady strings. Around 57 seconds in, thin out to just oud and ney. Around 62 seconds in, the ney answers the phrase with a fourth note that resolves home to D, and one oud chord rings out to the end. Tender, hopeful, folk, not cinematic, not epic, never loud."

## 7. MIX NOTES
- **Music vs voice:** music sits about 14 dB below the voice. Sidechain duck about 6 dB, release 400 ms. SFX sit about 4 dB under the music.
- **Where it lifts:** sunrise at 23.7 s, a small swell at 34 s, the third question at 63 s, and the hold.
- **Where it drops out:** music is near-silent at 13.2–15.7 s (the rooster) and at 36.7–40.2 s (the fire alone).
- **The 3 s hold (84.7–87.9 s):** the duck releases and music rises about 3 dB. The ney plays the fourth note and the oud chord rings. The fire crackles softly underneath. Both fade only over the last 0.5 s and never cut.

## 8. RISKS
1. **Music goes silent or fades out early.** Check: measure loudness in 1 s windows across the whole film. FAIL any window more than 25 dB below the mean outside 13–15.7 s and 36.7–40.2 s. FAIL if the music is below the drone level at 85 s.
2. **Key clash at the 23.5 s join.** Check: run a chroma/key check on both parts (both must read D). Then listen to the crossfade 3 times on headphones. If it clashes, regenerate Part B, not A.
3. **SFX sound fake (rooster, splash), or busy music fights the voice.** Check: generate 3 takes of each NEW sound and pick by ear, with no cartoon tone. Measure voice-to-music gap during speech (needs ≥12 dB). Do one phone-speaker listen-through for word clarity.
