## 1. SCORE IDEA
The same small melody lives beside both fires: first suspended in shame, then held in warmth.

**Instruments:** rounded, finger-plucked oud; low bowed cello; occasional breathy wooden flute, answering only between sentences.  
**Motif:** three rising notes—D, E, A—with an unhurried rhythm. At night they hang unanswered; at the beach, warm harmony supports them. After “Jesus fed Peter,” a final D brings them home.

## 2. WHY IT BEATS a sparse solo felt piano

- Oud gives the folk-print images a tactile musical identity; cello supplies warmth without orchestral weight.
- The returning melody makes the two fires feel connected before the narration explains them.
- Changing harmony, density and breathing space creates an arc without increasing loudness.

## 4. SPLIT
**Two parts, split at 23.7 seconds.** A 0.4-second crossfade, 23.5–23.9, anchors sunrise reliably. Both use D, identical instruments and the same motif. Part B’s prompt timings are local, starting at film time 23.5.

## 7. MIX NOTES
Start music roughly 18–22 dB below dialogue; duck another 3–5 dB during speech. Lift gently in gaps and after 84.7. Clear midrange space around every voice. No bread-tearing sound: the picture shows giving.

During the hold: resolved strings, oud decay, familiar embers and quiet water. Fade everything over 87.2–87.9.

## 8. RISKS

- **Generation misses timing:** audition against narration; manually place motif returns and the final resolution.
- **Plucks or fire mask consonants:** check on a phone speaker at low volume; soften transients and deepen ducking.
- **Emotion becomes sentimental:** audition without pictures; remove any flourish that tells us to cry before Peter speaks.
