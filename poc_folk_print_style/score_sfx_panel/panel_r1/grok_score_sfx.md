1. SCORE IDEA

A close Galilee shore trio — oud, one breathy ney, one solo cello — carries a three-note “coal” motif: three short descending notes for the three no’s, later the same three notes turned upward for the three yes’s. Key of D throughout: minor in the night, warming to major after sunrise.

2. WHY IT BEATS a sparse solo felt piano

- The motif means the story. You hear “three” before the narrator explains it, and you hear it flip at the second fire.
- Oud, ney, and cello belong to a fishing shore and a flat folk print: wood, breath, one bow. No piano, no choir, no trailer drums.
- Quiet is written on purpose. The score empties with the empty net, and thins again for the third question, so the rests are part of the arc.

4. SPLIT

Two parts, joined by a 0.8 s crossfade at 23.6 s. That is the hinge from teal night to gold sunrise, and Part A is already almost silent, so the join stays gentle. Same key center, same three instruments. Part B starts on the warmer motif so the new color is at the top of the file, not a loose cue deep inside one long piece.

5. MUSIC PROMPT(S)

Part A (~24 s):
Instrumental only, no vocals, no choir. Intimate Galilean folk trio: one oud, one breathy ney, one solo cello. Key of D minor, slow and close, like a folk-art print, never a film trailer. No drums, no percussion, no brass, no piano, no swell. A memorable motif: three short descending notes on the oud. Around 0 seconds, those three notes alone, then a soft cello drone. Around 4 seconds, the oud repeats the motif slower, sparse, sad, close to a coal fire. Around 12 seconds, thin to one low cello note. Around 16 seconds, near silence, then one quiet ney breath, small hope, no triumph. Around 20 seconds, almost empty, only a faint cello, still and hollow. Keep a soft tone at the end for a crossfade. Do not fade out early. About 24 seconds. Calm, human, under a spoken voice.

Part B (~65 s):
Instrumental only, no vocals, no choir. The same piece continued: one oud, one breathy ney, one solo cello, now warming from D minor into a gentle D major. Same slow feel, folk and close. No drums, no brass, no piano, no trailer hits, no big swell. About 65 seconds. Do not fade out. Do not go silent for long. Stay audible through the final second. Around 0 seconds, sunrise: the ney plays the three-note motif lifted and warmer, oud and soft cello under it, wide and calm. Around 6 seconds, a few gentle oud plucks like oars, a little fuller as a net fills, then ease back. Around 13 seconds, the same three descending oud notes return very quietly, a memory of the night fire, and the ney answers with those notes turned upward, tender. Around 25 seconds, warm and simple, oud like hands sharing bread, no flourish. Around 30 seconds, drop to almost only the oud, slow phrases with long gaps, kind and spare. Around 37 seconds, quieter still, solo cello, sad but gentle. Around 45 seconds, the ney returns like a shepherd, unhurried, open. Around 52 seconds, play the three descending notes once, then the three rising notes once, clear and quiet. Around 57 seconds, resolve: oud and cello hold a warm D major chord, ney plays the rising three notes one last time, like bread set into open hands. Around 61 seconds, no new melody, just that held chord, still clearly audible, gentle, no swell, no fade before the end.

Night fire and beach fire are the same library file. At night, low-pass it so it sits darker. On the beach, leave it warmer and a little closer. Do not generate a second fire.

7. MIX NOTES

Voice is always in front. Music sits under it, about 8–12 dB down, with a gentle sidechain duck whenever someone speaks. It may come a little closer at sunrise (23.6), on the rising motif (44–47 and 77–80), and on the last line (80.4–84.6). It never covers a word. It nearly drops out for “I am not.” (11.7), “Jesus died.” (15.8), “All night, nothing.” (21.3–22.9), and the sad third answer (60.4–67.4). In the hold (84.7–87.9) there is no voice: the warm D chord stays at the same gentle level, the coal fire continues, and nothing new enters. No final swell. The chord is still audible at 87.5, then ends clean.

8. RISKS

- Part B is long and may fade early or go blank. Check the full mix: the last 3 seconds should be within about 6 dB of the music level at 80–84 s, not near silence. If it dies, regenerate Part B. Do not loop a fade.
- The join at 23.6 may sound like two songs. Crossfade 0.8 s at the quietest point. Listen on headphones. Both prompts lock key of D and the same trio. If it clashes, regenerate. Do not transpose one part.
- The crow, the splash, or a late swell can feel fake or can step on the voice. Solo the SFX bus: never more than two layers at once. The rooster cue must be one call. Spot-check “I am not.”, “a fire of coals.”, and “Jesus fed Peter.” on a phone speaker. Every word stays clear.
