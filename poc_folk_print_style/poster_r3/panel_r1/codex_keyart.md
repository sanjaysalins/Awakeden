### THE LOAF  (title: THE SECOND FIRE)
- THE IDEA: The film’s title is cut into the bread Jesus gives Peter.
- WHAT YOU SEE: One enormous, irregular round loaf fills the upper two-thirds. Jesus’s cream sleeve and patterned cuff enter from the right; Peter’s broad, empty hands rise from below. Three small orange coals sit beneath the exchange. Deep teal surrounds everything. Thick ink, rough crust, generous empty corners. For 16:9, bread and hands sit right; the lettering remains inside the loaf.
- LETTERING: Drawn into the art by the image model: huge, cream, hand-cut capitals scored through the orange crust, stacked THE / SECOND / FIRE. Broad strokes; imperfect edges; very open counters.
- TAGLINE: None.
- WHY IT IS GREAT, not just good: The title becomes the thing Peter receives—every part of the poster carries the film’s final revelation.
- PHONE TEST: A loaf, receiving hands, three embers, three readable lines.
- IMAGE PROMPT: Vertical 9:16 cinema key art, flat folk-art woodblock print. Monumental burnt-orange round bread fills upper two-thirds against deep teal negative space. Jesus’s hand offers it from right, cream sleeve with teal and orange patterned cuff; Peter’s broad fisherman hands receive from below. Three small glowing coals beneath. Bread crust contains enormous cream scored lettering, three lines: “THE” / “SECOND” / “FIRE”, exactly this text, no other text. Bold legible handmade capitals, wide counters. Coarse paper grain, limited inks, no gradients, no halos.

### THE SPACE BETWEEN  (title: THE SECOND FIRE)
- THE IDEA: The empty space between Jesus and Peter forms one enormous flame.
- WHAT YOU SEE: Two solid teal silhouettes grow inward from the lower corners: Jesus at right, Peter at left. Their shoulders, knees and bowed profiles shape a tall orange flame between them. Across its narrow base, Jesus offers one cream loaf into Peter’s hands. The upper third is uninterrupted teal. For 16:9, widen the silhouettes’ shoulders while preserving the central flame.
- LETTERING: Added later: cream, broad, blunt woodcut capitals, tightly stacked above the flame. Flat ink; slightly irregular alignment.
- TAGLINE: None.
- WHY IT IS GREAT, not just good: The fire exists because two people have made room for an encounter.
- PHONE TEST: Two profiles, one flame, one small bridge of bread.
- IMAGE PROMPT: Vertical 9:16 film poster, pure silhouette and negative-space folk-art print. Deep teal field. Two completely solid teal seated human silhouettes merge into left and right edges: broad Peter with short curly hair and short beard at left; Jesus with long hair and beard at right. Carefully shape the burnt-orange negative space BETWEEN their profiles and bodies into one large upright flame. Their hands meet across its bottom, exchanging a cream loaf. No internal figure detail, no outlines. Upper third empty teal for typography. Rough ink grain, no halos. No lettering, no text.

### THE BURN-THROUGH  (title: THE SECOND FIRE)
- THE IDEA: A night print has burned open to reveal breakfast underneath.
- WHAT YOU SEE: A deep teal printed sheet fills the poster. Across its upper portion, a severely cropped woodcut of Peter’s worried eye and cheek. A huge, ragged opening interrupts that face; through it, a second print shows orange morning, Jesus’s outstretched bread and Peter’s receiving hands. The opening’s edge is charcoal-black with a thin coral rim. Bottom quarter: quiet teal. For 16:9, move the opening right and title left.
- LETTERING: Added later: substantial cream slab capitals, two lines, with the uneven impression of a hand-operated press.
- TAGLINE: None.
- WHY IT IS GREAT, not just good: The poster physically reveals the second encounter underneath the remembered first.
- PHONE TEST: Dark face, bright opening, unmistakable bread exchange.
- IMAGE PROMPT: Vertical 9:16 conceptual cinema poster made from two flat folk-art prints. Upper sheet deep teal, bearing a monumental cropped cream woodcut of worried Peter’s eye and cheek. One large irregular burned opening through its middle reveals a warm burnt-orange and mustard breakfast print underneath: close view of Jesus’s cream-sleeved hand offering round bread into Peter’s broad open hands. Flat charcoal torn edges, thin coral rim, tactile paper fibres, restrained grain. Graphic printed construction, strong empty teal bottom quarter. No photorealism, no halos. No lettering, no text.

### THREE EMBERS  (title: THE SECOND FIRE)
- THE IDEA: Peter’s face is printed across three separate coals.
- WHAT YOU SEE: Three enormous, flattened ember shapes float vertically on deep teal. Together, their cream woodcut markings assemble one continuous portrait: eyes above, nose and cheeks in the middle, beard and small smile below. Narrow teal gaps interrupt the face. The coals glow burnt orange; the surrounding field stays absolutely still. Title beneath. For 16:9, place the portrait left and title right.
- LETTERING: Added later: large cream capitals with rounded, carved shoulders; SECOND gets the widest line.
- TAGLINE: None.
- WHY IT IS GREAT, not just good: The film’s smallest recurring objects become its leading man.
- PHONE TEST: Three orange shapes resolve into one human face.
- IMAGE PROMPT: Vertical 9:16 minimalist folk-art cinema key art. Deep teal background. Three large irregular flattened burnt-orange coal shapes stacked closely with narrow teal gaps. Print one continuous cream woodcut portrait of Peter ACROSS the three separate shapes: upper coal carries forehead and tender wet eyes, middle carries nose and cheeks, lower carries short grey-flecked beard and slight relieved smile. Broad fisherman around forty, short curly dark hair. Clearly a graphic portrait printed on ember shapes. Flat inks, coarse grain, no flames, no halos. Bottom quarter empty. No lettering, no text.

### MY #1 and why
**THE LOAF.** The title, the central image and the film’s emotional answer become one object.  
At phone size it sells a mystery; at poster size it rewards you with the hands.
