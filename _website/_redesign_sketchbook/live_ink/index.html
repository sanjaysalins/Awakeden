Study no 4 — the bronze serpent.
Numbers 21:4–9  →  John 3:14.
This page is inked while you read it.
Follow the gold thread down. Go gently.

i. — I was there.

Moses, remembering · from the film Look and Live

I am Moses. For forty years I led Israel through the wilderness, and of all the strange works these two hands were given to do — a rod that became a serpent, a sea split dry, water struck from a rock — there is one that follows me still into the dark of my old age. A pole driven into the sand. And upon it, a serpent of bronze.

I was there the day the people were dying faster than I could dig the graves. And I was there when God told a dying man that he could be healed by nothing in his hands, nothing in his own strength — by nothing more than a look.

ii. — the world.

We were a freed people who had forgotten how to be grateful. The road was bitter and seemed to have no end. The manna fell as faithfully as the morning, and still we despised it; and the Scripture does not flatter us — it tells the plain truth:

the soul of the people was much discouraged because of the way.

Numbers 21 : 4 · KJV

Discouraged turned to anger, and anger turned its mouth against heaven. But this time the answer came not in thunder — it came in the dust between our tents, in a thing that slid and struck:

And the LORD sent fiery serpents among the people, and they bit the people; and much people of Israel died.

Numbers 21 : 6 · KJV

Fiery, we named them, for the bite burned like a coal beneath the skin. I watched strong men fall. And there was no remedy — not in all the wisdom of Egypt I had been schooled in, not in anything I knew to do.

iii. — the act.

Then the people came to me, and for once their words were not complaint but confession:

We have sinned, for we have spoken against the LORD, and against thee; pray unto the LORD, that he take away the serpents from us.

the people · Numbers 21 : 7 · KJV

So I prayed. I expected the LORD to take the serpents away. He did not. Instead He gave me a command so strange that I have turned it over in my mind every year since:

the LORD spoke · Numbers 21 : 8 · KJV

“Make thee a fiery serpent, and set it upon a pole: and it shall come to pass, that every one that is bitten, when he looketh upon it, shall live.”

He told me to forge the image of the very thing that was killing us, and lift it high where every eye could find it. And so, with these hands, I did exactly as I was told.

iv. — the strange detail.

A man would feel the fire spreading in his blood, lift his eyes to that bronze upon the pole — and live. No payment. No climbing. No cure of his own making. He looked, and the death let go of him.

Here is the thing that never let me rest. Why a serpent? The serpent was the curse itself — the very shape of what was striking them down. I would gladly have lifted up anything else. And the cure was a look — only a look: the strong and the weak, the child and the dying elder, all healed the same poor way, by lifting their eyes.

Why save a people by the sight of the very thing that slays them?

v. — the wrestling.

With these same hands I had ground a calf of gold to powder — and now I was charged to raise an image of bronze and bid the whole camp lift their eyes to it. It kept me awake: had God bidden the very sin I had just broken? Yet I knew the bronze had no power in it — I of all men knew that metal saves no one.

I felt as though I were holding up a signpost in the wilderness, pointing past itself, down a long road of years to something I could not yet see. I forged it. I could not have told you what it meant.

vi. — the reveal.

I did not see the answer in my own day. I tell you this from the far side of my life, by the light that came long after I laid down my staff. For there came a Teacher in Israel, and to a man who sought Him by night He spoke of me, and of my serpent, and said the very thing I had been waiting for without knowing it:

Jesus · John 3 : 14–15 · KJV

“And as Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, even so must the Son of man be lifted up: That whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have eternal life.”

There it was. My serpent was never the point of it. It was a shadow, and a shadow waits for the body that casts it. The body came. They lifted Jesus up upon a Roman pole of wood, where every eye could find Him — the very picture my bronze had held up in the wilderness. For so the apostle wrote of Him:

being made a curse for us: for it is written, Cursed is every one that hangeth on a tree:

Galatians 3 : 13 · KJV

And the riddle of the look was answered too. It was never the bronze; it was the looking that God honoured — the faith that takes Him at His word. The power was never in my handiwork. It was in the God who said look and live — and in the Son He always meant the look to find. For so the gospel was written:

For God so loved the world, that he gave his only begotten Son, that whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have everlasting life.

John 3 : 16 · KJV

vii. — the invitation.

So hear me — be still. I watched a camp dying. Strong men tried to walk the fire off, and fell; none was healed by his own hands. That is you. That is me. You cannot climb out of the poison — you were never asked to.

Jesus was lifted up where every dying eye could find Him — the least, the last, the child who can only turn his head. The cure is not in you, and never had to be. It is lifted up in plain sight, and costs you nothing but a look.

Look to Him, and live.
