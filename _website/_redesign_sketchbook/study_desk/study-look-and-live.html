Types & Shadows — the Old Testament, drawing Christ in advance

Look and Live

Moses, the serpent of brass, and the night Jesus said it had always pointed to Him.

Numbers 21:4–9 → John 3:14–15 About 12 minutes KJV, verbatim Red ink = His spoken words

Somewhere in the last stretch of the wilderness years, a camp full of dying people was told to do the one thing dying people find hardest to believe: look up, and live. No medicine, no march, no payment — a serpent of brass on a pole, and a promise attached to it. This study lays the whole passage open: the complaint, the serpents, the strange command, the cure that cost nothing but a look. Then it follows the pole across fourteen centuries, to a night conversation in Jerusalem where Jesus takes the scene and hangs His own cross inside it.

Read the text first. Read it slowly. Everything after it is margin.

the shape of the thing killing them — lifted where every eye could find it

How to use this page

The passage is set in full, King James text, verbatim. Grey notes are apparatus. Red ink is kept for the spoken words of Christ. Gold appears once, at the end — it always does.

The text

Numbers 21:4–9 · KJV, verbatim 4And they journeyed from mount Hor by the way of the Red sea, to compass the land of Edom: and the soul of the people was much discouraged because of the way. 5And the people spake against God, and against Moses, Wherefore have ye brought us up out of Egypt to die in the wilderness? for there is no bread, neither is there any water; and our soul loatheth this light bread. 6And the Lord sent fiery serpents among the people, and they bit the people; and much people of Israel died. 7Therefore the people came to Moses, and said, We have sinned, for we have spoken against the Lord, and against thee; pray unto the Lord, that he take away the serpents from us. And Moses prayed for the people. 8And the Lord said unto Moses, Make thee a fiery serpent, and set it upon a pole: and it shall come to pass, that every one that is bitten, when he looketh upon it, shall live. 9And Moses made a serpent of brass, and put it upon a pole, and it came to pass, that if a serpent had bitten any man, when he beheld the serpent of brass, he lived.

The route

Mount Hor, round the land of Edom — the long way. The text is plain about what the road did: the soul of the people was much discouraged because of the way.

Cross-reference

Deut 8:15 — the same wilderness, remembered: that great and terrible wilderness, wherein were fiery serpents, and scorpions, and drought.

The witness

From the film Look and Live — the witness account of Moses, spoken from the far side of his life. His words below are from the film’s narration; the Scripture he quotes is verbatim KJV.

I am Moses. For forty years I led Israel through the wilderness, and of all the strange works these two hands were given to do — a rod that became a serpent, a sea split dry, water struck from a rock — there is one that follows me still into the dark of my old age. A pole driven into the sand. And upon it, a serpent of bronze.

I was there the day the people were dying faster than I could dig the graves. And I was there when God told a dying man that he could be healed by nothing in his hands, nothing in his own strength — by nothing more than a look.

Fiery, we named the serpents, for the bite burned like a coal beneath the skin. I watched strong men fall. I watched mothers cradle children who had been laughing an hour before. And there was no remedy — not in all the wisdom of Egypt I had been schooled in, not in anything I knew to do.

Then the people came to me, and for once their words were not complaint but confession. So I did the one thing a mediator can do — I prayed for the people. I expected the Lord to take the serpents away. He did not. Instead He gave me a command so strange that I have turned it over in my mind every year since: forge the image of the very thing that was killing us, and lift it high where every eye could find it.

A man would feel the fire spreading in his blood, lift his eyes to that bronze upon the pole — and live. No payment. No climbing. No cure of his own making. He looked, and the death let go of him.

he dug the graves himself —

The strange detail

Here is the thing that never let me rest. Why a serpent?

The serpent was the curse itself. It was the very shape of what was striking them down. I would gladly have lifted up anything else — a rod, a lamb, the tables of the law in my arms. But God told me to lift up the likeness of the killer, high and plain, in the middle of the camp.

And the cure was a look — only a look: the strong and the weak, the child and the dying elder, all healed the same poor way, by lifting their eyes. I obeyed it, and the riddle of it walked home with me every evening. Why save a people by the sight of the very thing that slays them?

why a serpent? why not the lamb, or the law?

Hold the detail

The sign is not a medicine and not a relic. Verse 8 hangs everything on one verb: when he looketh upon it. The healing is placed entirely outside the dying man.

The wrestling

And I will be honest with you, for a man this near his grave has no reason to be otherwise. I feared for any who would not look. A cure so simple is its own stumbling-block: a proud heart can scorn to turn its head for a thing so plain — and the venom does not wait for a man to master his pride.

But the deeper trouble was mine. With these same hands I had ground a calf of gold to powder and made Israel drink it — and now I was charged to raise an image of bronze and bid the whole camp lift their eyes to it. It kept me awake: had God bidden the very sin I had just broken? Yet I knew the bronze had no power in it — I of all men knew that metal saves no one. It was God’s word that healed, and the look that took Him at His word. So what, then, was the bronze for? I felt as though I were holding up a signpost in the wilderness, pointing past itself, down a long road of years to something I could not yet see. I forged it. I could not have told you what it meant.

Cross-reference

The offence of simple

1 Cor 1:18 — For the preaching of the cross is to them that perish foolishness; but unto us which are saved it is the power of God.

The fulfillment

Fourteen centuries later, a teacher of Israel came to Jesus by night. Nicodemus was not given a parable. He was given Moses’ pole.

His words · John 3:14–15 · KJV And as Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, even so must the Son of man be lifted up: That whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have eternal life.

There it was. My serpent was never the point of it. It was a shadow, and a shadow waits for the body that casts it. The body came. They lifted Jesus up upon a Roman pole of wood, where every eye could find Him — the very picture my bronze had held up in the wilderness.

And mark why it had to be a serpent — the cursed thing, the killer’s own image. For when they hung Him there, the spotless Son of God was made a curse for us. So the apostle wrote:

Galatians 3:13 · KJV Christ hath redeemed us from the curse of the law, being made a curse for us: for it is written, Cursed is every one that hangeth on a tree:

He bore, upon that pole, the judgment our sin had earned, taken in our place — lifted up, so that we who are bitten and dying need only look to Him and live. He said it Himself, of the death He was going to die:

His words · John 12:32 · KJV And I, if I be lifted up from the earth, will draw all men unto me.

And the riddle of the look was answered too. It was never the bronze; it was the looking that God honoured — the faith that takes Him at His word. For long after me, when men forgot, they kept that serpent and burned incense to the metal of it, until a good king of Judah broke it to pieces and called it Nehushtan — a nothing, a scrap of brass. He was right to break it. The power was never in my handiwork. The power was in the God who said look and live — and in the Son He always meant the look to find.

The thread

  • Num 21:8 the lifted sign; life by a look
  • John 3:14 “even so must the Son of man be lifted up”
  • Gal 3:13 made a curse for us — why it was a serpent
  • John 12:32 lifted up, to draw all men

The honest objection

2 Kings 18:4 — Israel later burned incense to the bronze, and Hezekiah brake in pieces the brasen serpent that Moses had made… and he called it Nehushtan. If the power had been in the metal, breaking it would have been sacrilege. It was not. Signposts are for pointing past.

Where it lands

I watched a camp dying. Strong men tried to walk the fire off and fell; none was healed by his own hands. The venom was in the blood, and the blood could not put it out. That is you. That is me. You cannot climb out of the poison — you were never asked to.

Jesus was lifted up where every dying eye could find Him — the least, the last, the child who can only turn his head. Sit with that. The cure is not in you, and never had to be. It is lifted up in plain sight, and costs you nothing but a look.

So I ask what I once asked every man bitten in our wilderness — only now there is no fear left in it: will you lift your eyes and look? Not to the bronze, nor to me — but to Jesus, who was made your curse and carried it clean away. Look to Him, and live.

John 3:16 · KJV For God so loved the world, that he gave his only begotten Son, that whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have everlasting life.
