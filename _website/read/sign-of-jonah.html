The Sign of Jonah

Jesus was asked for one sign. He gave them a man swallowed by the deep.

Matthew 12:40 + Jonah 1-2 · KJV

Every panel below is a frame from the finished film, in order. Scripture is in red. The video version is coming to YouTube.

The narration

I told the sailors to throw me into the sea, and a great fish swallowed me whole, and buried me alive in the dark. I ran from God because His mercy was too wide. He sent me to Nineveh, the city I hated, I'd sooner drown than see her spared. The storm came for my sake; the lot fell on me. To spare the sailors, I had them throw me overboard. Three days buried alive in the fish. Then, long after I was dust, scribes demanded a sign, Jesus gave them only my name: For as Jonas was three days and three nights in the whale's belly; so shall the Son of man be three days and three nights in the heart of the earth. I went down guilty, the storm was mine. He went down innocent, bearing what was ours; He gave Himself. The grave no more held Him than the deep held me. He rose. You who are sure you've fallen too far, that is how far His mercy reaches. It cost Him the grave. Stop running, and turn: the One who went down into your dark and rose again is Jesus. Come up out of the deep, and live.

03

The meat

for the ones who want to dig

Why 'sign' is a loaded word

The scribes wanted a miracle performed on demand: proof without surrender. Jesus refuses their demand; that sign-seeking generation gets one sign only, the sign of Jonah: his death, burial and rising. He stakes the whole claim on it. If the tomb is not empty, you can walk away. That is not how frauds talk.

Matthew 12:39An evil and adulterous generation seeketh after a sign; and there shall no sign be given to it, but the sign of the prophet Jonas.

A psalm sung inside a grave

Jonah chapter 2 is a song, prayed from inside the fish in the language of the Psalms. Psalm 42:7 cries: all thy waves and thy billows are gone over me. Jonah prays that psalm-language back to God from inside what should have been his tomb, certain he will be heard. A thousand years of Scripture later, another Man went into the grave with a psalm on his lips.

Jonah 2:3All thy billows and thy waves passed over me.

Two sleepers, two storms

Jonah slept in the hold while the storm raged (Jonah 1:5). Jesus slept in the stern while the storm raged (Mark 4:38). Both were shaken awake by terrified men afraid to perish. The sea calmed when Jonah was thrown in. The sea calmed when Jesus simply spoke. Same scene, utterly different man.

Mark 4:39Peace, be still. And the wind ceased, and there was a great calm.

A greater than Jonas is here

Jonah preached one reluctant sermon and a whole violent city repented in sackcloth. Jesus' point cuts: pagan Nineveh repented at the lesser prophet, while the experts stood in front of the greater One and demanded paperwork.

Matthew 12:41They repented at the preaching of Jonas; and, behold, a greater than Jonas is here.
04

Today

why this is in your feed

"Show me a sign and I'll believe."

That is still the deal we offer God. Prove yourself first, fix this diagnosis, this marriage, this bank balance, and then I'll listen. Jesus answered that demand with one sign: a grave that could not keep him.

And Jonah is in the story for the ones who are running. He did not drift from God; he bought a ticket in the opposite direction, because he knew exactly how merciful God is and hated it. If you have gone down that far on purpose, the fish is the point: the deep you chose is not deeper than the mercy that follows you into it.

Stop running, and turn. The One who went down into your dark and rose again is Jesus. Come up out of the deep, and live.
