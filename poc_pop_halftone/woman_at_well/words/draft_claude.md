**(a) Script**

[narrator] She came to the well for water. She left without her jar. Why?

[narrator] A Samaritan woman comes to draw water at noon. Jesus, a Jew, tired, asks her for a drink.

[woman] "How is it that thou, being a Jew, askest drink of me?"

[narrator] Back then, Jews and Samaritans did not mix.

[jesus] "Whosoever drinketh of this water shall thirst again."

[narrator] He offers a different water: life from God, alive inside you, so you "shall never thirst."

[narrator] Then He tells her the truth about her life. All of it. He knew everything, and still offered the gift.

[woman] "I know that Messias cometh, which is called Christ."

[jesus] "I that speak unto thee am he."

(pause)

[narrator] She leaves her jar and runs to town.

[woman] "Come, see a man, which told me all things that ever I did: is not this the Christ?"

[narrator] The town comes. They call Him "the Saviour of the world."

[narrator] He died for our sins and rose the third day. She came for water. She found Him. The jar could stay.

**(b) Word count:** 168 spoken words (about 65–70 seconds).

**(d) Four lines**
- **Who it is for:** People who feel known for their worst, or keep trying things that leave them thirsty again. Also Bible readers who think they know this story.
- **Main take-away:** Jesus knew everything about her and still gave her the gift. Finding Him mattered more than the jar she came with.
- **Curiosity gap:** Why would a woman who walked to a well for water go home without her jar? The answer comes in the last line: she found Him, so the jar could stay.
- **Why a global newcomer gets it:** It's a simple picture: a well, a jar, being thirsty, a stranger. The words are short. The old words are framed by plain ones ("Messias… which is called Christ"; "life from God, alive inside you" instead of "living water"). And the ending pays off the question asked in the first line.
