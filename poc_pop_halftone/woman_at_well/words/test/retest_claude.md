# Judge: woman at the well narration

## Newcomer (English second language, no Bible knowledge)
- **Who:** Jesus, a tired Jewish man, and a Samaritan woman at a well.
- **What happened:** He asks her for water, even though their two peoples keep apart. He offers her a water that stops thirst for ever. He tells her about her five husbands. Then he says he is the promised Saviour. She leaves her jar and runs to tell the town, and the town believes.
- **Why it matters:** He knew all her past and still offered her life from God. That is why the jar did not matter any more.

## Lines a newcomer would need explained
- "Give me to drink." The word order is odd.
- "thou / askest / drinketh / whosoever." The old words are readable once, but they slow the listener down.
- "His water?" This comes too fast. Nobody has said that Jesus has his own water, so the newcomer asks: what water?
- "five husbands… not her husband." A newcomer may not see why this matters (shame, being exposed) or why it does not make her run away.
- "the Christ." Mostly fine, because the line before says "God's promised Saviour".
- "died for our sins and rose the third day." This is new information placed at the very end. It is too packed for one listen.

## Bible reader (accuracy)
- All the KJV quotes are word-for-word. Two of them are trimmed ("askest drink of me", "shall never thirst"), which is fine.
- "At noon": the sixth hour is usually read as noon. This is a minor point and acceptable.
- "runs": the text says "went her way". Small embellishment.
- "She says God's promised Saviour…": she says "Messias". The word "Saviour" is borrowed from v42. This is a light blend, not wrong.
- "The town comes and calls Him 'the Saviour of the world'": this squeezes two days, and the fact that they believed his own word, into one line. It is acceptable compression.
- "a spring that never runs dry" is a fair paraphrase of v14. "everlasting life" itself is softened into "life from God".
- The death and resurrection tag goes beyond John 4. It is true to the Bible (1 Cor 15:3-4), but it feels bolted on.

## Scores
- **Hook: 7/10.** "She left without her jar. Why?" is a real, concrete gap. The payoff ("found the One who knew her whole story") answers it, but does not link back to the water. The listener has to make the jump from "never thirst" to "she forgot the jar" alone.
- **Overall: 7/10.**

## Line fixes (old → new)
1. **Old:** "His water? You 'shall never thirst': life from God inside you, a spring that never runs dry."
   **New:** "Then He offers her a different water. Whoever drinks it 'shall never thirst': life from God, a spring inside that never runs dry."
2. **Old:** "The jar stayed. She had found the One who knew her whole story and still offered the gift: Jesus, who later died for our sins and rose the third day."
   **New:** "The jar stayed. She came for water and found the water that never runs dry: Jesus, who knew her whole story and still offered her life. He later died for our sins and rose the third day."

## Biggest risk
The "living water" idea is never set up. It is the bridge between "she came for water" and "she left her jar". Without it, the payoff reads as "she met someone important", not "she found the better water". On top of that, the last line is overloaded. It carries the payoff and the creed in one breath, so the newcomer loses both.
