# Blind re-test — Fable seat

## 1. NEWCOMER (one listen)
- **WHO:** A woman from a group called Samaritans, and Jesus, a Jew, at a well.
- **WHAT:** He asks her for a drink, though their peoples stay apart. He says His water means you never thirst again. He knows her private life (five husbands). He says He is the Christ. She leaves her jar, runs to town and tells everyone. The town comes and calls Him Saviour of the world.
- **WHY:** He knew everything about her and still offered her something good. He later died for sins and rose. *I get this, but only just: the last line goes by fast and I am not sure what "the gift" is, or exactly why the jar was left.*

**Lines I'd need explained**
- "the gift" (last line): never named before. I guess it is the water, but nobody says so.
- "You shall never thirst: life from God inside you": is the water real or a picture? Not said plainly.
- "I that speak unto thee am he": hard old English. Saved by the next line, "He is the Christ."
- "died for our sins": "sins" is new, arrives in the last 3 seconds, no setup.
- "Whosoever drinketh", "askest", "thou": old words, but short and the pictures carry them. OK.
- "Samaritan": not explained, but "Jews and Samaritans kept apart" is enough.

## 2. BIBLE READER
KJV quotes are word-for-word: v7, v9, v13, v14 ("shall never thirst"), v26, v29, v42 all check out. Small points only:
- **v9 is cut short.** KJV runs on: "...askest drink of me, which am a woman of Samaria?" The question mark is placed early. Words are exact, but it is a silent trim.
- **"At noon":** KJV says "about the sixth hour." Safer: "About noon."
- **"The town comes and calls Him":** KJV says "many" believed (v39, v41), not the whole town. Slight overstatement.
- **"The jar stayed. She had found...":** John does not say *why* she left the waterpot. The reading is common and fair, but it is an inference, stated as fact.
- **"still offered the gift":** the offer (v10-14) comes *before* He names her husbands (v18). Not wrong, since He knew all along, but "still" hints at a different order.
- "a spring that never runs dry" is a fair plain-word version of "a well of water springing up into everlasting life." But "everlasting" is dropped, so eternal life is only implied.
- Film note: her "crying and laughing" face is not in John 4. Fine as art, but it is invented emotion.
- Nothing doctrinally wrong. The landing on the death and resurrection is sound (1 Cor 15:3-4 wording).

## 3. HOOK — 7/10
- The opening is strong. "She came for water. She left without her jar. Why?" Short, concrete, a real gap.
- The pay-off is there but **implied, not said**. "The jar stayed" brings the jar back, but the film never says "she did not need it now" or links the jar to His water. A newcomer must join the dots alone.
- The pay-off also shares one 33-word sentence with the whole gospel. Both get blurred.

## 4. OVERALL — 7.5/10
Clear story, clean order, honest quotes, good simple words. It loses points on the crowded last line and the un-named "gift". Also about 186 words in about 65 s is fast for second-language ears.

## 5. Line fixes (max 2)
1. **Last line**
   - OLD: `The jar stayed. She had found the One who knew her whole story and still offered the gift: Jesus, who later died for our sins and rose the third day.`
   - NEW: `Why leave the jar? She had found better water. Jesus knew her whole story and still offered her life. Later He died for our sins and rose the third day.`
   - Why: answers the opening "Why?" out loud, in short sentences, and gives the gospel its own sentence.
2. **Water line**
   - OLD: `His water? You "shall never thirst": life from God inside you, a spring that never runs dry.`
   - NEW: `But His water is a gift. Drink it and you "shall never thirst": life from God inside you, for ever.`
   - Why: names the gift early (v10 "the gift of God") and puts back "everlasting" in plain words.

(Free extra, tiny: "At noon" -> "About noon".)

## 6. Biggest risk
**The ending.** The answer to "Why?" and the cross-and-resurrection are squeezed into one long, fast sentence. A newcomer may leave with the story but not the answer, and "died for our sins" may sound bolted on rather than earned.
