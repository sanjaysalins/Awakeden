Notes that apply to all three:
- John never says WHY she left the waterpot (v28). Every payoff is a fair reading, not a text fact. None of them claims it as Scripture, so it passes.
- "He knew everything and STILL offered" — in John the offer (v10-14) comes before the husbands (v16-18). It holds, because He goes on to reveal Himself (v26) after. Not an error.
- Nothing new for a Bible reader in any of them. The one sharp idea is "fully known, still offered the gift." A states it best. C states the facts best.

## 2. MY PICK: **B** (with the 3 fixes below)

Why B:
- Best hook. "She came to the well for water. She left without her jar. Why?" sets the gap in two plain facts. C's hook ("She left her water jar. Why?") has no setup, so the gap is thin.
- Best close. "She came for water. She found Him. The jar could stay." answers the hook in the hook's own words. Short. Easy for a second-language ear.
- It keeps the woman's surprised line (matches picture 2) and the "thirst again / never thirst" contrast, which C drops.
- Its faults are all one-line swaps. C's faults (flat hook, no woman's voice until the end, long narrator blocks) need a rebuild.

## 3. Line fixes for B

1. `[woman] I know that Messias cometh, which is called Christ.`
   -> `[narrator] She speaks of the Christ, God's promised Saviour.`
   (C's line. Removes "Messias", explains "Christ", and sets up "Saviour of the world" later.)

2. `Then He tells her the truth about her life. All of it. He knew everything, and still offered the gift.`
   -> `Then He tells her the truth about her life: five husbands, and the man she has now is not her husband. He knows it all, and still offers the gift.`
   (Gives the real fact from v18, fixes the tense slip, and makes "told me all things that ever I did" land.)

3. `[narrator] He died for our sins and rose the third day.` / `[narrator] She came for water. She found Him. The jar could stay.`
   -> `[narrator] She came for water. She found Him: Jesus, who died for our sins and rose the third day. The jar could stay.`
   (Joins the gospel line to the payoff so it is not a stray sentence, and keeps "The jar could stay" as the last words.)

## 4. The one biggest risk

**The gospel line is bolted on.** "He died for our sins and rose the third day" has no link to the well for a newcomer: nobody has said what "sins" are or why a death matters here. In A and B it arrives as a stray sentence just before the close and can break the payoff. A newcomer may be able to say who and what, but give a weak WHY ("she met a man who knew her secrets") and miss that the gift is for the viewer too. Fix 3 helps; it does not fully solve it. Test this line on real viewers before the words lock.
