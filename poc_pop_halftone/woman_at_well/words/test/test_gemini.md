2. YOUR PICK
**Candidate A.** It has the strongest thematic loop. The opening mystery of the water jar is perfectly paid off by the closing realization: she traded physical water for the "One who knew everything, and gave anyway."

3. Up to 3 concrete line fixes for your pick

*   **Fix 1 (Complete the KJV quote to avoid truncation):**
    *   *Old:* `[woman] How is it that thou, being a Jew, askest drink of me?`
    *   *New:* `[woman] How is it that thou, being a Jew, askest drink of me, which am a woman of Samaria?`
*   **Fix 2 (Smooth the abruptness):**
    *   *Old:* `[narrator] Then He tells her the truth about her life. Five husbands.`
    *   *New:* `[narrator] Then He tells her the truth about her life: she has had five husbands.`
*   **Fix 3 (Connect the floating Gospel landing):**
    *   *Old:* `[narrator] The town comes and calls Him "the Saviour of the world." / [narrator] He died for our sins and rose the third day.`
    *   *New:* `[narrator] The town comes and calls Him "the Saviour of the world"—the One who died for our sins and rose the third day.`

4. The one biggest risk
The transition into the Gospel landing ("He died for our sins and rose the third day") is jarring and feels tacked-on as a checklist item. It needs to flow naturally from the town calling Him the "Saviour of the world" so it doesn't break the narrative immersion right before the payoff.
