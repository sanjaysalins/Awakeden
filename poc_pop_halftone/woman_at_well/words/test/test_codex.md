All quoted KJV excerpts preserve the supplied wording. Across all three, running is an addition; the pictured tears/laughter and the precise reason for leaving the jar are interpretations. Jesus’ death and resurrection belong to the wider Christian account, beyond John 4:4–42.

2. **My pick: C.** It explains Jesus’ identity, gives concrete evidence of His knowledge, and connects the water to eternal life. Its shorter length also gives the unfamiliar language more room.

3. **Three line fixes for C**

- “He knows everything, and still offers the gift.” → “He knows her past, and offers her life with God.”
- “She speaks of the Christ, God's promised Saviour.” → “She speaks of the Christ—the Saviour God promised. Jesus says He is that Saviour.”
- “She came for water. She left without her jar. She had found Jesus, who died for our sins and rose the third day.” → “She came for water. Now she leaves her jar behind and tells her town about Jesus, who offers life with God.”

4. **The biggest risk:** a newcomer misses the central revelation because “I that speak unto thee am he” is difficult to understand on one hearing. Explain its meaning immediately beside the quotation.
