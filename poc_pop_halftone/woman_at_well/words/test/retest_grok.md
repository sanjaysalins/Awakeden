**NEWCOMER**

**Who:** A tired Jew named Jesus meets a Samaritan woman at a well.

**What:** She came for water. He asked her for a drink, knew she had five husbands, and said he is the Christ. She left her jar and ran to town. The town called him the Saviour of the world.

**Why:** She left the jar because she found someone who knew her life and still offered her a gift from God, a life inside her that does not run out. The film also says he later died for our sins and rose the third day.

**Needs explaining:** Samaritan. The old lines "Give me to drink," "askest drink of me," and "Whosoever drinketh… shall thirst again." "His water?" The Christ. "The gift." "Rose the third day."

**BIBLE READER**

- "At noon" is only one reading of "about the sixth hour."
- "Runs" is stronger than the text. She left her waterpot and went into the city.
- She does not say "God's promised Saviour." She says Messias, called Christ, will come and tell them all things.
- The talk in between is missing. She calls him a prophet, then they speak about worship, and only then he says he is the Christ.
- "The town comes and calls Him the Saviour of the world" is too fast and too big. Many believed her, he stayed two days, many more believed because they heard him, and then they said he is the Christ, the Saviour of the world.
- "Knew her whole story" is her claim. He stated the five husbands and the man she has now.
- He offered the living water before he named the husbands. "Still offered" turns that order around.
- "Died for our sins and rose the third day" is outside John 4.
- The KJV lines that are spoken are exact. Her question is cut before "which am a woman of Samaria."

**Hook:** 8. The jar question is clear and the end answers it. The last clause about the cross pulls away from that answer.

**Overall:** 7.

**Line fixes**

- Old: His water? You "shall never thirst": life from God inside you, a spring that never runs dry.
- New: Well water runs out. His gift does not. It is life from God inside you, a spring that never runs dry.

- Old: The town comes and calls Him "the Saviour of the world."
- New: He stays, they hear him, and many call him "the Saviour of the world."

**Biggest risk:** After one listen, a new English speaker can retell the jar and the five husbands and still not know what "the gift" is. The water lines are old English, and "His water?" never says it is a picture.

VERDICT: REVISE
Hook 8/10. Overall 7/10. A newcomer can say who, what, and why. Fix the water line and the town line before calling the telling fair to John 4.
