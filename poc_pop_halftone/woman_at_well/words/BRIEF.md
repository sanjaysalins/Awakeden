# Write ONE narration from scratch - the woman at the well (John 4). You are ONE blind writer; you have seen no other draft.

## Who it is for
Worldwide, many with English as a second language, many with no Bible knowledge. People who feel known for their worst, or who keep trying things that leave them thirsty again. Also Bible readers who think they know this story.

## The look (fixed)
Pop-art comic: black-and-white halftone people on loud orange/red/teal/yellow burst rays. Joyful faces. ~60-75 s vertical short.

## The angle (binding)
SPINE: "She came for water. She left without her jar." (4:28 "The woman then left her waterpot"). She came to draw water; she ran back to town and forgot the very thing she came with, because she had found something better.
TURN 1: a tired Jewish man asks a Samaritan woman for a drink (4:6-9) - Jews and Samaritans did not mix ("the Jews have no dealings with the Samaritans", 4:9; say it plainly, never blame a people group).
TURN 2: He offers water after which you "shall never thirst" (4:13-14).
TURN 3: He tells her the truth about her life (4:16-18) - say it simply and without shame or gossip: He knew everything, and He still offered the gift. Do not invent that she was an outcast or why she came at noon.
TURN 4: "I that speak unto thee am he" (4:26) - the Christ.
RUN: she leaves her jar and runs: "Come, see a man, which told me all things that ever I did: is not this the Christ?" (4:29). The town comes and says He is "the Saviour of the world" (4:42).
ENDING: lands on Jesus, the Saviour of the world, who died for our sins and rose the third day (1 Cor 15:3-4, may be said plainly). Earned by the spine (the jar / the thirst). Grace, not pressure.

## Hard rules
1. First 3 seconds: plain words; open the curiosity gap (why would she leave her jar?).
2. Name who's who simply: a Samaritan woman at a well; Jesus, a Jew, tired at noon.
3. KJV quotes verbatim and short. Never let an old word carry the meaning alone; plain English around it. Explain "living water" in plain words if used.
4. No invented feelings, sins or reasons beyond the text. No shaming her.
5. No stock closer ("come to Jesus", "just ask Him", "He's waiting", "will you...?"). No fear, no pressure, no blanket promise.
6. Never blame "the Jews" or "the Samaritans" as a people.
7. 130-170 spoken words (about 55-70 s). One line per voice: [narrator], [jesus], [woman]. Mark one (pause).
8. No wording about AI or how the film is made. No hashtags.

## Output (only this)
(a) the script; (b) word count; (c) KJV fragments with verse refs; (d) 4 lines: who it is for, main take-away, the curiosity gap, why a global newcomer gets it.
