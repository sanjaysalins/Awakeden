# Draft - seat: fable

## (a) Script

[narrator] She came for water. She went home without her jar. Why?
[narrator] Noon. Jesus, a Jew, sits by a well, tired. A Samaritan woman comes for water.
[jesus] Give me to drink.
[narrator] His people and her people did not mix. Still, He asks.
[jesus] Whosoever drinketh of this water shall thirst again.
[narrator] But His water? You "shall never thirst." He means life from God, inside you, that never runs dry.
[narrator] Then He tells her the truth about her life. Five husbands. He knew everything. And He had still offered the gift.
[narrator] She speaks of the Christ, the one God promised.
[jesus] I that speak unto thee am he.
(pause)
[narrator] She leaves her jar and runs to town.
[woman] Come, see a man, which told me all things that ever I did: is not this the Christ?
[narrator] The town comes, and calls Him "the Saviour of the world."
[narrator] This Saviour died for our sins, and rose the third day.
[narrator] The jar stayed at the well. She had found the One who knew everything, and gave anyway.

## (d) Four lines

- Who it is for: anyone who feels known for their worst, or keeps going back to things that leave them thirsty again; and Bible readers who never noticed the jar.
- Main take-away: Jesus knew her whole life and still offered the gift; He is the Saviour of the world, who died for our sins and rose.
- The curiosity gap: she came only for water, so why did she go home without her jar? Paid off at the end: she had found the One who knew everything and gave anyway.
- Why a global newcomer gets it: short plain sentences, everyday things (a well, a jar, thirst), each person named simply, and every old KJV phrase is wrapped in plain English.
