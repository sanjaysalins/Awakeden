## 1. WHO IS THIS FOR (one named viewer; who stops, who finishes, who shares; who is NOT served)
Named viewer: Ana, 24, Manila, raised near church, scrolls Shorts in her second language. The pop-art look and "She left without her jar. Why?" (line 1) stop her. She finishes, because the story is short and the colour change at lines 6-7 rewards her. Bible readers share it: it is faithful and pretty. NOT served: the total newcomer. They get the story but not why being told "five husbands" (line 8) is good news, or what "sins" (line 16) has to do with a well.

## 2. MAIN TAKEAWAY (one sentence a viewer could say back; which line carries it; did the film make it clear?)
"Jesus knew everything about her and still offered her life." Line 15 carries it. Clear, but late: it is told only at second 76. At line 8 itself the film shows only exposure ("the truth about her life"), not welcome. The teal flood sells "never thirst" (line 7) better than any line sells "known and still wanted".

## 3. GLOBAL AUDIENCE (who / what / why in my own words; where I got lost or misled, quote the line; words that fail in translation)
Who: a Samaritan woman and Jesus. What: He asks for water, offers her lasting life, says He is the Christ; she runs to tell her town. Why: partly unclear.
- Lost at lines 8 to 9: "five husbands" jumps straight to "She says the Christ... will come." No bridge. Picture 11 (she looks at the mountain) has no spoken line to explain it.
- Line 16: "died for our sins" arrives from nowhere. Sin was never named. It reads as a bolt-on after the real ending.
- Hard words: "askest drink of me" (line 4), "Whosoever drinketh" (line 6), "which told me all things that ever I did" (line 13). Line 10 is archaic too, but line 11 rescues it well. Line 9's gloss of "Christ" is good.
- "Saviour of the world" (line 14): saved from what? Not said.

## 4. BIBLICALLY ACCURATE (claim by claim: RIGHT / OVERCLAIMED / UNSUPPORTED / WRONG, quote the line; say only what you can support)
- Line 2 "About noon... tired": RIGHT (John 4:6, "sixth hour"; noon is the usual reading).
- Lines 3, 4, 6, 10, 13: RIGHT, KJV verbatim (4:7, 4:9 shortened, 4:13, 4:26, 4:29).
- Line 5 "kept apart": RIGHT (4:9).
- Line 7 "gift from God... shall never thirst... a spring": RIGHT (4:10, 4:14). Drops "everlasting"; acceptable.
- Line 8 "five husbands...": RIGHT (4:18).
- Line 9: RIGHT (4:25).
- Line 12: RIGHT (4:28).
- Line 14: RIGHT (4:39-42).
- Line 15 "The jar stayed... found better water": mildly OVERCLAIMED. The text says she left it, never why, and never says she believed; her own words are a question (line 13).
- Line 16: RIGHT (1 Cor 15:3-4), but not from this passage.
- Picture 13, her tears: UNSUPPORTED by the text. Harmless, but invented.

## 5. BEST VERSION POSSIBLE (what would the best explainer in the world do differently; the ONE highest-value change; score 0-10 against that bar)
The best version makes line 8 the heart, not a fact list: He knows the worst and does not leave. Then line 13 ("told me all things that ever I did") becomes joy, not gossip, and line 15 pays off something already felt. ONE change: rewrite line 8 so the viewer hears "known, and not turned away". Score: 7/10.

## CURIOSITY GAP (opens at line 1, pays off at line 15; score 0-10; worth the wait?)
Opens line 1, half-pays at line 12, pays at line 15. Score 7. Worth the wait, but the answer is implied, never said as an answer. Then line 16 steps past it.

## THREE SMALLEST FIXES (line number + exact replacement wording)
- Line 8: "Then He tells her what no stranger could know: five husbands, and the man she has now is not her husband. He knows it all. He does not turn away."
- Line 15: "Why did she leave the jar? She came for water and found better water: Jesus, who knew her whole story and still offered her life."
- Line 16: "To give that life, He died for our sins and rose the third day."
