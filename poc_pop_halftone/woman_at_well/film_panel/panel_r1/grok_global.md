## 1. WHO IS THIS FOR

Priya, 26, in Jakarta. Captions on. English is her second language. She knows the name Jesus. She does not know this story.

She can finish on the pictures: jar, well, a line slammed between two people, a teal flood, tears, the jar left, the town, the tomb.

She stops when the big words are old English. "GIVE ME TO DRINK." "I THAT SPEAK UNTO THEE AM HE." Auto-captions copy those lines. A translator cannot repair them.

She might send it to a church friend. She will not send it home. Line 16 says "our sins."

Not served: a viewer who hears "Samaritan" as "kind stranger." In this story it means a people Jews would not share water with. Not served: an honour-shame viewer. Line 8 lists five husbands and never says this was private, or that the film is not judging her.

## 2. MAIN TAKEAWAY

"She left the jar because Jesus knew her and offered her a life that outlasts well water."

Line 15 is the carrier. "Better water" and "offered her life" stay fuzzy. Line 16 then adds death and a tomb, so the last clear idea is a second message. The jar is clear. The gift is not.

## 3. GLOBAL AUDIENCE

Who: a woman whose people and Jesus's people did not mix, and a tired Jewish man at a well about noon. What: he asks for a drink, offers a gift from God, names five husbands, and says he is the promised one. She leaves the jar and brings the town. Why, as the film tells it: she found better water, and that water is Jesus. Then a tomb.

Lost at line 4, "askest drink of me." It sounds like "drink me." Plain: "Why do you ask me for a drink?"

Lost at line 7, "a spring that never runs dry." "Spring" is a season. "Runs dry" is an idiom. Plain: "God's life inside you, like water that never ends."

Lost at line 8. No link to the gift. Easy to hear gossip or blame. The point is that he knew a fact he had not been told.

Lost at picture 11. She looks at the mountain and no one says why. Readers of this story fill in a worship argument. Everyone else sees scenery.

Lost at line 10, "I that speak unto thee am he." "He" has no name. Line 11 explains it in speech. The screen keeps the old sentence, and caption-readers trust the screen. Plain: "I am the one God promised."

Lost at line 16, "rose the third day," beside a sunrise tomb. "Rose" can be the sun. "Our sins" builds a "we" the film never introduced. Plain: "came back to life on the third day."

Also fail, same plain swaps: line 3 "Give me to drink" → "Give me some water." Line 6 "Whosoever drinketh" → "Anyone who drinks." Line 13 "which told me all things that ever I did" → "who told me everything I have done." Line 15 "offered her life" → "offered her God's life."

"Christ" on screen still looks like a surname. Line 9 already says "the one God promised," and that gloss never reaches the captions.

A Muslim viewer can follow a prophet who knows a hidden life. Line 16 then states another faith's claim as fact. A Jewish viewer hears line 11, "He is the Christ," as the narrator's own confession.

What needs no lesson: thirst, a wall between people, being known, leaving the jar, telling the town.

## 4. BIBLICALLY ACCURATE

Line 2: RIGHT. Noon is the usual reading of the sixth hour. Line 3: RIGHT. Line 4: RIGHT. Line 5: RIGHT, and soft. The text says no dealings, not mere distance.

Line 6: RIGHT. Line 7: RIGHT as a paraphrase of the gift and of life inside a person. "Never runs dry" is the film's phrase. In the text, the offer begins before "shall thirst again."

Line 8: RIGHT, with no insult added. Her line that he is a prophet is skipped. Missing, not false. Line 9: RIGHT. Line 10: RIGHT. Line 11: RIGHT. Line 12: RIGHT. Line 13: RIGHT. "All things" is her wording in the text. Line 14: RIGHT, shortened. They say "Saviour of the world" after they hear him.

Line 15: OVERCLAIMED. "Whole story" widens one fact plus her excitement. "Better water: Jesus" makes him the drink. The text makes him the giver. "Offered her life" is fair only as everlasting life. A repaired daily life is UNSUPPORTED.

Line 16: later than this scene. Died, and the third day: RIGHT as later events. "For our sins": UNSUPPORTED by the well story. Flowers at the tomb are art.

## 5. BEST VERSION POSSIBLE

Keep these pictures. Put plain words on the screen. One bridge on the husbands: "He knew what no one had told him." One bridge on the mountain, or cut the glance. Let the jar finish the film, or state the tomb in words a child can translate.

The one highest-value change: replace on-screen "I THAT SPEAK UNTO THEE AM HE." with "I AM THE ONE GOD PROMISED." Speak that same sentence.

Score: 5/10. The eye can follow this. The caption track hides the claim.

## CURIOSITY GAP

Opens at line 1. The jar moves at line 12. The why arrives at line 15. Score: 6/10. The empty well is worth the wait. The sentence is not. A concrete question ends in a metaphor, then a tomb.

## THREE SMALLEST FIXES

Line 4: "You are a Jew. Why do you ask me for a drink?"

Line 7: "Then He offers her a different water, a gift from God. Drink it and you will never be thirsty for it again: God's life inside you, like water that never ends."

Line 10: "I am the one God promised."

VERDICT: REVISE — the pictures tell the story; the on-screen English hides the one claim that matters.
