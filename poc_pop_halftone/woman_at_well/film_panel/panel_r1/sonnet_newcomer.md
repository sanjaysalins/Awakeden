## 2. MAIN TAKEAWAY
"Jesus knew everything about a woman's life and still offered her a gift that never runs out, and she ran to tell everyone." Carried by line 15. Mostly clear, but I had to guess what "better water" means. Line 7 helps ("life from God inside you"), but "life from God" is still vague to me.

## 3. GLOBAL AUDIENCE
Who: a woman, a man called Jesus, a well. What: he asks for water, talks about a special water, says things about her life, tells her who he is. She runs to town. Why: she found something better than water.
Where I got lost:
- Line 1: "She left without her jar. Why?" I do not know why a jar matters until line 15. It works as a hook, but the answer ("found better water") is poetic, not a reason.
- Line 2: "a Samaritan woman" and "a Jew". I do not know these groups. Line 5 says they "kept apart" but not why, or that this is a big deal.
- Line 8: "five husbands, and the man she has now is not her husband". I do not know if this is shame, sin or just fact. The film does not say.
- Line 9: "the Christ, the one God promised". I only half understand "Christ" as a title. Line 11 "He is the Christ" is a big claim, and I do not know why she runs off because of it.
- Lines 4, 6, 10, 13: "thou", "askest", "drinketh", "unto thee", "told me all things that ever I did". Old English that I cannot parse well. "Shall never thirst" I can guess.
- "Saviour of the world" (line 14): saviour from what? Unknown.
- Line 16: "died for our sins and rose the third day". Sudden. Who died? What sins? "Our" includes me?
Words that fail in translation: "thirst" (physical or spiritual?), "Saviour", "Christ", "sins", "Samaritan".

## 4. BIBLICALLY ACCURATE
I cannot judge accuracy. What I was TOLD but could not follow: why Jews and Samaritans kept apart (5); what "a gift from God" is, concretely (7); why her husbands matter (8); what "Christ" means beyond "promised one" (9, 11); why "Saviour" (14); why death and rising are mentioned at the end and how they connect to the well (16). The tomb image (19) has no explanation on screen.

## 5. BEST VERSION POSSIBLE
The best explainer would show, in plain words, why this meeting is shocking and what she is missing in her life, so the jar is a real payoff and not a poem. Highest-value change: make the opening question be answered with a clear cause-and-effect line near the end, in simple words (see fix 1). Also cut or earn the abrupt line 16. Score: 6/10. The look and music are strong; the story logic needs a plain "because".

## CURIOSITY GAP
Opens at line 1; pays off at line 15. Score 6/10. The answer is emotionally warm ("found better water") but I wanted a clearer reason. Worth the wait for the visuals and the "never thirst" moment, but the payoff is more of a feeling than an answer.

## THREE SMALLEST FIXES
1. Line 15: replace with "She forgot her jar because she found what she was really thirsty for: Jesus knew everything about her and still welcomed her."
2. Line 5: replace with "Jews and Samaritans were enemies. Jews never spoke to them. He asks anyway."
3. Line 16: replace with "Later, Jesus died on a cross to forgive the wrong things people do, and came back to life three days later." (Tomb image then makes sense.)
