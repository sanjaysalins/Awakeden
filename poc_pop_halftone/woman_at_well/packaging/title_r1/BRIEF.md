# Blind writer brief: title + thumbnail words + thumbnail picture idea for a 91-second Bible short

You write ALONE. You have not seen any other writer's ideas or any earlier title.

## The film (what the viewer will actually see and hear)
Bold pop-art comic look: black ink drawings with halftone dots, bright sunburst rays (teal, yellow, orange, red). Vertical ~91 s short (YouTube Shorts / TikTok / Reels). Narrator + voice of Jesus + voice of the woman. Full words, in order:
- Narrator: "She came for water. She left without her jar. Why?"
- About noon, a Samaritan woman comes to a well. Jesus, a Jew, sits there, tired.
- Jesus: "Give me to drink."
- Woman: "How is it that thou, being a Jew, askest drink of me?"
- Jews and Samaritans kept apart. He asks anyway.
- Jesus: "Whosoever drinketh of this water shall thirst again."
- Then He offers her a different water, a gift from God. Drink it, and you "shall never thirst": life from God inside you, a spring that never runs dry.
- Then He tells her the truth about her life: five husbands, and the man she has now is not her husband. He knew it all, and stayed.
- She says the Christ, the one God promised, will come. Jesus: "I that speak unto thee am he." He is the Christ.
- (pause) She leaves her jar and goes back to town. Woman: "Come, see a man, which told me all things that ever I did: is not this the Christ?"
- Many come, hear Him, and call Him "the Saviour of the world."
- "The jar stayed. She came for water, and found the One who gives better water: Jesus, who knew her whole story and offered her life. To give that life, He died for our sins and rose the third day."

## Pictures that exist in the film (a thumbnail can use or recombine these)
Jesus alone, tired, sitting on a stone well. Jesus talking to the woman, she holds a big clay jar, surprised. Close-up of the woman laughing with tears. The jar left alone by the well while she runs, arms open, toward a village. All with pop-art sunburst rays behind.

## The job
The film's own hook is the empty jar: "She came for water. She left without her jar. Why?" The title + thumbnail must make a scrolling stranger want to know too.

## Rules
- Global audience: many viewers have English as a second language and know nothing about the Bible. Short, common words. No archaic words. No insider Bible terms ("Samaritan" alone means nothing to a newcomer).
- The owner likes SHORT, simple titles.
- Title + thumbnail words are ONE unit: the thumbnail must NOT repeat the title; together they open a question the film answers.
- Honest: the gap must really be paid off by the film above. No fake shock, no "you won't believe", no "then this happened", no claims the film does not make. Do not shame the woman or make her past the joke; the film's turn is that Jesus knew it all and stayed.
- Title <= 60 characters. A search word ("Jesus", "woman at the well") somewhere helps search, but curiosity comes first.
- Thumbnail text: max ~6 words total, 1-2 short lines, big poster type.

## Output (markdown, nothing else)
1. Six different PAIRS. For each: `TITLE` / `THUMB WORDS (line 1 | line 2)` / `THUMB PICTURE` (one line) / one line: what question it opens + where the film pays it off.
2. Your BEST pair, and why in 2 short lines.
3. One line: the biggest risk with your best pair.
