# Blind judge: title + thumbnail pairs

| Pair | Tap: newcomer | Tap: Bible reader | Honesty | One-line reason |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **P1** Why Did She Leave Her Jar? + "HE KNEW / EVERYTHING" | 7 | 6 | 6 | Strong gap ("knew what?"), but on its own it sounds like she got exposed. That leans toward shame, and the film is kind. |
| **P2** Why Did She Leave Her Jar? + "SHE MET / JESUS" | 5 | 4 | 9 | Clear and true, but flat. It gives the answer away and leaves no reason to tap. |
| **P3** Why Did She Leave Her Jar? + "HE KNEW IT ALL / AND STAYED" | 8 | 7 | 9 | The title asks, the thumb hints but doesn't answer. "And stayed" turns "caught" into "loved", and it's the film's own line. |
| **P4** Jesus Knew Her Whole Story + "SHE LEFT / HER JAR" | 7 | 6 | 8 | Good pull ("what story?"). The thumb just says what the picture already shows, so the text adds less. |
| **P5** The Woman at the Well + "SHE LEFT / HER JAR" | 4 | 6 | 9 | Good for search, and a Bible reader knows it. A newcomer sees no question and no stakes. |

**My pick: P3.** The title asks why. The thumb says someone knew everything and didn't leave. The picture shows a joyful run. All three work together, and the film pays off every part.

**One word fix:** "HE KNEW IT ALL / AND **STILL** STAYED". Adding "still" makes the contrast obvious to someone with English as a second language, scrolling fast.

**Biggest risk:** In the thumb, "he" has no name and the jar has to read as *left behind*. If the jar just looks like a decoration, the title's question has nothing to grab onto and the pair goes flat. Check at phone size that the jar looks abandoned, not posed.
