## TITLES: 6 movie titles (1-4 words)

* **She Left the Jar** | Why leave the jar you came to fill? | A plain sentence title, the kind true-story films use. The verb is the mystery.
* **Better Water** | What water is better than a full jar? | Two common words, like a fable with one idea. A poster can hold it.
**The Jar** | Whose jar is this, and where did they go? | One-noun titles put a whole film on one object.
**The Well at Noon** | Who sits out in the worst heat, and why? | A time and a place. Quiet films are named this way.
**He Stayed** | He already knew her life. Why is he still there? | Two words, like a moral drama. The stay is kindness, not a love story.
**Thirst Again** | The well works. Why does the thirst come back? | A puzzle title built on one line a stranger can repeat.

### STILL HERE  (title: The Jar)
- THE IDEA in one sentence: The hero is a full clay jar on an empty well, and the missing person is the point.
- WHAT YOU SEE: Vertical 9:16. Bottom half is the stone rim of a well and a huge two-handled clay jar with a rope, black ink, cross-hatch, halftone clay. The well mouth is a black circle. No people. Top half is giant sunburst rays, teal, yellow, orange, red, halftone dots. A thin far line of mud-brick roofs sits on the horizon. Noon. Nobody.
- LETTERING: Title across the upper sky, very large, about one fifth of the height, black or cream, wide and plain. Tagline on the stone under the jar, small: "Still here."
- THE CURIOSITY GAP: Who brought this jar and walked away? The film opens on that question and answers it when she leaves the jar, runs to town, and the end line says the jar stayed.
- PHONE TEST: A big jar, a black hole, color rays. The missing person still reads.
- IMAGE PROMPT: Vertical 9:16 key art, bold pop-art comic, Roy Lichtenstein meets a Bible storybook. Black ink line drawing, halftone dots, cross-hatching. A huge two-handled clay jar with a rope sits alone on the stone lip of a well at noon. The well mouth is a pure black circle. No people, no faces, no hands. Far horizon is a thin row of small mud-brick houses. Behind the jar, enormous sunburst rays in teal, yellow, orange, and red, packed with halftone dots. The jar and stone stay mostly black-and-white ink. Match the jar and well from film pictures 1 and 6. No lettering, no text.

### THE FLOOD  (title: Better Water)
- THE IDEA in one sentence: Ordinary ink water spills from the jar, then living teal water rises and takes the whole page.
- WHAT YOU SEE: A hard split across the middle. Above the line: black-and-white only. Jesus, long dark hair, beard, plain off-white robe, tan sash, sits tired on the well, head bowed. The woman stands on the far side of the well, not close, dark hair, light head-scarf, plain dress, rope belt, big jar in both hands, surprised. The well is between them. Wide gap. No gaze, no faces near each other. Below the line: teal and blue ink water floods up, halftone dots, and turns into orange and red sunburst rays. The spill from the jar crosses the line and becomes that color flood.
- LETTERING: Title centered on the split, large, sitting in a clean band so it does not cover faces. Tagline low in the color, small: "Never thirst."
- THE CURIOSITY GAP: Why does the picture drown in color? He says well water leaves you thirsty, then offers a gift from God, a spring that does not run dry. She leaves the jar after that.
- PHONE TEST: Two worlds. Ink people on top. A teal flood below. The jar on the line.
- IMAGE PROMPT: Vertical 9:16 key art, bold pop-art comic, black ink lines, halftone dots, cross-hatching, huge sunburst rays. A hard horizontal split. Top half, black and white only: at noon a tired man with long dark hair, a beard, a plain off-white robe and a tan sash sits on a stone well, head lowered. Across the well, far from him, a woman with dark hair, a light head-scarf, a plain dress and a rope belt holds a big two-handled clay jar on a rope. She looks surprised. The well is between them. They do not face each other closely. No romance, no touching. Bottom half: teal and blue living water floods upward with halftone dots and becomes orange, yellow, and red sunburst rays. Water spilling from the jar crosses the split and turns into that color. Match film pictures 2, 3, and 4. No lettering, no text.

### THE CUTOUT  (title: She Left the Jar)
- THE IDEA in one sentence: The jar is a hole cut out of the sunburst, and inside that hole she is running to town.
- WHAT YOU SEE: Full-bleed sunburst, teal, yellow, orange, red, halftone, rays from the center. In the middle, a tall jar-shaped window of blank cream paper. No clay texture. The jar is negative space. Two handle bumps. A rope suggested by the cut edge. Inside the cream jar, a small black-ink scene: the real jar left on the well in front, and behind it, tiny, she runs with arms open toward a mud-brick village. She is small, seen from behind, not a portrait. Jesus is not in this frame.
- LETTERING: Title below the jar window, very large, black, on the strongest ray. No tagline.
- THE CURIOSITY GAP: Why is a running woman trapped inside the shape of a jar? The film answers when she drops it and shouts, "Come, see a man," and the town comes to hear the one they call the Saviour of the world.
- PHONE TEST: A cream jar on loud rays. At thumb size the jar still reads. The run inside is the reward up close.
- IMAGE PROMPT: Vertical 9:16 key art, bold pop-art comic, black ink and halftone. The whole frame is enormous sunburst rays in teal, yellow, orange, and red with halftone dots. Centered, a large jar-shaped hole of blank cream paper cuts through the rays, negative space, two handle notches, rope only as the edge of the cut. No clay drawn. Inside that cream jar shape, a tiny black-ink scene with cross-hatching: a clay jar left on a stone well in the foreground, and far behind it a small woman seen from the back, dark hair, light head-scarf, plain dress, rope belt, arms open, running toward a mud-brick village. No man in the frame. No close faces. Match the jar shape from film picture 1 and the run from film picture 6. No lettering, no text.

### MY #1 and why (2 lines)
**THE CUTOUT**, title **She Left the Jar**. One shape does the whole job: the jar, the empty space, and the woman running home. At two centimeters it is still a jar on a sunburst, and the film's first line is the question it opens.
