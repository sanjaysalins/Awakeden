# Score plan: "Jesus and the woman at the well". One seat, written blind.

## 1. SCORE IDEA
**A single falling drip becomes a rising spring.** The motif that means "thirst" is turned upside down, and that turned-over motif means "living water".

- **Instruments:**
  - Oud plucks give 1st-century Samaria without trying too hard.
  - Frame drum and riq, plus tight handclaps, give the pop-comic punch.
  - A muted marimba plays the "drip" notes.
  - Upright bass holds the bottom.
  - A warm ney flute appears only once the water flows.
  - One soft string pad is kept for the calm beats.
- **The motif ("the Drip"):** three falling notes, D, C, A, plucked dry on marimba with an oud echo. That is thirst.
- **The flood:** the same three notes flipped to rise, A, C, D, and then one step higher to E. A spring "wells up". The ney carries it.
- **The ending:** the empty tomb plays the rising version, very slow, on one string note and a single drip.

## 2. WHY IT FITS
- **Pop-comic energy:** frame drum, claps and marimba are percussive and dotted, like Ben-Day dots. They can "slam" with the panel cuts without trailer booms or big drops.
- **Gentle gospel story:** oud and ney belong to the region and the period. The calm beats strip down to a pad and one note, so the KJV words stand alone.
- **One motif, two shapes:** falling turns to rising, so the music tells the thirst-to-living-water idea by itself. A newcomer hears it even if they miss the words.

## 4. SPLIT
**Split at 48.60 s.** This is the gap after "will come" (ends 48.24) and before "I that speak" (49.00).
- Part 1 runs 0–48.6 s, which is **48.6 s**.
- Part 2 runs 48.6–87.87 s, which is **39.3 s**.
- The join sits on a near-silent drone, so a 0.3 s crossfade cannot be heard.
- Part 2 opens calm, which is the easiest kind of opening to generate cleanly.

## 5. MUSIC PROMPTS

**PART 1 (49 s):**
> Instrumental only, no vocals, 49 seconds, 96 BPM, D minor, Middle Eastern folk meets bright pop percussion, warm and clean, not a trailer. Starts with only a dry muted marimba playing three falling notes D-C-A, echoed by an oud, no drums. Around 4 seconds in, one sharp handclap then frame drum, riq and upright bass start a light groove with an oud ostinato. Around 11 seconds in, drums drop to soft riq taps. Around 14 seconds in, the groove returns, slightly tense, with an oud string bend and two hard claps around 19 seconds in. Around 23 seconds in, everything thins out: drums and bass stop, only slow dry marimba drips D-C-A and a faint brush. Around 30 seconds in, a joyful lift: the motif now rises A-C-D-E on ney flute, full frame drum, handclaps, walking bass going up. Around 37 seconds in, pull back to a low soft oud drone and quiet bass. Around 44 seconds in, a gentle rising pulse. Ends softly on a held low note. Continuous sound throughout, keep the midrange light for a voice-over.

**PART 2 (40 s):**
> Instrumental only, no vocals, 40 seconds, 96 BPM, D major feel, Middle Eastern folk with bright pop percussion, warm, reverent then joyful, not a trailer. Starts very quiet: a soft sustained string pad on one note and one gentle marimba drip, no drums. Around 7 seconds in, the ney flute plays a rising A-C-D-E motif, frame drum and handclaps build into a happy running groove. Around 18 seconds in, the fullest warm moment with oud, ney, frame drum and bass together. Around 22 seconds in, sudden drop to a single plucked oud note. Around 25 seconds in, a warm half-level groove with the rising motif, settling down. Around 33 seconds in, only a soft string pad remains. Around 35 seconds in, one slow soft rising marimba phrase A-C-D-E. The pad holds gently to the end and fades out. Continuous sound, never fully silent, keep the midrange light for a voice-over.

## 6. MIX NOTES
- **Voice first:** voice at about −14 LUFS, music bed about 10 dB under it. Use voice-keyed ducking: −8 dB, 80 ms attack, 400 ms release.
- **EQ:** cut 2 to 4 kHz on the music by about 3 dB everywhere so the words stay clear.
- **Lifts:** let the music up to about −18 LUFS only in the word gaps. These are 32.6–33.4 (the flood peak), 54.0–54.6, 56–58 (the run) and 66.4–66.8.
- **Near-out:** 22.6–29.8 (dry), 40–44, 48.6–55.7 and 71.0–73.3. In these, music sits at about −32 LUFS, a pad or one note only.
- **3 s hold (84.74–87.87):** pad and the last rising drip at about −28 LUFS, with a linear fade from 86.87 to 87.87. No drum hit at the end.

## 7. RISKS
1. **Dead air or a sudden empty patch.**
   - Check: measure loudness in 0.5 s windows.
   - Flag any window below −45 dBFS outside the planned quiet zones. Flag any planned quiet zone that is fully silent.
   - Re-roll that part only.
2. **The cues land late or early, so the flood misses "never thirst".**
   - Check: run onset detection and find the biggest energy rise in part 1. It must fall between 29.5 and 31.0 s.
   - If it is off by less than 1 s, nudge the part's start. If it is off by more, re-roll.
3. **The music fights the voice** (oud and ney share the voice's range).
   - Check: measure music loudness under speech and against speech. Target 10 dB or more below.
   - Then listen once on phone speakers at the noisy beats: 19 s, 33 s and 67 s. Every word must be clear.
