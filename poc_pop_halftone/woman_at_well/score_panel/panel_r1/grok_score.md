1. SCORE IDEA

A four-note well-drop — three short rising plucks, then one longer note that falls — starts as lonely drops and later overlaps so each fall seeds the next rise: a spring that feeds itself. Instruments: dry oud, breathy ney, small frame drum, soft kalimba, muted bass, and a dot-like shaker. No singer.

2. WHY IT FITS

- The shaker and kalimba are the Ben-Day dots. The frame drum is the panel pulse. Density changes with the picture. Volume stays small.
- Oud and ney place the story at a hot well in Samaria. The muted bass keeps a pop spine under the comic rays.
- The same four notes carry thirst, the teal flood, the run to town, and the tomb. The quiet beats are the motif stepping aside so the KJV line is the event.

7. RISKS

- The flood or the tomb gets loud. Measure RMS: 81.5–87.87 must be the quietest stretch. The flood may be the fullest texture, and its peaks stay about 10 dB under the voice.
- Part 1 dies early, or the join clicks. Check that the ney-only fade is really present around 49–54 before you cut, and that the waveform at 54.40 has no spike and no hole longer than the breath.
- The fast line from 60.6–66.4 smears, or the kalimba sounds like a toy. By ear, every consonant in "all things that ever I did" stays crisp. If the drops sound cartoonish, lower the kalimba and let the oud carry the motif.
