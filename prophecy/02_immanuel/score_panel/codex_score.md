## 1. SCORE IDEA
One unfinished tune travels from Ahaz’s frightened refusal to Joseph’s trust, finding its answer only after the final words.

**Instruments:** full orchestral strings, restrained French horns, one soft timpani stroke; no vocals.

**Motif:** D–E–A–F♯, then E–D: step upward, reach upward, settle gently, finally come home. Early scenes reveal only fragments. Reserve the complete six-note phrase for the closing hold. Both parts: 72 BPM, D major destination, opening shaded toward B minor.

## 2. WHY IT FITS a bold inked graphic-comic look AND this story
- Low string pulses give the inked panels weight; changes in orchestral texture mark page turns without striking every comic word.
- Ahaz and Joseph share the same musical fragment, connecting two sons of David. Joseph’s obedience changes its harmony into warmth.
- The biggest emotional gesture belongs to resurrection. Grace emerges through release and simplicity, without making God’s answer sound threatening.

## 6. MIX NOTES
Keep music approximately **16–20 dB below active speech**, ducking another 4–6 dB during Isaiah and the angel. Let the resurrection swell approach 12–14 dB below narration. Mute music at 57.24–59.2. At 60.80, release ducking smoothly: the final phrase becomes audible without a second swell. Preserve all three seconds, including the ending decay.

## 7. RISKS
1. **Speech gets buried:** compare voice/music short-term levels; audition every KJV word on a phone in mono.
2. **Generated timing or seam drifts:** check the rendered waveform against cue markers; trim or extend sustained beds and audition the join on headphones.
3. **Emotion becomes threatening or sentimental:** listen to Ahaz, cross and resurrection consecutively. Remove aggressive attacks, sobbing string slides or excessive horn weight; hum the ending to confirm the tune remains simple.
