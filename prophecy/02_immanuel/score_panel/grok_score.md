**1. SCORE IDEA**

A four-note "With Us" tune, C–E–G–A, hangs unfinished until the last hold, when A steps home to C. Strings carry it, French horns wait for grace, and one soft timpani marks only the gold and the rising.

Ahaz lives in C minor: cellos play a slow human pulse, about 60 bpm, close and worried. The tune stays hidden. At "God chose the sign" only C–E glimmers, then the music thins so Isaiah can speak. After the 700-year cut the same A returns, warmer. Horns enter once, on "This child saves." The pulse stops for the cross and the tomb. One swell on "He rose." Jesus is nearly bare. In the 3 s hold the horns and violins finish C–E–G–C and let it ring.

**2. WHY IT FITS**

- Ink and flat colour want a tune you can hum in four notes, not a fog of pads. The hanging A is a musical splash: the sign is offered, and it does not land until "GOD WITH US."
- Ahaz is fear without horror. A close cello pulse and no horns. Horns are kept for the child, so grace is a new colour, the way a comic page turns from night-blue to lamplight.
- Same family as Bethlehem: strings, horns, timpani used twice. This film's own shape is refusal, then answer. The tune is new, and it is not allowed to finish early.
