## 1. WHO IS THIS FOR (one named viewer; who stops, who finishes, who shares; who is NOT served)
Named viewer: Grace, 34, Nairobi, churchgoer, knows "Immanuel" from Christmas songs but has never heard of Ahaz.
- Stops: scrollers, on line 1 plus the big "NO." It is a strong, plain hook.
- Finishes: Bible readers. The Ahaz / Jesus "save" contrast (line 8) is new to most of them.
- Shares: Christians, mostly at Christmas. Line 8 is the share line.
- NOT served: the total newcomer between 3 s and 17 s. Line 2 packs five events into about 9 seconds. The ESL viewer also loses lines 6-7 (thou, thee, unto).

## 2. MAIN TAKEAWAY
"A king said no to God, and God still sent Jesus, God with us, to save." Line 8 carries it; line 11 seals it. Clear for a Bible reader. For a newcomer it is half clear: they get "God with us" but may not link line 11 back to the "no" in line 1, 60 seconds earlier.

## 3. GLOBAL AUDIENCE
Who: a scared king, a prophet, then Mary, Joseph, a baby named Jesus. What: the king refuses God's help and pays a foreign king; God promises a child; 700 years later the child comes. Why: God wants to be with people and save them.

Where I got lost:
- Line 2, "offered any sign, as proof." Proof of what? The film never says God promised the enemies would fail.
- Line 2, "begged a foreign king." Which king, saved from whom? The picture of gold helps; the words do not.
- Line 6, "fear not to take unto thee Mary thy wife." Nothing says Joseph feared or planned to leave her. The "troubled" panel is the only clue.
- Line 9, "With us at the cross." A fragment with no subject; "He" has no clear owner.
- Unexplained boy beside Isaiah (0-12 s). Three crosses with no word on which is His.

Fails in translation: "alway" (line 10, reads as a typo on screen), "thou / thee / unto" (line 6), "conceive" and "Behold" (line 3), "sign" (road sign?), "begged" is fine.

## 4. BIBLICALLY ACCURATE
- Line 1, "told God no": RIGHT in sense (Isa 7:12), but Ahaz's refusal was dressed as piety ("I will not tempt the LORD"). Mild simplification.
- Line 2, David's family, fear, "fear not," any sign, refusal: RIGHT (Isa 7:2, 4, 11-13). "save me": RIGHT (2 Kings 16:7).
- Line 2, order "refused, and begged": slightly OVERCLAIMED. Scripture does not date the plea to after the refusal.
- Line 2, "God chose the sign": RIGHT (Isa 7:14, "the Lord himself").
- Line 3: RIGHT, KJV verbatim.
- Line 4, "Seven hundred years": RIGHT as a round number (about 730). "by God's Holy Spirit": RIGHT (Matt 1:18).
- Lines 6-7: RIGHT, verbatim, fairly trimmed.
- Line 8: RIGHT (Matt 1:23-24). The save contrast is the film's own link, but fair.
- Line 9: RIGHT (1 Cor 15:3-4).
- Line 10: RIGHT but trimmed. Picture is UNSUPPORTED in detail: Matt 28:16 sets it on a mountain, not a lake path.
- Temple "bowls": invented detail; the gold and silver from the temple is RIGHT (2 Kings 16:8).

## 5. BEST VERSION POSSIBLE
The best explainer would give Ahaz's problem one clear picture-sentence and then slow down. It would also say in one breath why Joseph was afraid.
ONE highest-value change: thin out line 2. It is the only place a newcomer falls off, and everything after depends on it.
Score: 7/10.

## CURIOSITY GAP
Opens at line 1 ("God answered anyway"), pays off at line 8, sealed at lines 10-11. Score 7/10. Worth the wait, but the gap goes quiet from line 4 to line 7; nothing reminds us an answer is still owed.

## PROMISE KEPT
God, through Isaiah, promised a frightened king a virgin-born son called Immanuel. About 700 years later Mary bore Jesus, who saves and stays. Yes, it feels kept: the "700 YEARS LATER" tag and line 8 do the work. 8/10.

## THREE SMALLEST FIXES
- Line 5: "An angel told Joseph:" -> "Joseph was afraid to marry her. An angel told him:"
- Line 9: "With us at the cross, where He died for our sins." -> "God with us at the cross, where Jesus died for our sins."
- Line 11: "That was God's answer." -> "That was God's answer to the king's no."
