## 1. WHO IS THIS FOR
One viewer: a person who likes comic-style stories and swipes through Shorts, with no Bible background. Who stops: the first line works ("A frightened king told God no", line 1), and the NO slam word is a clear hook. Who finishes: someone who wants to know what God's "answer" was. Who shares: little. The ending is warm, but nothing in the film is built to be passed on. Not served: anyone who needs the names explained. Ahaz, Isaiah, David, Jerusalem, Mary, Joseph and Matthew arrive faster than I can hold them. A slow reader on a phone is lost in the first 12 seconds.

## 2. MAIN TAKEAWAY
"A king refused God, God promised a child called Immanuel, and that child is Jesus, who saves people and is with us always." Line 8 ("This child saves... God with us") and line 10 carry it. It is mostly clear. But I was never told who Jesus is until line 7, and I had to guess that "the child" and "Jesus" are the same person.

## 3. GLOBAL AUDIENCE
Who / what / why, in my words: A scared king asked another king for help, not God. A man told him a special baby would come. Long after, a woman had a baby from God, an angel said to name him Jesus, and he saves people and later died and came alive again, and he says he stays with us.

Where I got lost:
- Line 2: "from King David's family" – who is David? Why does it matter?
- Line 2: "offered any sign, as proof" – a sign of what? "Begged a foreign king: 'save me'" is confusing. I did not know the king was a different person from the first king until the picture of messengers carrying gold.
- Line 2 ends "God chose the sign:" then a new voice says line 3. The link is thin.
- Line 3: "Behold, a virgin shall conceive" – "behold", "conceive", "virgin" are hard words for a second-language viewer. Nobody tells me this is the sign.
- Line 4: "Seven hundred years later" – later than what? The film never said when line 3 was spoken.
- Line 6: "thou", "unto thee", "thy" – old English; hard to follow when spoken.
- Line 7: "save his people from their sins" – what are sins? Saved from what?
- Line 8: "Ahaz begged a king to save him. This child saves." – this contrast is clever but compressed; I had to remember Ahaz.
- Line 9: "He died for our sins", "the third day", "rose from the dead" – who is "He"? The film jumps from baby to cross with no setup. The image of the manger, crosses and tomb is shown in under 6 seconds.
- Line 9: "He promised:" – the promise appears only in line 10.
- Line 11: "That was God's answer." – answer to what? I forgot the question.
Words that fail in translation: sign, behold, conceive, alway, sins, third day, "begged".

## 4. BIBLICALLY ACCURATE
I cannot judge accuracy. What I was TOLD that I could not follow: why a virgin birth is a "sign" for a frightened king (line 3); why Joseph is called "son of David" (line 6); what "sins" are (line 7); how a baby, a cross and a tomb connect (line 9); and who "He" is in "He promised" (line 9).

## 5. BEST VERSION POSSIBLE
A world-class explainer would do less. It would name three people, not seven, say the promise once in plain words, and let the viewer feel the waiting time. Score: 5/10. The pictures and slam words are strong. The spoken script is too dense for the first half. Highest-value change: slow the first 24 seconds and state the setup plainly: "A king was afraid. God offered help. The king said no. So God promised a baby."

## CURIOSITY GAP
Opens at line 1 ("God answered anyway"), pays off at line 11 ("That was God's answer"). Score: 6/10. Worth the wait for the final image (Jesus walking, "I am with you alway"). But the "answer" is only clear if you track the king, the name Immanuel, and Jesus together, which I mostly did not on a first watch.

## PROMISE KEPT
Who made a promise: God, through an old man, to a scared king. A child named "God with us" would come. How long before: the film says "seven hundred years later", though it never says later than what. What came true: a baby named Jesus was born, and at the end he says he is with us. I felt the ending emotionally, but the "promise kept" link is made by the slam words (IMMANUEL then GOD WITH US) more than by the spoken script. Score: 6/10.

## THREE SMALLEST FIXES
1. Line 2: replace "from King David's family, was afraid of war" with "was afraid. Two armies were coming for his city."
2. Line 4: replace "Seven hundred years later" with "Seven hundred years after that promise,".
3. Line 9: replace "He promised:" with "Before He went back to heaven, Jesus promised His friends:" (and replace "alway" in the on-screen text with "always").
