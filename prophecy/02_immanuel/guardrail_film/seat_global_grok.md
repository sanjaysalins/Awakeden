## 1. WHO IS THIS FOR

Amina, 19, in Lagos or Jakarta. She watches Shorts on a phone, sound low, English as a second language. She knows the word Jesus and the shape of a cross. She does not know David, Ahaz, or Immanuel.

She may finish on the pictures: scared king, gold carried off, baby, crosses, empty tomb, a man walking with friends. She shares only if someone already told her the meaning. She is not served if she needs a translation. The promise is spoken in old Bible English, and that English is baked into the frames. Translated captions cannot change pixels.

Also not served: a viewer who does not know the cross, and a Bible reader waiting for Isaiah’s nearer promise (the attacking kings fall). That promise is never said.

## 2. MAIN TAKEAWAY

"A scared king asked another king to save him. God sent a child who saves, and God stays with us."

Line 8 carries it: "Ahaz begged a king to save him. This child saves. Immanuel means: God with us." The end card repeats GOD WITH US. That one sentence is clear. Line 2 never says what the "sign" was proof of.

## 3. GLOBAL AUDIENCE

Who / what / why, as I can retell it: King Ahaz is afraid of war and refuses God. He sends gold and says "save me." Isaiah says a virgin will have a son named Immanuel. About 700 years later Mary is pregnant by God’s Spirit. An angel tells Joseph to name the baby Jesus, because he will save people from their sins. Jesus dies, lives on the third day, and says he is with you.

Where I get lost:

- Line 2, "offered any sign, as proof." A road sign? Proof of what? The enemy kings are only campfires.
- The boy beside Isaiah in the first page. Whose child? Never said.
- At 16.9 s the slam word is IMMANUEL, with no meaning. I wait until line 8.
- On screen, "2 KINGS 16:7" looks like "two kings," not a book. This film is about kings.
- Line 9, "With us at the cross" has no subject.
- The end card cites MATTHEW 1:23 under "I am with you alway." Those spoken words are Matthew 28:20. A lookup misses them.

Words that fail: line 3, "Behold," and "bear a son" (captions turn this into an animal or "carry a son"). Line 6, "thou," "thee," "thy," "take unto thee." Line 7, "thou shalt call his name." Line 9, "rose" (a flower). Line 10, on screen and in the voice: "alway" (looks like a typo; people hear "a way"). Line 4, "Holy Spirit," with no gloss, can sound like a ghost. Line 7, "sins," while "save" in line 2 already meant rescue from war. Line 2 and line 6, "David," who is never a person here.

What travels: NO, SAVE ME, 700 YEARS LATER, JESUS, HE SAVES, GOD WITH US, the gold, the empty tomb, the walk by the lake.

## 4. BIBLICALLY ACCURATE

- Line 1, "told God no." OVERCLAIMED. Isaiah 7:12 is a refusal to ask for a sign. He says he will not test God.
- Line 2, fear, war, "fear not," any sign. RIGHT (Isaiah 7:2-11). The film skips Isaiah 7:16 (the child is also a timer for those two kings). Jesus becomes the only meaning. OVERCLAIMED by the cut. "Save me" to a foreign king is RIGHT (2 Kings 16:7). "Begged" is soft: he also paid temple gold (2 Kings 16:8). The picture shows the gold. The words do not.
- Line 3. RIGHT as a KJV quote of Isaiah 7:14.
- Line 4. RIGHT enough: about 700 years, Matthew 1:18.
- Lines 6-7. RIGHT as a cut quote of Matthew 1:20-21. The cut drops "that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost." Line 4 already said the plain version.
- Line 8. RIGHT (Matthew 1:21-25).
- Line 9, died for sins, third day. RIGHT (1 Corinthians 15:3-4). "With us at the cross" is the film’s link, not the verse. OVERCLAIMED as the meaning of the name.
- Line 10. RIGHT as a short quote of Matthew 28:20. The lake path and one young man are not that scene (a mountain, the eleven). UNSUPPORTED picture. The manger is Luke, not in the named passages. Small UNSUPPORTED detail.
- Line 11, "That was God's answer." OVERCLAIMED. The direct answer to Ahaz is Isaiah 7:14.
- End card GOD WITH US / MATTHEW 1:23. RIGHT for that phrase. It does not source the "alway" line.

## 5. BEST VERSION POSSIBLE

The best explainer would let a newcomer get who, what, and why with the sound off. This film’s comic words already do half of that. Then it burns the hardest old-English lines into the same frames, and hides the meaning of Immanuel until line 8.

The one change: speak and print lines 3, 6, 7, and 10 in plain modern English, and put "God with us" on the IMMANUEL frame at 16.9 s.

Score: 5/10.

## CURIOSITY GAP

Opens at line 2 ("God chose the sign:"). Line 3 speaks the sign in words that do not travel. The payoff a newcomer can use is line 8. Score 6/10. The colon is a real hook. Line 3 spends it.

## PROMISE KEPT

God, through Isaiah, promised a son named Immanuel. About 700 years later the film shows the child, says the name means God with us, says he saves from sins, shows the cross and the empty tomb, and has Jesus say he is with you. The tag 700 YEARS LATER and the walk make time and presence feel real. It does not feel fully kept here, because the promise (line 3) and the kept words (line 10, "alway") are the lines that break. Score 6/10.

## THREE SMALLEST FIXES

- Line 3: "Look. A virgin will have a son, and she will name him Immanuel. Immanuel means God with us."
- Line 6: "Joseph, from King David's family, do not be afraid to take Mary as your wife."
- Line 10, voice and burned-in letters: "I am with you always."

Print those same words in the read-along. "alway" on the last frame teaches a typo.
