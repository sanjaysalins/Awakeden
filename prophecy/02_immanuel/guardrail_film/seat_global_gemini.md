## 1. WHO IS THIS FOR
A non-native English speaker who knows basic Christian concepts but relies on simple, direct language. Served: Global believers seeking encouragement, who will grasp the visual contrast between a frightened king and a saving God. Not served: Viewers with zero biblical background, who will get bogged down by the ancient political history in Line 2, and basic ESL learners who will hit a wall at the archaic 17th-century grammar in Lines 6 and 7.

## 2. MAIN TAKEAWAY
God's ultimate answer to human fear is His presence with us. This is carried directly by Line 8: "Immanuel means: God with us." The film made it very clear by contrasting Ahaz begging a human king to "save me" against Jesus actually saving His people.

## 3. GLOBAL AUDIENCE
The on-screen comic words ("NO", "SAVE ME", "IMMANUEL", "FEAR NOT", "HE SAVES", "GOD WITH US") are fantastic visual anchors. They use simple, universal vocabulary that helps non-native readers follow the story even if they cannot parse the audio quickly enough. 

However, the audio uses archaic English that completely breaks machine translation and auto-captions. In Line 6, the angel says, "fear not to take unto thee Mary thy wife." To a modern ESL reader, "take unto thee" sounds like physical grasping or kidnapping; it does not translate to "marry." In Line 7, "thou shalt" is confusing insider religious language. In Line 10, Jesus says, "I am with you alway." The missing 's' looks like a spelling error to a non-native reader, and auto-captions will likely "fix" it or misinterpret it entirely, breaking the final emotional moment.

## 4. BIBLICALLY ACCURATE
* Line 2: RIGHT. Ahaz refused the sign and begged a foreign king for help (Isaiah 7:10-12, 2 Kings 16:7).
* Line 3: RIGHT. The prophet promises a virgin will bear a son named Immanuel (Isaiah 7:14).
* Line 4: RIGHT. Mary was pregnant by the Holy Spirit (Matthew 1:18).
* Line 7: RIGHT. He shall save his people from their sins (Matthew 1:21).
* Line 9: RIGHT. Died for our sins, rose on the third day (1 Corinthians 15:3-4).

## 5. BEST VERSION POSSIBLE
The best explainer in the world would recognize that a global audience cannot emotionally connect with a landing line that sounds grammatically incorrect. The ONE highest-value change is updating the archaic pronouns and vocabulary so the promises feel immediate and personal to a modern listener, rather than locked in a 400-year-old dialect. Score: 7/10.

## CURIOSITY GAP
Opens at Line 1 ("God answered anyway"), pays off at Line 8 ("This child saves") and Line 11 ("That was God's answer"). Score: 8/10. Worth the wait, as the historical setup connects perfectly to the climax.

## PROMISE KEPT
God promised a frightened king a sign of a child named Immanuel. Seven hundred years later, that specific sign arrived to save people and literally be "God with us." The time jump on screen between Line 3 and Line 4 makes this feel like an ancient, unbreakable vow fulfilled across centuries. 9/10.

## THREE SMALLEST FIXES
* Line 6: "fear not to take unto thee Mary thy wife" -> "do not be afraid to take Mary as your wife"
* Line 7: "thou shalt call his name" -> "you shall name him"
* Line 10: "I am with you alway." -> "I am with you always."
