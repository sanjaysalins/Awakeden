### FATAL FLAGS
*   **I4:** Misses the mandate to land on Jesus. The insight is entirely about Ahaz and Hezekiah, making the New Testament connection an unearned, tacked-on parenthesis rather than the climax.
*   **I8:** Doctrinal overclaim of fulfillment. Connecting "smoking firebrands" to John 16:33 just because both involve "overcoming" is thematic wordplay, not a real prophetic link.
*   **I10:** Factual overreach. It states an extra-biblical inference ("checking the water for a siege") as fact and aggressively mashes together three distinct water sources (Upper Pool, Shiloah, Siloam) to force a muddy typology.

### TOP 3
1.  **I1 - Two sons of David, two "fear not"s**
    It brilliantly contrasts the royal, fearful king who refused God's sign with the humble, obedient carpenter who received it, tying the Old and New Testaments together through exact family lineage.
2.  **I5 - Ahaz begged the wrong "father" to save him**
    It exposes the dark irony of a king paying a foreign empire to "save" him, creating a perfect, cinematic foil for Jesus, the true Son given by God to actually save His people.
3.  **I7 - Ask for any sign, from the depth to the height. God gave a baby.**
    It is profoundly simple, universally understood, and requires zero historical background for a newcomer to grasp the stunning humility of God's sign. 

### COMBINE?
**Yes: I1 and I5.**
They fit together seamlessly. Ahaz acts out of terror, pays a pagan king with temple gold saying "I am thy son, save me," and ignores God's "fear not." Centuries later, a poor carpenter—another son of David—hears the exact same "fear not," obeys, and welcomes the true Son who actually saves.

### HOOK
God gave the exact same command to two sons of David.
