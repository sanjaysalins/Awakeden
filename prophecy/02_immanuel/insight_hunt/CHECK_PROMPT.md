# Insight pool - Isaiah 7:14 "Immanuel" (fulfilled Matthew 1:23)

Context: 60-second Bible explainer video, global audience (many new to the Bible, many English-as-second-language). Each video shows an Old Testament promise kept in Jesus. The usual line "Immanuel means God with us" is over-told. We want insights most viewers have never heard, but that are plainly true in the text and sound in doctrine. All KJV quotes below are checked word for word against the KJV text attached at the end.

## I1 - Two sons of David, two "fear not"s
Isaiah's message to the frightened royal family (Isaiah 7:2 "the house of David... his heart was moved... as the trees of the wood are moved with the wind"; 7:4 "fear not"). King Ahaz is offered any sign (7:11) and says "I will not ask" (7:12). The sign is then given to "O house of David" (7:13-14). In Matthew, Ahaz ("Achaz") is named in Jesus' family list (Matthew 1:9). Then the angel says "Joseph, thou son of David, fear not" (1:20), and Joseph "did as the angel of the Lord had bidden him" (1:24). One son of David refused; another obeyed, and the sign came true in his home.

## I2 - The name comes back in the flood: "thy land, O Immanuel"
After 7:14, the name appears again when Assyria floods Judah "even to the neck" and Isaiah calls the land "thy land, O Immanuel" (8:7-8). Then the name becomes a defiant sentence against the nations: "speak the word, and it shall not stand: for God is with us" (8:9-10). "God with us" is first said in the worst moment, not a cosy one. Matthew opens with "God with us" (1:23) and Jesus' last words are "I am with you alway" (28:20).

## I3 - Isaiah's "I and the children" is put in Jesus' mouth
Isaiah's sons carry message-names (7:3, 8:3). Isaiah says "Behold, I and the children whom the LORD hath given me are for signs" (8:18). Hebrews 2:13 quotes this as Jesus speaking: "Behold I and the children which God hath given me", and 2:14 explains: because the children are flesh and blood, "he also himself likewise took part of the same; that through death he might destroy him that had the power of death".

## I4 - Same spot, two kings
Isaiah gave Ahaz the sign "at the end of the conduit of the upper pool in the highway of the fuller's field" (7:3). Years later the Assyrian envoy stood "by the conduit of the upper pool in the highway of the fuller's field" (36:2; also 2 Kings 18:17) to mock God before Ahaz's son Hezekiah. Ahaz would not trust God there; Hezekiah did, and Isaiah told him "Be not afraid" (37:6). Same place, two answers. (Lands on Jesus via the sign Ahaz refused, Matthew 1:23.)

## I5 - Ahaz begged the wrong "father" to save him
In the same crisis (2 Kings 16:5 = Isaiah 7:1), Ahaz sent to Assyria: "I am thy servant and thy son: come up, and save me" (2 Kings 16:7), and paid with "the silver and gold that was found in the house of the LORD" (16:8). Result: "distressed him, but strengthened him not... he helped him not" (2 Chronicles 28:20-21). The Son God gave instead: "thou shalt call his name JESUS: for he shall save his people from their sins" (Matthew 1:21).

## I6 - Light where an empire took families away
Assyria took "Galilee, all the land of Naphtali, and carried them captive" (2 Kings 15:29). In the same Isaiah passage as Immanuel, Isaiah promises that very region light: "Galilee of the nations. The people that walked in darkness have seen a great light" (Isaiah 9:1-2). Matthew says Jesus went to live there to fulfil it (Matthew 4:13-16).

## I7 - Ask for any sign, from the depth to the height. God gave a baby.
"Ask it either in the depth, or in the height above" (Isaiah 7:11). The king refused; God chose the sign: a child (7:14). Centuries later the angel tells shepherds "this shall be a sign unto you; Ye shall find the babe... lying in a manger" (Luke 2:12). Twice the sign is a baby.

## I8 - The terrifying kings are "two tails of these smoking firebrands"
Two invading kings make Jerusalem shake like trees in wind (7:2). God calls them "two tails of these smoking firebrands" (7:4): the burnt-out ends of sticks, all smoke. (Lands on Jesus thematically: John 16:33 "I have overcome the world".)

## I9 - A stumbling stone stands right beside Immanuel
A few verses after "God is with us", Isaiah says the LORD "shall be for a sanctuary; but for a stone of stumbling and for a rock of offence" (8:14). Peter applies those words to Jesus (1 Peter 2:7-8). The same presence is shelter to some and a stumbling stone to others.

## I10 - The sign was given at the city's water supply
Ahaz met Isaiah at the conduit of the upper pool (7:3), likely checking the water for a siege. The people "refuseth the waters of Shiloah that go softly" (8:6). Jesus later sends a blind man to "the pool of Siloam" (John 9:7) and says "If any man thirst, let him come unto me" (John 7:37). (NOTE: "checking the water for a siege" is commentary, not text.)
