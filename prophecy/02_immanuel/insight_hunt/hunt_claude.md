# Isaiah 7:14 "Immanuel": 5 insights from the text

---

## 1. Two sons of David, both afraid, both told "fear not": one refused, one obeyed

**VERSES**
- Isaiah 7:2: "And it was told the house of David, saying, Syria is confederate with Ephraim. And his heart was moved, and the heart of his people, as the trees of the wood are moved with the wind."
- Isaiah 7:4: "Take heed, and be quiet; fear not, neither be fainthearted"
- Isaiah 7:12: "But Ahaz said, I will not ask, neither will I tempt the LORD."
- Isaiah 7:13-14: "Hear ye now, O house of David... Therefore the Lord himself shall give you a sign"

**WHY IT MATTERS:** The sign was first given to a scared king of David's family who would not trust God. He shook "as the trees of the wood are moved with the wind." It is a real scene with a real person and real fear. It is not just a far-off prediction.

**LANDS ON JESUS:** Matthew 1:20: "Joseph, thou son of David, fear not to take unto thee Mary thy wife." Matthew 1:24: "Then Joseph being raised from sleep did as the angel of the Lord had bidden him." A second son of David hears the same "fear not." This one obeys, and the sign comes true in his own home.

**HOW KNOWN:** 2. Commentators note that "house of David" links the two passages. The pairing of "fear not" with "fear not" is almost never preached.

**RISK:** Low. Every word is in the text. Don't claim Matthew wrote it on purpose as a contrast with Ahaz. Just show the two scenes side by side.

**HOOK LINE:** "This promise was first given to a king who said no."

---

## 2. Isaiah calls the land "thy land, O Immanuel," before the child is even born

**VERSES**
- Isaiah 8:8: "...he shall reach even to the neck; and the stretching out of his wings shall fill the breadth of thy land, O Immanuel."
- Isaiah 8:10: "Take counsel together, and it shall come to nought; speak the word, and it shall not stand: for God is with us."

**WHY IT MATTERS:** An enemy flood (Assyria) is rising "even to the neck." In the middle of it, Isaiah speaks to Immanuel as the owner of the land. The child is not yet born, but the land is already his. Then the people shout his name as a battle cry: "for God is with us."

**LANDS ON JESUS:** Matthew 1:23 gives the name to Jesus. The country that was flooded and threatened turns out to belong to him. That is why he later walks it as its rightful King (Matthew 21:5: "Behold, thy King cometh unto thee").

**HOW KNOWN:** 2. Scholars know it. Almost no viewers or churchgoers have heard 8:8.

**RISK:** "His wings" in 8:8 most likely means the Assyrian army spreading out, not Immanuel's wings. Don't say Immanuel's wings cover the land. On the Hebrew: "God is with us" in 8:10 is the same phrase as the name "Immanuel." That is solid.

**HOOK LINE:** "Isaiah named a country after a baby not yet born."

---

## 3. Matthew opens with "God with us" and closes with "I am with you"

**VERSES**
- Matthew 1:23: "...and they shall call his name Emmanuel, which being interpreted is, God with us."
- Matthew 28:20: "...and, lo, I am with you alway, even unto the end of the world. Amen."
- Compare Isaiah 7:14: "...and shall call his name Immanuel."

**WHY IT MATTERS:** The first promise in Matthew's book is "God with us." Jesus's very last words in the same book are "I am with you alway." The name in chapter 1 becomes a spoken promise from Jesus himself. Also notice the two names: Joseph is told "thou shalt call his name JESUS" (1:21), but "**they** shall call his name Emmanuel." Jesus is his given name. Immanuel is what people would come to call him.

**LANDS ON JESUS:** Directly. Jesus himself says the Immanuel words on the last page.

**HOW KNOWN:** 4 for the bookend: scholars and some preachers use it. 1-2 for the "they shall call" detail.

**RISK:** "They shall call" is the plain KJV wording, and Matthew's Greek also differs from the Greek Old Testament here. That is a solid fact. Keep it light, though, and don't build doctrine on one pronoun.

**HOOK LINE:** "The first promise in Matthew is also Jesus's last word."

---

## 4. Isaiah's own sentence about "the children" is spoken by Jesus in Hebrews

**VERSES**
- Isaiah 8:18: "Behold, I and the children whom the LORD hath given me are for signs and for wonders in Israel from the LORD of hosts, which dwelleth in mount Zion."
- Isaiah 8:17: "...I will wait upon the LORD... and I will look for him."

**WHY IT MATTERS:** Right after the Immanuel promise, Isaiah points to his own sons as living signs. Their names were messages (7:3, "Shearjashub thy son"; 8:3, "Mahershalalhashbaz"). The whole family was a sign of the child to come.

**LANDS ON JESUS:** Hebrews 2:13: "And again, I will put my trust in him. And again, Behold I and the children which God hath given me." Hebrews puts Isaiah's words in Jesus's mouth. Jesus stands with the people God gave him as his family. Hebrews 2:11: "he is not ashamed to call them brethren."

**HOW KNOWN:** 1-2. Very few people have heard that Hebrews quotes Isaiah 8.

**RISK:** Low, because the New Testament makes the link itself. Don't use the sons' meaning-names as the main point. Explain them simply if you show them at all.

**HOOK LINE:** "Jesus quoted a sentence Isaiah said about his own kids."

---

## 5. The Immanuel promise doesn't stop at Christmas. It runs on to Galilee

**VERSES**
- Isaiah 9:1-2: "...the land of Zebulun and the land of Naphtali... by the way of the sea, beyond Jordan, in Galilee of the nations. The people that walked in darkness have seen a great light"
- Isaiah 9:6: "For unto us a child is born, unto us a son is given... The mighty God"

**WHY IT MATTERS:** Isaiah 7 to 9 is one unbroken run of words. The child promised in 7:14 is the child of 9:6, called "The mighty God." His light shines first in Galilee, the forgotten northern edge of the land that was hit first.

**LANDS ON JESUS:** Matthew quotes this same section a second time. Matthew 4:15-16: "The land of Zabulon, and the land of Nephthalim, by the way of the sea, beyond Jordan, Galilee of the Gentiles; The people which sat in darkness saw great light." This is where Jesus starts preaching (4:17).

**HOW KNOWN:** 9:6 alone is a 9 (Handel, Christmas cards). The link "Matthew quotes Isaiah 7 in chapter 1, then the next part of the same prophecy in chapter 4" is about a 3.

**RISK:** I'm fairly sure of the Matthew 4:15-16 KJV wording, but check "Zabulon/Nephthalim" against a KJV text. Don't claim all of 7-12 is one single prophecy. Just say it is one connected passage.

**HOOK LINE:** "The Christmas promise had a second half: a map."

---

**My pick: #1.** It's a story with two men, one phrase ("fear not") and one choice, and every word of it is in the text.
