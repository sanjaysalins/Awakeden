## FATAL FLAGS

No insight has a plain doctrinal error. No insight is fatal. But these need care:

- **I10 - weakest, close to fatal.** "Checking the water for a siege" is commentary, not text. The jump from Shiloah (8:6) to John 9:7 and John 7:37 is our own link. No New Testament writer makes it. Do not state it as fact.
- **I2 - one wording fix.** "Jesus' last words" must be "the last words of Jesus in Matthew". He speaks later in Acts 1. Also, the name is first given in 7:14, so say "the name comes back in the worst moment", not "is first said".
- **I6 - one wording fix.** Isaiah 9 is not "the same passage" as 7:14. It is two chapters on, in the same section. Say "a little later in the same book". Also it is really a different prophecy, with its own fulfilment verse. It is a strong video, but not an Immanuel video.
- **I5 - framing is ours.** The word "father" is not in the text. Ahaz says "thy servant and thy son". "The Son God gave instead" is our contrast. Matthew does not make it. Say it as a contrast, not as a fulfilment.
- **I7 - do not overclaim.** Luke 2:12 does not quote Isaiah 7. "Twice the sign is a baby" is a fair observation. "Luke fulfils Isaiah 7:11" would be an overclaim.
- **I4 - Jesus link is thin.** It lands on Hezekiah, not Jesus. "Ahaz would not trust God there" is a fair reading, but the refusal in 7:12 is stated, the place of it is implied.
- **I1 - two small cautions.** (a) Ahaz's "I will not tempt the LORD" sounds holy. The film must show 7:13 so the viewer sees God counts it as a refusal. (b) Say Joseph "took Mary as his wife", never that Joseph was the child's father.
- **I8 - no error, but no real New Testament link.** John 16:33 is a theme only.

## TOP 3 (best first)

1. **I1 - Two sons of David.** It is all in the text, it has two men and one clear choice each, and Matthew himself puts "son of David", "fear not" and the Immanuel quote side by side.
2. **I5 - "I am thy son... save me".** A king sells the temple gold to buy a saviour who does not help, and then God names a child "he shall save" - very easy to picture, as long as the contrast is not called a fulfilment.
3. **I2 - "thy land, O Immanuel".** Almost nobody knows the name comes back when the flood is at the neck, and Matthew's first "God with us" and last "I am with you alway" give it a real ending on Jesus.

Close fourth: I6. Best as its own episode (Galilee), not this one.
Best deep cut for Bible readers: I3. Too hard for a newcomer in 60 seconds.

## COMBINE?

Yes. **I1 + I5.**

Why they fit: both end in the same two verses. Matthew 1:20 = "son of David, fear not". Matthew 1:21 = "he shall save".

One spine:
- Ahaz, son of David, is afraid. God says "fear not" and offers any sign.
- Ahaz says no. He writes to Assyria: "I am thy son... save me."
- God gives the sign anyway: a son, Immanuel.
- Years later: "Joseph, thou son of David, fear not." Joseph says yes.
- The son's name: JESUS - "he shall save".

To avoid overload: use only ONE line from 2 Kings (16:7). Drop the gold, drop 2 Chronicles, drop the names of the two invading kings.

Do not add a third insight. I1 + I7 adds a second Gospel (Luke) and blurs the ending.
