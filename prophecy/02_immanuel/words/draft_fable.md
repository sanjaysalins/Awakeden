## (a) Script

Spine: a name that is a sentence ("God with us").

[narrator] About seven hundred years before His birth, a baby was promised a name. That name is a whole sentence. Isaiah, a prophet — a man who spoke God's message — gave a sign: a promise to watch for.

[prophet] "Behold, a virgin shall conceive, and bear a son, and shall call his name Immanuel."

[narrator] A virgin — a woman who has never been with a man — will have a son. (pause) Centuries later, Matthew writes: it came true. Mary, a virgin, had a son by God's Holy Spirit: Jesus. Immanuel means: "God with us." With us as a baby. With us in death: He took the blame for our sins and died on a cross. On the third day He rose from the dead. His last words in that book:

[jesus] "I am with you alway, even unto the end of the world."

[narrator] Still true.

## (b) Count and length

138 spoken words. About 53 s of speech at 2.6 words a second, plus the pause: about 54 s. Voice ends before 56 s.

## (c) KJV fragments

- "Behold, a virgin shall conceive, and bear a son, and shall call his name Immanuel." — Isaiah 7:14
- "God with us." — Matthew 1:23
- "I am with you alway, even unto the end of the world." — Matthew 28:20
- Not quoted, but leaned on: Matthew 1:18 and 1:22 (child of the Holy Ghost; "that it might be fulfilled"); 1 Corinthians 15:3-4 (died for our sins, rose the third day).

## (d) Four lines

- Who it is for: anyone on a phone, anywhere, with no Bible knowledge; and Bible readers who know the Christmas story but have not seen the name follow Jesus to the last line of Matthew.
- Main take-away: the promised name "God with us" came true in Jesus — at His birth, at the cross, after He rose, and still.
- Curiosity gap: "a name that is a whole sentence" (0-6 s). First pay-off at "Immanuel means: God with us" (about 33 s); second pay-off when Jesus says the sentence Himself, "I am with you alway" (about 50 s).
- Why a global newcomer gets it: one idea only (a name that says something), short everyday words, "prophet", "sign" and "virgin" each explained once in plain English, the promise and the "it came true" both said out loud, and the cross and rising said plainly.
