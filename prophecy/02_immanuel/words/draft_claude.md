**(a) Script**

[narrator] A scared king could ask God for any sign. He refused.
[narrator] That was Ahaz, about 700 years before Jesus. The prophet Isaiah, God's messenger, told him:
[prophet] "Ask thee a sign of the LORD thy God."
[ahaz] "I will not ask."
[narrator] God gave a sign anyway, a promise to watch for:
[prophet] "Behold, a virgin shall conceive, and bear a son, and shall call his name Immanuel."
[narrator] A girl who'd never been with a man would bear a son, named God with us.
[narrator] (pause) An angel told Joseph his bride-to-be, Mary, was expecting by God's Holy Spirit, not by any man:
[angel] "Thou shalt call his name JESUS: for he shall save his people from their sins."
[narrator] Matthew says: the promise came true.
[narrator] Ahaz would not ask. God gave the sign anyway: Jesus, who died for our sins, and on the third day rose from the dead.

**(b) Length**

- 140 spoken words. At 2.6 words a second that is about 53.8 s, plus about 0.6 s for the pause, so about 54.5 s.
- The voice ends before 56 s, but only just. If the read runs slow, cut "a promise to watch for" first.

**(c) KJV fragments**

- "Ask thee a sign of the LORD thy God" (Isaiah 7:11)
- "I will not ask" (Isaiah 7:12)
- "Behold, a virgin shall conceive, and bear a son, and shall call his name Immanuel." (Isaiah 7:14)
- "thou shalt call his name JESUS: for he shall save his people from their sins." (Matthew 1:21)
- Paraphrased, not quoted:
  - Holy Spirit, not by any man (Matthew 1:18, 20)
  - the promise came true (Matthew 1:22–23)
  - God with us (Matthew 1:23)
  - died for our sins, and on the third day rose from the dead (1 Corinthians 15:3–4)

**(d) Four questions**

1. **Who it is for:** Newcomers anywhere, often on a phone with captions on. Also Bible readers who think they already know the Christmas story.
2. **Main take-away:** A king refused to ask God for a sign. God gave one anyway, and the sign is Jesus, "God with us." He died for our sins and rose on the third day.
3. **Curiosity gap:** "A scared king could ask God for any sign. He refused." Why refuse, and what happened next? It pays off in two steps. First Isaiah's promise: God gives a sign anyway. Then the last line names the sign: Jesus.
4. **Why a global newcomer gets it:** It follows one person and one choice, with short plain words. Each old word is said again in plain English right after it: virgin, conceive, Immanuel. There is only one king, and the promise and its coming true are both said out loud.
