# Independent review — seat: Fable

## X

1. **NEWCOMER.** Who: a scared king (Ahaz), a prophet (Isaiah), Mary, Jesus. What: God offers the king a sign, he says no, God promises a virgin will have a son called "God with us"; 700 years later Mary has Jesus. Why: God gives even when people will not ask. Lines I would not understand:
   - "shall call his name Immanuel" then "Her son was Jesus." Two names, never joined. Is this the same child?
   - "A king would not ask. God gave anyway. Jesus died for our sins." The jump to the cross has no bridge.
   - Why did the king say no? Why was he frightened? Never told.
2. **HOOK.** Good: a real story question (why refuse God?). But it is never answered; the film only repeats it. **6/10**
3. **PROMISE.** Who, what, when and "came true" are all clear, and the verse is spoken aloud. "God with us" is told but not felt before the end. **7/10**
4. **HARD WORDS.** "Ahaz", "Ask thee a sign", "thy", "Behold", "conceive", "Immanuel", "expecting a child" (idiom), "alway" (captions will write "always" or look like a typo). "Sign" alone may read as a road sign until "a promise" arrives.
5. **BIBLE.** Nothing wrong. "Matthew says the promise came true" is honestly attributed. Mild: it puts Ahaz on screen but stays silent on the sign's meaning for his own day (7:15-16), so a Bible reader notices the gap more here than in Y. "He said no" drops Ahaz's reason ("neither will I tempt the LORD"), fair as a summary.
6. **LANDING.** Lands on Jesus, no pressure. But rushed: cross and rising feel bolted on, and "I am with you alway" is cut short, so the echo of "God with us" is weaker. **6/10**
7. **Overall: 6/10**

## Y

1. **NEWCOMER.** Who: Isaiah, Mary, Joseph, an angel, Jesus. What: a promised baby whose name means "God with us"; Jesus is that baby. Why: God is with us, as a baby, at the cross, and still now. Lines I would stumble on:
   - "thou shalt call his name JESUS" right after "call his name Immanuel". Which name is the "whole sentence"? It clears up one line later, but only just.
   - "gave a sign" — to whom? Nobody is named.
2. **HOOK.** Short, odd, makes me want the answer. Answered clearly ("Immanuel means God with us") and paid off three times. **8/10**
3. **PROMISE.** Who, what, 700 years, came true: all there, and heard in the prophet's voice. The meaning is unpacked better than X. **8/10**
4. **HARD WORDS.** "Behold", "conceive", "Immanuel", "thou shalt", "expecting a child", "alway", "even unto the end of the world". Fewer names than X (no Ahaz).
5. **BIBLE.** Nothing wrong. "His last words in Matthew" is correct (28:20). "One baby's name is a whole sentence" is fair (Isaiah 8:10, "God is with us"). No overclaim on Isaiah 7; it simply leaves Ahaz's day out. "With us at the cross" is a reading, not a quote, but sound.
6. **LANDING.** Earned. "With us" builds baby, cross, risen, now. Full verse quoted. "Still true." is grace, no pressure. **9/10**
7. **Overall: 8/10**

Timing note: Y is about 142 words plus the pause, roughly 55.5 s. Almost no spare room.

## PICK: Y

One thread (the name) runs hook to last line, and the end is earned; X has a stronger first image but two loose ends.

**One small change** (join the two names). Replace

> Matthew says the promise came true. Immanuel means God with us.

with

> Matthew says Jesus is that promised son. Immanuel means God with us.
