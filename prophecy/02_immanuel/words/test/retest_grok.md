1. NEWCOMER: God sent a message by a man named Isaiah: a virgin would have a son called Immanuel. Mary, who had never been with a man, was pregnant by God's Spirit. An angel told Joseph to name the baby Jesus, because he would save people from their sins. Matthew's book says this is that old promise kept. Immanuel means "God with us." Jesus was born, died for sins, rose on the third day, and said he is with you always. That is still true.

Lines I would not get: "This baby's name is not a word. It is a sentence." (Which baby? "Sentence" can mean jail.) "Behold, a virgin shall conceive." "thou shalt." "I am with you alway" ("alway" sounds like a mistake). "Holy Spirit." "the cross," though "where He died" helps.

2. HOOK: 6. If a baby is already on screen, the riddle can hold me, and the film does answer it: "Immanuel is the sentence: God with us." On a phone, with no Bible, "sentence" sounds like punishment, and the first line gives no person and no stake.

3. PROMISE: 7. It is heard. God, through Isaiah, said a virgin would bear a son named Immanuel, about seven hundred years before Jesus. Mary had the child. The angel says the name Jesus means he will save his people from their sins. Matthew says the promise came true: God with us. I could retell that from sound. Jesus and Immanuel arrive so fast I might mix up which name was the promise.

4. HARD WORDS: "sentence," "Behold," "conceive," "thou shalt," "alway," "been with a man" (odd, and vague in captions), "Holy Spirit," "the cross." "Immanuel" is hard, then they translate it.

5. BIBLE: Overclaimed: "About seven hundred years before Jesus, God gave a promise through the prophet Isaiah." In the KJV this is a sign to King Ahaz and the house of David. Isaiah 7:16 puts it in that same war: before the child knows to refuse the evil and choose the good, the two enemy kings are gone. "Seven hundred years" is not in these verses. Matthew 1:22-23 does say the virgin birth fulfilled the prophet, and that Emmanuel means "God with us." That part is fair. "With us at the cross" is a sermon step. "Died for our sins" and "the third day" match 1 Corinthians 15:3-4. The Isaiah line and the angel line match the KJV. Joseph was already her pledged husband ("Mary thy wife"), which is more than "the man she would marry."

6. LANDING: 8. It ends on Jesus: "I am with you alway." Then "Still true." Birth, death, and rising earn a present word. No "you must." "Still true" is a little thin. The verse's "even unto the end of the world" is the part that would lock it.

7. Overall: 6. A newcomer who stays can get who, what, and why. Spoken old English, and the missing Ahaz clock, keep this from being the careful cut.

8. ONE small change. Replace "About seven hundred years before Jesus, God gave a promise through the prophet Isaiah, a man who spoke God's message:" with "In King Ahaz's own day, God gave a sign through the prophet Isaiah, a man who spoke God's message:" Leave the later Matthew line as it is.

VERDICT: REVISE — put the sign back in King Ahaz's day before these words lock.
