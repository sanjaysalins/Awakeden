1. NEWCOMER: God promised a baby 700 years in advance. A virgin named Mary had this baby by God's Spirit. He was named Jesus to save people from sins, but also represents the name "Immanuel." He died, rose, and is still with us. One point of confusion: the prophet says "call his name Immanuel," but the angel says "call his name JESUS." A newcomer might wonder, "Which name is it?"

2. HOOK: "This baby's name is not a word. It is a sentence." It creates immediate curiosity and is directly answered in the middle when it reveals, "Immanuel is the sentence: God with us." 9/10.

3. PROMISE: God promised through Isaiah 700 years prior that a virgin would have a son. It came true in Mary and Jesus. The timeline, the promiser, and the fulfillment are explicitly heard in the script. 9/10.

4. HARD WORDS: "alway" (from the KJV "I am with you alway"). Auto-captions and ESL viewers will likely read or hear this as a grammatical error for "always" or miscaption it as two words ("all way"). 

5. BIBLE: The claims are grounded and accurate to the KJV text. The script applies Matthew's messianic fulfillment of Isaiah 7:14 while safely bypassing the immediate historical sign to King Ahaz in Isaiah 7:15-16. It avoids falsely claiming the prophecy *only* applied to Jesus, stating simply that "the promise came true." No overclaims.

6. LANDING: The ending elegantly connects the "God with us" translation of Immanuel to Jesus's presence at the cross, the resurrection, and today. It is entirely grace-based, anchored on His work, with zero manufactured pressure. 10/10.

7. Overall 9/10.

8. ONE small change: To help newcomers bridge the gap between the two distinct names, change "Matthew's book about Jesus says the promise came true." to: "Matthew's book says Jesus is how that promise came true."
