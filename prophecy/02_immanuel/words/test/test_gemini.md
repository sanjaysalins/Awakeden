**X**
1. NEWCOMER: A scared king refuses a sign, so God gives a promise anyway: a virgin will have a son named "God with us." 700 years later, Mary has Jesus, who dies, rises, and stays with us. I would not understand "Matthew says"—who is Matthew? 
2. HOOK: "A frightened king was told he could ask God for any sign. He said no." Excellent narrative hook that creates instant curiosity. The question is answered perfectly by the end. 9/10.
3. PROMISE: God promises Ahaz 700 years prior. The promise is clearly heard spoken aloud by the prophet. 9/10.
4. HARD WORDS: "Ask thee", "alway" (archaic, might trip up auto-captions or ESL speakers).
5. BIBLE: Accurate to the KJV. Ahaz's refusal (Isaiah 7:12) and the resulting prophecy are correctly applied.
6. LANDING: "I am with you alway" perfectly pays off the "God with us" translation of Immanuel. It is earned, graceful, and pressure-free. 8/10.
7. Overall 8/10.

**Y**
1. NEWCOMER: A baby's name is a sentence. A prophet promises a virgin birth. An angel tells Joseph to name the baby Jesus. The name means "God with us," which remains true after he dies and rises. I would be confused by the naming: "thou shalt call his name JESUS" right after the prophet said "shall call his name Immanuel."
2. HOOK: "One baby's name is a whole sentence." This feels like trivia rather than a compelling story. 5/10.
3. PROMISE: Isaiah gives the promise 700 years before, and it is heard aloud. However, the connection between the promise and the fulfillment is slightly disjointed. 7/10.
4. HARD WORDS: "thou shalt", "alway, even unto". 
5. BIBLE: Accurate to the KJV, but it completely strips out the historical context of Ahaz that prompted the sign in the first place.
6. LANDING: "Still true." is a fantastic, punchy final thought that lands the grace message beautifully. 9/10.
7. Overall 7/10.

**PICK X**
X has a much stronger narrative spine; the king's stubborn refusal makes God's determination to give the promise anyway feel powerful. 

**CHANGE**
In X, change "Matthew says the promise came true." to "The Bible says the promise came true." (Removes the unexplained name for newcomers).
