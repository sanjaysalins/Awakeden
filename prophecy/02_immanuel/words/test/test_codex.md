X

1. NEWCOMER: A scared king refuses God's offer. God promises a miraculous baby; Mary later has Jesus. Jesus dies for sins, rises, and promises his presence. Why the king refuses remains unexplained. “Matthew says the promise came true”—who is Matthew?

2. HOOK: 8/10. The refusal creates curiosity. “God gave anyway” answers what happened, though not why the king refused.

3. PROMISE: 9/10. God promises through Isaiah, about seven hundred years before Jesus, that a virgin will bear Immanuel. Mary's pregnancy fulfils it. The promise is clearly heard.

4. HARD WORDS: “Ahaz,” “Isaiah,” “thee,” “thy,” “Behold,” “conceive,” “Immanuel,” “alway.” “Never been with a man” relies on understanding a euphemism.

5. BIBLE: “Named God with us” risks making the translation sound like Jesus' personal name; Matthew distinguishes Jesus from the designation Emmanuel. The presentation omits the sign's relevance to Ahaz's own day in Isaiah 7:15–16. That omission is not itself a contradiction.

6. LANDING: 6/10. Gentle and centred on Jesus, but the jump from the king's refusal to Jesus' death feels compressed. The shortened final quotation has less weight.

7. Overall: 7/10. Approximately 54.5 seconds including the stated pause; little room for additional dramatic pauses.

Y

1. NEWCOMER: God speaks through Isaiah about a virgin's son. Mary has Jesus, whose promised designation means God with us. His death and resurrection lead to his continuing presence. “An angel told Joseph”—who is Joseph? Matthew is also unidentified.

2. HOOK: 7/10. An intriguing word puzzle, though less urgent than X. The explanation of Immanuel answers it, but the introduction of JESUS creates temporary confusion.

3. PROMISE: 8/10. The virgin birth is promised about seven hundred years earlier and fulfilled through Mary. It is heard clearly. God's role as the promise's source could be more explicit.

4. HARD WORDS: “Conceive,” “Immanuel,” “thou shalt,” “sins,” “alway,” “unto.” The long final quotation challenges listening and caption reading.

5. BIBLE: “Isaiah … gave a sign” blurs the giver: Isaiah says “the Lord himself” gives it. No clear contradiction otherwise. Like X, it leaves out the sign's Ahaz-era horizon without explicitly denying it.

6. LANDING: 9/10. “God with us” connects birth, cross, and Jesus' closing promise. “Still true” lands gently, without pressure.

7. Overall: 8/10. Approximately 55.6 seconds including the pause; almost no delivery margin.

PICK Y: Its central meaning carries through to Jesus' ending.
