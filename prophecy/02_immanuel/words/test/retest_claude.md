**Independent review: "Promised, then kept", episode 2 (Immanuel)**

**1. NEWCOMER**
- **Who:** A baby called Jesus, his mother Mary, and Joseph.
- **What:** A prophet promised a special baby about 700 years earlier. An angel named him.
- **Why:** His name means "God with us", and he still is.
- **Where I got lost:** There are two names and nothing links them. The angel says "call his name JESUS". Then I hear "Immanuel is the sentence". So which is the baby's name? A newcomer will ask, "Was he called Jesus or Immanuel?" This is the biggest gap.

**2. HOOK: 7/10**
"Not a word. It is a sentence" is a good puzzle. But the answer ("God with us") only comes at about 35 seconds, after two KJV quotes. That is a long wait. It does pay off.

**3. PROMISE: 8/10**
You hear clearly that God promised, through Isaiah, about 700 years before Jesus. You also hear "a virgin… a son… Immanuel". Mary, the virgin, fits that, and Matthew says it came true. It all comes through the ear, not just the screen. The one weak spot is Jesus vs Immanuel again.

**4. HARD WORDS**
- "Behold", "conceive", "bear a son": second-language viewers may miss these. The narrator line after it helps.
- "thou shalt": archaic, but short.
- "alway": the caption will look like a typo.
- "sentence": can mean a court punishment. "Not a word" mostly saves it.
- "Immanuel": captions may spell it "Emmanuel". Lock the spelling.

**5. BIBLE**
No real errors. "Matthew's book… says the promise came true" is honest. It credits Matthew and doesn't overclaim, which matters because Isaiah 7:15-16 also points to King Ahaz's own day. "About seven hundred years" is fine. Using "Holy Spirit" for KJV "Holy Ghost" in the narrator's voice is fine. "With us at the cross" is a fair reading, backed by 1 Corinthians 15:3-4.

**6. LANDING: 7/10**
It lands on Jesus, with grace and no pressure. "I am with you alway" earns "Still true." But "Still true" is a bit thin. It never turns to the viewer ("with you"). It is quiet, not flat.

**7. OVERALL: 7/10**
- Timing: about 138 words plus the 1-second pause comes to about 54 seconds. That fits the 56-second limit, but only just.

**8. ONE CHANGE**
Change: "Immanuel is the sentence: God with us."
To: **"Jesus is Immanuel. And Immanuel means: God with us."**
This adds 3 words (about 55 seconds total). It joins the two names, and "means" clears up the "sentence" confusion.
