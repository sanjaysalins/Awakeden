**X**

1. NEWCOMER: A scared king, Ahaz, may ask God for a sign and says no. God gives one anyway: a virgin will have a son called Immanuel, "God with us." About 700 years later Mary has Jesus by the Holy Spirit. Matthew says that kept the promise. Jesus died for sins, rose, and said he is with us always.
I would not understand: "Ask thee a sign of the LORD thy God." "Behold, a virgin shall conceive." "alway." Who Matthew is. Whether the boy's name is Immanuel, "God with us," or Jesus.

2. HOOK: 8. "A frightened king... He said no" would keep me. The end answers that refusal.

3. PROMISE: 7. God, through Isaiah, to Ahaz: a virgin's son named Immanuel, about 700 years before Jesus, kept in Mary. The verse is spoken aloud. "Named God with us," then "Her son was Jesus," leaves the name muddy.

4. HARD WORDS: "thee," "thy," "Behold," "conceive," "Holy Spirit," "Matthew," "alway," "expecting." On captions, "alway" looks like a typo.

5. BIBLE: Matthew 1:22-23 does say this birth kept the prophet's word. Fear fits Isaiah 7:2 and 7:4. "Any sign" is a fair short form of Isaiah 7:11. Death and the third day match 1 Corinthians 15:3-4.
Overclaim: "named God with us." Matthew 1:25 says he is called JESUS. Matthew 1:23 says Emmanuel means God with us.
Isaiah 7:16 also points to Ahaz's own day: before the child knows good from evil, the two kings' land is empty. X makes 700 years sound like the only clock.

6. LANDING: 6. The last voice is Jesus, and nobody is pushed. Death and rising arrive in a rush, after the king story is already finished.

7. Overall: 7. About 139 words plus one silent second. It can end by 56 seconds. The scene is clear. The name is not.

**Y**

1. NEWCOMER: A baby's name is a whole sentence. About 700 years before Jesus, Isaiah promises a virgin will bear Immanuel. Mary is that virgin, by the Holy Spirit. An angel tells Joseph to name him Jesus, because he will save people from their sins. Matthew says the promise came true. Immanuel means God with us: as a baby, at the cross, and after he rose, to the end of the world. Still true.
I would not understand: "One baby's name is a whole sentence." "Behold, a virgin shall conceive." "thou shalt call his name JESUS..." "His last words in Matthew" (Matthew is a stranger, and "last words" sounds like a deathbed). Who Joseph is.

2. HOOK: 6. The first line is a puzzle, easy to miss if English is a second language. Stay, and the end pays it: God with us, still true.

3. PROMISE: 8. I hear Isaiah, I hear the angel, then a plain line: "Immanuel means God with us." Mary is the virgin. Jesus is the son. About 700 years. I never learn who received the sign.

4. HARD WORDS: "whole sentence," "Behold," "conceive," "thou shalt," "Holy Spirit," "Joseph," "cross," "alway," "unto." The old English runs longer, so captions stay hard. "alway" still looks misspelled.

5. BIBLE: The angel's line matches Matthew 1:21. The meaning matches Matthew 1:23 (spelled Emmanuel there). "Came true" matches Matthew 1:22. The Jesus line matches Matthew 28:20 and is his last speech in that book. Death and the third day match 1 Corinthians 15:3-4. "Still true" fits "unto the end of the world."
Wrong giver: "Isaiah ... gave a sign." Isaiah 7:14 says the Lord himself shall give it.
"With us at the cross" is the film's own step past the gloss "God with us."
Same hole as X: Isaiah 7:16's near child and the two kings are missing, so 700 years sounds like the whole sign.

6. LANDING: 8. Baby, cross, rising, and "I am with you alway..." are one thread from the name. "Still true" asks for nothing. The close has grace.

7. Overall: 8. About 142 words plus the pause. Speak the old lines slowly and it can pass 56 seconds. This version says the point of the episode.

VERDICT
PICK: Y. The promise is spoken, the meaning is a plain sentence, and the ending grows out of the name.
