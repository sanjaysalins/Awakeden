1. NEWCOMER: I hear that God promised a virgin would have a son. Mary became pregnant through God’s Spirit. Her son, Jesus, would save people from sins. Matthew says this fulfilled the promise. Jesus died, rose, and promised his presence. I would wonder why the promised name is Immanuel but the angel says “JESUS.” “Save his people from their sins” leaves me unsure what sins are or who his people are.

2. HOOK: 7/10. The strange claim would make me listen for an explanation. “God with us” answers the intended puzzle, but “not a word” is misleading: a name can be a word with a longer meaning. “Sentence” makes the explanation sound like a grammar claim.

3. PROMISE: 9/10. God promised, through Isaiah, a virgin bearing a son called Immanuel, about seven hundred years before Jesus. Mary’s pregnancy and Matthew’s explanation supply the fulfillment. This is clearly heard. The relationship between the two names needs one explicit connection.

4. HARD WORDS: “Behold,” “shall conceive,” “thou shalt,” and “alway” challenge second-language listeners. “Alway” may become “always” in captions. “Isaiah” and “Immanuel” need caption checking. “Never been with a man” is a euphemism that could confuse literal listeners. “Holy Spirit” and “sins” introduce unexplained religious concepts.

5. BIBLE: The quotations and claims about Jesus’ death and resurrection agree with the supplied KJV. “God gave a promise” is supported by Matthew’s fulfillment statement. However, presenting Isaiah solely as a distant prediction would flatten its context: 7:15–16 also places the sign within Ahaz’s crisis. “Immanuel is the sentence” is your rhetorical description; the KJV gives its interpretation as “God with us.”

6. LANDING: 8/10. The repeated “With us” earns the final promise. Jesus remains central, and there is no demand or pressure. The move from birth through death and resurrection is fast.

7. Overall: 7/10. About 140 spoken words plus the pause takes roughly 55 seconds at the stated rate, leaving little room for voice transitions.

8. ONE small change: Replace “Immanuel is the sentence: God with us” with “Immanuel describes Jesus: God with us.”
