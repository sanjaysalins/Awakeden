**X**

1. **Newcomer:** A scared king named Ahaz refuses to ask God for a sign. God gives a sign anyway: a virgin will have a son. Matthew says that son is Jesus, who died, rose, and says "I am with you." The confusing part is the name. The script says he will be "named God with us", then "Her son was Jesus." Which name is it? That is never explained.
2. **Hook:** "He said no." is a strong human puzzle: why would anyone refuse God? It gets answered, and "A king would not ask. God gave anyway" closes it neatly. **8**
3. **Promise:** Who promised (God, through Isaiah), to whom (Ahaz), how long before (about 700 years), and what came true (the virgin's son) are all clear. Isaiah's voice is heard, and Ahaz's "I will not ask" makes the scene dramatic. **8**
4. **Hard words:** "Ask thee a sign," "Behold," "conceive," "alway" (captions may write "always"). Captions may also mangle "Ahaz" and "Immanuel."
5. **Bible:** "God gave anyway" makes the sign sound like pure kindness. In 7:13 it comes with a rebuke ("will ye weary my God also?"), and 7:15-16 points to Ahaz's own day. This is a mild spin, not an error. "Matthew says" is honest wording.
6. **Landing:** The echo of the king is nice. But the cross and resurrection arrive as a quick creed bolted on ("Jesus died for our sins. On the third day..."), and "with you" is not tied back to Immanuel. **6**
7. **Overall: 7**

**Y**

1. **Newcomer:** Isaiah promised that a virgin would have a son. 700 years later Mary did, and an angel said to call him Jesus because he saves. His title, Immanuel, means "God with us": as a baby, at the cross, risen, and still now. Unclear line: "One baby's name is a whole sentence." Which name? "A whole sentence" is abstract for second-language listeners.
2. **Hook:** It is intriguing but cerebral, with no person in danger. It is never paid off out loud, because nobody says "that name is the sentence." **6**
3. **Promise:** Isaiah's voice is heard and the 700 years is clear. But there is no Ahaz, so we lose "to whom" and "why." The angel line helps, because it resolves Jesus vs Immanuel. **7**
4. **Hard words:** "Behold," "conceive," "thou shalt," "alway," "unto the end of the world," "Immanuel" (captions may write "Emmanuel"), "a whole sentence."
5. **Bible:** Clean. Matthew 1:21 and 28:20 are quoted correctly. "His last words in Matthew" is accurate. "Matthew says" avoids overclaiming, though like X it skips 7:15-16.
6. **Landing:** "With us as a baby. With us at the cross... Still true." grows out of Immanuel, so it is earned. It is grace-led with no pressure. **8**
7. **Overall: 7.5**

**Timing:** By my count, X is about 137 words (about 54s with the pause) and Y about 141 words (about 55-56s). Both fit, but Y is tight.

**PICK: Y.** It answers "which name?", and its landing comes out of the promise itself rather than being added on.

**One change:** Pay off the hook. Replace "Matthew says the promise came true. Immanuel means God with us." with:
"Matthew says the promise came true. Immanuel is that sentence: God with us."

That costs one word. To stay inside 56s, also change "His last words in Matthew:" to "He said:".
