# Write ONE narration from scratch - "Immanuel" (Isaiah 7:14 -> Matthew 1:18-25). You are ONE blind writer; you have seen no other draft.

## The series
"Promised, then kept": an Old Testament promise, and how it came true in Jesus. Every episode lands on Jesus. This is episode 2 (episode 1 was Micah 5:2, Bethlehem). Bold comic-book look, orchestral score. Released before Christmas.

## Who it is for
Worldwide, many with English as a second language, many with no Bible knowledge, on a phone, often with captions on. Also Bible readers who think they know the Christmas story and need a reason to keep watching.

## Length (hard)
UNDER 60 SECONDS. 120-140 spoken words total, including quotes (the narrator speaks about 2.6 words a second). Voice must end by 56 s.

## The facts you may use (all from the KJV pack below; use only what you need)
- About 700 years before Jesus, King Ahaz of Judah was afraid: two kings were coming against him (Isaiah 7). God, through the prophet Isaiah, told Ahaz: "Ask thee a sign of the LORD thy God" (7:11). Ahaz said, "I will not ask" (7:12).
- God gave a sign anyway: "Therefore the Lord himself shall give you a sign; Behold, a virgin shall conceive, and bear a son, and shall call his name Immanuel." (Isaiah 7:14). It was spoken to "the house of David" (7:13).
- Isaiah keeps coming back to this child: "O Immanuel" (8:8), "for God is with us" (8:10), "For unto us a child is born, unto us a son is given ... The mighty God ... The Prince of Peace" (9:6).
- Mary was engaged to Joseph. Before they came together, "she was found with child of the Holy Ghost" (Matthew 1:18). Joseph, "a just man", planned to end it quietly (1:19). An angel in a dream told him not to fear: the child is "of the Holy Ghost", and "thou shalt call his name JESUS: for he shall save his people from their sins" (1:20-21).
- Matthew says: "all this was done, that it might be fulfilled which was spoken of the Lord by the prophet" and quotes Isaiah: "they shall call his name Emmanuel, which being interpreted is, God with us." (1:22-23). Joseph obeyed and "called his name JESUS" (1:24-25).
- Mary herself asked, "How shall this be, seeing I know not a man?" (Luke 1:34). The angel: "The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee ... that holy thing which shall be born of thee shall be called the Son of God." (1:35)
- "And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us" (John 1:14).
- The last words of Jesus in Matthew's book: "lo, I am with you alway, even unto the end of the world." (Matthew 28:20). Matthew's book opens with "God with us" and ends with "I am with you".
- Immanuel / Emmanuel: the same name (Hebrew / Greek spelling). It means "God with us".

## Pick ONE spine (your choice) and hold it from first line to last
Options (or your own, if it stays true to the text): the king who refused to ask for a sign, and God gave one anyway; a name that is a sentence ("God with us"); Joseph's impossible night; the virgin's question "How shall this be?"; the book that opens with "God with us" and closes with "I am with you". One spine only. Do not list facts.

## Lessons from episode 1 (a blind panel found these; do not repeat them)
1. Say the PROMISE out loud in plain words, and say out loud when it CAME TRUE. Do not leave it to on-screen text.
2. Never say the same key word twice in a row from two voices ("a ruler ... ruler in Israel"). No old grammar the ear cannot parse.
3. Say the cross and the rising in plain words: "He died for our sins" needs plain help, and "rose again the third day" confuses ("again"? third day after what?). Plain: "On the third day He rose from the dead."
4. Only one person called "king" if you can help it, or make it very clear who is who.
5. Do NOT reuse episode 1's landing ("Before anyone went to Him, He came to us") or its hook shape ("X knew. Did they go?"). Find this story's own.

## Hard rules
1. First 3 seconds: plain words that open a curiosity gap a stranger must close by watching.
2. Say simply who Isaiah was ("a prophet - a man who spoke God's message") and what a sign or a promise is, in plain words.
3. KJV quotes verbatim and short. Never let an old word ("conceive", "virgin", "Holy Ghost", "interpreted") carry the meaning alone; say it in plain English too.
4. Honest and careful. This prophecy had a sign for Ahaz in his own day too (Isaiah 7:15-16) and Bible readers debate the Hebrew word for "virgin". Do not argue about it on air, and do not overclaim ("no one else could", "statistically impossible", "the Jews missed it"). Stand where Matthew stands: Matthew says it was fulfilled in Jesus; Matthew and Luke both say Mary was a virgin and the child was of the Holy Ghost. No invented feelings, dates, numbers or details beyond the pack ("about 700 years" is allowed).
5. Never blame "the Jews" as a people. Ahaz is one king, not a nation.
6. The ending lands on Jesus, earned by your spine, and says plainly that He died for our sins and on the third day rose from the dead (1 Corinthians 15:3-4). Grace, not pressure.
7. No stock closer ("come to Jesus", "just ask Him", "He's waiting", "will you...?", "God is with you"). No fear. No gain/loss.
8. Voices: one line per voice, e.g. [narrator], [prophet] (Isaiah's words), [ahaz], [angel], [mary]. A quote spoken by a character gets that character's voice. Mark one (pause).
9. No wording about AI or how the film is made. No hashtags. Write for the ear.

## Output (only this)
(a) the script, one line per voice turn;
(b) spoken word count and estimated length in seconds;
(c) KJV fragments with verse refs;
(d) 4 lines: who it is for, main take-away, the curiosity gap (and where it pays off), why a global newcomer gets it.
