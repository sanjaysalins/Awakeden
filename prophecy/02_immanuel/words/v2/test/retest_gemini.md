1. NEWCOMER: This is about a scared king who refused God's help, and a later man named Joseph who accepted it. God promised a sign—a baby named Immanuel. Centuries later, that baby (Jesus) was born, died, and rose again to save people from their sins and stay with them forever. It matters because it shows God keeps His promise to be with us, even when human leaders fail. Words I might not understand: "conceive," "thou," "shalt," "unto thee."

2. BIBLE READER: Yes. Connecting the famous Christmas "Immanuel" prophecy directly to King Ahaz's political fear and stubborn refusal of God's sign. Most churchgoers only hear Isaiah 7:14 in isolation, not as God's response to a king begging a foreign nation for salvation. The direct contrast between Ahaz's disobedience and Joseph's obedience is a fresh, compelling angle.

3. CURIOSITY GAP: First line: 9/10. It's a fantastic hook that immediately sets up a conflict. Ending payoff: 8/10. "Still true" is a solid, punchy landing that pays off the "God with us" theme perfectly.

4. GUARDRAILS: Who is it for: Anyone feeling afraid or looking for rescue in the wrong places. Main take-away: Jesus is the true Savior who promises to be with us always. Global audience OK?: The narrative is simple, but the KJV quotes ("thou son of David, fear not to take unto thee...") are archaic and hard for ESL speakers to process at spoken speed. Biblically accurate: Yes. Ahaz's refusal (Isa 7:12), the prophecy (Isa 7:14), the angel's words (Matt 1:20-21), and Jesus' promise (Matt 28:20) are all perfectly grounded. No overclaims. One change to improve: The angel's quote is too long and archaic for a global audience; trim it to just the core command and the name of Jesus, or have the narrator paraphrase the marriage part.

5. OVERALL: 8/10.

Top 3 fixes:
1. Trim or frame the angel's KJV quote. "Fear not to take unto thee Mary thy wife" is too archaic for a fast-paced, global video. 
2. Highlight the "fear not" parallel. God told Ahaz "fear not" (he failed) and the angel told Joseph "fear not" (he obeyed). Pointing this out explicitly makes the contrast much stronger.
3. Smooth the 700-year jump. Add a few words to explain that God didn't forget His promise to David's family during that long wait.
