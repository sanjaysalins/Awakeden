# Blind judge: Fable seat

X and Y are the same script except for the first line. So most answers are shared; the hook is where they differ.

## VERSION X

**1. NEWCOMER**
- Who: a scared king named Ahaz, long ago. Then a baby named Jesus, born 700 years later.
- What: God told the king "do not be afraid" and offered proof. The king said no and asked another king to save him. God gave a proof anyway: a virgin will have a son called Immanuel. 700 years later Mary, a virgin, has a baby by God's Spirit. An angel tells Joseph to name him Jesus, because he will save people. Jesus died, rose again, and said he is with us.
- Why it matters: God did not stay far away. He came to be with people, and he still is.
- Words or lines I would not understand:
  - "offered any sign": a sign of what? I would not know "sign" means proof from God.
  - "Behold", "thou", "shalt", "conceive", "alway": old English. "Conceive" is the hardest, but the next line ("was pregnant") explains it.
  - "sins": not explained.
  - "from King David's family" / "thou son of David": I do not know David, so I miss that the two men are linked.
  - "This child saves." Saves from what? The angel line says "sins", so it mostly lands.
  - First line: I do not yet know this is about Jesus. That is fine; it is a story hook.

**2. BIBLE READER**
Yes. Most churchgoers know "Immanuel = God with us" from Christmas, but not the story behind it:
- The sign was given to a king who refused to ask.
- Ahaz said "save me" to Assyria; the angel says this child "shall save". Fresh, and all from the text.
- Both Ahaz and Joseph are "house/son of David" and both are told "fear not". One refuses, one obeys.
X's first line puts this fresh part up front.

**3. CURIOSITY GAP**
- First line: **8/10**. A king saying no to God is a real story question: what did God answer?
- Payoff: **7/10**. "God chose the sign" answers it fast, and "Ahaz begged a king... This child saves" closes the loop. The last lines ("with us... Still true") pay off Immanuel more than they pay off the "no".

**4. GUARDRAILS**
- Who for: anyone, worldwide; works for newcomers and gives churchgoers something new.
- Take-away: God promised to be with us, came as Jesus to save us, and is still with us.
- Global audience: mostly OK. Short sentences. The KJV quotes are the hard part, but each is short and the narrator explains the key ones.
- Biblical accuracy:
  - Isaiah 7:14 quote: exact.
  - Matthew 1:21 quote: exact.
  - "I am with you alway": exact, clipped from Matthew 28:20. Fair.
  - "Joseph, thou son of David, fear not." The words are exact but cut mid-sentence. In the KJV it is "fear not **to take unto thee Mary thy wife**". The clip makes it a general "fear not". Small, but it is a real trim of meaning.
  - "God said, 'fear not'": true (Isaiah 7:4), spoken through Isaiah. Fine for 60 seconds.
  - "offered any sign": true (7:11).
  - "Ahaz refused, and begged a foreign king: 'save me'": true (7:12; 2 Kings 16:7). The script order suggests he begged after refusing; the Bible does not give the order. Small overclaim of sequence.
  - "told God no": fair. He refused the offer, though with pious-sounding words ("neither will I tempt the LORD").
  - "Seven hundred years later": round number, about right. "About seven hundred" is safer.
  - "pregnant by God's Holy Spirit", "her husband Joseph", "Joseph obeyed", "Immanuel means God with us", died for our sins, rose the third day: all match the KJV.
  - "He promised": said to the disciples; applying it to "us" is normal and fair.
  - No doctrinal error found.
- Best version? Close. **One change:** make "sign" clear in plain words, e.g. "offered him any proof he wanted" or "any sign, as proof".

**5. OVERALL: 8/10**

## VERSION Y

**1. NEWCOMER**
Same story and same hard words as X (see above). The difference:
- The first line tells me at once it is about Jesus. That helps me.
- But the next line jumps to "King Ahaz". I wonder: what has this king to do with Jesus's name? It feels like a jump for a moment.
- "It tells you where God is" is plain and easy.

**2. BIBLE READER**
The body has the same fresh material (Ahaz's refusal, "save me" vs "shall save", the two sons of David). But the first line sells the part a churchgoer already knows: Jesus is called Immanuel. A churchgoer may think "I know this one" and scroll.

**3. CURIOSITY GAP**
- First line: **6/10**. Clear and warm, but it half gives the answer away. Many people can already guess the name.
- Payoff: **8/10**. "Immanuel means: God with us... I am with you alway. Still true." answers "where God is" exactly. The cleanest payoff of the two.

**4. GUARDRAILS**
- Who for: newcomers and seekers; a little less for churchgoers.
- Take-away: Jesus's other name, Immanuel, means God is with us, and he still is.
- Global audience: OK. The first line is simpler than X's.
- Biblical accuracy: same checks and same notes as X (the clipped "fear not", the order of Ahaz's begging, the round "seven hundred"). Plus:
  - "Jesus has another name": fair. Matthew 1:23 applies Emmanuel to him ("they shall call his name").
  - No error found.
- Best version? Not quite. **One change:** bridge the hook to the king, e.g. "Jesus has another name. It was first given to a frightened king." That keeps the clarity and adds the story pull.

**5. OVERALL: 7/10**

## PICK: X

X opens with a story question (a king says no to God, what happens next?) that runs straight into the next line and shows off the part most churchgoers have never heard, while Y's opener is clear but gives away the answer and then jumps to a king with no link.

Caveat: this is one simulated viewer, not real ones. My confidence in X over Y is moderate, about 65% (give or take 15). Y pays off its own hook better. Real seed viewers should decide.
