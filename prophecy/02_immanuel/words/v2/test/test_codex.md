**X**

1. **NEWCOMER:** “This is about Jesus. A scared king refused God’s offer of a sign. God promised a special child. Much later, Jesus was born, died for people’s wrong actions, and came back to life. He shows that God is with people.”

   Hard words or lines: “sign,” “Behold,” “virgin,” “conceive,” “bear a son,” “Holy Spirit,” “thou,” “shalt,” “sins,” and “alway.” Does “son of David” mean David is Joseph’s father? How does a baby born seven hundred years later help the frightened king?

2. **BIBLE READER:** Yes. Many churchgoers may not have noticed the contrast: Ahaz and Joseph both belong to David’s family and hear “fear not,” but Ahaz refuses while Joseph obeys. Ahaz’s “save me” beside Jesus saving people from sins is another fresh connection.

3. **CURIOSITY GAP:** First line: **9/10**. A king refusing God creates immediate tension. Ending: **7/10**. God’s promised presence gives a strong finish, but we never learn what happened to the frightened king or his request for help.

4. **GUARDRAILS:** For curious newcomers and Christians who know the Christmas story. Main takeaway: **Jesus is God with us, saving people from sins and remaining with his followers.** Suitable worldwide, but the old English and quick movement through several people and centuries make understanding harder.

   **Bible check:** Ahaz’s family, fear, refusal, appeal to a foreign king, and God’s chosen sign are supported. “Any sign” fairly summarizes Isaiah 7:11. The prophecy quotation matches Isaiah 7:14. Mary’s virgin pregnancy through the Holy Spirit, Joseph being called her husband, both angel quotations, and Joseph’s obedience are supported by Matthew 1. “Immanuel” and Matthew’s “Emmanuel” are spelling differences, not an error. The meaning “God with us” is correct. Jesus dying for sins and rising on the third day are supported by 1 Corinthians 15:3–4. His presence as a baby fits John 1:14. The cross is biblically correct, although these excerpts do not explicitly state the method of his death. “I am with you alway” is an exact excerpt.

   **Limits:** “Seven hundred years” should say “about”; the supplied verses give no dates. Isaiah 7:16 also addresses Ahaz’s own crisis, which the jump forward leaves unexplained. Joseph’s “fear not” specifically concerns taking Mary as his wife. Jesus’ final promise addresses his disciples; “Still true” can sound like an unconditional promise to every viewer. These are context and scope concerns, not false quotations.

   **Best possible?** No. The biggest improvement would be to put the quoted speech into plain English, especially for viewers hearing these ideas for the first time.

5. **OVERALL: 7.5/10.**

**Y**

1. **NEWCOMER:** “Jesus has a name that means God is with us. A king once refused God’s offer, but God promised a child anyway. Jesus is that promised child. He died for people’s wrong actions, came back to life, and promised to stay with his followers.”

   Hard words or lines: “sign,” “Behold,” “virgin,” “conceive,” “bear a son,” “Holy Spirit,” “thou,” “son of David,” “shalt,” “sins,” and “alway.” I still do not understand why the frightened king’s story is needed, but I understand sooner that the video is about Jesus and what his name means.

2. **BIBLE READER:** Yes—the same less familiar connections as X: the two men from David’s family, the repeated “fear not,” their different responses, and the contrast between military rescue and rescue from sins. The opening itself will be familiar to many churchgoers.

3. **CURIOSITY GAP:** First line: **8/10**. It gives a clear subject and makes me wonder what the other name means. Ending: **9/10**. “God with us,” followed by Jesus’ continuing presence, directly answers the opening.

4. **GUARDRAILS:** For newcomers wondering who Jesus is, and Christians reflecting on Immanuel. Main takeaway: **Jesus’ name Immanuel expresses God’s presence with us, from his birth to his continuing promise.** Suitable worldwide, with the same language and pace barriers as X.

   **Bible check:** Every shared quotation and claim has the same support and limits listed for X. Y’s added claim, “Jesus has another name,” is supported by Matthew 1:23: Immanuel is a name or title applied to him. It does not mean Joseph gave him a second everyday name. “It tells you where God is” works as a hook about God’s presence, rather than a literal address.

   **Best possible?** No. The biggest improvement would again be plain English for the quoted speech. For example: “Joseph, a descendant of David, do not be afraid to take Mary as your wife.” That also restores the reason for Joseph’s fear.

5. **OVERALL: 8/10.**

**PICK Y.** It tells newcomers who the story is about immediately, and its ending answers the opening more clearly.
