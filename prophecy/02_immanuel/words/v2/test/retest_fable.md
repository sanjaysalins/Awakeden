## 1. NEWCOMER (no Bible, English second language)

**Who:** A scared king named Ahaz, long ago. Then a baby named Jesus, born to a woman named Mary.

**What happened:** God told the king "do not be afraid" and offered him proof. The king said no and asked another king to save him. God gave a promise anyway: a virgin will have a son called Immanuel. 700 years later Mary, a virgin, is pregnant by God. An angel tells a man, Joseph, to name the baby Jesus, because He will save people. Later He died on a cross, came back to life, and said "I am with you."

**Why it matters:** The king looked to a man to save him. God sent someone who really saves. "God with us" is still true today.

I got who / what / why. Good.

**Words or lines I would not understand:**
- **Who is Joseph?** He appears with no introduction. I only guess he is Mary's husband from "Mary thy wife".
- **Two names.** The promise says "Immanuel". The angel says "JESUS". The script never says plainly that they are the same person. I have to guess.
- **Baby to cross is a jump.** One line He is an unborn child, the next line He is dying on a cross. No "he grew up".
- **"the cross"** - not explained. I may not know it means an execution.
- **"sins"** - not explained (used twice).
- Old English: "Behold", "conceive", "thou", "thee", "thy", "shalt", "take unto thee", "alway" (sounds like a mistake for "always").
- "thou son of David" - I may think Joseph's father is named David. I will not link it back to King Ahaz's family.
- "virgin" - hard word for some, but it is needed. The line "Mary, a virgin, was pregnant by God's Holy Spirit" helps.
- "as proof" - helpful. Keeps "sign" clear.

## 2. BIBLE READER

Yes. Two things most churchgoers have not heard:
- **The "save me" contrast.** Ahaz sent to the king of Assyria, "come up, and save me" (2 Kings 16:7). The angel says of Jesus, "he shall save his people". Almost nobody links these. It is the best idea in the piece.
- **The Immanuel verse was given to a king who refused it.** Most people only know it as a Christmas card line. They do not know God gave it after a "no".

A quieter third one: "fear not" is said to the house of David twice (Isaiah 7:4 and Matthew 1:20, "thou son of David"). The script uses it but does not point at it. That is fine - do not add words for it.

## 3. CURIOSITY GAP

- **First line makes me keep watching: 8/10.** "A frightened king told God no. God answered anyway." Short, plain, a real question: what did God answer?
- **Ending pays it off: 7/10.** The real payoff is "Ahaz begged a king to save him. This child saves." That lands well. But the final words ("I am with you alway. Still true.") pay off "God with us", not the king. The king is forgotten at the end. It is a soft, true close, not a snap-shut close.

## 4. GUARDRAILS

**Who is it for:** Anyone worldwide, mostly people new to the Bible. Also gives a churchgoer something fresh.

**Main take-away (one sentence):** When a frightened king refused God and trusted a man to save him, God still promised a child - Jesus, God with us - who truly saves and is still with us.

**Global audience OK?** Mostly yes. Narrator lines are short and plain. The weak spots are the KJV quotes (old words) and the three gaps above (Joseph, two names, baby-to-cross).

**Errors:** none found.

**Small soft spots (not errors):**
1. **Order.** The script puts the begging before the sign ("Ahaz refused, and begged... God chose the sign"). The Bible does not say which came first. Low risk, but a careful reader could push back.
2. **"told God no"** - Ahaz's "no" sounded holy ("neither will I tempt the LORD"). The script makes it a flat no. Fair, because God's reply in 7:13 shows it was a real refusal.
3. **Near meaning skipped.** Isaiah 7:16 gives the sign a meaning in Ahaz's own day too. The script goes straight to Jesus. Matthew 1:22-23 does the same, so this is allowed. Not an overclaim.
4. **"With us at the cross"** - sound doctrine, but it is the narrator's link, not a quote. Fine.

**Best version possible? The one change that helps most:**
Say plainly that Jesus IS Immanuel. Right now the newcomer has to work it out. Example: "This child saves. His other name, Immanuel, means: God with us." Or simply: "Jesus is Immanuel: God with us."

## 5. OVERALL: 7.5 / 10

Strong hook. One truly fresh idea (the two "save" lines). Every quote is clean. It loses points on three small newcomer gaps, all cheap to fix.

## Top 3 fixes

1. **Join the two names.** Say that Jesus is Immanuel, in plain narrator words.
2. **Introduce Joseph in 3-5 words.** For example: "An angel told Joseph, the man she was to marry:" Right now he is a stranger.
3. **Bridge baby to cross.** Add two or three words so He grows up: "The child grew. With us, even at the cross, where He died for our sins." Also helps the newcomer who does not know what "the cross" is.

Optional, only if words allow: "About seven hundred years later" instead of "Seven hundred years later".
