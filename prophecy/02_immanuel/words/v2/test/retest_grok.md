1. NEWCOMER

This is about a scared king named Ahaz, and about a baby named Jesus. Ahaz was afraid of a war. God said, "Do not fear," and offered a sign to prove it. Ahaz said no, and asked a foreign king to save him. God gave the sign anyway: a virgin would have a son named Immanuel. About 700 years later, Mary, a virgin, was pregnant by God's Holy Spirit. An angel told Joseph to stay with her and name the baby Jesus, because this child would save people from their sins. Joseph did it. Immanuel means God with us. Jesus died for sins on the cross, rose on the third day, and said he is with us always. That promise still stands.

Hard words and lines:
- "conceive," "Behold," "thou," "thee," "thy," "shalt," "unto," "alway"
- "take unto thee Mary thy wife"
- "Holy Spirit"
- "Immanuel" (the meaning comes late)
- "King David's family" (who David is)
- "the cross" (what it is)

2. BIBLE READER

Yes. Many people know the virgin and the name Immanuel. Few hear that this sign was given to King Ahaz after he refused God's offer and sent gold to Assyria with the words "save me" (2 Kings 16:7–8). Fewer hear that Assyria did not truly help him (2 Chronicles 28:20–21). The echo is sharp: Ahaz begged a king to save him; the child saves people from their sins. The "fear not" to Ahaz and the "fear not" to Joseph also sit side by side, which most Christmas tellings skip.

3. CURIOSITY GAP

First line: 8. "A frightened king told God no. God answered anyway." I want to know what he refused, and what God did next.

Ending: 7. The child, the cross, the rising, and "I am with you alway" do pay off "God answered anyway" and "God with us." "Still true" is very short. The king story is already finished before the last line.

4. GUARDRAILS

Who it is for: anyone, including people who do not know the Bible. A scared person who looks for help in the wrong place. Also church people who only know the Christmas verse.

Main take-away: A frightened king refused God's sign and begged another king to save him; God still sent Jesus, God with us, who died for sins, rose, and is still with us.

Global audience: the story spine is simple enough. The three old-English quotes are not. A newcomer can lose the middle.

Bible check against the KJV:
- "fear not" matches Isaiah 7:4.
- The offer of any sign matches Isaiah 7:11.
- The prophet's line matches Isaiah 7:14 word for word.
- "save me" matches 2 Kings 16:7. Ahaz is of David's house (Isaiah 7:2, 7:13; Matthew 1:9).
- Mary, virgin, with child by the Holy Ghost, matches Matthew 1:18 and Luke 1:27, 1:35.
- The angel's words match Matthew 1:20–21, with "And she shall bring forth a son, and" left out. The words that remain are exact.
- Joseph obeyed: Matthew 1:24–25.
- "God with us": Matthew 1:23. Isaiah spells it Immanuel; Matthew spells it Emmanuel. The meaning is right.
- Died for our sins, rose the third day: 1 Corinthians 15:3–4.
- "I am with you alway" is exact inside Matthew 28:20. The verse also says "even unto the end of the world."

Errors and overclaims:
- "Told God no" is too flat. Ahaz said he would not ask, and would not tempt the LORD (Isaiah 7:12). He refused the sign while sounding religious.
- "God answered anyway" can sound like God replied to a prayer. Ahaz did not ask. The Lord himself gave the sign (Isaiah 7:14).
- "Seven hundred years later" is not in the KJV. As a round number it is fair. It makes the sign sound only far away. Isaiah 7:16 also gives a sign for Ahaz's own war: before the child knows good and evil, the land Ahaz hates will lose both its kings. Matthew 1:22–23 says the birth of Jesus fulfills the prophet's words. Both belong.
- "With us at the cross" is a sermon line. The death for sins is in the text. "With us at the cross" is not a KJV sentence.
- "This child saves" can sound like he saved Ahaz from the war. The verse means he saves his people from their sins (Matthew 1:21). The film never says the foreign king did not save Ahaz, though 2 Chronicles 28:20–21 says Assyria distressed him and did not help him.
- "Still true" fits "unto the end of the world," but the quote is spoken to the disciples Jesus is sending.

Best version: one change would help most. Say the three quotes in plain English, and say "God with us" in the same breath as the name.

5. OVERALL

7 out of 10. The hook is strong, the Ahaz contrast is fresh, and the main quotes are faithful. A worldwide viewer will stumble on the old English, and the sign is told as only a far-off birth.

Three fixes:
1. Use plain speech for the prophet, the angel, and Jesus. Say "God with us" the moment the name is spoken.
2. Say Ahaz would not ask for a sign, then begged Assyria, "save me," and that king did not save him. Add one short clause that the sign also spoke to Ahaz's war, and came true when Jesus was born.
3. Make the last line pay the first line in words a newcomer knows: he died for our sins, rose on the third day, and is still with us.
