=== VERSION X ===
1. **NEWCOMER:** This is about a scared king (Ahaz) who rejected God's help, and God's response to send a baby (Jesus) to save people instead. It matters because it shows God is always with us, even dying for our mistakes. Words I might not understand: "conceive," "thou shalt," "alway" (looks like a typo for always).
2. **BIBLE READER:** Yes. Most churchgoers only hear the "Immanuel" virgin birth prophecy at Christmas; they rarely hear the gritty historical backstory of King Ahaz refusing God's sign and begging a foreign king for salvation instead.
3. **CURIOSITY GAP:** First line (9/10) - "A frightened king told God no" is a fantastic hook that creates immediate tension and makes you wonder what God did next. Ending payoff (9/10) - "Still true" perfectly resolves the tension of God "answering anyway" by continually being "with us."
4. **GUARDRAILS:** For everyone (seekers and believers). Take-away: God answered human fear and rejection by coming down to be with us and save us Himself. Global audience OK, though KJV quotes are slightly archaic. Biblically accurate (Ahaz's refusal in Isa 7:12, begging Assyria in 2 Kings 16:7, exact KJV quotes used). One change to improve: add three words explaining *what* Ahaz was afraid of (e.g., "an invading army") so his fear makes immediate sense.
5. **OVERALL:** 9/10.

=== VERSION Y ===
1. **NEWCOMER:** This is about a baby named Jesus who has another name showing God is with us, contrasting with an old king who was scared. It matters because Jesus saves us from our sins and is still with us today. Words I might not understand: "conceive," "thou shalt," "alway."
2. **BIBLE READER:** Yes. The historical context of the virgin birth prophecy (King Ahaz relying on a foreign king instead of God) is a detail most have never heard connected to the Christmas story.
3. **CURIOSITY GAP:** First line (4/10) - "Jesus has another name" feels like a Sunday School trivia question, not a compelling story hook. Ending payoff (7/10) - The ending pays off the "where God is" promise, but the immediate jump to King Ahaz in line 2 feels completely disconnected from the hook.
4. **GUARDRAILS:** For everyone. Take-away: Jesus is God with us, sent to save us. Global audience OK, with the same KJV archaic word caveats. Biblically accurate (all quotes and historical claims match the provided KJV text). One change to improve: change the hook entirely, because jumping from "Jesus has another name" straight into "King Ahaz... was afraid" is a jarring and confusing subject change.
5. **OVERALL:** 6/10.

**PICK X.**
Version X has a much stronger, story-driven hook that seamlessly sets up the historical conflict with Ahaz, whereas Y's hook feels like trivia and creates a jarring transition into the script.
