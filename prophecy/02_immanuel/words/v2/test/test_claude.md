# Blind judge: X vs Y

X and Y are the same except for the first line, so most answers apply to both.

## X

**1. Newcomer.** A scared king named Ahaz would not ask God for help. He asked another king to save him instead. God gave a sign anyway: a virgin would have a son. 700 years later, that son was Jesus. His name means "God with us," and he promised to stay with us. It matters because God stays close even when people say no.
- Words I would not get: "Behold", "conceive", "thou shalt", "alway" (it sounds like a mistake for "always"), "Immanuel" (but it gets explained).
- Also unclear: "Holy Spirit", and which foreign king.

**2. Bible reader.** Yes, something new. Most churchgoers know Isaiah 7:14 only as a Christmas verse. They don't know it was first said to a scared king who said no. The contrast is fresh too: "Ahaz begged a king to save him. This child saves."

**3. Curiosity gap.**
- Hook: **8/10.** It has a person, a conflict and a twist.
- Payoff: **6/10.** The hook pays off in the middle, when the sign arrives. The ending ("with you alway… still true") does not go back to the king who said no, so it feels like two hooks.

**4. Guardrails.**
- **Who for:** newcomers and churchgoers.
- **Take-away:** even when we say no out of fear, God comes to be with us, in Jesus.
- **Global audience:** mostly OK. "Alway" and "Behold" are the weak words.
- **Bible accuracy:** the quotes are word-for-word. Isaiah 7:4, 7:11-14 and 2 Kings 16:7 back the claims.
  - The order of "refused, and begged" is not stated in the text, but it is fair.
  - The angel's "fear not." is cut short. The verse goes on: "fear not to take unto thee Mary". Cutting it is allowed, but say so.
  - "Seven hundred years" is about right (closer to 730).
  - "Husband" is fine (Matthew 1:19).
  - No real errors.
- **Best fix:** end back on the hook, e.g. "A king told God no. God came anyway. Still true."

**5. Overall: 7.5/10**

## Y

**1. Newcomer.** Same as X. Same hard words, with "alway" again the worst. The first line is clear and simple.

**2. Bible reader.** Same fresh angle as X (Ahaz, and "this child saves").

**3. Curiosity gap.**
- Hook: **7/10.** "Where is God?" is a big human question. But "Jesus has another name" can sound like a church quiz to a newcomer.
- Payoff: **9/10.** The answer, "God with us", lands. Then "with you alway… still true" answers "where is God" right up to today. One spine from start to end.

**4. Guardrails.**
- **Who for:** same as X.
- **Take-away:** same as X.
- **Global audience:** OK. The first line is easier than X's.
- **Bible accuracy:** same checks as X. No errors. Same small note on the cut "fear not."
- **Best fix:** help the newcomer with "alway". Make the last line "Still true. He is with you." That keeps the Bible quote word-for-word and makes the meaning plain.

**5. Overall: 8/10**

---

**PICK: Y.** The opening question ("where is God?") is answered by the last line, so the whole film pays off. X's stronger hook is used up halfway through.
