# Hooks — blind seat: Fable

## Hook 1 (King Ahaz refuses the sign)
HOOK: "God offered a scared king a sign. The king said no."  (11 words)
| BRIDGE: "and Immanuel means God with us: the sign God gave anyway."
| GAP: What does God do when a man turns Him down? First pay-off at ~10 s (the promise is spoken: God gives the sign anyway). Full pay-off at ~35 s (the sign is a son, and his name means God with us).
| RISK: The locked body never says Ahaz said no, so the new bridge must carry "gave anyway" alone; "sign" may read as a road sign to some second-language viewers.

## Hook 2 (the war and the baby)
HOOK: "Two armies were coming. God's answer was a baby."  (9 words)
| BRIDGE: "and Immanuel means God's answer to our fear: God with us."
| GAP: Why would a baby be the answer to a war? Part pay-off at ~10 s (the promised son). Full pay-off at ~35 s (the baby's name means God with us).
| RISK: The armies are never named again in the locked body, so the hook can feel dropped; "answer was a baby" squeezes Isaiah 7:1-14 very tightly, and a careful Bible reader may want the nearer meaning in Ahaz's own day.

## Hook 3 (the other name)
HOOK: "Jesus has another name. It tells you where God is."  (10 words)
| BRIDGE: "and His other name, Immanuel, means: God with us."
| GAP: What is the other name, and where is God? The name comes at ~10 s (Immanuel). The meaning comes at ~35 s (God with us), and "with us as a baby, at the cross" then proves it.
| RISK: Still name-led like the old hook, with no person in danger; the main pay-off is still late (~35 s).

## MY PICK
Hook 1. It has a real person, real fear, and a clear "what happens next", it leads straight into "in King Ahaz's day", and its first pay-off lands at ~10 s, not ~35 s.
