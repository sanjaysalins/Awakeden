## 1. WHO IS THIS FOR (one named viewer; who stops, who finishes, who shares; who is NOT served)
Named viewer: Grace, 34, Lagos, goes to church, scrolls at night. She stops on line 1 ("Did they go?"), finishes, and shares for line 9 and line 12. Bible readers finish and share. Scrollers stop for the hook and comic slams. NOT served: the total newcomer between 13 s and 28 s. ## 2. MAIN TAKEAWAY (one sentence a viewer could say back; which line carries it; did the film make it clear?)
"The experts knew where Jesus was and did not go, but He came to us first." Lines 9 and 12 carry it. Clear, yes. But the end card says a different thing (PROMISED. CAME TRUE.). Two takeaways compete: "go" and "promise kept". The "go" one wins; the promise one is under-built.

## 3. GLOBAL AUDIENCE (include the ON-SCREEN words and pictures; who / what / why in my own words; where I got lost or misled, quote the line; words that fail in translation)
Who: Jesus, Bible experts, wise men. What: a prophet named the town; the experts knew it; only the strangers went. Why: God kept His word, and came to us.
Lost:
- "King Herod asked the experts." (line 6) Asked what? Two kings in one line ("the King", "King Herod") will confuse a newcomer.
- "ruler in Israel." / "from everlasting." (lines 3, 5) Two fragments. "From everlasting" will not translate. Line 6 rescues it ("birthplace, not His beginning"), which is the best line in the film.
- "can the King come from Galilee?" (line 10) A newcomer does not know why this matters. The gold "?" helps; it is still a third thread 20 s from the end.
- "But Jesus came much farther." (line 11) Farther than whom?
- Big letters WENT appear over a line saying the experts did NOT go. On mute this can read backwards.

## 4. BIBLICALLY ACCURATE (claim by claim: RIGHT / OVERCLAIMED / UNSUPPORTED / WRONG, quote the line; say only what you can support)
- "The Bible experts knew where the King would be born" (1): RIGHT, Matt 2:4-6.
- "Micah named a small town: Bethlehem" (2): RIGHT, Micah 5:2 ("little").
- "ruler in Israel" / "from everlasting" (3, 5): RIGHT, KJV words; "It goes on" honestly marks the skip.
- "birthplace, not His beginning" (6): RIGHT as a mainstream reading (John 1:1; 8:58).
- "In Bethlehem of Judaea" (7): RIGHT, Matt 2:5. The text says "they said", not one scribe; fine.
- "Bethlehem was close by" (8): RIGHT by geography (about 6 miles); not a Bible statement.
- "The Bible never says the experts went" (9): RIGHT, and carefully worded. The hollow footprints push it toward "they did not go"; keep the words as they are.
- "found the child" (9): RIGHT, Matt 2:11. Watch the picture: Matthew says "house", not stable. Camels are tradition.
- Line 10: RIGHT, John 7:41-42.
- Line 11: RIGHT, John 6:38; 1 Cor 15:3-4.
- Line 12: RIGHT in sense (Rom 5:8; 1 John 4:19).
Nothing WRONG.

## 5. BEST VERSION POSSIBLE (what would the best explainer in the world do differently; the ONE highest-value change; score 0-10 against that bar)
The best explainer would make the wait felt. The film never says how long the promise stood. ONE change: put the time gap in line 2 ("about seven hundred years before"). It makes PROMISED / CAME TRUE earn its end card, for every viewer. Score: 7.5.

## NEW TO YOU (as a regular churchgoer: what here had you NOT heard before? quote it. 0-10)
"The Bible never says the experts went." and "Bethlehem would be His birthplace, not His beginning." Both fresh. The Galilee argument is less known too. Score 7.

## PROMISE KEPT (in your own words: who made what promise, how long before, and what came true? did the film make it feel like a promise kept, or only a story about who went? 0-10)
God, through Micah, promised a King from Bethlehem; Jesus was born there. How long before: the film never says. So it feels mostly like a story about who went, with a promise label on top. Score 5.5.

## THREE SMALLEST FIXES (line number + exact replacement wording)
- Line 2: "About seven hundred years before Jesus, God's prophet Micah named a small town: Bethlehem. God promised a King from there:" (the date rests on Micah 1:1 plus history, so keep "about").
- Line 6, last two sentences: "Wise men came from far away, asking: where is the new King? King Herod asked the Bible experts: where?"
- Line 11, first sentence: "But Jesus came much farther than the wise men."
