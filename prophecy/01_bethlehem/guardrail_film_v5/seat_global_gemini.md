## 1. WHO IS THIS FOR
This is for "Mateo," an ESL speaker in a global youth group who knows basic Christian concepts but relies on auto-translated captions. He stops for the comic-book visuals and the mystery of "Did they go?". He finishes because the pacing is fast. He shares it because the ending is uplifting. However, non-Christians and beginner English speakers are NOT served; the rapid jumps between Micah, Herod, Galilee, and Heaven require too much insider cultural context to process in 59 seconds.

## 2. MAIN TAKEAWAY
Even though the religious experts didn't bother to travel a short distance to find Jesus, Jesus traveled from heaven to find us. Line 12 carries this: "Before anyone went to Him, He came to us." Yes, the film makes this contrast very clear.

## 3. GLOBAL AUDIENCE
For an ESL audience and machine translation, several phrases fail. 
*   **Line 4:** "It goes on:" is an English idiom. Auto-translators often render this literally as "it continues walking," which makes no sense to a non-native reader. 
*   **Line 5:** "from everlasting." This is archaic, insider Bible language. Translators struggle with it, and ESL readers will not know what "everlasting" means as a noun. 
*   **Line 11:** "came much farther." "Farther" usually implies physical roads. Using it metaphorically for leaving heaven is poetic but risks confusing a literal-minded global viewer.
*   **On-screen:** The tag "A SCRIBE" (at Line 7) contradicts the audio "experts" (Line 1). "Scribe" is a rare, insider word; stick to "expert" or "scholar" in both places. The "CAME TRUE" stamp is universally understood and works perfectly.

## 4. BIBLICALLY ACCURATE
*   **RIGHT:** Line 1 ("The Bible experts knew where the King would be born.") - Supported by Matthew 2:4-5.
*   **RIGHT:** Line 9 ("The Bible never says the experts went.") - Supported by omission in Matthew 2.
*   **RIGHT:** Line 10 ("Later, people argued: can the King come from Galilee?") - Supported by John 7:41-42.
*   **RIGHT:** Line 11 ("He died for our sins. On the third day, He rose from the dead.") - Supported by 1 Corinthians 15:3-4.

## 5. BEST VERSION POSSIBLE
The best explainer in the world would simplify the vocabulary for a global audience and fix the jarring timeline jumps. The script expects the viewer to track Micah's prophecy, Jesus' birth, Herod's court, Jesus' adulthood in Galilee, and His crucifixion, all in under a minute. The ONE highest-value change is replacing archaic, insider idioms with plain, universally translatable English so the core contrast (they didn't go, but He came) hits harder. Score: 7/10.

## CURIOSITY GAP
Opens at line 1 ("Did they go?"), pays off at line 9 ("The Bible never says the experts went."). Score: 9/10. It is worth the wait and provides a great narrative hook that subverts expectations.

## NEW TO YOU
As a regular churchgoer, I had never explicitly considered the apathy of the religious scholars compared to the Magi. I had not heard before: Line 9, "The Bible never says the experts went." Score: 8/10.

## PROMISE KEPT
God made a promise through the prophet Micah that a ruler would come from Bethlehem. The film makes it feel like a promise kept visually (the gold star, the "CAME TRUE" stamp), but the script's emotional climax pivots away from the promise and focuses entirely on the irony of *who went*. Score: 7/10.

*   Line 5: "from the beginning of time."
