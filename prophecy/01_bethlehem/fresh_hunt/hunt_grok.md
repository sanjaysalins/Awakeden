1. Rachel's Cry Comes Back to Bethlehem

1. TITLE: Rachel's Cry Comes Back to Bethlehem
2. HOOK: Why does a mother who died near Bethlehem weep again?
3. THE VERSES:
- Genesis 35:18-19: "And it came to pass, as her soul was in departing, (for she died) that she called his name Benoni: but his father called him Benjamin. And Rachel died, and was buried in the way to Ephrath, which is Bethlehem."
- 1 Samuel 10:2: "thou shalt find two men by Rachel's sepulchre in the border of Benjamin at Zelzah"
- Jeremiah 31:15: "A voice was heard in Ramah, lamentation, and bitter weeping; Rahel weeping for her children refused to be comforted for her children, because they were not."
- Jeremiah 31:16-17: "Refrain thy voice from weeping, and thine eyes from tears: for thy work shall be rewarded, saith the LORD; and they shall come again from the land of the enemy. And there is hope in thine end, saith the LORD, that thy children shall come again to their own border."
- Jeremiah 40:1: "the captain of the guard had let him go from Ramah, when he had taken him being bound in chains among all that were carried away captive of Jerusalem and Judah"
- Matthew 2:16-18: "slew all the children that were in Bethlehem, and in all the coasts thereof, from two years old and under... Then was fulfilled that which was spoken by Jeremy the prophet, saying, In Rama was there a voice heard, lamentation, and weeping, and great mourning, Rachel weeping for her children, and would not be comforted, because they are not."
4. WHAT KIND OF LINK: DIRECT PROPHECY. Matthew says Jeremiah's word was fulfilled at the killing of Bethlehem's children. Genesis is the older text that puts Rachel's death and burial on the Bethlehem road. Jeremiah itself names Ramah, the place later tied to the exile march.
5. WHAT IT DOES NOT PROVE: This does not prove Jeremiah wrote the name Bethlehem, or that Rachel was alive and crying at a grave. Genesis puts that grave on the way to Bethlehem. 1 Samuel 10:2 puts her sepulchre on Benjamin's border. Those notes do not give a settled map pin. Matthew quotes the weeping only. He does not say the hope in Jeremiah 31:16-17 happened on that same day. This does not make the children's deaths good, or make Rachel's death a prediction of Herod.
6. WHY MOST CHURCHGOERS HAVE NOT HEARD IT: They may know the sad Christmas verse, but not the road, the grave, Ramah, and the hope lines read as one chain. Freshness: 7.
7. HOW IT LANDS ON JESUS: Matthew ties this old cry of Rachel to the days when Jesus, the young child, was in Bethlehem.
8. CONFIDENCE: 8

2. Scripture Said Bethlehem. They Said Galilee.

1. TITLE: Scripture Said Bethlehem. They Said Galilee.
2. HOOK: They had the right town. Why did they still reject Jesus?
3. THE VERSES:
- John 7:41-42: "Others said, This is the Christ. But some said, Shall Christ come out of Galilee? Hath not the scripture said, That Christ cometh of the seed of David, and out of the town of Bethlehem, where David was?"
- John 7:43: "So there was a division among the people because of him."
- Matthew 2:1: "Now when Jesus was born in Bethlehem of Judaea in the days of Herod the king, behold, there came wise men from the east to Jerusalem"
- Luke 2:4: "And Joseph also went up from Galilee, out of the city of Nazareth, into Judaea, unto the city of David, which is called Bethlehem"
4. WHAT KIND OF LINK: NT-CONFIRMED. John's crowd states the scripture test: the Christ comes from David's line and from Bethlehem. Matthew and Luke record that Jesus was born there. John 7 never tells the birth.
5. WHAT IT DOES NOT PROVE: This does not prove the crowd lied or knew his birth story and hid it. Their test was right. Their fact about his origin was short. John 7 alone does not narrate the birth, so this short must not pretend that it does.
6. WHY MOST CHURCHGOERS HAVE NOT HEARD IT: John 7 is known for the feast and the division, not for this Bethlehem test. Freshness: 8.
7. HOW IT LANDS ON JESUS: The scripture test they used against Jesus is the test his birth meets.
8. CONFIDENCE: 9

3. From Everlasting, Born in Bethlehem

1. TITLE: From Everlasting, Born in Bethlehem
2. HOOK: If he is born in Bethlehem, when did he begin?
3. THE VERSES:
- Micah 5:2: "But thou, Bethlehem Ephratah, though thou be little among the thousands of Judah, yet out of thee shall he come forth unto me that is to be ruler in Israel; whose goings forth have been from of old, from everlasting."
- Matthew 2:6: "And thou Bethlehem, in the land of Juda, art not the least among the princes of Juda: for out of thee shall come a Governor, that shall rule my people Israel."
4. WHAT KIND OF LINK: NT-CONFIRMED for the person. Matthew applies the prophet's Bethlehem-ruler word to the birth of Jesus. The "from everlasting" clause is in that same Micah sentence. Matthew's wording does not quote that clause.
5. WHAT IT DOES NOT PROVE: This does not prove Matthew used these words to argue that Jesus is God. The KJV says "from everlasting." Some careful readers hear "from ancient times," meaning the old royal line, so this one line is not a full doctrine of the Trinity. It also does not make the real birth in Bethlehem unimportant. He comes forth "unto me": the ruler is for God.
6. WHY MOST CHURCHGOERS HAVE NOT HEARD IT: They know Micah named the town. The second half, about where this ruler is from, is rarely the point of the talk. Freshness: 5.
7. HOW IT LANDS ON JESUS: The ruler Matthew ties to Jesus is described as one whose goings forth are from of old, from everlasting, and who is also born in Bethlehem.
8. CONFIDENCE: 7

4. Great to the Ends of the Earth

1. TITLE: Great to the Ends of the Earth
2. HOOK: Bethlehem is small. How far does this ruler reach?
3. THE VERSES:
- Micah 5:2: "out of thee shall he come forth unto me that is to be ruler in Israel"
- Micah 5:4: "And he shall stand and feed in the strength of the LORD, in the majesty of the name of the LORD his God; and they shall abide: for now shall he be great unto the ends of the earth."
- Matthew 2:6: "for out of thee shall come a Governor, that shall rule my people Israel."
4. WHAT KIND OF LINK: TYPE-ECHO. The "he" of Micah 5:4 is the ruler just named from Bethlehem. Matthew identifies that ruler with Jesus, then stops. The New Testament never quotes "the ends of the earth" from this place.
5. WHAT IT DOES NOT PROVE: This does not prove a verse of fulfillment for world conquest, and it does not make Micah 5:5-6 a list of events in Jesus' life. Those lines go on to the Assyrian, seven shepherds, and eight principal men. "Feed" means he shepherds his people. It does not by itself identify the nativity shepherds.
6. WHY MOST CHURCHGOERS HAVE NOT HEARD IT: Public reading often stops at the town name and never reads the reach of the same ruler. Freshness: 8.
7. HOW IT LANDS ON JESUS: The ruler who comes from Bethlehem is the one this oracle calls great unto the ends of the earth, and Matthew says that ruler is the Christ just born.
8. CONFIDENCE: 7

5. A Gate Blessing Names Bethlehem

1. TITLE: A Gate Blessing Names Bethlehem
2. HOOK: What did Bethlehem's elders ask God to do there?
3. THE VERSES:
- Ruth 4:11: "The LORD make the woman that is come into thine house like Rachel and like Leah, which two did build the house of Israel: and do thou worthily in Ephratah, and be famous in Bethlehem"
- Ruth 4:13: "So Boaz took Ruth, and she was his wife: and when he went in unto her, the LORD gave her conception, and she bare a son."
- Ruth 4:17: "There is a son born to Naomi; and they called his name Obed: he is the father of Jesse, the father of David."
- Matthew 1:5: "And Salmon begat Booz of Rachab; and Booz begat Obed of Ruth; and Obed begat Jesse"
- Matthew 2:1: "Now when Jesus was born in Bethlehem of Judaea"
4. WHAT KIND OF LINK: TYPE-ECHO. The New Testament does confirm the family line: Ruth, Obed, Jesse, David, then Jesus, and it records his birth in Bethlehem. It never says the elders' prayer was a prediction.
5. WHAT IT DOES NOT PROVE: This does not prove the elders knew they were speaking about Jesus. In the book itself, the fame they ask for lands on David. A later birth in that same town, in that same line, is something the whole canon lets us see. It is not a speech the elders addressed to Mary.
6. WHY MOST CHURCHGOERS HAVE NOT HEARD IT: They know Ruth ends with David. Few are shown the elders naming Ephratah and Bethlehem over that house. Freshness: 7.
7. HOW IT LANDS ON JESUS: Jesus is born in the town named in that gate blessing, in the line Matthew traces through Ruth's son Obed.
8. CONFIDENCE: 8

6. A Moabite Enters at Bethlehem

1. TITLE: A Moabite Enters at Bethlehem
2. HOOK: How did a woman from Moab enter Jesus' family?
3. THE VERSES:
- Deuteronomy 23:3: "An Ammonite or Moabite shall not enter into the congregation of the LORD; even to their tenth generation shall they not enter into the congregation of the LORD for ever"
- Ruth 2:12: "The LORD recompense thy work, and a full reward be given thee of the LORD God of Israel, under whose wings thou art come to trust."
- Ruth 4:10: "Ruth the Moabitess, the wife of Mahlon, have I purchased to be my wife, to raise up the name of the dead upon his inheritance"
- Ruth 4:11: "do thou worthily in Ephratah, and be famous in Bethlehem"
- Matthew 1:5: "and Booz begat Obed of Ruth"
4. WHAT KIND OF LINK: TYPE-ECHO. Deuteronomy states the bar. Ruth shows Bethlehem's gate receiving a Moabitess who has come under the wings of the LORD. Matthew puts her in the genealogy of Jesus. The New Testament never mentions Deuteronomy 23.
5. WHAT IT DOES NOT PROVE: This does not prove the law was a mistake, or that Ruth found a clever way around it. It does not prove a "tenth generation" count that lands on David or Jesus. The text gives no such sum. It also does not prove Jesus was born in Bethlehem because Ruth was.
6. WHY MOST CHURCHGOERS HAVE NOT HEARD IT: Ruth in the Christmas genealogy is common. The law beside the Bethlehem gate is not. Freshness: 6.
7. HOW IT LANDS ON JESUS: Matthew counts Ruth, received in Bethlehem, in the family line of Jesus, who is born in that town.
8. CONFIDENCE: 7

MY TOP PICK = Rachel's Cry Comes Back to Bethlehem, because Matthew says Jeremiah's word was fulfilled at Bethlehem, and Genesis had already put Rachel's death and grave on that road.
