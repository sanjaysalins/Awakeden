# Bethlehem: six fresh angles

---

## 1. The water David would not drink

**HOOK:** Why did a thirsty king pour out the water his heroes fought to bring?

**THE VERSES**
- 2 Samuel 23:14: "the garrison of the Philistines was then in Beth-lehem."
- 2 Samuel 23:15: "Oh that one would give me drink of the water of the well of Bethlehem, which is by the gate!"
- 2 Samuel 23:16: "he would not drink thereof, but poured it out unto the LORD."
- 2 Samuel 23:17: "is not this the blood of the men that went in jeopardy of their lives?"
- John 7:37: "If any man thirst, let him come unto me, and drink."
- Matthew 26:28: "For this is my blood of the new testament, which is shed for many for the remission of sins."

**KIND OF LINK:** TYPE-ECHO. The NT never mentions this well.

**WHAT IT DOES NOT PROVE:** Do not say the well "foretold" Jesus. Do not say Jesus drew water from this well. The pattern is the point: David called the water "blood" and would not take it, because it cost others their lives. Jesus pays the cost himself, and then he tells us to drink.

**WHY MOST HAVEN'T HEARD IT:** It is hidden in a list of David's mighty men, and it is never read at Christmas. **Freshness: 8/10**

**HOW IT LANDS ON JESUS:** David would not drink water bought with other men's blood, but Jesus, born in that same town, gives his own blood and says "come… and drink."

**CONFIDENCE:** 9/10 that it is in the text. 7/10 for the echo.

## 2. The half of Micah 5:2 nobody quotes

**HOOK:** How can a baby born in Bethlehem be "from everlasting"?

**THE VERSES**
- Micah 5:2: "yet out of thee shall he come forth unto me that is to be ruler in Israel; whose goings forth have been from of old, from everlasting."
- Matthew 2:6: "for out of thee shall come a Governor, that shall rule my people Israel." (Matthew stops before the "everlasting" line.)
- John 8:58: "Before Abraham was, I am."
- John 1:14: "And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us."

**KIND OF LINK:** The place is DIRECT PROPHECY (Matthew 2:6). The "everlasting" line is not quoted in the NT. Tying it to Jesus being there before his birth is NT-CONFIRMED only as a doctrine (John 1, John 8:58), not as a quote of Micah.

**WHAT IT DOES NOT PROVE:** Some readers take "from of old" as "from an ancient family line." Say that the line *fits* John 1. Do not say it alone proves Jesus is God.

**WHY MOST HAVEN'T HEARD IT:** Everyone quotes the first half (the town). Almost nobody stops on the last six words. **Freshness: 6/10**

**HOW IT LANDS ON JESUS:** The baby born in the small town is the one whose goings forth were "from everlasting": he did not begin in Bethlehem, he arrived there.

**CONFIDENCE:** 9/10

## 3. Son of my sorrow, son of the right hand

**HOOK:** Who was the first baby born on the road to Bethlehem?

**THE VERSES**
- Genesis 35:18: "as her soul was in departing, (for she died) that she called his name Benoni: but his father called him Benjamin."
- Genesis 35:19: "And Rachel died, and was buried in the way to Ephrath, which is Bethlehem."
- Jeremiah 31:15: "Rachel weeping for her children refused to be comforted for her children, because they were not."
- Jeremiah 31:17: "And there is hope in thine end, saith the LORD."
- Matthew 2:18: "Rachel weeping for her children, and would not be comforted, because they are not."
- Mark 16:19: "he was received up into heaven, and sat on the right hand of God."

**KIND OF LINK:** Rachel's weeping is NT-CONFIRMED (Matthew 2:18 quotes Jeremiah). The Benoni-to-Benjamin name change is a TYPE-ECHO only.

**WHAT IT DOES NOT PROVE:** The NT never calls Jesus a "new Benjamin." The meanings "son of my sorrow" and "son of the right hand" are standard name meanings, but say "the names mean" and not more. Do not claim Matthew had Benjamin in mind.

**WHY MOST HAVEN'T HEARD IT:** People know "Rachel weeping," but few know that Rachel died *at Bethlehem* giving birth. **Freshness: 7/10**

**HOW IT LANDS ON JESUS:** Bethlehem's first birth turned sorrow into "son of the right hand," and Bethlehem's greatest birth leads to the Son who sits at God's right hand.

**CONFIDENCE:** 9/10 for the facts. 6/10 for the echo.

## 4. The tower of the flock

**HOOK:** Why does Micah, just before Bethlehem, speak to a "tower of the flock"?

**THE VERSES**
- Genesis 35:21: "And Israel journeyed, and spread his tent beyond the tower of Edar." (This is right after Rachel's burial near Bethlehem.)
- Micah 4:8: "And thou, O tower of the flock, the strong hold of the daughter of Zion, unto thee shall it come, even the first dominion."
- Micah 5:4: "And he shall stand and feed in the strength of the LORD."
- Luke 2:8: "shepherds abiding in the field, keeping watch over their flock by night."

**KIND OF LINK:** TYPE-ECHO. The NT never names the tower.

**WHAT IT DOES NOT PROVE:** That "Edar" means "flock" is standard, and the KJV margin links the two verses. Do not claim the shepherds of Luke 2 stood at this tower, or that they raised temple lambs. That comes from later tradition, not from the Bible.

**WHY MOST HAVEN'T HEARD IT:** Micah 4:8 sits in the chapter before the famous verse, and few people read the run-up. **Freshness: 7/10**

**HOW IT LANDS ON JESUS:** Micah moves from a "tower of the flock" to a ruler who will "feed" his people, and the first to hear of Jesus' birth were men watching flocks near Bethlehem.

**CONFIDENCE:** 7/10

## 5. Bethlehem: from Israel's darkest days to its king

**HOOK:** Why do the Bible's darkest stories keep starting in Bethlehem?

**THE VERSES**
- Judges 17:7: "a young man out of Bethlehem-judah… who was a Levite."
- Judges 19:1: "in those days, when there was no king in Israel… a concubine out of Bethlehem-judah."
- Judges 21:25: "In those days there was no king in Israel: every man did that which was right in his own eyes."
- Ruth 1:1: "a certain man of Bethlehem-judah went to sojourn in the country of Moab."
- Ruth 4:22: "And Obed begat Jesse, and Jesse begat David."
- Matthew 1:5: "Booz begat Obed of Ruth."

**KIND OF LINK:** TYPE-ECHO for the "Bethlehem gives the missing king" arc. The book order and the repeated "no king" line plainly support it. Matthew 1:5 confirms that Ruth is in Jesus' family line.

**WHAT IT DOES NOT PROVE:** The Bible does not say Bethlehem was "cursed" or "redeemed." Judges 19 is violent, so keep it short and do not show it.

**WHY MOST HAVEN'T HEARD IT:** Judges 17–21 is almost never preached, so people miss that both of its stories are tied to Bethlehem. **Freshness: 8/10**

**HOW IT LANDS ON JESUS:** The town tied to Israel's "no king" chaos becomes the town that gives David, and then David's greater Son, Jesus.

**CONFIDENCE:** 8/10

## 6. A shepherd named David, promised after David died

**HOOK:** Why did God promise "my servant David" long after David was dead?

**THE VERSES**
- 1 Samuel 16:4: "And the elders of the town trembled at his coming."
- 1 Samuel 16:11: "There remaineth yet the youngest, and, behold, he keepeth the sheep."
- Ezekiel 34:23: "And I will set up one shepherd over them, and he shall feed them, even my servant David."
- John 10:11: "I am the good shepherd: the good shepherd giveth his life for the sheep."

**KIND OF LINK:** TYPE-ECHO. Ezekiel's promise plainly points to a future "David." Jesus calls himself the Good Shepherd, but he does not quote Ezekiel 34:23 by name.

**WHAT IT DOES NOT PROVE:** Do not say John 10 "fulfills" Ezekiel 34:23 word for word. Keep it as "Ezekiel promises it, and Jesus claims the role."

**WHY MOST HAVEN'T HEARD IT:** People know David the shepherd boy, but the strange timing of Ezekiel (written after David's death) is rarely pointed out. **Freshness: 6/10**

**HOW IT LANDS ON JESUS:** God promised a shepherd "David" after David was long dead, and the true one was born in David's shepherd town.

**CONFIDENCE:** 8/10

---

**MY TOP PICK = The water David would not drink.** It is a vivid, simple story set in Bethlehem that people have almost never heard. David himself calls the water "blood," so it lands on the cup of Jesus without forcing the text.
