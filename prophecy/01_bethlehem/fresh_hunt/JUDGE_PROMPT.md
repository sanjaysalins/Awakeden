# The angles (order is random; no authors shown)
Every quote below is checked word-for-word against the KJV. Hebrew notes are standard lexicon facts with their honest limits.

## ANGLE 1 - The town in the "no king" days
Judges 17-21 is Israel's darkest stretch, and the refrain is "In those days there was no king in Israel" (Judges 17:6; 21:25). Both of its stories begin with a man from Bethlehem-judah (Judges 17:7; 19:1). The very next book, Ruth, opens in Bethlehem and ends with "Jesse begat David" - the king. Later, Jesus is born there.
Link kind claimed: TYPE-ECHO (a pattern in the book order; the NT does not state it). Risk: Judges 19 is violent.

## ANGLE 2 - Son of my sorrow, son of the right hand
The Bible's first scene at Bethlehem is a birth and a death. Rachel dies in hard labour "in the way to Ephrath, which is Bethlehem" (Genesis 35:16-19). Dying, she names the boy Benoni; his father renames him Benjamin. (Ben-oni is usually read "son of my sorrow" - some read "son of my strength/vigour"; Benjamin = "son of the right hand".) Jeremiah 31:15 hears "Rahel weeping for her children"; Matthew 2:17-18 says that was fulfilled at Bethlehem when Herod killed the boys. Jesus: "a man of sorrows" (Isaiah 53:3) who "sat on the right hand of God" (Mark 16:19).
Link kind claimed: Rachel weeping = NT-stated fulfilment (Matthew 2:17-18). The two names -> Jesus = TYPE-ECHO only.

## ANGLE 3 - The water David would not drink
David, hiding from the Philistines who held Bethlehem, longs for "the water of the well of Bethlehem, which is by the gate". Three men break through the enemy and bring it. He will not drink: he pours it out unto the LORD - "is not this the blood of the men that went in jeopardy of their lives?" (2 Samuel 23:14-17). Jesus, born in that town: "this is my blood ... shed for many" and "Drink ye all of it" (Matthew 26:27-28); "If any man thirst, let him come unto me, and drink" (John 7:37).
Link kind claimed: TYPE-ECHO (a contrast; the NT never mentions the well).

## ANGLE 4 - They used Bethlehem to reject Him
Thirty years after the birth, a crowd argues about Jesus: "Shall Christ come out of Galilee? Hath not the scripture said, That Christ cometh of the seed of David, and out of the town of Bethlehem, where David was? So there was a division among the people because of him." (John 7:41-43). They had the right verse and the wrong assumption - He WAS born in Bethlehem (Matthew 2:1). Micah's same verse also says His goings forth are "from everlasting".
Link kind claimed: NT-CONFIRMED (John records the crowd using Micah 5:2). John leaves the irony unsaid.

## ANGLE 5 - The half of Micah 5:2 nobody quotes
Micah 5:2 ends: "whose goings forth have been from of old, from everlasting." When the Bible experts quote the verse to Herod (Matthew 2:6) the quote stops before that line. So the baby born in the small town is said to have been going forth "from everlasting" - He did not begin at Bethlehem, He arrived there. Compare "Before Abraham was, I am" (John 8:58).
Link kind claimed: place = DIRECT PROPHECY; "from everlasting" -> pre-existence = NT doctrine (John 1, 8), but the NT does not quote this half. Note: some read "from of old" as an ancient family line. Do not claim a motive for the shorter quote.

## ANGLE 6 - Read the next verses
Micah does not stop at the birthplace. The same ruler "shall stand and feed in the strength of the LORD ... for now shall he be great unto the ends of the earth. And this man shall be the peace" (Micah 5:4-5). Jesus: "I am the good shepherd" (John 10:11); "For he is our peace" (Ephesians 2:14).
Link kind claimed: verse 2 = DIRECT PROPHECY; verses 4-5 same sentence about the same ruler; the NT does not quote 4-5 by number.

## ANGLE 7 - The empty outsider who came home to Bethlehem
Naomi: "I went out full, and the LORD hath brought me home again empty" (Ruth 1:21). In Bethlehem, Boaz pays to redeem the land and takes Ruth the Moabitess, a foreigner (Ruth 4:9-10). The town says "be famous in Bethlehem" (4:11); the son is grandfather of David (4:17). Matthew 1:5 puts Ruth in Jesus' family line.
Link kind claimed: Ruth in the line = NT-CONFIRMED; Boaz as a picture of Christ the redeemer = TYPE-ECHO.

## ANGLE 8 - The tower of the flock
Right after Rachel's burial near Bethlehem, "Israel journeyed, and spread his tent beyond the tower of Edar" (Genesis 35:21; Edar = "flock"). One chapter before the Bethlehem verse, Micah says: "And thou, O tower of the flock ... unto thee shall it come, even the first dominion" (Micah 4:8). The first to hear of Jesus' birth were "shepherds abiding in the field, keeping watch over their flock" (Luke 2:8).
Link kind claimed: TYPE-ECHO. Micah 4:8 most naturally speaks of Zion/Jerusalem; the shepherds-at-the-tower and "temple lambs" ideas come from later tradition, NOT the Bible.

## ANGLE 9 - Found in the fields of Ephratah
Psalm 132:5-6: David vows "Until I find out a place for the LORD, an habitation for the mighty God of Jacob. Lo, we heard of it at Ephratah: we found it in the fields of the wood." Ephratah is Bethlehem's old name (Micah 5:2). The Word "dwelt among us" was found by shepherds in Bethlehem's fields.
Link kind claimed: TYPE-ECHO. Note: many read Ephratah here differently (the ark's story points to Kirjath-jearim, "the fields of the wood"); the Bethlehem reading is debated.

## ANGLE 10 - A shepherd named David, promised after David died
In Bethlehem, Samuel anoints the youngest son, the one who "keepeth the sheep" (1 Samuel 16:1, 11-13). Centuries after David died, God promises: "I will set up one shepherd over them, and he shall feed them, even my servant David" (Ezekiel 34:23). Jesus: "I am the good shepherd" (John 10:11).
Link kind claimed: TYPE-ECHO / prophecy of a coming "David"; Jesus claims the shepherd role but does not quote Ezekiel 34:23.

## ANGLE 11 - The weeping verse sits in a chapter of hope
Matthew 2:16-18 quotes Jeremiah 31:15 ("Rahel weeping for her children") over Herod's killing of the boys of Bethlehem. Read on in that same chapter: "Refrain thy voice from weeping ... there is hope in thine end" (Jeremiah 31:16-17), and then "Behold, the days come, saith the LORD, that I will make a new covenant" (31:31). Hebrews 8:8 quotes that new-covenant promise about Jesus; at the supper Jesus says "This cup is the new testament in my blood, which is shed for you" (Luke 22:20).
Link kind claimed: NT-CONFIRMED (Matthew quotes the lament; Hebrews quotes the covenant from the same chapter). Limit: Matthew does not say he means the whole chapter; Jeremiah's "come again" is about exile return, not the murdered boys coming back.

## ANGLE 12 - Two kings, two anointings, one fear
God sends Samuel to Bethlehem: "I have provided me a king among his sons." Samuel answers, "How can I go? if Saul hear it, he will kill me" (1 Samuel 16:1-2). A thousand years later wise men ask "Where is he that is born King of the Jews?" and King Herod "was troubled" (Matthew 2:2-3) - and he does kill, in Bethlehem (2:16). Both times, God's chosen king arrives in Bethlehem while the king on the throne wants him dead.
Link kind claimed: TYPE-ECHO (a pattern; Matthew does not call Herod a new Saul; Saul did not massacre Bethlehem).
