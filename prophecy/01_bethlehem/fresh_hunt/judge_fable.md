## One line each, with flags

1. **FACT ERROR.** Judges 19:1 does not begin with a man from Bethlehem. The Levite is from mount Ephraim; the *concubine* is from Bethlehemjudah. Only 17:7 has a Bethlehem man. Also "the very next book" is true in the English order only; the Hebrew order puts Ruth elsewhere. A book-order pattern is the weakest kind of link. Judges 19 is not for a 60 s global short.
2. Rachel weeping is NT-stated (Matthew 2:17-18) and honest. The two names to Jesus is a stretch: Ben-oni's meaning is disputed (the angle admits it), and Isaiah 53:3 + Mark 16:19 are joined by me, not by the text. Overclaim risk if spoken as a prophecy. Needs a whole new story.
3. Lovely and truly fresh. But it is a contrast the NT never draws, and it is hard to say fast: David refuses to drink "blood", Jesus says "drink". A newcomer may hear a clash, not a picture. It is its own short, not a fold-in.
4. Strong. All of it is in John 7:41-43, and Matthew 2:1 settles the fact. Label slightly generous: John confirms the *expectation*, the irony is unsaid (the angle admits it). It is a second "they knew and still missed Him". Cost: a new scene 30 years later, new pictures, about 25 words.
5. **Best.** Both halves are in the KJV text given. Matthew 2:6 does stop before "from of old, from everlasting". Label is honest. Care needed: (a) never say the experts *hid* or *cut* it. Matthew's quote is a loose rendering all through ("art not the least" vs "though thou be little"), so the short ending proves no motive. (b) "from of old" can be read as an ancient family line; quote the words, do not explain the Hebrew. (c) Do not say He "began" in eternity; say He did not begin in Bethlehem.
6. **Claim wrong:** verses 4-5 are *not* "the same sentence" as verse 2. Verse 3 sits between, and verse 5 runs straight on into "when the Assyrian shall come into our land". The same ruler, yes. "This man shall be the peace" to Ephesians 2:14 is an echo, not a quote. Usable only as a light touch.
7. True and sound, but told very often. The title blends two people: Naomi is the empty one, Ruth is the outsider. It is its own film.
8. Micah 4:8 names Zion and Jerusalem in the verse itself. The link to Luke's shepherds is not in the Bible. The label is honest about that, which is the reason to drop it.
9. In Psalm 132 the thing "found" is the ark, and the plain reading points to Kirjath-jearim. A debated reading carrying a type is two stretches stacked.
10. Ezekiel 34:23 is a real promise and John 10:11 lands well. But Ezekiel does not name Bethlehem, so the Bethlehem tie comes only through 1 Samuel 16. Better as support for another angle than as the insight.
11. True, and the Hebrews 8:8 link is NT-drawn. But it needs the killing of the boys, the exile limit, and the new covenant in one minute. Too dark and too many steps for this film. Good as a separate short.
12. Real pattern, clear picture, and Herod is already in the film. But: the title says "two anointings" and Jesus is not anointed at Bethlehem; Saul's threat is Samuel's fear, not an act; Matthew draws no such link. It lands on Herod, not on Jesus.

## TOP PICK: Angle 5 - rewrite of changed lines only

Why it fits: the film already says "But Jesus came much farther. He came down from heaven." Angle 5 gives that line a verse. Micah's own sentence says it. The spine does not move.

**Change 1**
- OLD: God promised a King from there. Micah called Him [prophet voice:] "ruler in Israel."
- NEW: God promised a King from there: [prophet voice:] "ruler in Israel."

**Change 2**
- OLD: Mary and Joseph lived far away. A tax order sent them to Bethlehem. There, God's promise came true: Jesus, the King, was born.
- NEW: A tax order sent Mary and Joseph to Bethlehem. There Jesus, the King, was born.

**Change 3**
- OLD: Herod, the king there, asked the experts.
- NEW: King Herod asked the experts.

**Change 4 (the new insight)**
- OLD: But Jesus came much farther. He came down from heaven. He died for our sins. On the third day, He rose from the dead.
- NEW: But the same verse says more: He comes [prophet voice:] "from of old, from everlasting." He did not begin in Bethlehem. Jesus came much farther. He came down from heaven. He died for our sins. On the third day, He rose.

**Unchanged:** the hook, the Micah intro line, the wise men line, the scribe quote, "Bethlehem was close by. The Bible never says the experts went. The wise men went, and found the child.", and the last line "Before anyone went to Him, He came to us."

## MISSING (optional)

- **The experts' answer hides a second verse.** Matthew 2:6 ends "that shall rule my people Israel", which is not in Micah 5:2. It echoes God's word to David: "Thou shalt feed my people Israel" (2 Samuel 5:2). So the answer given to Herod already points to a shepherd-king like David. Echo in the wording, not a named quote.
- **The shepherds ran.** "Let us now go even unto Bethlehem ... And they came with haste" (Luke 2:15-16). Plain NT text, and it sharpens "did they go?" at almost no cost. Not very fresh, but it is free.
