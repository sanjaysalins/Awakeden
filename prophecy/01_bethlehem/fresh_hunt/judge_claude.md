**Notes on the flags and close calls:**

- **Angle 1:** The claim "both stories begin with a man from Bethlehem" is half wrong. In Judges 19:1 the Levite lives on mount Ephraim. Only his concubine is "out of Bethlehemjudah." The book-order pattern is something the text allows, not something it says. Judges 19 is too violent for this audience.
- **Angle 2:** Ben-oni has two possible meanings, and you can't put that caveat into a Short. The name-to-Jesus link (man of sorrows, right hand) is a word-play stretch. 1 Samuel 10:2 puts Rachel's tomb near Benjamin's border, which muddies "at Bethlehem."
- **Angle 3:** The quotes are accurate. A side benefit: three men broke through an army to reach Bethlehem, while the experts would not walk a short road. That echoes "did they go?" The link to Jesus' blood is a contrast only. The NT never makes it.
- **Angle 4:** The quote is exact and the NT records it. Don't say "thirty years." John 7 is about 32 or more years after the birth, so say "years later." Don't claim John points out the irony. Also say "some." Others in the same crowd said "This is the Christ."
- **Angle 5:** "From of old" can mean an ancient family line. Matthew 2:6 is a loose quote that also blends in 2 Samuel 5:2. Never claim the experts cut the line on purpose.
- **Angle 6:** "This man shall be the peace" sits inside an Assyria setting. Use it lightly.
- **Angle 8:** Micah 4:8 is about Zion, not Bethlehem. The Edar-to-shepherds link is later tradition.
- **Angle 9:** Most scholars read this psalm as pointing to Kirjath-jearim, not Bethlehem.
- **Angle 11:** It invites the reading "the boys will come back," which is false. It also brings in the killing of the boys, which this film skips.
- **Angle 12:** The comparison is honest if you don't make Saul a Herod. It adds danger the film doesn't need.

## Top pick: Angle 4 (with a light touch of 5 optional)

It extends the spine instead of adding a side trip. The experts knew the verse and did not go. Later, people quoted the same verse to their face, and still turned away. Then "He came to us" hits harder.

**Changed lines (old → new):**

1. **Micah intro**
   - Old: "About seven hundred years earlier, a prophet called Micah, a man who spoke God's message, named a small town: Bethlehem."
   - New: "Seven hundred years earlier, God's prophet Micah named a small town: Bethlehem."
2. **Birth**
   - Old: "Mary and Joseph lived far away. A tax order sent them to Bethlehem. There, God's promise came true: Jesus, the King, was born."
   - New: "A tax order took Mary and Joseph there. God's promise came true: Jesus, the King, was born."
3. **Wise men**
   - Old: "Wise men reached Jerusalem, asking where this King was. Herod, the king there, asked the experts."
   - New: "Wise men asked in Jerusalem where this King was. King Herod asked the experts."
4. **New lines, inserted after "...and found the child."**
   - "Years later, Jesus stood in front of a crowd. Some said, [crowd voice:] 'Shall Christ come out of Galilee?' They quoted the Bethlehem verse against Him, not knowing He was born there."

**Words:** about +27 and −16, so roughly 150. Time it before anything else. If it runs over 60 s, cut "Bethlehem was close by" down to "It was close." Do not cut the gospel lines.

**Pictures:** one new shot (adult Jesus, a crowd arguing in the temple courts). It needs one new crowd voice line, which is a paid step and needs its own ask. Shots 2–3 just get shorter.

## My top 3

1. **Angle 4.** The NT itself records it. It is true, fresh, and the same idea as the spine.
2. **Angle 5.** It gives the "He came down from heaven" line a real anchor in the text. It needs a light hedge.
3. **Angle 3.** It is vivid and fits "did they go?" But the link to Jesus is only a contrast.

## Reject outright: Angle 8

Its main claim rests on tradition, not the text, and Micah 4:8 is about Jerusalem. A 60-second film can't carry the caveat, so viewers would leave believing a myth. Angle 9 is nearly as bad.

## Missing angle

"The city of David" in the Old Testament is Jerusalem/Zion (2 Samuel 5:7, 9). Luke gives that title to Bethlehem instead (Luke 2:4, 11: "born this day in the city of David a Saviour"). The NT itself says it, it's fresh, and it's sound.
