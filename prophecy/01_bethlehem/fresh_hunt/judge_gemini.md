**TOP PICK: ANGLE 5**
It weaponizes the exact elements already in your script (Micah, the experts, Jesus coming from heaven) to deliver a massive, undeniable text-based payoff without adding a new storyline.

**THE REWRITE (Old -> New)**
*OLD (44 words):*
> Herod, the king there, asked the experts. [scribe voice:] "In Bethlehem of Judaea." / Bethlehem was close by. The Bible never says the experts went. The wise men went, and found the child. / But Jesus came much farther. He came down from heaven.

*NEW (47 words):*
> Herod asked the experts. [scribe voice:] "In Bethlehem of Judaea." / They stopped quoting halfway. Micah also called this King "from everlasting." / Bethlehem was close by, but the experts never went. The wise men found the child. / Jesus didn't begin at Bethlehem. He came down from heaven.

**PICTURE CHANGES:**
Almost none. The visual of the experts answering Herod (or looking at the scroll) just needs to hold for 2–3 more seconds to cover the "stopped quoting halfway" line. You could optionally animate the text on the scroll fading out halfway, but no new scenes are required.

1. **Angle 5 (The half nobody quotes):** Perfectly integrates into your existing spine, exposing a blind spot in the experts' reading while elevating the ending (He came from heaven).

**REJECT OUTRIGHT**
**Angle 8 (The tower of the flock).** It violates your core rule ("surprising about the TEXT"). The angle itself admits that the shepherds-at-the-tower and "temple lambs" concepts come from later tradition, not the Bible. Freshness never excuses inserting extra-biblical lore into a "Bible reading" channel.

**MISSING ANGLE**
**The Bread of Life in the House of Bread:** Bethlehem literally translates to "House of Bread," the exact town where the "bread of life" (John 6:35) was born and laid in a feeding trough / manger (Luke 2:7).
