## 1. WHO IS THIS FOR
A phone viewer who likes a small mystery, such as a commuter who is curious but knows no Bible. The opening question (lines 1-2, "Did they go?") stops me. A person who already half-knows the story finishes it and shares it. I am not served in the second half. Lines 12-13 use words I do not know. Line 6 gives me "Jesus" with no introduction. A viewer with no religion at all may feel the end is a sermon.

## 2. MAIN TAKEAWAY
"A prophet promised a King's birthplace 700 years early. The experts knew it and did not go, but strangers did, and the King came to us first." Line 13 carries it ("Before anyone went to Him, He came to us"). It is only partly clear. I understood "experts did not go, wise men did." I did not understand why that matters.

## 3. GLOBAL AUDIENCE
Who: scholars, a prophet "Micah", Mary, Joseph, a baby, a King Herod, wise men, and "Jesus". What: they knew where a King would be born, and nobody walked 6 short miles. Why: unclear.
Where I got lost:
- Line 1 "the King". Which king? The film never says the baby Jesus IS this King. I guessed it by line 7.
- Line 6 "There, Jesus was born." I do not know who Jesus is. It is also unclear whether Herod and the "one born King" are rivals.
- Line 3 "a man who spoke God's message". I accept this, but "God" is not explained either.
- Lines 4-5 are split between two voices ("Out of it, he said, a ruler would come, / that is to be ruler in Israel"). On a phone this sounds like a stumble. "Israel" is unexplained.
- Line 8 "Judaea" and line 12 "died for our sins", "rose again the third day". All are unexplained and carry the ending.
- "KEPT" and "PROMISED, THEN KEPT" are good plain words. The on-screen "ABOUT 100 ... 700" is confusing.
- Line 7 shifts quickly from the baby to wise men "asking". I did not know why they came.
- Words that fail in translation: "experts", "tax order", "scribe", "Judaea", "sins".

## 4. BIBLICALLY ACCURATE
I cannot judge accuracy. What I was told that I could not follow: why a baby is a "King"; what "sins" are; why dying and rising matter; why "Israel" matters; the link between line 12 and the earlier story.

## 5. BEST VERSION POSSIBLE
The best explainer would say plainly, early: "Jesus is the King they were waiting for." It would also say one sentence on why the cross matters. Highest-value change: add one clear line at 17.8 s that names Jesus as the promised King. Score: 6/10.

## CURIOSITY GAP
Opens at line 2 ("Did they go?"). Pays off at lines 9-10 ("The Bible never says the experts went"), then 11. Score 7/10. The wait is worth it. The payoff is an absence ("never says"), which is clever but quiet. Line 12 then moves to a different question.

## PROMISE KEPT
A prophet named Micah promised, about 700 years before, that a ruler would come from Bethlehem. It came true when a baby was born there. The "KEPT" stamp and the end card helped. But after line 9 the film turns to "who went", and the promise feels secondary. Score 6/10. It feels more like a story about who went than a promise kept.

## THREE SMALLEST FIXES
1. Line 4-5: one voice, one sentence: "He said: a ruler will come out of Bethlehem, to lead God's people."
2. Line 6: "Jesus, the promised King, was born."
3. Line 12: "He came down from heaven. He died to take our wrongs. He came back to life."
