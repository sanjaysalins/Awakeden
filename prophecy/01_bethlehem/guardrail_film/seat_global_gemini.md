## 1. WHO IS THIS FOR
This is for a young, non-Western mobile viewer with basic or no prior Bible knowledge. The map and simple terms serve a global viewer who does not know Middle Eastern geography. It stops the scroll for people intrigued by irony (experts who know but don't act). It does NOT serve insiders expecting traditional "Christmas story" language (like "decree from Caesar Augustus").

## 2. MAIN TAKEAWAY
Even when religious experts wouldn't walk a short distance to see the King, Jesus crossed the distance from heaven to reach us. Line 13 carries this: "Before anyone went to Him, He came to us." The film makes it visually and verbally clear.

## 3. GLOBAL AUDIENCE
The script does a brilliant job bypassing heavy insider words. "Tax order" (Line 6) and "Bible experts" (Line 1) translate easily across all languages and cultures, avoiding the cultural baggage of "census" or "Pharisees/scribes." 

However, there are translation traps:
*   Line 5: "that is to be ruler in Israel." The grammar "that is to be" is archaic. Auto-captions and machine translators often stumble here, confusing "that" with an object rather than a person. 
*   Between Lines 6 and 7 (On-screen): The red stamp says "KEPT ✓". In English, "keeping a promise" is a common idiom. In many other languages (Spanish, Mandarin, Arabic), "kept" translates literally to "stored in a box" or "retained," making no sense here. 
*   Line 7: "came asking for the one born King." The phrasing "the one born King" is passive and grammatically complex for ESL readers.
*   Line 12: "Jesus had come much farther." This translates well, relying on physical distance to explain spiritual distance, a universally understood metaphor.

## 4. BIBLICALLY ACCURATE
*   RIGHT: "The Bible experts knew where the King would be born." (Line 1) Supported by Matthew 2:4-5.
*   RIGHT: "...named a small town: Bethlehem." (Line 3) Supported by Micah 5:2.
*   RIGHT: "A tax order sent them to Bethlehem." (Line 6) Supported by Luke 2:1-4.
*   RIGHT: "The Bible never says the experts went." (Line 10) Supported by the omission in Matthew 2; only the wise men are recorded leaving Herod to go to Bethlehem.
*   RIGHT: "He came down from heaven. He died for our sins. He rose again..." (Line 12). Supported by John 6:38 and 1 Cor 15:3-4.

## 5. BEST VERSION POSSIBLE
The best explainer in the world would highlight exactly *how short* the distance was for the experts, to heighten the global contrast. "Close by" is relative. A 5-mile (or 8-kilometer) walk is universally understood as a trivial distance, making the scholars' apathy shocking compared to the camels traveling from "the east" or Jesus coming "from heaven." 
Score: 8/10.

## CURIOSITY GAP
Opens at line 2 ("Did they go?"), pays off at line 10 ("The Bible never says the experts went."). 
Score: 9/10. Yes, the payoff is worth the wait because it subverts the expectation that religious leaders would be the first to greet their King. 

## PROMISE KEPT
God made a promise through Micah 700 years earlier that a ruler would come from Bethlehem. It came true precisely because a foreign "tax order" forced a couple to travel there. The video effectively shows God maneuvering empires to keep His word. However, the emotional climax is less about the prophecy being fulfilled and more about the irony of *who* showed up to witness it. 
Score: 8/10.

## THREE SMALLEST FIXES
1. Line 5: Change `that is to be ruler` to `who will be ruler`
2. Line 7 (On-screen): Change the red stamp `KEPT ✓` to `CAME TRUE ✓`
3. Line 9: Change `Bethlehem was close by.` to `Bethlehem was only five miles away.`
