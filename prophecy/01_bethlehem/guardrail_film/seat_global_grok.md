## 1. WHO IS THIS FOR

Luis, 19, in Mexico City, on a phone with auto-captions. English is school English. He knows the name Jesus. He does not know Micah, Herod, or Judaea.

He can finish. The map, the camels, the baby, and the empty road carry him. He shares line 13 only if he already trusts church talk. "He came to us" sounds like a sermon ending.

He stops at line 12 if he stayed for "Did they go?" Crosses and "our sins" open a second film.

Poorly served: anyone who cannot read Latin letters (every baked phrase is English); a viewer in East Asia who hears "the east" as home; a Jewish viewer who reads prayer shawls plus hollow prints as blame.

## 2. MAIN TAKEAWAY

"The teachers knew the town, the Bible never says they went, and Jesus came to us first."

Line 13 carries it, with lines 10 and 11. That travel point is clear. The promise point is a stamp. A viewer can miss "God said a king would come, 700 years before."

## 3. GLOBAL AUDIENCE

Who / what / why, as I can retell it: Scholars in Jerusalem know the birth town. Micah, God's messenger, had already said Bethlehem. Mary and Joseph come from Nazareth because of a tax rule. Jesus is born. Men on camels come asking for the new king. Herod asks the scholars. Bethlehem is near. The Bible never says those scholars walked there. The camel men find the child. Then Jesus has come from heaven, died, and lived again.

Lost and misled:

- Line 4, "Out of it": "it" has no clear noun. Captions cannot repair a dangling "it."
- Lines 4–5, "a ruler" / "that is to be ruler": "ruler" is also the school stick. "Is to be" is old grammar. Translation often breaks it.
- Line 3, "a prophet called Micah" and "named a small town": "called" also means phoned. "Named" can mean he gave the town its name.
- On screen, "ABOUT 100 ... 700 YEARS EARLIER": one glance reads 100. Speech says seven hundred.
- On screen, "A PROPHECY": often becomes "fortune-telling." Speech explains "prophet" and leaves this stamp bare.
- "KEPT ✓": in school a check means "right answer," so the stamp can mean "this fact is correct."
- Line 7 and the tag "the east": east of Jerusalem. In Asia, "the east" is us. Line 1's "King" and line 7's "King Herod" are two kings.
- Line 8, "In Bethlehem of Judaea": rare name; captions mangle it. The map labels Jerusalem later, and never labels Judaea. On-screen "A SCRIBE" fights the spoken word "experts." "MICAH 5:2" looks like a clock or a score.
- Line 12: "much farther" sounds like a longer road. "rose again" is a flower. "the third day" never says after the death. "for our sins" is creed language. This shot has no verse tag. The birth shots do.

Universal, and they work with no English: hollow prints, the music stop, gold prints, camels, a baby, open arms.

Insider: prayer shawls, chapter:verse tags, stable animals, three crosses, "us."

## 4. BIBLICALLY ACCURATE

- Line 1: **RIGHT.** Matthew 2:4–6. They can name the place when asked. The film puts that knowledge first, as a hook.
- Line 3: **RIGHT.** About 700 years, Micah, Bethlehem.
- Lines 4–5: **RIGHT** as a short Micah 5:2. Hard English. The claim matches the verse.
- Line 6: **RIGHT** as a short Luke 2. "Tax order" thins the census decree. "Far away" fits Nazareth, and the map shows it.
- Lines 7–8: **RIGHT.** Matthew 2:1–5, including "In Bethlehem of Judaea."
- Line 9: **RIGHT** as geography, Bethlehem near Jerusalem. The sentence is the film's, backed by the map.
- Line 10: **RIGHT.** The Bible does not say they went. The stern face and hollow prints add a motive the text does not give.
- Line 11: **RIGHT** that they found the child (Matthew 2:11, a house). The stable-and-camels picture invites "same night, at the manger."
- Line 12: **RIGHT** as John 6:38 and 1 Corinthians 15:3–4. Those are later claims. Micah's on-screen quote promises a ruler in Israel. The last stamp can fuse two promises into one.
- Line 13: sermon line. The order holds: he came, then people traveled to him.

## 5. BEST VERSION POSSIBLE

Keep the footprints. Speak one promise a caption can carry: who promised, what, how long before, what came true. One plain "king." Tie "east" to Jerusalem on the map. Give the cross its own short sentence and a visible verse. Keep "the Bible never says." Let the face stop delivering a verdict.

The one change: on the voice and on the scroll, lines 4–5 become "From Bethlehem, he said, a king would come to rule Israel." Then "KEPT" has a meaning.

**6/10.** The journey is visible. The hinge sentence and the closing creed fail in translation.

## CURIOSITY GAP

Opens at line 2. Pays off at line 10, with the contrast at line 11. **7/10.** Worth the wait: empty road, then gold road, works in weak English. "Ruler," "prophecy," and the counting number can lose people in the middle. Line 12 starts a new subject after the question is answered.

## PROMISE KEPT

God, through Micah, about 700 years before, said a king would come from Bethlehem. Jesus was born there. The map and the star show that match. **6/10** as a felt promise. The stronger feeling is who went and who did not. Nobody says "promise" out loud. "A PROPHECY," "KEPT ✓," and "PROMISED, THEN KEPT" are three codes. Death and rising, in line 12, are a second promise, absent from the Micah quote.

## THREE SMALLEST FIXES

1. Line 4: "Out of it, he said, a ruler would come," → "From Bethlehem, he said, a king would come,"
2. Line 5: "that is to be ruler in Israel." → "a king who would rule Israel."
3. Line 12: "He rose again the third day." → "Three days after he died, he lived again."
