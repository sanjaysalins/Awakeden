## 1. WHO IS THIS FOR
**Named viewer:** Priya, 19, grew up going to church at Christmas but has never read Matthew 2.
- **Who stops:** line 2, "Did they go?", is a good human hook.
- **Who finishes:** casual Christians and the curious.
- **Who shares:** youth leaders around Advent.
- **Not served:** a Jewish viewer. The stern scholars in prayer shawls are set against Gentile wise men. That can read as "the Jews ignored Him", which goes further than the text (see line 10).

## 2. MAIN TAKEAWAY
"God promised the town 700 years early, kept it, and came to us before we came to Him." Line 13 carries the last half. The film makes "who went" clear. It makes "promise kept" clear mostly through the stamps on screen, not the spoken words.

## 3. GLOBAL AUDIENCE
- **Who / what / why:** A prophet named the town. Jesus was born there because of a tax order. The experts knew where, but only the foreigners went. Jesus came even farther, from heaven.
- **Lost points:**
  - Lines 4-5, "a ruler would come, / that is to be ruler in Israel", say "ruler" twice. A second-language listener hears an echo, not new meaning.
  - "Wise men from the east" (line 7): east of where?
  - Line 12, "rose again the third day": the third day after what?
  - The three crosses are never explained. Which one is Jesus?
- **Translation:** "Bible experts" works well. "Tax order" works.

## 4. BIBLICALLY ACCURATE
- **Line 1** "experts knew where the King would be born": **RIGHT.** Matt 2:4-5 says the chief priests and scribes answered "In Bethlehem of Judaea." (Herod asked where "Christ" would be born. Close enough.)
- **Line 3** "About seven hundred years earlier": **UNSUPPORTED** by the KJV supplied, but it is the standard dating (Micah 1:1). "Named a small town" is **RIGHT**: "though thou be little among the thousands of Judah" (Mic 5:2).
- **Lines 4-5** "a ruler... that is to be ruler in Israel": **RIGHT**, verbatim from Mic 5:2.
- **Line 6** "A tax order sent them to Bethlehem. There, Jesus was born": **RIGHT** (Luke 2:1-7). "Lived far away" is fair: they went from Galilee to Judaea (Luke 2:4).
- **Line 7**: **RIGHT** (Matt 2:1-2, 2:4).
- **Line 8** "In Bethlehem of Judaea": **RIGHT**, verbatim from Matt 2:5.
- **Line 9** "Bethlehem was close by": **UNSUPPORTED** by the supplied text, but true geography (about 9 km). Matt 2:8-9 implies a short trip.
- **Line 10** "The Bible never says the experts went": **RIGHT** as worded. The pictures **OVERCLAIM** it: "Nobody walks it", the music stops, the stern finger. Silence in the text is not refusal. Matt 2:3 says Herod "was troubled, and all Jerusalem with him." That fear is the real textual point, and it is stronger.
- **Line 11**: **RIGHT** (Matt 2:9-11, "the young child with Mary his mother").
- **Line 12**: **RIGHT** (John 6:38; 1 Cor 15:3-4). The film drops "according to the scriptures", the one phrase that fits a prophecy film best.
- **Visual, 22.7 s: OVERCLAIMED.** The stable, the ox and the donkey share a frame with the wise men. Matthew puts the wise men in "the house" with "the young child" (2:11). The ox and donkey are tradition, not Luke.
- **Line 13**: theologically sound. It goes beyond the supplied text, but no text contradicts it.

**Church group?** Yes, after fixing the visuals around line 10.

## 5. BEST VERSION POSSIBLE
The best explainer would let Micah's own words pay off the twist. Mic 5:2 says "whose goings forth have been from of old, from everlasting." The film cuts exactly the clause that makes line 12 ("had come much farther") a promise kept and not just an add-on.

**ONE change:** the prophet voice speaks the "from everlasting" clause.

**Score: 6.5/10.**

## CURIOSITY GAP
- Opens at line 2. Half pays off at line 10 (soft, an argument from silence). Turns at line 13.
- **Score: 6/10.** Worth the wait, but line 10 lands as a shrug, not a reveal.

## PROMISE KEPT
Micah promised, about 700 years early, that a ruler would come out of little Bethlehem, one whose origins were "from everlasting." A Roman tax, which no believer arranged, moved Mary and Joseph there, and Jesus was born in that town. The film shows this but doesn't say it. The spoken words spend their time on "who went". The stamps (KEPT ✓, PROMISED, THEN KEPT) do the promise work.

**Score: 6/10.**
