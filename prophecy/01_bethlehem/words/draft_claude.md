**(a) Script**

[narrator] People once said Jesus could not be the Christ, the promised King. Because of His hometown.
[crowd] Shall Christ come out of Galilee?
[narrator] About seven hundred years earlier, Micah, a prophet (a man who spoke God's message), named one small town.
[prophet] But thou, Bethlehem… out of thee shall he come forth.
[narrator] Jesus's family lived in Nazareth, in Galilee. But a Roman tax order sent Joseph and pregnant Mary to Bethlehem.
[angel] For unto you is born this day in the city of David a Saviour.
[narrator] The crowd knew the promise. They did not know where He was born.
(pause)
[prophet] From of old, from everlasting.
[narrator] So where is Jesus from? He existed before He was born. He came to Bethlehem for us, died for our sins, and rose the third day.

**(c) KJV fragments**
- "Shall Christ come out of Galilee?" (John 7:41)
- "But thou, Bethlehem… out of thee shall he come forth" (Micah 5:2)
- "For unto you is born this day in the city of David a Saviour" (Luke 2:11)
- "from of old, from everlasting" (Micah 5:2)
- The tax order, Nazareth/Galilee and "great with child" are paraphrased (Luke 2:1-5). "Died for our sins… rose the third day" is paraphrased (1 Corinthians 15:3-4).

**(d) Four lines**
- **Who it is for:** Newcomers who don't know the story, and Bible readers who think they already know Christmas.
- **Main take-away:** God named the small town long before, and kept that promise through a tax order. And the baby born there existed before He was born.
- **The curiosity gap:** "They said He couldn't be the King because of His hometown." So where was He really from? The film answers it twice: Bethlehem, and "from everlasting."
- **Why a global newcomer gets it:** One question runs through it all ("where is He from?"). Every old word has plain English next to it, and the ending answers the same question the opening asked.
