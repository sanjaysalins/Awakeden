# Write ONE narration from scratch - "Born in Bethlehem" (Micah 5:2 -> Matthew 2, Luke 2). You are ONE blind writer; you have seen no other draft.

## The series
"Promised, then kept": an Old Testament promise, and how it came true in Jesus. Every episode lands on Jesus.

## Who it is for
Worldwide, many with English as a second language, many with no Bible knowledge, watching on a phone, often with the sound low. Also Bible readers who think they know the Christmas story. Released before Christmas.

## Length (hard)
UNDER 60 SECONDS of voice: 100-125 spoken words total, including quotes. Vertical short.

## THE TWO JOBS THAT MATTER MOST (the last version failed both)
1. THE HOOK. The first sentence, spoken inside the first 2 seconds, must open a curiosity gap a stranger MUST close by watching. It must be a real question the viewer feels, not a label. The viewer will likely see the title "Born in Bethlehem" or know Jesus was born in Bethlehem, so "which town?" is NOT a gap. Test: could a newcomer say in their own words what they are waiting to find out?
2. THE LANDING. The last 2-3 lines must PAY the exact gap the hook opened, out loud, in plain words, AND land on Jesus: that He died for our sins and rose the third day (1 Cor 15:3-4: "Christ died for our sins according to the scriptures; And that he was buried, and that he rose again the third day according to the scriptures"). The ending must feel like the same film as the hook, not a second idea or a tract bolted on. Grace, not pressure. The last line should be one a viewer could repeat to a friend.

## What reviewers said about the last version (do not repeat these faults)
- The hook's sting came too late (after 3 seconds) and the title already gave away the town.
- The gap was "they knew - did they go?" but nobody ever said the payoff aloud; it was only hinted.
- The ending jumped to a new idea ("from everlasting", then cross and tomb in 5 seconds) and felt like a second film.
- Old KJV words ("thou, thee, come forth unto me") lost non-native viewers for 7 seconds. "From everlasting" was misread as "forever from now on".
- Small overclaims: Micah says the ruler's "goings forth have been from of old, from everlasting" - not "this ruler is everlasting". The visitors of Matthew 2 arrived at a "house" to a "young child", not on the manger night. Matthew says "they" (chief priests and scribes) answered.
- "He" after "King Herod" was heard as Herod.

## The facts you may use (all from the KJV pack below; use only what you need)
- About 700 years before Jesus (Micah prophesied in the days of the kings Jotham, Ahaz and Hezekiah, Micah 1:1), the prophet Micah named a small town: "But thou, Bethlehem Ephratah, though thou be little among the thousands of Judah, yet out of thee shall he come forth unto me that is to be ruler in Israel; whose goings forth have been from of old, from everlasting." (Micah 5:2)
- Mary and Joseph lived in Nazareth, far away. A tax order from Caesar Augustus sent them to Bethlehem (Luke 2:1-5). She gave birth there and laid Him "in a manger" - an animal feeding box (Luke 2:7).
- Wise men from the east came to Jerusalem asking "Where is he that is born King of the Jews?" (Matt 2:1-2). King Herod was troubled and asked the chief priests and scribes where Christ should be born. They answered at once: "In Bethlehem of Judaea", quoting Micah (Matt 2:3-6). The wise men went and found the young child with Mary in a house (Matt 2:9-11). The Bible does not say the chief priests or scribes went. Do not invent why, and do not say "they never went" - say only what the text supports (e.g. "the Bible never says they went").
- Years later people argued Jesus could not be the Christ because He came from Galilee, quoting the Bethlehem promise (John 7:41-42).
- Bethlehem means "house of bread"; Jesus said "I am the bread of life" (John 6:35). A picture, not a prophecy.
- The angel to the shepherds: "For unto you is born this day in the city of David a Saviour, which is Christ the Lord." (Luke 2:11)

## Hard rules
1. Plain words. KJV quotes verbatim and short; a short quote is fine, but never let an old word carry the meaning alone - say it plainly too.
2. Say simply who Micah was ("a prophet - a man who spoke God's message").
3. Honest: no invented feelings, numbers, distances, dates or details beyond the pack ("about 700 years" is allowed). No overclaim. Do not invent why the experts did not go.
4. No stock closer ("come to Jesus", "just ask Him", "He's waiting", "will you...?"). No fear. No gain/loss.
5. Never blame "the Jews" as a people.
6. Voices: one line per voice: [narrator], [prophet] (Micah's words), [scribe], [angel]. Mark one (pause).
7. No wording about AI or how the film is made. No hashtags.

## Output (only this)
(a) the script; (b) spoken word count; (c) KJV fragments with verse refs; (d) 4 lines: the hook's gap in the viewer's own words, where and how it is paid, main take-away, why a global newcomer gets it.
