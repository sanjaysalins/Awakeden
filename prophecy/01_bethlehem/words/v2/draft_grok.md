(pause)
[narrator] About seven hundred years before Jesus, Micah, a prophet, a man who spoke God's message, named a small town.
[prophet] Out of thee shall he come forth.
[narrator] A ruler would come from Bethlehem.
[narrator] A tax order sent Mary and Joseph from Nazareth to Bethlehem. She laid her baby in a feeding box.
[angel] A Saviour, which is Christ the Lord.
[narrator] A rescuer, called the Lord.
[narrator] Herod asked the chief priests and scribes where the king should be born. They answered.
[scribe] In Bethlehem of Judaea.
[narrator] The Bible never says they went.
[narrator] Wise men worshipped the young child with Mary, in a house.
[narrator] This kind of king.
[narrator] Jesus died for our sins, was buried, and rose on the third day.

(c) KJV fragments

- [prophet] "Out of thee shall he come forth." — Micah 5:2
- [angel] "A Saviour, which is Christ the Lord." — Luke 2:11
- [scribe] "In Bethlehem of Judaea." — Matthew 2:5
- Landing uses 1 Corinthians 15:3-4 in plain words (died for our sins, buried, rose the third day). "King" in Herod's question is the plain word for "Christ" (Matthew 2:4) after the wise men's "King of the Jews" (Matthew 2:2).

(d) The four lines

- The gap, in a viewer's words: "What kind of king is this?"
- Paid in the last two lines, out loud: "This kind of king. Jesus died for our sins, was buried, and rose on the third day."
- Take-away: The ruler promised from Bethlehem is Jesus, the king who died for our sins and rose on the third day.
- A newcomer gets it because the town is only the address. The open question is the kind of king, and the last line answers that question in ordinary words.
