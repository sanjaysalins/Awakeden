## SCRIPT X — 5/10

**1. Newcomer**
Who: a ruler, then a baby, then Jesus. "Jesus" shows up first only as a date ("before Jesus"). The baby is named Jesus at the last line.
What: Micah names a small town. A ruler would come from Bethlehem. A tax order sends Mary and Joseph there. She lays the baby in a feeding box. An angel calls him a rescuer. Herod's priests and scribes say Bethlehem. The Bible never says they went. Wise men worship the child with Mary, in a house. Jesus dies, is buried, and rises.
Why: thin. "This kind of king" never says what kind. The death feels like a second ending.
Hard lines:
- "Out of thee shall he come forth." (Who is "thee"? The town name comes after.)
- "A Saviour, which is Christ the Lord." ("Christ" is never explained.)
- "The Bible never says they went." ("They" is easy to lose.)
- "This kind of king."

**2. Bible reader**
There is a real point. The chief priests and scribes name Bethlehem, and Matthew never says they went, while the wise men worship the child in a house. The cross, the burial, and the third day are the fullest gospel of the three. It still plays as a list of facts.

**3. Hook: 4/10**
"What kind of king?" In two seconds there is no place, no name, and no stake.

**4. Landing: 4/10**
"This kind of king" does not answer the opening in plain words. The death line is true, and a friend could repeat "Jesus died for our sins, was buried, and rose on the third day." That line does not say what kind of king.

**5. Accurate**
- "Out of thee shall he come forth." Real words from Micah 5:2. The town is missing inside the spoken line. The narrator adds Bethlehem and "ruler" after.
- "She laid her baby in a feeding box." Luke 2:7. Fair plain speech for a manger. "Box" adds a shape.
- "A Saviour, which is Christ the Lord." Real words from Luke 2:11. Those words were to shepherds in a field. Here they can sound like they were spoken over the feeding box.
- "where the king should be born." Matthew 2:4 says Herod asked where Christ should be born. The wise men's question, which caused his ask, is missing, so Herod's question comes from nowhere.
- "In Bethlehem of Judaea." Exact, Matthew 2:5.
- "The Bible never says they went." Fair. It does not say they refused.
- "Wise men worshipped the young child with Mary, in a house." Matthew 2:11. Solid.
- "died for our sins, was buried, and rose on the third day." 1 Corinthians 15:3-4. Solid.
- "About seven hundred years" is a usual date. It is not in these verses.
No blame of a whole people.

**6. Grace**
Yes. No push. The last facts are offered open-handed.

**7. Score: 5/10**

## SCRIPT Y — 8/10

**1. Newcomer**
Who: Jesus, born in Bethlehem. Also Mary, Joseph, Micah, Herod, the experts, and strangers from the east.
What: The experts knew the town. Micah had named Bethlehem as the place a ruler would come from. A tax order from Caesar sent Mary and Joseph from Nazareth. Jesus was born there. Wise men asked in Jerusalem. The experts said Bethlehem. The Bible never says the experts went. The strangers went and found the child. Jesus died for our sins and rose the third day. He came to us first.
Why: yes. Knowing the town is not the same as going. His coming, death, and rising is the journey that matters.
Hard lines:
- "that is to be ruler in Israel."
- "But Jesus made the greater journey." (A picture. The next lines unpack it.)
- "Before anyone went to Him, He came to us." ("Anyone" bumps into the strangers, who did go.)
- "Judaea."

**2. Bible reader**
This is the Christmas road with a real turn. Matthew 2 has the experts answer and the strangers arrive. The new note is the last turn: he came before we did. A reader who knows the manger story still gets something.

**3. Hook: 8/10**
"The experts knew where the King would be born. Did they go?" Two seconds in, there is a fact and a hole. You stay for the answer.

**4. Landing: 8/10**
The hole is filled out loud. "The Bible never says they went." Then "The strangers went, and found the child." Death and rising sit on that same road as "the greater journey." A friend can repeat "Before anyone went to Him, He came to us." The word "anyone" is the soft spot.

**5. Accurate**
- "that is to be ruler in Israel." Exact clause, Micah 5:2. The narrator's "From it would come one" carries the setup, so the quote stays honest.
- Nazareth, Caesar, tax, Bethlehem, Jesus born there. Luke 2:1-7. "Caesar" is Caesar Augustus.
- Wise men from the east, Jerusalem, the newborn King. Matthew 2:1-2. The verse says "King of the Jews." "Newborn King" is a fair short form.
- "In Bethlehem of Judaea." Exact.
- "The Bible never says they went." Stays inside the silence. Does not say they refused.
- "The strangers went, and found the child." Matthew 2:9-11. Worship and the house are left out. Nothing false is added.
- "He died for our sins. He rose again the third day." 1 Corinthians 15:3-4. Burial is unspoken.
- "Before anyone went to Him, He came to us." In the story, he is born first, and then the strangers go to him. "Anyone" is one word too wide. "Us" is the listener, which is application, not a fake event.
No people-group blame.

**6. Grace**
Yes. The last line is a gift. No "come to Jesus" line.

**7. Score: 8/10**

## SCRIPT Z — 7/10

**1. Newcomer**
Who: the clearest name-link of the three. "That child is Jesus."
What: Micah names one small town. A tax order sends Mary and Joseph. Her baby is born. Wise men come asking for the newborn King. Herod asks the experts. They say Bethlehem. They knew. The Bible never says they went. The strangers find the child with Mary. Jesus dies for our sins and rises the third day. The experts had the answer. The strangers found the King.
Why: the know-versus-find contrast is clear, and "for our sins" says why he matters. The last line goes back to experts and strangers, so the death can slide off the ear.
Hard lines:
- "out of thee shall he come forth."
- "Judaea."
- "as the Scriptures said."
- A chain of "there." Miss the prophet's "Bethlehem," and the town is gone.

**2. Bible reader**
The Matthew 2 irony is clean, and "with Mary" matches the visit. "As the Scriptures said" fits 1 Corinthians 15:3-4. The last line is sharp. The death and rising are correct, and they sit a step behind that last line.

**3. Hook: 8/10**
Same opening as Y. Same pull. You need "Did they go?" answered.

**4. Landing: 7/10**
"The Bible never says they went" answers the question. "That child is Jesus" ties the baby to the cross. "He died for our sins and rose the third day" is the right gospel. The line a friend would repeat is "The experts had the answer. The strangers found the King." That line answers the hook and leaves the cross one step back.

**5. Accurate**
- "Bethlehem… out of thee shall he come forth." Both pieces are in Micah 5:2. They are not one continuous phrase. Heard aloud, it is a short form. It skips "Ephratah" and "unto me." The substance holds.
- "A ruler would come from there." Matches "ruler in Israel."
- "lived far away… a tax order sent them there, and her baby was born." Luke 2. Nazareth and Caesar are unnamed. "Far away" is fair. The place hangs on "there."
- Wise men from the east, newborn King, Herod, experts. Matthew 2:1-4. The word "where" is not repeated. The answer supplies it.
- "In Bethlehem of Judaea." Exact.
- "They knew." They did give the answer. The pause can feel like a jab. The next line does not say they refused.
- "found the child with Mary." Matthew 2:11. Solid.
- Death, the third day, "as the Scriptures said." 1 Corinthians 15:3-4. Burial is unspoken.
No ethnic blame.

**6. Grace**
Yes. No command. The landing is a picture of who moved. Cooler than Y.

**7. Score: 7/10**
