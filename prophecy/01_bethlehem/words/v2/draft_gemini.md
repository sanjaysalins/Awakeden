(a) The script
[narrator] Why did a seven-hundred-year-old promise point the Savior to a tiny, overlooked village?
[narrator] Long before Jesus, Micah—a prophet who spoke God's message—named a small town.
[prophet] But thou, Bethlehem... out of thee shall he come forth.
[narrator] Centuries later, King Herod asked his experts where the Christ would be born. They knew immediately.
[scribe] In Bethlehem of Judaea.
[narrator] Travelers went and found the young child in a house. The experts knew the exact town, but the Bible never says they went.
(pause)
[narrator] So why did God choose that tiny village? Because Jesus didn't come for a palace. He came to the lowest place to die for our sins, and rose the third day to save us.

(d) The 4 lines
1. The hook's gap in the viewer's own words: Why would God announce the Savior's arrival in a small, forgotten town instead of a capital city?
2. Where and how it is paid: In the last three sentences, paid out loud by explaining that God chose a humble town because Jesus came for a humble purpose.
3. Main take-away: Jesus bypassed earthly palaces to come to the lowest place, so He could die for our sins and rise again.
4. Why a global newcomer gets it: It avoids complex geography and archaic idioms, relying on the universal, easy-to-grasp contrast between a "palace" and a "tiny village" to explain grace.
