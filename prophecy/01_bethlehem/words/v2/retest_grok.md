**1. Newcomer.** After one listen: experts knew the King would be born in Bethlehem. Micah had named that town long before. A tax order sent Mary and Joseph there, and Jesus was born. Wise men came, Herod asked the experts, and the wise men found the child. Jesus came from heaven, died for our sins, and rose on the third day. He came to us first.

Who and what land. Why it matters lands on the last line.

One line does not land clean: "The Bible never says they went." On a fast listen, "they" sounds like the wise men. The next line says the wise men went. It sounds like a contradiction.

Also muddy: "Judaea," and "that is to be ruler in Israel."

**2. Bible reader.** There is one new cut. The experts name Bethlehem, and the text never says they went. The outsiders go, and Jesus has already come. The birth itself is the Christmas story you know.

**3. Hook: 8.** First two seconds: "They knew where the King would be born. Did they go?" You stay for the answer.

**4. Landing: 7.** The middle answers "did they go?" The last line answers the deeper question out loud: "Before anyone went to Him, He came to us." You could repeat that line to a friend. "He died for our sins. He rose again the third day" sits in the same "He came" story. It still arrives fast, like a second beat.

**5. Accurate.** Spoken quotes match the KJV. "that is to be ruler in Israel" is Micah 5:2. "In Bethlehem of Judaea" is Matthew 2:5. "Died for our sins" and "rose again the third day" match 1 Corinthians 15:3-4. "Came down from heaven" matches John 6:51. "Small town" matches Micah's "little." "The Bible never says they went" stays inside the silence. It does not say they refused. No blame on a people group.

"About seven hundred years" is not in these verses. It is a fair "about." "Before anyone went to Him" is the strong word. Shepherds do go later, in Luke 2:15-16. The line means he came first.

**6. Grace.** Yes. The end states what he did, then "He came to us." No "come to Jesus" push.

**7. Overall: 7.**
