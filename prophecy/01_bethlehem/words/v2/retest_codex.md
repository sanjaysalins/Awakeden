1. NEWCOMER

I follow the main story: Micah predicted a king would come from Bethlehem; Jesus was born there; experts knew the place, and wise men visited. Jesus later died for our sins and rose again.

I hear why it matters, but “died for our sins” assumes I understand what sins are and how his death helps us.

“Wise men” and “Bible experts” sound like similar people. Their difference is unclear. Mary and Joseph are introduced without explaining their relationship to Jesus.

2. BIBLE READER

There is a fresh observation: the experts supply the answer, but Matthew records the wise men making the journey. That gives a familiar Christmas story something to notice. It supports reflection, provided their unrecorded journey does not become an accusation that they refused to go.

3. HOOK — 7/10

After the complete opening, I want to know: “Did the people who knew the answer actually visit?” I would stay.

Strictly within two seconds, I have only heard approximately “The Bible experts knew…” The explicit curiosity arrives several seconds later.

4. LANDING — 7/10

The hook receives an honest “we aren’t told” before the ending. The final line completes the going/coming theme and is easy to repeat.

The death and resurrection belong to Jesus’ story, but their connection to the Bethlehem promise feels compressed. “He had come much farther” connects the journeys; it leaves his purpose for coming mostly implicit.

5. ACCURATE + HONEST

The two spoken quotations match the supplied KJV exactly: “that is to be ruler in Israel” and “In Bethlehem of Judaea.”

Micah naming Bethlehem and describing its smallness are supported. “About seven hundred years earlier” is a historical dating claim that the supplied excerpts do not establish.

The tax decree, journey from Nazareth, and Bethlehem birth fit Luke 2. “Lived far away” is a reasonable geographical summary, though the excerpt gives no distance.

The wise men’s origin, question, visit, and discovery of the child, plus Herod consulting the experts, fit Matthew 2.

“The Bible never says they went” goes beyond what these excerpts alone can establish. Matthew does not record them going. Silence cannot establish that they stayed away.

Coming from heaven, dying for our sins, and rising the third day are supported by John 6:51 and 1 Corinthians 15:3–4. The last line works as a poetic summary of his coming before the visitors.

There is no explicit blame on a people group or invented motive.

6. GRACE

Yes. The ending centres on what Jesus did, without pressuring the listener. “Before anyone went to Him, He came to us” is memorable and gracious.

7. OVERALL — 8/10

The ONE fix it most needs:

Replace “But Jesus had come much farther. He came down from heaven.”

With: “But the King born in Bethlehem came from heaven to save us.”

That makes the promised King’s birth and saving work one continuous story.

Remaining newcomer confusion: who Mary and Joseph are, how wise men differ from Bible experts, and what “our sins” means.
