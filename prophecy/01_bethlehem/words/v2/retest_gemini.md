1. **NEWCOMER**: Yes. WHO: Jesus, wise men, and "Bible experts". WHAT: The experts knew exactly where the King would be born but didn't bother to go, while the wise men did. WHY: It matters because Jesus didn't wait for us to come to Him; He came from heaven to die for our sins. The vocabulary is incredibly simple and accessible. There are no confusing words; even "prophet" is defined in-line ("a man who spoke God's message").

2. **BIBLE READER**: It offers a fresh, stinging irony. While the Christmas story is familiar, focusing on the apathy of the chief priests and scribes—who knew the exact prophecy and location but didn't bother to travel to see the Messiah—is a striking and convicting new angle. 

3. **HOOK**: The question raised is: "Wait, did the experts go? If not, why wouldn't they?" It immediately creates a knowledge gap and a sense of mystery. Yes, I would stay. Score: 9/10.

4. **LANDING**: Yes, it explicitly answers the hook ("The Bible never says they went.") and executes a beautiful, thematic pivot: we failed to go to Him, so He came to us. The gospel elements (died for our sins, rose the third day) are perfectly integrated as the ultimate proof of Him "coming much farther." The final line ("Before anyone went to Him, He came to us.") is highly repeatable. Score: 10/10.

5. **ACCURATE + HONEST**: 
- "that is to be ruler in Israel" matches Micah 5:2 exactly.
- "In Bethlehem of Judaea" matches Matthew 2:5 exactly.
- "He died for our sins. He rose again the third day" matches 1 Corinthians 15:3-4 exactly.
- The claim "The Bible never says they went" is accurate; Matthew 2 records Herod sending the wise men, but the scribes/priests are never said to follow. There are no overclaims, invented details, or anti-Semitic tropes (it safely uses "Bible experts" and "King Herod").

6. **GRACE**: Yes. The ending is pure grace. It rests entirely on what Christ did for us, with zero manufactured pressure, guilt, or stock "come to Jesus" commands. 

7. **SCORE**: 9.5/10.

## Then
**The ONE fix it most needs**: The script misses the chance to show *how easy* it would have been for the experts to check. Change `"The Bible never says they went."` to `"It was only six miles away. But the Bible never says they went."` This sharpens the irony of their apathy.

**Still confuse a newcomer**: A newcomer might slightly stumble on `"A tax order sent them to Bethlehem"` since modern taxes don't require traveling to an ancestral hometown, but the preceding line `"Mary and Joseph lived far away"` provides just enough context to keep the narrative moving without getting bogged down in Roman census laws.
