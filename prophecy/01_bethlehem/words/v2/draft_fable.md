## (a) Script

[narrator] They knew where to find the King. Did they go?

[narrator] About seven hundred years earlier, a prophet called Micah — a man who spoke God's message — named a small town: Bethlehem. From it would come one —

[prophet] "that is to be ruler in Israel."

[narrator] Mary and Joseph lived far away, in Nazareth. A tax order from Caesar sent them to Bethlehem. There, Jesus was born.

[narrator] Wise men from the east reached Jerusalem, asking for the newborn King. King Herod asked the scholars where.

[scribe] "In Bethlehem of Judaea."

[narrator] The Bible never says they went. (pause) The strangers went, and found the child.

[narrator] But Jesus made the greater journey. He "died for our sins." He "rose again the third day."

[narrator] Before anyone went to Him, He came to us.

## (c) KJV fragments

- "that is to be ruler in Israel" — Micah 5:2
- "In Bethlehem of Judaea" — Matthew 2:5
- "died for our sins" — 1 Corinthians 15:3
- "rose again the third day" — 1 Corinthians 15:4

## (d) Four lines

- Gap, in the viewer's words: "Some people knew exactly where the King was. Did they go to Him or not?"
- Paid: said out loud after the scribe's answer — "The Bible never says they went. The strangers went, and found the child." Then turned once, on the same word "went": Jesus made the greater journey, and the last line closes it — "Before anyone went to Him, He came to us."
- Main take-away: God named the town about 700 years early and kept His word; and the real journey was not ours to Jesus but His to us — to Bethlehem, to the cross, out of the tomb.
- Why a newcomer gets it: one simple question (who went?), short plain sentences, every old word is short and explained by the line before it, and the ending answers the first line using the same verb.
