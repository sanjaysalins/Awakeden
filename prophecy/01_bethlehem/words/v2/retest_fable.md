# Blind re-test — Fable seat

## 1. NEWCOMER
- WHO: mostly yes. Jesus, a king born in a small town called Bethlehem. Wise men. A king called Herod. "Experts."
- WHAT: yes. A prophet named the town 700 years before. Jesus was born there. The experts knew the town but (it seems) did not go. Wise men from far away went and found Him.
- WHY: yes, at the end. Jesus came to us first, died for our sins, rose again.
- Lines I would trip on:
  - "From it would come one / that is to be ruler in Israel." The sentence breaks across two voices and the grammar is old. On one listen I hear "come one... that is to be ruler" and lose it. Hardest line in the piece.
  - "A tax order sent them to Bethlehem." Understandable, but "tax order" is odd. Why does a tax send you to another town? Passes, but only just.
  - "Wise men from the east came asking for the one born King." "the one born King" is a heard-once snag (born a king? the one who was born, the King?).
  - "King Herod asked the experts where." Ends on "where" with nothing after it. Two kings in two sentences (the born King, King Herod) can blur for a newcomer.
  - "In Bethlehem of Judaea." "Judaea" is unknown, but harmless — Bethlehem is the word that matters.
  - "died for our sins" — a newcomer does not know what this means. It is said, not explained. Accepted for the format, but it is the least-understood phrase.

## 2. BIBLE READER
Something new, yes. Not just Christmas again. The fresh point: the scribes gave the right answer from Micah and Matthew never records them walking the short road to Bethlehem, while foreigners crossed a long distance. "He came much farther" is a good turn on top of that. The tax-order line (God used an empire's paperwork to keep a 700-year-old promise) is hinted but not said, so that half stays flat.

## 3. HOOK
Question in my head: "Wait — they knew where the King was and did not go? Why not?"
Would stay: yes. Short, a real question, no jargon except "Bible experts," which is plain enough.
Weakness: "the King" is unnamed for two seconds. That is fine for curiosity.
Score: 8/10

## 4. LANDING
- Answers the hook out loud: yes. "The Bible never says they went." That is honest and clear. The pause helps.
- Lands on Jesus in the same story: mostly yes. "went / came" is one thread: experts did not go, wise men went, Jesus came farther, He came to us first. Death and rising are named plainly.
- The soft spot: "He came down from heaven. He died for our sins. He rose again the third day." is three fast creed lines. The first belongs to the "came farther" idea. The next two are joined only by rhythm, not by the going/coming thread. It is close to bolted-on, saved by the last line.
- Repeatable last line: yes. "Before anyone went to Him, He came to us." Easy to say to a friend.
Score: 8/10

## 5. ACCURATE + HONEST
- "that is to be ruler in Israel" — exact KJV, Micah 5:2. Correct.
- "In Bethlehem of Judaea." — exact KJV, Matthew 2:5. Correct.
- "About seven hundred years earlier" — the usual dating of Micah. Fair with "about."
- "named a small town: Bethlehem" — "little among the thousands of Judah." Fair.
- "Mary and Joseph lived far away. A tax order sent them to Bethlehem." — Luke 2:1-5 (Nazareth in Galilee, decree to be taxed). Fair. "far away" is a little loose (about 3-4 days on foot) but not wrong.
- "Wise men from the east came asking for the one born King." — Matthew 2:1-2. Fair. They came to Jerusalem, which the script skips; no error.
- "King Herod asked the experts where." — Matthew 2:4, "chief priests and scribes." Fair.
- "The Bible never says they went." — true, and carefully worded. It does not claim they stayed home. Good, honest phrasing.
- "The wise men went, and found the child." — Matthew 2:9-11. Correct.
- "He came down from heaven." — John 6:51. Correct.
- "He died for our sins. He rose again the third day." — 1 Corinthians 15:3-4. Correct, near-verbatim.
- Small overclaim risk: the opening "Did they go?" plus the answer leads the ear to "they did not go." The script itself stays on the honest side of that line. Keep "never says."
- "The Bible experts knew where the King would be born." — they knew the town from Scripture; fair.
- People-group blame: none. "Bible experts" names a role, not a people. Good.
- Voice tag: the [scribe] line is spoken by "they" (chief priests and scribes together) in KJV. A single scribe voice is a fair stand-in.
- No misquote found. No invented detail found.

## 6. GRACE
Yes. No pressure, no "come to Jesus" stock line, no gain or loss. The ending is a statement about what He did, not an order. The quiet contrast with the experts could feel like a small poke at the viewer ("are you the one who knows and does not go?") but the script does not say it, and the last line turns it to grace.

## 7. OVERALL
8/10

## THE ONE FIX
Mend the broken prophecy sentence. It is the line a newcomer loses, and it is the promise the whole short hangs on.

Now:
> [narrator] ...named a small town: Bethlehem. From it would come one
> [prophet] that is to be ruler in Israel.

Change to:
> [narrator] ...named a small town: Bethlehem. Out of it, he said, a ruler would come —
> [prophet] that is to be ruler in Israel.

The narrator says the idea in plain words first, then the KJV is heard as the echo. Nobody has to parse a sentence split across two voices.

## STILL CONFUSING FOR A NEWCOMER
- "the one born King" — say "asking, where is the newborn King?"
- "King Herod asked the experts where." — add the end: "where the King would be born."
- "died for our sins" — not explained. Leave it, but know it is heard, not understood, by a total newcomer.
- "A tax order" — "The ruler's tax order" or "An order from the emperor" would make it clear who sent them.
- Two kings close together (the born King, King Herod) — the Herod fix above helps.
