1. NEWCOMER: God announced where a King would come from, centuries beforehand. Jesus was born there. The Bible experts knew the place; the wise men went there. Jesus came from heaven, died for people’s sins, and returned to life.

“Mary and Joseph lived far away” introduces two people without explaining their connection to Jesus. “He died for our sins” leaves me unsure what sins are and how His death helps us.

2. PROMISE: God promised, through Micah, that a King would come from Bethlehem, about seven hundred years before Jesus’ birth. Jesus being born there fulfilled that promise. This is clearly HEARD: “God promised” and “God’s promise came true” make the connection explicit. 9/10.

3. TWO KINGS: Yes. “Jesus, the King” identifies the promised King. “Herod, the king there” introduces a different king, although a newcomer may wonder how their claims relate.

4. HARD WORDS: “Micah,” “Bethlehem,” “Jerusalem,” and “Judaea” may challenge listeners and auto-captions. “Ruler in Israel” introduces another unfamiliar place. “Tax order” is compressed wording. “Sins” needs religious background; “rose from the dead” may be harder than “came back to life.” The explanation of “prophet” helps.

5. BIBLE: No clear contradiction, but “this newborn King” suggests an age Matthew does not establish here. The supplied passage says “young child” and places the visit in a house.

“About seven hundred years earlier” cannot be checked from the supplied verses alone.

“The Bible never says the experts went” carefully describes silence. Empty footprints could nevertheless make viewers conclude that they definitely stayed away; the passage does not establish that.

6. Overall: 8/10. The promise and fulfilment are clear, but the unexplained people and religious language add difficulty for newcomers.

ONE small change: replace the wise men’s arrival line with:

“Wise men reached Jerusalem, asking where this King was.”
