Y

1. NEWCOMER: God announced a future King from Bethlehem through Micah. Mary and Joseph travelled there because of taxation, and Jesus was born. Scholars supplied the birthplace; wise men visited Jesus. Jesus later died and returned to life. Mary and Joseph’s roles remain unexplained. “That is to be ruler in Israel” is awkward to follow aloud.

2. PROMISE: God promised a King from Bethlehem about seven hundred years beforehand; Jesus’ birth fulfils that promise. It is heard, especially in “the King God promised.” However, the voice change into “that is to be ruler in Israel” makes the promise sentence harder to process. 8/10.

3. TWO KINGS: Yes. “The King God promised” identifies Jesus, while Herod is separately introduced as “the king there.”

4. HARD WORDS: “That is to be ruler in Israel” adds difficult sentence structure. “Tax order,” “wise men,” “sins” and “Judaea” present the same obstacles as in X.

5. BIBLE: No clear contradiction. The supplied excerpts do not establish “seven hundred years” or the child’s “newborn” age when sought. “Three days later” is less precise than “the third day.” The experts’ absence is unrecorded, rather than proved.

6. Overall: 7.5/10.

PICK X: its promise is easier to understand on first hearing.

ONE small change: Replace “Three days later, He rose from the dead” with “On the third day, He rose from the dead.”
