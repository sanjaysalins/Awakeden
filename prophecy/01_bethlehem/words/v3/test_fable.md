## X

1. NEWCOMER: Long ago a man called Micah said God would send a King from a small town, Bethlehem. About 700 years later Jesus was born there. Travellers came looking for Him. The experts knew the town but, as far as we are told, stayed home. The point: Jesus came all the way from heaven to us first. Unclear lines: "Did they go?" (go where? it only makes sense later) and "Bethlehem was close by" (close to what? Jerusalem is not named in that line).
2. PROMISE: God, through Micah, promised a King from Bethlehem, about 700 years before; Jesus was born there. It is heard: "God promised a King" and "God's promise came true" both say it out loud and match the stamp and end card. Weak spot: the prophet voice says only "ruler in Israel", a 3-word fragment that sounds like a clipped edit. 8/10.
3. TWO KINGS: Yes. "Jesus, the King, was born", then "this newborn King", then "Herod, the king there". The word "there" separates them. A newcomer still does not learn why Herod asks.
4. HARD WORDS: "Judaea" (captions vary: Judea), "Micah" (may caption as "Mika" or "Michael"), "prophet" (sounds like "profit"), "Herod" (may caption as "Harold"), "tax order" (unusual pair).
5. BIBLE: Nothing wrong. Slight looseness: "Three days later" where the KJV says "rose again the third day". "The Bible never says the experts went" is fair, because it claims silence, not that they stayed.
6. Overall: 8/10.

## Y

1. NEWCOMER: Same story and same point. Unclear lines: the same two as X, plus "a King would come, that is to be ruler in Israel". The old grammar "that is to be" is hard to follow by ear.
2. PROMISE: Same who, what and when. The KJV phrase joins the narrator's sentence more smoothly, but the words "came true" are never spoken. "The King God promised" is softer and no longer echoes the "CAME TRUE" stamp. 7/10.
3. TWO KINGS: Yes, for the same reasons. "The King God promised" then "Herod, the king there".
4. HARD WORDS: The same as X, plus "that is to be ruler". "There Jesus was born: the King God promised" has no verb after the colon and is hard to parse by ear.
5. BIBLE: Nothing wrong. The same "Three days later" looseness.
6. Overall: 7/10. Dropping "Mary and Joseph lived far away" weakens the far / close by / much farther thread that pays off at the end, and the map shows Nazareth with no line about it.

## PICK: X

Why: it says "promise" and "came true" in plain words, and it keeps "far away", which sets up "close by" and "much farther".

ONE small change: replace "Three days later, He rose from the dead." with "On the third day, He rose from the dead."
