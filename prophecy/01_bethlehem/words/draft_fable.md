# Born in Bethlehem - draft (spine: the experts who knew the verse)

## (a) Script

[narrator] They knew the exact town where God's promised King would be born. The Bible never says they went to look.

[narrator] About seven hundred years before Jesus, a prophet named Micah, a man who spoke God's message, pointed to one little town: Bethlehem.

[prophet] "Out of thee shall he come forth."

[narrator] From that town, a ruler would come.

[narrator] Centuries later, Jesus was born there. King Herod asked the religious experts where the promised King would be born. They answered at once.

[scribe] "In Bethlehem of Judaea."

[narrator] They knew the verse. The child was already there.

(pause)

[narrator] Knowing the place was not enough. So He came to us. Jesus, born in Bethlehem, died for our sins and rose the third day. The promise was kept, for you.

## (b) Spoken word count

122

## (c) KJV fragments

- "out of thee shall he come forth" - Micah 5:2
- "In Bethlehem of Judaea" - Matthew 2:5
- Paraphrase only (not quoted): "died for our sins ... rose again the third day" - 1 Corinthians 15:3-4; Jesus born in Bethlehem before Herod's question - Matthew 2:1-4

## (d) Four lines

- Who it is for: anyone, anywhere, with no Bible knowledge; and Bible readers who think they know the Christmas story.
- Main take-away: God named the town 700 years early and kept His word; knowing about Jesus is not the same as having Him, so He came to us.
- Curiosity gap: who knew the exact town of the King's birth, and why does the Bible never say they went?
- Why a global newcomer gets it: short plain words, one town, one question, one answer; "prophet" is explained, and each old-English quote is restated in plain English right after.
