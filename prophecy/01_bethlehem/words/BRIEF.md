# Write ONE narration from scratch - "Born in Bethlehem" (Micah 5:2 -> Matthew 2, Luke 2). You are ONE blind writer; you have seen no other draft.

## The series
"Promised, then kept": an Old Testament promise, and how it came true in Jesus. Every episode lands on Jesus.

## Who it is for
Worldwide, many with English as a second language, many with no Bible knowledge. Also Bible readers who think they know the Christmas story. Released before Christmas.

## Length (hard)
UNDER 60 SECONDS. 105-125 spoken words total, including quotes. Vertical short.

## The facts you may use (all from the KJV pack below; use only what you need)
- About 700 years before Jesus (Micah prophesied in the days of the kings Jotham, Ahaz and Hezekiah, Micah 1:1), the prophet Micah named a small town: "But thou, Bethlehem Ephratah, though thou be little among the thousands of Judah, yet out of thee shall he come forth unto me that is to be ruler in Israel; whose goings forth have been from of old, from everlasting." (Micah 5:2)
- "From everlasting": the one to be born there already existed before He was born.
- Mary and Joseph lived in Nazareth, in Galilee, far away. A tax order from the Roman emperor, Caesar Augustus, sent Joseph to Bethlehem, "the city of David", with Mary, "being great with child" (Luke 2:1-5). She gave birth there and laid Him "in a manger" (Luke 2:7) - a manger is an animal feeding box.
- When King Herod asked where the Christ would be born, the religious experts answered at once, "In Bethlehem of Judaea", quoting Micah (Matthew 2:4-6). They knew the verse; the Bible does not say they went to look.
- Years later, people argued Jesus could not be the Christ because He came from Galilee: "Hath not the scripture said, That Christ cometh of the seed of David, and out of the town of Bethlehem, where David was?" (John 7:41-42). They knew the promise, and did not know where He had been born.
- The name Bethlehem means "house of bread" in Hebrew. Jesus later said, "I am the bread of life" (John 6:35). This is a picture, not a prophecy: if you use it, never call the name itself a prediction.
- The angel to the shepherds: "For unto you is born this day in the city of David a Saviour, which is Christ the Lord." (Luke 2:11)

## Pick ONE spine (your choice) and hold it from first line to last
Options (or your own, if it stays true to the text): the tiny town God named in advance; the emperor's tax that moved a pregnant woman to the right town; the experts who knew the verse and did not go; the crowd that knew the promise and missed the fulfilment; the "house of bread" picture. One spine only. Do not list facts.

## Hard rules
1. First 3 seconds: plain words that open a curiosity gap a stranger must close by watching.
2. Say simply who Micah was ("a prophet - a man who spoke God's message") and what a prophecy is, in plain words, if you use the word.
3. KJV quotes verbatim and short. Never let an old word carry the meaning alone; plain English around it.
4. Honest: no invented feelings, numbers, distances, dates or details beyond the pack ("about 700 years" is allowed). No overclaim ("statistically impossible", "only Jesus could..."). Do not invent why the experts did not go.
5. The ending lands on Jesus, earned by your spine, and says plainly that He died for our sins and rose the third day (1 Cor 15:3-4). Grace, not pressure.
6. No stock closer ("come to Jesus", "just ask Him", "He's waiting", "will you...?"). No fear. No gain/loss.
7. Never blame "the Jews" as a people.
8. Voices: one line per voice, e.g. [narrator], [prophet] (Micah's words), [angel], [scribe]. Quotes spoken by a character get that character's voice. Mark one (pause).
9. No wording about AI or how the film is made. No hashtags. The look is not chosen yet: write for the ear.

## Output (only this)
(a) the script; (b) spoken word count; (c) KJV fragments with verse refs; (d) 4 lines: who it is for, main take-away, the curiosity gap, why a global newcomer gets it.
