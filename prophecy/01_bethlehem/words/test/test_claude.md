# Blind judge: X, Y, Z

## SCRIPT X: "Shall Christ come out of Galilee?"

**1. Newcomer:** I mostly get it. WHO: Jesus and a crowd. WHAT: people said He came from the wrong place, but He was born in the promised town. WHY: an old promise came true. Words I would not understand:
- "Galilee": I don't know it is a place.
- "Shall Christ come out of Galilee?": old English, and I may not know "Christ" means Jesus.
- "from everlasting": the line explains it, but it goes past fast.

**2. Bible reader:** Yes, something new. Using John 7 as the way in is fresh. It is not just Christmas again.

**3. Curiosity gap: 7/10.** The first line works: "could not be the King because of where He came from." The middle pays it off. The ending then moves to a new idea (everlasting, cross) and does not tie back to the Galilee mix-up.

**4. Accuracy:**
- The John 7:41 and Micah 5:2 quotes are exact. The "..." gap is fair.
- "Roman tax order" matches Luke 2:1.
- **Overclaim:** "They did not know where He was born." John never says this. It is only implied by the irony of 7:42. Softer: "They knew the promise. Did they know where He was born?"
- "the crowd" is fine. It does not blame a whole people.

**5. Grace:** No pressure. But "He came to die for our sins…" feels stuck on the end. It is a stock closer, not one the script earned.

## SCRIPT Y: "They knew the town. The Bible never says they went."

**1. Newcomer:** WHAT and WHY are clear: experts knew the verse and did not go, but God came anyway. **WHO is the problem.** In line 1, "They" has no name. I don't learn who "they" are until line 4. Words I would not understand:
- "But He had already come to them": who is "He"? Jesus is named only once, in passing.
- "from everlasting": same as in X.

**2. Bible reader:** Yes, something new. The scribes' answer to Herod (Matthew 2:5) is underused. "Knew the verse, did not go" is a sharp, true point.

**3. Curiosity gap: 8/10.** The best hook of the three. "Never says they went to look" makes me stay. The payoff ("He had already come to them") is the best turn in any script.

**4. Accuracy:**
- The Micah quote and "In Bethlehem of Judaea" (Matthew 2:5) are exact.
- "religious experts" is a fair plain word for "chief priests and scribes."
- Line 1 is honest: "The Bible never says…"
- **Overclaim:** "They did not go to Him" says as fact what the Bible only leaves out. Keep the hedge: "The Bible never says they went."
- Blame: it names a small group of leaders, not a whole people. That is OK, but keep it to "the experts" and never "the Jews."

**5. Grace:** "He had already come to them" is grace with no pressure, and it is earned. The cross line after it is stock and dulls that landing.

---

## SCRIPT Z: "Why that town?"

**1. Newcomer:** The clearest story of the three. WHO: Mary, Joseph, an emperor. WHAT: a tax order moved them to the promised town. WHY: God kept His promise. Words I would not understand:
- "city of David": who is David?
- "Christ the Lord": what does it mean?
- The angel just appears. Who is it talking to?

**2. Bible reader:** No, it is the Christmas story again. "An emperor gave the order. God kept the promise." is a good line, but most church people know this one.

**3. Curiosity gap: 6/10.** "Why that town?" is a real question and it is paid off neatly. But it is a mild hook for fast scrolling.

**4. Accuracy:**
- The Micah and Luke 2:11 quotes are exact.
- "every man went to his family's town" is a fair paraphrase of Luke 2:3-4.
- Nothing is invented and no one is blamed.
- Small gap: the angel spoke to shepherds, and the script hides that.

**5. Grace:** It lands on Jesus cleanly and without pressure. The emperor-and-God line earns the ending better than X does.

**My #1: Y.**

**The ONE fix (exact words), for line 1:**
> "The religious experts knew the exact town where God's promised King would be born. The Bible never says they went to look."

**Still confusing for a newcomer in Y:**
- "But He had already come to them": say "But Jesus had already come to them."
- "They did not go to Him" states as fact what the Bible only leaves out. Change it to "The Bible never says they went."
- "from everlasting": the line explains it, but the quote may still slip past.
- The death-and-rising line comes right after the best line. Think about a shorter join, so "already come to them" stays the landing.
