# Blind test — seat: fable

Judged alone, from TEST_PACK.md only. Words as heard, no pictures.

## SCRIPT X

1. **NEWCOMER** — Partly.
   - WHO: Jesus. Yes.
   - WHAT: muddled. I hear two towns (Galilee, Bethlehem) in 15 seconds and must work out that "grew up in" and "born in" are different. Galilee is a region, not a town, so "named a different town" does not match.
   - WHY: weak. The last line arrives with no bridge.
   - Hard lines: "Shall Christ come out of Galilee?" (is "Christ" the same as "Jesus"? what is Galilee?) · "out of thee shall he come forth unto me" · "He lived before He was born" (sounds like a riddle) · "The crowd knew the promise" (which promise? it was never called a promise before).
2. **BIBLE READER** — Yes, something new. John 7 is the freshest angle of the three: the crowd used the right prophecy to reach the wrong answer. Not the Christmas story again.
3. **CURIOSITY GAP — 6/10.** Line 1 is a fair hook but it is a statement, not a question. It pays off at "They did not know where He was born" (good, dry). Then the script carries on for another line on a new subject, so the payoff is not the ending.
4. **ACCURATE + HONEST**
   - "Shall Christ come out of Galilee?" — verbatim John 7:41. OK.
   - Micah quote — words are verbatim, but the "..." hides a cut ("Ephratah, though thou be little among the thousands of Judah, yet"). The ear cannot hear an ellipsis. Minor.
   - "People said" / "The crowd knew" — KJV says "**some** said". Mild overclaim; it was one part of a divided crowd (7:43).
   - "They did not know where He was born" — inference. John does not say it. Reasonable, but it is stated as fact.
   - "came 'from everlasting'" — KJV: "whose goings forth have been from of old, from everlasting". The quoted two words are right; "came" is paraphrase. OK.
   - "He lived before He was born" — interpretation, orthodox, not a quote. OK if kept in narrator voice.
   - No blame on a people group.
5. **GRACE** — No pressure, no stock line. But the gospel sentence is bolted on: three ideas (eternal, died, rose) in the last five seconds, none set up by the story.

## SCRIPT Y

1. **NEWCOMER** — Mostly, after the first line.
   - WHO: Jesus, plus "they". Line 1 does not say who "they" are. I wait 25 seconds to learn it is the religious experts.
   - WHAT: clear and simple. They knew the town. They gave the answer. They stayed home.
   - WHY: the best of the three. "He had already come to them" tells me what kind of King this is.
   - Hard lines: "They knew the exact town" (who?) · two kings in one sentence ("King Herod asked ... where the promised King would be born") · "out of thee shall he come forth unto me" · "He lived before He was born".
2. **BIBLE READER** — Yes. Most people remember the wise men and Herod. Few notice that the men who quoted the verse are never shown going. That is a real, text-based "I never saw that".
3. **CURIOSITY GAP — 8/10.** Best first line. "The Bible never says they went to look" makes me stay. Payoff is clean: "They knew the verse. They did not go to Him. But He had already come to them." The last line (everlasting / died / rose) runs past the payoff and softens it.
4. **ACCURATE + HONEST**
   - "In Bethlehem of Judaea." — verbatim Matthew 2:5. OK.
   - Micah quote — same hidden cut as X. Minor.
   - Line 1 is honest: "The Bible **never says** they went". Good.
   - **"They did not go to Him."** — OVERCLAIM. This turns silence into a fact. Matthew does not say they stayed. Line 1 was careful; this line is not. It also makes the charge against the priests and scribes harder than the text does.
   - "religious experts" for "chief priests and scribes" — fair, plain, names a role not a people. No group blame.
   - "When Jesus was born there, King Herod asked" — skips the wise men. Not wrong, just compressed.
   - "came 'from everlasting'" — same note as X.
5. **GRACE** — Yes. "But He had already come to them" is grace, not pressure, and it is earned by the story. No stock closer. The final gospel sentence is plain and true but a little stacked.

## SCRIPT Z

1. **NEWCOMER** — Yes, the clearest.
   - WHO: Mary, Joseph, Jesus. Yes.
   - WHAT: a tax order moved them; the baby was born in the town a prophet named. Yes.
   - WHY: "An emperor gave the order. God kept the promise." I get it in one listen.
   - Hard lines: "in the city of David" (I do not know this means Bethlehem, so the angel line does not prove the point for me) · "a Saviour, which is Christ the Lord" · "Nazareth" (new place name, not needed).
2. **BIBLE READER** — Just the Christmas story again. Told well, but nothing I have not heard every December.
3. **CURIOSITY GAP — 6/10.** "Why that town?" is a real question, but a small one. It is answered at second 20. After that there is no open gap. The ending line is the strongest single sentence in the pack, though.
4. **ACCURATE + HONEST**
   - Angel line — verbatim Luke 2:11. OK.
   - Micah quote — same hidden cut. Minor.
   - "every man went to his family's town" — KJV: "all went to be taxed, every one into his own city". "Family's town" is a fair gloss from 2:4 ("house and lineage of David"). "Every man" is a small change from "every one".
   - "far from home" — true (Galilee to Judaea) but not a KJV phrase. Fine.
   - "small town" — fits "little among the thousands of Judah". OK.
   - No overclaim, no invented detail, no group blame. The cleanest of the three.
5. **GRACE** — Yes. "This Saviour, Jesus" picks up the angel's own word, so the cross line is earned. No pressure, no stock line.

## RANK

**1. Y  2. Z  3. X**

- Y has the only hook that holds to the end, something new for a Bible reader, and an ending that is grace.
- Z is the safest and clearest, but it is the story everyone knows.
- X has the smartest idea and the most confusing telling.

## The ONE fix for #1 (Y)

The overclaim. Change:

> They knew the verse. (pause) **They did not go to Him.** But He had already come to them.

to:

> They knew the verse. (pause) **No verse says they went to Him.** But He had already come to them.

It keeps the punch, matches the honest line 1, and adds an echo (knew the verse / no verse).

## What would still confuse a newcomer in Y

- **"They"** in line 1 — nobody knows who. Say "The religious experts knew the exact town..." (second most needed fix).
- **Two kings** in one sentence: King Herod and "the promised King".
- **"He lived before He was born"** — sounds like a puzzle. No time to think about it.
- **"out of thee shall he come forth unto me"** — old English, said fast.
- The last sentence jumps from a birth to a death with no bridge. A newcomer does not know why He died.
