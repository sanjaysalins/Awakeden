# Blind judge: Script P vs Script Q

## SCRIPT P

**1. Newcomer**
- **Who:** Mary, Joseph, baby Jesus. **What:** a tax order sent them to Bethlehem, and an old promise named that town. **Why it matters:** "God kept the promise." That much comes through.
- **Hard words:** "thou," "come forth unto me," "Christ the Lord." "Christ" is never explained.
- **Confusing line:** in "For unto you is born…", I don't know who "you" is. The shepherds are never mentioned.
- **Ending:** "died for our sins and rose the third day" comes from nowhere. Nothing in the story leads to it.

**2. Bible reader:** This is the Christmas story again. "An emperor gave the order. God kept the promise" is a nice line, but most Bible readers already know that idea.

**3. Curiosity gap: 5/10.** "Why that town?" is a mild question. The answer ("Micah named it") is correct but flat. There is no twist.

**4. Accurate and honest:** No misquotes.
- The angel line is word for word Luke 2:11.
- The Micah ellipsis drops "Ephratah, though thou be little… yet." That's acceptable because it is marked.
- "The Roman emperor ordered everyone taxed in their own city" mixes Luke 2:1 (the order) with Luke 2:3 (people going to their own city). Small, but fine.
- "About seven hundred years" is fair.
- No blame on any group.

**5. Grace:** No pressure and no stock "come to Jesus" line. But the cross and resurrection line is tacked on, not earned.

**6. Length:** About 130 words, so about 55 seconds. It fits. If it runs long, cut "Mary and Joseph lived in Nazareth."

**7. Overall: 5.5/10**

## SCRIPT Q

**1. Newcomer**
- **Who:** religious experts, King Herod, Jesus. **What:** the experts knew the town, but the Bible never says they went. **Why it matters:** Jesus was alive before he was born, and came to die and rise.
- **Gap 1:** Why did Herod ask? The wise men who started it are left out, so the question seems to come from nowhere.
- **Confusing line:** "But Jesus had already come." Come where? It is unclear.
- **Hard words:** "from everlasting" is hard. But "He was alive before He was born" explains it in plain words, which is good.

**2. Bible reader:** Yes, there is something new here. They knew the address and still didn't go. And "from everlasting" turns a birth-town prophecy into a claim about who Jesus is. Most people skip that half of Micah 5:2.

**3. Curiosity gap: 7.5/10.** The first line makes me stay. "They knew where. (pause) But Jesus had already come" pays it off as irony. It does not explain *why* they didn't go, and that's honest, because the text doesn't say.

**4. Accurate and honest:**
- "In Bethlehem of Judaea" is word for word Matthew 2:5.
- "The Bible never says they went to look" is true. Matthew 2 is silent, and the script says "never says," not "they refused." Keep that wording.
- "Herod asked the experts where this promised ruler would be born" is a fair paraphrase of 2:4 ("where Christ should be born").
- "Micah said this ruler came 'from everlasting'" is a loose paraphrase. The KJV says "whose goings forth have been from of old, from everlasting." The quoted words themselves are exact.
- "He was alive before He was born" is a Christian reading of that line, and a sound one (John 1, John 8:58). Some read "from of old" as ancient family line. So it's a small stretch, but not false.
- Blame: "religious experts" means the chief priests and scribes, not "the Jews." That's safe.

**5. Grace:** Yes. "He came as a baby so He could die for our sins" grows out of the "everlasting" line. There is no pressure and no stock call.

**6. Length:** About 115 words, so about 50 seconds. Nothing needs cutting, and there is room to add one line.

**7. Overall: 7.5/10**

---

## RANK

**#1 is Q. #2 is P.**

**The one fix Q needs most:** give the reason Herod asked. Replace:
> "When Jesus was born there, King Herod asked the experts where this promised ruler would be born."

with:
> "After Jesus was born there, visitors from the east came asking for the newborn King. So King Herod asked the experts where this ruler would be born."

That's word count only, and the extra line keeps it under 60 seconds.

**Other things that would confuse a newcomer in Q:**
- "But Jesus had already come" is vague. Better: "But Jesus was already there."
- "from everlasting" needs the very next line to explain it. Keep "He was alive before He was born" right after it.
- "thou" in the prophet line. It is fine to quote once, but nothing should depend on it.
