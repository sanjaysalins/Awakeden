SCRIPT P

**1. Newcomer**
After one listen I can say it.
Who: Mary, Joseph, and their baby Jesus. Micah, a man who spoke God's message. A Roman emperor.
What: A tax sent them from Nazareth to Bethlehem, King David's town. Jesus was born there. About seven hundred years earlier, Micah had named that town.
Why: An emperor gave the order. God kept the promise. Jesus died for our sins and rose.
Lines I would not understand:
- "But thou, Bethlehem... out of thee shall he come forth unto me that is to be ruler in Israel."
- "For unto you is born this day in the city of David a Saviour, which is Christ the Lord."
- "rose the third day."

**2. Bible reader**
This is the Christmas story I already know. The tax, Nazareth, Bethlehem, Micah, and the angel's words. The "why that town" frame is tidy. Little here is new to me.

**3. Curiosity gap**
"Why that town?" makes me stay. The ending pays it off: David's family, David's town, Micah had named it, then "An emperor gave the order. God kept the promise."
Score: 8

**4. Accurate + honest**
The prophet's words match Micah 5:2. The skip is marked by "...". It leaves out "Ephratah" and "though thou be little among the thousands of Judah."
The angel's words match Luke 2:11. In that chapter he says them to shepherds. The film never says shepherds, so "unto you" floats.
"Taxed," "his own city," "house and lineage of David," and "the city of David, which is called Bethlehem" match Luke 2:1-5.
"Roman emperor" is plain speech for Caesar Augustus.
"Mary and Joseph lived in Nazareth" fits Joseph leaving Nazareth with Mary, great with child.
"About seven hundred years" is outside these verses. It is the usual date for Micah.
"Died for our sins and rose the third day" is outside these verses. It does not twist the birth story.
No blame of a people.

**5. Grace**
The ending lands on Jesus. No "come to Jesus" line. No push at the viewer.

**6. Length**
About 128 words. Plain speech is near 55 seconds. The pause and the two slow old lines can reach 60. Time it once. If it runs long, cut "a man who spoke God's message."

**7. Score: 8**

SCRIPT Q

**1. Newcomer**
After one fast listen I cannot give a clean who, what, and why.
Who I catch: Jesus, Micah, King Herod, and some religious experts.
What tangles. They knew a town. Herod asks them the town. They say Bethlehem. Then "Jesus had already come." Come where?
Why, only at the end: he was alive before he was born, he died for our sins, he rose. I half get that.
Lines I would not understand:
- "The Bible never says they went to look."
- "But thou, Bethlehem... out of thee shall he come forth unto me that is to be ruler in Israel."
- "In Bethlehem of Judaea."
- "from everlasting"
- "He was alive before He was born."
- "rose the third day."

**2. Bible reader**
There is something new here. The experts' answer to Herod, and Micah's "from everlasting," sit outside the usual manger retell. A reader will look for the wise men. Matthew 2:1-11 puts them in this same scene. The film leaves them out.

**3. Curiosity gap**
The first line makes me stay. It also spends the surprise at once.
The ending leaves "they never went to look" and turns to everlasting, the baby, the cross, and the rising.
"They knew where. But Jesus had already come." is a soft button.
Score: 5

**4. Accurate + honest**
"In Bethlehem of Judaea" matches Matthew 2:5.
The prophet's words match Micah 5:2, with the same marked skip as P.
Micah 5:2 says "whose goings forth have been from of old, from everlasting." The film says Micah said this ruler came "from everlasting," then "He was alive before He was born." The two quoted words are in the verse. The rest is an explanation, spoken as Micah's meaning.
"The Bible never says they went to look" is a silence. Matthew 2 does not send the chief priests and scribes to Bethlehem. It also does not say they refused. Matthew 2:8-11 shows the child could still be found.
"But Jesus had already come" makes the birth sound like a shut door.
"Religious experts" means those chief priests and scribes. The short lays fault on them through that silence. It does not name the Jewish people.
"Promised King" blends the wise men's "King of the Jews," Herod's "Christ," and Micah's "ruler."
"So He could die for our sins" is a purpose these verses do not state.
"About seven hundred years" sits outside the verses, same as P.

**5. Grace**
The last sentence lands on Jesus. There is no "come to Jesus" line. The opening still points a finger at the experts.

**6. Length**
About 120 words. It fits under 60 seconds. The last sentence is 32 words and holds three ideas. That pack is the problem.

**7. Score: 6**
