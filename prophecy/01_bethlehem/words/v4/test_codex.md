X

1. NEWCOMER: Religious experts knew the promised King’s birthplace. Visitors went to find Jesus; whether the experts went is unknown. Jesus existed before His birth, came from heaven, died for our sins, and rose again. “Bethlehem would be His birthplace, not His beginning” takes thought: it means He existed before becoming a baby. Why His death deals with sins is left unexplained.

2. NEW TO YOU: For a regular churchgoer, the likely fresh angle is that Scripture does not record the experts visiting. The main teaching is familiar. 2/10.

3. CURIOSITY: 8/10. “Did they go?” creates a clear question. The answer honestly stops at what is recorded. The ending gives a satisfying reversal of who went to whom, though the experts’ actual actions remain unknown.

4. HARD WORDS / CONFUSING LINES: “From everlasting” means eternally, but sounds unfamiliar. “In Bethlehem of Judaea” introduces an unexplained place name. “Galilee” needs the map. “Our sins” assumes some religious vocabulary.

5. BIBLE: No clear contradiction. “Some questioned Jesus” can suggest they addressed Him directly; John describes people debating about Him. “Micah added” introduces words from the same sentence, so it should not suggest a separate prophecy. The empty footprints could imply the experts definitely stayed away, which the text does not establish.

6. Overall: 8/10.

Y

1. NEWCOMER: Experts knew where the promised King would be born, and visitors found Jesus there. Later, people doubted Him because they associated Him with another place. Jesus came from heaven and died and rose for us. “Shall Christ come out of Galilee?” is difficult: I need to know that “Christ” refers to the promised King. “The verse said Bethlehem” leaves me asking which verse.

2. NEW TO YOU: The same potentially fresh observation about the experts’ unrecorded visit. The quoted crowd question is familiar church material. 2/10.

3. CURIOSITY: 7/10. The opening works, and the ending echoes it. The unfamiliar quotation creates an extra puzzle between the opening question and its final echo.

4. HARD WORDS / CONFUSING LINES: “Shall Christ come out of Galilee?” combines an unfamiliar title, a place name, and older English. “The verse said Bethlehem” has a vague reference. “From everlasting” and “Judaea” carry the same difficulties as X.

5. BIBLE: The crowd quotation is exact. “Some questioned Jesus” still risks implying a direct exchange. “The verse said Bethlehem” fairly summarizes the following verse, although viewers never hear that supporting quotation. The experts’ absence remains unproved; the narration correctly acknowledges this.

6. Overall: 7/10.

PICK X: It explains the crowd’s objection with fewer vocabulary barriers.
