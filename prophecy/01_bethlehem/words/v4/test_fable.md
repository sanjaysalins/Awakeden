# Blind review — seat: Fable

## X

1. **NEWCOMER.** Who: a King called Jesus, some Bible experts, some wise men. What: a prophet named the town long before; the King was born there; the experts knew the town but (it seems) never went; strangers did. Why: Jesus came a long way to us before anyone came to Him.
   Lines I would not follow:
   - "Years later, some questioned Jesus: the King comes from Bethlehem, not Galilee. He was born in Bethlehem." By ear there is no colon. I cannot tell who is speaking, and nobody has told me Jesus lived in Galilee, so I do not see the problem.
   - "ruler in Israel." / "from everlasting." Two bare fragments. I catch the sound, not the sense.
2. **NEW TO YOU: 6.** New: the experts gave the right answer and the text never shows them walking the short road. "Birthplace, not His beginning" is a fresh way to say an old truth. The Galilee beat adds nothing new in this form.
3. **CURIOSITY: 7.** "Did they go?" opens a real question. It is answered mid-film ("never says the experts went"), and the last line turns it round well. The Galilee beat sits between and cools it.
4. **HARD WORDS.** "from everlasting" (rare word, no gloss until the next line). "Bethlehem of Judaea" (captions will spell it oddly; fine as a quote). "Galilee" (never explained in words; the map must carry it).
5. **BIBLE.**
   - "some questioned Jesus" — overclaim. In John 7:41-43 the people argue among themselves about Him. Nobody questions Him.
   - "the King comes from Bethlehem" — John says "Christ", not King. A paraphrase dressed as their words.
   - "Bethlehem was close by." True in fact, but not in the KJV text given. Keep it; just know it is outside knowledge.
   - "Micah added:" — it is one verse, one sentence. Small. Acceptable.
   - "The Bible never says the experts went." Honest wording. Good. It does not claim a motive.
6. **Overall: 6.5**

## Y

1. **NEWCOMER.** Same who / what / why as X. The crowd voice makes it clear that other people are doubting Him.
   Lines I would not follow:
   - "Shall Christ come out of Galilee?" I think "Christ" is Jesus's last name, so the question sounds odd. "Shall" is old English. And I still have not been told He lived in Galilee.
   - "The verse said Bethlehem." Which verse? Micah was called a promise, not a verse.
2. **NEW TO YOU: 6.5.** Same as X, plus hearing the crowd's real words makes the irony land: they used the right verse to reach the wrong answer.
3. **CURIOSITY: 7.** Same as X. The crowd voice keeps more energy through the weak middle.
4. **HARD WORDS.** "Shall Christ come out of Galilee?" ("Shall", "Christ" as a title, "Galilee"). "from everlasting". "Judaea".
5. **BIBLE.** Crowd line is word-for-word KJV. Good. "some questioned Jesus" has the same overclaim as X. "The verse said Bethlehem. He was born there." is correct (John 7:42, Matthew 2:1). "Bethlehem was close by" and "Micah added" as in X.
6. **Overall: 7.5**

## PICK: Y

Why: the real crowd voice is exact Scripture and clear by ear; X's Galilee line is a run-on that sounds like the narrator arguing with himself.

**One small change (exact wording).** Replace
`[narrator] Years later, some questioned Jesus:`
with
`[narrator] Jesus grew up north, in Galilee. Years later, some doubted Him:`

This gives the newcomer the missing fact (why Galilee matters) and removes the overclaim that they questioned Him directly.

Still weak after that fix: "The verse said Bethlehem" (better: "The promise said Bethlehem"), and the two bare prophet fragments.
