## X

**1. Newcomer.** Experts knew a promised King would be born in Bethlehem. Jesus was born there. Foreign visitors went to find Him, but the experts apparently did not. The ending says God came to people first.
Lines I would not understand:
- "ruler in Israel." and "from everlasting." are cut-off phrases, and "everlasting" is a hard word. "Birthplace, not beginning" mostly rescues it.
- "Shall Christ come out of Galilee?" I would not know that "Christ" means the same as "the King". I would also miss why this argument matters.
- "Bethlehem was close by." Close to what? Jerusalem is never said out loud. Only the map shows it.

**2. New to me:** 4/10. "Birthplace, not beginning" is a neat line. "The Bible never says the experts went" is a fresh, honest angle. The rest is familiar.

**3. Curiosity:** 7/10. "Did they go?" opens a real question. The middle answers it, and the last line ("Before anyone went to Him, He came to us") turns it into a reversal. It lands, but the John 7 part is a slight detour.

**4. Hard words:** "everlasting", "Christ", "Shall… come out of" (old English), "Judaea" (captions may print "Judea" or worse), "Galilee". Four old-English quotes in 59 seconds is heavy for a second-language listener.

**5. Bible:**
- "The Bible never says the experts went" is accurate and carefully worded. Good.
- "Bethlehem was close by" is true (about 9 km), but it is not in the KJV text. That is acceptable.
- The John 7 line is quoted correctly. Nobody's motives are overclaimed.
- "Jesus grew up in Galilee" is supported by Luke 2:39 and Matthew 2:23.
- No errors.

**6. Overall:** 6.5/10

## Y

**1. Newcomer.** Same story as X. The difference is "can the King come from Galilee?", which I understand at once. It uses the word "King" that I already learned in line 1. I now see the point: people doubted Him because of where He grew up, but He was really born in the promised town.
Still unclear: "ruler in Israel." / "from everlasting." and "Bethlehem was close by."

**2. New to me:** 4/10, same as X.

**3. Curiosity:** 7.5/10. Same payoff as X. The Galilee beat now flows in one breath, so it feels less like a detour.

**4. Hard words:** "everlasting", "Judaea", "Galilee". There is no "Christ" and no "Shall". That is a clear win for captions and second-language listeners.

**5. Bible:**
- "can the King come from Galilee?" is a fair paraphrase. In John 7, "Christ" means the promised King. Because the narrator says it and not a "[crowd]" voice, it does not pretend to be a quote. That is honest.
- Same note as X on "close by".
- No errors.

**6. Overall:** 7/10

## Pick: Y

Why: it keeps the same Bible content but drops "Christ" and "Shall… come out of". Those are the two words most likely to lose a newcomer, and the "King" thread stays unbroken.

**One change (exact wording):**
"Bethlehem was close by." → "Bethlehem was close to Jerusalem."

This names Herod's city out loud, so "close" means something even without the map. It also makes it sharper that the experts did not go a short distance.
