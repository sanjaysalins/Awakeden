# Retest review (Sonnet) - X vs Y

## X
1. NEWCOMER: A prophet named Micah said a king would be born in Bethlehem. Jesus was born there. The religious experts knew this but (the Bible does not say) went to see him; the foreign wise men did. Jesus later came from heaven, died, rose. God came to us first. Unclear lines: "ruler in Israel." and "from everlasting." arrive as bare fragments after "God promised a King from there:" - fine with captions, but "Bethlehem would be His birthplace, not His beginning" is dense. "Shall Christ come out of Galilee?" - "Christ" is unexplained; a newcomer may read it as a name, not a title.
2. NEW TO YOU: Mild. "The Bible never says the experts went" and the birthplace-not-beginning point are fresh to many. Galilee objection linked to Bethlehem is somewhat known. 5/10.
3. CURIOSITY: "Did they go?" opens a clear question. Payoff comes at "The Bible never says the experts went" - honest but a soft answer (silence, not yes/no); the closing "Before anyone went to Him, He came to us" reframes it well. 7/10.
4. HARD WORDS: "everlasting", "Christ", "Galilee", "Judaea" (caption spelling), "ruler". "Shall" in the crowd line is archaic.
5. BIBLE: "The Bible experts knew where the King would be born" - OK (Mt 2:5). "The Bible never says the experts went" - accurate, argument from silence stated as such. "found the child" - KJV says "in a house", fine. "He was born there" - supported. "God's prophet Micah named a small town" fine. No "Micah added". Slight: "Later, people argued" ok per John 7. No wrong claims.
6. Overall: 7.5/10.

## Y
1. NEWCOMER: Same story. The Galilee objection is paraphrased as "can the King come from Galilee?" - clearer for a newcomer, no "Christ". No unclear lines beyond the shared ones above.
2. NEW TO YOU: Same content as X. 5/10.
3. CURIOSITY: Same hook and payoff. 7/10.
4. HARD WORDS: Same list minus "Christ" and archaic "Shall". Fewer failures for ESL and auto-captions.
5. BIBLE: Paraphrase of John 7:41 uses "King" for "Christ" - acceptable gloss, though the KJV speaker said "Christ" (Messiah). Loses the KJV quote but nothing wrong. The paraphrase "people argued" slightly flattens the crowd's voice. No overclaim.
6. Overall: 7.8/10.

## PICK: Y
Why: identical to X except it removes the unexplained "Christ" and archaic "Shall" for a worldwide audience, and one fewer voice swap.
Small change to Y: "Later, people argued: can the King come from Galilee?" -> "Later, some people argued: how can the promised King come from Galilee?" (adds "promised", ties to the end card PROMISED.)
