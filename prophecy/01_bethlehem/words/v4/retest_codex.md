HARD WORDS / CONFUSING LINES: “From everlasting” is archaic. “Bethlehem would be His birthplace, not His beginning” requires an inference about existence before birth. “Shall Christ come out of Galilee?” combines unfamiliar vocabulary with an old sentence structure. “Judaea,” “Micah,” and “Galilee” could challenge listeners and automatic captions.

5. BIBLE: No clear contradiction. “The Bible never says the experts went” correctly distinguishes an unrecorded visit from a claim that they refused. The eternal-existence reading of Micah is an interpretation consistent with this script’s Christian framing.

6. Overall: 7/10.

Y

1. NEWCOMER: God promised a King from Bethlehem, and that King was Jesus. Experts knew the prophecy; wise men acted on it. People later associated Jesus with Galilee, although He was born in Bethlehem. He came to save people through His death and resurrection. “From everlasting” remains unclear until I work out the next sentence.

2. NEW TO YOU: The same potentially fresh observation about the experts’ unrecorded visit. Most regular churchgoers will know the other teaching. 2/10.

3. CURIOSITY: The opening works, and the ending provides a meaningful reversal. The experts’ actual decision remains unknown. The simpler Galilee wording makes the intervening section easier to follow. 8/10.

4. HARD WORDS / CONFUSING LINES: “From everlasting,” “Judaea,” and “His birthplace, not His beginning” remain obstacles. “Can the King come from Galilee?” is much easier than X’s quotation. “Our sins” assumes some understanding of religious language.

5. BIBLE: No clear contradiction. “Can the King come from Galilee?” is a reasonable contextual paraphrase of the crowd’s question, rather than an exact KJV quotation. The same qualifications about the experts, geography, and Micah apply.

6. Overall: 8/10.

PICK: Y. It removes an unexplained title and an archaic question without losing the story.

ONE small change: Replace “Bethlehem would be His birthplace, not His beginning” with “Jesus was born in Bethlehem, but He had always existed.”
