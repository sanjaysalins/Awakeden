# Retest — independent blind seat (Fable)

## X

1. **NEWCOMER.** Who: a promised King, Jesus. What: long ago a prophet named the town where He would be born. Experts knew the town but (it seems) never went. Strangers went and found Him. Why: He came from heaven, died and rose, so He came to us first.
   Lines I would not understand:
   - "Shall Christ come out of Galilee?" — who is "Christ"? Until now He is "the King" and "Jesus". A new name arrives with no bridge. "Shall" is also old English.
   - "from everlasting." — as a two-word fragment it means nothing alone. The next line rescues it, just.
   - "It goes on:" — what goes on? (The promise.)
2. **NEW TO YOU: 6.** The experts knew the address and are never said to go. "Birthplace, not His beginning" is a neat way to say Micah's last phrase. The rest is familiar.
3. **CURIOSITY: 7.** "Did they go?" opens a real question. It is answered in the middle ("never says the experts went"), so the Galilee part sags. The last line pays it off again with a turn ("He came to us"). Good, not great.
4. **HARD WORDS.** "Shall Christ come out of Galilee?" (Christ + shall + "come out of"). "from everlasting". "Judaea" (captions will spell it oddly; harmless). "Galilee" is fine because the map shows it.
5. **BIBLE.**
   - "Bethlehem was close by." True, but not in the KJV text given. Low risk; the map carries it.
   - "The Bible never says the experts went." Correct and carefully worded. But the hook plus the pause push the viewer to hear "they did not go". That is an argument from silence. Keep the wording exactly this careful.
   - "God promised a King" — KJV says "ruler". Fair.
   - No "Micah added" problem. No motives claimed. Good.
6. **Overall: 7.**

## Y

1. **NEWCOMER.** Same who / what / why as X. The Galilee part is clearer: people argued because He grew up in the wrong place, but the promise said Bethlehem, and He was born there. I follow it in one hearing.
   Lines I would not understand: "from everlasting." and "It goes on:" (same as X). Nothing else.
2. **NEW TO YOU: 6.** Same as X.
3. **CURIOSITY: 7.** Same shape as X. The Galilee part moves faster, so the sag is shorter.
4. **HARD WORDS.** "from everlasting". "Judaea". That is all. "The King" stays one name the whole way — big win for second-language viewers.
5. **BIBLE.**
   - "can the King come from Galilee?" is a paraphrase of "Shall Christ come out of Galilee?" It is in the narrator's voice, not shown as a quote, so it is honest. "Christ" = the promised King; fair. Do NOT put it on screen in quote marks or with "John 7:41" as if verbatim.
   - Same two notes as X ("close by", "never says").
   - Y loses the [crowd] voice, so one less KJV quote. Acceptable cost.
6. **Overall: 8.**

## PICK: Y

Why: one name for Him all the way ("the King"), no unexplained "Christ", no "shall" — the Galilee beat lands in one hearing.

ONE small change (exact wording), last sentence of the Galilee line:

> Later, people argued: can the King come from Galilee? The promise said Bethlehem. **And Jesus was born in Bethlehem.**

(Replaces "He was born there." — "there" can be heard as Galilee by a second-language listener; naming the town twice closes the loop.)

Still weak in both: "from everlasting." as a bare fragment. The line after it does the real work.

Note: these are simulated viewers, not real ones. Test with a few real seed viewers.
