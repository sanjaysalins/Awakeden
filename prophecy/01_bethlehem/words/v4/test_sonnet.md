# Blind review (Sonnet seat)

## X

1. NEWCOMER: Experts knew where a King would be born, but did they go to see him? A prophet named a small town, Bethlehem. The King was born there; wise men went and found him. Later, people doubted Jesus because of Galilee. Jesus came from heaven, died, rose. He came to us before we came to him. Confusing: "Bethlehem would be His birthplace, not His beginning" (needs "from everlasting" to land; fine). "Years later, some questioned Jesus: the King comes from Bethlehem, not Galilee. He was born in Bethlehem." Muddled: who says what? The doubters' claim and the reply run together with no clear voice. "the experts" is vague (which experts?).
2. NEW TO YOU: Mildly new: the experts knew but did not go (Matthew never says they went); the Galilee objection answered by the birthplace. Many churchgoers have heard the first point. 5/10.
3. CURIOSITY: "Did they go?" opens a real gap. The answer ("The Bible never says the experts went") comes mid-film, and the ending "Before anyone went to Him, He came to us" reframes it nicely. 7/10.
4. HARD WORDS: "everlasting" (clear in context via "not His beginning"), "prophet", "Judaea" (captions may garble), "Galilee" (no map knowledge, but the pictures help), "ruler in Israel" is a fragment. "ruler in Israel." / "from everlasting." as bare quoted fragments are hard to follow by ear. The Galilee line in X is the weakest: no quote, hard to parse.
5. BIBLE: "The Bible experts knew where the King would be born": supported (Matt 2:5). "Micah added" implies a separate statement; it is the same verse, slightly loose but acceptable. "Years later, some questioned Jesus: the King comes from Bethlehem, not Galilee": John 7:41 says they asked "Shall Christ come out of Galilee?", which is a question, and they did not know he was born in Bethlehem. X's paraphrase states it as a flat claim, slightly flattened. "found the child" ok (house, not manger; the picture shows a manger, a small visual mismatch, not a script error). "The Bible never says the experts went": true, an argument from silence, stated honestly. "Bethlehem was close by" is outside KJV given, but true (about 6 miles), fine. Overall X is a bit overclaimed in the Galilee line.
6. Overall: 6.5/10.

## Y

1. NEWCOMER: Same story as X. The crowd line "Shall Christ come out of Galilee?" is a clear question in its own voice, then "The verse said Bethlehem. He was born there." resolves it. Much clearer. Small gap: "The verse" is unexplained for a newcomer (which verse?).
2. NEW TO YOU: Same as X, slightly higher because the Galilee exchange is shown as a gap in the crowd's knowledge. 5.5/10.
3. CURIOSITY: Same opening. Y adds a second small question (Galilee) answered immediately. Ending pays off the same. 7/10.
4. HARD WORDS: Same as X, but the quote "Shall Christ come out of Galilee?" uses "Christ" and "Galilee" for a newcomer; "Christ" is not explained (the film calls him Jesus and King; a newcomer may not connect). "The verse said Bethlehem" is vague.
5. BIBLE: Closer to John 7:41 (quote is exact). "The verse said Bethlehem. He was born there." is accurate to John 7:42 and the narrator supplies what the crowd did not know. Slight implication that the crowd lacked the facts is fair. No notable overclaim.
6. Overall: 7.5/10.

## PICK: Y

Why: it quotes John 7:41 faithfully in its own voice and the answer is clean, which beats X's muddled paraphrase.

Small change: replace "The verse said Bethlehem. He was born there." with "But the Bible said Bethlehem, and He was born there."
