Cold viewer: Ana, 23, on a bus in São Paulo, sound low, knows the Christmas picture, has never heard of Micah. She decides inside lines 1–2. At 3.0s the spoken line is still “would be born.” The twist arrives at line 3 (4.9s), after the swipe window. Slow pan, scholars at a table, no question. Manga art and the grey pills save some muted viewers. Most Anas are gone.

Stops next at line 4 (the “700 YEARS EARLIER” card freezes the story she just started) and at line 7 (7.3s of “thou / thee” over a lamp and a scroll).

Finishes: a church adult who already loves Micah 5:2 and wants the gold tags.

Shares: that same adult, on the strength of line 12’s cold king and “They knew the town.” The last frame (line 21) is a generic risen-Jesus glow, so the share is weaker than the film’s best idea.

Left out: Ana, and any parent who came from the title wanting a birth story for a child. Lines 18–20 become crosses, a tomb, and “our sins” with no warning. Herod is never given a reason to fear him.

## 2. MAIN TAKEAWAY (one sentence a viewer could say back; which line carries it; did the film make it clear?)

Best sentence in the film: “They knew the town, the Bible never says they went, and the child was already there.”

That sentence is split across line 3, line 12, and line 14. No one line holds it. Lines 16–17 add “he did not begin in Bethlehem.” Lines 18–20 add death and resurrection. A stranger walks away saying the title: “Jesus was born in Bethlehem.” The ending picture (line 21) is what they keep.

## 3. GLOBAL AUDIENCE (who / what / why in my own words; where I got lost or misled, quote the line; words that fail in translation)

Who: teachers in a stone hall, a prophet named Micah, King Herod, travelers from the east, a baby and his parents. What: Micah named Bethlehem about 700 years before the birth. Herod’s teachers still knew the name. Travelers came looking. The baby was already in the town. Then the film says his life started before that night, and that he died and rose. Why it should matter: the people with the address stayed put.

Lost at line 7: “out of thee shall he come forth unto me that is to be ruler in Israel.” On one listen I cannot tell who “me” is. Misled by the title, then by line 9: the visitors “came looking,” and the film never shows them arrive. Line 18 jumps from a baby to three crosses in one second.

Words that fail: “religious experts” (line 1), “thou / thee / unto” (line 7), “Judaea” (line 11), “from everlasting” (line 16), “for our sins” (line 19).

## 4. BIBLICALLY ACCURATE (claim by claim: RIGHT / OVERCLAIMED / UNSUPPORTED / WRONG, quote the line; say only what you can support)

- “knew the exact town where God’s promised King would be born” (lines 1–2): **RIGHT.** Matthew 2:4–6. They answer from Micah.
- “The Bible never says they went to look” (line 3): **RIGHT.** The text is silent. The film stays inside that silence.
- “About seven hundred years earlier” (line 4): **RIGHT** as a round figure. Micah 1:1 sits near 735–700 BC.
- “named one small town” (line 6): **RIGHT** via Micah 5:2 “little,” though the on-screen quote cuts that word, so the picture-scroll does not show “small.”
- Line 7 quote: **RIGHT** as a shortened KJV Micah 5:2. The ellipsis is fair.
- “When Jesus was born there, visitors from the east came looking” (lines 8–9): **OVERCLAIMED** on timing. Matthew 2:1–11 has them arrive later, at a house, to a “young child.” Luke’s manger night is the shepherds. Ox and donkey in the line 8 picture: **UNSUPPORTED.**
- “Herod asked… In Bethlehem of Judaea” (lines 10–11): **RIGHT.** Matthew 2:4–5. One “scribe” voice stands in for chief priests and scribes.
- “They knew the town. And Jesus was already there” (lines 12, 14): **RIGHT.**
- “this ruler is from everlasting” (line 16): **OVERCLAIMED.** Micah 5:2 says his goings forth are “from of old, from everlasting,” which is a harder, debated line than “this ruler is everlasting.”
- “He did not begin in Bethlehem” (line 17): **OVERCLAIMED.** An inference piled on that shortening. Micah does not say that sentence.
- “came as a baby, died for our sins, and rose… the third day” (lines 18–20): **RIGHT** as 1 Corinthians 15:3–4, and **UNSUPPORTED** inside this short. No verse on screen, and it is a different story than Matthew 2.

## 5. BEST VERSION POSSIBLE (what would the best explainer in the world do differently; the ONE highest-value change; score 0-10 against that bar)

The hook is late, the middle is a reading, and the peak is overwritten.

Second by second: lines 1–2 are a label. Line 3 is the only reason to stay, and it is already past 3 seconds. Line 4 pauses the sting for a history lesson. Lines 5–6 are a useful gloss that runs long. Line 7 is the big dead zone: 7.3 seconds, old English, scroll unfurling, face and lamp. Lines 8–13 rebuild a story the opening already promised, and they work. Line 14 is the payoff, paced right, quiet, worth a hold. Then lines 15–17 open a second film (galaxy, “everlasting”). Lines 18–20 speed-run the whole gospel in under 5 seconds. Line 21 holds silence for 3.1 seconds. No last word, no loop back to the empty road.

**One change:** Cold-open on line 3 fused with line 1, and cut to black on line 14. Speak by 1.5s: “They knew the exact town. The Bible never says they went.” Land by 25s on the mother: “And Jesus was already there.” One irony, paid once. The KJV block becomes six modern words plus the Micah 5:2 tag. “From everlasting,” the crosses, and the risen glow become the next short.

Score against that bar: **4/10.** The sting (line 3) and the baby (line 14) are real. The shape around them spends the audience.

Cold scroll, sound on, not a subscriber. Swipe before 3s: **60% ± 12%.** Watch to 51.6s: **15% ± 6%.** Share: **0.5% ± 0.3%.** Warm subscribers of this channel: swipe before 3s **35% ± 10%**, finish **32% ± 10%**, share **2% ± 1%.** Title “Born in Bethlehem” as a stranger’s reason to stop: **about 1 in 9 (11% ± 5%).** It names a fact the world has already heard.

## CURIOSITY GAP (opens at line __, pays off at line __; score 0-10; worth the wait?)

Opens at **line 3**. Pays, partly, at **line 14**. Score **5/10.** “Which town?” is dead on arrival because the title already says Bethlehem.
