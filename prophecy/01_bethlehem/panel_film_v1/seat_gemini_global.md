## 1. WHO IS THIS FOR
The scrolling mobile viewer who knows the traditional Christmas story but has never considered the irony of the religious elite. They stop at the hook in line 1-3 because it reframes a familiar tale as a mystery of apathy. Believers will finish and share this for the theological pivot at line 17. It does NOT serve viewers seeking secular history, as the final third (lines 18-20) transitions abruptly into a gospel summary without historical argumentation.

## 2. MAIN TAKEAWAY
The religious leaders ignored the exact King they were waiting for—a King prophesied centuries ago who actually has no beginning. The film makes this clear by contrasting line 3 ("The Bible never says they went to look") with line 17 ("He did not begin in Bethlehem").

## 3. GLOBAL AUDIENCE
I am a non-native English speaker relying on auto-captions or machine translation. I get lost at line 7: "But thou, Bethlehem... out of thee shall he come forth unto me". Archaic words like "thou" and constructions like "come forth unto me" will severely break machine translation and alienate international viewers. I am also misled at line 16: "from everlasting." In many languages, literal translations of "everlasting" imply "starting now and continuing forever into the future." The actual meaning here—"having no beginning" or "from eternity past"—is completely lost, undermining the payoff in line 17. 

## 4. BIBLICALLY ACCURATE
- Line 1-2: "The religious experts knew the exact town" -> RIGHT. Supported by Matthew 2:4-5.
- Line 3: "The Bible never says they went to look." -> RIGHT. Matthew 2 records the magi leaving for Bethlehem, but omits any mention of the scribes or chief priests accompanying them.
- Line 4: "About seven hundred years earlier" -> RIGHT. Micah prophesied in the late 8th century BC.
- Line 7: "But thou, Bethlehem..." -> RIGHT. Verbatim from Micah 5:2 (KJV).
- Line 14: "And Jesus was already there." -> RIGHT. Supported by Matthew 2:1.
- Line 16: "from everlasting." -> RIGHT. Verbatim from Micah 5:2 (KJV).
- Line 18-20: "died for our sins, and rose from the dead on the third day." -> RIGHT. Supported by 1 Corinthians 15:3-4.

## 5. BEST VERSION POSSIBLE
The best explainer in the world would fix the jarring transition between line 17 and line 18. Currently, the script beautifully builds a prophetic and geographic mystery, but then abruptly pastes a generic tract ending ("He came as a baby, died for our sins...") onto the final 10 seconds. The ONE highest-value change is linking Christ's eternal nature directly to his sacrifice, so the ending feels earned by the preceding 40 seconds rather than bolted on. Score: 7/10.
