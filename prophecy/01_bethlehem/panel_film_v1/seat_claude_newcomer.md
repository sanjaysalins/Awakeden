## 1. WHO IS THIS FOR
Named viewer: Ana, 34, a Christian mum in Brazil who knows the Christmas story. She stops at line 1 because she knows it. She finishes. She shares line 12 ("They knew the town") because it is a little sting she can use in a sermon chat.

Me, the newcomer: the art made me stop at line 1. I lost the thread around line 10, and I stayed only because the film is short. I would not share it, because I could not explain it to a friend. The film does not serve people who have never heard the story, and that is me.

## 2. MAIN TAKEAWAY
What I could say back: "Some experts knew where a special king would be born, but they did not go, and this king is Jesus, who is older than his birth and came back from the dead."

No one line carries all of this. Lines 12–14 carry half and lines 16–20 carry the other half. It feels like two films. I am not sure which half is the point.

## 3. GLOBAL AUDIENCE
**Who:** some old scholars, an old man called Micah, a baby called Jesus, travellers on camels, and a bad-looking king (line 12, cold eyes).
**What:** the old man named a town. Later the baby was born there. The king asked the scholars where.
**Why:** I don't know. Why did the travellers want a baby king? Why did the king care? Why did the scholars not go?

Where I got lost:
- Line 2, "God's promised King": promised to whom, and why?
- Line 3, "never says they went to look": look at what? Nothing has been born yet in the film.
- Line 7, "thou… thee… shall he come forth unto me": I could not read this English. I only caught "Bethlehem" and "ruler."
- Line 7, "Israel": a country? Today's Israel?
- Line 8, "Jesus": he is never introduced. Is he the king?
- Line 14, "already there": so what? I don't see why this matters.
- Line 16, "from everlasting": I don't know this word.
- Line 17, "did not begin in Bethlehem": then where did he begin?
- Lines 18–19: three crosses, but which one is him? "Our sins": whose, and why does his death fix them?
- Line 20, "third day": three days after what?

Words that fail in translation: thou, thee, come forth, unto, everlasting, Judaea, sins.

## 4. BIBLICALLY ACCURATE
I cannot judge accuracy. Here is what I was TOLD that I could not follow:
- Line 1: who the "religious experts" are.
- Line 3: why "the Bible never says" should matter to me.
- Line 9: how the visitors knew about a king.
- Line 12: why "they knew the town" is meant to sting.
- Lines 18–20: these three claims (born, died for sins, rose) arrive in 5 seconds with no reason given.

## 5. BEST VERSION POSSIBLE
A great explainer would ask ONE question and answer it. The film asks "why didn't they go?" and then leaves it for a second idea ("older than his birth"), then a third (cross and tomb).

The ONE highest-value change: after line 12, say plainly why it matters. For example: "They knew the town. They were one short walk away. They stayed home." That turns the hook into a payoff I can feel.

Score: **4/10**. The art is strong. The meaning does not land for a newcomer.

## CURIOSITY GAP
It opens at line 3 ("never says they went to look"). It half pays off at lines 12–14, but the film never says why it matters. A second gap opens at line 17 and pays off weakly at line 20.

Score: **4/10**. It is not quite worth the wait, because the answer is an echo of the question, not a reveal.

## THREE SMALLEST FIXES
1. **Line 8:** "When Jesus, the King Micah spoke of, was born there,"
2. **Line 7:** keep the old words, and add a plain pill under the scroll: "Bethlehem, a ruler will come from you."
3. **Line 16–17:** "Micah also said this ruler is from everlasting: he was alive before time began. He did not begin in Bethlehem."
