## 1. WHO IS THIS FOR

A church viewer who already knows Christmas, can read the English on the pictures, and likes a short sting. That person finishes and shares "they were so near." A Christian who speaks English as a second language can finish only by reading the on-screen lines.

Not served: a first-time viewer on auto-captions, anyone who cannot read the baked-in English, and a Jewish viewer. Scholars in prayer shawls, an empty road, and hollow footprints can be read as "the Jews knew and refused." No spoken line says that. The pictures can.

## 2. MAIN TAKEAWAY

"God promised a king from Bethlehem; strangers walked; Jesus came to us first." Line 14 is the line a viewer would repeat. The walk contrast is clear at lines 2, 11, and 12. Line 14 is not clear, because line 12 already showed people who went.

## 3. GLOBAL AUDIENCE

Who: Micah; Mary and Joseph; baby Jesus; Herod; camel visitors; scholars in Jerusalem. What: an old promise names Bethlehem, Jesus is born there, scholars tell Herod, the book never says those scholars went, the visitors did, then Jesus dies and rises. Why: the promise happened, and he moved first.

I got lost here:

- Line 3, "named a small town." "Named" also means "gave it its name." A translation will say Micah named the town Bethlehem.
- Line 6, "A tax order." In many countries that is a bill to pay, not a count of the people. The tax paper on screen teaches the wrong object.
- Line 8, "this King" and "Herod, the king there" in one breath. Two kings, one of them a baby.
- Line 9, "Judaea." Rare spelling. Captions will hear "Judea," "today," or a name. The viewer is not told it is the area around Jerusalem.
- Line 11, "never," underlined. That reads as "the Bible says they did not go."
- Line 13, "came much farther." That will be translated as a longer road on the map. "Rose" also means stood up, a flower, or the sun. "From the dead" saves it only if the caption keeps the whole clause.
- Line 14, "Before anyone went to Him," after line 12 showed the wise men going.

On-screen, "DID THEY GO?", "ABOUT 700 YEARS EARLIER", "WENT", and "PROMISED. CAME TRUE." are clear. "A SCRIBE" is a rare word; the narrator says "experts," the tag does not. "MICAH 5:2" and "MATTHEW 2:5" are insider codes. The voice never says "from the east" or "star," so "wise men" can mean "smart men," a second group of local experts. Camels fix that only if you are looking. Hollow white footprints can mean ghosts. Three crosses with no label are clear only to people who already know the sign. "Israel" in line 5 will be heard as the modern country.

## 4. BIBLICALLY ACCURATE

- Line 3, about seven hundred years, a small town: RIGHT as a round time and as Micah 5:2's little town. The verb "named" is the wrong action.
- Lines 4–5: RIGHT that Micah says "ruler in Israel." OVERCLAIMED if "King" is heard as Micah's word. The film does split the two words.
- Line 6, "A tax order sent them": OVERCLAIMED. Luke 2 is a registration. Joseph goes because his family is David's, not because a tax bill sent him.
- Line 7: RIGHT in Matthew's reading of the birth.
- Line 8: RIGHT (Matthew 2:1–4).
- Line 9: RIGHT. It matches Matthew 2:5. The word still fails on screen.
- Line 10, "close by": RIGHT.
- Line 11: RIGHT as silence. The empty road plus underlined "never" implies they refused. That implication is UNSUPPORTED.
- Line 12: RIGHT that they found the child (Matthew 2:9–11). The same page puts a manger, an ox, and a donkey beside the camels. The animals are not in Luke. Matthew's child is in a house, later. The page OVERCLAIMS the timing.
- Line 13: RIGHT as a compress of John 6:38 and 1 Corinthians 15:3–4. It is a second claim. Micah's promise did not say it.
- Line 14, "Before anyone went to Him": OVERCLAIMED. The wise men already went. Luke's shepherds go too.

## 5. BEST VERSION POSSIBLE

The best explainer would say one contrast out loud: short walk, the text does not say the experts walked, visitors from the east did, the birth is the promise. They would not put heaven, death, sins, and rising into eight seconds.

ONE change: rebuild lines 13–14 so the last sentence cannot mean "nobody went," and so "came true" stays the birth.

Score: 6/10.

## CURIOSITY GAP

Opens at line 2. Pays off at lines 11 and 12. Score: 6/10. The question is plain and the wait is short. "Go" never says "go to Bethlehem" in the voice, and the payoff is a gap in the text, smaller than the drum-stop around it.

## PROMISE KEPT

God, through Micah, promised a ruler from Bethlehem about 700 years before. Line 7 shows that birth as the promise coming true. The end card repeats it. From line 8, the film feels like a story about who went. Line 13 adds a promise the opening never made. Score: 6/10.

## THREE SMALLEST FIXES

- Line 3: "a prophet named Micah, a man who spoke God's message, spoke of a small town: Bethlehem."
- Line 6: "Mary and Joseph lived far away. An order to count the people sent them to Bethlehem."
- Line 11: "The Bible does not say that the experts went to Bethlehem." Do not underline "never."

## VERDICT

Clear enough for a Christian who already knows the story. Five traps remain for a first-time or translated viewer: "named," "tax order," "Judaea," underlined "never," and "before anyone went." 6/10.
