# Seat: Bible Insight Hunter (blind)

## 1. Nazareth heard a list of promises. Three chapters later, Jesus does them.

**A. THE INSIGHT**
Nazareth heard Jesus name two old miracles, Elijah's and Elisha's, and grew angry. A few chapters later Jesus does those same kinds of miracles, almost step by step.

**B. THE PROOF**
- Luke 4:26: "unto none of them was Elias sent, save unto Sarepta... unto a woman that was a widow."
- 1 Kings 17:10: "when he came to the gate of the city, behold, the widow woman was there."
- 1 Kings 17:23: Elijah "delivered him unto his mother: and Elijah said, See, thy son liveth."
- Luke 7:12: "when he came nigh to the gate of the city... the only son of his mother, and she was a widow."
- Luke 7:15: "And he delivered him to his mother."
- Luke 7:16: "a great prophet is risen up among us."
- Luke 7:22: "the blind see... the lepers are cleansed... the dead are raised, to the poor the gospel is preached."

The links:
- Same city gate.
- Same widow.
- Same dead son.
- The same words: "delivered him to his mother."
- The crowd calls Him "a great prophet." Nazareth would not accept their prophet (4:24). This crowd does.
- Luke 7:22 repeats the Luke 4:18 list (poor, blind) as a report of things already done.

**C. NEW TO WHOM**
Most churchgoers know Nain and Elijah's widow as two separate stories. Few notice Luke tying them together through Luke 4. Commentaries on Luke know this well.

**D. LANDS ON JESUS**
His sermon in Nazareth was not just talk. He kept every word of it, and He kept it for grieving outsiders.

**E. FILM LINE**
"Jesus named Elijah raising a widow's son. Nazareth was furious. Three chapters later, at a town gate, Jesus raises a widow's son, and gives him back to his mother."

**F. RISKS**
- Nain is in Israel. That widow was not a foreigner, so do not claim a full match.
- "Three chapters" is honest: Luke 4 to Luke 7.
- Luke 7:15 says "delivered him to his mother." 1 Kings 17:23 says "delivered him unto his mother." Quote each exactly.

**G. SCORES**
- Fresh to churchgoer: 8
- Certain in the text: 9
- Simple to say in 60s: 7

## 2. The foreign army captain Nazareth hated has a match in Acts

**A. THE INSIGHT**
Jesus reminded Nazareth that God healed a foreign army captain. Later, Peter tells a foreign army captain the same words, "God anointed Jesus of Nazareth."

**B. THE PROOF**
- Luke 4:27: "none of them was cleansed, saving Naaman the Syrian."
- 2 Kings 5:1: "Naaman, captain of the host of the king of Syria."
- Acts 10:1: "Cornelius, a centurion."
- Acts 10:34: "God is no respecter of persons."
- Acts 10:38: "How God anointed Jesus of Nazareth with the Holy Ghost and with power: who went about doing good, and healing all that were oppressed of the devil."

The links:
- The key words of Luke 4:18 come back: "anointed," "Spirit/Holy Ghost," "oppressed/bruised."
- They are told to a foreign soldier.
- And the town is named: "Jesus of Nazareth."

**C. NEW TO WHOM**
Most churchgoers never link Naaman to Cornelius. Scholars do see Acts 10:38 as an echo of Luke 4:18. Luke wrote both books.

**D. LANDS ON JESUS**
The town that pushed Him out became part of His name. And His welcome reached the very outsider Nazareth refused to picture.

**E. FILM LINE**
"Nazareth raged when Jesus praised a foreign soldier. Years later, Peter told another foreign soldier: 'God anointed Jesus of Nazareth.' The welcome reached the outsider."

**F. RISKS**
- Acts 10:38 does not quote Isaiah word for word. Call it an echo, not a quote.
- Do not claim Naaman "foretells" Cornelius. Say it is a pattern.

**G. SCORES**
- Fresh to churchgoer: 8
- Certain in the text: 8
- Simple to say in 60s: 7

## 3. "Proclaim liberty" is the Jubilee words, and the Jubilee began on the day of atonement

**A. THE INSIGHT**
The words Jesus read, "proclaim liberty," are the exact words of God's law for the Jubilee year. That was when debts were cancelled and people went home. And its trumpet was blown on the day sins were covered.

**B. THE PROOF**
- Isaiah 61:1: "to proclaim liberty to the captives."
- Isaiah 61:2: "the acceptable year of the LORD."
- Leviticus 25:9: "in the day of atonement shall ye make the trumpet sound throughout all your land."
- Leviticus 25:10: "proclaim liberty throughout all the land... it shall be a jubile unto you; and ye shall return every man unto his possession, and ye shall return every man unto his family."
- Jeremiah 34:8 uses the same phrase, "to proclaim liberty unto them."

**C. NEW TO WHOM**
A careful churchgoer may have heard "Jubilee" from a pulpit. The atonement-day timing is rarely said out loud. Most scholars teach the Isaiah 61 and Jubilee link.

**D. LANDS ON JESUS**
In God's law, freedom only came after sin was covered. Jesus announces the freedom. Then He becomes the atonement that opens it (Hebrews 9:12; 1 John 2:2).

**E. FILM LINE**
"'Proclaim liberty': God's words for the Jubilee year, when debts were wiped out and people went home. Its trumpet sounded on the day sins were covered."

**F. RISKS**
- "Acceptable year" = Jubilee is a reading, not a word-for-word match. "Proclaim liberty" is the exact shared phrase.
- Do not claim Jesus spoke on a Jubilee year. No one knows that.
- Name the KJV spelling "jubile" if the verse is shown on screen.

**G. SCORES**
- Fresh to churchgoer: 6
- Certain in the text: 8
- Simple to say in 60s: 8

## Noted, not ranked

Luke 4:18 adds a line Isaiah 61 does not have: "set at liberty them that are bruised." It comes from Isaiah 58:6, the chapter about the true sabbath (58:13). Jesus read it on a sabbath. But the link only shows in Greek, not in KJV English. That is too risky for a dubbed film.

Also, the "day of vengeance" is not dropped forever. Jesus says it later: "these be the days of vengeance" (Luke 21:22). Use this only if the film needs to answer "did He delete it?"

**YOUR PICK:** #1, the widow's son. The city gate and "delivered him to his mother" are plain in English and easy to show in pictures. It goes past the baseline: what Nazareth refused to hear, Jesus went and did, out of grace, for a grieving widow.
