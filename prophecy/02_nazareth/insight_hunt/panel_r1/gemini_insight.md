**1. THE GRACE FOR THE OUTSIDER (THE CLIFF)**
A. THE INSIGHT - The hometown crowd didn't try to kill Jesus because He claimed to be the promised Savior, but because He proved from history that God's grace was also for their worst enemies.
B. THE PROOF - At first, "all bare him witness, and wondered at the gracious words" (Luke 4:22). But they were "filled with wrath" (Luke 4:28) and tried to throw Him off a cliff (Luke 4:29) only *after* Jesus brought up Elijah being sent to a widow in "Sarepta, a city of Sidon" (Luke 4:26 / 1 Kings 17:9) and Elisha healing "Naaman the Syrian" (Luke 4:27 / 2 Kings 5:14). Sidon and Syria were Gentile enemies.
C. NEW TO WHOM - Known to commentators, but largely missed by regular churchgoers. Most readers assume the crowd got angry in verse 22 because Jesus claimed to be the Messiah. They miss the sudden, violent mood swing triggered entirely by the mention of grace for foreigners.
D. LANDS ON JESUS - Jesus refuses to let God's grace be a private, exclusive club for the religious or the respectable. His love relentlessly crosses enemy lines to reach the outsider, the outcast, and us.
E. FILM LINE - They loved His words until He mentioned a Syrian and a Sidonian. The crowd didn't try to kill Him for claiming to be God, but for offering grace to their enemies.
F. RISKS - It requires quickly explaining who Sidon and Syria were to a global audience, which burns precious seconds.
G. SCORES - fresh-to-churchgoer: 9, certain-in-text: 10, simple-to-say-in-60s: 7.

**2. THE WOVEN SCROLL (SIGHT AND BRUISES)**
A. THE INSIGHT - When reading the scroll, Jesus did not just read one straight sentence; He seamlessly wove three different ancient promises together to create a complete picture of how He would heal us.
B. THE PROOF - The prompt asks what Luke 4 adds to Isaiah 61. Luke 4:18 adds "recovering of sight to the blind" and "to set at liberty them that are bruised." You won't find those exact phrases in Isaiah 61:1-2. Instead, they perfectly match Isaiah 42:7 ("To open the blind eyes") and Isaiah 58:6 ("to let the oppressed go free").
C. NEW TO WHOM - Almost completely unknown to the average reader, who assumes Luke 4 is a simple verbatim quote of Isaiah 61. Well-documented by scholars as Jesus (or Luke) using ancient Jewish methods to link texts with shared themes of liberty. 
D. LANDS ON JESUS - Jesus is the master key to the whole Old Testament. He embodies not just the victorious King of Isaiah 61, but the suffering servant who brings light to our darkness and tenderly heals our deepest bruises (Isaiah 42 and 58).
E. FILM LINE - If you check the ancient scroll, Jesus actually wove three different prophecies together into a single breath. He didn't just come to rule; He came to heal our deepest bruises.
F. RISKS - Explaining the technical differences between the Hebrew Masoretic Text, the Greek Septuagint, and Jesus' spoken Aramaic is too complex. The script must stick strictly to "the visible differences in the verses."
G. SCORES - fresh-to-churchgoer: 10, certain-in-text: 10, simple-to-say-in-60s: 8.

**3. THE ULTIMATE JUBILEE**
A. THE INSIGHT - When Jesus spoke of "the acceptable year," He wasn't just talking about a good season; He was announcing the ultimate Year of Jubilee—the ancient law where all debts were cancelled and all slaves went free.
B. THE PROOF - Leviticus 25:10 commands Israel to "proclaim liberty throughout all the land... it shall be a jubile unto you." Luke 4:18-19 mirrors this exact language and theme: "to preach deliverance [liberty] to the captives... To preach the acceptable year of the Lord."
C. NEW TO WHOM - Scholars and pastors know this well. The average churchgoer misses the heavy historical and economic weight of the "acceptable year" and just thinks it means a generic time of God's favor.
D. LANDS ON JESUS - Jesus Himself is our Jubilee. He steps into our impossible, lifelong debt of sin, cancels it completely at His own expense, and sets us totally free.
E. FILM LINE - He wasn’t just reading poetry. He was declaring the ultimate Jubilee—the ancient law where every debt is wiped out and every slave goes free. Jesus is our Jubilee.
F. RISKS - The word "Jubilee" isn't explicitly in the text of Luke 4 or Isaiah 61, so it requires quickly connecting the concept of the 50th-year "release" to the "acceptable year."
G. SCORES - fresh-to-churchgoer: 8, certain-in-text: 9, simple-to-say-in-60s: 9.

**YOUR PICK:**
Insight 1 (The Grace for the Outsider). The baseline script ends with a warm, gentle welcome, but leaves out the explosive ending of the actual story—they tried to push Him off a cliff! Insight 1 perfectly explains *why* the cliff scene happens. It flips the script on "hometown rejection" and turns it into "grace rejection," proving that Jesus' welcome is shockingly, dangerously open to the exact people religion tries to keep out.
