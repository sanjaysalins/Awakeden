# Bible-explainer short (60 s, worldwide audience, dubbed): Jesus reads Isaiah 61 in Nazareth (Luke 4:16-30).
The team wants ONE cross-Bible insight that a regular churchgoer has probably never noticed, plainly true in the KJV, orthodox, landing on Jesus with grace (not fear). Five candidates:

C1 "ACCEPTED": Luke 4:19 "the acceptable year of the Lord" and Luke 4:24 "No prophet is accepted in his own country" use the same Greek word (dektos). Film idea: Jesus announced the year God accepts people; minutes later His own town would not accept Him - and the grace still stands.
C2 "JUBILEE ON ATONEMENT DAY": Isaiah 61:1 / Luke 4 "proclaim liberty" echoes Leviticus 25:10 "proclaim liberty throughout all the land" (the Jubilee), and Leviticus 25:9 says the Jubilee trumpet sounds "in the day of atonement". Film idea: freedom was announced on the day sin was covered - pointing to the cross.
C3 "HE DID WHAT HE NAMED": In Luke 4:25-27 Jesus names Elijah's foreign widow (Zarephath) and Elisha's foreign leper (Naaman). Later Luke shows Jesus raising a widow's son and "he delivered him to his mother" (Luke 7:15) = Elijah "delivered him unto his mother" (1 Kings 17:23); and a Samaritan leper He calls "this stranger" (Luke 17:16-18). Film idea: the town raged at grace for outsiders; Jesus then did the very same miracles.
C4 "THE PAUSED WORDS COME BACK": Jesus stops before "the day of vengeance" (Isaiah 61:2). In Luke 21:22 He says "these be the days of vengeance, that all things which are written may be fulfilled." Film idea: He did not delete the line; He paused it, and opened a time of welcome first.
C5 "A PREVIEW OF THE CROSS": Nazareth: "Physician, heal thyself" (4:23), thrust out of the city to be killed (4:29), "he passing through the midst of them went his way" (4:30). At the cross: "He saved others; let him save himself" (Luke 23:35), "suffered without the gate" (Hebrews 13:12) - and that time He stayed.
