**C1 — ACCEPTED**  
**VERDICT: SOLID**  
**Facts:** KJV Luke 4:19 has “acceptable year of the Lord”; 4:24 has “No prophet is accepted in his own country.” In Greek both use *dektos* (4:19 ἐνιαυτὸν κυρίου δεκτόν; 4:24 οὐδεὶς προφήτης δεκτός). The link is text-real, not KJV-only.  
**Risk:** You can imply Nazareth’s rejection cancels “acceptable year”; Luke’s point is irony—God’s welcome stands while the hometown refuses the herald.  
**Safest line:** “Luke repeats the same word: God’s acceptable year is announced—and his own country will not accept him.”

**C2 — JUBILEE ON ATONEMENT DAY**  
**VERDICT: REJECT**  
**Facts:** Isaiah 61:1 / Luke 4 “liberty” rightly echoes Lev 25:10 (*deror*). Lev 25:9 ties the Jubilee trumpet to the Day of Atonement. Luke 4 never dates the synagogue reading on Yom Kippur—a narrative leap.  
**Risk:** Saying freedom was “announced on the day sin was covered” at Nazareth misstates chronology and can smuggle calendar typology in as history. Jubilee-to-cross typology is sermons, not what Luke 4 claims.  
**Safest line:** “Jesus proclaims Jubilee-style liberty from Isaiah—without claiming it was literally Atonement Day.”

**C3 — HE DID WHAT HE NAMED**  
**VERDICT: NEEDS CARE**  
**Facts:** Luke 4:25–27 names the widow of Zarephath and Naaman; KJV matches. Luke 7:15 “delivered him to his mother” matches 1 Kings 17:23 wording—a real Lukan echo. Luke 17:16–18: Samaritan leper, “this stranger”—not Naaman; Gentile outsider parallel, not the same story.  
**Risk:** “Jesus then did the very same miracles” overreads sequence and identity (Naaman ≠ Samaritan; Nain ≠ Zarephath). Solid on outsiders-first offense; shaky as strict fulfillment.  
**Safest line:** “In the sermon he points to Gentiles God helped; later Luke shows Jesus showing that same mercy to outsiders.”

**C4 — THE PAUSED WORDS COME BACK**  
**VERDICT: NEEDS CARE**  
**Facts:** Luke stops before “the day of vengeance” (Isaiah 61:2)—mainstream, text-observed. Luke 21:22 “days of vengeance” is KJV-accurate but AD 70 / judgment context, not Isaiah 61 resumed in the synagogue.  
**Risk:** “Postponed vengeance, welcome first” can sound like grace overwrote judgment or that the cross only delays wrath—mistreats Isa 61’s two-part rhythm and Luke 21’s scope. Pause ≠ deletion is fine; Luke 21 bridge is a stretch for newcomers.  
**Safest line:** “He stopped before vengeance in Isaiah and opened his ministry with good news—the rest of Isaiah’s sentence was not forgotten.”

**C5 — A PREVIEW OF THE CROSS**  
**VERDICT: NEEDS CARE**  
**Facts:** Luke 4:23, 4:29, 4:30 KJV as cited. Luke 23:35 parallels “save others / save yourself.” Hebrews 13:12 is true KJV but not Luke; typological import for a Nazareth short. Contrast (4:30 escape vs cross stay) is homiletic, not one Lucan thread from Isaiah 61.  
**Risk:** Fold Nazareth into passion preview via Hebrews blurs corpus and distracts from the Isaiah scroll—the assigned anchor.  
**Safest line:** “Nazareth already previews ‘save yourself’ rejection—at the cross he did not come down.”

**RANKING (best → worst):** C1, C4, C3, C5, C2  

**Pick for the film:** **C1**—verifiable Greek wordplay, Lukan irony, orthodox, one sentence in 60s without fake calendar or forcing Luke 21 or Hebrews.
