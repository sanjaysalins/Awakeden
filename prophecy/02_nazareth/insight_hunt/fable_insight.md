# Bible Insight Hunter - Isaiah 61 -> Luke 4

## INSIGHT 1 - "Acceptable" and "accepted" are the same word

**A. THE INSIGHT** - Jesus announced the year when God accepts people, and a few lines later said the town would not accept Him: one word, used twice.

**B. THE PROOF**
- Luke 4:19 "To preach the **acceptable** year of the Lord."
- Luke 4:24 "No prophet is **accepted** in his own country."
- Same Greek word (dektos) both times; the KJV shows it plainly as acceptable / accepted. Luke uses it only once more:
- Acts 10:34-35 "God is no respecter of persons: But in every nation he that feareth him, and worketh righteousness, is **accepted** with him."
- That is exactly Jesus' point in Luke 4:26-27: a widow of Sidon and "Naaman the Syrian", two outsiders, received what Israel missed.
- Paul uses the word the same way: 2 Corinthians 6:2 "behold, now is the **accepted** time; behold, now is the day of salvation." "Now" matches Jesus' "This day".
- John 1:11 "He came unto his own, and his own received him not."

**C. NEW TO WHOM** - Most churchgoers have never noticed it; they read the two verses as separate topics. Well known to Luke commentators. Safe.

**D. LANDS ON JESUS** - God's door is open in Jesus to anyone, near or far. The only thing that can shut it is our own "no" to Him. The welcome is wider than the home town thought.

**E. FILM LINE** - "He read: the year God accepts people. Then He said: no prophet is accepted at home. Same word. God opened His door. Nazareth shut theirs. His is still open."

**F. RISKS** - Must not sound anti-Jewish: the first believers were Jews, and the point is "everyone", not "them out". The Acts link rests on Greek, but the English shows it. 2 Cor 6:2's second "accepted" is a near-relative word, so lean on Luke 4 and Acts 10.

**G. SCORES** - fresh 8 / certain 9 / simple 8

## INSIGHT 2 - "Proclaim liberty" is the Jubilee trumpet, and it sounded on the Day of Atonement

**A. THE INSIGHT** - The words Jesus read come from an old law: every fiftieth year a trumpet set every slave free and gave every family its land back, and that trumpet blew on the day sin was covered.

**B. THE PROOF**
- Leviticus 25:10 "And ye shall hallow the fiftieth year, and **proclaim liberty** throughout all the land unto all the inhabitants thereof: it shall be a jubile unto you; and ye shall return every man unto his possession, and ye shall return every man unto his family."
- Isaiah 61:1 "to **proclaim liberty** to the captives" - the same two words (Hebrew deror, used for this release).
- Leviticus 25:9 "in the **day of atonement** shall ye make the trumpet sound throughout all your land."
- So freedom was announced only on the day the blood was offered for sin. Jesus announces the freedom, then goes on to be the offering: Hebrews 9:12 "by his own blood he entered in once into the holy place, having obtained eternal redemption for us."
- John 8:36 "If the Son therefore shall make you free, ye shall be free indeed."

**C. NEW TO WHOM** - "Jubilee" is a known church word, but few connect it to this scene, and almost none know the trumpet blew on the Day of Atonement. Standard in commentaries.

**D. LANDS ON JESUS** - He is both the trumpet and the sacrifice. Our debt is cancelled because He paid; we go home free.

**E. FILM LINE** - "Every fiftieth year a trumpet freed every slave. It blew on the day sin was covered. Jesus read those words. Then He became the covering Himself."

**F. RISKS** - Do not claim that year was an actual Jubilee year (unknown). Do not turn it into a political programme only; the text holds real release and sin's release together. Needs two ideas explained in 60s - tight.

**G. SCORES** - fresh 6 / certain 9 / simple 6

## INSIGHT 3 - His first sermon is a small picture of the cross

**A. THE INSIGHT** - What happened in Nazareth that day happens again at the end: the same taunt, pushed out of the city to be killed - but this time He stays.

**B. THE PROOF**
- Luke 4:23 "Physician, **heal thyself**" / Luke 23:35 "He saved others; let him **save himself**"; 23:37 "**save thyself**"; 23:39 "**save thyself** and us."
- Luke 4:29 "thrust him **out of the city**" / Hebrews 13:12 "Jesus also... suffered **without the gate**."
- Luke 4:9 (same chapter, the devil) "**cast thyself down** from hence" / Luke 4:29 "that they might **cast him down** headlong."
- Luke 4:30 "he passing through the midst of them went his way" / John 10:18 "No man taketh it from me, but I lay it down of myself."

**C. NEW TO WHOM** - Rarely noticed in the pew; noted by Luke scholars as a programmatic foreshadowing. The "cast down" echo inside chapter 4 is less often remarked but is plainly in the text.

**D. LANDS ON JESUS** - In Nazareth He walked away, because the time was not yet. At the cross He could have walked away again, and did not. He would not save Himself, so that He could save us.

**E. FILM LINE** - "They dragged Him out of town to kill Him. He walked away. Three years later, outside another city, He could have walked away again. He stayed. For you."

**F. RISKS** - It is a pattern, not a stated prophecy; say "like", not "this predicted". Luke does not say how He passed through - do not invent a miracle. "Three years" is the usual estimate, not a verse.

**G. SCORES** - fresh 7 / certain 7 / simple 8

---

**YOUR PICK: Insight 1.** It is one word the viewer can see twice on screen, it explains why the crowd turned from wonder to rage (grace for outsiders), it reaches across to Acts and Paul, and it lands straight on an open door - with Insight 3's "He stayed" as the strongest possible closing image if a second beat fits.
