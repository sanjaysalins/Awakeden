1. SCORE IDEA
A five-note welcome tune that always stops one note short of home, until the last line lets that note arrive.

Instruments: solo cello (the sentence), warm violas and violins (the gold), one close French horn (only near the end), soft harp (the scroll and the light). No percussion. Slow and free, nothing you could clap. Key of G major. The tune rises and rests on F-sharp, the note just before home. Home, G, is saved for "His welcome is still open."

2. WHY IT FITS
- A comic page is clean ink plus one warm gold. The cello is one ink line. The harp is a small glint. The orchestra never crowds the panel.
- The story is a sentence cut in half. The tune is cut the same way, so the stop is felt at the scroll, before it is explained.
- Vengeance is only a soft minor shadow of that same tune, then it thins away. Fear never gets a theme of its own.

4. SPLIT
Join at 29.2 seconds, inside the pause after "Every eye was on Him" (ends 28.84) and before Jesus (starts 29.80). Part 1 is 0.0–29.2. Part 2 is 29.2–59.2. Each is about 30 seconds. Both sides are the same still chord, so the edit can hide. The sacred line begins clean, inside part 2.

6. MIX NOTES
Voice stays in front. Music sits about 8–12 dB under the narrator. Under Isaiah (20.9–23.3), Jesus (29.8–34.2), and the townsman (39.5–42.2), dip another 4–6 dB. Jesus is the lowest dip. Do not mute it. The still chord should still be felt. A small lift at the good-news lines and at the amazement, always under the voice. The real lift is after the last word: from 56.1 to 59.2, raise the music about 4 dB and let the home chord ring. Ease it. Do not cut it. No hit on the last frame. Crossfade the two parts about 400 ms, centered on 29.2, while the chord is still.

7. RISKS
1. Vengeance becomes a scare. Listen at 20.9–23.3 and look at the waveform. Fail if there is a brass hit, a dissonant stab, or a peak more than 3 dB over the bed. It should feel like a cloud.
2. A hole in the sound, above all at the join. Scan the full waveform. Fail if any silence lasts past about 150 ms. At 29.2 the level should match within about 1 dB, on one chord.
3. The tune comes home too soon, or Jesus is covered. By ear, F-sharp should still feel unfinished until about 53 seconds. Fail if home arrives before "still open." Under Jesus, music should sit at least 10 dB below his voice and barely move.
