# Score plan: "He stopped mid-sentence" (59.2 s)

## 1. SCORE IDEA
A five-note "welcome" tune that stops after four notes, waits through the whole film, and plays its fifth note only after the last spoken word.

- **Instruments:**
  - A warm string orchestra.
  - A solo clarinet carries the tune: human, close and plain.
  - A concert harp plays a soft "page-turning" figure.
  - Low strings and soft trombones sit underneath.
  - One French horn comes in at the end only.
  - No timpani. This keeps it apart from film 1.
- **Key and tempo:** D major, about 72 BPM, in both parts.
- **The tune:** five notes rising, D–E–F#–A … D (up to the high D). We hear the first four and then it stops. That is Isaiah's sentence cut off. The fifth note, the high D, comes only in the held ending.

## 2. WHY IT FITS
- **Bold inked comic look.** A harp and a clarinet sound clear and clean, like a black ink line. A thick wash of sound would blur the panel slams, and the sound effects need room to land.
- **The story is a sentence that stops.** The music stops too, so even a viewer who doesn't follow the English feels the "unfinished" moment. Dubbing doesn't change that.
- **Grace, not fear.** The "vengeance" moment gets a low, quiet chord that sounds serious. No hits, no booms, no minor-key dread. The welcome wins because the tune gets its last note.

## 4. SPLIT
**Split at 29.3 s.** That sits inside the ~1 s pause between "Every eye was on Him" and Jesus' line. Part 2 then opens on the stillness, so the join is hidden by quiet. Part 1 should render to about 31 s, with a 0.5 s crossfade at 29.3.

## 5. MUSIC PROMPTS

**PART 1 (31 s):**
> Cinematic orchestral underscore, instrumental only, no vocals, no choir, no drums, no percussion. D major, 72 BPM, gentle and reverent. Continuous sound throughout with no silence or gaps. Opens with a soft concert harp figure; a warm solo clarinet plays a simple rising four-note phrase and stops on the fourth note, leaving it unfinished. Around 3 seconds in, warm string orchestra enters, harp keeps a gentle page-turning pattern. Around 9 seconds in, the clarinet phrase rises again, strings grow warmer, a small hopeful lift around 14 seconds in. Around 17 seconds in, the melody stops on the same unfinished note and strings hold one plain sustained chord, thinning. Around 19 seconds in, only low cellos and basses holding a quiet open fifth, solemn but peaceful, a very soft trombone breath, no hits, no dread. Around 24 seconds in, harp and soft strings return gently. Around 27 seconds in, thin down to one sustained high violin note, quiet and still, held to the end.

**PART 2 (30 s):**
> Cinematic orchestral underscore, instrumental only, no vocals, no choir, no drums, no percussion. D major, 72 BPM, reverent and warm. Continuous sound throughout with no silence or gaps. Opens with one very soft, still, sustained high string chord, no melody, no movement, sacred stillness. Around 5 seconds in, soft harp and bright gentle strings, a feeling of wonder. Around 10 seconds in, pull back to quiet low strings, slightly cooler. Around 13 seconds in, a warm solo clarinet plays a simple rising four-note phrase and stops on the fourth note, unfinished; strings slowly build. Around 20 seconds in, full warm string orchestra swells with a French horn, broad and hopeful. Around 27 seconds in, the clarinet and horn finally complete the phrase, rising to the home note, a full resolved D major chord held and sustained, slowly fading to the very end.

## 6. MIX NOTES
- **Under the narrator:** music about 18 dB below the voice.
- **Under Isaiah and the townsman:** an extra −6 dB.
- **Under Jesus' line (29.8–34.2):** an extra −10 dB, so the music is close to silent. It is a bed, not a melody.
- **Short gaps between lines** (2.2–2.9, 15.8–16.8, 26.4–27.3): lift the music +3 dB, so the stopped tune is heard.
- **Last line (52.3–56.1):** −14 dB, then rise +5 dB right at 56.1 for the fifth note.
- **The 3 s hold:** the music carries it alone and fades over the final 1.5 s to −60 dB at 59.2 s. No hard cut. Audio length must equal video length.

## 7. RISKS
1. **Dead air, or a seam that doesn't match.**
   - Measure: sweep loudness in 0.5 s windows. No window should fall below −45 dBFS except where the mix dips it on purpose.
   - Check that both parts are in D at about 72 BPM.
   - Listen to the 29.3 s join on headphones.
2. **The stop isn't heard, or the tune is too busy under the voice.**
   - Listen: can you hear the 4-note stop at 2.2 s and 17 s?
   - Measure: energy in the 1–4 kHz speech band under the KJV lines should be at least 20 dB below the voice.
   - If the clarinet plays anywhere other than the planned spots, re-roll.
3. **"Vengeance" turns into a horror cue.**
   - Listen to 18.9–23.3 for booms, hits, low rumble or minor-key dread.
   - Measure: no spike in level below 60 Hz there.
   - If it sounds scary, re-roll part 1 or keep only the plain held chord.
