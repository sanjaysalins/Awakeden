# SCORE PLAN - "He stopped mid-sentence" (Fable seat, blind)

## 1. SCORE IDEA
A warm five-note "welcome" phrase that keeps stopping on its fourth note, and only gets its fifth note, the home note, when the narrator says "welcome" at the end.

- **Instruments:** solo cello (carries the phrase), warm string orchestra, two French horns, harp, one soft low drum. No choir, no piano, no ethnic colour.
- **Key / pulse:** D major, slow, about 66 bpm, both parts.
- **Motif:** five slow rising notes. Notes 1-4 are heard three times and stop, hanging. Note 5 (home) arrives once, at 50.2 s. The last chord then stays open and ringing, with no final thump: the door is still open.

## 2. WHY IT FITS
- **Ink look:** one clear melodic line over a plain warm bed is like one black line on gold paper. Bold, few notes, lots of space for the slam effects added later.
- **Story:** the film is about a sentence stopped on purpose. The music does the same thing, so even a viewer who misses words feels something unfinished, then finished as grace.
- **Dubbing:** the phrase is only played in voice gaps, and stays low (cello range), so any voice in any language sits on top.

## 4. SPLIT
**40.2 s**, inside the 0.6 s pause before "Later, at the cross". Part 1 ends on a calm held chord, part 2 starts on a low held chord in the same key, so the join is two long notes meeting. It is also the story's real turn: walked away, then stayed. Parts: about 40 s and 19 s.

## 5. MUSIC PROMPTS

**Part 1 (make 42 s, use first 40.4):**
Cinematic orchestral film score, instrumental only, D major, slow 66 bpm, warm and tender, continuous sound with no silence or gaps anywhere. Solo cello, warm string orchestra, two French horns, harp, one soft low drum. No choir, no vocals, no piano, no percussion hits, no ethnic scales. Begins at once with a solo cello playing four slow rising notes that stop unfinished over a soft held string note. Around 3 seconds in, strings grow warm and hopeful with gentle harp. Around 9 seconds in, soft French horns join. Around 12 seconds in, harp stops, low strings turn solemn and minor with a distant soft drum roll, grave but not scary. Around 17 seconds in, it returns to major and the cello plays the same four rising notes, stopping unfinished. Around 20 seconds in, only one high, still, quiet string note remains for five seconds. Around 26 seconds in, low strings return with a quiet uneasy pulse. Around 30 seconds in, a short warm horn melody. Around 35 seconds in, the strings push faster and stronger, urgent but tuneful. Around 38 seconds in, the push falls away to one calm held string chord that sustains to the end.

**Part 2 (make 21 s, use 18.9):**
Cinematic orchestral film score, instrumental only, D major, slow 66 bpm, reverent and full of grace, continuous sound with no silence or gaps anywhere. Solo cello, warm string orchestra, two French horns. No choir, no vocals, no piano, no drums, no trailer hits. Begins at once with low, bare, slow sustained strings, quiet and reverent. Around 6 seconds in, a solo cello plays four slow rising notes and stops unfinished. Around 10 seconds in, the cello plays the fifth note, the home note, and full strings and French horns bloom into one broad, warm, hopeful swell. Around 16 seconds in, everything settles onto one open D major chord, held and ringing, horns fading last, sustaining to the very end.

## 6. MIX NOTES
- Under narration: music about 14 dB below voice. Under the four KJV lines: about 20 dB below.
- 20.4-25.5: duck to a bare whisper (about 26 dB below). Do not cut to silence.
- Lifts: 0-0.3 s, the gaps at 2.4, 20.0, 39.9 and 49.7, and the swell at 50.2 (to about 8 dB below voice).
- Join: 300 ms crossfade centred on 40.2.
- Hold 56.1-59.1: music rises to full, then a 1.2 s fade ending exactly at 59.1. Audio length must equal video length.

## 7. RISKS
1. **Dead air or a gap.** Check: sweep loudness every 0.5 s across the mixed film; no window may fall to silence.
2. **The phrase does not really stop, or note 5 arrives early.** Check by ear on each part alone: count the notes at 0, 17, 46 s; the home note must first sound at 50.2. Reroll that part if not.
3. **Fear cue or masking.** Check by ear that 12-17 and 35-38 feel grave, not horror; measure music against voice in the 1-4 kHz band under each KJV line and confirm the join at 40.2 has no key or tempo jump.
