# Cinematic score plan: "He stopped mid-sentence"

## 1. SCORE IDEA
A five-note "welcome tune" that keeps breaking off after four notes, the way Jesus stopped Isaiah's sentence, and is finished only on the last line, "It is still open, for you, today."

- **Instruments:** a warm solo cello carries the tune. A harp gives a gentle ripple. Full strings sit under it. One French horn arrives late, with soft low pizzicato, and there is a single soft timpani roll.
- **Different from film 1:** film 1 was led by horns. This film is led by the cello and harp, and the horn is saved for "He stayed."
- **Key and tempo:** D major, slow (about 66 bpm). No exotic scales.
- **The tune:** it climbs D–E–F♯–A, and the fifth note (a high D, home) is held back. Each time the tune breaks off, the A hangs over a soft string sustain. It never just goes silent.

## 2. WHY IT FITS
- **The pictures:** inked comic pages are bold, clean outlines. A single cello line is the musical version of one clean ink stroke, and the strings are the golden colour fill. Slams and page turns are left to sound design, so the music never has to "hit" panels.
- **The story:** the film's idea is an unfinished sentence, so the music is built as an unfinished phrase. The viewer hears the gap before they understand it, and the ending pays it off.
- **Grace, not fear:** the pulse for the cliff and the mob is urgent but never dark or distorted. The cross is quiet and full, not trailer-style.

## 4. SPLIT
**Split at 20.40 s**, inside the 0.8 s pause (20.01–20.81) before "This day is this scripture fulfilled."
- Part 1 ends on a held, unfinished sustain.
- Part 2 opens in near-silent stillness, so the join is hidden under the quietest moment of the film.
- Lengths: part 1 is 20.4 s and part 2 is 38.7 s. Both are short enough to avoid dead air.

## 5. MUSIC PROMPTS

**Part 1 (20.4 s):**
> Cinematic orchestral film score, D major, slow 66 bpm, warm and tender, continuous sound with no silences or gaps. A solo cello plays a simple rising four-note melody (D, E, F sharp, A) and stops on the A, leaving it unresolved over a soft string sustain. Gentle harp ripples underneath. Around 3 seconds in, warm full strings enter with a soft harp pulse, and the cello plays the same four-note phrase twice, each time stopping on the A without resolving. Around 11 seconds in, the melody stops and the music thins to a hushed, sustained low string chord, still and solemn but not scary. Around 16.5 seconds in, the solo cello begins the four-note phrase once more and stops on the A around 17.5 seconds, then a very soft, high, sustained string chord holds to the end. No choir, no drums, no percussion hits, no Middle Eastern scales, no build-ups.

**Part 2 (38.7 s):**
> Cinematic orchestral film score, D major, continuous sound with no silences or gaps. It opens with one very quiet, high, sustained string chord, completely still, no melody, no rhythm. Around 5 seconds in, soft low pizzicato strings begin a gentle, slightly uneasy repeated note. Around 10 seconds in, harp warms up and a solo cello hums a short tender phrase. Around 15 seconds in, the pulse quickens with urgent low strings and one soft timpani roll: tense but never dark or frightening. Around 18 seconds in, everything falls away to a single calm, held French horn note. Around 20 seconds in, slow, sparse, reverent low strings, very quiet and peaceful. Around 26 seconds in, one slow, warm swell of full strings, with the French horn entering, peaking around 29 seconds. Around 30 seconds in, harp and solo cello play a rising melody D, E, F sharp, A, and around 36 seconds in it finally completes on the high D and resolves home. A warm full chord rings to the end. No choir, no rock drums, no trailer hits, no Middle Eastern scales.

## 6. MIX NOTES
- **Base level:** music about 18 dB below the voice under narrator lines.
- **Under the four KJV lines** (13.98–16.62, 20.81–25.14, 27.51–29.88, 43.36–45.82): dip a further 6 dB. Under Jesus' first line the music is near silent, just the high chord.
- **Lifts:** in the 0.6 s pause at 39.87–40.47 (the walk-through to the cross, about −3 dB), and during "This time, He stayed" (46.1–49.6, about −12 dB under the voice).
- **The 3 s hold** (56.08–59.1): un-duck within 0.2 s, lift about 6 dB, and let the resolution ring. Fade only the last 0.4 s, so the audio runs to the full 59.1 s (landing-hold rule).
- **Dubbing:** no melody sits in the 1–3 kHz voice band under any spoken line, so the bed works under any language.

## 7. RISKS
1. **The seam shows at 20.4** (a key, level or tone jump).
   - Check: crossfade 150 ms, measure loudness 1 s either side (within 2 dB), and listen on headphones.
   - If needed: re-pitch part 2 by a semitone, or re-roll.
2. **The tune doesn't land on cue.** The generator can drift on "around N seconds" cues, and the final high D may miss 56.3 or never arrive.
   - Check: find the note onsets and listen.
   - If needed: slide part 2 by up to ±0.6 s inside the 20.4 pause, or trim part 2's start.
3. **Dead air, or a horror/trailer moment** (the cliff or the cross gets a boom).
   - Check: sweep the loudness in 0.5 s windows across the whole score; no window should be below −50 dBFS. Look for sharp low-frequency hits at 35–50 s and listen at that point.
   - If needed: re-roll only the part that failed, which is within the 2-generation budget.
