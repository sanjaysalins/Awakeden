# FILM: "He stopped mid-sentence" (Isaiah 61 -> Luke 4 -> Luke 23) - 59.1 s vertical gospel short, worldwide audience
Second film in a series: Old Testament prophecies fulfilled in Jesus. Many viewers know nothing about the Bible; many have English as a second language. The film will be DUBBED into other languages (the music bed must work under any voice).
STORY: In His home town Nazareth, Jesus reads Isaiah's promise of good news and God's welcome, then STOPS in the middle of Isaiah's sentence, before "and the day of vengeance of our God", sits down, and says "This day is this scripture fulfilled in your ears." He knows they will say "Physician, heal thyself." He reminds them God once helped foreigners; furious, they take Him to a cliff to throw Him down - He walks right through them. Later, at the cross, leaders mock: "He saved others; let him save himself." This time, He stayed, and gave His life. He stopped reading at the welcome - it is still open, for you, today.
LOOK: bold inked graphic-comic pages (clean black ink, warm golden colour), 2-panel pages, panel SLAMS, page flips, iris and ink-bleed transitions. The cross section is quiet and reverent (nothing drawn, slow).
KEY PICTURES (film seconds): 0-3 Jesus + the old prophet writing; 3-11.6 Nazareth village, the scroll handed over, Jesus reads, a poor widow, a broken shackle, men listening, soft light on "welcome"; 11.6-17 the old prophet Isaiah under a stormy sky (hushed halftone page), scroll hands; 17-21 Jesus closes the scroll and sits; 20.9-25.6 Jesus seated, ONE still full-screen picture, NOTHING moves (sacred stillness); 25.6-30 a doubting townsman, stern men, Jesus close-up; 30-35.6 the prophet Elijah with a foreign widow and her son (a full flour jar); 35.4-38.7 the furious crowd pushing Jesus toward the cliff; 38.4-40.5 Jesus walks calmly through the parted crowd; 40.2-49.9 the cross on a hill outside Jerusalem at dusk, three leaders mocking below, then the cross alone (reverent); 49.9-59.1 HERO: Jesus at golden sunset above Nazareth, reaching His open hand toward the viewer (held to the end).
Voices: a warm narrator; Isaiah (old prophet, 1 KJV line); Jesus (2 KJV lines); a leader (1 KJV line, mocking). Voice is final; music sits UNDER it.
OWNER'S NOTE: the series' first film used a CINEMATIC orchestral score (full strings, French horns, sparing timpani, one reverent swell, a simple unfinished 'promise tune' completed only in the final hold) and the owner liked it. This film should also be cinematic, but its OWN piece. Grace, not fear. No choir with words, no trailer 'braaam', no cheesy Middle-East scales, no rock drums.
Comic SLAMS and page turns get their own sound effects later (sound design, not the music).
Score tool: ElevenLabs Music free-text prompt (music_v1). Long single generations can render dead air, so the score is made in TWO parts joined at a split second, ideally inside a natural pause; each prompt can use "around N seconds in" cues. Never use composition_plan. Silences and dips are done in the MIX, so prompts must ask for continuous sound with no gaps. Budget ~2 generations.
