**Independent review: Nazareth (Isaiah 61 → Luke 4), ~60 s script**

**1. Newcomer**
- They would get most of it. **Who:** Jesus, in His home town. **What:** He reads, stops, is nearly killed, and later dies on the cross. **Why it matters:** "the welcome is still open."
- Lines they would not get:
  - **"Isaiah's sentence goes on."** Isaiah is never named as an old prophet, and the picture alone won't carry that once the film is dubbed. Fix: "The prophet Isaiah's sentence goes on."
  - **"This day is this scripture fulfilled in your ears."** The word order is hard for English learners. A dub will smooth it, but the English version stays hard.
  - **"He reminded them that God once helped foreigners. Furious…"** The anger makes no sense without the barb: God passed over Israel's own people and helped outsiders. A newcomer will ask "why furious?"

**2. Bible reader**
- Stopping before "day of vengeance" is known to preachers but fresh to most people in the pews. Fair.
- Pairing the cliff (He walks away) with the cross (He stays) is the real insight. Good.
- **Missed gift:** Luke 4:23, "Physician, heal thyself," echoes Luke 23:35, "save himself." Nazareth and Calvary taunt Him the same way. That is the line almost no churchgoer has noticed, and the script leaves it out.

**3. Curiosity gap: 7/10.** "Stopped mid-sentence" hooks well and pays off at the end. The middle (prophet proverb, foreigners) sags and loses the thread.

**4. Accuracy**
- **"He left the day of vengeance for later"** goes past the text. Luke says only that He closed the book. "For later" is a common reading (judgment comes later), but here it is stated as fact. Safer: "He did not read it. He closed the book and sat down." That is the same length and straight from Luke 4:20.
- **"Middle of a sentence"** is fair, because the KJV runs on with a semicolon.
- **"Leaders mocked"** fits "rulers derided" (Luke 23:35).
- I see no doctrinal risk. The close is grace-based, not pressure.

**5. Weakest line:** "Then He said they would not welcome Him / No prophet is accepted…" plus the vague foreigners line. My fix is to swap the first for the proverb and sharpen the second. It costs about the same time:
- [narrator] "He knew what they would say:"
- [jesus] "Physician, heal thyself."
- [narrator] "He said God's prophets once helped foreigners, not them. Furious…"

Then "let him save himself" at the cross lands as a clear echo.

**6. Overall: 7/10.** It's clean, accurate and has a good landing. Naming Isaiah, taking out "for later," and using the heal-thyself / save-himself echo would lift it to about 8.
