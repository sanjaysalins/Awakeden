# Seat: Fable (blind, film-only)

**1. NEWCOMER**
- WHO: yes. Jesus, in His home town.
- WHAT: yes. He read a promise, stopped early, the town turned on Him, He walked away; later He stayed on a cross and died.
- WHY: mostly. "The welcome is still open for you" lands. But the film never says why His death keeps the welcome open. A newcomer has to guess that link.
- Lines at risk:
  - "Isaiah's sentence goes on" - Isaiah is never introduced. The viewer was told it is "God's promise", not who wrote it.
  - "the day of vengeance" - "vengeance" is a hard word for second-language English, and the whole insight rests on it. It is said twice and never explained.
  - "fulfilled in your ears" - old English; the meaning (it is happening now, in front of you) is not given in plain words.
  - "God once helped foreigners" - clear, but why that makes them furious is left to guess.

**2. BIBLE READER**
Yes, two things.
- Jesus stopped mid-sentence, just before "the day of vengeance". Some have heard this.
- Fresher: the "welcome" thread. The "acceptable year" and "no prophet is accepted" are tied together, then the cliff (He leaves) is set against the cross (He stays). Most churchgoers have not joined those two scenes.

**3. CURIOSITY GAP: 8/10**
The first line is strong and true. It pays off twice: quickly at line 10, and again in the last line. A little heat is lost because the answer comes so early; the cross section runs on a different question.

**4. ACCURATE**
- All quotes are KJV-exact (the mockers' line is cut short, which is fine).
- "for later" is an inference. Luke says only that He closed the book. It is a common and sound reading, but it is not in the text.
- "leaders mocked" matches "rulers", but the voice tag is [crowd]. Minor.
- Verse 22 (they first wondered at His gracious words) is skipped, so the anger looks instant. Acceptable for 60 s.
- "This time, He stayed" implies He could have left. Sound (same power as the cliff), but implied, not stated.
- No doctrinal error. Small risk: "still open" hints it will close, with no reason given.

**5. WEAKEST LINE**
Line 10: "He left the day of vengeance for later, and sat down."
Fix (same length): "He left that day of judgment for later, and sat down."
This explains "vengeance" in plain words without adding time.
Second fix, also free: line 8 -> "The prophet's sentence goes on:"

**6. OVERALL: 7.5/10**
Real insight, honest, clean landing. Held back by one hard word and a missing "why the cross".
