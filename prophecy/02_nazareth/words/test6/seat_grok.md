1. NEWCOMER: Partial. They can say Jesus, in his home town Nazareth, read a promise and stopped; a crowd tried to throw him off a cliff; he walked through them; later he died on a cross; a welcome is still open. They cannot say why the town turned violent.

Lines that fail:
- "This day is this scripture fulfilled in your ears." "Scripture" and "in your ears" will not survive a dub.
- "He left the day of vengeance for later." Later than what is never said.
- "God once helped foreigners." The fury comes next, with no spoken reason. "Prophet" may also be unknown.

2. BIBLE READER: The stop before "the day of vengeance" is real and already a common sermon. The sharper links are missing. Wrath follows Luke 4:25-27: Israel’s widows and lepers were passed over, while a widow in Sidon and Naaman the Syrian were helped. Luke 4:23, "Physician, heal thyself," is the line that matches "let him save himself" (Luke 23:35). Isaiah 61:2 also does not end at vengeance. The same verse continues, "to comfort all that mourn."

3. CURIOSITY GAP: 6. "Stopped reading in the middle of a sentence" is a strong open. The answer arrives two lines later. The cliff and the cross do not deepen it. The ending repeats the welcome as an invitation.

4. ACCURATE: Nazareth, the reading, the stop, sitting down, the fulfillment line, the prophet saying, the hill, passing through, the rulers’ mock, and his death all match the KJV.

Not supported:
- "Middle of a sentence." Luke 4:19 is a full sentence. Isaiah 61:2 is the middle of a verse.
- "Left the day of vengeance for later." He closed the book. He never set a time. Viewers can hear the cross as that "later." That is a doctrinal risk.
- "Welcome" is a gloss of "the acceptable year of the Lord." The welcome/welcome pun is the script’s.
- "Still open, for you, today" is the narrator. Luke says "this day" to that synagogue.
- "Helped foreigners" hides the real sting: Israel was passed over.

5. WEAKEST LINE: "He left the day of vengeance for later, and sat down."
Fix, no added length: "He closed the book before vengeance, and sat down."

6. OVERALL: 6. The spine matches the pictures and the passage. The thesis claims a timetable the KJV does not give, the mob’s motive is unspoken, and the clearer cross-Bible link is left out.

VERDICT
Newcomer: partial. Curiosity: 6. Accuracy: thesis overclaimed. Overall: 6.
