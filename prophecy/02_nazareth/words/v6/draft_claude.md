# "He stopped mid-sentence": blind second-half draft

## 1. Full narration

**First half (kept, one small trim)**

[narrator] Jesus stopped reading in the middle of a sentence.
[narrator] In Nazareth, His home town, He stood to read from the prophet Isaiah. *(trim: drop "written long before")*
[narrator] He read God's promise of someone sent with good news for the poor, freedom for prisoners, and a time of God's welcome.
[narrator] Then He rolled up the scroll. Isaiah's sentence goes on:
[isaiah] and the day of vengeance of our God.
[narrator] He left the day of vengeance unread, and sat down. Every eye was on Him. (pause)
[jesus] This day is this scripture fulfilled in your ears.

**Second half (new)**

[narrator] He had just read them God's year of welcome. Then He told them: no prophet is welcomed in his own town.
[narrator] They took Him to a cliff, to throw Him down. He walked away through the crowd. (pause)
[narrator] At the cross, they mocked:
[crowd] He saved others; let him save himself.
[narrator] This time, He stayed. (pause)
[narrator] His welcome is still open, for you, today.

## 3. What is new

**New recording (4 narrator takes and 1 crowd take):**
- "He had just read them God's year of welcome. Then He told them: no prophet is welcomed in his own town." This is Luke 4:24 told in the narrator's own words, not quoted, so "welcome" can match "welcome" in every dubbed language.
- "They took Him to a cliff, to throw Him down. He walked away through the crowd." (Luke 4:29–30)
- "At the cross, they mocked:"
- [crowd] "He saved others; let him save himself." (Luke 23:35, KJV word for word)
- "This time, He stayed."

**Reused audio:**
- "His welcome is still open, for you, today." This is cut from the end of the old take.
- The "written long before" trim is a cut, not a re-record.
- "Is not this Joseph's son?" and "local boy" are dropped.

**New pictures (4):**
- The angry crowd pushing Him up the hill to the cliff edge.
- Jesus walking calmly out through the middle of them.
- The cross, with rulers mocking below. This should be wide and reverent.
- A closing shot that links back to the open scroll or synagogue, for "His welcome is still open."

## 4. Why it works

A newcomer can follow it because the welcome word repeats, plain and spoken: He read God's welcome, and His town did not welcome Him. Then comes one simple flip: at the cliff He walked away, and at the cross He stayed.

A Bible reader may never have noticed two things in Luke 4. "Acceptable year" (4:19) and "accepted" (4:24) are the same word, minutes apart in the same room. And the crowd that tried to throw Him off the cliff is a quiet early picture of the cross, where they told Him to save Himself and He chose not to.
