# Draft (blind seat: Fable) - "He stopped mid-sentence"

## (a) Script

[narrator] Jesus stopped reading in the middle of a sentence.
[narrator] Nazareth, where He grew up. In the synagogue, the place of prayer, He is handed Isaiah's book, written about seven hundred years before.
[isaiah] "The Spirit of the Lord is upon me..."
[narrator] Good news for the poor. Freedom for captives. A year of God's welcome. Then He closed the book and sat down.
[narrator] Every eye was on Him. (pause)
[jesus] "This day is this scripture fulfilled in your ears."
[crowd] "Is not this Joseph's son?"
[narrator] In Isaiah, the sentence goes on: "and the day of vengeance of our God."
[narrator] He stopped at the welcome. It is for you. "This day."

## (b) Spoken word count

105 words (9 + 23 + 8 + 21 + 5 + 9 + 5 + 14 + 11). Voice tags and "(pause)" not counted.
Jesus speaks after word 66 of 105 = about 63% in.

## (c) KJV fragments

- "The Spirit of the Lord is upon me" - Luke 4:18
- "This day is this scripture fulfilled in your ears." - Luke 4:21
- "Is not this Joseph's son?" - Luke 4:22
- "and the day of vengeance of our God" - Isaiah 61:2
- "This day" (landing, His own words repeated) - Luke 4:21

Plain-word paraphrase, not quoted: "Good news for the poor. Freedom for captives. A year of God's welcome." (Luke 4:18-19); "He closed the book and sat down", "Every eye was on Him" (Luke 4:20).

## (d) Four lines

- Who it is for: anyone, anywhere, with no Bible knowledge; and Bible readers who never saw where He stopped.
- Main take-away: Jesus read the promise of God's welcome, stopped there, and said it starts today.
- The curiosity gap: why did He stop in the middle of a sentence, and what were the words He did not read? (Opened in line 1, paid off in the last two lines; the script says where He stopped, never why.)
- Why a global newcomer gets it: short plain words, one room, one book, one action (He closes it); "synagogue" is explained, and the old words "anointed" and "acceptable year" are replaced by plain English.
