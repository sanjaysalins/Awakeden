**1. ENDING: 4/10.** It feels added on. The film already closes on the line before it: "He stopped reading at God's welcome, and said: 'This day.'" That line answers the open hook. The Paul line then brings in a new person, a new time ("Later") and a new verse after the climax. The word "day" does echo, but it reads like a sermon tag, not a payoff.

**2. NEWCOMER: it fails.**
- **"salvation"** is church language. It is never explained, and dubbing turns it into a formal religious word.
- **"Paul"**: "a follower of Jesus" helps, but he is still a stranger who shows up in the last 4 seconds.
- **"Later"** is vague. How much later? Who was he writing to?

**3. HONESTY: no false claim, but it hints at a false link.** The line never says Paul is quoting Isaiah 61. But placing it straight after "This day" makes it sound like Paul is finishing Jesus' sentence. He is quoting Isaiah 49:8. The real bridge in the KJV is the half you left out: "now is the **accepted** time," which matches Isaiah 61:2's "**acceptable** year." You quoted the weaker half. The context is also Paul pleading with believers ("receive not the grace of God in vain"). Using it as an invitation to everyone is common, but it's a stretch. Fair enough to use, not the strongest choice.

**4. LANDING:** It is gentle, with no pressure. But it does not land on Jesus. The last name spoken is **Paul**, and in the dub the spoken words are what count, not the picture. "Now is the day of salvation" is also one of the most worn tract lines there is. It's stock, and only half earned by the "day" thread.

**5. Overall film words: 7/10.** The hook, the stop in mid-sentence and the "local boy" irony are strong. The ending is the weak spot.

**CHANGE it.** Cut the Paul line and end on Jesus' own word:

> [narrator] He stopped reading at God's welcome. Not someday. He said: "This day."

This stays faithful to Luke 4:21 ("fulfilled"), needs no new person and no church word, and ends on Jesus. Then the 3 seconds of silence over the sunrise.

If you have to keep Paul, use the half that really links:

> [narrator] Years later, Paul, a follower of Jesus, wrote: "Now is the accepted time."

It's weaker for newcomers, but more honest.
