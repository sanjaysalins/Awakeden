1. ENDING: 3/10. It feels tacked on. It attempts to echo the "This day" theme, but introducing a completely new character and era in the final seconds derails the story's climax. It pulls the viewer out of Nazareth right when the tension peaks.

2. NEWCOMER: A non-Christian viewer will stumble. "Paul" is an unexplained stranger stepping in at the last moment. "Salvation" is Christian jargon that hasn't been defined; the script successfully used plain English ("welcome", "freedom", "good news") up to this point, only to drop a heavy theological term at the very end.

3. HONESTY: While it accurately quotes 2 Corinthians 6:2, splicing it directly onto the Nazareth story is a textual stretch. Paul is referencing Isaiah 49, not the Isaiah 61 passage Jesus read. It forces a bridge that the text itself isn't building. 

4. LANDING: It shifts the final landing onto a "follower" instead of Christ. The image holds on Jesus at sunrise, but the script gives the final word to Paul. It dilutes the power of Jesus’ own "This day" declaration and feels like a stock preacher's wrap-up rather than an earned close.

5. Overall film words: 7/10. The hook and the observation of Jesus stopping mid-sentence are brilliant and deeply grounded. The ending is the only weak link.

CHANGE it. Keep the focus entirely on Jesus and the plain-English contrast established earlier.
