# Seat: Fable (blind, read PACK.md only)

**1. ENDING: 5/10.** It half closes. The echo "This day" -> "Now is the day" is real. But the line before it already closes the film: "He stopped reading at God's welcome, and said: 'This day.'" That is the payoff, in Jesus' own words. The Paul line then opens a new person, a new book and a new word in the last 4 seconds. It reads like a footnote added after the ending.

**2. NEWCOMER: partly fails.**
- "Paul" is OK, because "a follower of Jesus" explains him. But he is a brand-new name at the very end, and "Later" gives no sense of when.
- "salvation" fails. The film never says it or explains it. The film's own word is "God's welcome". A newcomer cannot tell that "salvation" and "welcome" are the same thing. It is also a hard word for second-language viewers and a church word in most dubs.

**3. HONESTY: fair, with one soft spot.**
- The words are KJV, but clipped: KJV says "behold, now is the day of salvation". Minor.
- Paul is quoting Isaiah 49:8, not Isaiah 61. The film does not say he is talking about Nazareth, but putting it here lets viewers assume it. That is an implied link, not a stated one.
- The link is still honest: "acceptable year" (Luke 4:19) and "accepted time" (2 Cor 6:2) are the same idea, and Paul says it is "now". Not a misuse. No over-claim.

**4. LANDING: grace yes, pressure no, but the last voice is no longer Jesus.**
The picture lands on Jesus; the words land on Paul. "Day" is earned. "Salvation" is not earned; it is the stock church word arriving from outside the story. The line before it is the earned landing.

**5. Overall film words: 7/10** with the new line. **8/10** without it. The hook, the unread sentence and "Is not this Joseph's son?" are strong and clear.

**Verdict: CUT.** End on: "He stopped reading at God's welcome, and said: 'This day.'" then the 3-second silent hold.

If a closing line is a must, CHANGE to: **"That day of God's welcome is still open. This day."** (uses the film's own words, supported by 2 Cor 6:2, last word stays Jesus' word).
