# Review: two versions of the Nazareth script (Luke 4 / Isaiah 61)

## Version 1

**1. Newcomer:** Who and what are clear. The place comes early, which helps. The "why" is weak, because the script never says why the crowd wanted to kill Him. One line would lose them: "This day is this scripture fulfilled in your ears." It is old English, and no line explains it.

**2. Bible reader:** Yes, they get something new. Jesus stopped mid-verse, before "the day of vengeance." Most churchgoers have never noticed that.

**3. Curiosity gap: 6/10.** The question opens well but pays off by line 4. The ending does not loop back to the stopped sentence.

**4. Accuracy:** Mostly sound. "Welcome" for "acceptable year" is a fair paraphrase. Two risks:
- It suggests the old proverb alone made them angry. In Luke 4:25-28, the anger comes from the stories of the foreign widow and Naaman.
- "Left the day of vengeance unread" could sound like judgment is cancelled. It is held back, not deleted.

**5. Weakest line:** "Then He said no prophet is welcomed at home." It is too packed, Jesus does not speak it, and it does not explain the anger. **Fix:** let [jesus] say the KJV line, then add: "He said God's help once went to outsiders. They were furious."

**6. Overall: 6.5/10**

## Version 2

**1. Newcomer:** Who, what and why are all clearer. "And gave His life" tells a newcomer what the cross means. "Years later" connects the two events. The same old-English line is still unexplained. "Nazareth" comes late, so the first 15 seconds have no place.

**2. Bible reader:** Same insight as Version 1, plus the cliff/cross contrast: "He walked away" there, "He stayed" here. That contrast is strong.

**3. Curiosity gap: 8/10.** "He stopped reading at the welcome" closes the loop the hook opened. A good payoff.

**4. Accuracy:** Luke 23:46 supports "gave His life." The same two risks as Version 1 apply: the cause of the anger, and vengeance being held back rather than cancelled.

**5. Weakest line:** "This day is this scripture fulfilled in your ears." **Fix:** keep the KJV, then add a narrator line: "Today, this comes true, as you listen."

**6. Overall: 7.5/10**

## My pick: Version 2

**The one change:** add the proverb from Luke 4:23, "Physician, heal thyself." At Nazareth the line was "heal yourself." At the cross it became "save yourself." That echo is a real insight, it is in the KJV, and it makes "This time, He stayed" land much harder.

Be careful how you frame it. In 4:23, Jesus is predicting what they will say; the crowd does not say it. So the narrator should say: "He knew they'd say: Physician, heal thyself."
