For version 1:

1. NEWCOMER: Who and what are clear: Jesus reads a promise, claims its fulfilment, faces violence, and later faces mockers at the cross. Why His death matters remains unexplained. “This day is this scripture fulfilled in your ears” is difficult English; “vengeance” also needs simpler wording.

2. BIBLE READER: Yes. Comparing Isaiah’s continuation with where Jesus finishes reading offers a real discovery. The cliff/cross contrast adds another connection, though it is the narrator’s interpretation.

3. CURIOSITY GAP: 7/10. The implied question—why did He stop?—gets a partial answer: He omitted vengeance. His reason for doing so remains unstated.

4. ACCURATE: The sequence misleadingly suggests that “no prophet is welcomed at home” provoked the attack. Luke places their anger after His examples of God helping people outside Israel. Calling the acceptable year “God’s welcome” is interpretive; omitting vengeance does not establish that judgment is cancelled.

5. WEAKEST LINE: “This day is this scripture fulfilled in your ears.” A plain paraphrase: “Today, God’s promise is coming true in me.”

6. OVERALL: 6/10. Clear setting, but the central claim needs decoding and the crowd’s reaction needs its missing cause.

For version 2:

1. NEWCOMER: The setting arrives late, though viewers eventually learn who and where. “Gave His life” makes the death explicit. The same fulfilment quotation remains difficult, and “his own country” may suggest an entire nation rather than His home town. The connection between His death and today’s invitation is still assumed.

2. BIBLE READER: Yes: the unread vengeance clause, followed by the contrast between walking away and remaining at the cross. The ending recalls the opening more clearly.

3. CURIOSITY GAP: 8/10. Stronger closure, although “He stopped reading at the welcome” repeats the observation without proving His motive.

4. ACCURATE: The same missing cause and judgment risks apply. “Years later” is not established by the supplied passages; “Later” is sufficient. The present invitation is an application, rather than something these verses explicitly state.

5. WEAKEST LINE: “They led Him to a cliff” lacks the necessary bridge. Add before it: “He reminded them that God had helped people outside Israel. They became furious.”

6. OVERALL: 7/10.

My pick is 2. The single best change is that bridge explaining the crowd’s anger.

Both contain roughly 200 spoken words; sixty seconds, including pauses and the final hold, would feel rushed.
