# Seat: Fable (blind)

## VERSION 1

1. **NEWCOMER.** WHO: yes (Jesus, home town). WHAT: mostly. WHY: weak.
   - "day of vengeance" - hard word, never explained.
   - "fulfilled in your ears" - old English, no plain gloss.
   - "no prophet is welcomed at home" then a cliff - why would that make them want to kill Him? The jump is not explained.
   - "At the cross, they said" - no bridge. A newcomer does not know what the cross is, or that time has passed.
   - "This time, He stayed" - stayed for what? Never said.
2. **BIBLE READER.** The stop mid-sentence is a fairly well-known sermon point. The fresher things: "welcome" read out, then "not welcomed" at home (same word in Luke 4:19 and 4:24), and cliff (walked away) against cross (stayed). Good, but only hinted.
3. **CURIOSITY GAP: 5/10.** Opens well. Never says why He stopped. The last line does not point back to the scroll.
4. **ACCURATE.** "At the cross, they said" - "they" sounds like the Nazareth crowd. KJV says the rulers in Jerusalem (Luke 23:35). Misleading. "Welcomed at home" is a paraphrase, fine in narrator voice. Rest holds.
5. **WEAKEST LINE.** "This time, He stayed." Fix: "This time, He stayed, and gave His life."
6. **OVERALL: 6/10.**

## VERSION 2

1. **NEWCOMER.** WHO and WHAT: yes. WHY: better, because "gave His life" and the last line say it.
   - Still hard: "vengeance", "fulfilled in your ears".
   - "No prophet is accepted in his own country" - the cliff still comes from nowhere.
   - Nazareth arrives late (line 7). The first three lines have no place. Small cost, but the hook lands faster.
2. **BIBLE READER.** Same insights. But the KJV word "accepted" hides the echo with "welcome", so the best find is harder to hear than in Version 1.
3. **CURIOSITY GAP: 8/10.** "He stopped reading at the welcome. It is still open" closes the loop out loud. Works when dubbed.
4. **ACCURATE.** "Years later" - Luke gives no number. Not in the KJV text. Say "Later". "Gave His life" is supported (Luke 23:46). Quiet risk in both: a viewer may hear that God's judgment was cancelled, not held back. The text only shows He left it unread.
5. **WEAKEST LINE.** "Then He said: No prophet is accepted in his own country." It does not earn the cliff. Fix below.
6. **OVERALL: 7.5/10.**

## MY PICK: Version 2

**The single change:** explain the anger in one plain line, from Luke 4:25-27, after the prophet quote:

> [narrator] He said God's welcome had gone to foreigners before. They were furious.

This makes the cliff make sense for a newcomer. It also gives the churchgoer the real insight: the welcome was wider than they wanted.

(Small extra: change "Years later" to "Later".)
