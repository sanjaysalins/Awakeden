1. NEWCOMER: Jesus returns to His hometown and reads an old promise from God about help and freedom. He stops before words about punishment, then says the promise is fulfilled today. His neighbours wonder how someone they know could make that claim. Why He stops remains unexplained.

“This day is this scripture fulfilled in your ears” would be hard to understand. “Is not this Joseph’s son?” also needs interpretation: a newcomer does not know Joseph, though “local boy” helps.

2. HOOK -> MIDDLE -> LANDING: The opening creates a clear question: why stop there? The middle reveals the missing words, and Jesus’ statement connects the promise to the present. The ending returns to both “welcome” and “This day.”

“But they knew Him as a local boy” briefly shifts the thread toward local familiarity. It connects to the ending, but “But” suggests doubt more strongly than the supplied passage establishes.

3. HONESTY: “Jesus stopped reading in the middle of a sentence” is an inference from comparing Isaiah with Luke. Isaiah’s sentence continues; Luke ends the recorded reading there and says Jesus closed the book. Luke does not explicitly describe an interruption.

“That local boy was the one Isaiah promised” states an interpretation as fact. Jesus’ fulfilment statement strongly supports that reading, but the narrator makes the identification more explicit.

The script gives no explicit reason for stopping and claims no timetable for judgement. “God’s welcome” is an accessible interpretation of “the acceptable year,” rather than its literal wording.

4. LANDING: **8/10.** It lands on Jesus and His claim, with no demand placed on viewers. The silent final image gives that claim room. Its meaning depends partly on understanding the difficult KJV sentence.

5. Overall: **8/10.**

ONE small change: Replace “That local boy was the one Isaiah promised” with “Jesus was saying: God’s promise had come true through Him, that very day.”
