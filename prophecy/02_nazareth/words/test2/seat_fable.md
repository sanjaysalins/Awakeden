# Seat: Fable (blind, independent)

**1. NEWCOMER**
Who: Jesus, a man back in his small home town, Nazareth.
What: He stands up and reads an old promise from a prophet called Isaiah: good news for the poor, freedom, a time when God welcomes people. He stops halfway through a sentence, just before the words about God's day of punishment, shuts the book, sits down and says the promise is coming true today.
Why: The neighbours like his words but only see the boy from down the street. The film says he is the person the old promise was about.

Lines I would trip on:
- "This day is this scripture fulfilled in your ears." Old word order, and "fulfilled in your ears" is strange for a second-language viewer. The last line partly explains it, but late.
- "and the day of vengeance of our God." "Vengeance" is a hard word. The next line ("day of judgement") rescues it, so this is fine.
- "He closed the book" while the picture is a scroll. Small bump.

**2. HOOK -> MIDDLE -> LANDING**
Hook is strong and plain; it opens a real question (which sentence, why stop?). Middle follows in order and the Isaiah voice finishing the sentence is the best moment. Nothing wanders.
One weak link: line 3 says he read a promise of *things* (good news, freedom, welcome). The landing then says he was "the one Isaiah promised". A newcomer was never told Isaiah promised a *person*. The landing claim arrives without its set-up.

**3. HONESTY**
No reason for stopping is given, and no timetable. Good restraint.
- "That local boy was the one Isaiah promised" goes a small step past Luke 4:21 (he says the scripture is fulfilled; the script names him as its subject). It is a fair reading of "anointed me / sent me", but the script cut those words, so it sounds asserted, not shown.
- "But they knew Him as a local boy": the "But" adds a little doubt that verse 22 alone does not state. Mild, acceptable.
- "kind words" for "gracious words": fine.

**4. LANDING: 8/10**
Lands on Jesus, uses his own two words, no pressure, no ask. "He read as far as God's welcome" pays off the hook cleanly. Loses points only because "the one Isaiah promised" is not set up.

**5. Overall: 7.5/10**

**ONE small change** (line 3):
"He read God's promise of someone sent to bring good news to the poor, freedom for prisoners, and a time of God's welcome."
