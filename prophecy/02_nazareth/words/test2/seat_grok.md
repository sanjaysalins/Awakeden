1. NEWCOMER: A man named Jesus, from his home town of Nazareth, reads an old promise out loud, stops before a line about God's vengeance, sits down, and says that promise is happening that day. The people are amazed, then ask if he is Joseph's son. The narrator says he is the one Isaiah promised.

I would not understand this line: "This day is this scripture fulfilled in your ears." "Scripture" and "fulfilled" are hard words, and "in your ears" sounds like the writing is inside their ears. "Day of vengeance" is also a hard phrase.

2. HOOK, MIDDLE, LANDING: The opening line makes me ask why he stopped. The middle answers it: his town, the promise, the shut book, the unread line, every eye, his words, then the town's question. Each step leads to the next. No line wanders. The last line repeats the stop ("as far as God's welcome") after the story already showed it. It still stays on the same thread.

3. HONESTY: "He left the day of judgement unread" says more than the KJV. The KJV says "the day of vengeance of our God." The verses show him closing the book after "the acceptable year of the Lord." They give no reason for the stop. "That local boy was the one Isaiah promised" is the narrator deciding who Jesus is. Jesus' own line says the reading is fulfilled this day. Isaiah 61:2 also still has "to comfort all that mourn" after the vengeance line. The script treats the unread part as judgement alone. "In the middle of a sentence" matches the English verse, and it is stronger than "he closed the book."

4. LANDING: 7. It ends on Jesus, with welcome and kind words, and with no push at the viewer. The silent picture helps. It is only partly earned. The plain meaning sits in the narrator's last claim, while Jesus' own sentence is hard to follow.

5. Overall: 7.

ONE small change: replace "He left the day of judgement unread, and sat down." with "He stopped before the day of vengeance, and sat down."
