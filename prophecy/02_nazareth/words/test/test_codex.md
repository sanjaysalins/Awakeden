X

1. NEWCOMER: Jesus reads an old promise in his hometown and says it is coming true now. The ending suggests he skipped punishment to welcome me. “They handed Him the prophet Isaiah” sounds like handing him a person. “This day is this scripture fulfilled in your ears” needs explanation. Joseph’s identity and the significance of his son are unclear.

2. CURIOSITY GAP: 7/10. “Why?” creates interest. The omitted words provide a reveal, but the script never establishes why Jesus stopped.

3. HONESTY: “He stopped at the welcome. It is for you” turns the stopping point into a personal assurance these verses do not explicitly give. Following “Why?”, it also sounds like an explanation of his motive. “God’s welcome” is an interpretation of “acceptable year.” The seven-hundred-year date is not established by the supplied verses.

4. HARD WORDS: “Captives,” “vengeance,” “scripture,” “fulfilled,” and “in your ears.” “Is not this” is unfamiliar sentence structure. “Nazareth” and “Isaiah” could be miscaptioned.

5. BIBLE READER: Yes, potentially: hearing Isaiah’s next clause draws attention to where Luke’s recorded reading ends.

6. LANDING: It offers grace without demanding anything, but “It is for you” feels like an added devotional slogan. The direct address briefly shifts attention from Jesus to the viewer. The silent final picture can restore that focus.

7. Overall: 6/10.

Y

1. NEWCOMER: Jesus reads God’s promise in the town where he grew up, stops before words about punishment, and announces its fulfilment. The contrast is clearer than in X. I still need help with “This day is this scripture fulfilled in your ears” and why people mention Joseph.

2. CURIOSITY GAP: 8/10. The unusual stopping point creates a question without stating it. Hearing the next clause pays off what he omitted; his motive remains open.

3. HONESTY: It does not explicitly claim a motive. “He stopped at God’s welcome” still gives the omission an interpretive emphasis. “A time of God’s welcome” is a gloss, and the date remains outside the supplied evidence.

4. HARD WORDS: “The acceptable year of the Lord” adds another difficult phrase. Also “captives,” “vengeance,” “scripture,” “fulfilled,” and “Is not this.” The names carry the same caption risks.

5. BIBLE READER: Yes: the immediate comparison with Isaiah makes the stopping point noticeable.

6. LANDING: Jesus remains central, with grace and no pressure. “And He said: ‘This day’” is earned through repetition, although newcomers may still miss what happens that day.

7. Overall: 8/10.

PICK Y: it makes the textual contrast clearer and avoids X’s unsupported personal assurance.

ONE small change: replace the final narration with “Jesus said God’s promise was coming true that day.”
