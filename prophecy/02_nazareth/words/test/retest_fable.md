# Retest — Fable seat (blind)

**1. NEWCOMER**
Who: Jesus, back in the small town where He grew up. What: He reads an old promise from a prophet's book — good news, freedom, God's welcome — stops before the next words, closes the book, and says the promise comes true today. Why: the video shows that He stopped at the kind part and left out the harsh part. I get who / what / why.

Lines I would not fully understand:
- "and the day of vengeance of our God." — the whole twist rests on "vengeance". If I do not know that word, I do not know what He left out.
- "Is not this Joseph's son?" — who is Joseph? Is the man doubting, proud, or rude? Nothing tells me. The frowning picture helps a little.
- "This day is this scripture fulfilled in your ears." — "fulfilled in your ears" is strange, but the last line rescues it.

**2. CURIOSITY GAP: 8/10**
Strong first line. I want to know what the rest of the sentence was. The pay-off is real (we hear the unread words), but it is carried by one hard word, so a second-language viewer may get "He stopped" and miss "at what".

**3. HONESTY**
Clean. It never says why He stopped. "He left that unread" matches Luke 4:19-20. "about seven hundred years before" is fair with "about". Two small stretches, neither serious:
- "And He said when it begins" — KJV says "fulfilled", not "begins". Mild.
- The summary drops "sight to the blind" and "brokenhearted". Fine for a summary.
- "closed the book" is KJV, but the picture is a scroll rolled shut. Tiny mismatch.

**4. HARD WORDS**
"vengeance" (worst — load-bearing), "fulfilled in your ears", "wondered at" (reads as "were unsure about", not "were amazed"), "gracious", "unread", "prophet". Auto-captions may mangle "Isaiah".

**5. BIBLE READER: yes**
Most readers know "This day is this scripture fulfilled". Few have noticed He stopped mid-verse, before "the day of vengeance". Hearing Isaiah's voice finish the sentence is the fresh part.

**6. LANDING**
Lands on Jesus. Grace, no pressure, no stock invitation. Earned: "This day" is His own word, and "God's welcome" was set up earlier. Weak part: "And He said when it begins" is a little stiff, and the "Joseph's son" line sits just before it with no meaning given, so the ending has to recover from a small bump.

**7. Overall: 7.5/10**

**ONE small change**
Replace
> He left that unread, and sat down.

with
> He left the day of punishment unread, and sat down.

This glosses "vengeance" in plain words at the exact moment the twist lands, and claims no reason.
