# Blind review: seat Fable

## X

1. **NEWCOMER:** Jesus goes back to his home town, reads an old promise from God aloud, and stops half way. He says it is coming true today. The cut-off part was about God's revenge, so he stopped on the kind part. One line fails: "In Isaiah, that sentence goes on:". It comes straight after the townsman, so "that sentence" sounds like Joseph's son, or Jesus's line. The hook is 30 seconds old by then. "Is not this Joseph's son?" is never explained (who is Joseph, is he doubting?).
2. **CURIOSITY GAP:** 7. "Why?" pulls hard. The payoff is late and a bit tangled, and the "Why?" is never truly answered.
3. **HONESTY:** "Why?" promises a reason the Bible does not give. The end only says where He stopped, which is fair, but "It is for you." reads as the answer to "Why?", so it leans to a claimed motive. "place of prayer" is loose for "synagogue". "about seven hundred years before" is not in the KJV text given.
4. **HARD WORDS:** "vengeance" (the whole twist hangs on it), "fulfilled in your ears", "captives", "scripture", "prophet Isaiah".
5. **BIBLE READER:** Yes. Most never notice He stopped at a comma in Isaiah 61:2.
6. **LANDING:** On Jesus, gentle. "It is for you." is the weak part: a stock turn to the viewer, told not shown.
7. **Overall:** 6.5

## Y

1. **NEWCOMER:** Jesus reads God's promise in his home town. He shuts the book just before the line about God's revenge, leaves it unread, sits down, and says the promise is true today. Clear order: I hear the missing words at the moment He closes the book. Unclear: "Is not this Joseph's son?" (same problem), and "the acceptable year of the Lord" is heavy, though it is explained at once.
2. **CURIOSITY GAP:** 7.5. No "Why?", but the first line still opens the gap. Paid off cleanly at the mid point. The second half then rests on Jesus's own line, which works.
3. **HONESTY:** Cleanest. "He left that unread" and "He stopped at God's welcome" are plain facts. No motive claimed. Only "about seven hundred years before" is outside the KJV.
4. **HARD WORDS:** "vengeance", "the acceptable year of the Lord", "fulfilled in your ears", "captives", "prophet Isaiah".
5. **BIBLE READER:** Yes, and stronger: the unread words are heard exactly where the book shuts.
6. **LANDING:** On Jesus, in His own words, no pressure. Earned. Slightly thin: a newcomer may not link "This day" to the welcome. "And He said:" is flat.
7. **Overall:** 7.5

## PICK

**PICK Y**: it shows the cut where it happens, claims no reason, and ends on Jesus's own words instead of a stock "It is for you."

**ONE small change (last line):**
`[narrator] He stopped at God's welcome. And He said when: "This day."`
