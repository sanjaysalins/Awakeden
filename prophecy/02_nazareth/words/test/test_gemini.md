**Version X**
1. NEWCOMER: Jesus reads an old prophecy in his hometown, stops early, and claims it is happening now. The townsman is confused because he knows Jesus's dad. Unclear: "fulfilled in your ears" is a very strange idiom for modern or ESL speakers.
2. CURIOSITY GAP: 7/10. "Why?" is a strong hook, but the payoff is clumsy. The script jumps from Jesus speaking, to the townsman, and then backward to explain Isaiah. The tension leaks out.
3. HONESTY: It borders on overclaiming by explicitly answering its own "Why?" with "It is for you." The Bible does not state His internal motive for stopping there.
4. HARD WORDS: "fulfilled in your ears" (KJV verbatim, but confusing literalism). 
5. BIBLE READER: Yes, pointing out that He stopped mid-sentence right before the "day of vengeance" is a profound structural insight many miss.
6. LANDING: "It is for you" feels like a stock, bolted-on application. It lands on grace, but the phrasing feels slightly preachy rather than earned by the story.
7. Overall: 7/10.

**Version Y**
1. NEWCOMER: Jesus reads an old text, deliberately skips the scary part about vengeance, and tells the room the good part is happening today. Unclear: "the acceptable year of the Lord" is clunky religious jargon.
2. CURIOSITY GAP: 9/10. The hook is great. By revealing the skipped "vengeance" line *before* Jesus speaks, the audience understands the weight of His silence.
3. HONESTY: Very honest. It states exactly what He did ("He left that unread") without presuming to invent a psychological "why" not found in the KJV.
4. HARD WORDS: "the acceptable year of the Lord", "fulfilled in your ears".
5. BIBLE READER: Yes, highlighting exactly where He stopped in Isaiah 61 gives a fresh appreciation for Christ's focus on grace.
6. LANDING: "He stopped at God's welcome. And He said: 'This day.'" Excellent. It relies entirely on the text's own weight. Deeply graceful, no pressure.
7. Overall: 9/10.

**PICK Y.** 
Revealing the skipped "vengeance" line *before* Jesus speaks makes His final quote infinitely more powerful and keeps the timeline moving forward.

**CHANGE:** 
In the third line, remove the KJV jargon from the narrator's prose. Change `and "the acceptable year of the Lord": a time of God's welcome.` to just `and a time of God's welcome.`
