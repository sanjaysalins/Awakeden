## X

1. **Newcomer:** Jesus read an old promise in His home town. He stopped before a line about God's anger. Then He said "this is happening now." People asked, "Isn't this just Joseph's boy?" I can follow it. Two lines are unclear:
   - "fulfilled in your ears": the words "in your ears" sound odd to a second-language listener.
   - "Is not this Joseph's son?": I don't know who Joseph is. Is the speaker angry or amazed?
2. **Curiosity gap: 6/10.** "Why?" grabs me. But the video never answers it, and it can't honestly answer it. Asking a question out loud and then leaving it open feels like a broken promise. The late Isaiah reveal does pay off what he left out. It does not pay off "why."
3. **Honesty:** There is no false claim, but "Why?" invites one. One framing issue: the sentence also goes on to "to comfort all that mourn," which He left unread too. So "welcome vs vengeance" is a selection, not the whole picture. It's a standard reading, but a careful reader will notice. "It is for you" is application, not text. That's acceptable.
4. **Hard words:** "captives", "vengeance", "fulfilled in your ears". "Place of prayer" works.
5. **Bible reader:** Yes. Hearing the skipped Isaiah line as the last reveal is a real "oh."
6. **Landing:** "It is for you. 'This day.'" is warm, with no pressure. But it is choppy, and "It is for you" is a stock line. As a lone caption, "This day." looks broken.
7. **Overall: 6.5/10**

## Y

1. **Newcomer:** Same story, told in order. I learn what was left out before He sits, so His "this day" makes more sense when it comes. The Joseph line has the same problem as in X: who is Joseph, and what is the tone?
2. **Curiosity gap: 7/10.** "Stopped in the middle of a sentence" is a hook without promising a "why." The payoff ("He left that unread") comes at mid-point. It's clear, though a little early.
3. **Honesty:** Cleaner than X. It never claims a reason. "He left that unread" is exactly true. The same "comfort" note from X applies.
4. **Hard words:** "the acceptable year of the Lord" is hard, but it is explained right away, which is fine. Also "vengeance", "captives", "fulfilled in your ears". "Unread" is fine.
5. **Bible reader:** Yes. The same insight, and the order makes the contrast sharper.
6. **Landing:** "He stopped at God's welcome. And He said: 'This day.'" lands on Jesus, gently, with no pressure. It is earned, because it echoes His own words. It is a little understated, since it never says what "this day" means for the viewer.
7. **Overall: 7.5/10**

## Pick: Y

Y is more honest: it raises no unanswerable "why," and it says plainly what He left out.

**One change, to Y's last line:**
"He stopped at God's welcome. And He said that welcome starts: 'This day.'"

This ties "this day" to the welcome in plain words. The viewer no longer has to work out what "This day" means.
