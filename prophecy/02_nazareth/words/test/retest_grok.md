1. NEWCOMER: Jesus is in his home town, Nazareth. He reads an old writing from Isaiah: good news for the poor, freedom for prisoners, and a time of God's welcome. He stops, shuts the scroll, and sits. The next words he does not read are about a day of God's vengeance. He says this writing comes true today, while they listen. People stare, like the words, and ask if he is Joseph's son.

Lines I would not get: "This day is this scripture fulfilled in your ears." "Scripture" is unknown, and "in your ears" does not mean "while you hear it" to me. "Is not this Joseph's son?" Who is Joseph, and why ask? "written about seven hundred years before." Before what?

2. CURIOSITY GAP: The first line does the job. I want to know where he stopped. The video answers with the vengeance line, then "This day." The Joseph question starts a new puzzle and drops it. 6/10.

3. HONESTY: Luke says he read through the acceptable year, closed the book, and said the scripture is fulfilled this day. Luke does not say why he stopped.

Overclaim: "And He said when it begins: 'This day.'" He said it is fulfilled this day. He did not say this is the moment God's welcome starts.

"Jesus stopped reading in the middle of a sentence." Luke 4:19 is a finished line. "Middle of a sentence" is a guess about Isaiah's wording.

"Isaiah's sentence goes on: and the day of vengeance of our God." Isaiah 61:2 also says "to comfort all that mourn." The script makes vengeance the whole unread part.

"about seven hundred years before" is not in the KJV. "About" keeps it soft.

4. HARD WORDS: "prophet," "vengeance," "gracious," "scripture," "fulfilled in your ears," "Joseph's son." Captions will show "ears" as body parts. "This day is this scripture fulfilled" is old word order. "Joseph's son" sounds like a name, not a doubt.

5. BIBLE READER: Yes. Someone who knows Luke 4 sees the stop before Isaiah's "day of vengeance," and that Luke's quote is shorter than Isaiah 61:2.

6. LANDING: It ends on Jesus and on welcome. No push to pray or choose. The last picture can hold him in quiet. The last words are a narrator slogan after Jesus already said "This day." Weak part: "And He said when it begins: 'This day.'"

7. Overall: 6/10.

ONE small change: replace "He stopped at God's welcome. And He said when it begins: 'This day.'" with "He read as far as God's welcome. Then He said, 'This day.'"

VERDICT: REVISE — the cutoff is real; the last line adds a timetable the KJV does not say.
