**X**

1. NEWCOMER: Jesus, in his hometown, reads an old promise in a prayer room: good news for the poor, freedom for prisoners, God welcoming people. He shuts the book, sits, and says it is happening today. A man asks if he is Joseph’s son. Then we hear the next words were about God’s vengeance, and Jesus stopped before them. I would not understand “This day is this scripture fulfilled in your ears,” or “Is not this Joseph’s son?” (who is Joseph, and is this praise or a jab?). “It is for you” — the room, or me?

2. CURIOSITY GAP: “Why?” makes me want an answer. The missing words come after Jesus has already spoken, and the answer is a slogan. 5/10.

3. HONESTY: Luke shows the reading ends at the acceptable year, then he closes the book. It does not say why. X asks “Why?” and answers “He stopped at the welcome. It is for you.” That reason is not in the text. “In your ears” is the room, not the viewer. Isaiah 61:2 also says “to comfort all that mourn,” not only vengeance. “They handed Him the prophet Isaiah” skips that he “found the place.”

4. HARD WORDS: “scripture fulfilled in your ears”; “captives”; “vengeance”; “prophet.”

5. BIBLE READER: Yes — the cutoff before “the day of vengeance.” A known sermon point, then flattened.

6. LANDING: The picture holds on Jesus; the words grab me. Weak part: “It is for you.” Pressure, and not earned.

7. Overall: 5/10.

**Y**

1. NEWCOMER: Same story, clearer order. Jesus reads good news, freedom, and a time of God’s welcome, then stops before a line about vengeance, shuts the book, and sits. Everyone stares. He says the promise is fulfilled today. A man asks if he is Joseph’s son. I still would not understand “This day is this scripture fulfilled in your ears,” or who Joseph is. The words never say he is in a meeting room.

2. CURIOSITY GAP: The first line withholds the missing half. I hear “and the day of vengeance of our God” before he speaks, so his line pays it off. 7/10.

3. HONESTY: “in the middle of a sentence” is stronger than the text. Luke 4:19 is a full sentence; the “middle” comes from comparing Isaiah. “He left that unread” matches what Luke records. “He stopped at God’s welcome” states a motive the text does not. Same shared gaps: the comfort clause is unmentioned; verse 22’s “wondered at the gracious words” is dropped, so the question sounds only like a sneer. Order slip: he closes the book, then we are told he left the line unread.

4. HARD WORDS: “acceptable” (today it means “just OK” — the wrong sense; the gloss “a time of God’s welcome” saves it only if the viewer catches it); “scripture fulfilled in your ears”; “vengeance”; “captives.”

5. BIBLE READER: Yes. “The acceptable year of the Lord,” then the vengeance line, then “This day,” is the real insight.

6. LANDING: It lands on Jesus and on welcome, with no push at the viewer. Earned by the cutoff. Weak part: “And He said: ‘This day.’” A fragment, if you did not understand the hard line.

7. Overall: 7/10.

PICK: Y — the omitted words arrive before Jesus speaks, and the ending does not claim the viewer. One change: replace the last narrator line with “He left the day of vengeance unread. He said that promise is fulfilled today.”

VERDICT: Y is the stronger cut. X fails honesty by answering its own "Why?" with "It is for you."
