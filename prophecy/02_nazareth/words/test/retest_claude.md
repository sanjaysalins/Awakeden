1. **NEWCOMER**
- **Who:** Jesus, in His home town.
- **What:** He reads an old promise out loud, stops partway through, sits down and says it is coming true today. The locals are impressed, but they also doubt Him.
- **Why:** I can't tell for sure. I don't clearly get that He is saying *"Isaiah wrote about me."*
- **Lines I would not understand:**
  - "This day is this scripture fulfilled in your ears." The word order is old. I would rely on the captions.
  - "Is not this Joseph's son?" Who is Joseph? Why does it matter? The frowning villager helps a little, but the point (doubt about a local boy) is never said.
  - "They wondered at His gracious words." A second-language listener may hear "wondered" as "doubted." That clashes with the frown.

2. **CURIOSITY GAP: 7/10.** The opening line is strong and concrete. The video only half pays it off. It shows *what* He left out, and that is good. But "why did He stop?" is replaced by "He stopped at the welcome," and the vengeance part is left hanging. A newcomer may ask: "So is the vengeance coming later?"

3. **HONESTY:** Mostly clean. It never says *why* He stopped. Small points:
   - "about seven hundred years before" is not in the KJV text. Scholars argue about the date of Isaiah 61. "Long before" is safer.
   - "closed the book" fits the KJV, but the picture is a scroll. Fine, just slightly off.
   - "He stopped at God's welcome" quietly hints at a reason. It stays on the right side of the line because it only describes the fact.

4. **HARD WORDS:** "vengeance", "gracious", "wondered at", "fulfilled in your ears", "Is not this", "prophet". Also "a time of God's welcome", which is a lovely paraphrase but abstract. Auto-captions should handle "Isaiah" and "Nazareth".

5. **BIBLE READER: Yes.** Putting the left-out line in the prophet's own voice is new, and it makes the missing half something you can hear. Many readers know about the mid-verse stop, but few have *heard* it like this.

6. **LANDING:** Gentle, full of grace, no pressure, and earned from the text. "This day" is a good word to land on. Two weak spots:
   - "And He said when it begins" is clumsy to say out loud.
   - It lands on a *time*, not clearly on *Jesus*. "Fulfilled" never turns into "it's about Him." There is also no quiet turn to the viewer.

7. **OVERALL: 7/10.** The idea and the structure are smart. It loses points because the newcomer may miss the claim to be the fulfillment, and because the doubt line comes without context.

**ONE small change:** Make the last line: **He stopped at God's welcome. And He said it starts now, with Him: "This day."**
