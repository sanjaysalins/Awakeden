# Write ONE narration from scratch - "He stopped mid-sentence" (Isaiah 61:1-2 -> Luke 4:16-22). You are ONE blind writer; you have seen no other draft.

## The series
"Promised, then kept": an Old Testament promise, and how it came true in Jesus. Every episode lands on Jesus. Bold graphic-comic look, vertical short.

## Who it is for
Worldwide, many with English as a second language, many with no Bible knowledge. Also Bible readers who have read Luke 4 and never noticed where He stopped.

## Length (hard)
30-60 seconds, the shorter the better. 70-110 spoken words total, including quotes.

## The facts you may use (all from the KJV pack below; use only what you need)
- About 700 years before Jesus, the prophet Isaiah wrote of someone God would send with good news for the poor, the broken-hearted and the captives (Isaiah 61:1-2).
- Jesus came to Nazareth, the town where He grew up. On the Sabbath, as was His custom, He stood up in the synagogue (the local meeting place for prayer and Scripture) to read. He was handed the book of Isaiah and found this place (Luke 4:16-17).
- What Luke records Him reading: "The Spirit of the Lord is upon me, because he hath anointed me to preach the gospel to the poor ... To preach the acceptable year of the Lord." (Luke 4:18-19). Quote Luke's wording for what Jesus read, not Isaiah's KJV wording; they differ slightly.
- In Isaiah the same sentence goes on: "To proclaim the acceptable year of the LORD, and the day of vengeance of our God" (Isaiah 61:2). Jesus stopped before "and the day of vengeance of our God". Luke 4:19-20: He closed the book, gave it back to the attendant, and sat down.
- "The eyes of all them that were in the synagogue were fastened on him." (Luke 4:20)
- Jesus: "This day is this scripture fulfilled in your ears." (Luke 4:21)
- They wondered at His gracious words, and said, "Is not this Joseph's son?" (Luke 4:22) - the boy they had watched grow up.
- "The acceptable year of the Lord" = a time of God's favour and welcome. Plain English for it is fine.

## Honesty about WHY He stopped (important)
The text does not say why. You may state what He read, where He stopped, what the next words were, and what He said ("This day"). You may point out, as a reading and not a fact, that He announced the time of favour and left the day of judgement unread. Never claim judgement is cancelled. Never invent His thoughts or the crowd's feelings beyond the text.

## Formula (fixed skeleton)
- Hook said in the first 2.5 seconds; a stranger must want the answer. Hook shape: one small detail in the text. ("Jesus stopped reading in the middle of a sentence. Why?" is one option; write your own if better.)
- One plain set-up line: who and where.
- 3-4 voices: [narrator], [jesus], [crowd] (or [townsman]), and optionally [isaiah] for the old words. One line per voice. Mark a (pause) before Jesus speaks.
- Jesus speaks once, late, about 55-70% in: "This day is this scripture fulfilled in your ears."
- Landing: short, lands on Jesus, one plain invite, earned by your spine. Grace, no pressure, no fear, no gain/loss. Do NOT use the "Not X. Y." trade-up shape (used last film). Pick one of: repeat 2-4 of His own words then stop; end on one plain word from the quote; one honest quiet question with no answer given; or one plain sentence and let the picture finish.
- The last picture will be Jesus or the place; write the last line so a 3-second silent hold can follow.

## Hard rules
1. KJV quotes verbatim and short. Never let an old word ("acceptable", "anointed") carry the meaning alone: say it plainly around it.
2. No invented numbers, feelings, distances or details beyond the pack ("about 700 years" is allowed). No overclaim.
3. Never blame "the Jews" or Nazareth as a people. (Luke 4:28-29, the anger, is outside this short; leave it out.)
4. No stock closer ("come to Jesus", "just ask Him", "He's waiting", "will you...?"). No AI or film-making wording. No hashtags.
5. Global English: clever in structure, never in vocabulary.

## Output (only this)
(a) the script, one voice per line; (b) spoken word count; (c) KJV fragments with verse refs; (d) 4 lines: who it is for, main take-away, the curiosity gap, why a global newcomer gets it.
