1. **NEWCOMER:** A newcomer can easily grasp the core story: Jesus claimed a prophecy, angered His town by favoring foreigners, escaped a cliff, but later chose to die on a cross to keep God's welcome open. However, ESL viewers will struggle with "[jesus] This day is this scripture fulfilled in your ears." "Fulfilled in your ears" is an archaic idiom that won't translate well or make sense to beginners.

2. **UNDERSTANDING:** The claim is clear ("It was about Him"). The town's anger is perfectly explained ("help to foreigners, not to their own"). The link between "heal thyself" and "save himself" works thematically, contrasting His escape at the cliff with His submission at the cross. However, the setup for "heal thyself" is slightly confusing because Jesus is predicting their words, which might blur who is actually demanding the miracle.

3. **BIBLE READER:** Yes. Most churchgoers know Jesus read from Isaiah, but few realize He stopped *mid-sentence* to deliberately leave out "the day of vengeance," defining His first coming entirely by grace. The parallel between the Nazareth cliff and the cross is also a fresh, striking connection.

4. **CURIOSITY GAP:** 9/10. "Jesus stopped reading in the middle of a sentence" is an elite hook. It demands the viewer stick around to find out what He skipped and why. The payoff ("He stopped reading at the welcome") resolves it beautifully.

5. **ACCURATE:** Yes. The script perfectly aligns with Luke 4 and Isaiah 61. Jesus really does stop exactly before the vengeance clause in Isaiah. There are no doctrinal risks; it centers entirely on Christ, the cross, and grace.

6. **WEAKEST LINE:** "[jesus] This day is this scripture fulfilled in your ears." It is too archaic for a global, ESL audience to parse quickly.
**Fix:** Shorten it to the core KJV truth: "[jesus] This day is this scripture fulfilled." (This drops the confusing "in your ears" while staying KJV-accurate and saving a second of audio).

7. **OVERALL:** 9/10. A brilliant, tight script that uses a tiny textual detail to explain the entire gospel.
