**(a) Script**

[narrator] Jesus stopped reading in the middle of a sentence.
[narrator] In His home town, Nazareth, He stood to read Isaiah, written about seven hundred years before.
[narrator] He read: "The Spirit of the Lord is upon me ... to preach the gospel to the poor."
[narrator] "To preach the acceptable year of the Lord." That means a time of God's welcome.
[narrator] Then He closed the book. Isaiah's sentence goes on:
[isaiah] "And the day of vengeance of our God."
[narrator] He left that line unread, and sat down.
[narrator] Every eye was on Him.
(pause)
[jesus] "This day is this scripture fulfilled in your ears."
[townsman] "Is not this Joseph's son?"
[narrator] He stopped on God's welcome. And said: "This day."
*(3-second silent hold on Jesus, seated, the closed book beside Him.)*

**(b) Spoken word count:** 109 (about 45 seconds)

**(c) KJV fragments**
- "The Spirit of the Lord is upon me ... to preach the gospel to the poor": Luke 4:18
- "To preach the acceptable year of the Lord": Luke 4:19
- "And the day of vengeance of our God": Isaiah 61:2 (the unread words)
- He closed the book and sat down: Luke 4:20 (paraphrased)
- "Every eye was on Him": a plain-English paraphrase of Luke 4:20, not a quote
- "This day is this scripture fulfilled in your ears": Luke 4:21
- "Is not this Joseph's son?": Luke 4:22

**(d) Four lines**
- **Who it is for:** Newcomers anywhere in the world, and Bible readers who never noticed where He stopped.
- **Main take-away:** Jesus read Isaiah's promise of God's welcome, stopped before the words about judgement, and said it was coming true in Him that very day.
- **Curiosity gap:** Why stop in the middle of a sentence? We hear the missing words, and we never claim the reason. We only show where He stopped and what He said next.
- **Why a global newcomer gets it:** It is one man, one book and one pause. The old word "acceptable" is explained in plain words. A separate voice reads the missing line, so you can hear what He left out. The ending repeats two of His own words.
