# Seat: Fable (blind, independent)

**1. ENDING — 8/10.** It closes the film. The script opens a gap (He stopped mid-sentence), names where He stopped ("God's welcome"), and leaves "This day" hanging. The last line answers both: the welcome He stopped at has not ended. It is not tacked on; it is the point of the stop. One small cost: line 17 ends on "This day." and line 18 ends on "This day." eight words later. Back to back, the echo is a little heavy. The voice read must separate them (Jesus' word quoted, then the narrator's own), or it sounds like a repeat.

**2. NEWCOMER — yes, mostly.** "Welcome", "still", "open" are all plain. "That welcome" points back clearly because "God's welcome" was said twice. "This day" as a two-word fragment is slightly odd English for "today", but it was just heard twice, so it holds. The word that fails is not in the last line: **"vengeance"** (lines 12-13). A newcomer who does not know it misses what Jesus left out, and then the ending loses half its weight.

**3. HONESTY — fair.** "Still open" is application, but the KJV carries it: Jesus says "This day" (Luke 4:21), and 2 Corinthians 6:2 says "now is the accepted time... now is the day of salvation." Same idea, same "accepted" word. "Still" quietly implies it will not be open for ever. That is true to Isaiah 61:2 and is not pushed. No overclaim.

**4. LANDING — earned.** Built from the piece's own words (welcome, This day), not a stock call. Grace, no pressure, no gain/loss. Jesus is not named in the last line, but line 17 ("about Him") plus the silent last picture carry it. Acceptable.

**5. OVERALL — 8/10.** Clear, one thread, honest. Loses points for "vengeance" unexplained and "fulfilled in your ears" (rescued by line 17).

**Verdict: KEEP the new line.**
