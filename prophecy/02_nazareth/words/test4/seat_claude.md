**1. ENDING: 7/10.** It closes the film. It picks up "welcome" and "This day", so it pays off the open question: why stop there? But "This day" is now heard three times in the last 15 seconds, and the last one is a bare fragment. That makes it feel a little like a slogan added on. It is close to earned, not fully there.

**2. NEWCOMER:** Mostly clear. Three weak spots:
- **"still open"**: open to whom? It never says "you", so a newcomer may not realise the line is about them.
- **"This day."**: the fragment is fine in English, but dubbed it may come out stiff or odd. In most languages "today" is the natural word.
- **"welcome"**: some dubbing will turn it into a greeting ("Welcome!"). The earlier line "a time of God's welcome" carries the meaning, so this risk is small.

**3. HONESTY:** It is fair. "Acceptable year" as "God's welcome" is a sound paraphrase. 2 Cor 6:2 has Paul telling his readers "now is the accepted time", so applying it to today has apostolic precedent. One honest note: 2 Cor 6:2 quotes Isaiah 49:8, not 61:2. It is the same idea, but don't cite it as the same verse. "Stopped in the middle of a sentence" is an inference from comparing Luke 4:19 with Isaiah 61:2. It is well established and the script shows the evidence on screen, so that's fine. The script never says vengeance is cancelled, only left unread. That's good.

**4. LANDING:** Grace, no pressure. But the last line lands on the welcome, not on Jesus. "That welcome" is never tied to Him by name in the final breath. The line is earned in content because it uses the film's own words, but the shape is close to the stock "the door is still open" closer.

**5. Overall words: 8/10.** The hook is strong. The unread line spoken by a second voice is the best device in the script. "Local boy" twice is a bit much.

**Verdict: CHANGE it to:**
> "The welcome He read is still open — to you. This day."

This ties the welcome to Jesus and names who it is for. It keeps the echo and stays grace-only. If the dub teams find "This day" stiff, let them translate it as "today".
