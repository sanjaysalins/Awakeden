## 1. WHO IS THIS FOR
**Named viewer:** Grace, 40, a churchgoer in Nairobi. She knows Luke 4 roughly but has never set it beside Isaiah 61.
- **Who stops:** anyone who hears line 1 ("stopped reading in the middle of a sentence"). It is a strong hook.
- **Who finishes:** believers and church-curious viewers.
- **Who shares:** small-group leaders and preachers. The cliff-versus-cross contrast in line 12 makes a ready-made sermon hook.
- **Who is not served:** total newcomers. They get lost at lines 7–9, where the reason for the anger is never explained.

## 2. MAIN TAKEAWAY
"Jesus stopped before the judgment line, so God's welcome is still open to me today." Line 13 carries it and line 5 sets it up. It is clear. But the film never says the judgment is *delayed*, not *cancelled* (see 4).

## 3. GLOBAL AUDIENCE
**In my own words:** Jesus reads Isaiah in His home town. He stops just before "vengeance" and says the promise is coming true now. The town turns on Him and tries to throw Him off a cliff. He walks away. Later, on the cross, He does not escape. The welcome is still open.

**Where viewers get lost:**
- **Line 8, "Physician, heal thyself."** Archaic, and no gloss on screen. A second-language viewer won't know what it means or why it is an insult.
- **The skipped turn.** The film cuts Luke 4:22 ("wondered at the gracious words… Is not this Joseph's son?"). So the anger looks unprovoked.
- **Line 6, "fulfilled in your ears."** Odd in translation, though the picture helps.
- **Hard words:** "vengeance" (lines 4–5) is a hard word for second-language viewers, but the whole film turns on it. Keep it, but make sure the read-along lettering is strong.
- **Last picture:** golden sunset above Nazareth, *after* the cross. It may read as "before the cross" and confuse the timeline.

## 4. BIBLICALLY ACCURATE
- **Line 1, "stopped reading in the middle of a sentence": RIGHT (inference).** Luke 4:19 ends at "acceptable year of the Lord", then 4:20 "he closed the book". Isaiah 61:2 continues with a comma. One caveat: Luke never literally says "stopped". It comes from comparing the two texts. Fair.
- **Line 2, "Nazareth, His home town": RIGHT.** Luke 4:16, "where he had been brought up".
- **Line 2, "good news for the poor, freedom for prisoners": RIGHT.** Luke 4:18.
- **Line 2, "a time of God's welcome": RIGHT, but loose.** KJV says "acceptable year" (4:19). "Welcome" is a fair gloss. "Year" would be closer.
- **Lines 3–4: RIGHT.** Quoted exactly from Isaiah 61:2. "Goes on" is true, though Isaiah goes further still ("to comfort all that mourn").
- **Line 5, "left… unread, and sat down": RIGHT.** Luke 4:20. Leaving out the handing back to the minister is fine.
- **Line 6: RIGHT.** Exact text of Luke 4:21.
- **Lines 7–8: RIGHT.** Luke 4:23. Cut short, but faithful.
- **Line 9, "God once sent help to foreigners, not to them": OVERCLAIMED.** In 4:25–27 the people passed over were *Israel's widows and lepers in Elijah's and Elisha's day*. "Them" sounds like the Nazareth crowd. Also "once" when Luke gives two cases.
- **Line 9, "Furious… to a cliff to throw Him down": RIGHT.** Luke 4:28–29, "filled with wrath… cast him down headlong".
- **Line 9, "walked right through them": RIGHT.** Luke 4:30. The "parted, stunned crowd" in the picture is UNSUPPORTED, but harmless.
- **Lines 10–11: RIGHT.** Luke 23:35, the rulers "derided him". Cutting "if he be Christ" is acceptable.
- **Line 12, "gave His life": RIGHT.** Luke 23:46, "gave up the ghost".
- **Line 12, "This time, He stayed": RIGHT as interpretation.** It is the film's own contrast, but a fair one.
- **Line 13, "still open… today": sound application,** drawn from "This day" in 4:21. But nothing in the film says the vengeance still comes later (Luke 21:22 calls it "the days of vengeance"). A careful reader could hear it as "Jesus deleted judgment". This is the one real doctrinal gap.

**Would I send it to my church group?** Yes, after the line 5 fix.

## 5. BEST VERSION POSSIBLE
The best explainer would make the pause *mean* something: the judgment is postponed, so the welcome is urgent. It would also give the crowd's real trigger in plain words.

**Highest-value change:** add "for now" to line 5.

**Score: 7/10.**

## CURIOSITY GAP
- **Opens:** line 1.
- **Pays off:** lines 4–5 (around 20 s), and again at line 13.
- **Score:** 7/10.
- **Worth the wait?** Yes. The middle (lines 6–11) wanders, but line 12 ties it back.

## NEW INSIGHT
- **The stop mid-sentence:** most lifelong readers have heard this from a pulpit. Not new to them.
- **The cliff then the cross:** "He walked through them… this time, He stayed" is fresher, and it holds up against Luke 4:30 and 23:35–46.
- **Score:** 6/10.

## THREE SMALLEST FIXES
1. **Line 5:** "He left the day of vengeance unread, for now, and sat down."
2. **Line 9:** "He reminded them God once sent help to outsiders, not to Israel's own."
3. **Line 2:** "…freedom for prisoners, and the year of God's welcome."
