## 1. WHO IS THIS FOR
A phone viewer who likes a clear story with a twist, like a 19-year-old who scrolls Shorts in a second language. They stop at line 1 ("stopped reading in the middle of a sentence"), because it is odd. They finish if they are curious about why. They share only if they feel the ending, which is weak for me. Not served: anyone who does not know who "Jesus" is. The film never says. I guessed He is an important teacher.

## 2. MAIN TAKEAWAY
"A man called Jesus read half a promise, left out the part about vengeance, and later died instead of saving Himself, and the welcome is open to me." Line 13 carries it ("It is still open, for you, today"). Not fully clear. I understood "welcome" but not why skipping vengeance matters, or what "open" means.

## 3. GLOBAL AUDIENCE
Who: Jesus (a man who reads in a hall), Isaiah (old writer), a crowd. What: He reads a promise, stops, they get angry, He is killed on a cross. Why: I guess He chose welcome over punishment. I am not sure.

Where I got lost:
- Line 1: "stopped reading in the middle of a sentence". Which sentence? I only learn at line 3-5.
- Line 2: "God's promise of someone sent". Who is sent? Jesus Himself? Not said.
- Line 4: "day of vengeance of our God". "Vengeance" is hard English. Who is "our God"?
- Line 6: "This day is this scripture fulfilled in your ears." Very strange English. "Scripture" and "fulfilled" mean nothing to me. I did not know He is saying "I am that someone".
- Line 8: "Physician, heal thyself." Old English, "thyself". I do not know what it means or why it is an insult.
- Line 9: "God once sent help to foreigners, not to them." Too fast. Who is the woman with the jar? Elijah is never named on screen as far as I can tell. Then "He walked right through them" arrives with no gap.
- Line 11-12: "He saved others; let him save himself." Why are they mocking? What does "saved" mean here? "This time, He stayed, and gave His life." The word "This time" links to the cliff, but I only half caught it.
- Line 13: "the welcome". Which welcome? Where does the film say what "it" is?

Words that fail in translation: vengeance, scripture, fulfilled, physician, thyself, welcome (as a noun), "in your ears", "home town".

## 4. BIBLICALLY ACCURATE
I cannot judge accuracy. Things I was TOLD that I could not follow: that stopping mid-sentence matters (line 1, 5); that "day of vengeance" belongs to the same sentence (line 3-4); that the cross scene is "later" and the same people type (line 10); why the crowd turned furious (line 9); who Elijah is; why "foreigners" is an insult to them.

## 5. BEST VERSION POSSIBLE
A top explainer would say plainly, early, who Jesus is and what the half-sentence was, then show the cut visibly on screen (the two halves of the line, one crossed out). The ONE highest-value change: say in one plain sentence what Jesus claimed in line 6 ("I am the one this promise is about"). Without it the middle is a puzzle. Score: 5/10.

## CURIOSITY GAP
Opens at line 1, pays off at line 5 (He left the vengeance part out). Pays off the second layer at line 12-13, but only partly. Score 6/10. Worth the wait for the image, less for the meaning.

## NEW INSIGHT
I did not know anything to compare with. The idea that stopping in the middle of a sentence is a choice was surprising to me. Whether it is true to the KJV I cannot say. 6/10 as a surprise.

## THREE SMALLEST FIXES
1. Line 6: replace with "Today this promise comes true. I am the one it talks about." (keep the KJV quote on screen only).
2. Line 8: replace with "You will say: Doctor, fix yourself first."
3. Line 13: replace "the welcome" with "He stopped at the part that says God welcomes people. That welcome is still open for you today."
