## Beat 1 — I was there

I have heard this account told many times, and I will set it down here as it was first given to me. There was a man born blind who sat at the gate of the temple every morning of his life. He stretched out his hand, and the crowds passed him by, some with pity and some with scorn, while the long years wore on without any change at all for the patient beggar at the roadside.

## Beat 2 — The act

One day a stranger stopped before him, made clay, and spread it upon his eyes, and sent him down to the pool to wash himself there. He washed, and the darkness broke apart, and for the first time the beggar beheld the morning light, the passing faces, and his own two trembling hands held up against the bright and astonishing sky.

**[the Pharisees]** They summoned the healed beggar and demanded to know who had dared to do such a work upon the sabbath day.

## Beat 3 — The reveal

When the rulers pressed him hard, the man answered them with the one plain thing he could not be argued out of:

**"one thing I know, that, whereas I was blind, now I see."**

And the meaning ran deeper than the miracle, for the stranger who had healed those eyes was Christ, the true Light of the world, come for every soul still sitting in the dark.

## Beat 4 — The invitation

If you have lived in such a darkness, then hear this account of what was done. He is still the One who opens blind eyes upon the earth. Come to Jesus, and trust Him, and receive the very light that the beggar at the gate received.
