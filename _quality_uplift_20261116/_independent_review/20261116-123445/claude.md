# Independent review — claude (OK, 113s)

**Scope note:** The lens asks for a fact sheet. This file is a list of 10 "fresh insight" lines instead. I checked each one against the KJV as quoted, using both the lens and the file's own four questions. I checked from memory of the KJV, without opening any files.

---

### 1. Joanna: Herod's household vs the empty tomb
- **Text:** Luke 8:3, 23:8-9 and 24:10 are quoted correctly.
- **Problem:** The claim says "Herod **demanded** a sign." The verse says he "**hoped** to have seen some miracle." Luke 23:9 says he "questioned with him in many words." That is not a demand for a sign.
- **Other limits:**
  - Joanna was one of several women, not the only one.
  - She "got" the empty tomb and the angels' message (24:4-9), not a miracle done by Jesus.
  - "Household" is fair (she was his steward's wife). It assumes the Herod of 8:3 is the Antipas of 23:7, which is safe.
- **Sound:** A fair contrast inside Luke, not over-reach.
- **Fresh:** About 5-10% of churchgoers know it.
- **Verdict: USE WITH CARE.** Say "hoped to see a miracle… got silence." Never say "demanded."

### 2. Jonah came from Galilee
- **Text:** 2 Kings 14:25, John 7:52 and Matt 12:41 are correct.
- **Citation error:** Joshua 19:13 in the KJV spells it "**Gittahhepher**," not Gath-hepher. Reading them as the same place is standard, but you cannot quote Joshua as saying "Gath-hepher."
- **Over-reach:**
  - Zebulun is "Galilee" only in later geography. Isaiah 9:1 ("Zebulun… Galilee of the nations") is the bridge; cite it.
  - John 7:52 (Jerusalem, Feast of Tabernacles) and Matt 12:38-41 are different scenes. Jesus never answered John 7:52 with Jonah. The claim's "and Jesus pointed to Jonah" must not suggest he did.
- **Sound:** A fair fact. The two-scene link only holds as the narrator's own juxtaposition, not as something Jesus answered.
- **Fresh:** About 10-20%.
- **Verdict: USE WITH CARE.**

### 3. "Remember me" and Joseph's prisoners
- **Text:** All verses are correct, apart from the dropped "Verily I say unto thee" (fine with an ellipsis).
- **Roles are inverted:**
  - In Gen 40 the **innocent** man (Joseph) asks to be remembered. The forgetter is the freed butler.
  - In Luke 23 the **guilty** man asks, and the innocent one remembers.
  - "Two condemned men beside an innocent man" is not in Gen 40. They are "in ward" for offending (40:1-3), not condemned.
  - "One restored, one hanged" has no match in Luke 23. Both thieves die on crosses.
- **Sound:** Only a loose echo. It holds as "two men were asked to remember. One forgot. One did not."
- **Fresh:** About 10%.
- **Verdict: USE WITH CARE.** Frame it as a contrast only. No "two condemned men," and no echo of who lived and who died.

### 4. First and last words in Luke are "Father"
- **FACTUAL ERROR:** Luke 2:49 begins "**How is it that ye sought me?**" "Father's" comes at the end, so "both begin with the Father" is false.
- **"Last words" is also off.** Luke 23:46 is his last word on the cross. His last recorded words in Luke are 24:44-49, and they still say "the promise of **my Father**" (24:49). That makes a stronger, true version.
- **Fresh:** About 25-35%. This one is fairly well known.
- **Verdict: USE WITH CARE.** Say "his first words in Luke are about his Father. His last words from the cross are to his Father."

### 5. "My God" becomes "your God"
- **Text:** Ps 22:22, Heb 2:12 and John 20:17 are correct.
- **Doctrinal care:** Jesus says "my God, **and** your God," not "our God." The Church has long read that distinction as the Son by nature vs sons by adoption. The title "becomes your God" must not blur it.
- **Cross-gospel join:** The "My God, my God" cry is in Matthew and Mark, not John. That is acceptable.
- **Stronger support:** "Brethren" here is new in John. It comes after "friends" in John 15:15. Matt 28:10 ("go tell my brethren") backs it up.
- **Sound:** A real link, sealed by Heb 2:12.
- **Fresh:** About 5-10%.
- **Verdict: USE.** Keep "my God and your God" verbatim.

### 6. Lazarus still wore his napkin
- **Text:** John 11:44 and 20:7 are correct.
- **INVENTED DETAIL:** The KJV says "**wrapped together** in a place by itself," not "**folded**." The folded napkin ("I'm coming back") is a modern internet myth. It must not appear, even as a hint.
- **Missing contrast:** Lazarus was raised to die again. Jesus is raised never to die (Rom 6:9).
- **Fresh:** About 25-35%.
- **Verdict: USE WITH CARE.** Use the KJV wording only. Never say "folded."

### 7. Three "finished" works, and God moves in
- **Text:** Gen 2:1, Exod 40:33-34 and Matt 27:51 are correct.
- **OVER-REACH:**
  - Gen 2:1-3 ends with God **resting**. No presence fills anything there. "Each time… God's presence opens" fails on the first example. It only works through the scholarly "Eden as temple" reading, which is speculative.
  - "Veil torn = presence opens to his people" is Hebrews 10:19-20's reading, not Matthew's. Cite Heb 10:20.
  - The torn veil (Matthew) and "It is finished" (John) come from different gospels.
- **Better 3rd example:** 1 Kings 7:51 ("So was ended all the work") then 8:10-11 (the cloud filled the house). That is a true second pattern match.
- **Verdict: USE WITH CARE / near REJECT as written.** Drop Gen 2 or swap in 1 Kings 8. Cite Heb 10:20.

### 8. Jesus told the Samaritan leper's story in advance
- **Text:** Luke 4:27, 17:18 and 2 Kings 5:15 are correct.
- **OVER-REACH:**
  - "Told the story **in advance**" makes Luke 4:27 a prophecy. It is a historical example.
  - "The one leper healed was a foreigner" is true only of Elisha's time.
  - Naaman is a Syrian, the man in Luke 17 a Samaritan. Both are "strangers," but they are not the same.
- **Missing strength:** Both men were cleansed after obeying and going away (2 Kings 5:14 and Luke 17:14 "as they went"). Then both turned back to give thanks.
- **Fresh:** About 10%.
- **Verdict: USE WITH CARE.** Call it an echo. Never say "in advance" or "foretold."

### 9. Peter copied Jesus but had to pray
- **Text:** Mark 5:37, 5:41 and Acts 9:40 are correct.
- **Over-reach:** "Almost word for word" does not fit the KJV, which reads "Talitha cumi" in Mark and "Tabitha, arise" in Acts. The one-letter Aramaic similarity is a guess, not text.
- **Missing parallels (they strengthen it):**
  - Both cleared the room (Mark 5:40 "put them all out" and Acts 9:40 "put them all forth").
  - Both took her by the hand (Acts 9:41).
- **Tone risk:** "Copied" sounds like copying a technique. "Jesus just spoke" must not suggest Jesus never prayed before miracles (John 11:41-42).
- **Fresh:** About 15-20%.
- **Verdict: USE WITH CARE.** Say "Peter did what he had seen, but knelt and prayed first. The power was not his."

### 10. Luke echoes Elijah
- **Text:** 1 Kings 17:21, 17:23 and Luke 7:14-15 are correct. The Greek Old Testament wording of 1 Kings 17:23 matches Luke 7:15 almost exactly, so this is a real verbal link.
- **Over-reach:** "Pleaded three times" merges two things. He **stretched himself** on the child three times and **cried** to the LORD (17:20-21). Keep that split.
- **Tone:** The contrast must not belittle Elijah. The point is authority ("I say unto thee").
- **Missing details that guard it:**
  - Both are widows.
  - Both meet at "the gate of the city" (1 Kings 17:10 and Luke 7:12).
  - The crowd itself says "a great prophet is risen up" (Luke 7:16).
  - Only Luke says "only son." Do not import that into 1 Kings.
- **Fresh:** About 15-25%.
- **Verdict: USE.**

---

### Overall
- **Errors stated as fact:** #4 ("begins with"), #6 ("folded"), and #2 (the Joshua spelling).
- **Over-reaches:** #7 ("each time," Gen 2), #8 ("in advance"), #1 ("demanded"), and #9 ("word for word").
- **No orthodoxy problems,** provided #5 keeps the "my/your" distinction.

**Top 3 to use:** #5 (My God / your God), #10 (Elijah, delivered him to his mother), #8 (Naaman and the Samaritan, reworded as an echo).

Most are usable, but several lines are false or invented as written. They need the wording fixes above before they lock.

VERDICT: REVISE

1. Fix the false or invented wording. #4: Luke 2:49 does not *begin* with "Father," so say "about his Father… to his Father." #6: the KJV says "wrapped together," never "folded" (avoid the folded-napkin myth).
2. Cut the over-reach in #7 and #8. #7: Gen 2:1 shows God's rest, not his presence filling anything, so swap in 1 Kings 7:51 to 8:10-11 and cite Heb 10:20 for the veil. #8: Luke 4:27 is an example, not a story told "in advance."
3. Match the wording to the verses. #1: "hoped," not "demanded." #2: Joshua 19:13 reads "Gittahhepher," cite Isa 9:1 for "Galilee," and do not suggest Jesus answered John 7:52. #9: drop "word for word." #10: split "stretched three times" from "cried."
