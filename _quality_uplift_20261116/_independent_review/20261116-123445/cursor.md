## Per insight — concrete problems

### 1. Joanna / Herod (Luke 24)

| Issue | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Over-reach** | “**A woman from his own household**” — Luke 8:3 ties Joanna to **Chuza, Herod’s steward**; it does **not** say she still belongs to Herod’s house at Easter, or that her report is “from inside Herod’s world” in the narrative sense of Luke 24. |
| **Citation softening** | Luke 23:8–9: Herod was **glad** and **hoped to see a miracle**; Jesus **answered nothing**. That is not the same as **“demanded a sign”** (Matt 12:38 / 16:1 style). |
| **What holds** | Luke 24:10 **does** name Joanna among those who told the apostles — paired with Luke 23:9 silence is a **sermon irony**, not a verse-stated contrast. |

### 2. Jonah / Galilee (Matt 12)

| Issue | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Argument stitch** | John 7:52 (“**out of Galilee ariseth no prophet**”) is **one party’s line in a不同的 controversy** (Jesus’ Galilean ministry / origin). Matt 12:41–40 is **sign of Jonah / repentance**, not “Jonah was Galilean, so you’re wrong.” The insight **fuses two passages** that the text does not link. |
| **Geography** | 2 Kgs 14:25 + Josh 19:13 support **Gath-hepher in Zebulun** (northern / “Galilee” in a broad sense) — but **John 7:52 is not refuted inside John** by naming Jonah; you’re bringing 2 Kings into John’s debate. Fair apologetic, **not TEXT-closed**. |
| **Christ-lens** | Matt 12’s punch is **burial / resurrection sign** (12:40), not regional one-upmanship — risk of **sideways hook** vs the Gospel’s own emphasis. |

### 3. “Remember me” / Joseph (Luke 23)

| Issue | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Labeled echo — good** | “**Echo (not a stated prophecy)**” is honest. |
| **Over-structure** | Gen 40: **two Pharaoh servants** with Joseph is not **two criminals beside one innocent** in the legal sense of the passion; parallel is **thematic**, not scene-specified. |
| **What holds** | 40:14 / 40:23 vs Luke 23:42–43 — **remembered / forgot** vs **Jesus’ word** — sound **if kept as echo**, not “the cross fulfills Genesis 40.” |

### 4. First and last “Father” in Luke — **TEXT FAIL**

| Issue | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **False claim** | “**Both begin with the Father**” — Luke **23:46** opens **“Father, into thy hands…”** ✓. Luke **2:49** opens **“How is it that ye sought me? wist ye not…”** — **Father** appears mid-clause (**“my Father’s business”**), not as the first word. |
| **Also unstated** | Luke does not label 2:49 “**first recorded words**” or 23:46 “**last**”; that’s **narrative ordering**, defensible, but the **“both begin with Father”** line is **wrong on the KJV quoted**. |

### 5. Ps 22 / “my God” → “your God” (John 20)

| Issue | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Indirect support** | Artifact cites **Ps 22:22** + **Heb 2:12** + **John 20:17** — not **Ps 22:1** (“**My God, my God**”) in the block; the arc **depends on** 22:1 mentally. |
| **John 20:17** | **“my God, and your God”** and **“my brethren”** (via “**Go to my brethren**”) — **cited text supports** the movement from forsaken cry to communal declaration **as canonical Christology**, not as Ps 22 quoted on the lips of Jesus in John. |
| **Stretch watch** | Implying Ps 22 **“turns to”** brethren **and** that resurrection speech **is that turn** — orthodox, but **typological**, not a single-verse proof. |

### 6. Napkin — Lazarus vs Jesus (John 20)

| Issue | Detail |

| **What holds** | John 11:44 — still bound, others loose him; John 20 — grave clothes / napkin **left behind** — **contrast is in the text** if you drop “folded.” |

### 7. Three “finished” works — **weak TEXT, missing anchor verse**

| Issue | Detail |
|--------|--------|

### 8. Samaritan leper “in advance” (Luke 17)

| **Luke 17:18** | “**this stranger**” / Samaritan — **foreigner** parallel to Naaman — **sound as Luke-wide irony**, not advance storytelling. |
