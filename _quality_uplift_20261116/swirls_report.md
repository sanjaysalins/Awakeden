## 0. Three things true for the whole group (read this first)

**0.1 Every storyboard-page episode breaks the "no freeze / no boomerang" rule.**
The unit lists say so in plain text. Ep 02, 07, 10, 11, 13 = every page is `"freeze"` (often plus `tail_loop_seconds`). Ep 08 = `"boomerang"` on five pages. Jacob's Ladder = freeze + boomerang.
Why it happens: clips are 4-10 s long, slots are 8-16 s long. Example ep 02: clips are 4.0 s, 4.0 s, 4.0 s, 7.0 s. The slots are 10 s, 14 s, 10 s, 10 s. So more than half of that film is a held last frame.
This is the single biggest quality gap in the group. The fix is the owner's own rule: **more shots, each one longer than its slot, hard cuts, trim only.**

**0.2 Three hero moments are Ken Burns stand-ins, not real clips.**
- Ep 07 F03 "touched the bier" (file name says `SUPERSEDED_generative_replaced_with_kenburns`).
- Ep 07 back cover (same).
- Ep 10 F03 "kissed his feet" (`SUPERSEDED_generative_nsfw_reading_replaced_with_kenburns`).
The owner's rule says hero images are real animated clips. These need a new still angle that animates safely, not a prompt retry.

**0.3 Every film ends on a 10-16 s back cover while the landing is still being spoken.**
Ep 07: 16 s. Ep 10: 14 s. Ep 13: 14 s. Ep 02, 08: 12 s. The most important words in the film play over the least alive picture. Each one needs 2 landing shots under the words, then a short (3-4 s) back cover for the hold.

**Safe tools for this style** (pages have baked hand lettering, ink motif must stay still):
- New paid clips = the main lever.
- Remotion on top: page-turn / paper slide between pages, a hand-drawn ink underline or circle on a baked caption word as it is spoken (same idea as `annotators-circle`), pencil margin sketches (`margin-study`), a field note with a leader line (`marginalia`), `ink-transition` between pages.
- `focal-tour` (a soft light that visits the 3 small top panels in the order the narrator names them). The top panels are tiny on a phone. Today nothing tells the eye when to look up.
- Do NOT use on Swirls: `line-boil` over the whole page (wobbles the motif and the baked text), `parallax-panel` on pages with the motif, any burned captions.

### Task B - ep 02
**Verdict:** the red heifer itself is new to most people (~20% of churchgoers could tell you what it is). The turn "He touched the unclean and was not made unclean" is known (~50%). So: fresh subject, familiar landing.

**Fresh 1 - no third day, no seventh day.** (small narration change - the film already quotes half the verse)
- Numbers 19:12: "He shall purify himself with it on the third day, and on the seventh day he shall be clean: but if he purify not himself the third day, then the seventh day he shall not be clean."
- 1 Corinthians 15:4: "And that he was buried, and that he rose again the third day according to the scriptures:"
- The point: the law's own calendar says the cleansing that counts on day seven depends on what happens on day three. Miss the third day and there is no clean at the end.
- Why missed: people stop reading at "unclean seven days". The film itself cuts the verse off before the "but if".
- Familiarity: ~5%.
- Risk: MEDIUM. The New Testament does not make this link itself. Say "an echo", never "a prophecy". Hebrews 9:13-14 does tie the heifer's ashes to Christ, so the frame is sound; the third-day detail is our reading.

**Fresh 2 - the three things thrown into the fire are all at the cross.** (small change, one line + one picture)
- Numbers 19:6: "And the priest shall take cedar wood, and hyssop, and scarlet, and cast it into the midst of the burning of the heifer."
- John 19:29: "Now there was set a vessel full of vinegar: and they filled a spunge with vinegar, and put it upon hyssop, and put it to his mouth."
- Matthew 27:28: "And they stripped him, and put on him a scarlet robe."
- Wood, hyssop, scarlet - all three are in the Passion story. Familiarity: ~5%.
- Risk: MEDIUM. It is a pattern, not a stated fulfilment. The cross is "wood", not said to be cedar. Keep it as "look what shows up again".

**Fresh 3 - one death, kept, and used later for people who were not there.** (small change)
- Numbers 19:9: "...it shall be kept for the congregation of the children of Israel for a water of separation: it is a purification for sin."
- One heifer, burned once, outside the camp. Its ashes were stored and put on people long after. Lands on Hebrews 9:14, which the film already quotes. Familiarity ~10%. Risk: LOW.

### Task B - ep 07
**Verdict:** "Jesus touched the unclean and life flowed the other way" has been preached a lot in the last ten years. ~45% have heard it. Well told here, but not new.

**Fresh 1 - Luke copies a line from Elijah, then shows the gap.** (small change - the film already quotes the line)
- 1 Kings 17:23: "And Elijah took the child, and brought him down out of the chamber into the house, and delivered him unto his mother..."

- Lands on grace: this miracle starts in Him, not in her.
- Familiarity: ~20%. Risk: LOW.

**Fresh 3 - the only son whose funeral was stopped, and the only Son whose was not.** (new ending or new script)
- Luke 7:12: "the only son of his mother".
- John 19:25 (not re-checked today): "Now there stood by the cross of Jesus his mother..."

- Familiarity: ~10%. Risk: LOW-MEDIUM. Sound as a contrast. Keep it a contrast, do not build doctrine on it. No fear framing.

### Task B - ep 08
**Verdict:** "Jesus is Jacob's ladder" - ~35% have heard it. "He saw you under the fig tree" - ~60%. The film says the ladder, but it misses the reason Jesus brings up Jacob at all.

**Fresh 1 - "no guile" is a Jacob joke, and it sets up the ladder.** (small change, one line - best value in the group)
- Genesis 27:35: "And he said, Thy brother came with subtilty, and hath taken away thy blessing."

- Familiarity: ~5%. Risk: LOW. It is grammar, visible in the KJV itself. A gift for a KJV series.

**Fresh 3 - the fig tree and the Branch.** (new script or a part 2)

- On the day the Branch comes, a man calls his neighbour from under the fig tree. In John 1, Philip calls Nathanael from under the fig tree - to Jesus.
- Familiarity: ~3%. Risk: MEDIUM. John does not quote Zechariah. Some scholars see the echo, some do not. Say "listen to this", not "this fulfils".

**Fresh 4 - the man who sneered at Nazareth lived next door.** (small change)

- John 2:1: "And the third day there was a marriage in Cana of Galilee..."
- The "greater things" begin three days later, in Nathanael's own village. Familiarity ~5%. Risk: LOW.

### Task B - ep 10
**Verdict:** ~55% know this story and "forgiven much, loves much". The film's best line - "love was the receipt, not the price" - is a sharp way to say a known thing. Not new information.

**Fresh 1 - who is the creditor?** (small change, 1-2 lines)
- Luke 7:41-42: a creditor, two debtors, "he frankly forgave them both".
- Luke 7:49: "And they that sat at meat with him began to say within themselves, Who is this that forgiveth sins also?"
- In the story only the one who is owed can cancel the debt. Then Jesus cancels hers. So He is quietly saying her sins were owed to Him.
- The film quotes the parable and skips the guests' question. That question is the point of the scene and the film never asks it.
- Familiarity: ~10% have had this spelled out. Risk: LOW. This is plain, orthodox teaching on who Jesus is.

**Fresh 2 - the same sentence, four times, always to an outsider.** (new script, or a series bridge)
- Luke 7:50: "Thy faith hath saved thee; go in peace." (the sinful woman)
- Luke 8:48: "...thy faith hath made thee whole; go in peace." (the unclean woman)
- Luke 17:19: "Arise, go thy way: thy faith hath made thee whole." (the Samaritan leper - ep 11)
- Luke 18:42: "Receive thy sight: thy faith hath saved thee." (the blind beggar)
- In Luke's Greek these four are the same words. The KJV turns it "saved" twice and "made whole" twice, so English readers do not see it. A sinner, an unclean woman, a foreigner, a beggar.
- Familiarity: ~5%. Risk: LOW, but it rests on the Greek, so one honest line is needed ("the same words in Luke's own language"). Needs a Greek check by the panel.
- This ties ep 10, ep 11, ep 06 (the woman on the road) and Bartimaeus into one thread.

**Fresh 3 - the feet nobody washed.** (small change, new last line)
- Genesis 18:4: "Let a little water, I pray you, be fetched, and wash your feet, and rest yourselves under the tree:" (Abraham, when the LORD visits)
- Luke 7:44: "thou gavest me no water for my feet" (not re-checked today)
- John 13:5: "After that he poureth water into a bason, and began to wash the disciples' feet..."
- Abraham gave the LORD water for His feet. Simon did not. And the one whose feet went unwashed is the one who later kneels and washes twelve pairs.
- Familiarity: ~5%. Risk: LOW-MEDIUM. Genesis 18 as a visit of the LORD is the plain reading of that chapter. The link to Luke 7 is ours.

### Task B - ep 11
**Verdict:** ~75% know "only one said thank you". "They obeyed before they were healed" - ~40%. "Luke calls it thanks, Jesus calls it faith" is a good line, ~25%. Mostly familiar.

**Fresh 1 - Jesus already told this story, thirteen chapters earlier.** (small change, 2 lines - strongest insight in the group)
- Luke 4:27: "And many lepers were in Israel in the time of Eliseus the prophet; and none of them was cleansed, saving Naaman the Syrian."
- Luke 17:18: "There are not found that returned to give glory to God, save this stranger."
- 2 Kings 5:14-15: "...and he was clean. And he returned to the man of God, he and all his company, and came, and stood before him..."
- Naaman: a foreign leper, told to go and do a plain thing, cleansed on the way, and he came back. In Nazareth Jesus said only the foreigner got it - and they tried to kill Him for saying so. Now it happens in front of Him: ten cleansed, and the one who comes back is the foreigner.
- Luke 17:11 sets it "through the midst of Samaria and Galilee" - Elisha's own country.
- Why missed: Luke 4 and Luke 17 are never read together. "saving Naaman" / "save this stranger" is the same shape of sentence.
- Familiarity: ~8%. Risk: LOW. Scholars widely see Naaman behind Luke 17.
- Series bonus: Naaman is already Swirls ep 4. This makes ep 4 and ep 11 a pair.

**Fresh 2 - he gave glory to God at a man's feet, and the man did not stop him.** (small change, 1-2 lines)
- Luke 17:15-16: he "with a loud voice glorified God, and fell down on his face at his feet, giving him thanks".
- Luke 17:18: Jesus calls that "to give glory to God".
- Acts 10:26: "But Peter took him up, saying, Stand up; I myself also am a man."
- Peter stops a man who falls at his feet. Jesus does not. He calls it glory to God.
- Familiarity: ~10%. Risk: LOW. Orthodox: it shows who Jesus is.

**Fresh 3 - the priest who goes out of the camp to the leper.** (new script)
- Leviticus 14:3: "And the priest shall go forth out of the camp; and the priest shall look, and, behold, if the plague of leprosy be healed in the leper;"
- In the law the priest walks out to where the leper is. In Luke 17 Jesus is outside the village, where the lepers are. Nine go to find a priest. One goes back to the one who came out to them.
- Familiarity: ~5%. Risk: MEDIUM. Jesus as priest is Hebrews' own teaching, but Luke 17 does not say it. Do not say "the Samaritan had no priest" - Samaritans had their own. Keep to what Leviticus says.

### Task B - Jacob's Ladder
**Verdict:** "Jesus is the ladder" ~35-40%. "God came down, Jacob never climbed" ~30%. The new words are clean and kind. Not new information for a churchgoer.

**Fresh 1 - Jacob named three things that night. Jesus claims all three.** (small change, 2 lines)
- Genesis 28:12: "...a ladder set up on the earth, and the top of it reached to heaven..."
- Genesis 28:17: "...this is none other but the house of God, and this is the gate of heaven."
- John 1:51 - He is the ladder. John 2:21 (not re-checked today): "But he spake of the temple of his body" - He is the house. John 10:9 (not re-checked today): "I am the door" - He is the gate.
- Jacob woke up and named a ladder, a house and a gate. One Gospel gives all three names to one man.
- Familiarity: ~10% for all three together. Risk: LOW-MEDIUM. Each link is sound on its own; putting the three side by side is ours.

**Fresh 2 - the first thing ever anointed in the Bible is a stone called God's house.** (new ending; fits the new landing page above)
- Genesis 28:18: "And Jacob rose up early in the morning, and took the stone that he had put for his pillows, and set it up for a pillar, and poured oil upon the top of it."
- "Christ" means "anointed". The first anointing in Scripture is not a king or a priest. It is the stone a guilty man slept on, set upright, and named the house of God (28:22, not re-checked today).
- Familiarity: ~3%. Risk: MEDIUM-HIGH. Stone-as-Christ is biblical elsewhere (1 Peter 2), but nothing in the Bible ties THIS stone to Him. Old writers did; that is not proof. Use as a quiet picture, not a claim. Panel must rule on it.

**Fresh 3 - "ye shall see" is plural** - same as ep 08 Fresh 2. It belongs in whichever of the two films gets the John 1:51 page.

**Fresh 4 - the promise had no "if".** (small change)
- Genesis 28:15: "...for I will not leave thee, until I have done that which I have spoken to thee of."
- Read 28:13-15: God lays down no condition at all. Then read 28:20: "If God will be with me..." (not re-checked today). The first "if" in the story is Jacob's. God gave a promise; Jacob answers with a bargain; God keeps the promise anyway.
- Familiarity: ~10%. Risk: LOW. Plain from the text. It sharpens the film's own point ("Before Jacob did anything to make things right").
