3 · Who is the audience? (the question that decides the style)

Here is the honest finding: no channel-level audience has ever been written down. The charter demands "who is this for?" per script — and that discipline is excellent — but the series as a whole never named its person. Each art style quietly implied a different one. That is the real engine of the style churn.

A · The scrolling seeker (18–35)

Spiritually curious, biblically illiterate, watches muted, decides in 2 seconds. Needs: readable panels, one felt turn, a gentle open door — never a lecture.
Fit: comic strip best · ink good · oil weakest (reads as "church art", scrolls past)

B · The young believer who wants depth (20–40)

Churched, tired of shallow content, shares things that make faith look serious and fresh. Needs: the overlooked detail, the study page, the long film, the read link.
Fit: any style · comic gives them something distinctive to share

C · The family / teacher (30–55)

Wants Bible content for kids and teens. The Action Bible / Picture Bible buyer. Needs: safety, structure, series order.
Fit: comic strongly — but your conviction beats are written for adults (the ashamed, the exhausted performer). Serving C would change the words, not just the pictures.
Look at your own scripts: the doubter, the exhausted performer, the admirer who won't bow, the grieving, the ashamed. Your words already chose A, with B as the depth audience. And for A, the comic page is genuinely the strongest format — so your instinct and your words agree. What is missing is not more taste. It is one week of evidence.

4 · What you are not considering (the honest list)

4.1 The loop cannot exit from the inside

Five looks in three months, each declared "locked" or "chosen", four now dead — every verdict reached the same way: your eye, my analysis, panel reviews. All inside the building. The kitsch-test page was built to show real people and was never sent. Another $100 of pilots will not settle what 20 real viewers can settle in a week for $0.

4.2 The launch-bar trap

Your bar — "nothing breaks the visual promise" — is right for a channel with an audience. But with zero subscribers there is no promise to break yet. Right now the bar works in reverse: every style evolution re-flunks the whole catalogue, launch recedes, and the channel ages at zero. 13 GREEN publish packs have been sitting ready since July 8.

4.3 The muted feed

A large share of Shorts viewers scroll with sound off. Your narration-first pipeline assumes ears. The comic page with in-world text boxes is the only one of your five styles that fully works silent. Nobody ever weighed this — and it may be the strongest practical argument for the comic.

4.4 The sea of slop

Feeds are flooding with photoreal AI-Jesus content. It all looks the same and it ages badly. A hand-drawn comic language is instantly recognisable, harder to imitate, and signals craft and intent — which is exactly the trust a CTA-to-Jesus channel needs.

4.5 One asset, three courses

This is the quiet economic winner: a comic page is natively a video panel source, a long-form page, and a website read-strip. Your read pages already consume strips. Oil and ink clips feed only one course. Comic pages feed all three from one render.

4.6 The catalogue debt

~$800 of finished oil/ink inventory exists. If comic is the brand, decide its fate on purpose: ship it as an opening season (channels evolve; nobody minds), or hold it back. Do not let it be written off silently.

4.7 One style for everything — says who?

"Hand-inked across shorts, longs and web" was decided in the ink era. Re-confirm it under comic-strip. It is legitimate for courses to plate differently — comic shorts + inked longs — as long as the ink DNA, wordmark and red-letter bar unify them. An assumption, not a law.

4.8 The success metric was never chosen

Is success reach (views, subs) or depth (watch-through, shares, read-page visits, comments answered, prayers prayed)? Your charter implies depth; analytics dashboards default to reach. Decide the metric before you see the first numbers, or the numbers will decide it for you.

4.9 Sustainability at your real cadence

The comic short cost ~$16 — cheaper than the $23 shorts pipeline — but it needed five human catches to get right. At 8 shorts + 2 longs a month, the scarce resource is not dollars, it is your eyes. Production wiring (design §12) fixes this, but only once the style stops moving — which is one more reason to settle it with evidence now.
