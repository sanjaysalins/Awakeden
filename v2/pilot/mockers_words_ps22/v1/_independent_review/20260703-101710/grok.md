## Independent adversarial review

### Doctrinal soundness
Mostly sound and orthodox. The Psalm 22 → Matthew 27 fulfillment move is standard, faithful typology, not a contrarian reading. **“He could have come down… by staying on it”** is grounded in the text (mockers’ taunt vs. voluntary submission).

**Problems:**
- **“They were reciting prophecy”** overstates it. They were not reciting Psalm 22. They were **fulfilling** it. “Reciting” sounds like they spoke the psalm aloud. That is not what Matthew records.
- **“nearly line for line”** oversells the parallel. Psalm has **“trusted on the LORD… that he would deliver him”**; Matthew has **“trusted in God… let him deliver him now… I am the Son of God.”** Same insult *shape*, not line-for-line wording. A careful listener may feel the proof is padded.

---

### KJV verbatim
**FAIL on the Psalm quote.**

Artifact:
> “…they shake the head**,** saying, He trusted on the LORD…”

KJV Psalm 22:7:
> “they shake the head **saying**, He trusted on the LORD…”

There is an **extra comma after “head”** inside quotation marks. That breaks the verbatim rule.

Matthew 27:43 quote is fine for the words shown:
> “He trusted in God; let him deliver him now… for he said, I am the Son of God.”

Ellipsis is acceptable; included words match KJV.

**Unquoted but still sloppy:** **“Save yourself and come down… if you really are the Son of God”** blends taunts and modernizes KJV (**“save thyself”**, **“If thou be the Son of God”**). Not a quote violation, but it weakens the “words matter” spine right after two KJV hits.

---

### Clarity on first hearing
Hook and arc are followable for a stranger who knows “Jesus on the cross” at a basic level.

**Friction points:**
- **“reading from a script”** is metaphorical. A zero-Bible listener may hear it literally for a beat.
- **“and yours”** in **“He stayed under their scorn, and yours”** lands fast. A new listener may not yet know *how* “yours” fits. Feels like a logic jump, not a gentle invitation.
- **“He never did”** (come down) can sound, for half a second, like Jesus failed the test — before the next line rescues it.

---

### Grace-anchored conviction
**PASS with a small watch.** No fear, works, or gain/loss manipulation. **“bearing the scorn He could have silenced, for the very people throwing it”** is grace-anchored.

**“to win you”** is mild victory language, but acceptable here — not a pressure CTA.

---

### Freshness = faithful depth
**PASS.** The “unwilling witnesses / script they didn’t know” angle is a real textual insight, not clickbait. Fresh about the **text**; landing stays orthodox.

---

### One thread spine
**PASS.** One thread holds: unknown script → Psalm words → cross echo → “come down” taunt → He stayed → come to the One who would not come down. No thread swap.

---

### Hook score: **7/10**
**“The crowd mocking Jesus at the cross was reading from a script — they just didn't know it.”**

Not a template open. Clever and specific. But for a cold scroller it needs a half-beat to decode “script,” and it assumes cross context up front. Strong — not stop-scroll strong. **Under 8 → REVISE trigger.**

---

### Landing score: **8/10**
**“Come to the One who would not come down.”**

Built from this piece’s own verb/image (“come down” threaded all the way). Not a stock **“Come to Jesus”** closer. Could not cleanly end a random other short. Lands on the inversion of the mockers’ line. Passes the bar.

### Additional findings
- **[david]** voice reads only a third-person slice of Psalm 22, with no “me/I.” Audio may feel tagged wrong unless the listener already knows David wrote the psalm.
- Proof beat is thin: two strong KJV quotes, then narrator paraphrase for the “come down” taunt instead of a third KJV hit (e.g. Matthew 27:40/42).

VERDICT: REVISE

1. Fix KJV Psalm quote: remove the comma in **“they shake the head, saying”** → **“they shake the head saying”**.
2. Sharpen the hook for a cold scroller (clearer stakes or sharper surprise in the first ~5 seconds); current hook scores **7/10**.
3. Replace **“nearly line for line”** and **“reciting prophecy”** with accurate language (**echoed / fulfilled / unwilling witnesses**), and quote or tightly attribute the **“come down”** taunt in KJV to protect the proof beat.
