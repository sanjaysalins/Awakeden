## Doctrinal soundness

Mostly sound and orthodox. The Psalm 22 → Matthew 27 mockery parallel is standard, defensible exegesis, not a contrarian stunt.

**Problems:**

1. **Overclaim vs. the text.** After `"nearly line for line"`, both KJV blocks are trimmed in ways that hide the *best* parallel — Psalm 22:8’s `"seeing he delighted in him"` and Matthew 27:43’s `"if he will have him"`. That is not “nearly line for line”; it is selectively edited to look cleaner than it is. A hostile listener can clip this.

2. **"He could have come down"** is implied (temptation / divine power), not stated in Matthew 27. Acceptable inference, but it is **asserted as fact** in the same breath as gospel quotation. Zero-Bible listeners may hear it as something Matthew records, not as theological inference.

3. **"They told the King to come down"** — `"King"` never appears in the quoted KJV or prior narrator lines. Messiah/kingship is assumed, not earned in-script. Small leap; a cold listener may stumble.
