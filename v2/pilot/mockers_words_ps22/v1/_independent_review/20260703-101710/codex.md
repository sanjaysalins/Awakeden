# Independent review — codex (OK, 24s)

Hook Score: 8/10. Strong concrete image: “reading from a script” is clear and tied to the text.

Landing Score: 7/10. “Come to the One who would not come down” is built from the piece’s own image, but “Come to the One…” is still close to a stock closer. Under 8, so revise.

Findings:

- KJV quotation issue: Matthew 27:43 is selectively quoted as `"He trusted in God; let him deliver him now... for he said, I am the Son of God."` The KJV reads, “He trusted in God; let him deliver him now, if he will have him: for he said, I am the Son of God.” The omitted phrase “if he will have him” is significant because it sharpens the mockery around God’s approval.
- KJV quotation issue: Psalm 22:7-8 is quoted as `"...they shake the head, saying..."` but the leading ellipsis inside quotation marks is not KJV text. If the rule is strict, keep ellipses outside the quote or use bracketed excerpt formatting.
- Doctrinal caution: “He stayed under their scorn, and yours, to win you” risks implying the listener personally mocked Christ in the same sense as the crowd. That can be theologically defensible if grounded in sin, but here it lands abruptly and may feel accusatory without explanation.
- Clarity issue: “the One the whole song was about” is evocative, but a zero-Bible-knowledge listener may not know Psalm 22 is being called a “song.” Consider “the whole psalm” unless the musical point matters.
- Strongest line: “He proved He was the Son not by escaping the cross, but by staying on it” is faithful, clear, and text-rooted.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Correct the KJV quotation handling, especially Matthew 27:43’s omitted phrase.
2. Clarify “and yours” so conviction is grace-anchored, not abrupt accusation.
3. Strengthen the final sentence so it lands less like a general invitation and more like the script/scorn image.
