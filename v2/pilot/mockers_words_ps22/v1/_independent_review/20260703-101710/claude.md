## Findings

**KJV verbatim — CLEAN.**
- "...they shake the head, saying, He trusted on the LORD that he would deliver him: let him deliver him..." — word-for-word KJV, span matches the 22:7-8 label.
- "He trusted in God; let him deliver him now... for he said, I am the Son of God." — verbatim; the ellipsis correctly marks the dropped "if he will have him:".
- "Save yourself and come down, they jeered" is a paraphrase of Matt 27:40/42 kept **outside** quote marks and in narrator voice — legal per the rules, but see Fix 1.

**Narrative facts — accurate.** Passers-by wag heads (Matt 27:39), the rulers speak the 27:43 line (27:41-43) — the script assigns each act to the right party, and "nearly line for line" is an honest hedge for "trusted on the LORD" vs "trusted in God". This avoids the recurring witness-over-claim failure.

**Doctrine — one soft over-reach.** "He **proved** He was the Son not by escaping the cross, but by staying on it." Scripture ties the *proof* of sonship to the resurrection (Rom 1:4); the cross-staying moment yields the centurion's confession (Matt 27:54), not a proof-claim. "Proved" is a homiletic stretch, not heresy — soften to "showed" or anchor it to the centurion.

**Grace check — passes.** "He stayed under their scorn, and yours, to win you" is conviction without fear/gain-loss framing; "to win you" keeps it grace-anchored. "and yours" lands the listener among the mockers — biblically defensible (Isa 53:3), and it's what makes the CTA personal.

**Thread spine — one thread.** Mockery-as-unwitting-prophecy runs hook → proof → conviction → CTA with no swap; the closer is the spine inverted.

**HOOK: 8/10.** "The crowd mocking Jesus at the cross was reading from a script — they just didn't know it." Concrete, ironic, no template opener. Not a 9 because "reading from a script" needs a half-beat of processing before the payoff lands.

**LANDING: 9/10.** "Come to the One who would not come down." This exact sentence could end **no other piece** — it's the piece's own image inverted. Note for the $0 gate: "come" is technically the stock-family verb, and the piece's **quoted** KJV never contains "come down" — the earning is done by the unquoted paraphrase. Quoting Matt 27:42 KJV ("let him now come down from the cross") instead of the paraphrase would make the closer verb gate-proof *and* add a second exact-fulfilment line for free.

VERDICT: PASS
