# With His Stripes — Isaiah 53:5 short (v2 pilot, A/B build)

- **Built via:** the v2 skill discipline (`/study` → `/narrate` → `/review`), agent-mode ($0).
- **Spine (one thread):** Isaiah 53:5 promised healing through *someone else's* wounds 700 years
  early; the apostle Peter takes up Isaiah's "stripes/healed" line and lays it on Christ — "in his
  own body on the tree" — shifting the pronoun from "we" to "ye" to aim the promise straight at
  the reader. The wounds were His; the healing is yours.
- **Cross-ref (airtight):** Isaiah 53:5 → 1 Peter 2:24 (Peter echoes Isaiah and applies it to Christ; the
  small shifts — "with/by", "we/ye" — are the lever, NOT a claim of verbatim quotation).
- **Format:** 9:16, ~60s, Gospel Five-Beat, grace-anchored CTA-to-Jesus.
- **Doctrine guard:** "healed" = healed from sin/guilt (1 Pet 2:24 "bare our sins… live unto
  righteousness"), NOT a physical-health / prosperity claim. Grace-anchored landing, no fear/gain-loss.

---

**[narrator]**
You carry a wound nothing has been able to close — a guilt that keeps reopening, no matter how hard you try to make it right.

**[narrator]**
Seven hundred years before the cross, Isaiah wrote that the healing you can't reach would come through someone else's wounds.

**[isaiah — KJV, Isaiah 53:5]**
"But he was wounded for our transgressions, he was bruised for our iniquities: the chastisement of our peace was upon him; and with his stripes we are healed."

**[narrator]**
Stripes — the welts of a flogging, the wounding Isaiah foresaw centuries before it fell on Jesus' back. And the apostle Peter took up Isaiah's words and laid them on Christ at the cross:

**[peter — KJV, 1 Peter 2:24]**
"Who his own self bare our sins in his own body on the tree, that we, being dead to sins, should live unto righteousness: by whose stripes ye were healed."

**[narrator]**
Isaiah said "we are healed." Peter, writing to people who had come to Christ, could say "ye were healed" — for them it was already done. Not guilt managed: sins borne, carried away in His body, so a new life could start clean.

**[narrator]**
You were never meant to close that wound yourself. The healing was finished at the cross, carried in His own wounds. So bring Him the guilt that keeps reopening, and let His wounds close yours. His stripes — your healing.

## DEPTH & SOURCING  *(not spoken)*
- **KJV verbatim** (re-verified vs data/kjv_cache.json, 2026-07-03): Isaiah 53:5 and 1 Peter 2:24 are
  now quoted as FULL verses — the 2026-07-03 repaired-panel run (5/5 voices, `_independent_review\20260703-101552\`)
  convergently flagged that the earlier "..." elisions silently dropped substitution ("the chastisement of
  our peace was upon him") and righteousness ("dead to sins... live unto righteousness"), letting "healed"
  drift toward guilt-therapy. Elisions removed.
- **The fresh, faithful lever:** Peter shifts Isaiah's pronoun — Isaiah 53:5 "with his stripes **we** are
  healed" → 1 Peter 2:24 "by whose stripes **ye** were healed." Surprising about the *text* (the apostle
  applies the prophecy and aims it at his readers), never about the *truth*.
- **"Stripes" (Heb. chaburah) = welts/bruises** — the wounding Isaiah foresaw, fulfilled in the scourging.
  The prophecy foretells suffering-by-wounding, NOT Rome's specific instrument (corrected after the panel
  flagged the original "Roman scourge predicted before Rome" as eisegesis). NT-cited: Peter applies it to Christ.
- **Peter's authority is apostolic + Spirit-inspired, not eyewitness proximity** — Scripture places Peter at
  the high priest's courtyard denying Christ and weeping before dawn (Matt 26:58, Luke 22:62); the Roman
  scourging was the next morning at Pilate's praetorium (John 19:1). The original draft's "Peter watched the
  scourging" was an invented detail (INV-4 violation) — removed after the panel caught it.
- **Doctrine:** "healed" is healed from sin/guilt (1 Pet 2:24 names it: "bare our sins… should live unto
  righteousness"), not a health claim. The sheep echo (Isa 53:6 / 1 Pet 2:25) underwrites the substitution.
- **Grace-anchored landing (rebuilt 2026-07-03):** the panel unanimously scored the old closer
  ("Come to Him, and receive it") 5/10 — a stock line that could end any piece. Rebuilt from the piece's
  own imagery ("bring Him the guilt that keeps reopening, and let His wounds close yours. His stripes —
  your healing."). Response named (bring/let), healing OFFERED not pre-declared (claude: "ye" addresses
  believers — don't tell the listener their wound is closed before they come). The we/ye beat now frames
  Peter honestly (writing to people who had come to Christ) instead of "points straight at you" as a
  textual claim. AUDIO (if any) NOW STALE — text changed.
