<speaker name="narrator">You carry a wound nothing has been able to close — a guilt that keeps reopening, no matter how hard you try to make it right. Seven hundred years before the cross, Isaiah wrote that the healing you can't reach would come through someone else's wounds. "But he was wounded for our transgressions, he was bruised for our iniquities... and with his stripes we are healed." Stripes — the welts of a flogging, the wounding Isaiah foresaw centuries before it fell on Jesus' back. And the apostle Peter took up Isaiah's words and laid them on Christ at the cross: "Who his own self bare our sins in his own body on the tree... by whose stripes ye were healed." And watch what Peter does with it. Isaiah wrote "we are healed." Peter writes "ye were healed" — and points the promise straight at the one who's listening: at you. You were never meant to make it right yourself. The healing isn't something you produce — it was finished at the cross, in His own wounds, for you. The wound that keeps reopening, He has already closed. Come to Him, and receive it.</speaker>
