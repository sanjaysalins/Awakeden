<speaker name="narrator">You carry a wound nothing has been able to close — a guilt that keeps reopening, no matter how hard you try to make it right. Seven hundred years before the cross, Isaiah wrote that the healing you can't reach would come through someone else's wounds.</speaker>
<speaker name="isaiah">"But he was wounded for our transgressions, he was bruised for our iniquities: the chastisement of our peace was upon him; and with his stripes we are healed."</speaker>
<speaker name="narrator">Stripes — the welts of a flogging, the wounding Isaiah foresaw centuries before it fell on Jesus' back. And the apostle Peter took up Isaiah's words and laid them on Christ at the cross:</speaker>
<speaker name="peter">"Who his own self bare our sins in his own body on the tree, that we, being dead to sins, should live unto righteousness: by whose stripes ye were healed."</speaker>
<speaker name="narrator">Isaiah said "we are healed." Peter, writing to people who had come to Christ, could say "ye were healed" — for them it was already done. Not guilt managed: sins borne, carried away in His body, so a new life could start clean. You were never meant to close that wound yourself. The healing was finished at the cross, carried in His own wounds. So bring Him the guilt that keeps reopening, and let His wounds close yours. His stripes — your healing.</speaker>
