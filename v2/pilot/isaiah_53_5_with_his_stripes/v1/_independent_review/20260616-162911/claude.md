## Real findings

**1. Internal contradiction in the DEPTH notes — residual error.** The fresh-lever bullet still says the shift is *"Surprising about the text (**the eyewitness** personalises the prophecy)."* But two bullets down, the notes explicitly correct exactly this: *"Peter's authority is apostolic + Spirit-inspired, **not eyewitness proximity**... The original draft's 'Peter watched the scourging' was an invented detail (INV-4 violation) — removed after the panel caught it."* You removed the eyewitness claim from the script but left "the eyewitness" in the sourcing. It contradicts your own correction and re-asserts the flagged error. Fix the word ("the apostle," not "the eyewitness").

**2. The pronoun lever slightly overclaims what the text does.** The script says Peter *"points the promise straight at the one who's listening: at you."* Grammatically, Peter's "ye" addresses the **recipients of the epistle** (the scattered believers, 1 Pet 1:1), not "anyone listening today." Treating "ye" as if it aims at the modern hearer is a legitimate *homiletical application* — but the script presents it as what the text plainly *does* ("watch what Peter does with it"). That's a small exegesis-vs-application slip. It's defensible, but tighten the framing so you're applying the verse, not claiming Peter's grammar targets the listener.

**3. Mild tension between "already healed" and "come and receive."** The landing blends *"by whose stripes ye were healed"* / *"He has already closed"* (accomplished, past tense) with *"Come to Him, and receive it"* (still pending the listener's response). The gospel offer does hold both (objective accomplishment, subjective reception), so this is orthodox — but on first hearing a zero-Bible listener could hear "it's already done for you" *and* "but you still have to come," which can read as a contradiction. One clause distinguishing "done at the cross" from "yours when you come" would remove the ambiguity. Borderline, not fatal.
