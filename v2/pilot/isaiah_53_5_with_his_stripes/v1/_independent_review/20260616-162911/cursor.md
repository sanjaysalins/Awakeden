## Independent adversarial review — *With His Stripes* (Isaiah 53:5 short)

Reviewing the **spoken lines only**, against your lenses. KJV checked against `data/kjv_cache.json`.

### Doctrinal soundness — **Conditional**

**Orthodox core, but the spoken arc under-defines *healed*.**

The artifact’s DEPTH section correctly pins *healed* to sin/righteousness via the omitted 1 Peter clause (*"that we, being dead to sins, should live unto righteousness"*). That clause is **not spoken**. What *is* heard:

- Isaiah ends on *"and with his stripes we are healed"* right after *transgressions/iniquities* — helpful, but still the word most prosperity listeners hear as physical/emotional relief.
- Peter’s spoken quote includes *"bare our sins"* — good — but the righteousness half that locks Peter’s meaning is again cut.

For a cold listener (or anyone with Isaiah-53-as-healing baggage), the **spoken** track does not fully close *healed* = forgiveness/righteousness. The writer knows the risk and leaves the fix in unspoken notes.

**Soft eisegesis in the middle beat:**

> *"Stripes — the welts of a flogging, the wounding Isaiah foresaw centuries before it fell on Jesus' back."*

- *Jesus' back* — not in Isaiah 53:5 or 1 Peter 2:24. *Chaburah* = bruise/welt; scourging is a reasonable NT fulfillment, but **back** is imported detail.
- *welts of a flogging* — tighter than the prior “Roman scourge” draft, but still adds instrument specificity the prophecy does not name.

Not heresy, but it is the kind of “Bible-sounding fill-in” a skeptical listener may treat as text, not inference.

**Metadata self-contradiction (signals weak final audit):** DEPTH line 45 still says Peter *"the eyewitness personalises the prophecy"* while lines 50–53 explicitly deny eyewitness placement. Spoken text is clean; notes are not.

### KJV verbatim — **Isaiah FAIL; Peter PASS**

**1 Peter 2:24** — Pass.  
*"Who his own self bare our sins in his own body on the tree... by whose stripes ye were healed."*  
Ordered ellipsis fragment of the cached KJV; acceptable omission of the righteousness clause.

**Isaiah 53:5** — **Fail on interior punctuation.**

- **KJV:** *"...he was bruised for our iniquities:**:** the chastisement..."*
- **Artifact:** *"...he was bruised for our iniquities**,**... and with his stripes..."*

The colon after *iniquities* is replaced by a comma before the ellipsis. Under your rule (*every word inside quotation marks must be exact KJV*) and the project’s own G1 (*interior punctuation must match*), that is not a clean fragment. Fix: keep the colon — e.g. *"...he was bruised for our iniquities: ... and with his stripes we are healed."*

### Clarity on first hearing — **Mixed**

**Hook — Pass (~5s).**  
*"You carry a wound nothing has been able to close — a guilt that keeps reopening, no matter how hard you try to make it right."*  
Concrete, emotional, immediate. Strong open.

**Middle — assumes insider knowledge:**

- *"Seven hundred years before the cross"* — **cross** before **Jesus** is named; a zero-Bible listener asks *whose cross?*
- *"Isaiah"* / *"the apostle Peter"* — no one-line grounding (*a prophet* / *Jesus’ disciple who wrote to Christians*).
- *"And watch what Peter does with it"* — fine for church folks; opaque for the stated zero-knowledge audience.

**Unexplained textual move:** The fresh lever is framed as *"we are healed"* → *"ye were healed"* (pronoun only). Peter also shifts **with → by** and **present → past** (*we **are** healed* vs *ye **were** healed*). Ignoring tense is not heresy, but on first hearing it sounds like a one-word swap when Peter actually re-aims **and** re-tenses an accomplished fact. Clever-but-thin for anyone who knows Isaiah’s prophetic present.

**Landing ambiguity:** *"Come to Him, and receive it"* — *it* is grammatically tied to *healing*, but after two *healed* lines that may still mean emotional/physical relief to a cold ear. Weak referent.

*"no matter how hard you try to make it right"* describes failed self-effort as the problem, not the gospel path — acceptable setup.

No fear, gain-loss, or manufactured pressure. *"Come to Him, and receive it"* invites; it does not threaten.

3. Landing does not harvest the pronoun insight (see below).
