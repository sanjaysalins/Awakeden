# Independent review — claude (OK, 57s)

I checked both quoted spans against KJV, traced the spine's factual claims against the Gospel narrative, and pressure-tested the doctrine guard. KJV is clean; one dramatic claim is fabricated.

## Findings

**1. KJV verbatim — PASS (verified).**
- Isaiah 53:5: "But he was wounded for our transgressions, he was bruised for our iniquities... and with his stripes we are healed." — exact; the ellipsis correctly omits only "the chastisement of our peace was upon him;". ✓
- 1 Peter 2:24: "Who his own self bare our sins in his own body on the tree... by whose stripes ye were healed." — exact; ellipsis omits only "that we, being dead to sins, should live unto righteousness:". ✓
- The pronoun shift ("we are healed" → "ye were healed") is real and verbatim. Genuinely fresh-about-the-text, orthodox in claim. This is the strongest part of the piece.

**2. INVENTED FACT — Peter did NOT witness the scourging. (Blocking.)**
The spine and line 4 both assert it as fact:
- Spine: "Peter — who stood near the scourging"
- Line 4: *"And Peter, who stood near enough to watch that scourging, reached back to Isaiah's exact words"*

Scripture places Peter following "afar off" to the **high priest's courtyard** (Matt 26:58, Luke 22:54), where he denied Christ three times and then "went out, and wept bitterly" (Luke 22:62) — *before* dawn. The Roman scourging happened the next morning at Pilate's praetorium (John 19:1, Matt 27:26), executed by Roman soldiers. There is no text putting Peter there; the narrative places him gone. This is an invented eyewitness detail used to heighten drama — exactly the "no invented detail to chase freshness" violation. It also weakens the real point: Peter's authority here is apostolic and Spirit-inspired, not "he watched it happen." Cut the eyewitness claim; the verbatim quotation carries the weight on its own.

**3. Over-specified prophecy claim — "the marks of a Roman scourge, written down centuries before Rome ever used one."**
The Hebrew of Isaiah 53:5 (*chaburah*) means a bruise/welt — it does not specify a *Roman* flagellum. Reading "Roman scourge" into the word, and the "before Rome ever used one" flourish, overstates the specificity of the prediction (Rome existed in Isaiah's day as a small settlement; scourging was widespread, not Roman-invented). Faithful version: the prophecy foretold *suffering by wounding/stripes*, which the scourging fulfilled — don't claim the word predicted Rome's specific instrument. Soften to avoid the eisegesis.

**4. Doctrine guard — PASS.** "Healed" is correctly anchored to sin/guilt via 1 Pet 2:24's own context ("bare our sins"), explicitly fencing off the prosperity/physical-health reading. Good catch by the builder.

**5. Grace-anchored landing — PASS.** "The stripes that mend you have already been borne... Come to Him." No fear/gain-loss/works. The pronoun-aimed-at-you close does new work. ✓

**6. Hook — adequate, not gripping.** "You carry a wound nothing has been able to close — a guilt that keeps reopening" lands the felt-need but is a familiar opener; it leans on assumption ("no matter how hard you try to be good enough"). Acceptable, not a problem — but it's the floor, not a scroll-stopper.

The piece is doctrinally sound and KJV-clean; the single real defect is the fabricated "Peter watched the scourging," which is asserted twice as fact.
