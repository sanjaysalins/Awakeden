## Independent adversarial review — *With His Stripes* (Isaiah 53:5 short)

### Doctrinal soundness — **problems**

**1. Fabricated eyewitness claim (most serious)**

> *"And Peter, who stood near enough to watch that scourging, reached back to Isaiah's exact words"*

Scripture does not place Peter at the scourging. In Mark 14:53–72 / Luke 22:54–62 / John 18:15–27, Peter follows **at a distance** to the high priest’s courtyard, denies Jesus three times, weeps, and goes out — during the **Jewish trial**, before Jesus is delivered to Pilate. Scourging happens later (Mark 15:15). John is at the cross; Peter is not recorded as a scourging witness anywhere.

This is invented drama sold as biblical fact. It undermines the very “eyewitness personalises the prophecy” lever the script builds on.

**2. Overstated “Roman scourge before Rome” claim**

> *"Stripes — the marks of a Roman scourge, written down centuries before Rome ever used one."*

Two problems:
- **Stripes/whipping were not a Roman invention.** Deuteronomy 25:3 limits “forty stripes”; ANE cultures used beating long before Isaiah. The Hebrew (*chăbûrâ*) means wound/stripe from beating — not “Roman flagellum” specifically.
- Rome as an imperial scourging-and-crucifixion machine is ~700 years after Isaiah — but **scourging itself** is not. The line sounds prophetically clever but trades on a historical slip. Freshness here edges past faithful depth.

**3. Doctrinal guard on “healed” is mostly in the ellipsis, not the ear**

The spoken 1 Peter quote omits the clause that pins meaning:

> *"that we, being dead to sins, should live unto righteousness:"*

The hook’s “guilt” helps, but an audio-only listener still hears *"by whose stripes ye were healed"* right after a metaphorical “wound.” Without the omitted line, the prosperity/therapeutic reading is not fully closed in what is **spoken** — only in the unspoken DEPTH notes.

---

### KJV verbatim — **pass on words; tension on “exact”**

Quoted **word sequences** match the pinned KJV corpus:

- Isaiah: *"But he was wounded for our transgressions, he was bruised for our iniquities... and with his stripes we are healed."* ✓ (middle clause omitted via ellipsis — acceptable fragment pattern)
- 1 Peter: *"Who his own self bare our sins in his own body on the tree... by whose stripes ye were healed."* ✓

No altered words inside the fragments.

**But** the narration contradicts its own verbatim standard in commentary:

> *"reached back to Isaiah's **exact words**"* → then → *"Watch the **one word** Peter changes."*

Peter does not quote Isaiah verbatim, and he changes more than one word (*with his stripes we are healed* → *by whose stripes ye were healed*). That is a spoken logic error, not a KJV typo.

---

### Clarity on first hearing — **problems**

**1. Self-contradiction in adjacent beats**

“Exact words” → “one word changes” forces the listener to reconcile opposites. A zero-Bible-knowledge hearer will not know which claim to trust.

**2. “One word” is factually false**

Peter also shifts:
- *with his stripes* → *by whose stripes*
- *we are healed* → *ye were healed* (person **and** tense)

Calling this a single-word change is a logic trick, not a faithful textual observation. The **we → ye** insight is real; overselling it damages credibility.

**3. Roman-scourge detour adds noise**

The pronoun spine is clear and strong. The Roman-flagellum beat is a second, shakier argument that a new listener must swallow before the main payoff.

---

### Grace-anchored conviction — **mostly pass, one edge**

> *"a guilt that keeps reopening, no matter how hard you try to be good enough"*

Mostly grace-framed, but *"try to be good enough"* introduces a subtle **works** echo (self-repair by effort). Not full gain/loss preaching, but it brushes the lane your constitution says to avoid. The landing itself is grace-clean:

> *"already been borne — in His own body, on the tree. Come to Him."*

No fear, no bargain, no “or else.”

---

### Freshness = faithful depth — **mixed**

**Strong:** The Isaiah *we* → Peter *ye* observation is orthodox, text-grounded, and genuinely surprising about the **text** — exactly the right lever.

**Weak:** The Peter-at-the-scourging and pre-Roman-flagellum beats manufacture drama the text does not support. That is freshness chasing effect, not depth in the passage.

---

### One thread spine — **mostly pass, minor wobble**

Hook (unclosable guilt) → substitution prophecy → Isaiah → Peter’s application → pronoun aim → invitation: coherent.

Wobble: the middle briefly swaps from **textual pronoun thread** to **historical eyewitness + Roman prediction thread**, then back. Not full thread-swapping, but the detour is load-bearing on bad history.

---

### Landing does NEW work — **partial pass**

> *"You were never meant to heal yourself. The stripes that mend you have already been borne..."*

This pays off substitution better than a bare “will you trust Him?” — grace-given, not self-earned.

> *"Come to Him."*

Still a familiar CTA shape. The **ye** insight — the claimed fresh close — is not echoed in the final line. The landing does **some** new work (anti-self-healing + already borne); it does not fully cash the pronoun cheque the middle writes.

---

### Hook (~5 seconds) — **pass with reservation**

> *"You carry a wound nothing has been able to close — a guilt that keeps reopening..."*

Immediate, emotional, and it steers “healing” toward guilt/sin before Isaiah is read. It grips.

Reservation: “wound/guilt/good enough” is a recognizable gospel-short template — effective, not distinctive.

---

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Delete or rewrite *"Peter, who stood near enough to watch that scourging"* — Peter is not a scourging eyewitness in Scripture; keep Peter as post-cross/apostolic applier of Isaiah, not invented spectator.
2. Fix the *"exact words" / "one word Peter changes"* contradiction — honestly name the we→ye shift (and optionally tense) without claiming verbatim quotation or a single-word edit.
3. Remove or replace *"written down centuries before Rome ever used one"* — stripes are not a Roman innovation; anchor the marvel on the Servant bearing wounds for our transgressions, not a flagellum-before-Rome conceit.
