# Independent review — claude (OK, 72s)

Reviewed as a hostile independent auditor. Findings below, each tied to an exact phrase.

## Findings

**1. Stock closer — the landing fails the earned-landing rule.**
Final sentence: **"Come to Him, and receive it."** That exact sentence could end almost any piece in the catalogue — it's the "come to Jesus" family your own `narration_gate.py` exists to FAIL. Neither quoted verse uses the verb "come" (Isaiah 53:5: *wounded, bruised, healed*; 1 Peter 2:24: *bare, healed*), so it isn't earned by this piece's KJV. Painful, because the sentence right before it ("The wound that keeps reopening, He has already closed") IS built from this piece's own image — the piece ends one sentence too late. Landing capped at 5.

**2. Both KJV quotes are spliced elisions.**
- Isaiah 53:5 as quoted skips **"the chastisement of our peace was upon him"** behind a "...". 
- 1 Peter 2:24 as quoted skips **"that we, being dead to sins, should live unto righteousness"** behind a "...".

The words kept are verbatim, but a listener cannot hear an ellipsis — the audio presents a spliced verse as the verse. This project was already burned by exactly this (the Acts 8:35 elision on the Isaiah 53 long-form, caught by 4/5 panelists). The Isaiah cut is the worse one: it removes the substitution clause the whole piece stands on.

**3. The Peter "we → ye" move over-claims.**
"Peter writes 'ye were healed' — and **points the promise straight at the one who's listening: at you.**" Textually the we→ye shift is real and a genuinely fresh observation. But Peter's "ye" is his letter's readers — believers who "are now returned unto the Shepherd" (1 Pet 2:25), and "were healed" is past tense *of them*. Peter is not addressing the unconverted scroller. The same over-reach repeats in "The wound that keeps reopening, **He has already closed**" — declaring the healing already applied to a listener who hasn't yet come. The accomplished work is finished at the cross (sound); saying it's already *applied* to "you" before "receive it" blurs accomplishment and application. Fixable with small wording: Peter turns the promise into direct address / He has already *paid for* the closing of it.

**4. Hook is decent but not startling.** "You carry a wound nothing has been able to close — a guilt that keeps reopening" — direct, second-person, felt-need, not a template open. But wound-that-won't-heal is common devotional language; it earns a stop, not a jolt. Hook: 7.

**What's good:** one clean spine (unclosable wound → stripes → healed → wound closed); "stripes — the welts of a flogging" is accurate and vivid; healing is read as guilt/sin-healing per Isaiah's own context, not prosperity-style physical healing; conviction is grace-anchored, zero fear or gain-loss framing; "seven hundred years" is fine.

**HOOK: 7 · LANDING: 5**

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Cut or rewrite the final sentence — end on the piece's own image/verbs ("healed", the wound He closed), not the stock "Come to Him"; the previous sentence already does the work.
2. Repair the two KJV elisions: quote Isaiah 53:5 with "the chastisement of our peace was upon him" intact (or restructure to quote a shorter continuous span), and do the same for 1 Peter 2:24 — no inaudible "..." splices.
3. Soften the Peter application: "ye" addresses believers already healed — offer the finished healing ("He has already borne the closing of it — receive it") rather than declaring the listener's wound "already closed" before they come.
