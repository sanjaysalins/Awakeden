# Independent review — codex (OK, 30s)

**Findings**

- KJV verbatim problem: `"But he was wounded for our transgressions, he was bruised for our iniquities... and with his stripes we are healed."` The KJV has `iniquities:` then the omitted clause, not an ellipsis. If quoting “KJV verbatim,” this should not be presented as a continuous quotation without marking omission more formally or reading the full verse.

- KJV/context problem: `"Who his own self bare our sins in his own body on the tree... by whose stripes ye were healed."` This omits Peter’s purpose clause: “that we, being dead to sins, should live unto righteousness.” That omission makes the piece lean therapeutic while leaving out Peter’s repentance/holiness frame.

- Doctrinal precision issue: `the healing you can't reach would come through someone else's wounds` is basically sound, but `The wound that keeps reopening, He has already closed` risks promising an immediate subjective emotional closure. Christ has finished atonement; a listener may still experience conviction, grief, or struggle.

- Clarity issue: `Isaiah wrote "we are healed." Peter writes "ye were healed" — and points the promise straight at the one who's listening: at you.` This overreads the pronoun shift. Peter is addressing his Christian readers, not creating a direct individual “you” promise to every passive listener without the call to faith and repentance being clear.

- Grace-anchored conviction: The opening `a guilt that keeps reopening, no matter how hard you try to make it right` is strong and grace-oriented. But the script should name sin plainly before resolving it as “wound,” otherwise guilt becomes mainly pain rather than guilt before God.

- Thread spine: The wound/stripes/healing thread is coherent. The weak point is the middle’s pronoun observation, which becomes a clever textual move instead of deepening the main image.

- Hook score: 7/10. `You carry a wound nothing has been able to close` is direct and emotionally clear, though familiar.

- Landing score: 5/10. `Come to Him, and receive it` is stock. It could end many different gospel shorts. The stronger image is “stripes/wounds/closed,” but the CTA does not land through that image.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Fix the KJV quotations or read the full verses verbatim.
2. Restore Peter’s holiness/repentance context so “healing” does not become merely therapeutic.
3. Replace the stock closer with a CTA built from the piece’s own stripes/wounds image.
