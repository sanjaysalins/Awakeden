# Independent review — gemini (OK, 44s)

The script fundamentally works and successfully bridges the Old Testament prophecy to its New Testament fulfillment, but it suffers from a major auditory collision and a slight drift into therapeutic language at the end.

**1. Auditory Collision / Ambiguous Antecedent:**
In the sixth paragraph, you write:
*"...present turned to past, because the wounding is finished, and aimed straight at you."*
When spoken aloud, the listener will hear that the *wounding* is aimed straight at them. The grammatical subject of the clause becomes horribly muddled. You mean the *promise* or the *pronoun* is aimed at them, but the proximity to "wounding" makes it sound like an attack on the viewer. This will derail the listener's focus right before the climax. 

**2. Therapeutic Drift in the CTA:**
Your depth notes explicitly state this is a "healed from sin/guilt" claim, not a physical/health claim, and your hook perfectly establishes the "wound" as "guilt". However, your CTA concludes with:
*"Stop straining to be whole; come to Him..."*
"Whole" is the ultimate buzzword of the therapeutic, self-help gospel. It abandons the sharp "guilt/justification" framing you built and replaces it with vague emotional wellness. If the wound is guilt, the resolution must be pardon, cleansing, or the specific "healing" referenced in the verse—not generalized "wholeness."

**3. Mixed Metaphor in the Hook:**
*"You carry a wound nothing has been able to close — a guilt that keeps reopening, no matter how hard you try to be good enough."*
"Try to be good enough" is a tired evangelical cliché that slightly breaks the medical metaphor. You are talking about a wound that won't close; the natural contrast is trying to *heal* it or outwork it, rather than just being "good." 

**4. The "Ye" Logic Trick (Cautionary Note):**
The textual pivot—that Peter shifts "we" to "ye"—is engaging. However, remember that "ye" is technically plural in Early Modern English (addressing the scattered church), just as "we" is plural. Claiming Peter "makes it personal" works for a modern ear that reads "ye" as "you" (singular/personal), but functionally, Peter is just shifting from first-person plural to second-person plural. It survives the "clever trick" rule because of how it lands emotionally, but it must be phrased carefully so it doesn't sound like a manufactured linguistic gimmick.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Rewrite "because the wounding is finished, and aimed straight at you" to clearly separate the finished work on the cross from the promise being aimed at the listener.
2. Remove "Stop straining to be whole" from the CTA; replace it with language that directly resolves the specific "guilt" introduced in the hook.
3. Tighten the hook's cliché ("try to be good enough") to better sustain the "wound/healing" metaphor (e.g., trying to scrub your own record clean).
