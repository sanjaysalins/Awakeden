No altered words inside quotation marks.

## Real problem — the thread contradicts itself
The whole fresh lever is that **Peter changed Isaiah's pronoun** ("we are" → "ye were"). But the narration first says:

> "the apostle Peter took **those exact words** and laid them on Christ"

then four lines later says:

> "Watch what Peter does with it. Isaiah wrote 'we are healed.' Peter **makes it personal** — 'ye were healed'..."

If Peter changed the words, they are not "exact words." A first-time listener hears you assert *verbatim*, then immediately watch Peter *alter* it. That's a self-contradiction on first hearing and it undercuts the spine's punch. **The supporting note repeats the same error**: the cross-ref line claims "Peter quotes Isaiah **verbatim**" — which is flatly false and contradicts your own pronoun-shift thread. Peter *echoes/alludes to* Isaiah (shared "stripes"/"healed"); he does not quote verbatim. Fix the spoken line to "took those same words" / "took up Isaiah's words," and fix the note from "verbatim" to "echoes/applies."

## Secondary flags
- **"present turned to past, because the wounding is finished"** — this builds the point on the KJV *English* tenses ("are healed" vs "were healed"). The underlying Hebrew perfect / Greek aorist don't map cleanly to that present→past story. It's homiletically plausible and not heretical, but it's leaning on a translation artifact to carry the "because the wounding is finished" logic. Keep the pronoun shift (textually solid); soften or drop the tense argument so you're not teaching a conceit.
- **"aimed straight at you"** — "ye" is plural (Peter addressing scattered believers, 1 Pet 1:1), not singular. Individual application is fine homiletically; just don't lean on "ye" as if it were singular "you."
- **"it was finished in His wounds, the moment they fell"** — locates the finished atonement at the *scourging*. Precisely, the work is finished at the cross/death (the "It is finished" you're echoing is from John 19:30, at death, not the flogging). Minor, but it risks pinning completion to the stripes alone.

## Holds up well
Doctrine is sound: "healed" is anchored to guilt/sin in both the hook ("a guilt that keeps reopening") and the close, not prosperity/health. The landing is grace-anchored, anti-works ("Stop straining to be whole; come to Him, and receive it"), no fear/gain-loss, and does new work via the pronoun aimed at the reader. Hook grips. The earlier "Roman scourge predicted before Rome" eisegesis and the "Peter watched the scourging" invented detail are both correctly removed.
