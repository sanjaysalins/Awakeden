I read the artifact closely and verified the two Scripture quotes against KJV.

## KJV verbatim — checks out
- **Isaiah 53:5**: "But he was wounded for our transgressions, he was bruised for our iniquities... and with his stripes we are healed." — the ellipsis correctly drops only "the chastisement of our peace was upon him;". Exact.
- **1 Peter 2:24**: "Who his own self bare our sins in his own body on the tree... by whose stripes ye were healed." — the ellipsis correctly drops only "that we, being dead to sins, should live unto righteousness:". Exact.
No altered words inside quotation marks. The pronoun lever (Isaiah "we" → Peter "ye") is genuinely in the text, not manufactured. The "stripes/healed" cross-ref is airtight and the prior eisegesis (Roman scourge / Peter-as-eyewitness) has clearly been scrubbed per the DEPTH notes.

## Real findings (adversarial)

**1. "Watch the one word Peter changes." — risks implying Peter edited Scripture.** Peter did not *change* Isaiah's word; under inspiration he *applies* the prophecy with a different pronoun. To a hostile/precise listener "the one word Peter changes" can sound like one apostle correcting/altering another's text. The DEPTH notes correctly say "shifts." The spoken line should match: "shifts" / "turns" / "makes personal," not "changes."

**2. The landing under-delivers the freshness it claims.** The DEPTH section says the close "does NEW work (the pronoun aimed at YOU)" — but that work happens in the *previous* beat ("aims it straight at you"). The actual landing — "Come to Him." — is a generic CTA and the "in His own body, on the tree" phrase simply re-echoes the 1 Peter quote already spoken. The close restates rather than advances. It's not the banned "Will you trust Him?", but it's adjacent. Consider letting the landing land the *healing-is-already-yours / nothing-left-to-pay* note distinctly instead of repeating the verse image.

**3. Tense shift is silently dropped (minor, optional).** Isaiah "we **are** healed" (present) vs Peter "ye **were** healed" (past/accomplished). The script spotlights only the pronoun, which is a legitimate single-lever choice — but "the one word" framing forecloses the arguably *stronger* point (the healing is already finished). Not a defect; flagging that the chosen lever leaves the better one on the table.

**4. Hook presumes the listener's state.** "You carry a wound nothing has been able to close — a guilt that keeps reopening" asserts guilt onto a cold viewer. This is genre-appropriate and grips in ~3s, so I'm not flagging it as a fault — just noting it's an assertion, not an invitation, and is the one line a skeptic could bounce off. Acceptable as-is.

Doctrine is sound (healed-from-sin is explicit, no prosperity drift, no fear/gain-loss, grace-anchored). Spine is single and unbroken hook→middle→CTA. Clarity is good for a zero-knowledge listener. The two wording items (1 and 2) are worth fixing before LOCK but neither is doctrinally or textually disqualifying.
