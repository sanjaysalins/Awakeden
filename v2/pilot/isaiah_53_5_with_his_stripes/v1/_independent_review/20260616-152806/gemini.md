# Independent review — gemini (OK, 46s)

**Doctrinal Soundness & Grace-Anchored Conviction:**
- *“You carry a wound nothing has been able to close — a guilt that keeps reopening…”* and *“The stripes that mend you have already been borne.”* Excellent. This perfectly guards against prosperity-gospel misreadings by framing the "healing" explicitly as the cure for guilt and sin. It is a high-grace, zero-works landing.

**Freshness & Spine:**
- *“Isaiah wrote ‘we.’ Peter writes ‘ye’ — and aims it straight at you.”* This is a superb observation. It acts as a powerful lever, taking a widely known verse and making it intimately personal. The entire script hinges beautifully on this one transition.

**Clarity / KJV Exactness (The Flaw):**
- *“Watch the one word Peter changes. Isaiah wrote ‘we.’ Peter writes ‘ye’…”*
  **Issue:** This is textually inaccurate in the English KJV. Peter doesn't just change one word. Isaiah says "we **are** healed" and Peter says "ye **were** healed". Peter changes the pronoun *and* the tense (because the cross is now a finished historical act). By claiming it is a "one word" change, you invite the listener to check the text, where they will immediately see two changed words, breaking trust.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Fix the "one word" claim. Change "Watch the one word Peter changes" to something airtight like "Watch the pronoun Peter changes" or "Watch the shift Peter makes."
2. Lean into the tense change if time permits (e.g., Peter also changes "are" to "were" because the work is finished), but at minimum remove the "one word" trap.
3. No other structural fixes needed; the script is otherwise exceptionally strong.
