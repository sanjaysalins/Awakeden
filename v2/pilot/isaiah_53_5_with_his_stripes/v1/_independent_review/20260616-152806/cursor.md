## Findings

### Doctrinal soundness — mostly pass, one soft overreach

The core claim is orthodox: substitutionary bearing of sin, Peter applying Isaiah to Christ, “healed” read through *bare our sins… on the tree* (spoken in the 1 Peter quote). No prosperity pivot, no contrarian reading, no invented scene at the scourging (good revision).

**Soft overreach:** *“the wounding Isaiah foresaw centuries before it fell on Jesus' back”* — Isaiah 53:5 says wounded/bruised/stripes; it does not specify the back or a Roman flogging. That is inference, not text. Minor, but it is the kind of “clever fill-in” a skeptical listener may treat as biblical fact.

**Metadata slip (unspoken):** DEPTH still calls this *“the eyewitness personalises the prophecy”* while the same block correctly notes Peter was **not** a scourging eyewitness. Spoken lines are clean; the notes contradict themselves.

### KJV verbatim — words pass; commentary fails its own standard

Verified against project KJV cache:

- Isaiah fragment: exact word sequence.
- 1 Peter fragment: exact word sequence.

No altered words inside the quoted spans.

**But the narration contradicts itself:**

1. *“Peter took those **exact words** and laid them on Christ”* — Peter does **not** quote Isaiah verbatim. Compare:
   - Isaiah: *“with his stripes **we are** healed”*
   - Peter: *“by whose stripes **ye were** healed”*
   That is adaptation, not transcription.

2. *“Watch the **one word** Peter changes. Isaiah wrote ‘we.’ Peter writes ‘ye’”* — false on its face. At minimum Peter also shifts **with his stripes → by whose stripes** and **are → were** (tense). The **we → ye** insight is real; calling it a one-word change is a logic trick.

### Clarity on first hearing — problems for a zero-Bible-knowledge listener

**1. Self-contradiction in adjacent beats**

*“exact words”* → *“one word changes”* forces the ear to pick between two incompatible claims. That erodes trust in the textual payoff.

**2. Assumed Christian vocabulary with no grounding**

Early lines assume the listener already knows **the cross**, **Isaiah**, **the apostle Peter**, **stripes**, **the tree**. A cold listener gets emotion (*wound*, *guilt*) but not a clear “who said what, when, and why it matters” map before the quotes land.

**3. “Healed” ambiguity is only partly closed in the ear**

The hook’s *“wound”* + *“healing”* is metaphorical guilt; Isaiah adds *transgressions/iniquities*; 1 Peter adds *bare our sins… on the tree*. That helps. But the **omitted** 1 Peter clause — *“that we, being dead to sins, should live unto righteousness”* — is exactly what pins *healed* to righteousness, not therapy. Without it, many listeners will still hear Isaiah’s *“we are healed”* in a physical/therapeutic register (prosperity-gospel baggage on this verse is real).

**4. Temporally muddy line**

*“Peter took those exact words and laid them on Christ **at the cross**”* — Peter wrote the epistle **after** the cross, applying prophecy retrospectively. A new listener may picture Peter **at** Golgotha doing something. Small, but it adds noise.

### Grace-anchored conviction — pass with one edge

Landing is clean grace, not bargain:

> *“The stripes that mend you have already been borne — in His own body, on the tree. Come to Him.”*

Given, not earned. No fear, no “or else.”

**Edge in the hook:**

> *“no matter how hard you try to be good enough”*

This brushes the **works/self-repair** lane your constitution warns against. It describes failure of effort, but still centers effort. A grace-first hook would name the reopening guilt without the “try to be good enough” echo.

---

### Freshness = faithful depth — strong lever, oversold delivery

**Strong:** Isaiah *we* → Peter *ye*, aimed at the listener — surprising about the **text**, orthodox in claim. Good spine.

**Weak:** Overselling it as *“exact words”* / *“one word”* trades faithful textual observation for a gimmick. The depth is real; the packaging is not.

Immediate, emotional, steers “healing” toward guilt before Scripture. Grips in ~5s.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Fix the *“exact words” / “one word Peter changes”* contradiction — Peter adapts Isaiah (*with his stripes we are healed* → *by whose stripes ye were healed*); name the **we → ye** personalization honestly without claiming verbatim quotation or a single-word edit.
2. Close the prosperity/therapeutic misread **in spoken audio** — keep or restore enough of 1 Peter 2:24’s righteousness clause (or one plain narrator line after the quote) so *healed* is unmistakably sin/righteousness, not physical healing.
3. Rewrite the landing so it **cashes the ye pronoun** (e.g. echo *you* / *ye were healed*) instead of ending mainly on generic *“Come to Him”*; trim assumed-vocabulary load (*Isaiah*, *Peter*, *the cross*) or ground them in one plain phrase for cold listeners.
