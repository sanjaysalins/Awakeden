# The One They Pierced — Zechariah 12:10 short (v2 reuse + music test)

- **Built via:** the v2 skill discipline (`/study` → `/narrate` → `/review`), agent-mode ($0).
- **Spine (one thread):** in Zechariah 12:10 the LORD, speaking in the first person, says "they shall
  look upon ME whom they have pierced" — then "mourn for HIM, as for his only son." The pierced one is
  God Himself, the Son; John, an eyewitness at the cross (John 19:35), cites the verse when the soldier's
  spear pierces Jesus' side (John 19:34, 37). The "me → him → only son" shift holds the deity AND the
  distinction of persons. Grace: God answers the piercing not with wrath but by pouring out "the spirit
  of grace" so they look, mourn, and are forgiven. Look at Him — and live.
- **Cross-ref (airtight):** Zechariah 12:10 → John 19:37 (John explicitly cites it at the spear-piercing).
- **Format:** 9:16, ~60s, Gospel Five-Beat, grace-anchored CTA-to-Jesus.
- **Doctrine guard:** Trinitarian — the pierced Son IS God, distinct from the Father who speaks (no
  patripassianism, no modalism: "me… him… his only son" keeps the persons). Grace landing, no fear/gain-loss.

**[narrator]**
A Roman soldier drove a spear into a dead man's side to be sure He was gone. He had no idea he was finishing a sentence God wrote five centuries earlier.

**[narrator]**
In Zechariah, God speaks in the first person about the day His people would pierce someone — and says something staggering:

**[the_lord — KJV, Zechariah 12:10]**
"...they shall look upon me whom they have pierced, and they shall mourn for him, as one mourneth for his only son..."

**[narrator]**
Read it twice. God says they pierced Me — then, mourn for Him. The pierced man is somehow God Himself — God's own Son.

**[narrator]**
At the cross, John watched the spear go in, and reached for that exact verse:

**[john — KJV, John 19:37]**
"They shall look on him whom they pierced."

**[narrator]**
And here is the mercy: the God they pierced answers not with wrath, but with grace poured out — so that when they finally look, they don't just see what they did. They mourn, and they are forgiven.

**[narrator]**
The man on that cross was not a victim God failed to save. He was God, pierced for you. Look at Him — and live.

## DEPTH & SOURCING  *(not spoken)*
- **KJV verbatim** (self-verified via `pipeline/scripture.fetch_kjv`, 2026-06-16): Zechariah 12:10 and
  John 19:37. Ordered, non-overlapping ellipsis fragments; ≤2 substantial quotes (Rule-8).
  - Zech 12:10 span omits the opening "And I will pour…supplications:" and the closing "and shall be in bitterness…firstborn."
  - John 19:37 quoted whole and verbatim. The spear-piercing (John 19:34) is narrator paraphrase (not a 3rd quote).
- **The fresh, faithful lever:** the LORD's own pronoun shift — "they pierced **me**" → "mourn for **him**…
  his **only son**" — the pierced one is God, the Son, distinct from the Father who speaks. Surprising about
  the *text*, never the *truth*.
- **'John watched the spear go in'** is text-supported (John 19:35, "he that saw it bare record" = the
  eyewitness disciple at the cross) — NOT an invented-presence claim (cf. the narrative-presence gate;
  John is legitimately present, unlike Peter/Matthew at the scourging).
- **Doctrine:** the deity claim is the point (Zech is the LORD speaking); guarded against patripassianism/
  modalism by keeping the persons ("me/him/only son"). Grace-anchored: "the spirit of grace" poured out,
  look-mourn-forgiven; no fear / gain-loss; CTA does new work ("Look at Him — and live").
