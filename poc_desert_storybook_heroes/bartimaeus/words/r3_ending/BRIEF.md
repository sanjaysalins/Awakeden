# Rewrite the OPENING and the ENDING of a locked 70-second narration. Blind Bartimaeus (Mark 10:46-52). You are ONE blind writer; you have seen no other rewrite.

## Who it is for
Worldwide, many with English as a second language, many with no Bible knowledge. People who feel unheard or told to "be quiet". Also Bible readers who know the story.

## The film as it is now (locked words, already recorded; pictures already made)
```
L1  [bartimaeus] "Jesus, thou Son of David, have mercy on me."
L2  [narrator] Many in the crowd told the blind beggar to be quiet. Would Jesus even stop?
L3  [narrator] His name was Bartimaeus, begging by the road near Jericho. "Son of David" meant God's promised King.
L4  [narrator] But he shouted even louder.
L5  [bartimaeus] "Thou Son of David, have mercy on me."
    (pause)
L6  [narrator] "And Jesus stood still." A whole crowd was moving. He stopped for one man, and called for him.
L7  [narrator] Bartimaeus threw off his cloak and came. Jesus asked him what he wanted:
L8  [jesus] "What wilt thou that I should do unto thee?"
L9  [bartimaeus] "Lord, that I might receive my sight."
L10 [jesus] "Go thy way; thy faith hath made thee whole."
L11 [narrator] He trusted Jesus, and Jesus made him well. At once he could see.
L12 [narrator] He was free to go. But he followed Jesus, on the road to Jerusalem. There Jesus would die for our sins, the wrong we have done. On the third day, He would rise to life. On His way to the cross, Jesus stood still for one man.
```

## The problems (from the owner and a blind 6-seat panel)
P1. The ending does not land. L12 holds too much in one breath (free to go, followed, Jerusalem, die, sins, a definition of sins, rise, the callback). The last line gets buried.
P2. The cost of stopping is only revealed at the very end. Nobody knows Jesus is walking to His death when He stands still, so the stop plays as just a kind act, not a costly one. (Mark 10:32-34: on this very road up to Jerusalem Jesus had just told the twelve He would be killed and rise the third day. Jericho is the last town on that road.)
P3. The curiosity gap ("Would Jesus even stop?") pays off at 21 s, and nothing new pulls the viewer on until the end.
P4. A newcomer is asked to take death + sins + rising in one breath; the link "why does this matter to me" is not earned.

## Your job
Rewrite ONLY:
- the OPENING (L1-L3; you may also touch L4), and
- the ENDING (L11-L12).
L5-L10 stay word for word (they are recorded). Keep L1 as Bartimaeus's own cry if you can (it is recorded); you may move or reword narrator lines freely.

Pictures already made, in order: shout (beggar), crowd hushing him, Jericho road + beggar, Jesus far up the road inside a big crowd | ... middle ... | his eyes open on Jesus' face, he follows Jesus on the road (from behind), long road climbing to Jerusalem, one empty cross on a hill at dusk, empty tomb at dawn, Jesus and Bartimaeus walking together at sunset. Your opening and ending should fit these pictures (one narrator line per picture is a good guide).

## Hard rules
1. First 3 seconds: plain words; the curiosity gap opens at once.
2. Plant the stake early (P2), in plain words, without spoiling the answer to the gap.
3. Ending: short sentences, one idea each. The last line lands alone and is quotable. It lands on Jesus, the cross and the rising (1 Cor 15:3-4, Mark 10:34), said plainly. Grace, not pressure.
4. KJV quotes verbatim and short. No old word carries the meaning alone.
5. No invented feelings or reasons. Tell what the text says.
6. No stock closer ("come to Jesus", "just ask Him", "He's waiting", "will you...?", "call out today"). No fear, no blanket promise of healing.
7. Never blame "the Jews" or any people: "the crowd", "many".
8. Whole film (your opening + L5-L10 unchanged + your ending): 140-185 spoken words (now 170). Simple words a second-language listener knows.
9. No wording about AI or how the film is made.

## Output (only this)
(a) the FULL script L1..end with your new lines in place; (b) total word count; (c) one line: what changed and why; (d) one line each: who it is for, main take-away, the curiosity gap (opens at / pays off at), why a global newcomer gets it.
