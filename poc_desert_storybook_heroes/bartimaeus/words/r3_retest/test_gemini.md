## Candidate P

**Newcomer**
- **Who is in it?** A blind beggar named Bartimaeus, a crowd, and Jesus (a promised King).
- **What happens?** The blind man shouts for help, the crowd tries to silence him, but the King stops, calls for him, and heals his eyes. Then the King goes to a city to die for people's wrongs and come back to life.
- **Why does it matter?** It shows that even though this King was walking to his own death, he still cared enough to stop for one unimportant beggar. 
- **Confusing words:** `"What wilt thou that I should do unto thee?"` and `"thy faith hath made thee whole"` (the old English is strange, but the narrator explains it right after). `"the wrong we have done"` feels a little bit like a dictionary definition tacked on.

**Bible Reader**
- **Anything new?** Linking the healing in Jericho directly to the context of Mark 10:32-34 (Jesus predicting His death on the road to Jerusalem) is an excellent, fresh theological tie-in. It gives the miracle a ticking clock.
- **Anything inaccurate?** No. KJV quotes are perfectly preserved and accurate to Mark 10.

**Curiosity Gap**
- **Opens:** `"Would He stop for a beggar?"`
- **Pays off:** `"He stopped for one man, and called for him."`
- **Score: 9/10.** Placed perfectly. You establish the massive stakes (He is going to be killed) *before* asking if He will stop, which makes the pause carry immense weight.

**Ending**
- **Does it land?** Yes. `"On His way to die and rise again, Jesus stood still for one man."` It beautifully loops the intimate miracle into the grand gospel message. Clear, standalone, and powerful. 
- **Score: 10/10.**

**Overall Score:** 9.5/10

## Winner: Candidate P
**Why:** Candidate P builds the tension correctly. By establishing the stakes first (Jesus marching to His death) and *then* asking "Would He stop for a beggar?", the curiosity gap has real emotional weight. Candidate Q blows the question too early.

## Top 3 Fixes for Candidate P

1. **Chronology in the opening lines:**
   *Quote:* `[narrator] Many told the blind beggar to be quiet. / [narrator] His name was Bartimaeus, begging by the road near Jericho.`
   *Fix:* You have Bartimaeus shout, the crowd shush him, and *then* you introduce his name and location like a flashback. Flip this so the listener is grounded instantly before the crowd reacts. 

2. **Redundancy after the KJV quote:**
   *Quote:* `[narrator] He trusted Jesus. Jesus made him well. At once he could see.`
   *Fix:* The KJV line just said "thy faith hath made thee whole." You don't need two sentences explaining it. Drop "He trusted Jesus. Jesus made him well" and just say "At once he could see" to keep the pacing brisk.

3. **Clunky definition:**
   *Quote:* `[narrator] There Jesus died on a cross for our sins, the wrong we have done.`
   *Fix:* "the wrong we have done" breaks the poetic rhythm and feels a bit patronizing, even for a global audience. "Sins" is globally understood enough in this context, or you can weave the meaning in more naturally without appending a dictionary definition.
