# Blind test — seat: Fable

Note: P and Q are the same words except ONE thing — where the question sits.
- P: "Would He stop for a beggar?" comes AFTER "He would be killed there."
- Q: "Would Jesus even stop?" comes right after "told the blind beggar to be quiet."

## Candidate P

**Newcomer**
- Who: a blind beggar, Bartimaeus. Jesus, called "God's promised King". A crowd.
- What: he shouts for mercy. People tell him to be quiet. He shouts louder. Jesus stops, asks what he wants, and he can see. He follows Jesus to Jerusalem, where Jesus dies and rises.
- Why it matters: Jesus was walking to His own death, and still stopped for one poor man.
- Confused me:
  - "His name was Bartimaeus, begging by the road near Jericho." — broken sentence. A name cannot beg.
  - "thy faith hath made thee whole" — old words, but the next line explains it well ("He trusted Jesus. Jesus made him well.").
  - "for our sins, the wrong we have done" — I had to hear it twice. Sounds like a list of two things.
  - "He was free to go." — free from what? Small bump.
  - Until line 6 I do not know Jesus is walking past. Lines 1-5 I only hear a man shouting a name.

**Bible reader**
- New: the link to Mark 10:32-34. He had JUST said He would be killed, and He still stood still. "Free to go, he followed" is a nice read of "Go thy way ... followed Jesus in the way".
- Inaccurate: nothing real. "near Jericho" is safe (Mark: going out; Luke: coming near). "threw off his cloak" = "casting away his garment". "At once" = "immediately". "Died for our sins ... third day" = 1 Cor 15:3-4.
- Small: "He would be killed there" is right, but He also said He would rise (10:34). The ending pays that, so fine.

**Curiosity gap**
- Opens softly at line 2 (he is told to be quiet — what now?). Opens fully at line 6: "Would He stop for a beggar?" — with the full weight of "He would be killed there" right before it.
- Paid at "And Jesus stood still." Paid again, deeper, in the last line.
- Weak point: the spoken question arrives late (about 15 s in).
- Score: 8

**Ending**
- "On His way to die and rise again, Jesus stood still for one man." Lands. Clear alone. Answers the exact question asked at line 6. No pressure.
- Score: 9

**Overall: 8**

## Candidate Q

**Newcomer**
- Who / what / why: same as P.
- Confused me:
  - "Would Jesus even stop?" — stop what? I do not yet know Jesus is walking by, or who He is. The question comes before I can understand it. "even" is also a tricky small word for a second-language ear.
  - Same four bumps as P (broken Bartimaeus sentence, "the wrong we have done", "free to go", Jesus not placed on the road early).
- Line 4 now ends flat: "He would be killed there." — and nothing is asked of it. I do not see why I was told this until the very end.

**Bible reader**
- New: same as P. But the Mark 10:32-34 link is weaker here, because the question is not tied to it.
- Inaccurate: nothing.

**Curiosity gap**
- Opens early (about 5 s). Good for the first seconds.
- But it is a thin question: "will a man stop?" with no stake yet. The stake (He is going to die) comes later and is left hanging.
- Paid at "And Jesus stood still." The last line still works, but feels less like an answer.
- Score: 7

**Ending**
- Same line. Still lands. A little less earned, because the death was never part of the question.
- Score: 8

**Overall: 7**

## Winner

1. **P** — the question lands on the right fact. "He would be killed there. Would He stop for a beggar?" is the whole film in two lines, and the last line answers it word for word. Clearer for a newcomer too.
2. **Q** — earlier hook, but the question comes before the newcomer can understand it, and the death line is left with no job.

## Top 3 fixes for P

1. **"His name was Bartimaeus, begging by the road near Jericho."**
   Broken grammar, and nobody has said Jesus is passing by yet. Fix both:
   "His name was Bartimaeus. He sat begging by the road near Jericho. Jesus was walking past."
   (This also gives a newcomer the early hook Q was reaching for, without moving the question.)

2. **"There Jesus died on a cross for our sins, the wrong we have done."**
   The comma makes it sound like two things. Make it one plain idea:
   "There Jesus died on a cross for our sins — for the wrong things we have done."
   Or split it: "There Jesus died on a cross. He died for our sins, the wrong things we have done."

3. **"He was free to go. He followed Jesus."**
   "Free to go" is unclear alone, and the turn is the best small beat in the piece. Join them so the choice shows:
   "Jesus said he could go. He chose to follow Jesus."

Smaller, only if there is room: "Would He stop for a beggar?" could be "Would He stop for one blind beggar?" — it sets up "for one man" in the last line.
