- Bible reader: The approaching death gives Jesus’s stop fresh weight. KJV quotations match. No clear inaccuracies; Bartimaeus continuing toward Jerusalem is inferred from his following Jesus.
- Curiosity gap: Opens explicitly at “Would He stop for a beggar?” Pays off at “And Jesus stood still.” Strong stakes, quickly resolved. **8/10.**
- Ending: Clear and moving alone. However, its final emphasis falls on the individual encounter after mentioning death and resurrection. **8/10.**
- Overall: **8/10.**

Q

- Newcomer: Jesus hears Bartimaeus despite the crowd, restores his sight, and Bartimaeus follows him. Jesus cares about a poor person others dismiss. “Thy faith hath made thee whole” needs the explanation that follows.
- Bible reader: The familiar healing gains weight from the Jerusalem context. KJV quotations match. No clear inaccuracies; “near Jericho” is less precise than Mark’s departure from Jericho.
- Curiosity gap: Opens at “Would Jesus even stop?” Pays off at “And Jesus stood still.” The question gives the intervening background a purpose. **9/10.**
- Ending: Understandable and emotionally complete, though death and resurrection could receive the final emphasis more firmly. **8/10.**
- Overall: **8.5/10.**

Winner: Q, narrowly ahead of P. Its earlier question creates anticipation while the newcomer learns who Bartimaeus and Jesus are.

Top 3 fixes for Q:

1. “On His way to die and rise again, Jesus stood still for one man.”
   Move this callback before the cross and resurrection lines, finishing with “On the third day, He rose to life.”

2. “He trusted Jesus. Jesus made him well. At once he could see.”
   Make the explanation more direct: “Bartimaeus trusted Jesus. Jesus gave him his sight at once.”

3. “His name was Bartimaeus, begging by the road near Jericho. "Son of David" meant God's promised King.”
   Tighten and clarify the location: “Bartimaeus was begging outside Jericho. He called Jesus ‘Son of David’—God’s promised King.”
