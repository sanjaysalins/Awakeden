# Blind test (Sonnet seat): P vs Q

The two candidates are identical except for one question line.
- P: "Would He stop for a beggar?" sits after the "killed there" line.
- Q: "Would Jesus even stop?" sits in line 2, right after the hushing.

## Candidate P

**Newcomer**
- Who: a blind beggar, Bartimaeus, and Jesus. Clear.
- What: the beggar shouts, the crowd hushes him, Jesus stops, heals him, and the man follows Jesus. Clear.
- Why it matters: Jesus was heading to die, yet He stopped for one poor man. Clear. The question "Would He stop for a beggar?" after "He would be killed there" sets the stakes.
- Confusing words and lines:
  - "Son of David" is glossed. Good.
  - "thy faith hath made thee whole" is archaic. "Whole" could be misread. The next line, "Jesus made him well", rescues it.
  - "Jericho" and "Jerusalem" are just names. That is fine.
  - "He was free to go. He followed Jesus." The first "He" is a little unclear: who is free?
  - "Many told ... to be quiet" comes before we know who is speaking. The opening quote is a cold open, but it works.
  - "the wrong we have done" is a good plain gloss.

**Bible reader**
- Nothing new in the story. The link to Mark 10:32-34 is a good fresh angle.
- Accurate to Mark 10 and the KJV. "Called for him" is fine (the crowd relayed the call). The cloak, "stood still", and "followed in the way" are all there. Mark 10:32-34 supports "road to Jerusalem" and "killed there".
- Nothing is wrong. Quotes are verbatim.

**Curiosity gap**
- Opens: "Would He stop for a beggar?" The stake is "on his way to be killed".
- Pays off: "And Jesus stood still." Then the final line repeats and answers it.
- Score: 8.

**Ending**
- "On His way to die and rise again, Jesus stood still for one man." It lands alone and clear. It echoes the opening question. It ends on death and rising, with no pressure.
- Score: 8.

**Overall: 8**

## Candidate Q

**Newcomer**
- Who, what and why are mostly the same as P.
- "Would Jesus even stop?" comes at the very start. A newcomer has barely met Jesus, and the stake ("He will be killed") only comes two lines later. So the question is weaker. It reads as "will he help?" and not "will the man on his way to die stop?".
- The "killed there" line then ends with no tension, and the tension returns only in the last line.
- Same vocabulary issues as P ("whole", and the ambiguous "He was free to go").

**Bible reader**
- Same accuracy as P. Nothing is wrong. Nothing is new.

**Curiosity gap**
- Opens: in line 2, early. Good for the first seconds.
- Pays off: "stood still", about 8 lines later. The wait is longer and the stake is thinner.
- Score: 7.

**Ending**
- Same line. It lands, but it is less set up, because the "on his way to die" stake was not tied to the question.
- Score: 8.

**Overall: 7.5**

## Winner: P

P puts the question right after "He would be killed there". The question then carries the weight of the whole story, and the last line answers it. Q's earlier question is a stronger first-seconds hook, but it asks before the stake exists.

## Top 3 fixes for P

1. "He was free to go. He followed Jesus."
   - The "He" is ambiguous.
   - Suggested: "Bartimaeus was free to go. He followed Jesus."
2. "He trusted Jesus. Jesus made him well. At once he could see."
   - This is fine, but it is the only gloss for "made thee whole". Keep it, and do not cut it.
   - Optional: add "Jesus made him well" before the quote, or keep it as is.
3. "On the third day, He rose to life."
   - "Rose" is a little abstract for a newcomer.
   - Suggested: "On the third day, He came back to life." This must not break the series rule that the ending lands on rising. It is the same meaning in plainer words.

Also consider, as a minor point: "Would He stop for a beggar?" could be "Would He stop for a poor beggar?" No. Leave it as is.
