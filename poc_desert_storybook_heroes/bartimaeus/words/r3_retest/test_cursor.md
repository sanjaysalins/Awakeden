## Candidate P

**Newcomer**
- Who: Jesus, blind man Bartimaeus, many people on the road.
- What happens: People tell him to be quiet; he shouts louder; Jesus stops; Jesus gives him sight; he goes with Jesus toward Jerusalem; story says Jesus later died on a cross and came back to life.
- Why it matters: Even on a hard, important journey, Jesus stops for one suffering person; trusting him changes your life; the ending ties that stop to Jesus dying and rising for people’s wrongdoing.
- Confused: *“What wilt thou that I should do unto thee?”* (old English); *“thy faith hath made thee whole”* (“whole” sounds like more than eyes); *“Son of David”* (helped by the king line).

**Bible reader**
- New: Passion prediction before Jericho (Mark 10:32–34) as frame; Isaiah/Luke “opening blind eyes” echo implied, not quoted.
- Inaccuracy: Light — *cloak* vs KJV *garment*; no *son of Timaeus*; no *Be of good comfort, rise; he calleth thee*; paraphrase *Jesus made him well* vs Mark’s immediate sight. *Go thy way* + follow matches Mark. Death/resurrection is theological bridge (1 Cor 15), not in Mark 10:46–52 — allowed by your rules.

**Curiosity gap**  
Opens after Jerusalem/death forecast: *“Would He stop for a beggar?”* Paid off in *“And Jesus stood still”* and fully in the last line. **8/10**

**Ending**  
*“On His way to die and rise again, Jesus stood still for one man.”* Clear, alone, on death/rising without a pressure CTA. **9/10**

**Overall: 8.5/10**

## Winner

**P** — The gap question comes after *promised King* + *killed in Jerusalem*, so *“Would He stop for a beggar?”* carries real weight; Q’s earlier *“Would Jesus even stop?”* is snappier but thinner until the middle catches up.

## Top 3 fixes for P (exact lines)

1. **`"Go thy way; thy faith hath made thee whole."` … `He was free to go. He followed Jesus.`** — One plain bridge for newcomers: “Go” meant he was healed and sent, but he chose to follow anyway (Mark’s tension).

2. **`There Jesus died on a cross for our sins, the wrong we have done.`** — Tighten or soften so it doesn’t feel like a guilt push; keep death/rising without doubling “sins” + “wrong we have done.”

3. **`Jesus made him well.`** — Align with Mark: sight returned at once (“received his sight”), since “whole” in the KJV line already sounds broader than “my sight.”
