## Candidate X

**Newcomer**  
- **Who:** Jesus, a blind man named Bartimaeus, and a crowd.  
- **What happens:** People tell him to be quiet; he keeps calling Jesus; Jesus stops; Bartimaeus comes; Jesus heals his eyes; he follows Jesus toward Jerusalem.  
- **Why it matters:** Jesus listens when others try to silence someone; the story ties that road to Jesus dying and rising.  
- **Confused by:** `"thou Son of David"` (who is David to him?); `"What wilt thou that I should do unto thee?"` (hard English); `"thy faith hath made thee whole"` (does “whole” mean only eyes or everything?).

**Bible reader**  
- **Anything new:** The stress on the moving crowd and Jesus stopping for one man is a good emphasis; not a novel reading.  
- **Accuracy:** Core flow matches Mark 10:46–52 / KJV dialogue; narrator bits are fair. No Timaeus, no “hold thy peace,” no “Jesus of Nazareth” before the cry—normal compression. Healing + following fit the text.

**Curiosity gap:** **8/10** — “Watch what Jesus did” opens clearly; payoff is stop, heal, follow, then the quiet / stood-still echo.

**Overall:** **8/10**

## Candidate Y

**Newcomer**  
- **Who:** Crowd, blind Bartimaeus, Jesus.  
- **What happens:** Crowd yells “Be quiet!”; he shouts more; Jesus stops; meeting, question, sight; he follows; road to Jerusalem and Easter are named.  
- **Why it matters:** Jesus sides with the man the crowd silences.  
- **Confused by:** Same KJV lines as X; `"pushed aside"` is clear and helps.

**Bible reader**  
- **Anything new:** “Stopped for the one they told to be quiet” / “pushed aside” threads the crowd well.  
- **Accuracy:** Same as X on substance. After `[jesus] "What wilt thou…"` the line `[narrator] Jesus asked a blind man what he wanted.` is redundant with the quoted question (not unbiblical, just repeated).

**Curiosity gap:** **9/10** — Opens with conflict + “What did Jesus do?”; closes by tying quiet → pushed aside → Jesus stopped.

**Overall:** **8.5/10**

## Winner

**Y** — Slightly stronger for ESL and blind viewers: immediate “Be quiet!,” one clear question, and an ending that answers it without relying on “watch” as much as X’s bookend (which is strong but leaves the last beat on stood still, not the gospel landing).

## TOP 3 fixes for Y (exact lines)

1. **Series landing:** The gospel beat sits in the penultimate line; the last line is thematic only. Either fold death and rising into the final beat or make the last spoken line unmistakably Easter, e.g. adjust so `"He stopped for the one pushed aside."` does not close after `"rose again the third day"` without the closer still carrying that landing.  
   - Quote: `"That road went up to Jerusalem, where Jesus died for our sins and rose again the third day. He stopped for the one pushed aside."`

2. **Tighten redundancy** after the KJV question:  
   - Quote: `[narrator] Jesus asked a blind man what he wanted.` (drop or merge—Jesus’s words already ask it.)

3. **Global audience on titles** (one plain phrase before/after KJV, no changing the quote):  
   - Quote: `[bartimaeus] "Jesus, thou Son of David, have mercy on me."` — newcomers need a sliver of context (e.g. that “Son of David” means the promised king) without editing the KJV string.
