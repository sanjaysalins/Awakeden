## Candidate X

**Newcomer**
- Who is in it? A blind man named Bartimaeus, a crowd, and Jesus.
- What happens? A blind man shouts for help, the crowd tells him to shut up, but Jesus stops the whole crowd to heal his eyes, and the man follows him.
- Why does it matter? It shows Jesus cares about a single person even when everyone else ignores them.
- Confusing words: "What wilt thou that I should do unto thee?" and "made thee whole" are very old English and hard to understand quickly, though the context helps.

**Bible Reader**
- Anything new? Highlighting the specific phrase "And Jesus stood still" as a contrast to the moving crowd is a beautiful, fresh observation.
- Anything inaccurate? No, it faithfully follows Mark 10:46-52 and the KJV text. 

**Curiosity Gap**
- "Watch what Jesus did" is a very generic, templated hook. The payoff ("They said, be quiet. Jesus stood still.") is poetic but a bit dry. Score: 6/10.

**Overall Score**
- 7/10

## Candidate Y

**Newcomer**
- Who is in it? An angry crowd, a blind man named Bartimaeus, and Jesus.
- What happens? The crowd yells at a blind man to stop shouting, he yells louder, Jesus stops for him, heals him, and the man follows him to Jerusalem.
- Why does it matter? Because Jesus notices and stops for people who are rejected or pushed aside.
- Confusing words: Same old English ("wilt thou", "whole"). Also, this sequence confused me: `[jesus] "What wilt thou..." / [narrator] Jesus asked a blind man what he wanted.` The narrator explains the question *after* Jesus already asked it.

**Bible Reader**
- Anything new? Framing the entire narrative around the crowd's rejection versus Jesus's acceptance makes the story feel deeply personal and relevant.
- Anything inaccurate? The chronological sequence is missing a beat. The narrator says `[narrator] He shouted even louder.` but doesn't mention the crowd rebuking him in that specific moment (it was only mentioned in the hook), so it feels like he's just shouting twice for no reason.

**Curiosity Gap**
- Starting *in media res* with `[crowd] "Be quiet!"` is a fantastic hook. The payoff (`"He stopped for the one pushed aside."`) perfectly answers the opening question (`"What did Jesus do?"`). Score: 9/10.

**Overall Score**
- 8.5/10

## Winner and Why
**Candidate Y wins.** The cold-open audio hook of the crowd shouting is much more engaging for a vertical short, and the thematic landing ("stopped for the one pushed aside") connects the healing to the cross much more powerfully than X.

## Top 3 Fixes for Candidate Y
1. **Fix the backward dialogue attribution:** Cut the redundant narrator line that comes *after* the quote. 
   *Quote to fix:* Remove `[narrator] Jesus asked a blind man what he wanted.` entirely. The dialogue speaks for itself.
2. **Fix the missing chronological beat:** Add the crowd's rebuke back into the middle so his louder shout makes sense.
   *Quote to fix:* Change `[narrator] He shouted even louder.` to `[narrator] Many told him to be quiet, but he shouted even louder.`
3. **Smooth the gospel transition:** The handoff to the cross is slightly clunky and repetitive with the word "road".
   *Quote to fix:* Change `[narrator] At once he could see, and he followed Jesus on the road. That road went up to Jerusalem, where Jesus died...` to `[narrator] At once he could see. He followed Jesus up the road to Jerusalem, where Jesus died...`
