# Blind test — seat: Fable

## Candidate X

**Newcomer**
- Who: a blind beggar, Bartimaeus. A crowd. Jesus.
- What: he shouts for Jesus. The crowd says be quiet. He shouts louder. Jesus stops, asks what he wants, and he can see. He follows Jesus.
- Why it matters: Jesus stops for one man the crowd wanted silent. Then Jesus goes on to die and rise.
- Confusing:
  - "thou Son of David" — who is David? Why call Jesus that? Never explained.
  - "thy faith hath made thee whole" — "whole" is unclear. The next line ("At once he could see") saves it.
  - "What wilt thou that I should do unto thee?" — hard English, but the line before it ("Jesus asked the blind man what he wanted") tells me the meaning first. Good.
  - "die for our sins" — arrives fast, no link to the story. I accept it but do not feel it.

**Bible reader**
- New: "A great crowd was moving, and He stopped for one man" — the stillness as the centre of the story. And the sting of asking a blind man what he wants. Small but real.
- Accuracy:
  - All four quotes are word-for-word KJV. "And Jesus stood still" is also exact (v49).
  - "jumped up" — KJV says "rose". Other versions say "sprang up". Minor stretch.
  - Skips "commanded him to be called" (v49). So "and came" is a little sudden: a blind man comes with no call.
  - "up the road to Jerusalem" — KJV says "followed Jesus in the way". Jerusalem is fair from Mark 10:32-33 and 11:1. OK.
  - "would die ... and rise again the third day" — sound (1 Cor 15:3-4; Mark 10:34).

**Curiosity gap: 8/10**
- Opens: "Watch what Jesus did." Clear question. A command, not a question, but it works for video.
- Pays off: yes, twice — the pause + "Jesus stood still", and the last line mirrors the first line word for word.

**Overall: 8/10**

## Candidate Y

**Newcomer**
- Who: a crowd, a blind man Bartimaeus, Jesus.
- What: same story.
- Why it matters: "He stopped for the one pushed aside."
- Confusing:
  - "He shouted even louder." — louder than what? The crowd's "stop" was two lines back, before his first shout. The order feels mixed. I hear the "Be quiet!" before I know anyone is shouting.
  - "What wilt thou that I should do unto thee?" — comes with no help first. The plain meaning comes only after. For a second-language listener that is the wrong order.
  - "thou Son of David" — same problem as X, not explained.
  - "thy faith hath made thee whole" — same as X.
  - The last narrator line is long: sight, following, road, Jerusalem, death, rising, and the moral, all in one breath.

**Bible reader**
- New: "He stopped for the one they told to be quiet" is a good line. "Jesus asked a blind man what he wanted" lands a bit harder here, said after the quote.
- Accuracy:
  - [crowd] "Be quiet!" in quote marks as a spoken voice. The KJV has no direct crowd speech here ("many charged him that he should hold his peace"). An invented quote sits next to word-for-word KJV quotes. That blurs the series rule.
  - Drops "Many" — the hook says "the crowd told him". KJV says "many", not all. Small.
  - "jumped up" and the missing call — same as X.
  - "died ... and rose again the third day" — sound.
  - The film ends on "He stopped for the one pushed aside", a moral tag after the death and rising. Softer landing on Jesus than X's (X ends on "Jesus stood still").

**Curiosity gap: 7/10**
- Opens: the shout "Be quiet!" is a strong first second. "What did Jesus do?" is a clear question.
- Pays off: yes at "And Jesus stood still". But the end swaps to new words ("pushed aside") and does not close the loop as cleanly.

**Overall: 7/10**

## Winner: X

X is easier to follow in order, and it explains the hard KJV question before it is spoken. Its last line answers its first line in the same words. Y has the better first second, but it uses an invented crowd quote and its order confuses a newcomer.

## Top 3 fixes for X

1. **"He heard Jesus was passing, and shouted:"** — a newcomer does not know "Son of David". Add a plain cue in the narrator line, for example: "He heard Jesus was passing, and shouted the name of the promised King:". The KJV quote stays untouched.

2. **"Bartimaeus threw off his cloak, jumped up, and came."** — add the call and match the KJV verb: "Jesus called for him. Bartimaeus threw off his cloak, got up, and came." Now a blind man has a reason to come, and "jumped" no longer goes past "rose".

3. **"He followed Jesus up the road to Jerusalem, where Jesus would die for our sins, and rise again the third day."** — the cross arrives with no link to the story, and the film's very last words are the callback, not the death and rising. Tie them with the story's own picture and put the callback first, for example: "They said, be quiet. Jesus stood still. And the man followed Him up the road to Jerusalem, where Jesus would die for our sins, and rise again the third day." If the callback must stay last, keep X's order but that is a call for the owner against the series rule.

Smaller note: X says "told him to be quiet" twice in the first four lines (hook, then "Many told him to be quiet"). It is fine for clarity. Do not cut the second one; it is the accurate KJV "many".
