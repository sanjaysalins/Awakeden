## Candidate W

**Newcomer:** Blind man Bartimaeus by the road near Jericho; people tell him to be quiet; he keeps shouting to Jesus; Jesus stops, heals his eyes; he follows Jesus. It matters because Jesus cared for one person on the way to save everyone. Confused by: *"What wilt thou that I should do unto thee?"*, *"thy faith hath made thee whole"*, and *"Son of David"* (explained, but still heavy).

**Bible reader:** Clear retelling; Mark’s dialogue is verbatim; cloak throw, stand still, sight, follow—all match. Nothing fresh beyond “promised King” and Jerusalem/death foreshadow (Mark 10:32–34, not in 46–52). No “son of Timaeus,” no “Be of good comfort, rise”—acceptable shortenings.

**Curiosity gap:** Opens with *"Would Jesus even stop?"*; pays when *"And Jesus stood still"* and the crowd-vs-one contrast. **8/10**

**Ending:** Cross and rising are clear two lines before the close, but the **last** line is *"And He stopped for one man"*—gospel facts are not the final beat. **6/10**

**Overall: 7.5/10**

## Candidate X

**Newcomer:** Same story—beggar, shout, Jesus stops, sight, follow. End talks about Jerusalem, cross, rising, and stopping for one man, but it’s **one long block**—hard to track. Confused by: same KJV lines as W, plus *"for our sins, the wrong we have done"* (two phrases, same idea).

**Bible reader:** Accurate to Mark; follow-on-road preserved in one sentence. Jerusalem/cross/risen compressed into the tag—doctrinally fine, dramaturgically crowded.

**Curiosity gap:** Same opening question; payoff at stand still, but the ending doesn’t return to the question—it **states** the moral in one gulp. **7/10**

**Ending:** Death and rising appear inside the last sentence, but the line still **lands** on *"stood still for one man"*, not on rising as the final word. **5/10**

**Overall: 6.5/10**

## Candidate Y

**Newcomer:** Bartimaeus, road, shout, heal, follow; then Jerusalem, cross, third day, then Jesus stopped for one man. Steps are **separated**—easier than X. Confused by: *"What wilt thou…"* / *"made thee whole"* (KJV); *"for our sins, the wrong we have done"* is mildly redundant.

**Bible reader:** Matches Mark; adds Mark 10 passion prediction (*"told His followers He would be killed there"*). Clean follow after *"Go thy way."* Pause before the recap helps pacing.

**Curiosity gap:** *"Would Jesus even stop?"* → louder cry → *"stood still"*; final line ties die/rise **and** the one-man stop. **8.5/10**

**Ending:** Cross and rising get their own lines; last line folds in *"die and rise for us"* but still closes on *"one man"*—strong **bookend**, slightly weak on series rule if the last word must be resurrection. **7/10**

**Overall: 8/10**

## Candidate Z

**Newcomer:** Bartimaeus, king title explained, Jesus walking to Jerusalem where He would be killed—**Would He stop for one man?** Then heal, follow, cross, rise, stopped for one man. Story feels **one thread**; less “many told him quiet” tension after line 1. Confused by: KJV question line; *"our sins"* without much setup (ok for short).

**Bible reader:** Accurate; passion context in the **middle** (not only at tag); good Mark 10:32–34 echo. Slightly drops the second crowd-shush beat before the louder cry (minor).

**Curiosity gap:** Question in the **Jerusalem setup** (*"Would He stop for one man?"*) is sharper than a generic “would Jesus stop”; paid at stand still and echoed in the closer. **9/10**

**Ending:** Cross and rise as short lines; last line again bookends *"stood still for one man"*—memorable, not a pure death/rise **landing**. **6.5/10**

**Overall: 8/10**

## Rank (best first)

1. **Y** — Best **global pacing**: short beats, clear gospel lines, strong gap/payoff; only slightly loses to Z on mid-story curiosity.

2. **Z** — Best **hook architecture** (Jerusalem + “one man?”); ending a bit thinner on plain-language “wrong we have done” than Y.

3. **W** — Solid and clear; Jerusalem/death split is good; opening gap slightly weaker than Z; ending same bookend issue as Y/Z.

4. **X** — Faithful but **one-sentence ending** is hardest for ESL and weakest curiosity payoff.

## TOP 3 fixes for the winner (Y)

1. **Last line (series ending rule):** After  
   *"On the third day, He rose to life."*  
   either end there, or add one **final** plain line on rising (e.g. he lives)—not only  
   *"On His way to die and rise for us, Jesus stood still for one man."*

2. **Redundant sin phrase:** In  
   *"There Jesus died on a cross for our sins, the wrong we have done."*  
   pick **one** plain phrase for a global audience (keep theology, cut repetition).

3. **ESL bridge before KJV closure:** Before  
   *[jesus] "Go thy way; thy faith hath made thee whole."*  
   one short narrator line in simple English that “faith” here means **trust in Jesus**—so newcomers aren’t lost on the landing quote.
