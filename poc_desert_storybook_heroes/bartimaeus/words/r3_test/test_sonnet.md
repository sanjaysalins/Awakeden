# Blind test: Sonnet seat

Shared notes. Newcomer words that trip everyone, in all four: "mercy", "cloak", "faith", "made thee whole" (KJV, locked), and Jericho vs Jerusalem (two long names that look alike). "Son of David meant God's promised King" is explained in all four, good.

## W
- Newcomer: Who: a blind beggar Bartimaeus, Jesus, a crowd. What: he shouts, Jesus stops, heals him, he follows. Why it matters: Jesus was heading to die, yet stopped for one man. Confusing: "He was free to go" (free from what?). "He was on His way to die for us" comes AFTER we were told He died, so the tense jumps back. Clear plus: "He had said men would kill Him there" sets the stakes early.
- Bible reader: New and good: ties to Mark 10:32-34 (the prediction) in one line. Nothing inaccurate. "Men would kill Him" is faithful.
- Curiosity gap: opens at "Would Jesus even stop?", stakes added by the killing line, paid at "Jesus stood still". 8/10.
- Ending: "He was on His way to die for us. And He stopped for one man." Lands, and the last line stands alone. But the rising is not in the last line, and the tense jump blurs it a little. 7.5/10.
- Overall: 8/10.

## X
- Newcomer: Who/what is clear. Why: weakest. Jerusalem and the killing are first said at the very end, in one long block. "Would Jesus even stop?" has no stake when it is asked, so the question is soft. The last paragraph is a dense run (road, die, sins, third day, rise, cross, one man). A second-language ear will lose the thread. "Would die ... would rise" future tense is harder.
- Bible reader: Accurate. Nothing new. The compressed ending reads like a creed list.
- Curiosity gap: opens at "stop?", pays at "stood still", but the bigger gap (why does the stop matter) is only answered in a rush. 6/10.
- Ending: Last line is good, but it arrives inside a crowded paragraph, not alone. No pause, no breathing room for the death and rising. 5.5/10.
- Overall: 6.5/10.

## Y
- Newcomer: Who/what/why all clear. Stakes come before the shout ("That road climbs to Jerusalem. Jesus had told His followers He would be killed there."). "the wrong we have done" explains "sins" in plain words. Short lines, simple past tense. Slightly confusing: "he shouted even louder" right after the Jesus line (who is "he"?). "rise for us" is a little odd.
- Bible reader: Accurate to Mark 10 and 10:32-34. "Followers" for disciples is fine. The last line names both dying and rising, which matches the series rule. Nothing wrong.
- Curiosity gap: opens at "Would Jesus even stop?", deepened by the killing line, paid at "stood still", then paid again at the last line. 8.5/10.
- Ending: pause, then one line with die, rise, and "stood still for one man". Lands alone and clear. 8.5/10.
- Overall: 8.5/10.

## Z
- Newcomer: Strong order: the stakes and the question sit together ("...where He knew He would be killed. Would He stop for one man?"). That is the tightest gap of the four. But "died on a cross for our sins" has no plain-words gloss. "Sins" is churchy for a newcomer. The opening "Many told the blind beggar to be quiet" has no question yet.
- Bible reader: Accurate. "Where He knew He would be killed" is faithful.
- Curiosity gap: opens and is loaded in one beat, paid at "stood still". 8/10.
- Ending: "On His way to the cross, Jesus stood still for one man." Clear and short, but the line names only the cross, not the rising. 7/10.
- Overall: 7.5/10.

## Rank
1. Y: stakes first, plain-words "wrong we have done", a pause, and a last line with both death and rising.
2. W: best stake line, but the ending's tense jump and the missing rising in the last line hold it back.
3. Z: best question placement, but "sins" is unexplained and the ending is thinner.
4. X: all the weight is dumped in one end paragraph; the gap has no stake until too late.

## Top 3 fixes for Y
1. Line: "But he shouted even louder." The line before is about Jesus and the road, so "he" can read as Jesus. Change to: "But Bartimaeus shouted even louder."
2. Line: "On His way to die and rise for us, Jesus stood still for one man." "Rise for us" is odd for a newcomer. Try: "On His way to die and live again, Jesus stood still for one man."
3. Line: "That road climbs to Jerusalem. Jesus had told His followers He would be killed there." Jericho and Jerusalem blur, and the stake comes before the listener knows why it matters. Try: "Jesus was walking to the city of Jerusalem. He had told His followers He would be killed there." (Keep "Would Jesus even stop?" before it, or move the question after it, as Z does.)
