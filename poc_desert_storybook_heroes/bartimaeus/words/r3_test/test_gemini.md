### Candidate W
**Newcomer:**
- **Who:** A blind beggar named Bartimaeus and Jesus.
- **What:** Bartimaeus calls for help, the crowd tries to silence him, but Jesus stops and heals his sight.
- **Why:** To show that even as Jesus was walking to His death, He cared enough to stop for one insignificant person.
- **Confusing:** "whole" in "thy faith hath made thee whole." To a non-native speaker, "whole" usually means "entire," not "healed." (I know KJV is locked, but it might trip them up). Also, the transition "He had said men would kill Him there. / But the beggar shouted..." is confusing because the "but" contrasts with the crowd, not Jesus's death.

**Bible reader:**
- **Anything new:** The contrast between Jesus's cosmic mission (walking to Jerusalem to die, Mark 10:32) and His micro-compassion (stopping for one beggar, Mark 10:46).
- **Inaccurate:** Nothing. The KJV quotes match Mark 10 perfectly.

**Curiosity gap:**
- Opens at "Would Jesus even stop?" but loses momentum because the script immediately pivots to explain his name and Jerusalem. Paid off at "And Jesus stood still." 
- **Score:** 7/10

**Ending:**
- "He was on His way to die for us. And He stopped for one man." Clean, beautifully paced, lands well.
- **Score:** 8/10

**Overall score:** 7.5/10

### Rankings & Fixes

**Rank:** **Z, Y, W, X**
Candidate Z wins because it constructs the curiosity gap perfectly. By pairing the stakes ("walking... where He knew He would be killed") directly with the hook ("Would He stop for one man?"), the moment Jesus stands still carries immense narrative and theological weight. 

**TOP 3 Fixes for Candidate Z (the Winner):**

1. **Fix the disconnected flow between the crowd and the shout.** The crowd silences him in line 2, but he doesn't shout back until line 5. 
   - *Quote to fix:* Move `[narrator] Many told the blind beggar to be quiet.` down to right before `[narrator] Bartimaeus shouted even louder.`
2. **Clarify "sins" for the global/ESL audience.** W, X, and Y used a great plain-English appositive that Z dropped. 
   - *Quote to fix:* Change `[narrator] There Jesus died on a cross for our sins.` to `[narrator] There Jesus died on a cross for our sins, the wrong we have done.`
3. **Add contrast to the shout.** Once you move the crowd's silencing down next to Bartimaeus's shout, it needs a conjunction to hit the resistance hard.
   - *Quote to fix:* Change `[narrator] Bartimaeus shouted even louder.` to `[narrator] But Bartimaeus shouted even louder.`
