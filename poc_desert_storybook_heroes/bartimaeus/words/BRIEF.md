# Write ONE narration from scratch — Blind Bartimaeus (Mark 10:46-52). You are ONE blind writer; you have seen no other draft.

## Who it is for
Worldwide, many with English as a second language, many with no Bible knowledge. People who feel unheard, pushed aside, told to "be quiet" or "you don't matter". Also Bible readers who know the story but have never noticed its turns.

## The chosen angle (owner-approved; binding)
SPINE: "They told him to be quiet. Jesus stood still."
- A blind beggar sits by the road out of Jericho. He hears Jesus is passing and cries out: "Jesus, thou Son of David, have mercy on me" (10:47).
- "Many charged him that he should hold his peace" — many told him to be quiet. "But he cried the more a great deal" (10:48).
- "And Jesus stood still, and commanded him to be called" (10:49). Jesus was on the move, with a great crowd — and stopped for the one they tried to hush.
- He threw off his cloak, jumped up, came to Jesus (10:50).
- The question: "What wilt thou that I should do unto thee?" (10:51) — Jesus asks a blind man what he wants. Let him ask; let the man say it: "Lord, that I might receive my sight."
- "Go thy way; thy faith hath made thee whole." Immediately he saw — "and followed Jesus in the way" (10:52).
OPTIONAL depth (use at most one, only if it fits plainly): (a) "Son of David" was a name for the promised King from King David's family — the blind man called Jesus that before he could see Him; (b) the road Jesus was on went up to Jerusalem, where (as Jesus had just told His friends, 10:32-34) He would be killed and rise the third day — so the man followed Him on that road.
ENDING: lands on Jesus — He stops for the ones others push aside; He went on up that road to die for our sins and rose again the third day (1 Cor 15:3-4 or Mark 10:34, may be said plainly). Earned by the spine. Grace, not pressure.

## Hard rules
1. First 3 seconds: plain words; open the curiosity gap (the crowd said "be quiet" — what did Jesus do?).
2. Name who's who simply: Bartimaeus, a blind man begging by the road near the town of Jericho; the crowd; Jesus.
3. KJV quotes verbatim and short. Never let an old word ("charged", "hold his peace", "whole", "wilt thou") carry the meaning alone; plain English around it.
4. No invented feelings or reasons ("he felt worthless", "Jesus saw his pain"). Tell what the text says.
5. No stock closer ("come to Jesus", "just ask Him", "He's waiting", "will you...?", "call out to Him today"). No fear, no pressure, no blanket promise (do not promise every viewer a healing).
6. Never blame "the Jews" or any people group: "the crowd", "many".
7. 110-140 spoken words (about 45-58 s). One line per voice: [narrator], [jesus], [bartimaeus], [crowd]. Mark one (pause).
8. No wording about AI or how the film is made. No hashtags.

## Output (only this)
(a) the script; (b) word count; (c) KJV fragments with verse refs; (d) the 4 questions, one line each: who it is for, main take-away, the curiosity gap, why a global newcomer gets it.
