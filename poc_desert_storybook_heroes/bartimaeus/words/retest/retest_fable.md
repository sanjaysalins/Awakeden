# Retest — Fable seat (blind, words only)

## 1. Newcomer (no Bible, English second language)
- **Who:** A blind beggar, Bartimaeus. Jesus. A crowd.
- **What:** He shouts for Jesus. People tell him to be quiet. He shouts louder. Jesus stops, calls him, asks what he wants. He asks to see. He sees, and follows Jesus.
- **Why it matters:** Jesus stops for one man nobody wanted to hear. Then Jesus goes on to die and rise. I get the first part clearly. The second part I only half get.
- **Confusing words / lines:**
  - "thy faith hath made thee whole" — "whole" = healed? Not clear. "faith" in what? Nobody explains. The next line ("At once he could see") saves it, but only just.
  - "have mercy on me" — "mercy" is not an easy word; opens the film cold with "thou".
  - "die for our sins" — "sins" is not explained, and "our" comes from nowhere. Why would His death be about me?
  - "What wilt thou that I should do unto thee?" — hard, but the narrator says the meaning first ("asked the blind man what he wanted"). Good. This works.
  - "Son of David" — explained. Good.

## 2. Bible reader
- **Anything new?** A little. "A whole crowd was moving. He stopped for one man" is a nice plain read of "stood still". Placing the story on the road up to Jerusalem (Mark 10:32-34 just before) is a true and useful link. Not a big surprise, but honest.
- **Accuracy:** All five KJV quotes are word-for-word (10:47, 48, 49a, 51, 52a). Cloak, "at once", "followed" all match. "Rise again the third day" fits 10:34. "Up the road to Jerusalem" is a fair reading of "in the way" (10:32, 11:1), not stated in v.52 itself — acceptable.
- **Small notes (not errors):**
  - v.48 says "many", line 4 says "The crowd" — fine as a summary.
  - Left out: the crowd's change, "Be of good comfort, rise; he calleth thee" (v.49). Not needed, but it is the best free twist in the passage: the same people who said "be quiet" now say "get up".
  - "Near Jericho" — Mark says leaving Jericho. Fine.
- **Doctrine:** Sound. No pressure closer. Lands on death and rising as required.

## 3. Curiosity gap — 6/10
- Opens a question: "What did Jesus do?" Clear, but soft and generic. Low stakes — most viewers guess "He helped him".
- Pays off: yes, plainly — "And Jesus stood still." The callback in the last line ("They told him to be quiet. Jesus stood still for him.") closes the loop well.
- The gap is answered at the midpoint, so nothing pulls the viewer through the second half.

## 4. Overall — 7/10
Clear, accurate, simple. A newcomer gets who/what. The "why" is strong for the healing, weak for the cross. The ending is true but reads as attached, not earned.

## TOP 3 fixes

1. **The landing does not connect to the story.**
   Line: "The man followed Him up the road to Jerusalem, where Jesus would die for our sins, and rise again the third day."
   Fix: carry "stopped for one man" into the cross, and say "sins" in plain words. E.g. "He followed Jesus up the road to Jerusalem. There, the One who stopped for one man would die for the wrong in all of us, and rise again the third day."

2. **The hook is repeated, and the question is flat.**
   Lines: "The crowd said, be quiet. What did Jesus do?" and then "Many told him to be quiet. But he shouted even louder." (also said a third time in the last line).
   Fix: say "be quiet" once in the hook and once in the close. Use line 6 for something new — "But he shouted even louder" alone is enough. Sharpen the question, e.g. "Everyone told him to be quiet. Would Jesus even stop?" — then "And Jesus stood still" hits harder.

3. **"Faith" and "whole" are left unexplained.**
   Lines: "Go thy way; thy faith hath made thee whole." / "At once he could see."
   Fix: one plain bridge after the quote (KJV untouched), e.g. "He trusted Jesus, and Jesus healed him. At once he could see."

Minor: ~165 words with a pause is tight for 60 s; fix 2 frees room for fixes 1 and 3.
