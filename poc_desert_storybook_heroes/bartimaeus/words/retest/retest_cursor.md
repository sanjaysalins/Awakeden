## 1. Newcomer (no Bible background, ESL)

**Who:** A blind poor man named Bartimaeus, Jesus, and a crowd on a road.

**What happens:** The man shouts for help. People tell him to stop. He shouts more. Jesus stops and listens, asks what he wants, gives him sight. Then he walks with Jesus toward Jerusalem.

**Why it matters (as told):** Jesus notices one person when everyone else tries to silence him; faith connects to healing; the road leads to Jesus dying and rising for sins.

**Confused me (quoted):**  
- `"What wilt thou that I should do unto thee?"` — I don’t know **wilt** / **thee**.  
- `"thy faith hath made thee whole"` after he asked only for **sight** — does **whole** mean eyes only or everything?  
- **Son of David** is explained, good; **Jericho** is just a place name, no context.  
- **cloak** — not sure what he threw off.

## 2. Bible reader (Mark 10:46–52, KJV)

**Anything new:** Nice emphasis: moving crowd vs **Jesus stood still**; **Son of David** as king title; tie from healing to **followed … toward Jerusalem** and passion/resurrection (Mark has him follow **in the way** — this is a fair gospel frame for the series rules).

**Inaccuracy vs KJV:** Spoken lines match Mark 10:47–48, 51–52 KJV well. Omissions are normal for 60s (Timaeus, **Be of good comfort, rise; he calleth thee**, disciples calling Bartimaeus). Narrator’s **die for our sins, and rise again the third day** is theological summary, not a misquote of the pericope.

---

## Curiosity gap: **8/10**

Opens: *“What did Jesus do?”* after crowd vs beggar. Pays off: stood still, called him, healed, followed toward Jerusalem → death and resurrection. Slight stretch: middle is mostly “what happened” retelling, not a lingering mystery.

---

## Overall: **8/10**

Clear arc, respectful tone, KJV lines in place, ending fits series rules (Christ’s death and rising, no “come to Jesus” pressure). Archaic quotes and **whole** vs **sight** are the main global-audience friction; crowd/“be quiet” beats repeat.

1. **After** `"Go thy way; thy faith hath made thee whole."` — add one plain narrator line (not changing KJV): e.g. that **whole** here means healed / sight restored, so ESL listeners aren’t left guessing.  
   *Quote to anchor:* `"Go thy way; thy faith hath made thee whole."`

2. **Trim or merge** the second crowd-silence beat — it echoes the hook.  
   *Quote:* `Many told him to be quiet. But he shouted even louder.`  
   (Keep the hook line: `The crowd said, be quiet. What did Jesus do?`)

3. **Soften the jump** from miracle to atonement with one short bridge (still landing on death/resurrection).  
   *Quote:* `The man followed Him up the road to Jerusalem, where Jesus would die for our sins, and rise again the third day.`  
   (e.g. one line that he didn’t go home—he went with Jesus on the way to Passover/cross, *then* the death-and-rising line.)
