Newcomer: Bartimaeus is a blind beggar. The crowd tries to silence him; Jesus stops, listens, and heals him. It matters because someone the crowd dismisses receives attention and help. “Whole” confused me briefly; “At once he could see” explained it. “Our sins” needs a little explanation.

Bible reader: No new facts, but the emphasis on “Jesus stood still” made that detail stand out. The KJV quotations are accurate. Following Jesus specifically toward Jerusalem is a plausible extension; these verses only say he followed Jesus “in the way.”

Curiosity gap: **8/10.** “What did Jesus do?” opens a clear question. His stopping and healing answer it. The death-and-resurrection ending feels slightly rushed.

Overall: **8/10.** Clear, moving, and easy to follow, with a few language and transition issues.

Top 3 fixes:

1. **“The man followed Him up the road to Jerusalem, where Jesus would die for our sins, and rise again the third day.”**  
   Separate Bartimaeus’s recorded action from Jesus’s destination, and explain “sins”: “Bartimaeus followed Jesus. Jesus was heading to Jerusalem, where he would die for our sins—the wrong we do—and rise again the third day.”

2. **“"Son of David" was a name for the promised King.”**  
   “Name” may sound like another personal name. Use: “‘Son of David’ was a title for God’s promised King.”

3. **“Many told him to be quiet. But he shouted even louder.”**  
   The opening already establishes this. Shorten to: “But Bartimaeus shouted even louder.” That leaves more breathing room for the stopping, healing, and ending.
