**1. Newcomer (No Bible knowledge, ESL)**
*   **Who is in it?** A blind beggar named Bartimaeus, an angry crowd, and Jesus (a promised King).
*   **What happens?** The blind man shouts for Jesus to help him. The crowd tells him to shut up, but he shouts louder. Instead of ignoring him, Jesus stops the whole moving crowd just for him, asks what he wants, and heals his blindness. The man then follows Jesus. 
*   **Why does it matter?** It shows that Jesus pays attention to ignored people when everyone else tells them to be quiet, and it introduces that this King ultimately sacrificed Himself for our sins.
*   **Confusing words:** Because English is my second language, the old words in the quotes were a bit hard to process quickly: *"What wilt thou that I should do unto thee?"* and *"thy faith hath made thee whole"* (using "whole" instead of "healed"). I understood it from the context, but it tripped me up for a second.

**2. Bible Reader**
*   **Anything new?** Yes. Emphasizing the contrast of the momentum of the crowd against the phrase *"And Jesus stood still"* is a sharp, fresh angle. Also, explicitly tying the end of the story (Bartimaeus following Him on the road) directly to Jesus's destination—the cross in Jerusalem—is fantastic contextual framing that usually gets left out of this Sunday School story.
*   **Anything inaccurate?** No. The narrative perfectly tracks Mark 10:46-52, and the KJV quotes for verses 47, 49, 51, and 52 are perfectly accurate. 

**3. Curiosity Gap**
*   **Score: 8/10**. 
*   The start opens a direct, simple gap: *"What did Jesus do?"* The ending pays it off with a beautiful mirrored callback: *"They told him to be quiet. Jesus stood still for him."* It answers the immediate question while pivoting smoothly to His ultimate act of salvation.

**4. Overall Score**
*   **Score: 8/10**. It is a tight, touching, and highly effective short script that obeys all series rules.

**TOP 3 FIXES**

1.  **Remove the redundant recap right after the hook.**
    *   *Line:* `[narrator] A blind beggar shouted for Jesus. The crowd said, be quiet. What did Jesus do?`
    *   *Fix:* We literally just heard him shout in the first second. Make it tighter: `[narrator] The crowd told the blind beggar to be quiet. What did Jesus do?`
2.  **Smooth out the exposition facts.**
    *   *Line:* `[narrator] His name was Bartimaeus. He sat begging by the road near Jericho. "Son of David" was a name for the promised King.`
    *   *Fix:* This reads slightly like a Wikipedia list of three separate facts. Blend them to maintain momentum: `His name was Bartimaeus, a beggar by the road in Jericho who knew "Son of David" meant the promised King.`
3.  **Bridge the ending to the cross more naturally.**
    *   *Line:* `The man followed Him up the road to Jerusalem, where Jesus would die for our sins, and rise again the third day.`
    *   *Fix:* The pivot to the gospel landing feels just a fraction sudden. Tie it directly to the physical road they are now walking on: `The man followed Him up that very road to Jerusalem—the place where Jesus would die for our sins, and rise again the third day.`
