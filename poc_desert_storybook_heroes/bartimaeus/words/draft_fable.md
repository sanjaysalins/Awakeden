## (a) Script

[narrator] A crowd told a blind man to be quiet. Watch what Jesus did.

[narrator] His name was Bartimaeus. He sat begging by the road, near the town of Jericho. He heard Jesus was passing, and shouted:

[bartimaeus] Jesus, thou Son of David, have mercy on me.

[narrator] Many told him to be quiet. He shouted louder.

[narrator] "And Jesus stood still." (pause) He stopped, and called for him.

[narrator] Bartimaeus threw off his cloak, jumped up, and came. Then Jesus asked the blind man what he wanted:

[jesus] What wilt thou that I should do unto thee?

[bartimaeus] Lord, that I might receive my sight.

[jesus] Go thy way; thy faith hath made thee whole.

[narrator] At once he could see. He followed Jesus up the road to Jerusalem, where Jesus would die for our sins, and rise again the third day.

[narrator] They said, be quiet. Jesus stood still.

## (d) The 4 questions

- Who it is for: anyone who has been told to be quiet or pushed aside, plus Bible readers who never noticed that Jesus stopped a whole journey for one man.
- Main take-away: the crowd hushed him, but Jesus stood still for him, and then went on up that road to die for our sins and rise again.
- The curiosity gap: a crowd told a blind man to be quiet, so what did Jesus do? Paid off by "And Jesus stood still" and closed again in the last line.
- Why a global newcomer gets it: short plain words, three clear players (a blind beggar, a crowd, Jesus), and every old KJV line has plain English right beside it ("asked the blind man what he wanted", "at once he could see").
