## 1. WHO IS THIS FOR
A named viewer: Amara, 24, in Lagos or Manila, English is her second language, scrolling Shorts, knows "Jesus" but not Mark or Jericho. She stops at the first 3 s (line 1: a crying blind man, red letters, ripples). She finishes because of the pause at line 7 ("stood still", music stops). She shares with a friend who feels ignored. Not served: a viewer wanting teaching depth, a skeptic who rejects the cross claim, a child under about 8 (the death and tomb lines come fast), and anyone with sound off who misses the oud and silence beat.

## 2. MAIN TAKEAWAY
"Jesus, on His way to die, stopped for one blind beggar who would not be quiet." Carried by line 7 ("He stopped for one man, and called for him") and line 17 (the same words, repeated). The film makes it clear. The risk is that the sentence has two ideas (stopping, and dying and rising), and lines 14-16 move quickly from the healing to the cross. Line 17 joins them, but a newcomer may only half-feel why dying and stopping belong together.

## 3. GLOBAL AUDIENCE
Who: Bartimaeus, a blind beggar. What: he shouts, the crowd hushes him, Jesus stops and heals him. Why: Jesus cares about one person, even on His way to die. My own words after one watch: "Jesus stopped for a blind man who kept shouting, healed him, then went on to die and come back to life." I got it.
Where I could get lost:
- Line 3 / line 4: "Son of David meant God's promised King." This is explained on screen and spoken, which is good. But it comes after the cry has already been heard twice, so the first 10 s are unexplained.
- Line 4: "He had told His followers He would be killed there." There is no picture of this, only a walking crowd. A newcomer may not know why Jerusalem matters until line 15 shows the cross mark.
- Line 8: "threw off his cloak." The picture shows it, but the meaning (leaving his only possession) is missing. It is fine.
- Line 15: "for our sins, for the wrong things we have done." A good plain gloss.
- Line 16: "rose to life." Clear picture, but a newcomer is given no reason to believe it. It is a claim, not a story.
Fail in translation: "mercy" (line 1, 6) is never glossed and is the title cry. "Whole" (line 11, "made thee whole") is archaic and the on-screen text keeps it. "Wilt thou" and "thy way" are KJV and only partly clear. "Jericho" and "Jerusalem" may blur together for a non-native ear. The word "sight" as an empty outline (line 10) is a smart picture that needs no translation.

## 4. BIBLICALLY ACCURATE
- Line 3 "sat begging by the road near Jericho": RIGHT (Mark 10:46).
- Line 3/4 "Son of David meant God's promised King": RIGHT, a fair gloss (messianic title).
- Line 2 "Many told ... to be quiet": RIGHT (v.48, "many charged him").
- Line 4 "road to Jerusalem ... He had told His followers He would be killed there": RIGHT (Mark 10:32-34).
- Line 7 "And Jesus stood still" quoted, "called for him": RIGHT (v.49). "A whole crowd was moving" is a fair inference.
- Line 8 "threw off his cloak and came": RIGHT (v.50). The red colour of the cloak is not in the text, so it is an artistic detail, not a claim.
- Lines 9-11: RIGHT, KJV matches v.51-52.
- Line 12 "He trusted Jesus. Jesus made him well": RIGHT in sense ("thy faith hath made thee whole"). Slightly flat; "whole" can mean saved, and this narrows it to physical healing.
- Line 13 "Jesus told him to go his way. But Bartimaeus followed": RIGHT (v.52, "followed Jesus in the way").
- Line 14 "followed ... up the road to Jerusalem": OVERCLAIMED slightly. The text says "in the way", and the context implies Jerusalem, but it does not say he went there. UNSUPPORTED that he walked "side by side" with Jesus in the last picture.
- Line 15 "died on a cross for our sins": RIGHT (1 Cor 15:3).
- Line 16 "on the third day, He rose": RIGHT (1 Cor 15:4).
- Line 17 "On His way to die and rise again, Jesus stood still for one man": RIGHT as a summary of Mark 10:32-34 and v.49.
- "He is smiling" (picture, 44.8 s): UNSUPPORTED but harmless.

## 5. BEST VERSION POSSIBLE
The best explainer would give the viewer a reason to feel the cost of stopping. Here, Jesus is walking toward His death, but the film mostly tells this once (line 4) and shows it with a small dotted-road picture. The best version would let that tension return right before line 7, for example one beat of Jerusalem or the cross mark flashing back as the music stops. Then "stood still" would feel heavier. The ONE highest-value change: seed the cross inside the stop, so the viewer feels "He is going to die, and He still stopped."
Score: 7.5/10. Strong structure, a real silence beat, and a clean landing. It loses points for a rushed 56-69 s stretch and for the unglossed "mercy."

## CURIOSITY GAP
Opens at line 4 ("Would He stop for a beggar?"); pays off at line 7 ("And Jesus stood still"), then deepens at line 17. Score 8/10. Worth the wait: the question is asked early, answered at 26 s with a music drop, and the last line reframes it. A weakness is that the wait from 15 to 26 s is the same cry repeated.

## THREE SMALLEST FIXES
1. Line 1 / 6: add a short gloss for "mercy". Replace the narrator line 5 "But Bartimaeus shouted even louder." with "But Bartimaeus shouted even louder, asking for mercy: please help me."
2. Line 12: "He trusted Jesus. Jesus made him well." becomes "He trusted Jesus. Jesus made him well and whole." This keeps the KJV word "whole" and ties it to line 11.
3. Line 14: "Up the road to Jerusalem." becomes "Up the road toward Jerusalem." This avoids implying that Mark says he arrived there.
