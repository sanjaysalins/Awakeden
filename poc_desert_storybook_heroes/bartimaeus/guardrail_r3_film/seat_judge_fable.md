## 1. WHO IS THIS FOR (one named viewer; who stops, who finishes, who shares; who is NOT served)
**Named viewer:** Grace, 26, Nairobi, a churchgoer with English as her second language, scrolling Shorts.
- **Stops:** anyone caught by a man shouting in red letters (line 1) and being hushed (line 2).
- **Finishes:** believers and the curious; the story is simple and line 4 keeps them.
- **Shares:** parents, Sunday-school teachers, people who feel overlooked (line 17 is the share line).
- **Not served:** the sceptic (no reason to believe is offered), and the viewer who asks "what about me?" Line 17 ends on "one man" and never turns to the watcher.

## 2. MAIN TAKEAWAY (one sentence a viewer could say back; which line carries it; did the film make it clear?)
"On His way to die, Jesus stopped for one blind beggar."
Line 17 carries it, set up by line 4 and line 7. Yes, it is clear: it is said at line 7, shown on screen at 30 s, and said again at line 17. This is the film's strongest feature.

## 3. GLOBAL AUDIENCE (include the ON-SCREEN words and pictures; who / what / why in my own words; where I got lost or misled, quote the line; words that fail in translation)
- **Who:** a blind beggar, Bartimaeus, and Jesus, called God's promised King.
- **What:** he shouts, is hushed, shouts louder; Jesus stops, asks, heals; he follows.
- **Why:** Jesus was walking to His death and still stopped for him.

Where I got lost or misled:
- **10-15 s:** the screen reads "SON OF DAVID means God's promised King" while the voice says line 4 about Jerusalem. That is two messages at once. The card belongs with line 3.
- **Line 3** packs three facts into 5 seconds.
- **Line 13**, "Jesus told him to go his way," can read as "Jesus sent him away." The "But" then sounds like he disobeyed.
- **Line 11**, "made thee whole": "whole" translates as "complete." Line 12 ("made him well") rescues it.
- **Line 9**, "What wilt thou that I should do unto thee?" is hard KJV, but line 8 explains it first. Good.
- **Line 1** opens in KJV with no help for 5 seconds, but "Jesus" and "mercy" get through.

## 4. BIBLICALLY ACCURATE (claim by claim: RIGHT / OVERCLAIMED / UNSUPPORTED / WRONG, quote the line; say only what you can support)
- Lines 1, 6, 9, 10, 11: **RIGHT**, KJV verbatim (Mark 10:47, 48, 51, 52).
- Line 2, "Many told the blind beggar to be quiet": **RIGHT** (v48).
- Line 3, "near Jericho": **RIGHT** (v46). "God's promised King": **RIGHT**, a fair plain gloss.
- Line 4, "He had told His followers He would be killed there": **RIGHT** (Mark 10:32-34).
- Line 7, "And Jesus stood still … called for him": **RIGHT** (v49). "A whole crowd was moving": **RIGHT** ("a great number of people").
- Line 8, "threw off his cloak and came": **RIGHT** (v50).
- Line 12, "At once he could see": **RIGHT** ("immediately").
- Lines 13-14, "followed Jesus. Up the road to Jerusalem": **SLIGHTLY OVERCLAIMED**. The text says "followed Jesus in the way." The road led to Jerusalem; how far he went is not stated.
- Line 15, "died on a cross for our sins": **RIGHT** (1 Cor 15:3).
- Line 16, "On the third day, He rose": **RIGHT** (1 Cor 15:4).
- Pictures: the bowl (3 s) is **UNSUPPORTED** but harmless. The last shot shows them side by side; the text says "followed." Minor.

## 5. BEST VERSION POSSIBLE (what would the best explainer in the world do differently; the ONE highest-value change; score 0-10 against that bar)
The best version would keep one message on screen at a time and let the ending reach the viewer.
**ONE change:** turn line 17 toward the watcher. The film proves Jesus stops for one man, then leaves the viewer outside the story.
**Score: 7/10.**

## CURIOSITY GAP (opens at line 4, pays off at line 7; score 0-10; worth the wait?)
Opens at line 4 ("Would He stop for a beggar?"), pays off at line 7. A second gap (will he see?) pays off at line 12.
**Score: 7/10.** Worth the wait; the music stop at 26.6 s lands it. Weak spot: both gaps are closed by 56 s, so lines 14-16 run on goodwill until line 17 ties them back.
