## 1. WHO IS THIS FOR
A lonely, overwhelmed scroller who feels invisible to society. They stop for the raw, disruptive emotion of the loud cry and red visuals (Line 1). They finish to see if the "important" figure actually notices the outcast (Line 7). They share it because the ending reframes the massive, historical gospel story into an intimate act of personal care (Line 17). 
**Who is NOT served:** Viewers looking for deep theological exposition, historical context of Jericho, or fast-paced entertainment.

## 2. MAIN TAKEAWAY
Even when on His way to save the entire world, Jesus has time to stop for one invisible, hurting person. 
Line 17 ("On His way to die and rise again, Jesus stood still for one man.") carries this perfectly. The film makes it undeniably clear by contrasting the massive crowd and the ultimate mission to Jerusalem (Line 4) with the sudden, intimate halt for a single beggar (Line 7).

## 3. GLOBAL AUDIENCE
The visual storytelling is universally understood: the dark, oppressive edges lifting to warm gold light (49.0s) instantly communicates healing and hope across any language barrier. 
However, the archaic KJV dialogue in Line 9 ("What wilt thou that I should do unto thee?") and Line 11 ("made thee whole") will confuse non-native English speakers. "Wilt thou" is obscure, and "whole" is easily misunderstood when referring to physical sight. The narrator successfully rescues the meaning in plain English in Line 12 ("Jesus made him well"), but the on-screen text during the dialogue may cause a momentary disconnect for international viewers.

## 5. BEST VERSION POSSIBLE
The transition from Bartimaeus walking up the road (56.0s) to the sudden shot of the empty cross (61.0s) feels visually abrupt. The best explainer in the world would visually bridge this gap by showing Bartimaeus *at* the cross or in the crowd in Jerusalem. Since Line 13 establishes that he "followed Jesus," showing him witnessing the crucifixion would seamlessly tie the historical gospel (Lines 15-16) to the personal narrative of the healed man. 
**Score: 8/10** against that bar.

## CURIOSITY GAP
Opens at Line 4 ("Would He stop for a beggar?"), pays off at Line 7 ("And Jesus stood still."). 
**Score: 9/10.** It is absolutely worth the wait because the intervening sequence (Lines 5-6) escalates the tension by showing Bartimaeus shouting even louder against the resistance of the crowd before the music cuts and the frame freezes.
