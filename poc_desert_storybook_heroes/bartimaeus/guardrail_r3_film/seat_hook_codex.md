## 1. WHO IS THIS FOR (one named viewer; who stops, who finishes, who shares; who is NOT served)

Priya, 34, a Christian mother finding Bible stories for her child. The cry and shushing would stop her (1–2); healing and following would keep her watching (12–14); Jesus’ personal attention gives her something to share (7,17). Beginners in English and viewers unfamiliar with Christianity face substantial language barriers (9–11).

## 2. MAIN TAKEAWAY (one sentence a viewer could say back; which line carries it; did the film make it clear?)

“Jesus was heading toward His death, yet stopped for someone the crowd dismissed.” Line 17 carries it. Yes: the frozen crowd, silence and “He stopped / for one man” establish it visually (7).

## 3. GLOBAL AUDIENCE (include the ON-SCREEN words and pictures; who / what / why in my own words; where I got lost or misled, quote the line; words that fail in translation)

A blind man trusts Jesus, asks for sight, receives it and follows Him (10–14).

Red ripples and “Be quiet!” communicate conflict (1–2); opened eyes and gold “see” communicate healing (12). However, the “Son of David” explanation appears onscreen one shot after its narration, competing with the Jerusalem information (3–4).

“Thou,” “wilt,” “thee,” “hath” and “whole” obstruct comprehension and literal translation (1,9,11). “For our sins” states the cross’s purpose without explaining the connection (15). After the tomb, the sunset walk could suggest Bartimaeus met the risen Jesus (16–17).

## 4. BIBLICALLY ACCURATE (claim by claim: RIGHT / OVERCLAIMED / UNSUPPORTED / WRONG, quote the line; say only what you can support)

- **RIGHT:** “blind beggar…near Jericho” (3), cries and rebukes (1–2,5–6), “stood still” and calling (7), discarded cloak (8), quoted dialogue (9–11), immediate sight and following (12–13). These follow [Mark 10:46–52](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Mark+10%3A46-52&version=KJV).
- **RIGHT, explanatory gloss:** “God’s promised King” (3) conveys the messianic significance; it omits Davidic ancestry. Compare [Mark 12:35–37](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Mark+12%3A35-37&version=KJV).
- **RIGHT:** “He would be killed there” (4). Following toward Jerusalem (14) is a reasonable contextual inference from [Mark 10:32–34](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Mark+10%3A32-34&version=KJV).
- **RIGHT:** “died on a cross for our sins” (15), “third day…rose to life” (16): supported by [Mark 15:22–25](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Mark+15%3A22-25&version=KJV) and [1 Corinthians 15:3–4](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1+Corinthians+15%3A3-4&version=KJV).
- **UNSUPPORTED if read chronologically:** the final walk after resurrection (17). Mark records following immediately after healing, without this later meeting. No clearly **WRONG** spoken claim or necessary **OVERCLAIM**.
