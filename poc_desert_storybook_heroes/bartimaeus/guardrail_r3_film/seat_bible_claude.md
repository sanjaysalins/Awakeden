## 1. WHO IS THIS FOR
**Named viewer:** Ana, 35. She lives in Manila, uses English as a second language, and is curious about faith but doesn't go to church. She feels unseen at work.
- **Who stops:** People who see the shouting beggar and the red rings (line 1). The opening sound and picture are strong.
- **Who finishes:** People who feel overlooked, because "He stopped for one man" (line 7) speaks to them.
- **Who shares:** Christian parents and Sunday-school teachers. It is clean, warm and kid-safe.
- **Who is NOT served:**
  - A doubter who asks "why did He have to die?" Lines 15-16 are a creed added on at the end, not argued for.
  - A Bible reader looking for something new. Nothing here is fresh to them.

## 2. MAIN TAKEAWAY
"Even on His way to die, Jesus stopped for one ignored man." Line 17 carries it, and line 7 sets it up. It is clear. The "on His way to die" half only works because line 4 planted it. It is said once, at speed, so some viewers will miss it.

## 3. GLOBAL AUDIENCE
**What I understood:** A blind beggar shouts for Jesus. The crowd tells him to be quiet. He shouts louder. Jesus stops, heals him, and the man follows Jesus toward Jerusalem, where Jesus will die and rise.

**Where I got lost:**
- **Line 3 does too much.** Name, place and a definition all come at once. Then the on-screen card "SON OF DAVID means God's promised King" (10-15 s) appears while the narrator is already on line 4 (Jerusalem, death). So the screen and the voice teach two things at the same moment.
- **15.0-20.8 s has no speech.** The small cross mark on a dotted road is a nice idea, but a newcomer won't read the cross as "death ahead" until line 15.
- **Archaic words:** "thou," "wilt," "unto thee," "hath made thee whole." "Whole" does not translate as "healed." Line 12 rescues it with "made him well," so that's fine.
- **Line 13 repeats line 11.** "Jesus told him to go his way" says again what Jesus just said. A non-native viewer may think "But" means he disobeyed.

## 5. BEST VERSION POSSIBLE
The best explainer would use Mark's own structure. In v46 the beggar sits **beside the road** ("by the highway side"). In v52 he follows **on the road** ("in the way"). In Greek it is the same word, *hodos*. Jesus says "go thy *way*," and the man picks Jesus' way.

The film is one sentence away from that turn and never says it.

**ONE highest-value change:** Rewrite line 13 so "by the road → on the road" carries the second half. This also builds the bridge to the cross, so lines 15-16 stop feeling added on.

**Score: 7/10.** The craft is strong and the stillness beat (line 7, music drops) is excellent. It loses points on the line 3 overload, the 6 s with no speech, and a cross ending the story hasn't earned.

## CURIOSITY GAP
- Opens at line 4 ("Would He stop for a beggar?").
- Pays off at line 7.
- **Score: 6/10.** The payoff is real, but a newcomer can guess the answer in a Jesus video. After 30 s there is no new question to hold viewers through the last 49 s. A "beside the road → on the road" thread would open a second gap.

## THREE SMALLEST FIXES
1. **Line 13:** "Jesus told him to go his way. He chose Jesus' way. He sat beside the road. Now he walked on it, behind Jesus."
2. **Line 12:** "He trusted Jesus. Jesus made him well." → "Jesus said his trust had made him well."
3. **Line 15:** "There Jesus died on a cross…" → "Just outside that city, Jesus died on a cross for our sins, for the wrong things we have done."

**Plus one timing fix:** Move the "SON OF DAVID means God's promised King" card to 5-10 s, under line 3. Then put "He knew He would die there." on screen at 10-15 s.
