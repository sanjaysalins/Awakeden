## 1. WHO IS THIS FOR
**Named viewer:** Grace, 30, a nurse in Lagos. She grew up near church, but English is her second language and she has never read Mark.
- **Who stops:** line 1. A raw cry for mercy is a strong cold open.
- **Who finishes:** line 2 asks "Would Jesus even stop?" and that question holds her to line 7.
- **Who shares:** small-group leaders and parents. Line 13's "He stood still for one man" is a line people will pass on.
- **Who is NOT served:** a total newcomer who doesn't know why Jerusalem matters. That only arrives in the last 8 seconds (line 13).

## 2. MAIN TAKEAWAY
"Jesus was walking to His death, and He still stopped for one ignored beggar."
- It is carried by line 7 ("He stopped for one man") and line 13 ("On His way to the cross, He stood still for one man").
- It is clear, but it lands late. The cost of stopping, the cross, is only revealed in the final line, so line 7 plays as a kind act, not a costly one.

## 3. GLOBAL AUDIENCE
**In my own words:** a blind beggar shouts for Jesus. The crowd hushes him and he shouts louder. Jesus stops, heals him, and the man follows Jesus toward the cross.
- Line 3 explains "Son of David" in plain words. Good.
- Lines 9 and 11 ("wilt thou… unto thee", "hath made thee whole") are archaic. Line 12 rescues them with a plain paraphrase, which is the right pattern.
- Line 13 is overloaded: five ideas in one breath. The aside "our sins, the wrong we do" will trip a second-language listener.
- "He was free to go" is a quiet irony (told to go, he chooses to follow), but it is easy to miss.

## 5. BEST VERSION POSSIBLE
The best explainer would plant the stakes early. Jesus has just told the twelve He will be killed in Jerusalem (Mark 10:33–34). Saying that by line 2 makes the stop at line 7 costly, and line 13 then only needs to land it, not explain it.

A second, smaller miss: the crowd's flip, from "hold his peace" to "Be of good comfort, rise; he calleth thee" (10:49). It is a built-in twist from the text itself, and it is left out.

**ONE change:** move the Jerusalem stake up to line 2.
**Score: 7/10.**

## CURIOSITY GAP
- Opens at line 2 ("Would Jesus even stop?").
- Pays off at line 7, with a second, deeper payoff at line 13.
- **Score: 7/10.** Worth the wait, but the deeper payoff is rushed.

## THREE SMALLEST FIXES
1. **Line 2:** "Many in the crowd told the blind beggar to be quiet. Jesus was on His way to Jerusalem, to die. Would He even stop?"
2. **Line 8:** "Now the crowd said, 'Be of good comfort, rise; he calleth thee.' Bartimaeus threw off his cloak and came. Jesus asked him what he wanted:"
3. **Line 13:** "He was free to go. Instead, he followed Jesus up the road to Jerusalem. There Jesus would die for our sins and rise again on the third day. On His way to the cross, He stood still for one man."
