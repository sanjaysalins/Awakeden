## 1. WHO IS THIS FOR

Priya, 19, in Jakarta. English is her school language. She watches Shorts with captions, often muted. She knows the name Jesus. She does not know David, Jericho, or the cross.

She stops if the first captions are line 1, "thou Son of David." That looks like broken English.

She finishes with the sound on. "Blind beggar," "be quiet," "shouted even louder," "stopped for one man," and "he could see" are easy.

She does not share it. She cannot repeat what Jesus says. The last line mixes up the two men.

Not served: muted viewers, because the captions will print the old English. Viewers who hear "have mercy" as "forgive me." Viewers who need words in the pictures. No picture-words exist yet.

## 2. MAIN TAKEAWAY

A viewer can say: "A blind man shouted, Jesus stopped, and he could see."

The film's own point is the end of line 13: on the way to the cross, he stood still for one man. Line 7 plants it ("He stopped for one man"). The healing is clear at line 12. The death-journey point is muddy. In line 13, "He" starts as Bartimaeus and becomes Jesus. "The cross" is never explained.

## 3. GLOBAL AUDIENCE

Who: Bartimaeus, a blind man begging by a road. What: he shouts, Jesus stops, he can see. Why: he trusted Jesus. The film adds: Jesus was going to die and still stopped for one man.

Lost at line 1. "Have mercy on me" comes before anyone says "blind." In many churches, "Lord, have mercy" means "forgive me." This can sound like a guilt story until line 10.

Words that fail:

- Line 1 and line 5: "thou." Captions leave it, or treat it as a name. Plain: "Jesus, Son of David, help me."
- Line 3: "Jericho" is only a name. Plain: "a town called Jericho." Keep "Son of David meant God's promised King." It is the best gloss in the film, and it comes one shout late. Paint "Bartimaeus" once, in big letters.
- Line 7: "had him called." Plain: "told them to call him over."
- Line 8: "cloak." Plain: "coat."
- Line 9: "What wilt thou that I should do unto thee?" Worst line. "Wilt" also means a plant drooping. Translators break this. Line 8 already said "what he wanted," then Jesus says the hard version.
- Line 10: "might" means "maybe" to a learner. Plain: "Lord, I want to see."
- Line 11: "made thee whole." "Whole" sounds like "hole," and it also means "entire." The healing disappears.
- Line 13: "the cross" assumes a Roman killing. "Rise to life" can mean "stand up." "The third day" is insider counting. The gloss "the wrong we do" is the model. Copy it onto every hard word.

Line 12 can also be misread as a slogan: trust harder, get a healing. "Trusted Jesus" keeps it safer.

Lines that travel: line 4, and line 12, "At once he could see." Do not paint the old English on the art. Paint "I want to see" and "Jesus stopped."

## 4. BIBLICALLY ACCURATE

- Line 1: RIGHT. Mark 10:47.
- Line 2: RIGHT that many told him to be quiet (Mark 10:48). "Would Jesus even stop?" is a question, not a fact.
- Line 3: RIGHT on the name, the begging, and Jericho (Mark 10:46; he is there as Jesus leaves the town). "God's promised King" is a fair meaning. This scene does not define the title. The film adds the help.
- Lines 4–5: RIGHT. He shouted louder (Mark 10:48).
- Line 7: RIGHT. Jesus stood still and had him called (Mark 10:49).
- Line 8: RIGHT. He threw his garment aside and came (Mark 10:50).
- Lines 9–11: RIGHT. They match the KJV of Mark 10:51–52. They still fail a non-native ear.
- Line 12: RIGHT. He received sight at once (Mark 10:52). "Trust" is a fair plain word for "faith."
- Line 13: RIGHT that he could go and that he followed (Mark 10:52). RIGHT on Jerusalem, death, and the third day (Mark 10:33–34; 1 Cor 15:4). "For our sins" is from 1 Cor 15:3, a source this film may use. Nobody says it in the Jericho scene. The word "cross" is not in the cited verses. It is the usual Christian name for that death.

Every fact-claim above can be supported. The break is the English.

## 5. BEST VERSION POSSIBLE

The best explainer would say he is blind in the first sentence. Every character line would be today's English. One short Bible sentence would stay: "Jesus stood still." The last line would say both names again. The death would be "be killed," or "the cross" plus a few plain words for what it was.

The one change: put lines 1, 5, 9, 10, and 11 into plain English. The story shape can stay.

Score: 5/10. Line 3 and "the wrong we do" are already the right kind of help. The climax is still 1611 English. Line 13 can pin the point on the wrong man. On mute, the captions are the film, and those captions fail.

## CURIOSITY GAP

Opens at line 2 ("Would Jesus even stop?"). Pays off at line 7 ("He stopped for one man"). Score: 8/10. Worth the wait. The wait is short, and line 3 uses it to teach the name and the title. Line 9 is a weak second question, because the question itself is the muddy part.

## THREE SMALLEST FIXES

- Line 9: "What do you want me to do for you?"
- Line 11: "You may go. Your faith has made you well."
- Line 13: "Bartimaeus could go. He followed Jesus to Jerusalem. There Jesus would die for the wrong we do, and come back to life on the third day. On the way to that death, Jesus stopped for one man."

VERDICT: REVISE
The facts hold. A global viewer cannot parse the climax, and cannot safely retell the last line.
