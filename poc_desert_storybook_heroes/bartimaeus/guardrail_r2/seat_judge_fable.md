Named viewer: Ana, 24, Manila, English second language, went to church as a child, feels overlooked. She stops at line 2 ("told the blind beggar to be quiet") because being silenced is universal. She finishes because line 7 pays off early and lines 9-11 are a real conversation. She shares line 13's last sentence. Bible readers stay for the verbatim KJV and the "stood still" observation (line 7).
NOT served: the cold scroller in the first two seconds. Line 1 is an unknown voice saying "thou Son of David" with no context; it only makes sense after line 2. Also not served: the sceptic, who gets no reason to care before the miracle.

## 2. MAIN TAKEAWAY (one sentence a viewer could say back; which line carries it; did the film make it clear?)
"Jesus was on His way to die, and He still stopped for one blind beggar." Carried by line 13's final sentence, set up by line 7. Clear, yes, but line 13 nearly buries it: one sentence holds Jerusalem, death, sins, a definition of sins, resurrection and the third day before the payoff arrives. The takeaway survives; it does not land as hard as it could.

## 3. GLOBAL AUDIENCE (who / what / why in my own words; where I got lost or misled, quote the line; words that fail in translation)
Who: a blind beggar named Bartimaeus, and Jesus. What: the crowd silences him, he shouts louder, Jesus stops, asks what he wants, heals him. Why: he trusted Jesus; and Jesus stops for one person even on the way to His death.
Lost or slowed:
- Line 3 interrupts the action. The man is shouted down (line 2), then we get name, place and a vocabulary lesson before "he shouted even louder" (line 4). Momentum drops.
- Line 7 "had him called": causative grammar, hard for second-language ears.
- Line 13 "our sins, the wrong we do,": a spoken aside inside a long sentence; by ear it sounds like a list of two things.
- Line 13 "He was free to go" glosses "Go thy way" (line 11) two lines late.
- KJV "wilt thou", "hath made thee whole": well handled, lines 8 and 12 gloss them. Good practice.

## 4. BIBLICALLY ACCURATE (claim by claim: RIGHT / OVERCLAIMED / UNSUPPORTED / WRONG, quote the line; say only what you can support)
- Lines 1, 5, 9, 10, 11 and "And Jesus stood still": RIGHT, verbatim KJV Mark 10:47-52.
- Line 2 "Many ... told the blind beggar to be quiet": RIGHT (10:48). "Would Jesus even stop?" is a narrator question, not a claim. Fine.
- Line 3 "begging by the road near Jericho": RIGHT (10:46). "'Son of David' meant God's promised King": RIGHT as a plain summary of a Messianic title; the passage itself does not explain it.
- Line 4 "shouted even louder": RIGHT ("cried the more a great deal").
- Line 7 "A whole crowd was moving": RIGHT, mild inference from "a great number of people" leaving Jericho. "had him called": RIGHT (10:49).
- Line 8 "threw off his cloak and came": RIGHT (10:50).
- Line 12 "that trust made him well. At once he could see": RIGHT (10:52). "Whole" may carry more than "well"; a small underclaim, not an error.
- Line 13 "He was free to go. He followed Jesus": RIGHT (10:52). "on the road to Jerusalem": RIGHT (10:32). "die for our sins ... rise ... third day": RIGHT (1 Cor 15:3-4; Mark 10:34). "On His way to the cross": RIGHT (10:33-34).
No WRONG, no UNSUPPORTED found.

## 5. BEST VERSION POSSIBLE (what would the best explainer in the world do differently; the ONE highest-value change; score 0-10 against that bar)
The best version would keep the shout-silence-louder run unbroken, and give the gospel sentence room to breathe. ONE highest-value change: split line 13 into short sentences so the cross and the final "stood still" each land alone. That helps the newcomer (sins defined cleanly), the second-language listener (one idea per sentence) and the sharer (a quotable last line). Score: 7.5/10.

## CURIOSITY GAP (opens at line 2, pays off at line 7; score 7/10; worth the wait?)
Opens at line 2 ("Would Jesus even stop?"), pays off at line 7. Worth the wait, and the pause (line 6) is right. Weakness: it pays off about a third of the way in, and nothing new opens until line 13 reframes it. Line 13's reframe (where He was going) is the real second payoff, and it is strong.

## THREE SMALLEST FIXES (line number + exact replacement wording)
1. Line 13: "He was free to go. But he followed Jesus, on the road to Jerusalem. There Jesus would die for our sins: every wrong we have done. On the third day He would rise to life. On His way to the cross, He stood still for one man."
2. Line 7: "'And Jesus stood still.' A whole crowd was moving. He stopped for one man, and called for him."
3. Line 3: "He was Bartimaeus, a blind beggar by the road near Jericho. 'Son of David' was a name for God's promised King." (and trim line 2 to "Many in the crowd told him to be quiet. Would Jesus even stop?" only if the picture shows he is blind; otherwise leave line 2 as is.)
