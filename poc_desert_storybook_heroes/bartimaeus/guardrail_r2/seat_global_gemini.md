## 1. WHO IS THIS FOR
This is for a young ESL student in Brazil or the Philippines scrolling through Shorts on a crowded bus. Those who stop are hooked by the loud, desperate plea of a marginalized person. Those who finish are rewarded with a clear picture of a God who pauses for the individual. Those who share will do so to comfort a friend who feels invisible. However, non-native English speakers who rely on auto-translate or basic vocabulary are NOT served by the archaic dialogue, which will break their comprehension.

## 2. MAIN TAKEAWAY
Jesus is never too busy for one hurting person, even when He is on His way to save the world. Line 13 carries this perfectly: "On His way to the cross, He stood still for one man." The film makes this beautifully clear by contrasting the moving crowd with His sudden stop.

## 3. GLOBAL AUDIENCE
The core dynamic—a beggar ignored by society but elevated by a leader—translates across every human culture. Explaining "Son of David" in Line 3 is a massive help; it prevents the viewer from needing insider Sunday School knowledge. 

However, I get completely lost at the dialogue. Archaic English is a severe barrier for ESL viewers and machine translation. 
* Line 9: "What wilt thou that I should do unto thee?" Auto-captions frequently mangle "wilt thou," and non-native speakers do not learn these pronouns. 
* Line 11: "thy faith hath made thee whole." The word "whole" is a double meaning. In global English, "whole" means "entire" or "unbroken" (like a whole pizza). It is rarely used to mean "medically healed." While Line 12 clarifies this ("made him well"), the quote itself creates a momentary cognitive stumble. 

## 4. BIBLICALLY ACCURATE
* Line 3: "Son of David meant God's promised King" — RIGHT. A culturally accurate translation of the Messianic title.
* Line 7: "Jesus stood still" — RIGHT. Matches Mark 10:49.
* Line 11: "thy faith hath made thee whole" — RIGHT. Exact KJV quotation of Mark 10:52.
* Line 13: "die for our sins... and rise to life on the third day" — RIGHT. Accurately summarizes the 1 Corinthians 15:3-4 gospel addition.

## 5. BEST VERSION POSSIBLE
The best explainer in the world would not force a global audience to mentally translate 17th-century English while trying to feel the emotional weight of a 60-second video. The ONE highest-value change is to modernize the direct dialogue so the emotional connection is instant and machine-translatable. If archaic quotes are absolutely mandatory, the narrator must speak over them or instantly paraphrase them, but ideally, the characters should just speak plain global English. 
Score: 7/10 against that bar.

## CURIOSITY GAP
Opens at line 2: "Would Jesus even stop?"
Pays off at line 7: "And Jesus stood still."
Score: 9/10. It is a fast, highly effective wait that perfectly frames the rest of the interaction.

## THREE SMALLEST FIXES
* Line 1: Change "Jesus, thou Son of David, have mercy on me." to "Jesus, Son of David, have mercy on me." (Dropping "thou" removes an instant translation hurdle).
* Line 9: Change "What wilt thou that I should do unto thee?" to "What do you want me to do for you?"
* Line 11: Change "Go thy way; thy faith hath made thee whole." to "Go your way; your faith has made you well."
