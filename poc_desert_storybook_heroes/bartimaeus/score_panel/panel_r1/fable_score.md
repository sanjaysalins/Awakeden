# FABLE SEAT - SCORE PLAN - "Blind Bartimaeus" (79.17 s)

## 1. SCORE IDEA
**The music is the moving crowd, so when Jesus stands still, the music stands still too.**

- **Instruments (4 only):** oud (plucked, the road), soft frame drum played with fingers (footsteps), ney reed flute (Bartimaeus's cry), one warm cello (Jesus).
- **Motif, "the Cry":** four notes on the ney. Step up, leap up, hang, fall back down without landing. It sounds like a question nobody answers.
- **The answer:** after Jesus stops, the cello plays the same four notes, but the last note lands on the home note. Same cry, now answered. From 49 s the ney and cello play it together.
- **The pulse:** a slow walking pattern (about 76 bpm) on oud and drum. It stops dead at 25.6 s and only comes back when Bartimaeus walks behind Jesus.

## 2. WHY IT FITS
- **The look:** gouache is dry, matte and hand-made. Plucked wood, skin drum and breathy reed are the same texture. No shine, no synth pads, no big drums.
- **The story:** the text says a whole crowd was moving and He stopped. A walking pulse that halts makes the listener feel the key verse, with no extra words.
- **The gospel turn:** the pulse returns as footsteps up the road to the cross. It never turns triumphant, so the cross stays grave and the rising stays warm.

## 5. MUSIC PROMPTS
**Part 1 (ask for 28 s):**
> Instrumental, intimate ancient Middle Eastern folk, warm and dry, small room, no reverb wash. Only four instruments: oud, soft finger-played frame drum, ney reed flute, one cello. Slow walking tempo, 76 bpm, D minor. Starts with a very quiet low cello drone alone. Around 3 seconds in, the ney plays a short four-note rising call that falls back and does not resolve. Around 5 seconds in, a gentle plucked oud walking pattern and soft frame drum begin, like footsteps on a dusty road. Around 13 seconds in, the cello adds a low, sorrowful line under the walking pattern. Around 21 seconds in, the pattern grows a little more urgent and the ney repeats its call higher. Around 25 seconds in, everything stops suddenly, a hard stop, total silence to the end. No vocals, no choir, no synths, no cinematic drums, no trailer build.

**Part 2 (ask for 55 s):**
> Instrumental, intimate ancient Middle Eastern folk, warm and dry, small room. Only four instruments: cello, oud, ney reed flute, soft finger-played frame drum. D minor moving to D major. Starts with one single soft held cello note, alone, no rhythm at all, very still. Around 8 seconds in, three bright plucked oud notes, then stillness again. Around 20 seconds in, the cello plays a tender four-note rising phrase that resolves home. Around 23 seconds in, the music lifts warmly: ney and cello play that phrase together in D major over full, gentle oud chords, joyful but small. Around 29 seconds in, a soft walking oud pattern and frame drum begin at 76 bpm, like footsteps. Around 35 seconds in, it thins to the drum and a low mournful cello, minor. Around 40 seconds in, the cello rises and the oud opens into a warm major chord that rings. Around 43 seconds in, the walking pattern returns softly, slows, and stops. Around 50 seconds in, the ney alone plays the four-note phrase once, resolved, and one last oud note rings out to silence. No vocals, no choir, no synths, no big drums, no trailer build.

## 6. MIX NOTES
- Voice stays as is. Music bed sits **about 18 dB under the voice** when anyone speaks.
- Under the three-voice exchange (38.4-48.9 s): drop 4 dB more. Barely there.
- **Lift:** 48.9-54.8 s, raise 5 dB, but duck back under "At once he could see".
- **Drop-outs:** 25.6-26.6 s is digital silence. 26.6-33.7 s is one note only.
- **Hold (76.2-79.17 s):** raise the music to near voice level, the only time it leads. Fade to silence by 79.0 s. Audio length must equal video length.

## 7. RISKS
1. **Dead air or wrong timing in Part 2** (53 s is long). Check: a timeline sweep of loudness in 1 s windows, not just mean/max. No window under -50 dB except where planned. If it fails, re-roll Part 2 only, or split it again at 48.9 s.
2. **The duck kills the pulse**, so the "stop" at 25.6 s means nothing. Check by measurement: pulse must be audible at 5-25 s (bed no lower than about -34 dB), then by ear: does the stop feel like a stop?
3. **The model goes "desert trailer"** (big drums, wailing voice, heavy reverb) or the motif differs between parts. Check by ear on Part 1's first 6 s and Part 2's 20-26 s side by side. If the two phrases do not match, keep the cello answer and trim the ney call to the cleaner take.
