## 1. WHO IS THIS FOR
A young adult in Manila or São Paulo scrolling Shorts on a budget smartphone, relying heavily on auto-translated captions. Viewers who feel ignored or silenced by society will stop and watch this. They finish the video to see if the VIP (Jesus) actually cares about the beggar. However, ESL (English as a Second Language) viewers are NOT served by the 17th-century dialogue; they will likely drop off or become confused when the characters start speaking archaic English that their translation apps cannot parse.

## 2. MAIN TAKEAWAY
Even when the crowd tells you that you don't matter, Jesus will stop everything to listen to you and heal you. Line 7 ("He stopped for one man, and called him.") carries this perfectly. Yes, the film makes this contrast very clear.

## 3. GLOBAL AUDIENCE
The social dynamic of a marginalized beggar and an important teacher is universally understood. You did an excellent job in Line 3 ("meant God's promised King") and Line 13 ("all the wrong we do") explaining insider religious concepts to a secular, global audience. 

However, I get completely lost at the spoken dialogue. Line 9: "What wilt thou that I should do unto thee?" is incredibly difficult to parse if English isn't your first language. Auto-captions frequently mangle "wilt thou." 

Furthermore, the word "whole" in Line 11 ("thy faith hath made thee whole") fails in translation. To a non-native speaker, "whole" strictly means "entire" or "not in pieces." An ESL viewer will wonder, "Was his body cut in half?" The plain meaning here is "healed" or "well."

## 5. BEST VERSION POSSIBLE
The best explainer in the world would not mix globally accessible, plain-English narration with impenetrable 1600s dialogue. The ONE highest-value change is to update the character dialogue to match the clear, modern vocabulary of the narrator, so the emotional climax of the story isn't buried under archaic grammar.
