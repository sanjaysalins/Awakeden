# Ideas — "Lazarus, come forth" (opus seat)

## Angle A — Alive, but still tied up
1. **Name:** Alive, but still tied up
2. **First 3 seconds:** "He walked out of his grave. And he still could not move his hands."
3. **Verse + turn:** John 11:44 — "he that was dead came forth, bound hand and foot with graveclothes". The turn: Jesus gave the life, but then He told the people standing there, "Loose him, and let him go." The dead man came out still wrapped. The crowd's job was to unwrap him. Most people see only the shout; they miss the second command.
4. **Last line:** "A few weeks later, Jesus was wrapped and laid in a grave too. The third day, He rose. The One who said 'let him go' had the right to say it."

## Angle B — The miracle that got Jesus killed
1. **Name:** The miracle that got Jesus killed
2. **First 3 seconds:** "This is the miracle that got Jesus killed."
3. **Verse + turn:** John 11:53 — "Then from that day forth they took counsel together for to put him to death." The turn: a dead man walks out, and the very same day the chief priests and Pharisees plan Jesus' death. Their high priest even says "one man should die for the people" — and John says he spoke truer than he knew: Jesus would die "for that nation; and not for that nation only." (They even planned to kill Lazarus too — John 12:10.)
4. **Last line:** "Lazarus walked out of a grave. That day, the plan began to put Jesus into one. He died for us, He was buried — and the third day, He walked out too."

## Angle C — A dead man heard his name
1. **Name:** A dead man heard his name
2. **First 3 seconds:** "Jesus shouted at a dead man. And the dead man answered."
3. **Verse + turn:** John 11:43 — "he cried with a loud voice, Lazarus, come forth." The turn: He did not shout "come out" to the whole tomb. He said one name. Earlier, Jesus said His sheep "hear his voice: and he calleth his own sheep by name, and leadeth them out" (John 10:3). At the grave, that is exactly what happened. (An old saying adds that if He had not said the name, every grave would have opened — that is a saying, not Scripture.)
4. **Last line:** "Jesus said, 'all that are in the graves shall hear his voice.' The voice that said 'Lazarus' is the same voice that went into death itself — and came back out on the third day."

## My pick: B
B has the strongest stop line and the plainest gap (how can a miracle get someone killed?), and the text itself carries the twist from Lazarus's grave to Jesus' cross and rising, with no invented detail. A's "still tied up" beat can be the middle line of B for viewers who feel stuck.
