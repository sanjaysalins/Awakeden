# Ideas — "Lazarus, come forth" (John 11:38-44)

## Angle 1 — The miracle that cost Him

1. **Name:** The grave He opened, the grave He entered.
2. **First 3 seconds:** "A dead man walked out of his grave. Because of it, a plan began to kill Jesus."
3. **Verse and turn:** John 11:53 — "Then from that day forth they took counsel together for to put him to death." Most people stop at the man walking out (v44). Keep reading. Some who saw it went and told the Pharisees (v46). The chief priests and Pharisees called a council (v47). The high priest said "that one man should die for the people" (v50), and John says he spoke more than he knew: it was a prophecy that Jesus would die (v51). The turn: the loud cry "Lazarus, come forth" is the cry that set the cross in motion. One man out of a grave; one Man on the road to His own.
4. **Last line:** "One man came out of the grave. One Man would go in, for the people. He was buried. And He rose again the third day."

## Angle 2 — Alive, but still tied up

1. **Name:** Alive, but still tied up.
2. **First 3 seconds:** "The dead man walked out alive. But he could not use his hands, and he could not see."
3. **Verse and turn:** John 11:44 — "bound hand and foot with graveclothes: and his face was bound about with a napkin" (grave wrappings on his hands and feet, a cloth tied over his face). The turn: the miracle has two commands, not one. "Lazarus, come forth" gave him life. Then a second word, to the people standing there: "Loose him, and let him go." Jesus did not leave him alive and still wrapped like a dead man. Bookend it with the first order at the tomb, "Take ye away the stone" (v39): a stone at the start, the wrappings at the end, and both come off at His word.
4. **Last line:** "The One who said 'let him go' was Himself buried, and rose again the third day. 'If the Son therefore shall make you free, ye shall be free indeed.'"

## Angle 3 — "Too late" was said out loud

1. **Name:** Four days too late.
2. **First 3 seconds:** "Jesus said, open the grave. The dead man's own sister said: don't. It is too late."
3. **Verse and turn:** John 11:39 — "Lord, by this time he stinketh: for he hath been dead four days" (the body already smells; four days dead). The turn, in two parts. First: the "too late" comes from Martha, a friend, not an enemy. Second, the part people miss: Jesus thanks His Father BEFORE anything happens. The stone is off, the man is still dead, and He says, "Father, I thank thee that thou hast heard me" (v41). He says it aloud for the people standing by, "that they may believe that thou hast sent me" (v42). Then the loud voice. Then "he that was dead came forth."
4. **Last line:** "Four days dead was not too late for the One the Father sent. And when He Himself was buried, He rose again the third day."

## My pick

**Angle 1.** It is the one thing even Bible readers miss (the miracle leads straight to the plot, v53), and it lands on the cross and rising from the text itself, not from a bolted-on ending.
For the viewer who feels buried, it says the most with the least pressure: getting one man out of a grave is what put Jesus on the road to His own. Angle 2 is my second choice (strongest picture for ink: a living man still wrapped).
