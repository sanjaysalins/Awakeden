# Lazarus, come forth: 3 angles (Sonnet)

## Angle 1: Four days late on purpose

1. **Name:** Four days late on purpose
2. **Opening line:** "Jesus got the message, and then He waited two more days."
3. **Verse and turn:** John 11:3-6. "Lord, behold, he whom thou lovest is sick." Next: "Now Jesus loved Martha, and her sister, and Lazarus. When he had heard therefore that he was sick, he abode two days still in the same place." The word "therefore" is the turn. The text does not say "although He loved them, He stayed." It says He loved them, "therefore" He stayed. Viewers read the delay as neglect. The text ties it to love and to "the glory of God" (v.4). Then at the grave: "Said I not unto thee, that, if thou wouldest believe, thou shouldest see the glory of God?" (v.40). Do not explain why beyond what the text says.
4. **Last line:** "He let a grave get four days old, and then He called a name. Later He was in a grave Himself, three days, and rose. Late is not lost, when He is the one who loves you." (Ties to 1 Cor 15:3-4, "buried... rose again the third day". Grace, no pressure.)

## Angle 2: The only thing He ordered

1. **Name:** The only thing He ordered
2. **Opening line:** "At a grave, Jesus gave two orders. One was for a dead man. One was for the living."
3. **Verse and turn:** John 11:39, 44. "Take ye away the stone" (to the people standing there) and "Loose him, and let him go." Viewers focus on the shout. Look at the people. The one who can raise the dead asks the living to lift a stone, and then to untie the grave clothes: "bound hand and foot with graveclothes: and his face was bound about with a napkin." The man came out walking, still wrapped. Say only what the text says: He called, the dead man came, and then Jesus told the others to loose him. Do not say why He did not do it Himself.
4. **Last line:** "He called the man out. Others unwrapped him. Later, on the cross, Jesus was the one who was bound, so no one else would stay bound." (Ties to John 8:36, "If the Son therefore shall make you free, ye shall be free indeed", and 1 Cor 15:3. Keep it as the Son who frees, not a command.)

## Angle 3: The man whose name was called

1. **Name:** One name, one voice
2. **Opening line:** "Four days dead. And the first thing he heard was his own name."
3. **Verse and turn:** John 11:43 and John 10:3. "Lazarus, come forth." Earlier in John, Jesus says the shepherd "calleth his own sheep by name, and leadeth them out." The text at the tomb says "Lazarus," not "all the dead." There is an old saying that if He had not named him, every grave would open. Label that clearly as an old saying, not Scripture. The Scripture-supported turn: John 5:28, "all that are in the graves shall hear his voice, And shall come forth." Today a name. A day is coming for all. Keep the claim exactly that large, and no larger.
4. **Last line:** "He called one name out of one grave. He said a day is coming when His voice reaches every grave. And He went into a grave first, and came out." (1 Cor 15:3-4. No threat, no "choose now". Just who He is.)

## Pick

**Angle 2, "The only thing He ordered."** It opens a real curiosity gap (two orders, who were they for?), and it stays on what the text states. It also speaks to people who feel stuck: the one called out was still wrapped, and others were told to loose him.
