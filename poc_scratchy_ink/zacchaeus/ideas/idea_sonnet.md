# Zacchaeus (Luke 19:1-10) - 3 angles

## Angle 1: He Never Asked To Be Invited
1. Name: He Never Asked To Be Invited
2. Opening line: "Everyone in town said no to this man. Then one person said, 'I'm coming to your house today.'"
3. Verse and turn: Luke 19:5, "Zacchaeus, make haste, and come down; for to day I must abide at thy house." What we did not notice: Zacchaeus only climbed up to SEE (19:3). Jesus spoke first, used his name, and chose his house. The invitation came before any change in him. The crowd's complaint (19:7, "a man that is a sinner") did not stop Jesus.
4. Last line: "He went to that house for a man the crowd had written off. Later He went to a cross for people just like him, and rose again, so that house would not be the only one."

## Angle 2: Four Times Back
1. Name: Four Times Back
2. Opening line: "He said he'd pay back four times what he took. Nobody asked him to."
3. Verse and turn: Luke 19:8, "if I have taken any thing from any man by false accusation, I restore him fourfold." What we did not notice: Jesus never gave him a list or a demand in this story. The giving comes after Jesus is already in his house, and Jesus' words about salvation (19:9) come after it, with "this day is salvation come to this house", so the house is saved by Jesus arriving, not by the payment. (Background, not Scripture: Exodus 22:1 asks four sheep for a stolen sheep, so he went to the same number the law names for a sheep.)
4. Last line: "The payment did not buy the door open. The One who came in is the One who later paid for every debt on a cross, and rose again."

## Angle 3: Same Road, Same Town
1. Name: Same Town, Two Men, Two Answers
2. Opening line: "In one town, a rich man walked away sad. In the same town, another rich man ran toward Jesus."
3. Verse and turn: Luke 18:23 ("he was very sorrowful: for he was very rich") next to Luke 19:6 ("received him joyfully"); both stories sit just before and in Jericho (18:35, 19:1). What we did not notice: Jesus told the first man "the things which are impossible with men are possible with God" (18:27). Then, in 19:9-10, it happens in front of us: salvation comes to a rich man's house. Jesus came to seek and save "that which was lost" (19:10), and that is how the impossible happens.
4. Last line: "It is not possible for us. So the Son of man came, died for our sins, rose again on the third day, and did what we could not."

## My pick
Angle 1. It is the clearest for a newcomer: one person speaks first, and the line "Zacchaeus, make haste, and come down" is short and plain. It also keeps salvation as Jesus coming (19:9), not Zacchaeus earning, and lands on the cross naturally.
