# Zacchaeus ideas — blind voice: Fable

## Angle 1

1. **The Blame Changed Sides**
2. First 3 seconds: "A whole crowd got angry at Jesus. All He did was choose whose house to stay in."
3. Pinned to Luke 19:7 — "they all murmured, saying, That he was gone to be guest with a man that is a sinner." The turn: read who the complaint is about. Not Zacchaeus. Jesus. He stopped under the tree, said the man's name, and said "to day I must abide at thy house" (must stay at your house). The moment He walked in that door, the crowd's bad words landed on Him. Zacchaeus "received him joyfully"; Jesus received the murmur. And He did not answer the crowd by defending Himself. He said, "This day is salvation come to this house."
4. Last line: "He took one man's bad name in Jericho. Then He went on to a cross and took far more: 'Christ died for our sins' — and He rose again the third day."

## Angle 2

1. **Who Was Looking For Whom**
2. First 3 seconds: "A rich man climbed a tree to look at Jesus. He did not know Jesus had come to look for him."
3. Pinned to Luke 19:3 and 19:10 — "he sought to see Jesus who he was" and "the Son of man is come to seek and to save that which was lost." The turn: the same word at both ends of the story. Zacchaeus seeks first, and only wants a look. But Jesus stops at the exact place, looks up, and says his name — "Zacchaeus" — before the man says one word. Then the echo most people miss: long before, God said, "I will seek that which was lost" (Ezekiel 34:16). Jesus takes God's own words as His own job.
4. Last line: "The man in the tree wanted to see who Jesus was. Here is who He is: the God who goes looking for the lost — who died for our sins, and rose again the third day, still looking."

## Angle 3

1. **The Camel Got Through**
2. First 3 seconds: "Jesus said a rich man getting in is harder than a camel going through a needle's eye. One chapter later, a rich man got in."
3. Pinned to Luke 18:25-27 and Luke 19:9 — "Who then can be saved?" ... "The things which are impossible with men are possible with God" ... "This day is salvation come to this house." The turn: two rich men, back to back. The ruler is told to give to the poor and walks away "very sorrowful." Zacchaeus is told nothing about money at all. Jesus only says, come down, I am staying at your house. Jesus comes in first; then the hand opens — "the half of my goods I give to the poor." The giving did not buy the salvation. Salvation came to the house when Jesus came to the house.
4. Last line: "What no man can do, God did — He came to the house Himself. And then He did the bigger impossible thing: He died for our sins, and rose again the third day."

## My pick

**Angle 1, The Blame Changed Sides.** It speaks straight to the viewer who feels judged, it is the detail the kids' song skips, and it is pinned to plain words in 19:7.
It also earns the cross with no jump: Jesus carrying a sinner's blame in Jericho is a small picture of what He did in full when He died and rose.
