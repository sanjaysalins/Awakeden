# Zacchaeus: 3 angles (opus seat)

## Angle A: Who was really looking?
1. **Name:** Who was really looking?
2. **First 3 seconds:** "He climbed a tree to find Jesus. He had it the wrong way round."
3. **Verse + turn:** Luke 19:3 says he "sought to see Jesus." Then 19:5 says Jesus "looked up, and saw him," and called him by name. Before anyone spoke, Jesus already knew who he was. The last verse says who was really searching: "the Son of man is come to seek and to save that which was lost" (19:10). The man thought he was the seeker. Jesus was the one seeking him. Jesus came to his house first. Only after that did he give his money away. So the gift came after he was found. It was not his way to be found (19:9).
4. **Last line:** "He came to look for lost people. He died for them and rose again, and He is still the one doing the looking."

## Angle B: Two rich men, one page apart
1. **Name:** Two rich men, one page apart
2. **First 3 seconds:** "Jesus said it was nearly impossible. A few verses later, it happened."
3. **Verse + turn:** In Luke 18:23 a rich ruler goes away "very sorrowful." Jesus says a camel through a needle's eye is easier, and then: "The things which are impossible with men are possible with God" (18:27). Next chapter, in the next town, another rich man is "chief" of the tax men (19:2). Nobody tells him to sell anything. Jesus just comes to his house. Then he gives half away "joyfully," on his own (19:6, 19:8). The impossible thing from Luke 18 happens in Luke 19, and God does it.
4. **Last line:** "What no person can do, God did. Jesus died and rose to do it, starting with the man nobody thought could change."

## Angle C: What they called him, what Jesus called him
1. **Name:** Two names for one man
2. **First 3 seconds:** "The crowd had one word for him. Jesus picked a different one."
3. **Verse + turn:** The crowd stopped him twice. First they blocked his view (19:3). Then they complained about him: "a man that is a sinner" (19:7). Jesus never argues with that word. He gives him two others: his own name, "Zacchaeus" (19:5), and "a son of Abraham" (19:9). That means he still belongs to God's family. To Jesus he is "lost" (19:10), which is a word for something you go after, not something you throw away. (Background, not Scripture: the "fourfold" payback echoes the old law for stolen sheep, Exodus 22:1.)
4. **Last line:** "Paul called himself the worst sinner. He also wrote this: 'Christ Jesus came into the world to save sinners.' And then He died for them, and rose."

## My pick: A
It runs one simple turn the whole way through (he looked, but Jesus was the real seeker), and 19:10 pays it off word for word. It also puts the order of grace on screen with no works framing: Jesus comes first, and the giving follows.
