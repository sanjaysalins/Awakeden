The Search Goes Both Ways
“He climbed to see Jesus. But who was looking for whom?”
Luke 19:3, 10 — Zacchaeus seeks a look at Jesus; Jesus comes “to seek and to save that which was lost.” The tree story reveals a rescue mission.
“Jesus came for more than a look. He came to save the lost.”

The Welcome Before The Money
“Jesus chose his house before he offered any money.”
Luke 19:5, 8–9 — Jesus announces His visit before Zacchaeus promises generosity and repayment. Making things right matters; it does not buy Jesus’ welcome.
“Salvation has a cost—but Jesus paid it: He died for our sins and rose again.”

The Crowd’s Word Against His
“The crowd called him a sinner. What would Jesus call him?”
Luke 19:7, 9 — The crowd says “a man that is a sinner”; Jesus says “he also is a son of Abraham.” Notice “also”: the man they condemn belongs, too.
“Jesus does not excuse the wrong. He is the Saviour who seeks and saves the lost.”

My pick: **The Search Goes Both Ways.**
It turns the familiar tree story inside out, with Jesus’ own words supplying the surprise and the ending.
