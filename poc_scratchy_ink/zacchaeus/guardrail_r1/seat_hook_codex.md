## 3. GLOBAL AUDIENCE (who / what / why in my own words; where I got lost or misled, quote the line; words that fail in translation)

A wealthy, disliked official cannot see Jesus; Jesus singles him out, accepts his hospitality, and receives a public commitment to generosity (lines 2–11). The missing explanation is **why people dislike him**: “People called him a sinner” can sound like arbitrary bullying (line 2).

“Make haste,” “abide,” “Behold,” and “goods” add decoding work; “Son of man” introduces an unexplained identity precisely when the conclusion needs clarity (lines 4, 10, 14). “Makes you His” may suggest possession rather than belonging (line 12).

No title is supplied, so tap appeal cannot be judged. A candidate grounded in the hook: **“He Climbed to See Jesus. Jesus Called His Name.”** (lines 1, 3–4).

## 4. BIBLICALLY ACCURATE (claim by claim: RIGHT / OVERCLAIMED / UNSUPPORTED / WRONG, quote the line; say only what you can support)

- **RIGHT:** “chief tax collector,” rich, short, climbing, being called down, joyful reception, and crowd complaint follow Luke’s account (lines 1–6).
- **OVERCLAIMED:** “said his name first” supplies an emphasis, not an explicitly established first encounter or first exchange (line 3).
- **UNSUPPORTED as narrated detail:** “Jesus went in anyway” turns receiving Jesus into an explicit entrance scene (line 7).
- **RIGHT chronology, interpretive emphasis:** “Jesus came first. The giving came after” matches the narrated sequence (line 9).
- **RIGHT but selective:** the half-to-the-poor pledge omits the fourfold repayment pledge, weakening the account’s restitution dimension (line 10).
- **RIGHT quotation; interpretive gloss:** salvation comes to the house; “rescues you and makes you His” is an explanation, not Luke’s wording (lines 11–12).
- **RIGHT broader Christian proclamation:** death and resurrection extend beyond this episode; signal that transition (line 15).
