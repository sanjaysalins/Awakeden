# Seat: Fable — blind test, Zacchaeus (Luke 19:1-10)

Word count (my count): X = 157. Y = 155. Both fit 150-165.

## Script X

**1. Newcomer (no Bible, English second language)**
- Who: yes. A rich tax man, Zacchaeus, and Jesus.
- What: yes. He climbs a tree to see Jesus. Jesus calls him by name and goes to his house.
- Why: mostly. "Jesus was looking for him" is clear. Why Jesus died is not tied to the story.
- Lines I would not understand:
  - "little of stature" — "stature" is a rare word. The script never says he was short.
  - "make haste" and "abide at thy house" — old words. The picture must carry them.
  - "They all murmured" — "murmured" is hard.
  - "That he was gone to be guest with a man that is a sinner" — starts with "That". Odd to the ear, but the lead-in "They all murmured:" helps.
  - "the Son of man" — a newcomer does not know this means Jesus.
  - Last line: "He came to look... He died" — the first "He" is Zacchaeus, the second "He" is Jesus. Confusing.
- Never told: why a tax collector is called a sinner.

**2. Bible reader**
- "little of stature" — exact.
- "looked up, and saw him" — exact.
- "Zacchaeus, make haste, and come down; for to day I must abide at thy house." — exact.
- "That he was gone to be guest with a man that is a sinner." — exact.
- "Behold, Lord, the half of my goods I give to the poor." — exact words, but cut short. KJV has a semicolon and goes on (the fourfold part). Fair cut.
- "This day is salvation come to this house. For the Son of man is come..." — words exact, but it is a SPLICE. "forsomuch as he also is a son of Abraham" is dropped, with no break. One voice turn makes it sound like one unbroken quote. Put a pause or a narrator beat between the two halves.
- "said his name first" — fair. The text has no word from Zacchaeus before this.
- "Jesus went to his house anyway" — inferred, but backed by "was gone to be guest".
- "Only then did Zacchaeus stand" — the place he stood is not stated in the text. The order is right.
- Works-based? Low risk. "Only then" guards it. But it is a soft guard. An ESL ear can miss it and hear "he gave half, so he was saved."
- Blame on a people group? None. "They all" is just the crowd.
- Last line is 1 Cor 15:3-4 in other words, no quote marks. Fine.

**3. Hook**
- Stops me: yes. A man in a tree is a picture. Fits bold ink art.
- Gap: partly spent. Line 2 gives the answer at once. But "why was Jesus looking for HIM?" stays open and pays off at "to seek and to save that which was lost."
- First 3 seconds: **8/10**

**4. Overall: 8/10**

## Script Y

**1. Newcomer**
- Who: yes.
- What: yes. The order of events is the clearest of the two (crowd, short, ran, tree).
- Why: yes. "The giving came after. Jesus came first." is the plainest line in either script.
- Lines I would not understand:
  - "who was really looking for whom?" — "whom" is hard, and the line is abstract.
  - "little of stature" — same as X.
  - The crowd line comes COLD. No lead-in. A voice just says "That he was gone to be guest..." Nobody knows who is talking or what "That" hangs on. "They all murmured" comes after. This is the worst line for a newcomer.
  - "seeker" — less common than "look" and "find".
  - "the Son of man" — same as X.
  - "He thought he was the seeker. Jesus was. He died..." — same "He" mix-up.

**2. Bible reader**
- All quotes: same words as X, all exact. Same splice in the last Jesus line.
- "He looked up, saw him" — own words, no quote marks. Fine.
- "ran ahead and climbed a tree" — true to "ran before, and climbed up".
- "Jesus stayed with him anyway" — inferred. Text says "gone to be guest".
- Works-based? Best guard of the two. "The giving came after. Jesus came first." sits right between the gift and "salvation".
- Blame on a people group? None.

**3. Hook**
- Stops me: not much. "A rich man wanted one look at Jesus" has no picture. The question is a stock hook shape.
- Gap: stays open longer than X, and pays off at the end. But the opening is weak.
- First 3 seconds: **5/10**

**4. Overall: 6.5/10**

## Winner: X

Why: a real picture in second one, simpler words ("look / find" beats "seeker / whom"), and the crowd line has a lead-in. Take one thing from Y: its plain grace guard.

### Top 3 line fixes for X (old -> new)

1. Say he was short, in plain words.
   - Old: `The crowd was in his way, and he was "little of stature."`
   - New: `He was a short man, "little of stature," and the crowd blocked his view.`

2. Make the grace order loud (borrowed from Y).
   - Old: `Jesus went to his house anyway. (pause) Only then did Zacchaeus stand and say:`
   - New: `Jesus went to his house anyway. (pause) Jesus came first. Only then did Zacchaeus stand and say:`

3. Fix the "He" mix-up and tie the cross to the story.
   - Old: `He came to look. Jesus came to find. He died for our sins, and rose again the third day.`
   - New: `Zacchaeus came to look. Jesus came to find him. And to save the lost, Jesus died for our sins, and rose again the third day.`

Also worth doing (not top 3):
- Split the last Jesus quote into two turns with a (pause), so the dropped "son of Abraham" clause is not hidden inside one unbroken quote.
- "the Son of man" is never explained. Fix 3 helps: "Jesus came to find him" right after it shows who the Son of man is.

## 5. Length

- X = 157 words. With the 3 fixes, about 172. Trim to get back near 165:
  - `in the town of Jericho` -> `in Jericho` (saves 3).
  - `"looked up, and saw him," and said his name first` -> `looked up and said his name first` (saves 3).
  - If still long, drop `Only then did Zacchaeus stand and say:` to `Then Zacchaeus stood and said:` (saves 2).
- Y = 155 words. Line that could be cut: `But who was really looking for whom?` Also `The crowd blocked him` could fold into the next clause.
