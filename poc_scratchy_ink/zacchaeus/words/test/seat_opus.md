# Seat: opus (blind)

## Script X (~152 words)

**1. Newcomer (who / what / why)**
- Who and what: yes. A short rich man climbs a tree, and Jesus calls him down and stays at his house.
- Why: only half clear. Nobody says WHY a tax man counts as "a sinner" (he took people's money). A newcomer won't know tax men were hated.
- Hard lines: "abide at thy house" (abide = ?), "make haste", "little of stature" (it helps that "crowd in his way" comes first), "murmured", "Son of man", "that which was lost".
- "He died for our sins, and rose again the third day": who is "He"? Why is this here? It feels bolted on. Nothing links it to Zacchaeus.

**2. Bible reader**
- Quotes are exact KJV, but cut short: "half of my goods I give to the poor" stops before "restore him fourfold". "This day is salvation come to this house" then jumps to "For the Son of man..." with "forsomuch as he also is a son of Abraham" left out and no mark. Fine for a Short, but be aware.
- "said his name first": "first" is an inference. Fair (Jesus named a stranger), but it is not in the text.
- "Only then did Zacchaeus stand": good. It shows grace came first and the giving came after. Not works-based.
- "They all murmured": these are the people in the story, not a people group. No group blame. OK.
- Missing: "received him joyfully". That is the turn of the story, and it is lost.

**3. Hook**: "A man climbed a tree to look for Jesus. He did not know Jesus was looking for him." It is easy to picture (tree), it is plain English, and it opens a gap. But the second line gives the twist away in second 3. The payoff "He came to look. Jesus came to find." is neat, but we already heard it. **First 3 s: 7/10.**

**4. Overall: 6.5/10.** Clear, good order (the "They all murmured:" set-up before the crowd line helps). It loses points for giving the twist away early and for a landing that comes out of nowhere.

**5. Length**: ~152, OK. You could cut "in the town of Jericho" down to "in Jericho".

## Script Y (~147 words)

**1. Newcomer**
- Who and what: yes. "So he ran ahead and climbed a tree" gives the picture X skips (X puts the tree only in the hook).
- Why: same gap as X. Nobody says why he is "a sinner".
- Hard lines: the crowd line comes BEFORE "They all murmured." A newcomer hears "That he was gone to be guest with a man that is a sinner" with no one set up to say it, from an unknown voice, starting with "That". It is the hardest line in either script. Also "abide" (but "Jesus stayed with him anyway" explains it afterwards, which is good), "Son of man", "that which was lost".
- "He thought he was the seeker. Jesus was.": "Jesus was." is too clipped for an ESL ear.

**2. Bible reader**
- Quotes exact, with the same cuts as X (fourfold, son of Abraham).
- "The giving came after. Jesus came first.": the best line in either script. It shuts down a works reading clearly. Strong.
- "He looked up, saw him, and called his name": a fair paraphrase, not posing as a quote. Good.
- No group blame. Same missing "received him joyfully".
- Landing: same bolted-on "He died for our sins..." as X.

**3. Hook**: "A rich man wanted one look at Jesus. But who was really looking for whom?" The gap stays open to the end and pays off ("He thought he was the seeker"). But "who was looking for whom" is a stock, template-ish question, and there is less to picture (no tree yet). **First 3 s: 6.5/10.**

**4. Overall: 7.5/10.** Better shape: the gap is held, the tree is shown, and the line about grace before giving is explicit. It is let down by the crowd-line order and the clipped ending.

**5. Length**: ~147. Room for about 10 more words (use them for the "why" fix below). Could cut: "in the town of" leaving "Jericho".

## Winner: **Y**

(Option: borrow X's hook image. "A rich man climbed a tree for one look at Jesus. But who was really looking for whom?" That gives a picture AND keeps the gap.)

## 3 most important fixes for Y

1. **Crowd line order (newcomer can't follow it)**
   - old: `[crowd] That he was gone to be guest with a man that is a sinner.` / `[narrator] They all murmured. Jesus stayed with him anyway.`
   - new: `[narrator] The crowd grumbled:` / `[crowd] That he was gone to be guest with a man that is a sinner.` / `[narrator] Jesus stayed with him anyway.`

2. **Say WHY he is "a sinner"**
   - old: `Zacchaeus, chief tax collector in the town of Jericho.`
   - new: `Zacchaeus. Chief tax collector of Jericho. Rich. And everyone called him a sinner.`

3. **Landing: tie the cross to the story, not clipped**
   - old: `He thought he was the seeker. Jesus was. He died for our sins, and rose again the third day.`
   - new: `He thought he was the one looking. But Jesus was looking for him. Jesus died for our sins, and rose again the third day. He still looks for the lost.`

Nice to have (if words allow): after Jesus's call, add `[narrator] He came down fast, full of joy.` (Luke 19:6, "received him joyfully"). This is the emotional turn, and both scripts skip it.
