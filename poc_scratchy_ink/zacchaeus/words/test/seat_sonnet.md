# Seat Sonnet - blind test, Zacchaeus X vs Y

## Script X
1. Newcomer: Who = Zacchaeus, rich tax man. What = climbed tree, Jesus called him down, ate at his house. Why = Jesus came to find lost people. Clear. Hard words: "little of stature" (quoted, but easy from context), "abide", "make haste", "murmured", "tax collector" (ok), "salvation" (unclear to many), "Son of man" (unclear). The last line "He came to look. Jesus came to find." is clear. "He died for our sins, and rose again" lands but is a sudden jump for a newcomer.
2. Bible reader: Quotes match KJV. "looked up, and saw him" exact. "murmured" fine. Crowd line is exact ("saying" dropped, fine). Joy of receiving him is left out (fine). Added "said his name first" - true to text (Jesus speaks first). "Only then did Zacchaeus stand" - true order. "He did not know Jesus was looking for him" - mild inference, OK. "Jesus went to his house anyway" fine. No works-based message: giving shown as response, not price. But Jesus's "salvation is come" follows the giving directly, which could read as reward. The "Only then" line helps. No blame on a people group ("They all" = crowd, same as KJV). Dropped the "fourfold" restore and "son of Abraham" - acceptable.
3. Hook: "A man climbed a tree to look for Jesus. He did not know Jesus was looking for him." Strong picture plus reversal. Gap pays off at "He came to look. Jesus came to find." Score 8.
4. Overall 8.
Cut candidate: "Zacchaeus was a rich chief tax collector..." can be tightened; the "Jesus went to his house anyway. (pause) Only then" line is the one to keep.
5. Length: about 150 words. Good. Could cut "in the town of Jericho".

## Script Y
1. Newcomer: Who/what clear. Why is clear from "The giving came after. Jesus came first." Hook line "who was really looking for whom?" is a bit abstract for second-language ears. "Chief tax collector" fine. Crowd line has no lead-in, so "That he was gone to be guest with a man that is a sinner" is hard to parse (who is "he"?). That is the line a newcomer would not understand.
2. Bible reader: Quotes exact. Narration "Jesus came to that place. He looked up, saw him, and called his name" fine. "The giving came after. Jesus came first." Clear anti-works guard, strong. "He thought he was the seeker. Jesus was." Good but slightly over-claims what Zacchaeus thought (inference). No blame on a group. Drops "rich" from the narration after hook (it is in hook, fine).
3. Hook: "A rich man wanted one look at Jesus. But who was really looking for whom?" Question is clear but tells you the twist early and is less visual. Score 7.
4. Overall 7.5. Strong theology lines, weaker handoff on the crowd.
5. Length: about 145 words. Cut candidate: "So he ran ahead and climbed a tree" is needed for the picture; "He thought he was the seeker. Jesus was." could merge.

## Winner: X
X opens with a picture, handles the crowd with a clean lead-in, and has the clearer landing. Y has the best anti-works line ("The giving came after") which X should borrow.

## 3 fixes for X (old -> new)
1. "Jesus went to his house anyway. (pause) Only then did Zacchaeus stand and say:" -> "Jesus went to his house anyway. (pause) The giving came after. Zacchaeus stood and said:" (clearer anti-works guard; keep it before the Zacchaeus line)
2. "He came to look. Jesus came to find. He died for our sins, and rose again the third day." -> "He came to look. Jesus came to find. That is why Jesus died for our sins, and rose again the third day." (links the cross to the finding, less of a jump for a newcomer)
3. "...a rich chief tax collector in the town of Jericho. The crowd was in his way" -> "...a rich tax collector in Jericho. The crowd blocked his view" (shorter, plainer words; also saves a few words)

Also: add one plain gloss for "salvation", for example "This day, God's rescue has come to this house" is NOT KJV, so keep the quote and add a short narrator line after: "Salvation means rescue." Cut "Zacchaeus was" if time is tight.
