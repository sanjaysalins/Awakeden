# Write ONE narration from scratch — Zacchaeus (Luke 19:1-10). You are ONE blind writer; you have seen no other draft.

## Who it is for
Worldwide, many with English as a second language, many with no Bible knowledge. People who feel judged, written off, "too bad", or who are quietly searching. Also Bible readers who only know the kids' song.

## The chosen angle (owner-approved; binding)
SPINE: "Who was looking for whom?" Zacchaeus "sought to see Jesus who he was" (19:3) — he only wanted a look. But Jesus came to the place, "looked up, and saw him", and said his NAME first: "Zacchaeus, make haste, and come down; for to day I must abide at thy house" (19:5). The last verse turns it inside out: "For the Son of man is come to seek and to save that which was lost" (19:10). The man thought he was the seeker; Jesus was.
MIDDLE (one short beat): the crowd "all murmured" that He "was gone to be guest with a man that is a sinner" (19:7). Jesus went in anyway.
ORDER OF GRACE: Jesus comes to the house FIRST. Only after that does Zacchaeus give half to the poor and pay back fourfold (19:8). Then "This day is salvation come to this house" (19:9). Never make the giving the price of being found.
ENDING: lands on Jesus — He came to seek the lost, died for our sins, and rose again the third day (1 Cor 15:3-4, may be said plainly, no quote needed). Earned by the spine. Grace, not pressure.

## Hard rules
1. First 3 seconds: plain words; open the curiosity gap (who was really looking).
2. Name who's who simply: Zacchaeus, a rich chief tax collector in the town of Jericho; the crowd; Jesus. ("Publicans" = tax collectors — say it plainly.) Background like "tax men worked for Rome" may be said only as plain background, never as Scripture; keep it to a few words or leave it out.
3. KJV quotes verbatim and short. Never let an old word ("abide", "press", "fourfold") carry the meaning alone; plain English around it.
4. Do not mock his height ("little of stature" may be quoted once, plainly). No invented feelings or reasons ("he felt ashamed", "Jesus knew he was lonely").
5. No stock closer ("come to Jesus", "just ask Him", "He's waiting", "will you...?"). No fear, no pressure, no blanket promise.
6. Never blame "the Jews" or any people group: "the crowd", "they all".
7. 110-140 spoken words (about 45-58 s). One line per voice: [narrator], [jesus], [crowd], [zacchaeus]. Mark one (pause).
8. No wording about AI or how the film is made. No hashtags.

## Output (only this)
(a) the script; (b) word count; (c) KJV fragments with verse refs; (d) the 4 questions, one line each: who it is for, main take-away, the curiosity gap, why a global newcomer gets it.
