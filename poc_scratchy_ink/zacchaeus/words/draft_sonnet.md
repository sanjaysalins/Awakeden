# Zacchaeus - draft (sonnet)

## (a) Script

[narrator] A rich man wanted one look at Jesus. But who was really looking for whom?

[narrator] Zacchaeus, chief tax collector in Jericho. The crowd blocked him, and he was "little of stature." So he ran and climbed a tree.

[narrator] Jesus came to that place. He looked up, saw him, and called his name.

[jesus] Zacchaeus, make haste, and come down; for to day I must abide at thy house.

[crowd] That he was gone to be guest with a man that is a sinner.

[narrator] They all murmured. Jesus went in anyway. (pause)

[zacchaeus] Behold, Lord, the half of my goods I give to the poor.

[narrator] Giving came after. Jesus came first.

[narrator] He thought he was the seeker. Jesus was.

[jesus] The Son of man is come to seek and to save that which was lost.

[narrator] He died for our sins, and rose again the third day.

## (b) Word count
140 spoken words (about 55 s at a calm pace).

## (c) KJV fragments
- "little of stature" - Luke 19:3
- "Zacchaeus, make haste, and come down; for to day I must abide at thy house" - Luke 19:5
- "That he was gone to be guest with a man that is a sinner" - Luke 19:7 (they all murmured)
- "Behold, Lord, the half of my goods I give to the poor" - Luke 19:8
- "The Son of man is come to seek and to save that which was lost" - Luke 19:10
- Plain paraphrase, no quote: died for our sins, rose again the third day - 1 Cor 15:3-4

## (d) The 4 questions
1. Who it is for: anyone who feels judged or written off, and Bible readers who only know the kids' song.
2. Main take-away: Jesus came looking for the lost first; the changed life followed, it was not the price.
3. Curiosity gap: Zacchaeus climbs a tree to look for Jesus, but who was really looking for whom?
4. Why a global newcomer gets it: plain who/what (rich tax man, crowd, Jesus), one short quote each, and the twist is said out loud ("He thought he was the seeker. Jesus was.").
