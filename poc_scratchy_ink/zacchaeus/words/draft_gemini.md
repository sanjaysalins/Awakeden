**(a) Script**

[narrator] He thought he was looking, but he was being found. In Jericho, a chief tax collector named Zacchaeus couldn't see Jesus through the crowd, being "little of stature." So he climbed a tree to watch. But Jesus stopped, "looked up, and saw him," and called him.
[jesus] Zacchaeus, make haste, and come down; for to day I must abide at thy house.
[narrator] The crowd hated it.
[crowd] That he was gone to be guest with a man that is a sinner.
[narrator] Jesus brought grace first. (pause) Only then did the man change.
[zacchaeus] Behold, Lord, the half of my goods I give to the poor.
[narrator] He didn't have to get clean first. Jesus died for our sins and rose the third day for this exact reason.
[jesus] For the Son of man is come to seek and to save that which was lost.

**(d) Questions**
- **Who it is for:** People who feel written off or judged, and newcomers who only know the basic story or nothing at all.
- **Main take-away:** Jesus brings grace first—He seeks us out and invites us in before we ever clean up our lives.
- **The curiosity gap:** Opening with "He thought he was looking, but he was being found" makes the viewer instantly wonder who is actually being hunted.
- **Why a global newcomer gets it:** The language is plain, the characters are simply defined without insider jargon, and the radical order of grace is universally understood.
