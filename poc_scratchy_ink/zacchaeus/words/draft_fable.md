# Zacchaeus — draft (seat: fable)

## (a) Script

[narrator] A man climbed a tree to look for Jesus. He did not know Jesus was looking for him.

[narrator] Zacchaeus: a rich chief tax collector in the town of Jericho. He only wanted to see "who he was."

[narrator] Jesus "looked up, and saw him," and said his name first.

[jesus] "Zacchaeus, make haste, and come down; for to day I must abide at thy house."

[narrator] Today I stay at your house.

[narrator] They all murmured.

[crowd] "A man that is a sinner!"

[narrator] Jesus went in anyway. (pause)

[narrator] Only then, Zacchaeus stood:

[zacchaeus] "The half of my goods I give to the poor."

[jesus] "This day is salvation come to this house."

[jesus] "For the Son of man is come to seek and to save that which was lost."

[narrator] He came to look. Jesus came to find. He died for our sins and rose the third day.

## (b) Word count

138 spoken words (voice tags and the pause mark not counted; "to day" counted as two).

## (d) The 4 questions

- **Who it is for:** people who feel judged or written off, quiet searchers, and Bible readers who only know the kids' song.
- **Main take-away:** you may think you are the one looking for Jesus, but He came looking for the lost first.
- **The curiosity gap:** who was really looking for whom? Opened in line 1, paid off by Luke 19:10 and the last line.
- **Why a global newcomer gets it:** short plain sentences, three named parties (a rich tax collector, the crowd, Jesus), and each old word ("abide") is said again in plain English.
