1. Newcomer: I follow who and what: a short, rich tax collector wants to see Jesus; Jesus calls him and visits him despite the crowd’s anger. Why Jesus chooses him becomes clear at the end. Why people dislike him is less clear.

Hard phrases: “little of stature,” “make haste,” “abide at thy house,” “murmured,” “the half of my goods,” “salvation,” and “Son of man.” A newcomer may not know that “Son of man” means Jesus.

2. Bible reader: All quoted words match the supplied KJV. Some are shortened excerpts, with changed end punctuation. Zacchaeus’s promise to repay fourfold and Jesus’s words about Abraham are omitted.

“Jesus was looking for him” is a fair inference from verse 10. “Jesus came first. The giving came after” explains the sequence, though sequence alone does not explain grace. The script does not make giving the price of salvation, but placing the gift directly before “salvation” could leave that impression.

The death and resurrection ending comes from outside Luke 19 and agrees with the supplied Corinthians passage. No people group is blamed.

3. Hook: 7/10. The tree gives a clear picture. The stronger surprise comes after the first three seconds. Jesus calling his name pays it off.

4. Overall: 7/10. Strong focus on Jesus, but too much difficult English. At roughly 250 words, it needs a rushed pace for 60 seconds, especially with pauses.

5. Three line fixes:

- “He was short, ‘little of stature,’ and the crowd blocked his view.” → “He was too short to see past the crowd.”
- “They all murmured:” → “The crowd complained:”
- “Jesus came first. The giving came after.” → “Jesus came to save him. His gift did not buy God’s love.”
