# Zacchaeus — blind draft (opus)

## (a) Script

[narrator] He climbed a tree to see Jesus. Who was really looking for whom?

[narrator] Zacchaeus, a rich chief tax collector in Jericho, "sought to see Jesus" — just a look. He was "little of stature"; the crowd was thick.

[narrator] Jesus came, "looked up, and saw him" — and said his name first.

[jesus] "Zacchaeus, make haste, and come down; for to day I must abide at thy house."

[narrator] They all murmured:

[crowd] "...gone to be guest with a man that is a sinner."

[narrator] Jesus stayed with him anyway. (pause) Only then Zacchaeus said:

[zacchaeus] "Lord, the half of my goods I give to the poor."

[jesus] "This day is salvation come to this house. For the Son of man is come to seek and to save that which was lost."

[narrator] He thought he was the seeker. Jesus was. He died for our sins and rose the third day.

## (d) The 4 questions

- **Who it is for:** anyone who feels written off or is quietly searching, plus Bible readers who only know the kids' song.
- **Main take-away:** Zacchaeus thought he was looking for Jesus; Jesus was the one looking for him, and found him before he changed.
- **Curiosity gap:** "Who was really looking for whom?" — paid off when Jesus says his name first and then says He came "to seek ... that which was lost."
- **Why a global newcomer gets it:** one man, one tree, one crowd, plain words; the old word "abide" is explained by "Jesus stayed with him", and the turn is said plainly: "He thought he was the seeker. Jesus was."
