## 3. GLOBAL AUDIENCE (who / what / why in my own words; where I got lost or misled, quote the line; words that fail in translation)

A wealthy, disliked tax collector cannot see Jesus; Jesus chooses to visit him; he responds by giving and making restitution (2–10). The missing emotional context is why that visit scandalises everyone: “called a sinner” labels him without fully explaining the social betrayal (2).

“Make haste,” “abide,” “Behold,” and “Son of man” demand interpretation (4, 9, 13–14). “Salvation” receives a useful gloss (12), but “lost” can still sound geographically lost rather than spiritually estranged (14). These are comprehension costs, especially through subtitles.

## 4. BIBLICALLY ACCURATE (claim by claim: RIGHT / OVERCLAIMED / UNSUPPORTED / WRONG, quote the line; say only what you can support)

- **RIGHT:** “chief tax collector,” rich, short, and called by name match Luke’s account (2–4).
- **RIGHT, with qualification:** “Tax men often took extra money” is background, not something Luke 19 explicitly establishes about Zacchaeus; the script appropriately uses “anyone he cheated” later (2, 10).
- **RIGHT:** The welcome, grumbling, half to the poor, fourfold restitution and salvation declaration follow the account (5–11).
- **OVERCLAIMED if presented as explicit chronology:** “Jesus came first. The change came after” is an interpretation of the narrative sequence, not Luke’s stated description of Zacchaeus’ prior inner condition (8).
- **RIGHT as Christian explanation:** The salvation gloss and death/resurrection summary extend beyond this episode; the latter reflects the wider gospel proclamation (12, 15).
