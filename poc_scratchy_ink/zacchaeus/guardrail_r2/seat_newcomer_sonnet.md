## 1. WHO IS THIS FOR
A phone viewer who likes a short human story, like a 30-year-old shop worker who scrolls past tax or money drama. She stops at line 1 (a man in a tree, "looking for him" is odd). She finishes if she wants to know what happens. She shares only if a friend is in a "nobody likes me" moment. NOT served: someone who needs to know who Jesus is. Line 1 says "Jesus" like I should know him. I do not.

## 2. MAIN TAKEAWAY
"Jesus found a rich, disliked man first, and then the man changed." Lines 8 and 15 carry it ("Jesus came first. The change came after." and "Zacchaeus came to look. Jesus came to find him."). It was fairly clear up to line 14. Line 15 then adds a new big claim that I could not connect.

## 3. GLOBAL AUDIENCE
In my words: A short rich man, hated for taking extra money, climbs a tree to see a famous teacher. The teacher says he will stay at his home. People complain. The man gives away money. The teacher says something about "salvation" and about finding lost people.
Where I got lost:
- Line 1 and others: I never learn WHO Jesus is. A teacher? A king? A god?
- Line 2: "called a sinner". What is a sinner? Who calls him that, and why is it bad?
- Line 2: "Jericho" is a place name, fine, but unexplained.
- Line 4: "make haste", "abide", "thy" are old English. I cannot parse them. Line 5 repeats them in simple words, which helps, but it feels like hearing it twice.
- Line 11: "salvation come to this house". Unclear until line 12.
- Line 13 and 14: "Son of man". Whose son? Is it Jesus or someone else? "That which was lost": lost what? Lost how? Zacchaeus was not lost in a place.
- Line 12: "God rescues you and brings you back to Him". Rescues from what? Back from where? I did not know I was away.
- Line 15: "died for our sins, and rose again the third day". Total surprise. Who killed him? What are "our sins"? Why does his death matter for this man? Why is "third day" important? This feels like a different film glued on the end.
- Line 7: a silent pause with no picture explanation I can use. I guessed it is a beat for the walk to the house.
Words that fail in translation: sinner, salvation, lost, abide, make haste, Son of man, rose again, third day.

## 4. BIBLICALLY ACCURATE
I cannot judge accuracy. What I was TOLD that I could not follow: "sinner" (2, 6), "Son of man" (13, 14), "that which was lost" (14), "salvation" meaning (11, 12), "died for our sins" and "rose again the third day" (15), and who Jesus is (1).

## 5. BEST VERSION POSSIBLE
The best explainer would say in the first 10 seconds who Jesus is in one plain phrase ("a famous teacher many believed was sent by God"). It would keep all old English out of the spoken lines or give the plain version first. It would end on the Zacchaeus story, or give the death and rising claim at least two lines of setup. The ONE highest-value change: line 15, either explain it or cut it. Score: 6/10. The tree story and the "he gave half" turn are clear and moving. The end and the missing intro hurt.

## CURIOSITY GAP
Opens at line 1 ("Jesus was looking for him"). Pays off at line 8 to 9 (he changes after Jesus comes), and again at line 15. Score 6/10. Worth the wait for the story, less so for the ending, which opens new questions instead of closing the first one.

## THREE SMALLEST FIXES
- Line 1: "A man climbed a tree to see Jesus, a teacher many believed was sent by God. But Jesus was looking for him."
- Line 14 follow-up, replace line 12 with: "Salvation means God saves you from being cut off from Him, and brings you home."
- Line 15: "Zacchaeus came to look. Jesus came to find him. Jesus came for everyone like him, even to die in their place, and then rise again."
