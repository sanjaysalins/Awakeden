## 1. WHO IS THIS FOR (one named viewer; who stops, who finishes, who shares; who is NOT served)
Named viewer: Amara, 24, Lagos, English as a second language, knows the name "Jesus" and little else, feels judged by church people.
- **Stops:** scrollers who like a reversal. Line 1 gives one in 14 words.
- **Finishes:** newcomers and ESL viewers. Lines 5 and 12 translate the hard parts for them.
- **Shares:** Bible readers and Sunday-school teachers. Line 8 ("Jesus came first. The change came after.") is the line they will quote.
- **NOT served:** the sceptic who asks "why should I care?". The film never turns to the viewer. Line 15 says "our sins" but nobody has told the viewer he is in the story.

## 2. MAIN TAKEAWAY (one sentence a viewer could say back; which line carries it; did the film make it clear?)
"Jesus goes looking for people before they fix themselves."
Line 8 carries it, and line 15 ("Zacchaeus came to look. Jesus came to find him.") seals it. It is clear. But there are two takeaways fighting: grace-before-change (line 8) and seeking-the-lost (lines 14-15). They are compatible, but the film never joins them in one sentence.

## 3. GLOBAL AUDIENCE (who / what / why in my own words; where I got lost or misled, quote the line; words that fail in translation)
**Who:** a rich, hated tax man. **What:** he climbs a tree to see Jesus, Jesus invites Himself to his house, and the man gives his money away. **Why:** Jesus came to find and rescue lost people.

Where it slips:
- Line 2: "called a sinner" is not explained. A newcomer does not know that it means "far from God, not welcome". Line 2 also stacks four facts in one breath.
- Line 9: "Behold, Lord, the half of my goods" is archaic. Line 10 rescues it well.
- Line 13: "the Son of man" is a title with no meaning for a newcomer. The gloss "Jesus, the Son of man" is the minimum; it works, just.
- Line 14: "that which was lost" — lost what? A coin? Line 15 fixes this ("the lost"), but late.
- Line 15: the jump from a house in Jericho to "died for our sins, and rose again the third day" is abrupt. It reads as a creed bolted on.

Words that fail in translation: "make haste", "abide", "thy" (line 4, fixed by line 5); "grumbled" (line 6); "goods" (line 9).

## 4. BIBLICALLY ACCURATE (claim by claim: RIGHT / OVERCLAIMED / UNSUPPORTED / WRONG, quote the line; say only what you can support)
- Line 1, "Jesus was looking for him": RIGHT in sense (v10), slightly OVERCLAIMED as narrative. The text says He "looked up, and saw him".
- Line 2, chief tax collector, rich, short, "called a sinner": RIGHT (vv2-3, 7). "Often took extra money": RIGHT as background (Luke 3:12-13).
- Line 4: RIGHT, KJV verbatim (v5).
- Line 5, "Today I will stay": OVERCLAIMED downward. The KJV says "I **must** abide". The "must" is the point: divine purpose, not a whim.
- Line 6: RIGHT (v7). "Jesus went anyway" is a fair inference.
- Line 8: RIGHT. The order in the text supports it.
- Line 9: RIGHT, verbatim, truncated honestly.
- Line 10, "four times back to anyone he cheated": OVERCLAIMED. The text is conditional: "**if** I have taken any thing". The line states the cheating as fact.
- Line 11: RIGHT, verbatim. It drops "son of Abraham", which is fine at this length.
- Line 12: RIGHT as a plain definition.
- Line 14: RIGHT, verbatim (v10).
- Line 15: RIGHT (1 Cor 15:3-4). It is not in Luke 19, but "Later" flags that honestly.

No WRONG claims. No UNSUPPORTED claims.

## 5. BEST VERSION POSSIBLE (what would the best explainer in the world do differently; the ONE highest-value change; score 0-10 against that bar)
The best version would make the viewer feel he is the man in the tree. This film tells a good story about someone else, then recites the gospel.

**The ONE change:** rebuild the last sentence of line 15 so the cross is the same seeking, aimed at the viewer, not an appendix.

It would also thin line 2, and cut the double "Hurry down" in lines 4-5 to buy time.

Score: **7/10**. Clear, faithful, well ordered. Not yet personal.

## CURIOSITY GAP (opens at line 1, pays off at line 14; score 0-10; worth the wait?)
Opens at line 1, pays off at lines 14-15. Score: **6/10**. Line 1 answers its own question in the second sentence, so the open gap is only "why him?". The payoff is real and the echo in line 15 is satisfying. Worth the wait, but the tension is low after line 6.

## THREE SMALLEST FIXES (line number + exact replacement wording)
1. **Line 5:** "Hurry down, Jesus said. Today I must stay at your house. Zacchaeus came down fast, full of joy."
2. **Line 10:** "Half of all he had. And if he had cheated anyone, four times back."
3. **Line 15:** "Zacchaeus came to look. Jesus came to find him. He is still seeking the lost. For that, He died for our sins, and rose again the third day."
