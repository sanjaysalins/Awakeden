## 1. WHO IS THIS FOR

Priya, 34, on a bus in Bengaluru. English is her third language. Captions are on. Auto-translate is on. She knows the name Jesus and not this story.

She finishes if she catches the plain lines (2, 5, 10, 12, and the first half of 15). She stops at line 4, when the voice turns into old English and the caption cannot translate it. She might share “he looked, Jesus found him.” She will not share the ending. “Died for our sins” and “rose again” start a second story with no time left.

Not served: a first-time viewer with no church words, and anyone who depends on translation. No words baked into the pictures were in this pack, so those frames could not be checked. If they show “make haste,” “abide,” “Behold,” or “goods,” they fail the same way the voice fails.

## 2. MAIN TAKEAWAY

“The short man climbed a tree to see Jesus, but Jesus had come to find him.”

Line 15 carries it. Line 1 plants it. That one sentence is clear.

It is not the only point the film asks her to keep. Line 8 (“the change”), line 12 (“salvation”), and the death-and-rising tail of line 15 each want to be the point. She can repeat the tree. She cannot repeat what “salvation” did inside that house.

## 3. GLOBAL AUDIENCE

Who: Zacchaeus, a short rich man in Jericho who took extra tax money from his own people. What: he climbs a tree, Jesus says he must stay at his house, the crowd objects, the man promises half his money and a four-times payback, Jesus says rescue has come to that house. Why: Jesus says he came to find and save lost people. The last line adds that he later died and rose.

Lost at line 8. “Jesus came first. The change came after.” “Came first” means he arrived earlier, not “he chose him before he changed.” “The change” never says a change of what.

Misled by line 4 next to line 5. “I must abide” and “I will stay” are not the same strength. “Abide” often means “put up with,” not “stay.”

Words that fail in translation and captions:

- Line 4: “make haste,” “to day,” “abide,” “thy.” Plain words: hurry, today, stay, your.
- Line 9: “Behold,” and “goods.” “Goods” becomes “products.” Plain: “Lord, I give half of what I own to the poor.”
- Line 10: “four times back.” “Back” does not mean “pay.”
- Line 11: “is salvation come.” The grammar breaks. “House” here is the family, not the building. Same trap in lines 4, 5, and 6.
- Lines 13–14: “Son of man” sounds like “his father was a man.” In some places it sounds like “he is only human.” “Is come” and “that which was lost” do not survive translation. “Lost” can mean dead, or missing in the street.
- Line 15: “for our sins” can mean “because of our wrongs” or “in our place.” “Rose again” becomes “stood up a second time.” “The third day” never says after his death.
- Line 3: “came by,” and “called” (gave him a name, or shouted to him). Line 6: “anyway.”

What travels with no church background: too short to see, hated for taking money, joy, public shame, giving half away. Line 2 (“took extra money from their own people”) is the best gloss in the film. Inviting himself into the house can sound rude unless line 5’s joy is unmistakable.

## 4. BIBLICALLY ACCURATE

Checked against Luke 19:1–10 as this film uses it.

- Line 1, the climb: RIGHT. “Jesus was looking for him”: RIGHT as the point of Luke 19:10, said before the story shows it.
- Line 2: job, Jericho, rich, short, called a sinner: RIGHT. “Often took extra” is background, not a verse.
- Lines 3 and 4: RIGHT. Line 4 is the old wording.
- Line 5: the event is RIGHT. “I will” drops “I must.” Weaker, not false.
- Line 6: RIGHT.
- Line 8: the order is RIGHT. Jesus speaks, then the promise. “The change came after” as a closed fact is OVERCLAIMED. Luke does not use the word “change,” and the promise can be read as a new choice or as what he already does.
- Lines 9 and 10 together: RIGHT. Line 9 alone cuts the four-times promise. Line 10 puts it back.
- Line 11: RIGHT, and it cuts “he also is a son of Abraham.”
- Line 12: “rescues” is a fair plain sense. “Brings you back to Him,” and the sudden “you,” go past “this house.” OVERCLAIMED.
- Lines 13–14: RIGHT, old wording of Luke 19:10.
- Line 15, first half: RIGHT. “Later… died for our sins, and rose again” is outside this scene. “Later” keeps the clock honest. Luke 19 does not say it.

## 5. BEST VERSION POSSIBLE

The best explainer would use one plain register. The man, Jesus, and the crowd would speak today’s words. An old Bible line would sit on screen, short, in the viewer’s language, not as a spoken riddle. “House” would be said once as “family.” The film would end on find-and-save. Death and rising would get their own plain sentence, or wait.

The one highest-value change: do not speak the old sentences in lines 4, 9, 11, and 14. The plain meanings are already written. They are buried under the old ones.

Score: 5/10. The tree, the tax gloss, the joy, and the rescue definition are real work. The old English and the last clause throw that work away for this viewer.

## CURIOSITY GAP

Opens at line 1. Pays off early at line 5, then again at line 15. The wait from 1 to 5 is worth it. The wait inside lines 4, 9, 11, and 14 is not. The viewer is stalled in sentences she cannot translate. The last clause of line 15 opens a new gap, why he died and what “rose again” means, and does not pay it off.

Score: 5/10.

## THREE SMALLEST FIXES

1. Line 4: “Zacchaeus, hurry and come down. Today I must stay at your house.”
2. Line 8: “Jesus came to him before he changed. Then Zacchaeus stood up and said:”
3. Line 15: “Zacchaeus came to look. Jesus came to find him. Later, Jesus died for our wrongs. On the third day after his death, he was alive again.”
