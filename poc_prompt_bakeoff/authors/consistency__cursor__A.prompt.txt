At dawn half-light before a rock-cut garden tomb, a great heavy disc roll-stone rests rolled aside from the dark empty opening where no figure lies within; three startled first-century Judean women stand close — thirtyish Mary Magdalene in a terracotta wool tunic and rust mantle, dignified middle-aged Joanna in olive-brown wool with a charcoal mantle, and a grey-haired elder in dusty beige — each holding small clay spice jars, mantles drawn over their heads, sandals on packed earth.
