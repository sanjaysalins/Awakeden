Morph Test — Emmaus

A composition-matched before/after pair for “Knew It Not,” crossfaded at 25/50/75% to preview what the on-screen morph would actually look like.

The honest result: it doesn't read as a morph. The modern shot came out excellent on its own. But the biblical render didn't match its geometry closely enough — the hands are in a different position, the empty chair and the seated figure don't line up, and Christ ended up reading a book instead of reaching for the bread. The 50% blend proves it: it reads as two images overlapping, not one thing becoming another. A real morph needs frame-accurate alignment — same hand pixels, same table edge, same negative space — which two independently generated stills can't reliably deliver. That's a real production requirement to solve (tight manual compositing, or an actual morph/video tool), not a flaw in the idea itself.

What this actually tells us

The idea's mechanic depends entirely on geometric precision the current still-by-still pipeline doesn't naturally produce. Worth deciding: try again with much tighter composition instructions (locking exact hand/chair pixel positions), or treat the morph as a real animation-time problem (image-to-video with a designed transformation) rather than something to prove with two static renders.
