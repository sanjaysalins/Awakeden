Lettering redesign — why the caption box feels alien, and 4 real replacements

2026-07-28 night. Every frame below is a REAL composited render over our own Jericho art (or a real extracted frame from ArkAIology's own project) — not mockups or descriptions.
DIAGNOSIS The caption box isn't loosely inspired by ArkAIology — it IS ArkAIology's own vox-motion VerseQuoteCard component, just recolored. Their own rendered reference (below) shows the exact same structure we shipped in Jericho: a translucent rectangular card, bold sans-serif caps, a yellow highlighter band on the emphasis phrase, a small caps reference line. That grammar is a data-explainer / Instagram-infographic convention — built for ArkAIology's documentary-data brand. Painting it warm colors doesn't change what it structurally IS: a UI chip laid on top of a hand-painted page, like a phone notification over a museum painting. It will always read as alien no matter the palette, because the STRUCTURE is the problem, not the color.

ArkAIology's actual look (real frames, their repo)

Their data/countup treatment — line chart card + 3D printed numerals. Built for THEIR docu-data brand, not ours

What we shipped in Jericho (the one you hate)

Structurally identical to ArkAIology's card above — cream instead of gray, italic instead of Bangers, but the same rectangular translucent chip sitting on top of hand-painted ink wash

Four real replacements — all rendered on the same Jericho art

RECOMMENDED1 — Scribed Ink. The verse hand-written in real ink calligraphy (Kunstler script), no box at all. Ties directly into the "page draws itself" ink-in device already built for the comic pipeline — same DNA, one visual language across the whole series.
STRONG ALT2 — Illuminated Rubric. A gold-leaf dropped capital + printed-Bible serif body + hairline page rules + red rubric reference. Reads like an actual historic Scripture page. Best choice if a beat needs to feel more FORMAL/canonical than handwritten.
FALLBACK3 — Pinned Scrap. A torn parchment note physically laid on the page, rotated, wax-sealed. Closest cousin to the old box, but transformed into an OBJECT rather than a UI card. Safe if a verse is too long for hand-lettering to stay legible.
SECONDARY USE4 — Marginalia. A small neat annotation in the page's own margin with a hand-drawn leader pointing at the detail. Elegant, but too subtle at video size for a MAIN verse reveal — better reserved for a secondary "field note" beat, not the primary Scripture display.

The display-type beats ("13 LAPS", "THE WALL FELL DOWN FLAT") need the same fix

CURRENTArial Black + yellow highlighter — the same internet-explainer grammar, lifted directly, on the headline beats too.
RECOMMENDEDInk Stamp. Rough-pressed rubric-red serif caps, unevenly inked like a real stamp — no box, no highlighter. Reads as PRESSED onto the page, not typed onto a screen.

My recommendation

Adopt Scribed Ink for verse reveals and Ink Stamp for display-type beats as the new default. Both share one root idea — everything on the page should look HAND-MADE, pressed or written, never a floating screen element — which finally makes the lettering system feel like it grew from the same hand that drew the art, instead of being pasted on top of it. Keep Illuminated Rubric in reserve for formal/glory beats (e.g. the gospel pivot verse) where a more ceremonial register earns its weight.
