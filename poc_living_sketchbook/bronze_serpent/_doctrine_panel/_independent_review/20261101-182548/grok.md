# Independent review — grok (OK, 207s)

The John 3:14 line matches the KJV word for word. The spine holds: snake lifted, Jesus lifted, look and live. The gospel offer is still thinner than the text it quotes, and the middle will lose a first-time listener.

**Hook: 8.** “I am Moses. My people were dying of snakebite — and God told me to forge a snake of bronze” is concrete and strange in the first breath. It is not a “Did you know” open.

**Landing: 6.** “Look, and live” does reuse this piece’s own verb. “Lift your eyes to Jesus, lifted up for you” sits next to the stock closer “look to Jesus.” The close never lands faith, the curse, or eternal life. It could end almost any “Jesus was lifted up” short.

**Doctrine**

- “I begged Him to take the snakes away. He would not.” Numbers 21:7 says the people asked Moses to pray and “Moses prayed for the people.” The LORD’s recorded answer is the serpent (21:8). A flat refusal is an added beat, put in Moses’s mouth as fact.
- “They lifted Jesus on a Roman pole, made a curse for us” chases the pole rhyme. John says “lifted up.” Galatians 3:13 says “being made a curse for us” and “hangeth on a tree.” “Pole” is the contested stake reading. The grammar also gives the curse-making to “They.” In Galatians 3:13 and 2 Corinthians 5:21, that act is God’s, in Christ, for us. “Bearing our judgment in our place” is the sound half of the same sentence.
- The quote stops at John 3:14. John 3:15 is the point: “whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have eternal life.” “Costs you nothing but a look” swaps belief for a glance and never says eternal life. “The bitten had only to look — and live” is right for the bronze snake. It is the wrong last word for Jesus.

**First hearing**

- “From the far side of my life, by the light that came after — a night I never saw, when one they called Teacher answered a seeker” is a clever frame. A listener with no Bible hears a dead man describe a night he missed, a nameless Teacher, and a nameless seeker.
- The KJV line is not marked as someone else’s words. Moses then says “as Moses lifted up the serpent,” so he seems to switch into third person about himself.
- “Teacher,” “Son of man,” and “Jesus” arrive as three names. “Son of man” is never glossed.
- The forge order is said twice before the story moves: “forge a snake of bronze and lift it on a pole,” then “forge the image of the very thing killing us, and lift it high.” The second line is the real insight. The repeat stalls the open.

**What holds**

“We had spoken against God” and death as judgment match Numbers 21:5–6. “My bronze was only a shadow” is fair typology because John makes the link. “The cure was never in you” keeps the rescue outside the hearer. That grace note is undercut by reducing the response to a free glance.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Carry the quote through John 3:15 and make “look” mean “believeth”; cut “costs you nothing but a look” and name eternal life.
2. Drop “Roman pole” and “They … made a curse for us”; say Jesus was lifted up on the cross, made a curse for us by God, bearing our judgment in our place.
3. Cut the “night I never saw / Teacher” frame, mark the John 3 words as the Teacher’s, and replace “I begged… He would not” with Moses prayed and the LORD answered with the serpent.
