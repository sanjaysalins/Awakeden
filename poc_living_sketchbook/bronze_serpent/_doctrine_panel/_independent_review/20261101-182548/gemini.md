# Independent review — gemini (OK, 67s)

**Doctrinal soundness:** 
Pass. The typological link between the bronze serpent and Christ on the cross is orthodox and grounded perfectly in John 3:14. Framing Christ as "made a curse for us, bearing our judgment" is biblically faithful (Galatians 3:13).

**KJV verbatim:** 
Pass on the words, but fail on the formatting. The text uses John 3:14 exactly ("And as Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, even so must the Son of man be lifted up:"), but it lacks quotation marks. Because you kept the KJV's trailing colon, the scripture bleeds directly into the next thought ("...lifted up: My bronze was only a shadow"), which will confuse a voice actor and blur the line between scripture and script. 

**Clarity on first hearing:** 
Fail. "a night I never saw, when one they called Teacher answered a seeker" is a clever-but-confusing conceit. You are referencing Nicodemus coming to Jesus by night (John 3:2), but a listener with zero Bible knowledge will have no idea what "night" or "seeker" means here. It is an inside baseball reference that distracts from the core message. 

**Grace-anchored conviction:** 
Pass. "The cure was never in you; it hangs in plain sight, and costs you nothing but a look" is a fantastic, grace-heavy distillation of the gospel. No works framing here.

**Freshness = faithful depth:** 
Pass. Having Moses look back from eternity to see the fulfillment of his bronze serpent is a fresh, engaging narrative device. 

**One thread spine:** 
Pass. The thread holds beautifully from the snakebite (hook) to the bronze serpent (middle) to the cross (climax) to "Look, and live" (CTA).

**SCORE THE HOOK:** 7 
"I am Moses" is a weak, Sunday-school-play opening. It wastes the crucial first second. The actual hook is the second sentence: "My people were dying of snakebite..." Start with the immediate stakes and conflict to stop the scroll, rather than a theatrical roll call. 

**SCORE THE LANDING:** 9
"Lift your eyes to Jesus, lifted up for you. Look, and live." This is an excellent, earned landing. It uses the exact imagery and verbs of the piece and couldn't be pasted onto a different video. 

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Cut the Nicodemus riddle ("a night I never saw... answered a seeker"). It confuses a non-churched listener. Transition to Jesus' words more directly and plainly.
2. Add quotation marks around the KJV text to clearly separate it from Moses' commentary, and address the trailing colon so the voice actor knows where the quote ends.
3. Punch up the hook. Drop "I am Moses" and start immediately with the visceral stakes: "My people were dying of snakebite..."
