# Independent review — codex (OK, 67s)

**Hook: 8/10.** “I am Moses. My people were dying of snakebite” establishes a speaker and immediate danger. The strange remedy creates curiosity quickly enough to stop a stranger.

**Landing: 8/10.** “Look, and live” grows directly from the serpent narrative and its repeated looking/lifting imagery. It earns its place here. The problem is the explanation immediately before it.

1. **“He would not” turns an inference into God’s explicit refusal.** Numbers records the people requesting removal, Moses praying, and God providing a remedy. It does not record God refusing to remove the serpents. “I begged Him” also adds emotional detail beyond the account. The categorical refusal manufactures a sharper dramatic opposition than Scripture supplies. [Numbers 21:7–9, KJV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Numbers+21%3A7-9&version=KJV)

2. **“Costs you nothing but a look” leaves the saving response dangerously unclear.** The intended claim is grace, but the wording makes looking sound like a minimal payment or an effortless visual action. Jesus explicitly connects the lifted serpent to believing in Him in the next verse. For someone with no Bible knowledge, “Lift your eyes” never clearly becomes trusting Christ. This needs an explicit connection between looking and faith, without presenting faith as the price of salvation. [John 3:14–15, KJV](https://biblia.com/bible/kjv/john/3/14-15)

3. **“I speak now from the far side of my life, by the light that came after — a night I never saw” creates an unnecessary perspective puzzle.** Is Moses speaking from heaven, receiving later revelation, or functioning as an imagined narrator? The script leaves that unresolved while putting Christian exposition into his mouth. This is not necessarily invented doctrine, but it is invented testimony, and the literary mechanism competes with the biblical event on first hearing.

4. **“One they called Teacher answered a seeker” conceals information the novice needs.** Neither person is identified before the quotation. “Son of man” then introduces another unexplained designation. Listeners must retrospectively work out that Jesus is speaking and referring to Himself. Naming Jesus at this transition would remove that avoidable burden.

5. **“They lifted Jesus on a Roman pole” weakens clarity at the central connection.** Repeating “pole” preserves the image, but an unfamiliar listener needs to hear that Jesus was crucified on a cross. Otherwise “lifted up” can remain a vague physical action.

**KJV check:** There are no quotation marks in the supplied narration. The clearly introduced quotation beginning “And as Moses lifted up” nevertheless matches John 3:14 KJV verbatim; no altered word was found. “Bronze” occurs in paraphrase, so it is not a quotation violation.

**Doctrinal and structural assessment:** The serpent-to-Christ connection is explicitly biblical, and “made a curse for us, bearing our judgment in our place” has an orthodox evangelical basis. The judgment language describes the passage rather than directly threatening the listener. The script maintains one main thread, but its freshness relies partly on an elaborate fictional speaking position instead of deeper explanation of the text. [John 3:14–15](https://biblia.com/bible/kjv/john/3/14-15), [Galatians 3:13](https://kjvstudy.kennethreitz.org/book/Galatians/chapter/3/verse/13)

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Remove the unsupported categorical claim “He would not.”
2. Explain looking as trusting Christ; remove the implied price in “costs you nothing but a look.”
3. Simplify Moses’ retrospective voice and explicitly identify Jesus, His words, and His crucifixion.
