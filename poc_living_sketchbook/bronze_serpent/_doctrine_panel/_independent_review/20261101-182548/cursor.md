## Independent adversarial review — `bronze_serpent` doctrine-panel narration

### Doctrinal soundness
- **Mostly orthodox** (Numbers 21 → John 3 typology, substitution language: **“made a curse for us, bearing our judgment in our place”** aligns with Galatians 3:13 and standard evangelical reading).
- **Overstated narrative about God:** **“I begged Him to take the snakes away. He would not.”** Numbers 21:7–8 records the people asking Moses to pray, Moses praying, and God giving the bronze serpent remedy. The passage does **not** record God refusing to remove the serpents. You’ve turned inference into a flat **“He would not”** — that paints God as withholding the very mercy the people asked for, when the text shows Him answering with a different (stranger) cure. That’s not contrarian doctrine, but it **misrepresents the text** and weakens grace in the middle.
- **Truncated gospel link:** The piece stops John’s analogy at **“even so must the Son of man be lifted up:”** and never carries **John 3:15** (*believeth … eternal life*). Typology alone is fine; stopping there while saying **“costs you nothing but a look”** risks a **look-only** cure without anchoring **trust in Christ** the way Jesus himself does in the next verse.

### KJV verbatim
- The embedded line **“And as Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, even so must the Son of man be lifted up:”** matches project KJV (John 3:14) **word-for-word** (including **“Son of man”**).
- **No quotation marks** in the artifact despite the header promising **“KJV quotes”**. That matters for delivery: after **“answered a seeker:”** the listener cannot tell where Scripture ends and Moses resumes — **“lifted up: My bronze was only a shadow”** sounds like one continuous utterance.
- Non-quoted narration uses **“bronze”** / **“forge”** where KJV has **“brass”** / **“Make thee a fiery serpent”** (Numbers 21:8) — acceptable in prose, not a quote violation.
- **“Look, and live”** is not KJV verbatim (21:9: *“when he looketh upon it, he lived”*) and is not marked as quotation; fine as paraphrase, but don’t treat it as a KJV line.

### Clarity on first hearing (zero Bible knowledge)
- **Opening story is followable:** dying → pole → look → live.
- **Middle collapses for strangers:** **“I speak now from the far side of my life, by the light that came after — a night I never saw, when one they called Teacher answered a seeker:”** is insider poetry (post-death Moses + Nicodemus night + unnamed Jesus). A listener with no Bible background gets **no name for “Teacher,” no scene, no who the seeker is** — the Christ link arrives as **“They lifted Jesus”** with almost no runway.
- **“Roman pole”** is accurate historically but **breaks Moses’s voice** and adds a label a bitten Israelite wouldn’t use; it’s a narrator’s wink, not Moses’s mouth.
- **“you who are bitten — that is every one of us”** works as metaphor once sin=bite is established; the bite thread is clear enough.

### Grace-anchored conviction
- **Strong grace beats:** **“The cure was never in you”** and **“costs you nothing but a look”** push away self-cure.
- **Friction:** **“the venom was the judgment our sin had earned”** sits in the same breath-world as **“earned”** and **“bearing our judgment in our place”** without a clear **“gift / believe / not perish”** line from John. A fearful listener can hear **judgment earned** louder than **grace given** — not works-salvation, but ** emotionally judgment-forward** for a 60s invite.
- No obvious fear/gain-loss CTA (“or else”), which is good.

### Freshness = faithful depth
- **Surprise is front-loaded** (**“forge the image of the very thing killing us”**) — that’s faithful to the strangeness of Numbers 21.
- **Pivot is familiar:** bronze shadow → cross is standard John 3 preaching, not a clickbait reading. **“My bronze was only a shadow”** doesn’t mine an overlooked textual detail; it states the usual typology in one line.

### One thread spine (hook → middle → CTA)
- **Single spine holds:** snakebite → lifted serpent → look/live → Jesus lifted → look to Him.
- **No full thread swap**, but the **Nicodemus frame** is a **second register** (prophetic hindsight) layered on Moses — it doesn’t abandon the thread, it **thickens the voice** in a way that costs clarity.

### Hook score: **7/10**
- **Not a template open** (“I am Moses” beats “Did you know”).
- First ~5 seconds deliver **death + bizarre divine order** (**“forge a snake of bronze and lift it on a pole”**), which has scroll potential.
- **“I am Moses”** is slow for anyone who doesn’t instantly care about that persona; the **sharpest irony** (**image of the thing killing us**) lands **after** the setup, not in the very first beat. Good, not irresistible.

### Landing score: **6/10**
- **“Look, and live”** is earned from the piece’s own verb and Numbers idiom — stronger than a naked **“Come to Jesus.”**
- **Penultimate line is stock:** **“Lift your eyes to Jesus, lifted up for you”** is interchangeable with many cross shorts (look/turn/lift eyes family).
- **Could the exact final sentence end another piece?** **“Look, and live.”** — yes: any “look/faith” short, and your own Bronze Serpent long-form copy already uses that phrase generically. The **pole, bronze, and snake** are gone from the last breath; the close is **verb-only**, not **image-specific** (e.g. bronze on the pole / lifted curse).
- Rubric: under **8** on landing → **REVISE**.

VERDICT: REVISE  
TOP FIXES:  
1. Remove or rewrite **“I begged Him to take the snakes away. He would not.”** to match Numbers 21:7–8 (people ask, Moses prays, God gives the serpent remedy) — stop attributing a refusal Scripture doesn’t record.  
2. Quote John **3:14–15** verbatim with clear start/stop (voice or **“the Teacher said”**), so **“look”** is tied to **“whosoever believeth in him … eternal life”** — not a vague visual act alone.  
3. Replace the opaque **“a night I never saw, when one they called Teacher answered a seeker”** block with one plain sentence a stranger gets (e.g. Jesus naming this story to Nicodemus), and rebuild the close from **this** image (**bronze serpent / pole / bitten camp**) before **“Look, and live”** — cut or compress **“Lift your eyes to Jesus”** as a stock half-step.
