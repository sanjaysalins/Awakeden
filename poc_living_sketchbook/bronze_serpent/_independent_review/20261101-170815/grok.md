# Independent review — grok (OK, 209s)

The spine holds: snakes, a bronze snake on a pole, Jesus lifted up, look and live. The John 3:14 line matches the KJV word for word. It is not in quotation marks. No quoted word is altered. The piece still fails the first-hearing test, overstates the text, and lands soft.

**HOOK: 7**
**LANDING: 6**

Hook is 7. The first words a stranger hears are "I am Moses. My people were dying of snakebite." The real stop — God tells him to forge a snake — sits at the end of that long sentence, past a cold 5-second scroll. "I am Moses" is a costume open, so many will swipe before the paradox.

Landing is 6. "Lift your eyes to Jesus, lifted up for you" is the stock closer "look to Jesus" with this piece's verb glued on. The exact final sentence, "Look, and live," could end a different piece (any "just look" short, or Isaiah 45). The pole, the bronze, and the bite are gone by the last breath.

**"He would not" is not in the text.** Numbers 21:7 records the ask: pray that the LORD take the serpents away. Moses prays. God answers with the bronze snake. The script states a refusal — "I begged Him to take the snakes away. He would not." That puts a "no" in God's mouth the passage does not record, and it makes the God of the piece sound like He withheld mercy.

**The command is told twice.** First: "God told me to forge a snake of bronze and lift it on a pole." Later: "Instead He told me to forge the image of the very thing killing us, and lift it high." On one hearing, that sounds like two orders. The timeline doubles back. "Forge" is also extra. The KJV says "make," then "set it upon a pole."

**The middle is a riddle.** "I speak now from the far side of my life, by the light that came after — a night I never saw, when one they called Teacher answered a seeker." A listener with no Bible knowledge does not know this is Moses after death, Nicodemus at night, or Jesus. "Son of man" arrives with no name. Jesus is named only after the quote: "They lifted Jesus on a Roman pole." Too late. "A night I never saw" is an Easter egg for readers. For everyone else it is fog. "Roman pole" hides the word they do know: cross.

**"They ... made a curse for us" points at the wrong actor.** The line is "They lifted Jesus on a Roman pole, made a curse for us, bearing our judgment in our place." On one hearing, "They" lifted him and "They" made the curse. Galatians 3:13 says Christ was made a curse for us. Rome crucified him. God is the one who made him the curse and the substitute. The grammar blurs that.

**"Costs you nothing but a look" prices the look.** "The cure was never in you" is grace. "Costs you nothing but a look" puts the look on a ledger. The look becomes the fee. That fights the grace the line is trying to say.

The judgment line is sound: "we had spoken against God, and the venom was the judgment our sin had earned" matches Numbers 21:5-6. "My bronze was only a shadow" is ordinary typology, not a new doctrine. The thread does not swap. The conceit in the middle is what breaks it.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Replace "far side of my life / a night I never saw / one they called Teacher / a seeker" with one plain line that names Jesus before the KJV sentence, and say "cross" once.
2. Cut "He would not" and the second "forge" retell. One timeline only: sin, snakes as judgment, they begged, God gave a bronze snake on a pole, look and live.
3. Rebuild the last sentence from the bite and the pole, drop "costs you," and make God (not "They") the one who made Jesus a curse in our place.
