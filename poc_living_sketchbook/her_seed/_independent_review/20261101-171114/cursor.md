## Independent adversarial review (narration)

### KJV verbatim
- The only material in quotation marks is Galatians 4:4. It matches the project KJV cache exactly: *“But when the fulness of the time was come, God sent forth his Son, made of a woman, made under the law.”*
- **Problem (boundary, not a typo):** After that full verse, you say *“He writes: made of a woman, the woman promised in the garden.”* A first-time listener will hear **Paul** as the author of *“the woman promised in the garden”* — that phrase is **not** KJV and not in Gal 4:4. This is the worst kind of near-miss: it looks like a stitched “quote” and will fail a careful KJV check even though only the first clause is technically outside the marks.

### Doctrinal soundness
- **Mostly orthodox:** Protoevangelium (Genesis 3 / “her seed”), Christ planned before the cross, pushback on “God improvised Plan B” — standard evangelical Christ-from-the-garden preaching, not clickbait novelty.
- **Over-tightened exegesis (flag, don’t fail):** *“Paul could have written son of David, son of Abraham… Here, he doesn’t.”* Paul **does** use David/Abraham lineage elsewhere (e.g. Rom 1:3; Gal 3:16). The move is rhetorically fine for a short, but it is **not** “Paul never uses those titles” — it is “in **this** sentence he chose *made of a woman*.” Without that nuance, a Bible-literate listener may think you are claiming something false.
- **“Made of a woman” → only Eve/garden:** Many readers hear **Mary / true humanity / incarnation** in Gal 4:4, with Genesis 3:15 as **echo**, not sole referent. Collapsing the line to *only* “the woman promised in the garden” is preachable but **contestable**; presented as what Paul *“writes”* (see above) it reads like you are putting words in Paul’s mouth.
- **Context drop:** Gal 4:5 (*“To redeem them that were under the law…”*) is the immediate gospel “why” of the sending. You quote v.4 and pivot to Plan A/B and self-help — **redemption/adoption** never gets a plain-English toehold. Not heresy, but the doctrinal center of the pericope is muted.

### Clarity on first hearing (zero Bible knowledge)
- **Strong:** Plan B vs “the plan,” and *“the fix, the resolution, the version of you that finally gets it right”* — concrete, followable.
- **Weak:** *“Genesis already named this seed”* — no KJV snippet; “seed” is metaphor-heavy without *“her seed”* or “offspring who would crush the serpent.”
- **Weak:** *“Paul writes”* — no “apostle,” no “letter to Galatia”; fine for churchy Shorts, rough for a total stranger.
- **Weak / ambiguous:** *“kept word for word”* — word of **what**? God’s promise? Scripture? The phrase sounds like legal precision but doesn’t tie visibly to “seed” or “made of a woman” on one pass.
- **Mis-attribution risk (serious):** *“He writes: made of a woman, the woman promised in the garden”* — see KJV section; this is a logic/clarity bug, not a stylistic nit.
