# Independent review — grok (OK, 162s)

The Galatians 4:4 quotation matches the KJV. The problems are the identification of the woman, the argument Paul is made to stage, and a close that leaves this text behind.

**Quote check.** Inside the quotation marks, "But when the fulness of the time was come, God sent forth his Son, made of a woman, made under the law" is exact KJV. No word there is altered.

**The seed and the woman change places.** "Genesis already named this seed — a promise spoken over a woman in the garden." Then: "He writes: made of a woman, the woman promised in the garden." Genesis 3:15 promises her seed. The speech is addressed to the serpent. "The woman promised" makes the woman the promised thing. The words after "He writes:" add a gloss Paul does not write, so a first hearing takes "the woman promised in the garden" as his line. "Named first" will also be heard as the garden text saying the name Jesus. "Kept word for word" treats "her seed" and "made of a woman" as the same wording.

**The fresh turn is a contest with Paul.** "Paul could have written son of David, son of Abraham — a line that runs through the man, like every title before it. Here, he doesn't." Galatians 3:16 already says Abraham's seed "is Christ." Romans 1:3 says the Son was "made of the seed of David according to the flesh" — Paul's own "made of" line through David. Galatians 4:4–5 is the sending under the law, to redeem and to adopt. "Made under the law" is quoted and then dropped. "Like every title before it" has no anchor a listener can point to.

**First-hearing knots.** "You've heard it as Plan B" assumes the frame. "Eden," "this seed," "fulness of the time," "made under the law," "son of David," and "son of Abraham" arrive with no gloss. "Jesus wasn't a rescue invented in the fulness of time" collides with the verse just quoted, where the fulness of the time is when God sent him. The sense "decided earlier" only appears on a second pass.

**Spine and close.** The hook and the proof are God's plan from the garden. "You still reach for your own rescue plans — the fix, the resolution, the version of you that finally gets it right" opens a self-fix thread the verse never built. "He has already arrived for you" is gift language, so that clause stays on the grace side of the test. It is also a closer that could end a different piece. The seed, the woman, and "made of a woman" never reach the last sentence.

**Hook: 6/10.** "Plan B" and "God improvised a rescue" are concrete in the first sentence. "You've heard it as" is a soft correction open, and "Eden" is unexplained for a stranger. Under 8.

**Landing: 5/10.** "This was never Plan B" belongs to this piece. "Kept word for word" does not survive the two texts. "He has already arrived for you" is a portable arrival line. Under 8.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Keep the promise on her seed, spoken to the serpent. Cut "the woman promised in the garden," "named first," and "kept word for word," and keep Paul's words inside the quote only.
2. Drop the "son of David, son of Abraham" contest. Let "made of a woman, made under the law" mean the Son really sent under the law to redeem. Galatians 3 and Romans 1:3 already carry Abraham and David.
3. Run one spine to the last sentence: Plan B, the garden seed, He has come. Cut the late self-fix turn, gloss Eden and "seed" on first hearing, and build the final line from this piece's seed so it cannot close another film.
