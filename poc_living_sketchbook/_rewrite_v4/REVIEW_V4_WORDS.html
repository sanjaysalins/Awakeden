1. Rahab and the red cord

Hook 6-9   Last line 5-8   Clear 7-9   (4 readers)   ~62 s

When Jericho fell, one family walked out alive.

Her name was Rahab. She lived in Jericho's wall. The Bible calls her a prostitute.

Two spies came from God's people, Israel. She hid them, because she believed in their God.

They told her: hang a red cord in your window, and your family will be safe.

[silence 1.0s]

Israel marched. The people shouted. And "the wall fell down flat".

Rahab and her family were spared.

The cord was the sign. Trusting the promise saved her. A book of the Bible, Hebrews, says it in two words: "By faith".

She is named in the family line of Jesus, God's Son, who "taketh away the sin of the world".

[silence 1.0s]

Rahab could not stop a wall. She tied one cord to a promise.

Tie your trust to Jesus, and bring everyone you love inside.

Still weak

  • Last line is still a general 'trust Jesus' shape. Two readers rate it 5-6.
  • 'prostitute' is plain, but it is our word for 'harlot' (not a quote).
  • Jump from Rahab to Jesus is a bit sudden for a newcomer.

2. Aaron and the two goats

Hook 8-9   Last line 6-9   Clear 7-9   (4 readers)   ~61 s

One goat died. One goat lived. Why two?

I am Aaron, the high priest. I lived long before Jesus, so watch my goats.

The first I killed, to pay for the people's sin. I carried its blood behind the curtain, where God's presence was, and no one else could go.

The second I did not kill. I laid both hands on its head, and confessed the people's sins. Then it was sent away alive, into the wilderness.

[silence 1.0s]

The dead goat could not walk away. The living goat could not pay.

Jesus, God's Son, did both. He came "to put away sin by the sacrifice of himself".

When He died, the temple curtain tore, "from the top to the bottom".

[silence 1.0s]

I went in once a year, alone. Through Jesus, the curtain is open. Walk through, to God.

Still weak

  • 'The living goat could not pay' is a Christian reading of the two goats, not Leviticus' own words. It is in Aaron's voice.
  • 'high priest' is still unexplained.
  • I added 'to pay for the people's sin' after round 2. NOT re-tested.

3. Moses and the snake on a pole

Hook 8-9   Last line 8-9   Clear 7-9   (4 readers)   ~65 s

Snakes were killing my people. God's cure? A snake.

I am Moses.

My people spoke against God. He sent snakes. Many died.

They cried, "We have sinned". I prayed.

God did not take the snakes away. He said: make a snake, set it on a pole.

I made one of brass, and lifted it up.

Anyone bitten who looked at it, lived.

[silence 1.5s]

The cure looked like the thing that was killing them.

I lived long before Jesus, God's Son. But He said:

"as Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, even so must the Son of man be lifted up".

Lifted up: He meant Himself, on a cross.

A later writer, Paul, said that on the cross God "made him to be sin for us".

[silence 1.5s]

The bitten looked at a snake, and lived. Believe in Jesus, lifted up on the cross, and live.

Still weak

  • I swapped the Paul line to 'made him to be sin for us' (2 Cor 5:21) after round 2. NOT re-tested.
  • Runs about 65 s. It is the longest.
  • 'Believe in Jesus ... and live' is partly a stock ending. The snake half carries it.
