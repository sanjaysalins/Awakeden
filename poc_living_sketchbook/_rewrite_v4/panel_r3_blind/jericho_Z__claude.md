**1. Who / what / why**
- **WHO:** Rahab, a woman in the city of Jericho.
- **WHAT:** She hid two spies. They told her to hang a red cord in her window. The city's wall fell, and she and her family were saved.
- **WHY it matters:** She trusted God's promise and it saved her. The film says Jesus is the same kind of rescue for you.

**2. Who is it for?**
People who are curious about faith. It also works for Christians who want a short, easy retelling.

**3. Main takeaway**
Rahab was saved by trusting a promise, so trust Jesus the same way.

**4. Words or lines I would not understand**
- "prostitute" is a hard word for second-language viewers.
- "spies" and "marched" are fine if the pictures show them.
- "spared" is hard. "Saved" would be clearer.
- "She lived in Jericho's wall" is odd. I picture a hole in a wall.
- "By faith" in "two words" is unclear. Does faith just mean trust?
- "taketh away the sin of the world" is old English. What is "sin"?
- "the family line of Jesus" comes from nowhere. Why does Jesus appear?
- "invite everyone you love" is unclear. Invite them to what?
- Who is "God's people, Israel"? It is a lot to take in.

**5. Bible check**
NONE that is wrong. I checked these:
- "the wall fell down flat" matches Joshua 6:20.
- "taketh away the sin of the world" matches John 1:29.
- Rahab is in Jesus's family line in Matthew 1:5.
- Hebrews 11:31 says "By faith the harlot Rahab perished not."
- Her house was on the town wall (Joshua 2:15).

Small points:
- The Bible says "scarlet thread" or "line", not "red cord". That is only a plain-English choice.
- "Trusting the promise saved her" is a Christian reading. It is fair and backed by Hebrews and James.
- The film does not say the cord stands for Jesus's blood. Good, because that is a later reading.

**6. Hook and payoff**
- **Hook: 6/10.** "One family walked out alive" makes me ask who and how. But I don't know what Jericho is, so it is weaker.
- **Payoff: 7/10.** It answers the question: the cord and her trust. The jump to Jesus is quick.

**7. Landing**
- **Score: 6/10.**
- "Rahab could not stop a wall. She tied one cord to a promise." is specific to this story.
- "Tie your trust to Jesus" reuses the cord image, which is good.
- "Invite everyone you love" is generic. It could close almost any short.

**8. Overall clarity: 7/10**

It is not the best version yet. The one thing I would change is the jump from Rahab to Jesus. Add one plain line before it, such as "God keeps His promises. Jesus is the biggest one." That would make the sin line and the family-line fact easier to follow. I would also swap "prostitute" and "spared" for plainer words.
