1. **Who / what / why**
   - **Who:** Aaron, an old priest, speaking about two goats. It's also about Jesus.
   - **What:** One goat was killed and its blood taken behind a curtain. The other goat had the people's sins put on it and was sent away alive. Jesus did both jobs, and the curtain tore when He died.
   - **Why it matters:** The way to God is now open to everyone, not just one priest once a year.

2. **Who it's for:** Curious viewers and Christians. A newcomer can follow it, and a Bible reader gets the link between the goats and Jesus.

3. **Main takeaway:** Jesus did what both goats did, so you can now go straight to God.

4. **Global (words I'd have to re-hear):**
   - "high priest" is a bit unclear.
   - "the curtain" and "behind the curtain": I don't know which curtain it is until the temple curtain tears. Nothing connects the two curtains.
   - "confessed the people's sins" is OK.
   - "pay" in "could not pay" is unclear. Pay what?
   - "put away sin by the sacrifice of himself" is heavy language.
   - "the temple curtain tore" comes with no setup. Why does a torn curtain matter?
   - Aaron says "watch my goats" and then talks about Jesus dying. That's confusing, because Aaron lived long before Jesus.

5. **Bible check:**
   - The two quotes match the KJV. Hebrews 9:26 reads "to put away sin by the sacrifice of himself". Matthew 27:51 reads "from the top to the bottom".
   - The goat ritual matches Leviticus 16. This version leaves out the bull Aaron offered for himself first.
   - "Jesus did both" is a Christian reading (typology). No verse says the live goat stands for Jesus. It is stated as plain fact.
   - Aaron talking about Jesus' death is an anachronism. It's a storytelling device, but it could mislead.
   - The temple veil in Matthew is not the same as the tabernacle curtain Aaron used. Close enough, but the script doesn't explain it.

6. **Hook:** 8/10. "One goat died. One goat lived. Why two?" is clear and curious. **Payoff:** 7/10. "Jesus did both" answers it, but the explanation is fast and not fully spelled out.

7. **Landing:** 6/10. "Walk through, to God" is tied to the curtain image, but "Walk through, to God" could close other Jesus shorts. The line "I went in once a year, alone" does give it some specificity.

8. **Clarity: 7/10.** Not the best version yet. **One change:** Say plainly that the curtain blocked people from God, and that Jesus' death tore it open. That would link the two curtains and explain why the tear matters.
