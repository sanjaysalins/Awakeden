# Cold read — Fable seat (blind, round 3)

Read only the three Z candidates. Two passes each: newcomer (ESL, no Bible), then strict KJV checker.

---

## 1. jericho_Z.txt (Rahab)

**(1) Who / what / why**
- Who: Rahab, a woman in Jericho, called a prostitute.
- What: She hid two Israelite spies, hung a red cord in her window, and her family alone survived when the wall fell.
- Why: She believed in Israel's God and trusted the spies' promise; the cord was the sign.

**(2) Main takeaway**
Trust in a promise saved her ("By faith"); tie your trust to Jesus and bring your people with you.

**(3) Newcomer stumbles**
- "When Jericho fell" — nothing says Jericho is a city until "Jericho's wall." A newcomer may hear a person's name.
- "cord" — ESL learners know "rope/string" better. The picture will carry it.
- "spared" — OK but less common than "lived" / "were safe."
- "taketh away" — archaic, but short and parseable in passing.
- "Hebrews" — "A book of the Bible, Hebrews" is enough, but ESL ears may still hear "the Hebrew people."
- "She tied one cord to a promise" — figurative; a literal reader may stall for a beat. Acceptable as the turn line.

**(4) Doctrinal / factual / misquote check (KJV)**
- "the wall fell down flat" — Joshua 6:20, verbatim. OK.
- "By faith" — Hebrews 11:31 opens "By faith the harlot Rahab perished not." OK.
- "taketh away the sin of the world" — John 1:29, verbatim fragment. OK.
- "She lived in Jericho's wall" — KJV: "her house was upon the town wall, and she dwelt upon the wall" (Josh 2:15). "In" vs "upon" — minor paraphrase, fine.
- "The Bible calls her a prostitute" — KJV says **harlot** (Josh 2:1; 6:17; Heb 11:31; Jas 2:25). Not in quote marks, so not a misquote, but "the Bible calls her" + a non-KJV word reads to a strict ear like a quotation claim. Minor.
- "hang a red cord" — KJV: "this line of scarlet thread" (Josh 2:18). Paraphrase, unquoted, fine.
- "your family will be safe" — the promise was conditional: only those inside the house (Josh 2:19). Omitted, not wrong.
- "named in the family line of Jesus" — Matt 1:5 "Rachab." Identification with Jericho's Rahab is the standard reading, not spelled by the text. Fine.
- No doctrinal error. No Christian reading mis-stated as the Bible's own claim. "Trusting the promise saved her" is the Bible's own attribution (Heb 11:31).

**(5) Hook 7 / Payoff 7**
Hook: good curiosity ("one family walked out alive") but "Jericho" undefined for a newcomer. Payoff: cord → promise → "By faith" lands well; the jump to the genealogy + John 1:29 is a little bolted on (a fact, then a quote about sin, not about faith).

**(6) Last-line specificity: 8**
"Tie your trust" echoes the cord; "everyone you love" echoes her family. Good. "Invite" is softer than what Rahab actually did.

**(7) Clarity: 8**

**(8) The ONE change**
Change the CTA to the story's own action: "Tie your trust to Jesus — and bring everyone you love inside." Joshua 2:18-19: she had to bring her household into the house; only those inside were safe. "Inside" closes the loop with the cord and the wall; "invite" does not.

## 2. two_goats_Z.txt (Aaron)

**(1) Who / what / why**
- Who: Aaron, the high priest, long before Jesus.
- What: Two goats on one day — one killed and its blood carried behind the curtain; the other had the people's sins confessed over it and was sent away alive.
- Why: Together they picture what Jesus did alone — paid for sin and carried it away; the curtain tore when He died.

**(2) Main takeaway**
Jesus did what both goats did; the way to God is now open — walk through.

**(3) Newcomer stumbles**
- "high priest" — a newcomer may not know what a priest is; one word ("God's priest" / "the chief priest") would help.
- "wilderness" — KJV word; "desert" is the global word. Minor.
- "confessed" — religious word, but the sentence shows what it means.
- **"The living goat could not pay"** — pay for WHAT? The narration never said the first goat died for the people's sins. So "pay" arrives with no set-up. This is the one real comprehension gap.
- "the temple curtain" — Aaron's curtain is never called a temple curtain; a newcomer may not be sure it is the same curtain. Mostly carried by the repeated word.

**(4) Doctrinal / factual / misquote check (KJV)**
- "laid both hands on its head, and confessed the people's sins" — Lev 16:21 "lay both his hands upon the head of the live goat, and confess over him all the iniquities." Exact. OK.
- "sent away alive, into the wilderness" — Lev 16:21-22. OK.
- "carried its blood behind the curtain, where God's presence was, and no one else could go" — Lev 16:2, 15; Heb 9:7. OK ("vail" paraphrased as "curtain," unquoted).
- "to put away sin by the sacrifice of himself" — Heb 9:26, verbatim. Strong choice: "put away" = scapegoat, "sacrifice" = slain goat.
- "from the top to the bottom" — Matt 27:51 / Mark 15:38, verbatim. OK.
- "I went in once a year, alone" — Heb 9:7 "alone once every year"; Lev 16:34. OK.
- "Through Jesus, the curtain is open" — Heb 10:19-20. Christian claim, grounded, stated as claim not quote. OK.
- **Flag (mild):** "The living goat could not pay." Lev 16:10 says the live goat was presented "to make an atonement with him." The Bible does not say the live goat could not atone; the two-halves reading (blood pays, scapegoat carries) is traditional Christian typology (with Heb 9:22 behind it). It is in Aaron's voice, not "the Bible says," so it is not stated as Scripture's own claim. Acceptable, but know it is a reading.
- Minor: Aaron also killed a bullock first (Lev 16:11); omitted, fine for 60s.
- No misquote. No doctrinal error.

**(5) Hook 8 / Payoff 8**
Hook: three short sentences, a real question. Payoff: "could not walk away / could not pay / Jesus did both" answers the question cleanly — but "pay" is under-prepared (see 3).

**(6) Last-line specificity: 9**
"Once a year, alone" / "curtain is open" / "walk through" — only this story can end this way.

**(7) Clarity: 8**

**(8) The ONE change**
Line 3: say why the first goat died, so "pay" lands. E.g. "The first I killed, for the people's sins. I carried its blood behind the curtain..." (Lev 16:15: "the goat of the sin offering, that is for the people"). Five words; fixes the only gap.

## 3. bronze_serpent_Z.txt (Moses)

**(1) Who / what / why**
- Who: Moses, leader of a people (Israel is not named; "my people" is enough).
- What: The people spoke against God; snakes came and many died; God had Moses make a brass snake on a pole; anyone bitten who looked at it lived.
- Why: Jesus said He would be lifted up like that snake — on a cross — and whoever believes in Him lives.

**(2) Main takeaway**
The cure looked like the thing that was killing them. Jesus, lifted up on the cross, is that cure — look / believe and live.

**(3) Newcomer stumbles**
- "serpent" (in the quote) — never glossed as "snake." Context carries it, barely.
- "Son of man" — unexplained title. "He meant Himself" rescues it, just.
- "brass" — OK; "bronze" is the more global word but KJV says brass. Fine.
- "wilderness" — see above.
- **"made a curse for us"** — "curse" is abstract and not unpacked. The piece's logic ("the cure looked like the killer") needs this line to mean "He became what was killing us," but a newcomer will not get there from the word "curse" alone.
- "The bitten looked at a snake" — "the bitten" as a noun is hard for ESL; "Those who were bitten" is plainer.

**(4) Doctrinal / factual / misquote check (KJV)**
- "spoke against God" — Num 21:5 "the people spake against God." OK.
- "He sent snakes. Many died." — Num 21:6 "fiery serpents... much people of Israel died." OK.
- "We have sinned" — Num 21:7, verbatim. OK. "I prayed" — 21:7. OK.
- "God did not take the snakes away" — the people asked that He "take away the serpents"; the text records the pole as the answer, not an explicit refusal. Fair inference, not a stated fact. Acceptable.
- "make a snake, set it on a pole" — Num 21:8 paraphrase, unquoted. OK.
- "one of brass" — Num 21:9 "a serpent of brass." OK.
- "Anyone bitten who looked at it, lived" — Num 21:9. OK.
- "as Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, even so must the Son of man be lifted up" — John 3:14, verbatim. OK.
- "Lifted up: He meant Himself, on a cross" — John 12:32-33 ("signifying what death he should die"). John's own gloss. OK.
- "made a curse for us" — Gal 3:13, verbatim fragment; "on the cross" matches "hangeth on a tree." OK.
- "Believe in Jesus... and live" — John 3:15. The look → believe mapping is John's own. OK.
- No misquote, no doctrinal error, no Christian reading mis-attributed to the text.

**(5) Hook 9 / Payoff 8**
Hook: the best of the three — a paradox in eight words. Payoff: strong through "He meant Himself, on a cross"; the Paul line is the right idea but the weakest sentence for a newcomer.

**(6) Last-line specificity: 9**
"Looked at a snake, and lived / believe in Jesus, lifted up, and live" — a direct mirror.

**(7) Clarity: 8**

**(8) The ONE change**
Make the Paul line pay off "the cure looked like the killer" in plain words. Best option: swap Gal 3:13 for 2 Cor 5:21 — "A later writer, Paul, said that on the cross God "made him to be sin for us"." "Sin" is already in the piece ("We have sinned"), so the snake = sin = what Jesus became chain closes without the unexplained word "curse." If Gal 3:13 must stay, add one clause: "...He was "made a curse for us" — He became what was killing us."

## Cross-file notes (brief)

- Two of three use the same line "I lived long before Jesus" (goats, serpent); Jericho has no narrator-self but the same structural slot. Fine once; as a series tic it will show.
- All three CTAs mirror the story's own action (tie / walk through / look-believe). This is the strongest shared trait — keep it.
- Ranking on clarity for a total newcomer: serpent > goats > Jericho. Ranking on KJV tightness: goats = serpent > Jericho (only soft spot is "prostitute" for "harlot," unquoted).
