1. **WHO:** Rahab, a prostitute in Jericho who believed in Israel’s God.
   **WHAT:** She hid Israel’s spies; she and her family survived Jericho’s destruction.
   **WHY:** Her deliverance illustrates faith, and she appears in Jesus’ genealogy.

2. **AUDIENCE:** People unfamiliar with the Bible, especially those open to hearing about Jesus.

3. **MAIN TAKEAWAY:** Trust Jesus as Rahab trusted God, and invite your loved ones to do the same.

4. **GLOBAL:** NONE.

5. **BIBLE:** The quotations match the KJV. Two qualifications: the family’s protection required gathering inside Rahab’s house and keeping the spies’ business secret (Joshua 2:18–20); Hebrews connects her faith explicitly with receiving the spies (Hebrews 11:31). The cord-to-Jesus connection is a Christian application, rather than an explicit biblical explanation.

6. **HOOK: 9/10. PAYOFF: 8/10.** Yes, I wanted to know which family survived and why. Both questions were answered, though the opening stresses the family more than the ending does.

7. **LANDING: 5/10.** “Tie” recalls the cord, but the rest could close many Christian shorts.

8. **CLARITY: 9/10.** Not the best possible version. My one change: make the promise’s condition explicit—“Hang this scarlet cord in your window, and gather your family inside this house; everyone who stays inside will be safe.”
