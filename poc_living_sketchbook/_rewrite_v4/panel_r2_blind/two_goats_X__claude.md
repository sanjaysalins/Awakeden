1. **WHO / WHAT / WHY**
- WHO: Aaron, an old priest who served God for his people. Also Jesus.
- WHAT: Aaron used two goats. One was killed. One was sent into the desert with the people's sins. Jesus did both jobs in one.
- WHY: Because the "curtain" between people and God is now open. I can go to God.

2. **FOR WHOM:** People curious about Christianity, or Christians who want to see how the old rituals point to Jesus.

3. **TAKEAWAY:** Jesus did what both goats did. He died for sin and took it away, so anyone can now come to God.

4. **GLOBAL (would need to re-hear):**
- "high priest" (a little unclear).
- "the curtain between people and God." I get it is a barrier, but I don't know what it is or where.
- "laid both hands on its head, confessing" is odd. I don't know why he does it.
- "iniquity" (hard word, but the quote carries it).
- "taketh away" (old English).
- "the LORD hath laid on him" (old English).
- "Walk through, to God." Walk through what? I have to guess it means the curtain.
- Who is "Jesus" and why does the goat story lead to Him? I only know it from the line "Jesus did both."
- "A living goat has not died for it" is dense. I had to think.
- "once a year" is unexplained.

5. **BIBLE:**
- The quotes look right. Isaiah 53:6 is "the LORD hath laid on him the iniquity of us all." John 1:29 is "taketh away the sin of the world." Matthew 27:51 and Mark 15:38 say the veil tore "from the top to the bottom".
- The quotes are not credited the same way. Isaiah is named. The John line is not named ("He ... taketh away"). That line was said about Jesus by John the Baptist, not by Jesus. The narration blends it in as if it follows on.
- "Jesus did both" is a Christian reading. Hebrews builds it, and Leviticus 16 never says it. Calling it Bible fact without saying so is a small overreach.
- Leviticus 16 has the goat of the sin offering and the scapegoat. The narration is mostly faithful. One small gap: Aaron says "I went in once a year" in the past tense, but the practice continued after him.
- Aaron "I lived long before Jesus" is true.
- Nothing is clearly wrong. The main thing is the Christian reading, which is not flagged as one.

6. **HOOK:**
- Hook: 8/10. "One goat died. One walked away alive. Why two?" makes me curious.
- Payoff: 7/10. "A dead goat cannot carry sin away. A living goat has not died for it. Jesus did both." answers it. But "Why two?" is answered more by implication, and the answer is dense for a newcomer.

7. **LANDING:** "Now the curtain is open. Walk through, to God." could close another short about the temple curtain or access to God. It ties to "the curtain" and "once a year, alone", so it is fairly specific to this story. Score: 7/10.

8. **CLARITY: 6/10.** It is not the best version possible.
- ONE change: say what the curtain is in one plain line early on ("a thick cloth wall inside God's house, hiding God's room"). Then say who Jesus is in a few words ("Jesus, God's Son") when He first appears. Right now both are left for the viewer to guess, and the ending depends on them.
