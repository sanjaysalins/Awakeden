**1. Who / what / why**
- **Who:** Rahab, a woman in the city of Jericho. Two spies from Israel. Jesus, later on.
- **What:** She hid the spies. They promised her a red cord would save her family. The city fell, and her family was spared.
- **Why it matters:** Trust is what saved her, and Jesus is the one to trust now.

**2. Who it's for:** Christians and people curious about faith. It suits anyone who feels their past counts against them.

**3. Main takeaway:** Rahab trusted God's promise and her family was saved, so trust Jesus and bring your family too.

**5. Bible check**
- The quotes match the KJV: Joshua 6:20, Hebrews 11:31 ("By faith"), and John 1:29.
- Rahab is in Jesus' line in Matthew 1:5, and she lived in the wall in Joshua 2:15.
- **Mild issue:** "The cord did not save her" and "what saved her, in two words" are a reading, not a direct claim. Joshua says the cord was the sign. James 2:25 adds works. I'd soften it.
- **Mild issue:** "Bring everyone you love" could read as a guarantee that your family is saved. The Bible doesn't promise that.
- Otherwise: NONE.

**6. Hook and payoff**
- **Hook: 7.** "One family walked out alive" makes me ask who and how.
- **Payoff: 7.** The ending answers it: faith, and the cord as a mark.

**7. Landing: 6**
"Tie your trust to Jesus, and bring everyone you love" is tied to the cord image, so it fits this story. But "trust Jesus, bring your people" could close many other shorts. The cord echo is the only part specific to Rahab.
