**1. WHO is speaking or who is this about, and WHAT happened, and WHY does it matter?**
* **WHO:** An ancient priest named Aaron.
* **WHAT:** He used to use two goats to deal with people's sins, but Jesus replaced the goats by doing both jobs Himself. 
* **WHY:** Because Jesus did this, a curtain blocking God tore open, meaning anyone can go to Him directly now.

**2. WHO is this short for?**
Random people scrolling who might stop for a weird animal riddle and end up hearing a message about reaching God.

**3. MAIN TAKEAWAY**
Jesus permanently opened the door to God that used to be blocked.

**4. GLOBAL**
"iniquity" (I'd have to guess from context that it means sin, but it's a weird word).

**5. BIBLE**
NONE. The quotes are accurate to the KJV (Isaiah 53:6, John 1:29, Mark 15:38/Matt 27:51) and the Day of Atonement two-goat typology (Leviticus 16 / Hebrews 9) is standard, sound doctrine.

**6. HOOK**
* **Hook: 9/10.** The goat riddle is weird, short, and makes you want to know the answer.
* **Payoff: 10/10.** "A dead goat cannot carry sin away. A living goat has not died for it. Jesus did both." perfectly and satisfyingly answers the opening question.

**7. LANDING**
* **Score: 8/10.** "Now the curtain is open. Walk through, to God." You couldn't just slap this on any random video; it relies heavily on the curtain setup from the first half of the script. 

**8. OVERALL CLARITY**
* **Score: 9/10.** 
* **ONE thing I would change:** Swap the word "iniquity" for something normal so a scroller doesn't get tripped up trying to figure out what it means (even though it's a quote, it breaks the flow for someone who doesn't know church words).
