## 1. Jericho X

1. **Who / what / why**
   - Who: Rahab, a woman in Jericho known for her sin; two Israelite spies; Jesus at the end.
   - What: she hides the spies, hangs a red cord, the wall falls, her family alone survives.
   - Why: faith in the promise saved her, and she ends up in Jesus' family line.
2. **Takeaway:** Tie your trust to Jesus the way Rahab tied her cord to a promise, and bring your family with you.
3. **Newcomer blocks:** "Israel marched" (who is Israel?); "Hebrews says" (a people? a book?); "lived in its wall" (a house in a wall is odd without a picture); "taketh" (archaic, but short enough to pass).
4. **KJV / doctrine check:**
   - "the wall fell down flat" — Josh 6:20, exact.
   - "By faith" — Heb 11:31, exact.
   - "taketh away the sin of the world" — John 1:29, exact.
   - Her name in His line — Matt 1:5 ("Rachab"), correct.
   - "both hands"/other claims n/a. "She believed in their God" — Josh 2:11, correct.
   - CAUTION, not error: "The cord did not save her." The cord was the required sign of the oath (Josh 2:18-19: no cord, no protection). Heb 11:31 credits faith, so the point is sound, but the flat "did not save" is stronger than the text. Y's wording ("The cord was the sign. Trusting the promise saved her.") is the safer form.
5. **Hook 7 / Payoff 8.**
6. **Last line specific to this story: 8** ("tie" = cord, "bring everyone you love" = her family).
7. **Clarity: 7.**
8. **The one change:** "Hebrews says..." → "The Bible says, in two words, what saved her: 'By faith'." A newcomer has no idea what "Hebrews" is.

## 2. Jericho Y

1. **Who / what / why**
   - Who: Rahab in Jericho's wall; two spies; Jesus.
   - What: red cord in the window, city falls, her family spared.
   - Why: the cord was a sign; trusting the promise saved her; she is in Jesus' line.
2. **Takeaway:** You cannot stop the wall; tie your trust to Jesus and bring your people in.
3. **Newcomer blocks:** "thou shalt bind this line of scarlet thread" — archaic AND a new name for the same object (line 1 says "red cord", line 4 says "scarlet thread"; an ESL viewer may not link them). "Hebrews", "Matthew" (two bare book names in two lines). "Israel marched." "Hebrews calls that faith: 'By faith'" reads doubled.
4. **KJV / doctrine check:**
   - "thou shalt bind this line of scarlet thread in the window" — Josh 2:18, exact. But framed as "They promised:" — it is a command, not a promise. Minor framing slip.
   - "the wall fell down flat" — Josh 6:20, exact.
   - "By faith" — Heb 11:31, exact.
   - Matthew lists her — Matt 1:5, correct.
   - "taketh away the sin of the world" — John 1:29, exact, but it sits in the same sentence as "Matthew lists her", so an ear may credit the quote to Matthew. Not wrong, slightly misleading.
   - Cord-as-sign / faith-saved wording is the more precise of the two.
5. **Hook 6 / Payoff 7.** Hook is a description, not a stake. Payoff loses X's best line (sin → "taketh away the sin").
6. **Last line specific: 8** (same CTA as X).
7. **Clarity: 7.**
8. **The one change:** Drop the archaic quote in line 4 and keep plain "red cord" throughout; the story is carried by one object and it must keep one name.

**Jericho verdict: X is better** — it has the stake in the hook and the story's own irony ("the woman known for sin, in the family of the One who taketh away the sin"); Y's one real win is the safer cord-is-the-sign wording, which should be grafted into X's line 8.

## 3. Two Goats X

1. **Who / what / why**
   - Who: Aaron, the high priest, speaking; two goats; Jesus.
   - What: one goat killed, blood carried behind the curtain; the other has sins confessed over it and is sent into the desert alive.
   - Why: a dead goat cannot carry sin away, a live one has not died; Jesus did both, and the curtain tore.
2. **Takeaway:** The curtain between you and God is open; walk through to God because of Jesus.
3. **Newcomer blocks:** "high priest"; "I went in to God for the people" (went in where?); "iniquity" (hard word, though "sins" two lines earlier helps); "Isaiah" (bare name); "A living goat has not died for it" (awkward English, the hinge sentence).
4. **KJV / doctrine check:**
   - Blood of the slain goat "within the vail" — Lev 16:15, correct.
   - "laid both hands on its head, confessing the people's sins" — Lev 16:21 "lay both his hands upon the head of the live goat, and confess", correct.
   - Sent into the wilderness carrying their iniquities — Lev 16:21-22, correct.
   - "the LORD hath laid on him the iniquity of us all" — Isa 53:6, exact.
   - "taketh away the sin of the world" — John 1:29, exact.
   - "from the top to the bottom" — Matt 27:51 / Mark 15:38, exact.
   - Once a year, alone — Lev 16:17, 16:34; Heb 9:7, correct.
   - CAUTION: "that curtain tore" — Aaron's curtain was the tabernacle vail; the one that tore was the temple veil centuries later. Same type, different cloth. "That" presses the identity harder than the text does. Y's "the temple curtain" is literally safer.
   - Doctrine sound: both goats as one sin offering (Lev 16:5) is standard; landing matches Heb 10:19-20.
5. **Hook 8 / Payoff 8.**
6. **Last line specific: 8** ("the curtain is open. Walk through").
7. **Clarity: 7.**
8. **The one change:** Rewrite line 8 as a clean parallel. "A dead goat cannot carry sin away. A living goat has not died for it." → "The dead goat could not walk away. The living goat could not pay." (Y's form). This is the thesis line and it must be effortless.

## 4. Two Goats Y

1. **Who / what / why**
   - Who: Aaron, the high priest; two goats; Jesus.
   - What: one killed, blood taken behind the curtain where no one else could go; the other has sins confessed on it and is sent away alive.
   - Why: the dead goat cannot walk away, the live goat cannot pay; Jesus did both; curtain torn; open to you.
2. **Takeaway:** Through Jesus, the curtain is open to you.
3. **Newcomer blocks:** "high priest"; "iniquity"; "Isaiah"; "could not pay" (pay what? intuitive but unstated); "wilderness" (fine but "desert" is plainer). Otherwise very clean.
4. **KJV / doctrine check:**
   - "where no one else could go" — Lev 16:17 / Heb 9:7, correct.
   - "both hands... confessed... sent away alive, into the wilderness" — Lev 16:21-22, correct.
   - "the LORD hath laid on him the iniquity of us all" — Isa 53:6, exact.
   - "taketh away the sin of the world" — John 1:29, exact.
   - "the temple curtain tore, 'from the top to the bottom'" — Matt 27:51, exact and correctly located.
   - No errors found.
5. **Hook 8 / Payoff 8.**
6. **Last line specific: 7** — "the curtain is open to you" is on-story, but it describes rather than calls; X's "Walk through" is the stronger action.
7. **Clarity: 8.**
8. **The one change:** End with the action: "I went in once a year, alone. Through Jesus the curtain is open. Walk through, to God."

**Two Goats verdict: Y is better** — tighter, one fact per sentence, the thesis line is a true parallel, and "where no one else could go" explains the curtain for a newcomer; take X's "Walk through" imperative for the last beat.

## 5. Bronze Serpent X

1. **Who / what / why**
   - Who: Moses speaking; his dying people; Jesus; Paul quoted.
   - What: people speak against God, snakes kill many, God says make a snake on a pole, whoever looks lives.
   - Why: the cure was shaped like what bit them; Jesus said He would be lifted up the same way; look at Him and live.
2. **Takeaway:** Look up at Jesus lifted on the cross, like the bitten looked at the snake, and live.
3. **Newcomer blocks:** "brass"; "Son of man" (then rescued by "He meant Himself" — good); "Paul" (bare name); "made a curse for us" / "the curse that bit us" (a newcomer hears "curse" as magic; a curse does not bite — this is the one abstract line in a very concrete script); "lifted like my snake" ("my snake" reads a little comic).
4. **KJV / doctrine check:**
   - People spake against God, LORD sent fiery serpents, much people died — Num 21:5-6, correct.
   - "We have sinned" — Num 21:7, exact. "Moses prayed for the people" — correct.
   - "make a snake, set it on a pole" — Num 21:8 (paraphrase, not in quotes, fine); "I made one of brass" — Num 21:9, correct, and correctly keeps brass as Moses' act, not God's words.
   - Anyone bitten who looked, lived — Num 21:9, correct.
   - "as Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, even so must the Son of man be lifted up" — John 3:14, exact (leading "And" dropped, fine).
   - Lifted up = the cross — John 12:32-33, correct.
   - "made a curse for us" — Gal 3:13, exact.
   - NOTE, not error: "God did not take the snakes away" — the text is silent on removal; the inference is from the remedy given instead of the removal asked for. Widely preached, defensible.
   - "The cure took the shape of the curse that bit us" — consistent with Gal 3:13 / Rom 8:3 / 2 Cor 5:21. Sound.
5. **Hook 9 / Payoff 9.**
6. **Last line specific: 9.**
7. **Clarity: 8.**
8. **The one change:** Line 15 — replace the abstract "curse that bit us" with something a newcomer can see: "The cure took the shape of the thing that was killing us." Keep Gal 3:13 in line 13 as the proof.

## 6. Bronze Serpent Y

1. **Who / what / why**
   - Who: Moses; his people; Jesus; Paul quoted.
   - What: snakes sent, many die, brass serpent on a pole, look and live.
   - Why: the cure looked like the killer; Jesus said the Son of man must be lifted up like it; He meant a cross.
2. **Takeaway:** Look up at Jesus on the cross and live.
3. **Newcomer blocks:** "serpent of brass" after "snakes" (ESL viewer may not know serpent = snake; two names for one object); **"the Son of man" is never explained** — Y says "He said the Son of man must be lifted up... He meant a cross", so a newcomer can hear the Son of man as a third person until line 11 names Jesus; "Paul"; "made a curse for us"; "The bitten" (noun use, small hurdle).
4. **KJV / doctrine check:**
   - Num 21:5-7 facts correct; "We have sinned" exact.
   - SLIP: "He told me to make a serpent of brass" — God said "Make thee a fiery serpent" (Num 21:8); the brass was Moses' choice (21:9). Not quoted, so not a misquote, but it puts Moses' material into God's command. X gets this right.
   - "lifted up" — John 3:14, two-word quote; the rest paraphrased faithfully. Thin for a KJV-verbatim house, but not wrong.
   - "made a curse for us" — Gal 3:13, exact.
   - "God did not take the snakes away" — same inference-from-silence note as X.
   - No doctrinal error.
5. **Hook 8 / Payoff 8.** Hook is a statement; X's question form has more pull.
6. **Last line specific: 9** (clean two-beat parallel, best single CTA sentence of the six).
7. **Clarity: 7** (Son of man gap; serpent/snake switch).
8. **The one change:** Line 8 → "But He said that He Himself, the Son of man, must be 'lifted up' like that serpent." One clause closes the newcomer gap.

**Bronze Serpent verdict: X is better** — it quotes John 3:14 in full and says "He meant Himself", so a newcomer knows the Son of man is Jesus; Y is cleaner English and has the best final sentence, but leaves the Jesus-link implicit and puts brass into God's mouth.
