1. **WHO / WHAT / WHY**
- WHO: Rahab, a woman in the city of Jericho. People there looked down on her for her sin.
- WHAT: She hid two spies from Israel because she believed in their God. They told her to tie a red cord in her window. The city wall fell, and she and her family were saved.
- WHY: Trusting God's promise saved her. The story says Jesus is the same kind of rescue for everyone.

2. **FOR WHOM:** Someone who feels judged for their past. Also Christians who already know the story.

3. **TAKEAWAY:** Trust Jesus like Rahab trusted the promise, and bring your family with you.

4. **GLOBAL (hard to understand):**
- "Thou shalt bind this line of scarlet thread". Old English. I understand it only because "red cord" came first.
- "the wall fell down flat". Fine.
- "taketh away the sin of the world". Old English, and "sin" is not explained.
- "Hebrews calls that faith: 'By faith'". Confusing. I don't know what Hebrews is. I guess it is a book.
- "Matthew lists her in the family line of Jesus". Who are Matthew and Jesus? I don't know.
- "Jesus" appears with no introduction, as if I already know him.
- "Israel" is unclear. Is it a country or a people?
- "She lived in Jericho's wall". I would picture her living inside the wall. It is odd.
- "Rahab could not stop a wall. She tied one cord to a promise." It is poetic, so I have to think about it.

5. **BIBLE:**
- Joshua 2:18 says "line of scarlet thread". The quote is close, but the actual verse is "thou shalt bind this line of scarlet thread in the window which thou didst let us down by". The narration trims it, which is OK.
- Joshua 6:20 has "the wall fell down flat". That is correct.
- John 1:29 says "taketh away the sin of the world". That is correct. But the narration never says it was John the Baptist speaking about Jesus. It only says "the One who...".
- Hebrews 11:31 says "By faith the harlot Rahab perished not". "By faith" is correct.
- Matthew 1:5 lists Rahab in Jesus's line. That is correct.
- "Her city knew her for her sin" is only implied. The Bible calls her a harlot, but it does not say what the city thought of her.
- "Trusting the promise saved her" is a Christian reading, backed by Hebrews 11.
- "The cord was the sign" is the Bible's own wording.
- "The red cord points to Jesus" is only implied. It is a traditional Christian reading, not stated in the Bible. The narration doesn't claim it outright, but it leans that way.
- "Bring everyone you love" is a gentle application. Rahab did bring her family in, so it is not wrong.
- One caution: "Tie your trust to Jesus" is fine. The cord is not made into a symbol of Jesus, which is good.

6. **HOOK / PAYOFF**
- Hook: **7/10**. "One red cord marked one family when a whole city fell." It is a strong picture and it raises a question: why a cord?
- Payoff: **7/10**. The ending answers it: the cord stood for trust in a promise. It is a bit abstract and does not really connect the cord to Jesus.

7. **LANDING:** **5/10**. "Tie your trust to Jesus, and bring everyone you love." It uses the cord idea (tie), but the line could close many other gospel shorts. Only the word "tie" and the "family" part are specific to this story.

8. **CLARITY: 6/10.** It is not the best version yet.
- ONE change: add one plain line that says who Jesus is. For example, "Jesus is the Son of God, who came to save people." Then the last third makes sense to a newcomer.
