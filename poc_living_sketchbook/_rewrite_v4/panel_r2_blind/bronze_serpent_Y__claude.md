1. **Who/what/why:**
   - WHO: Moses tells it. It's about Moses, his people, and God. Then Jesus.
   - WHAT: People spoke against God. Snakes came and many died. God told Moses to put a brass snake on a pole. Anyone bitten who looked at it lived.
   - WHY: The story points to Jesus on the cross. Looking to Him gives life.

2. **For:** Anyone curious about Jesus. It works best for people who know a little Christian language.

3. **Takeaway:** Like the bitten people who looked up and lived, look up to Jesus on the cross and you will live.

4. **Global:**
   - "made a curse for us". I don't know what "curse" means here.
   - "lifted up". It is unclear until the next line explains it.
   - "Son of man". Who is that?
   - "serpent of brass". Old words. I guessed "snake" and "metal".
   - "spoke against God". This is a bit vague.
   - "He meant a cross". I had to re-hear this. Why would a cross be a snake?
   - "the cure looked like the killer". This is clever, but it is fast.

5. **Bible:**
   - The Moses story is told as fact. It follows Numbers 21.
   - "The Son of man must be lifted up" is John 3:14. That is fine.
   - "Made a curse for us" is Galatians 3:13. The KJV says "being made a curse for us". That is fine.
   - Calling this "the cure" is a Christian reading. The text is plain about the story. The link to Jesus is Jesus' own claim in John 3. So it is not just a reading.
   - "He meant a cross" is a fair reading of John 3:14 and John 12:32-33.
   - One small gap: "Look up at Jesus and live" skips the word "believe". John 3:15 says "whosoever believeth in him". A careful Bible reader might want faith named. "Look" stands in for it, but the line never says so.
   - Also, in the story Moses speaks in first person, then narration switches to Paul. That is fine.

6. **Hook:** 8/10. "The cure for snakebite was a snake" is odd and made me ask how. The ending pays it off: the cure looked like the killer, and Jesus was made a curse. **Payoff: 7/10.** The link from snake to cross is stated, not shown. Why does a snake picture match Jesus? The "curse" idea is thin for a newcomer.

7. **Landing:** 8/10. "The bitten looked up at a snake, and lived. Look up at Jesus on the cross, and live." This is tied to the story. A different short about another story could not use it unchanged. The "look up and live" part is a bit generic. It could close any Jesus-saves short if you cut the snake half.

8. **Clarity: 7/10.** It is not the best version yet. **One change:** Add one plain line that explains why the snake fits the cross. For example: "The snake stood for the curse. Jesus took that curse on Himself." Then "made a curse for us" would make sense to a newcomer.
