**1. Who / what / why**
- **Who:** Aaron, an old priest, talks about two goats. Jesus is the second person in the story.
- **What:** One goat was killed and its blood taken behind a curtain. The other had the people's sins put on its head and was sent away alive. Jesus is said to do both jobs. When He died, the temple curtain tore.
- **Why it matters:** Sin is dealt with, and the way to God is now open to me.

**2. Who is it for?** Christians and curious people who know a little Bible. A total newcomer would struggle.

**3. Main takeaway:** The two goats show what Jesus did, and because of Him you can now come close to God.

**4. Words or lines I'd have to re-hear**
- "high priest"
- "the curtain" and "behind the curtain". What is back there? It's never said.
- "confessed the people's sins"
- "wilderness"
- "iniquity of us all". It's old, hard English.
- "laid on him". Who is "him"?
- "taketh away the sin of the world"
- "the temple curtain tore". I don't know why that matters.
- "Jesus did both". Who is Jesus? It's never said.
- "I lived long before Jesus", then Aaron talks about Jesus' death. That's confusing.

**5. Bible check**
- The quotes match the KJV: Isaiah 53:6, John 1:29 and Matthew 27:51. John 1:29 is quoted loosely.
- The goat details match Leviticus 16.
- "Jesus did both" is a Christian reading (Hebrews 9–10), but it's stated flat as fact. It's sound, though.
- Aaron speaking about Jesus' death and the torn curtain is an anachronism. It's a storytelling device, but it could look like Aaron "knew".
- Leviticus 16 treats the two goats as one sin offering. The line "One goat died. One goat lived. Why two?" fits that.

**6. Hook:** 7/10. It's a clear puzzle. **Payoff:** 7/10. "Dead goat can't walk away, living goat can't pay" is a good answer. But I still don't know what the curtain protects.

**7. Landing:** 6/10. "Curtain is open to you" is tied to this story. "Through Jesus… to you" could close many other shorts.

**8. Clarity:** 6/10. It is not the best version yet.

**ONE change:** Add a plain line saying what is behind the curtain: "the place where God lived." Then the torn curtain and the open way make sense.
