# EVIDENCE PACK: a clarity revision of a 60-second narration: Rahab and the scarlet cord (Jericho)

## 1. The task
You are ONE independent writer on a blind narration panel. Independent readers found real problems, listed below. Your job is NOT to be more creative. Your job is to fix these problems for a total newcomer WITHOUT losing what already works. Write your own revised narration from scratch, using the current text only as the baseline to beat. Nobody will show you another writer's work.

## 2. The film in one paragraph
About 60 seconds, vertical, ink-and-watercolor sketchbook pages, one calm narrator voice (third person), a quiet music bed, no sound effects. A woman named Rahab lives in the wall of the city of Jericho. She hides two spies sent by Israel, and asks them to spare her family. They tell her to hang a red cord in her window. Israel marches around the city, the wall falls, and her house is spared. The New Testament names her as a woman of faith, and Matthew lists her in the family line of Jesus. The series reads the whole Bible as pointing to Jesus. The audience is worldwide, mostly people with no Bible background, many reading English as a second language, plus Bible readers who will check the facts.

## 3. The CURRENT text (the baseline to beat). 15 lines, 180 words.
1. One red cord kept a woman alive when a city fell.
2. Her name was Rahab, and people knew her for her sin.
3. But she hid two of God's spies, because she believed in Him.
4. They promised her: "thou shalt bind this line of scarlet thread in the window".
5. That cord was the sign of the promise.
6. Then God's army marched around her city, and on the seventh day "the people shouted with a great shout, that the wall fell down flat".
7. The city fell.
8. But Rahab and her family were saved, because she trusted what they said.
9. Hebrews gives the reason: "By faith the harlot Rahab perished not with them that believed not".
10. It was her faith, not her past.
11. Matthew even names her in the family line of Jesus: "And Salmon begat Booz of Rachab".
12. Christians see a picture in that cord: the blood of Jesus, which covers all who trust Him.
13. Rahab could not hold up a wall.
14. She only hung a cord in her window, and trusted the promise.
15. Hang your hope on Jesus the same way.

## 4. What the independent readers found (problems only; solving them is YOUR job)
P1. The cause of her rescue is unclear. The hook says one red cord kept her alive. The middle says the cord was the sign of the promise. Then it says she was saved because she trusted. A stranger cannot say after one hearing what actually saved her.
P2. The Christ link is not grounded. The close says Christians see the cord as the blood of Jesus. No verse in the source text says that. A careful Bible reader may hear it as the cord symbolism carrying the whole gospel, and may object.
P3. "the harlot" and "Salmon begat Booz of Rachab" lose a worldwide audience. "Harlot" is an unfamiliar word. "Rachab" is spelled differently from "Rahab", and a stranger does not know why a list of fathers matters.
P4. The landing "Hang your hope on Jesus the same way" is abstract and generic ("hope"), and "She only hung a cord" understates what the text says her faith did.
P5. The Hebrews 11:31 quote ("perished not with them that believed not") is hard to understand on a first hearing.
P6. The film is far too long (about 71 seconds).

## 5. HARD RULES (do not break)
H1. Every Bible quote must be word-for-word King James (KJV), taken only from the source text in section 8 or already in the current text. You may quote a SHORT exact piece. Never alter a quoted word. Never put quote marks around a paraphrase.
H2. Plain, globally common English. The viewers are worldwide, many read English as a second language. Be clever in structure and irony, never in vocabulary. An old KJV word may be quoted once in passing, but never carry the meaning of a key line on it.
H3. The last line must land on Jesus, and it must be an invitation, not pressure. No fear, no gain-or-loss framing, no manufactured urgency.
H4. Do not use a stock closer ("come to Jesus", "Just ask Him", "Will you...?", "Ready to...?", "Come and see"). The last line must be built from this story's OWN nouns and verbs, so it could not close any other film.
H5. Anything the Bible itself does not say outright (a picture, a type, a symbol) must be either tied to a verse in section 8 that says it, or marked as what Christians see, never stated as the text's own claim.
H6. Do not invent facts that are not in the source text. Do not add names, numbers or details.
H7. Quotes spoken by other people (a spy, Jesus, the crowd) are fine, they will be voiced by other speakers. Keep each short.
H8. Write for the ear: short sentences, one idea each, no sentence a stranger must hear twice.
H9. A person with no Bible knowledge must be able to say, after one hearing: who, what happened, and why it matters.
Speaker: One narrator (third person). Short quotes may be voiced by others.

## 6. The PICTURES are locked (you cannot change them).
Pictures (already drawn, cannot change, in order): tall walls of Jericho; Rahab's face; her feet and the spies climbing down; the scarlet thread in the window; trumpets; the wall in three stages of falling; the house spared among the ruins; a quiet line picture; the final landing picture. When a line has no matching picture the nearest one is held on screen.

## 7. Budget (a hard limit)
- Spoken length is a hard limit. At natural speed the voice reads about 2.4 words a second. The film should be about 60 seconds including a 3 second quiet ending.
- The current text is 180 words. That is TOO LONG. Target 120 to 145 words. Hard maximum 150 words.
- Keep a clear hook line first. Cutting words is welcome. Every word must earn its place.
