## MY NARRATION
1. One woman trusted God inside a city about to fall.
2. Her name was Rahab. She lived in Jericho’s wall.
3. She was a prostitute.
4. She believed in Israel’s God, hid two spies, and helped them escape.
5. They promised to spare her family because she helped them.
6. The red cord in her window would mark their house.
7. Israel marched. Trumpets sounded. The wall fell.
8. Rahab and her family were spared. The promise was kept.
9. The cord marked her home; her faith moved her to welcome the spies.
10. Hebrews names her as a woman of faith.
11. Matthew places her in the family line of Jesus.
12. Her past did not keep her out.
13. The Bible says that through Jesus’ blood we have “the forgiveness of sins.”
14. Rahab trusted God and opened her home.
15. Like Rahab opening her home, you can welcome Jesus.
16. [Silence — about 3 seconds. Hold the final landing picture.]

## WORD COUNT
138 spoken words.

## WHAT I CHANGED, AND WHY

- **P1:** Distinguished the cord’s role as a marker, the promise of rescue, and the faith expressed through helping the spies.
- **P2:** Grounded the Jesus connection in Matthew’s genealogy and Ephesians’ teaching about forgiveness through his blood. Removed the unsupported cord symbolism.
- **P3, P5:** Used plain language for Rahab’s occupation, Hebrews’ assessment, and Matthew’s family list.
- **P4:** Replaced “only hung a cord” with her active welcome and protection of the spies. Built the invitation around that welcome.
- **P6:** Reduced the narration to approximately 58 seconds, including the ending silence.

## WHAT I DELIBERATELY KEPT, AND WHY
The red cord, falling wall, and spared family preserve the story’s visual movement.
Rahab’s faith and place in Jesus’ family line preserve its biblical meaning.

## THE FIRST TWO SECONDS AND THE MOMENT THE VIEWER KNOWS WHO TO ROOT FOR
The stranger first hears “One woman trusted God”—a person making a choice amid danger. Rahab is named around second six, giving that woman an identity before her actions unfold.

## THE RISK
Welcoming Jesus is an invitation drawn by analogy from Rahab’s welcome; Joshua does not explicitly make that comparison. “Her past did not keep her out” summarizes her inclusion in Jesus’ family line rather than quoting Matthew.
