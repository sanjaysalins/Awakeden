## MY NARRATION

1. One goat died.
2. One goat was sent away alive.
3. Why two?
4. One goat cannot be killed and sent away alive.
5. [Silence ~1.5s — hook goat held]
6. I am Aaron.
7. Each year, two goats were one offering for the people's sin.
8. I killed the first; its blood went inside the veil—the curtain before God's presence.
9. I laid both hands on the live goat and confessed the people's sins over it.
10. They led it into the wilderness, to a land not inhabited.
11. [Silence ~1s — goat led into desert]
12. Death paid; sins sent away.
13. Christians see Jesus in both goats.
14. "the LORD hath laid on him the iniquity of us all."
15. "which taketh away the sin of the world."
16. He put away sin by the sacrifice of himself.
17. When He died, "the veil of the temple was rent in twain from the top to the bottom."
18. The curtain is open.
19. [Silence ~3s — torn curtain, landing picture]
20. Enter the holiest by His blood, through the veil He opened.

## WORD COUNT

135

## WHAT I CHANGED, AND WHY

- **Line 4** — States plainly why two goats were needed (one cannot die and be sent away alive). **P2**
- **Lines 6–10** — Aaron stays in his own year; “offering for the people's sin” and “curtain before God's presence” replace unexplained “high priest,” “sin offering,” and bare “veil.” **P4**
- **Line 12** — Short payoff instead of abstract “one rescue.” **P2**
- **Line 13** — Typology flagged as what Christians see, not Leviticus’s literal claim. **H5, P4**
- **Lines 14–16** — “Taketh away” and “put away sin” come from KJV (John 1:29, Hebrews 9:26); dropped unsupported “He bore them away.” **P3**
- **Lines 17–18** — Wilderness stays on the goat; the open curtain is separate. **P1**
- **Line 20** — Invitation uses veil, blood, and holiest from this story—not “Walk in.” **P5**
- **Overall length** — Cut from ~195 to 135 spoken words. **P6**

## WHAT I DELIBERATELY KEPT, AND WHY

- The three-beat hook (died / sent away / why two) — it matches the locked pictures and pulls strangers in immediately.
- The Isaiah and Matthew veil quotes — they anchor the Jesus turn and the curtain beat the film already shows.
- Aaron in first person for the Leviticus action — that is who the sketchbook voice is.

## THE FIRST TWO SECONDS AND THE MOMENT THE VIEWER KNOWS WHO TO ROOT FOR

A stranger hears “One goat died” first—two stark facts before any name. They know the film is about **Aaron** once line 6 lands (“I am Aaron”), roughly **5–7 seconds** in at normal pace after the hook and the “why two” answer.

## THE RISK

A careful listener may still want Leviticus 16 itself to call the goats a “picture” of Jesus—line 13 only claims what Christians see. “Holiest” may still feel churchy unless they already tied it to “God's presence” in line 8.
