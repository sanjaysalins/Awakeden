## MY NARRATION
1. One goat died. One goat lived. Both carried the same sin.
2. I am Aaron. Once a year, I stood before God for my people.
3. God gave me two goats, one offering for sin.
4. The first I killed. I carried its blood behind the curtain, where no one else could go.
5. The second I did not kill. I laid both hands on its head, and confessed the people's sins.
6. Then it was sent away alive, into the wilderness.
7. Why two? The dead goat could not walk away. The living goat could not pay.
8. Long after me, Jesus came and did both.
9. Isaiah wrote: "the LORD hath laid on him the iniquity of us all".
10. He came "to put away sin".
11. When He died, the temple curtain tore in two, from top to bottom.
12. I went in once a year, alone. Through Jesus, the curtain is open to you.
(silence 3s, final landing picture holds)

## WHAT I CHANGED, AND WHY
- Hook "One died, one lived, same sin" sets up the riddle; line 7 answers it in one plain line (P2).
- Ending no longer mentions the wilderness, so "open to you" can only mean the curtain (P1).
- "Bore them away" and "land where no one lives" removed; "put away sin" is quoted from Hebrews 9:26 (P3).
- "Priest", "veil" explained in place; "Long after me" fixes the time view (P4).
- Landing built on the once-a-year curtain, not "Walk in" (P5). Cut from 195 words (P6).

## WHAT I DELIBERATELY KEPT, AND WHY
The two-goat hook and the Isaiah line: both are strong and exact KJV.

## THE FIRST TWO SECONDS AND THE MOMENT THE VIEWER KNOWS WHO TO ROOT FOR
First: "One goat died. One goat lived." Aaron is named at about 5 seconds; the viewer follows him from there.

## THE RISK
"Jesus did both" is the Christian reading, backed only by the two quotes; a newcomer may not know why a torn curtain matters.
