## MY NARRATION
1. Why two goats?
2. I am Aaron, Israel's high priest, serving God for the people.
3. Once a year, I brought an offering for their sins.
4. I killed one goat and carried its blood behind the curtain.
5. I laid both hands on the live goat and confessed Israel's sins.
6. It carried them into the wilderness.
7. A dead goat could not carry them away alive.
8. Look beyond my lifetime: Christians see both goats pointing to Jesus.
9. Isaiah wrote: “the LORD hath laid on him the iniquity of us all”.
10. Jesus died for sins.
11. John wrote: “which taketh away the sin of the world”.
12. The temple curtain kept people from God's presence.
13. When Jesus died, it tore from top to bottom.
14. His blood opens the way to God.
15. Through the torn curtain, you may draw near to God through Jesus.
16. [Silence: about 3 seconds. Hold the final landing picture.]

136 spoken words.

## WHAT I CHANGED, AND WHY
- **P1, P5:** The invitation explicitly leads through the torn curtain into God's presence and ends on Jesus.
- **P2:** A plain physical fact answers the hook: a dead goat cannot also leave alive carrying sins.
- **P3:** John's exact words support taking sin away. The wilderness stays within Aaron's account, without becoming a literal destination for forgiven sins.
- **P4:** Aaron explains his role. “Look beyond my lifetime” signals the time shift, and “Christians see” identifies the interpretation. “Offering for their sins” and “curtain” replace unexplained terms.
- **P6:** Cut to approximately 57 seconds of speech, plus the quiet ending. Related beats are merged to fit.

## WHAT I DELIBERATELY KEPT, AND WHY
Aaron's first-person account, both hands, the blood, and the living goat preserve the concrete ritual.
The Isaiah quotation and tearing from top to bottom preserve the biblical turning points.

## THE FIRST TWO SECONDS AND THE MOMENT THE VIEWER KNOWS WHO TO ROOT FOR
A stranger hears “Why two goats?” Aaron identifies himself by about second 3; his service for the people is clear by about second 6. Jesus becomes the focus around second 30.

## THE RISK
Aaron's invitation to look beyond his lifetime is a storytelling device, not recorded speech. The two goats pointing together to Jesus remains a Christian interpretation, explicitly marked as such.
