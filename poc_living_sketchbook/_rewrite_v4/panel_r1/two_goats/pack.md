# EVIDENCE PACK: a clarity revision of a 60-second narration: Aaron and the two goats (Day of Atonement)

## 1. The task
You are ONE independent writer on a blind narration panel. Independent readers found real problems, listed below. Your job is NOT to be more creative. Your job is to fix these problems for a total newcomer WITHOUT losing what already works. Write your own revised narration from scratch, using the current text only as the baseline to beat. Nobody will show you another writer's work.

## 2. The film in one paragraph
About 60 seconds, vertical, ink-and-watercolor sketchbook pages, Aaron the high priest speaks in first person as himself, a quiet music bed, no sound effects. Once a year, on the Day of Atonement, Aaron handled two goats that together made one sin offering for the people of Israel. One goat was killed and its blood carried inside the curtain (the veil) that kept people out of God's presence. The other goat had both of Aaron's hands laid on its head while he confessed the sins of the people, and it was led away alive into the wilderness. The New Testament says Jesus both died for sins and took them away, and that the temple curtain tore from top to bottom when He died. The series reads the whole Bible as pointing to Jesus. The audience is worldwide, mostly people with no Bible background, many reading English as a second language, plus Bible readers who will check the facts.

## 3. The CURRENT text (the baseline to beat). 19 lines, 195 words.
1. One goat died.

4. I am Aaron, the first high priest, and once a year I handled both.

## 4. What the independent readers found (problems only; solving them is YOUR job)
P1. The ending is confused. The wilderness image ("a land where no one lives") sits right before "Walk in", so a listener cannot tell where the invitation leads: the wilderness or the open curtain.
P2. The hook asks "Why two?" but the answer ("two goats pictured one rescue") stays abstract. A stranger cannot say in one plain line why one goat could not do both jobs.
P3. "He bore them away" has no verse behind it, and "sin has gone where that goat went, to a land where no one lives" can be heard as a literal place.
P4. Aaron speaks as a man long before Jesus, yet says "Jesus" and "Long after me". The time viewpoint is unclear, and "high priest", "sin offering" and "veil" are unexplained for a newcomer.
P5. The landing "Walk in." is generic. It could end any film in the series.
P6. The film is far too long (about 71 seconds).

## 5. HARD RULES (do not break)
H1. Every Bible quote must be word-for-word King James (KJV), taken only from the source text in section 8 or already in the current text. You may quote a SHORT exact piece. Never alter a quoted word. Never put quote marks around a paraphrase.
H2. Plain, globally common English. The viewers are worldwide, many read English as a second language. Be clever in structure and irony, never in vocabulary. An old KJV word may be quoted once in passing, but never carry the meaning of a key line on it.
H3. The last line must land on Jesus, and it must be an invitation, not pressure. No fear, no gain-or-loss framing, no manufactured urgency.
H4. Do not use a stock closer ("come to Jesus", "Just ask Him", "Will you...?", "Ready to...?", "Come and see"). The last line must be built from this story's OWN nouns and verbs, so it could not close any other film.
H5. Anything the Bible itself does not say outright (a picture, a type, a symbol) must be either tied to a verse in section 8 that says it, or marked as what Christians see, never stated as the text's own claim.
H6. Do not invent facts that are not in the source text. Do not add names, numbers or details.
H7. Quotes spoken by other people (a spy, Jesus, the crowd) are fine, they will be voiced by other speakers. Keep each short.
H8. Write for the ear: short sentences, one idea each, no sentence a stranger must hear twice.
H9. A person with no Bible knowledge must be able to say, after one hearing: who, what happened, and why it matters.
Speaker: Aaron speaks in first person as himself. Quotes may be voiced by others.

## 6. The PICTURES are locked (you cannot change them).
Pictures (already drawn, cannot change, in order): the hook picture of the goat; the goat to be killed; the live goat; the goat led into the desert; two goats side by side; the Jesus turning point; the Isaiah line; 'finished'; the temple curtain whole; the curtain tearing; the curtain torn; the final landing picture. When a line has no matching picture the nearest one is held on screen.

## 7. Budget (a hard limit)
- Spoken length is a hard limit. At natural speed the voice reads about 2.4 words a second. The film should be about 60 seconds including a 3 second quiet ending.
- The current text is 195 words. That is TOO LONG. Target 120 to 145 words. Hard maximum 150 words.
- Keep a clear hook line first. Cutting words is welcome. Every word must earn its place.
