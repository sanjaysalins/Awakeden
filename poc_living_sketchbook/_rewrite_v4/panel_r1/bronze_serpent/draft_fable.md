## MY NARRATION
1. Snakes were killing my people. God's cure? A snake.
2. I am Moses.
3. My people spoke against God. He sent snakes. Many died.
4. They cried, "We have sinned". I prayed.
5. God did not take the snakes away. He said: make a snake, set it on a pole.
6. I made one of brass, and lifted it up.
7. Anyone bitten who looked at it, lived.
8. [silence ~1.5s — the pole stands alone against the horizon]
9. The cure was shaped like what bit them.
10. I lived long before Jesus. But He said:
11. "as Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, even so must the Son of man be lifted up".
12. Lifted up — He meant Himself, on a cross.
13. There, Paul wrote, He was "made a curse for us".
14. [silence ~1.5s — the shadow of the cross falls across Golgotha]
15. The cure took the shape of the curse that bit us.
16. Do what my bitten people did.
17. Look up at Jesus, lifted like my snake, and live.

## WORD COUNT
142

## WHAT I CHANGED, AND WHY
- P1: the order is now spoken in order: people spoke against God (3) → God sent snakes (3) → they confessed, I prayed (4) → God's remedy (5-7). The hook keeps the irony but puts the killing snakes first and the cure second.
- P2: the landing uses the story's own twist, said as a pair: "shaped like what bit them" (9) and "took the shape of the curse that bit us" (15). Last line: "lifted like my snake".
- P3: "Nicodemus" dropped. "He meant Himself, on a cross" (12) tells a stranger who "the Son of man" is and what "lifted up" means (John 12:33).
- P4: "I lived long before Jesus" states the viewpoint. Jesus's words (11) and Paul's words (13) are both exact quotes on their own lines, so other voices can carry them.
- P5: God's command (5) and my act (6) are two separate lines. "Only a picture" is gone; Jesus Himself names my serpent. "Paul wrote" names the source of "made a curse for us".
- P6: 164 → 142 words. Old beat 8 split into lines 10-12; old 9-11 became 13-17.

## WHAT I DELIBERATELY KEPT, AND WHY
- "I prayed" as its own tiny beat — it matches the locked praying picture and the pause before God's strange answer.
- "made a curse for us" — the one exact phrase that carries the irony into the New Testament.
- "and live" at the close — it is the story's own promise (Numbers 21:8, John 3:15), not an added reward.

## THE FIRST TWO SECONDS AND THE MOMENT THE VIEWER KNOWS WHO TO ROOT FOR
First heard: "Snakes were killing my people" — a man, his people, dying. At about second 4, "I am Moses" — the one who will be told to make the cure, and whose people must look at it.

## THE RISK
A source-literate viewer may say "the cure took the shape of the curse that bit us" is an inference from John 3:14 plus Galatians 3:13, not a sentence either verse says. A newcomer may need a beat to hear that "what bit us" means sin, not a snake.
