# EVIDENCE PACK: a clarity revision of a 60-second narration: Moses and the snake on a pole (bronze serpent)

## 1. The task
You are ONE independent writer on a blind narration panel. Independent readers found real problems, listed below. Your job is NOT to be more creative. Your job is to fix these problems for a total newcomer WITHOUT losing what already works. Write your own revised narration from scratch, using the current text only as the baseline to beat. Nobody will show you another writer's work.

## 2. The film in one paragraph
About 60 seconds, vertical, ink-and-watercolor sketchbook pages, Moses speaks in first person as himself, a quiet music bed, no sound effects. In the desert Israel complained against God, snakes bit the people and many died. The people confessed and asked Moses to pray. God did not remove the snakes. He told Moses to make a serpent of brass and set it on a pole, and everyone who looked at it lived. Jesus later told a man named Nicodemus that the Son of man must be lifted up the same way, and Paul wrote that Christ was made a curse for us. The series reads the whole Bible as pointing to Jesus. The audience is worldwide, mostly people with no Bible background, many reading English as a second language, plus Bible readers who will check the facts.

## 3. The CURRENT text (the baseline to beat). 11 lines, 164 words.
1. God answered snakebite with a snake on a pole.
2. I am Moses, and I made it.
3. My people were dying of snakebites, because they had spoken against God.
4. They came to me: "We have sinned, for we have spoken against the LORD, and against thee".
5. I prayed.
6. Instead of taking the snakes away, God told me to make a serpent of brass, and set it on a pole.
7. Whoever was bitten looked at it, and lived.
8. Long after me, Jesus told a man named Nicodemus: "And as Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, even so must the Son of man be lifted up: That whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have eternal life." My brass serpent was only a picture.
9. On the cross, Jesus took on Himself the sin that bites us all, and Paul says He was "made a curse for us".
10. Lifted up where all can see, He is the cure.
11. Lift your eyes to Him, and live.

## 4. What the independent readers found (problems only; solving them is YOUR job)
P1. The hook "God answered snakebite with a snake on a pole" blends two different things: the snakes that bit were a judgment, the brass serpent on the pole was the remedy. The order (people rebel, snakes bite, remedy on the pole) is not clear on first hearing.
P2. The landing "Lift your eyes to Him, and live" could close any film about looking to the cross. It does not use this story's own strange irony: the cure was shaped like the thing that bit them.
P3. "lifted up" is never explained as being put on a cross. "Nicodemus" and "Son of man" are unexplained for a newcomer.
P4. Moses speaks in first person yet talks about Jesus and Paul as people who came after him. The viewpoint is not explained, and Jesus's words and Paul's words are carried by the same single voice.
P5. "My brass serpent was only a picture" understates it, and blurs two things: God's command (Numbers 21:8) and Moses's act. The Paul quote ("made a curse for us") has no source named.
P6. The film is far too long (about 65 seconds, with the narrator talking about 62).

## 5. HARD RULES (do not break)
H1. Every Bible quote must be word-for-word King James (KJV), taken only from the source text in section 8 or already in the current text. You may quote a SHORT exact piece. Never alter a quoted word. Never put quote marks around a paraphrase.
H2. Plain, globally common English. The viewers are worldwide, many read English as a second language. Be clever in structure and irony, never in vocabulary. An old KJV word may be quoted once in passing, but never carry the meaning of a key line on it.
H3. The last line must land on Jesus, and it must be an invitation, not pressure. No fear, no gain-or-loss framing, no manufactured urgency.
H4. Do not use a stock closer ("come to Jesus", "Just ask Him", "Will you...?", "Ready to...?", "Come and see"). The last line must be built from this story's OWN nouns and verbs, so it could not close any other film.
H5. Anything the Bible itself does not say outright (a picture, a type, a symbol) must be either tied to a verse in section 8 that says it, or marked as what Christians see, never stated as the text's own claim.
H6. Do not invent facts that are not in the source text. Do not add names, numbers or details.
H7. Quotes spoken by other people (a spy, Jesus, the crowd) are fine, they will be voiced by other speakers. Keep each short.
H8. Write for the ear: short sentences, one idea each, no sentence a stranger must hear twice.
H9. A person with no Bible knowledge must be able to say, after one hearing: who, what happened, and why it matters.
Speaker: Moses speaks in first person as himself. Quotes may be voiced by others.

## 6. The PICTURES are locked (you cannot change them).
Pictures (already drawn, cannot change, in order): a wide desert camp; a grieving family; the people complaining; the biting serpents; Moses praying; the forge; the pole against the horizon; the cross-and-pole picture; the shadow of the cross; Golgotha; 'hear me'; the lifted-up landing picture. When a line has no matching picture the nearest one is held on screen.

## 7. Budget (a hard limit)
- Spoken length is a hard limit. At natural speed the voice reads about 2.4 words a second. The film should be about 60 seconds including a 3 second quiet ending.
- The current text is 164 words. That is TOO LONG. Target 120 to 145 words. Hard maximum 150 words.
- Keep a clear hook line first. Cutting words is welcome. Every word must earn its place.
