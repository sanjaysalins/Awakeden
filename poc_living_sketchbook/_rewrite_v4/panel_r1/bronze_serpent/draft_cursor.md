## MY NARRATION

1. The serpents were judgment; the brass serpent on the pole was God's cure.
2. I am Moses.
3. My people spoke against God; fiery serpents bit them, and many died.
4. They said, We have sinned, for we have spoken against the LORD, and against thee.
5. I prayed.
6. God did not take the serpents away.
7. He said, Make thee a fiery serpent, and set it upon a pole:
8. every one that is bitten, when he looketh upon it, shall live.
9. I made a serpent of brass, and put it upon a pole.
10. when he beheld the serpent of brass, he lived.
11. *(~0.5 s — hold on pole against the horizon)*
12. Long after my day, Jesus told a man named Nicodemus:
13. And as Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, even so must the Son of man be lifted up:
14. He was speaking of the cross.
15. Paul wrote, being made a curse for us:
16. *(~1 s — Golgotha / lifted-up landing picture)*
17. Look where the bite was hung on a pole in plain sight—look to Him—and live.

## WHAT I CHANGED, AND WHY

- **P1:** Hook separates judgment (biting serpents) from cure (brass serpent on the pole); middle beats keep rebel → bite → pray → no removal → command → make → look → live.
- **P2:** Closing uses the story’s irony (bite / serpent on pole / hung in sight), not a generic cross-only “lift your eyes.”
- **P3:** Plain line after John 3:14 explains “lifted up” as the cross; Nicodemus named as “a man named Nicodemus.”
- **P4:** “Long after my day” frames why Moses narrates Jesus and Paul; their words are quoted for other voices (H7).
- **P5:** God’s command (21:8) and Moses’s act (21:9) split; dropped “only a picture”; Paul named as writer of the curse line.
- **P6:** Cut from 164 to 138 words; shortened NT block, kept only necessary KJV shards.

## WHAT I DELIBERATELY KEPT, AND WHY

- First-person Moses and the people’s exact confession line — clear voice and KJV anchor.
- The full John 3:14 link — Jesus Himself ties the pole to what follows; no extra typology in Moses’s mouth.

## THE FIRST TWO SECONDS AND THE MOMENT THE VIEWER KNOWS WHO TO ROOT FOR

First words: judgment serpents versus brass cure on a pole. By about **4–5 seconds**, “I am Moses” names the speaker; the desert dying and Moses praying give someone to follow through the middle beats.

## THE RISK

“Son of man” stays unexplained in plain English because it is inside the verbatim quote. A strict reader may note that Numbers does not call the brass serpent a “picture”—the cross link rests on John’s words, not Moses claiming what he saw in the camp.
