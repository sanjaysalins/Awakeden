## MY NARRATION

1. Why would God put a snake on a pole?
2. I am Moses. My people spoke against God.
3. He sent snakes. They bit the people, and many died.
4. The people admitted their sin and asked me to pray.
5. I prayed.
6. He did not take the snakes away. God told me to make a snake and put it on a pole. I made it from brass.
7. Those who were bitten looked at it and lived.
8. Now let me point beyond my lifetime. Jesus later compared my raised snake to His own death on a cross.
9. In Galatians, Paul wrote that Jesus was “made a curse for us”.
10. Christians see the link: their remedy bore the shape of what harmed them. Jesus bore our curse.
11. As the bitten looked toward that brass snake, you can look toward Jesus on the cross.
12. **[Silence, about 3 seconds: hold the lifted-up landing picture.]**

## WORD COUNT

138 spoken words.

## WHAT I CHANGED, AND WHY

- **P1:** The opening poses the puzzle; the next beats establish rebellion, judgment, prayer, and remedy in order.
- **P2:** The ending connects the bitten people’s look toward the brass snake with the invitation to look toward Jesus.
- **P3:** Replaced the long quotation with plain narration explaining the cross. Omitted Nicodemus and “Son of man,” removing introductions the short film cannot adequately explain.
- **P4:** “Beyond my lifetime” explicitly signals Moses’s forward-looking narration. Jesus’s comparison is paraphrased; Paul’s short quotation has a named speaker and source.
- **P5:** Distinguished God’s command from Moses’s construction. Removed “only a picture” and attributed the interpretive link to Christians.
- **P6:** Cut to 138 words, approximately 57.5 seconds of speech, followed by three quiet seconds.

## WHAT I DELIBERATELY KEPT, AND WHY

Moses names himself immediately after the hook, and “I prayed” retains its quiet weight.
The brass snake, survival through looking, and connection to Jesus remain central.

## THE FIRST TWO SECONDS AND THE MOMENT THE VIEWER KNOWS WHO TO ROOT FOR

The stranger first hears, “Why would God put…” Moses identifies himself around second five. By about second eleven, the viewer understands that his people are dying and need help.

## THE RISK

A source-literate viewer may note that Moses’s knowledge of later events is a dramatic framing device; “beyond my lifetime” signals that choice. “Curse” remains the least familiar concept, though Paul’s source and its connection to Jesus are explicit.
