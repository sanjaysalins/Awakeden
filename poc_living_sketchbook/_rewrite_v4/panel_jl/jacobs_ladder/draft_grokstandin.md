## MY NARRATION
1. Jacob cheated his brother Esau, and tricked their blind father. Esau planned to kill him. So Jacob ran.
2. Alone at night, he lay down in the open, with a stone for a pillow.
- SILENCE 0.8s: wide empty field, the stone, one gold thread starts.
3. He had earned nothing. Still, he dreamed of a ladder reaching to heaven, and "the angels of God ascending and descending on it."
4. And the LORD stood above it and said: "I am with thee."
- SILENCE 0.8s: ladder glows, gold thread runs up it.
5. Jacob woke, afraid and amazed. "Surely the LORD is in this place; and I knew it not."
6. Centuries later, Jesus told a man named Nathanael: "Ye shall see heaven open, and the angels of God ascending and descending upon the Son of man."
- SILENCE 0.8s: the open sky, angels, the blue thread joins the gold.
7. Jacob slept on a stone, not knowing. And the promise was already spoken: "I will not leave thee."
- SILENCE 2.5s: the stone, the open sky above it, hold.

## WHAT I CHANGED, AND WHY
- Opened with who and why: cheated Esau, tricked his father, Esau planned to kill him, he ran (P6).
- Removed "guilt and exhaustion" and "guilty man's dream"; said only what the text says (P5).
- Cut "God does not audition people" and the "holy place" slogan (P7).
- Quotes are single unspliced KJV runs, no ellipses (P2).
- Dropped "How dreadful is this place"; used "afraid and amazed" in plain narrator voice (P4).
- Replaced "Jesus said He is that ladder" with Jesus's own John 1:51 words, quoted (P3).
- New last line uses the stone and "I will not leave thee", spoken before Jacob did anything (P1).
- Kept length near 129 words plus silences (P8).

## WHAT I DELIBERATELY KEPT, AND WHY
The stone pillow, the ladder quote, "I knew it not", and the Jesus link, because they already worked. Plain short sentences throughout.

## THE FIRST TWO SECONDS AND THE MOMENT THE VIEWER KNOWS WHO TO ROOT FOR
They hear "Jacob cheated his brother Esau" first, so they meet a flawed man at once. They know it is about a runaway at about 6 seconds ("So Jacob ran"), and root for him once he lies alone on the stone.

## THE RISK
"He had earned nothing" is an inference a strict reader may call mild, though Gen 28:15 comes before any good act. A newcomer may not know why a ladder matters until the Jesus line.
