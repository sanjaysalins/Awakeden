# EVIDENCE PACK: a clarity + rules revision of a 60-second narration: Jacob's ladder (Genesis 28)

## 1. The task
You are ONE independent writer on a blind narration panel. Independent checks found real problems, listed below. Fix them for a total newcomer WITHOUT losing what works. Write your own revised narration from scratch; the current text is only the baseline to beat. Nobody will show you another writer's work.

## 2. The film in one paragraph
About 60 seconds, vertical, hand-drawn ink-and-watercolor storyboard pages with a blue-and-gold ink thread, one calm narrator voice (a second voice may speak only God's quoted words), quiet music. Jacob has cheated his brother Esau and his father; Esau wants to kill him; Jacob runs away alone and sleeps outdoors with a stone for a pillow. He dreams of a ladder to heaven with angels going up and down, and the LORD stands above it and speaks to him. He wakes afraid and amazed. Centuries later Jesus tells Nathanael he will see heaven open and the angels going up and down on the Son of man. The audience is worldwide, mostly people with no Bible background, many reading English as a second language, plus Bible readers who will check every fact.

## 3. The CURRENT text (baseline to beat). About 150 words.
1. A man fled his own brother's threat to kill him, and slept on open ground with a rock for his pillow.
2. God does not wait for you to reach a holy place before He speaks. He meets the runaway exactly where guilt and exhaustion finally put him down.
3. Scripture says he dreamed of "a ladder set up on the earth... and the angels of God ascending and descending on it."
4. And the LORD Himself stood above it and said: "I am with thee... I will not leave thee."
5. Some would call it just a guilty man's dream. But God does not audition people before He comforts them.
6. Jacob woke and said: "Surely the LORD is in this place; and I knew it not... How dreadful is this place!"
7. You may be lying in your own version of that field right now, certain you are alone. You are not.
8. Jacob saw a ladder to heaven in a dream. Jesus said He is that ladder, standing open, permanently.
9. Ready to stop running, and climb toward Him?

## 4. Problems found (problems only; solving them is YOUR job)
P1. STOCK CLOSER. The last line "Ready to stop running, and climb toward Him?" is the tired invite shape used on other films; it could close any short unchanged. The landing must be earned by a concrete word or image from THIS story (the stone, the ladder, the open place, "I will not leave thee", "I knew it not") and be grace-anchored.
P2. BROKEN QUOTES. Quotes are spliced with "..." to join separate verses. Every quote must be a verbatim KJV run, unspliced, no ellipses. Short phrases are fine.
P3. A READING STATED AS FACT. "Jesus said He is that ladder" is not what the text says. Jesus (John 1:51) says Nathanael will see heaven open and the angels of God going up and down upon the Son of man. The link may be told, but only as what Jesus actually said, quoted, not paraphrased into a claim.
P4. ARCHAIC LOAD. "How dreadful is this place" means awe/fear, not "bad"; a newcomer may hear it as insult. Either quote it with plain framing (awe) or paraphrase the concept plainly. Never make an archaic word carry the landing.
P5. UNSTATED MOTIVES AS FACT. "where guilt and exhaustion finally put him down" and "a guilty man's dream" claim Jacob's inner state. The text does not say. Say what the text says (he cheated his brother, Esau meant to kill him, he ran, he slept).
P6. WHO/WHAT/WHY GAP. A newcomer is never told WHY Jacob is running (he deceived Esau and their father, Genesis 27). The hook should open a question the ending pays off.
P7. "God does not audition people" is a slogan, not in the text. Remove or earn it from the text.
P8. Length must stay about 55-62 seconds of speech at a calm pace (target 125-150 spoken words), because the picture track is already built to that length.

## 5. Hard rules
- KJV verbatim for every quote. Attribution frames in plain narrator voice.
- Grace-anchored: God comes to the runaway BEFORE he does anything right (Gen 28:15). No fear, loss or gain framing, no pressure.
- Lands on Jesus, only through what Jesus actually said (John 1:51) or what the New Testament says; no invented doctrine.
- Plain global English. Clever in structure, not vocabulary.
- No dashes used as slop (no long em-dash chains), no "Ready to...", "Will you...", "Just ask Him", "Come and see", "Come to Jesus" closers.
- Do not name verses aloud unless needed; the book name Genesis once is fine.
