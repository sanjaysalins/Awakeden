## MY NARRATION

1. Jacob had deceived his father and taken the blessing meant for his brother. Now Esau wanted him dead.
2. He ran. What would God say to a man like that?
3. Alone outdoors, Jacob lay down with a stone for his pillow.
4. In a dream, he saw a ladder reaching from earth to heaven, with angels going up and down.
5. The LORD stood above it and promised: “I will not leave thee.”
6. Before Jacob had done anything to make things right, God promised to stay with him.
7. Jacob woke afraid and amazed: “Surely the LORD is in this place; and I knew it not.”

[Silence, about 1.5 seconds. Jacob sits beside the stone as dawn touches the open ground.]

8. Centuries later, Jesus told Nathanael, calling himself the Son of man:
9. “Hereafter ye shall see heaven open, and the angels of God ascending and descending upon the Son of man.”

## WORD COUNT

132 spoken words.

## WHAT I CHANGED, AND WHY

- **P1:** Replaced the invitation with Jesus’ actual words. The returning angels and open heaven give this story its ending.
- **P2:** Used only continuous, verbatim KJV excerpts, without ellipses.
- **P3:** Removed the claim that Jesus called himself the ladder. His quotation supplies the connection.
- **P4:** Framed Jacob’s response plainly as fear and amazement.
- **P5, P6:** Established the deception and death threat; removed invented guilt and exhaustion.
- **P7:** Replaced the audition slogan with the sequence of events: God promises his presence before Jacob makes amends.
- **P8:** Kept nine spoken beats and 132 words, allowing a calm delivery within the allotted time.

## WHAT I DELIBERATELY KEPT, AND WHY

The stone pillow, ascending and descending angels, and promise of God’s presence preserve the story’s concrete images and grace.
Jacob’s waking words retain his discovery that God was present before he knew it.

## THE FIRST TWO SECONDS AND THE MOMENT THE VIEWER KNOWS WHO TO ROOT FOR

A stranger first hears “Jacob had deceived his father.” Jacob is identified immediately. The threat establishes his danger around second seven; God’s promise, around second thirty, makes clear that his wrongdoing has not placed him beyond God’s care.

## THE RISK

“Before Jacob had done anything to make things right” summarizes the narrative sequence rather than quoting it. A newcomer may still need the pictures to recognize that Jesus’ words recall Jacob’s dream.
