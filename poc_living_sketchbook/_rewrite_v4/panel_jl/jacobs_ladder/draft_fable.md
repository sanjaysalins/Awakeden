## MY NARRATION
1. He tricked his blind father and stole his brother's blessing.
2. His brother wanted him dead. So Jacob ran.
3. [silence ~1s: the road, the sun going down]
4. He slept on open ground, a stone for his pillow.
5. What does God say to a man like that?
6. [silence ~1s: Jacob's closed eyes, the stone]
7. Genesis says he dreamed of a ladder to heaven, and "the angels of God ascending and descending on it."
8. The LORD stood above it.
9. [second voice, the LORD] "I am with thee."
10. Jacob had fixed nothing. God spoke first.
11. [second voice, the LORD] "I will not leave thee."
12. Jacob woke and said: "Surely the LORD is in this place; and I knew it not."
13. He was afraid: God had been that close.
14. [silence ~1.5s: the stone in morning light]
15. Centuries later, Jesus said: "ye shall see heaven open, and the angels of God ascending and descending upon the Son of man."
16. Jacob's angels. Jacob's open heaven. Now over the Son of man: Jesus.
17. Heaven opened over a cheat asleep on a stone. Jesus says it is open still.

(Beat shape: baseline beats 1 and 4 are split so the LORD's two lines stand alone for the second voice; baseline beat 7 "your own field" is folded into the landing. Jesus' line stays in narrator voice unless the producer counts His words as God's.)

## WORD COUNT
150

## WHAT I CHANGED, AND WHY
- P1: Last line is built from this story's own stone and open heaven, not an invite formula. It ends on grace: heaven opened over a cheat before he fixed anything, and Jesus says it is open still.
- P2: Every quote is one unspliced KJV run. Gen 28:15 is given as two separate short quotes with a narrator beat between, no ellipsis.
- P3: The link to Jesus is only His exact words (John 1:51). The narrator sets Jacob's angels and open heaven beside "upon the Son of man" and lets the viewer hear the match; it never says "Jesus is the ladder."
- P4: "How dreadful" is gone. The fear is framed plainly as awe at nearness ("God had been that close"), straight from "I knew it not."
- P5: No guilt, no exhaustion, no "guilty dream." Only what the text says: tricked, stole, wanted dead, ran, slept.
- P6: The why is in the first two lines; line 5 opens the question; lines 10 and 17 pay it off.
- P7: Slogan removed. Replaced by the order Gen 28:15 itself shows: "Jacob had fixed nothing. God spoke first."
- P8: 150 spoken words, three short silences.

## WHAT I DELIBERATELY KEPT, AND WHY
- "Surely the LORD is in this place; and I knew it not" as the waking line; it is the story's own surprise.
- The stone pillow as the single recurring image, from hook to landing.
- The Genesis-to-John angel echo as the spine, now in exact matching words both times.

## THE FIRST TWO SECONDS AND THE MOMENT THE VIEWER KNOWS WHO TO ROOT FOR
First heard: "He tricked his blind father." A crime with a victim, no setup needed. By about 8-10 seconds ("a stone for his pillow... What does God say to a man like that?") the stranger knows the film is about the hunted man on the ground, and the question is whether God will say anything to him at all. That is settled at about 22 seconds, when God speaks first.

## THE RISK
A source-literate viewer may note Isaac also sent Jacob away to find a wife (Gen 28:1-2), so "ran" is only half the reason, and that "heaven opened over" Jacob is the John 1:51 reading laid back onto Genesis, not Genesis's own wording. A newcomer may still need "Son of man: Jesus" to land in one hearing, and "blessing" is thin for someone with no Bible background.
