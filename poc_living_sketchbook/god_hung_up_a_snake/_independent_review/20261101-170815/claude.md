# Independent review — claude (OK, 52s)

**Findings**

**KJV verbatim:** The Numbers 21:9 quote is exact KJV. No altered words. Everything else is paraphrase, and Gal 3:13 ("made a curse for us") is never quoted.

**Clarity on first hearing (the biggest problem):**
- The piece never says why there is a cure. A newcomer isn't told that snakes were killing people in the camp. The first mention of snakes is the cure itself ("God hung up a snake").
- "The precise shape of their death": "their" has no antecedent. Who are they?
- Opening line: "You'd expect the cure to be hung up looking hopeful" is tangled. "Hung up looking hopeful" reads as if the cure is looking hopeful.
- "Dressed it up as something else" is an idiom that ESL listeners will struggle with.
- "He didn't become a general sacrifice" is the least clear line. "General sacrifice" is jargon that doesn't map to anything the listener has heard. It also sits oddly against the doctrine that Christ *was* the sacrifice. The "gentler, less costly version" it answers is a strawman the piece has not set up.
- "Call it too grim for a cure if you like" sounds defensive. It argues with a critic nobody introduced.

**Doctrinal soundness:**
- "He became your curse" and "carrying your exact curse" assert Christ's substitution (Gal 3:13, 2 Cor 5:21) without quoting any Scripture. The one NT text that makes the link, John 3:14 ("as Moses lifted up the serpent… even so must the Son of man be lifted up"), is missing. The central Christ claim therefore stands on the narrator's authority alone.
- "Exact" overclaims. It makes the serpent and the curse identical.
- A zero-Bible listener could also hear "God hung up a snake… He hung up Christ" as Christ equals snake. The piece never says the serpent *stood for* the judgment on sin that Christ bore.
- "God never once dressed it up" is an unsupported assertion.

**Grace-anchored conviction:** There is no fear or gain/loss framing. "We keep reaching for a gentler version" is mildly accusatory but acceptable.

**Thread spine:** It is consistent: snake, then Christ, then snake. That is a strength.

**Landing:**
- There is no invitation at all. The text says "behold… he lived," but the closing never uses *look* or *behold*. It ends on "so you could too," which is vague about what "too" is. That means living, but the piece never says live *how* or *how to receive it*.
- The "snake" image is reused, but the final move is a statement, not a CTA.

**Scores**
- **Hook: 6/10.** The "you'd expect X, God did the opposite" contrast is a mild template. The first line is muddled and has no concrete stake. The strongest hook material, "God hung up a snake," arrives too late.
- **Landing: 6/10.** It is anchored to the snake image, but it has no earned verb (*behold*) and no invitation. The Christ claim is asserted rather than shown from the text.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Open with the problem so a newcomer knows the stakes: snakes are killing the people, and God's answer is a bronze snake on a pole. Cut the muddled "hung up looking hopeful" line and lead with "God hung up a snake."
2. Ground the Christ link in Scripture. Quote John 3:14 in exact KJV, or at least name it. Say the serpent stood for the judgment and the death Christ took. Replace "exact curse" and "general sacrifice" with plain words.
3. Build the CTA from the piece's own verb, "beheld… he lived." End on a concrete invitation to look at Christ lifted up and live. Cut the defensive "call it too grim" line and the "dressed it up" idiom.
