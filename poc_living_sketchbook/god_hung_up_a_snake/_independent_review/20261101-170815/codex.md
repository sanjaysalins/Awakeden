# Independent review — codex (OK, 68s)

**Hook: 7/10. Landing: 5/10.**

1. **The central contrast is undefined and doctrinally careless.** “He didn't become a general sacrifice. He became your curse.” “General sacrifice” has no explained meaning, and sacrifice versus curse is a false opposition: Christ’s sacrifice bears the curse. “Your exact curse” adds a precision the narration never establishes. Galatians identifies the curse as the law’s condemnation; without that explanation, a newcomer could hear personal misfortune or a supernatural hex. [Galatians 3:13–14, KJV](https://biblia.com/bible/kjv/galatians/3/13-15)

2. **The script omits the biblical bridge that makes its central connection trustworthy.** “God hung up a snake … He hung up Christ” presents the parallel without explaining that Jesus Himself makes it. His stated connection is being lifted up so that believers receive eternal life. The narration instead centres the objects’ grim appearance, then imports curse-bearing without explaining the connection. The typology is biblical; the script’s emphasis needs firmer textual grounding. [John 3:14–16, KJV](https://search.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John+3%3A14-16&version=KJV)

3. **The hook creates curiosity before providing basic orientation.** “You'd expect the cure to be hung up looking hopeful—a light, a flag” assumes an expectation strangers probably do not have. Neither a light nor a flag ordinarily signifies a cure. “God hung up a snake” supplies the arresting image, but listeners must wait through several phrases to discover that people have been bitten and that this is a brass representation.

4. **The middle substitutes accusation for invitation.** “Call it too grim for a cure if you like” sounds combative. “We keep reaching for a gentler version … something less costly” assigns the listener a motive without establishing it. Less costly to whom? The surrounding emphasis makes divine severity clearer than divine love. There is no explicit works demand or fear ultimatum, but the conviction is driven by rhetorical pressure more than a gracious invitation.

5. **The landing preserves the image but supplies no CTA.** “He hung up Christ, His own Son, carrying your exact curse, so you could too” echoes the lifting motif, so it is not simply a stock closer. Nevertheless, “could too” leaves the listener to recover “live” from the previous sentence. It neither identifies eternal life nor invites the response Jesus names: believing. Its emotional weight depends on an unexplained “exact curse.”

6. **The thread weakens at the application.** The opening concerns the surprising form of God’s provision. “We keep reaching for a gentler version” shifts to an alleged modern preference; “general sacrifice” then shifts again to personal specificity. Those connections are asserted rather than developed. “It stayed exactly what it was” also leaves its referent unclear: a brass image was never the actual snake that inflicted the bite.

**KJV check:** The complete quotation of Numbers 21:9 is word-for-word exact. No altered words found. [Numbers 21:9, KJV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Numbers+21%3A8-9&version=KJV)

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Remove the sacrifice-versus-curse opposition and explain curse-bearing without unsupported “exact curse” language.
2. Ground the serpent-to-Christ connection in Jesus’ own explanation, carrying looking and believing through to the closing invitation.
3. Orient newcomers sooner and replace the accusatory middle with a clear account of God’s gracious provision.
