### Doctrinal soundness
**Mostly orthodox** (bronze serpent lifted up → Christ lifted up; curse borne for life). No contrarian Numbers reading, no denial of substitution.

**Problems:**

1. **“He became your curse.”** Galatians 3:13 is *“being made a curse for us”* — Christ bearing curse **for** us, not morphing into “your curse” as a thing. Evangelicals say this in sermons, but on **first hearing** it can sound like Jesus *turned into* the curse itself rather than bore it in our place. Tighter: *made a curse for you* (still plain English).

2. **“carrying your exact curse”** — rhetorically strong, biblically fuzzy. The camp’s problem was **serpent bite / death in the wilderness**; the NT link is sin, law’s curse, condemnation (Gal 3, John 3:14–15). **“Exact”** over-promises a one-to-one without naming what curse means. A skeptical listener asks: *exact how?*

3. **Faith mechanism dropped.** Numbers 21:9 and Jesus in John 3:14–15 are **look / be lifted up → live**. The script ends on **God hung Christ up** (objective atonement only). That’s half the bronze-serpent doctrine for a **CTA** piece: no invitation to the same response the type requires (**behold**). Risk: sounds like “God fixed it; you’re covered” without *look and live* — not heresy, but **incomplete** for this series’ model.

4. **“He didn’t become a general sacrifice.”** Unclear without Bible background. *General* as opposed to what — generic? universal? non-specific? Sounds like in-house theology jargon, not a contested reading, but **not grounded in a cited line** in the piece.
