# Independent review — grok (OK, 179s)

The quote checks out. The gospel claim does not. Hook **6**. Landing **5**. Both under 8.

**KJV.** The line in quotation marks matches Numbers 21:9 word for word: "And Moses made a serpent of brass, and put it upon a pole, and it came to pass, that if a serpent had bitten any man, when he beheld the serpent of brass, he lived." No altered word.

**Doctrine.** "He didn't become a general sacrifice. He became your curse." is a false claim. Christ is the sacrifice (Ephesians 5:2, Hebrews 9–10, 1 Corinthians 5:7). "General sacrifice" is not a biblical category. The sentence denies the sacrifice in order to sound sharp. Galatians 3:13 says he was "made a curse for us" under "the curse of the law," then redeemed us from it. "He became your curse" drops "for us," drops the law, and drops the redemption. A first-time listener can hear that Jesus became evil, or became you.

"Carrying your exact curse" overreaches. The brass serpent matched the kind of snake that was biting them. It was one serpent for the whole camp. John 3:14–15 ties Jesus to that scene by the lifting up, and by believing. It does not say he was the precise shape of each person's curse. "Precise" and "exact" are the writer's intensifiers, not the text's.

"God hung up a snake" flattens Numbers 21:8–9. The LORD commanded it. Moses made it and put it on the pole. The later parallel, "He hung up Christ," needs that distinction. John credits Moses with lifting the serpent, and says the Son of man must be lifted up.

**Hook (6).** The first five seconds are: "You'd expect the cure to be hung up looking hopeful - a light, a flag." Nobody expects a cure to be hung up, and nobody expects that cure to be a light or a flag. The wind-up argues with an expectation the listener does not have. "You'd expect" works like a template open. "God hung up a snake." is the real stop, and it arrives at the edge of the five seconds, after the conceit. A stranger still does not know who is dying, or of what.

**Clarity.** "The precise shape of their death" and "the whole camp" arrive before any people, any bite, or any sin. The camp shows up as a pronoun. The KJV sentence then has to carry the whole story in one breath. "He didn't become a general sacrifice" is opaque. "General" can sound military or generic. "Your curse" is never defined. The judgment, the confession, and Moses' prayer (Numbers 21:5–7) are missing, so the snake plays as a grim aesthetic choice.

**Landing (5).** The last sentence is: "He hung up Christ, His own Son, carrying your exact curse, so you could too." That sentence can end a different piece that never mentions a snake. "Hung up" plus "curse" is a portable Galatians 3:13 closer. Cap at 5. "So you could too" has no clear verb. The previous verb is "live," but the words in between are "carrying your exact curse." On first hearing, "too" can mean "so you could carry the curse too." That inverts the gospel. The verse's own verb, "beheld," never becomes the invitation. John 3's verb is believe. The script states a fact and stops. The quoted action is look and live. The close does not ask anyone to look.

**Grace.** No hell-threat, no works, no "or else." That part holds. The close still fails the invite test. "We keep reaching for a gentler version of what Christ did - something less costly." scolds a stranger who just arrived and has not reached for anything.

**Spine.** One thread almost holds: God lifts up the real ugly thing, and that is the cure. It wobbles at "general sacrifice," which is a second contrast the snake image never set up. The snake stays a snake. Christ made a curse for us is the matching claim. "Not a sacrifice" is a different sermon.

**Freshness.** "God hung up a snake" is a real surprise in the text. The sharpening around it leaves the text. Faithful depth would stay on look and live, and on the curse he bore for us. "Exact," "precise," and "didn't become a general sacrifice" chase a harder tone than Numbers 21 or John 3 or Galatians 3 will bear.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Remove "He didn't become a general sacrifice. He became your curse." Say he was made a curse for us, under the curse of the law, so he could redeem us — and that this does not cancel his sacrifice.
2. Rewrite the last sentence so "too" can only mean live, and so the close uses this piece's own verb, behold. A portable "hung up Christ... curse" closer stays capped at 5.
3. Open on the bite and the camp, not on "You'd expect the cure to be hung up looking hopeful - a light, a flag." Name who is dying before "their death," and drop "exact" and "precise."
