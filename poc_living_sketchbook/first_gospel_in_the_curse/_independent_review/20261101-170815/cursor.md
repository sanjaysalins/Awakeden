## Independent adversarial review (narration)

### Doctrinal soundness
Mostly orthodox protoevangelium: serpent first, messianic “Seed,” head/heel pattern, grace before personal judgment. No contrarian readings spotted.

**Nit (precision, not heresy):** *“buries a promise **for them** inside that curse”* — Genesis 3:15 is addressed to the serpent; the promise benefits the woman/her seed (and by extension humanity). Saying God buried it “for them” is standard homiletical extension, but a careful listener could hear direct speech *to Adam and Eve* that the text does not give. Safer: “in their hearing” or “for her line.”

**Nit:** *“Jesus, the Seed who **crushed** the serpent's head”* — KJV 3:15 is *bruise*, not crush; cross/resurrection fulfillment is fine as **narrator theology outside quotes**, but *crushed* sounds like a done deal while the heel is still bruised in the same verse — slight over-triumph without a beat of “already / not yet.”

### KJV verbatim
The block in quotes matches project KJV for Genesis 3:15 exactly (including *it shall bruise* and *thy seed / her seed*). **No altered words inside quotation marks.**

### Clarity on first hearing
**Timeline compression:** Opening places Adam and Eve *“in the trees, waiting”* as if the confrontation has not happened. In Genesis 3 they already hid, answered God, and confessed (3:8–13) **before** the serpent curse (3:14–15). A listener who knows the story may feel a small rewind; a zero-Bible listener may think God has not spoken to them yet, then suddenly *“Only then does He turn to Eve, then Adam”* without the prior dialogue — **gap in causal chain**.

**Label:** *“Call it a curse formula”* — insider homiletical jargon; meaning is recoverable from context but is not plain global English on first pass.

**Capital-S:** *“Jesus, the Seed”* helps, but *“That promise has a name now: Jesus, the Seed…”* assumes the listener already links “Seed” to a person from one KJV line (*her seed*) — weak for total outsiders until the name lands.

### Grace-anchored conviction
**Strength:** *“before you'd done one thing to earn it, or even asked”* — anti-works, pre-fall-of-sentence timing; invitation not terror.

**Edge (read charitably):** *“or even asked”* could be misheard as “you don't need to seek God” rather than “mercy preceded your plea.” One clarifying word (*“before you could ask”*) would shut that down.

No fear/gain-loss / works ladder detected.

### Freshness = faithful depth
The **order** hook (rescue before ruin) is a known preaching move, not a gimmick. The fig-leaf opening and *“Voice that spoke first”* give some texture. Not clickbait-fresh; **faithful but familiar.**

### One thread spine
Single spine holds: sentence dread → serpent-first promise → grace before earning → Jesus/Seed → *Voice that spoke first*. No obvious thread swap.
