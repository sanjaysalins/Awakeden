# Independent review — claude (OK, 46s)

**Narration review: Genesis 3:15 "rescue spoken before ruin"**

**KJV check:** The quote is exact KJV (Gen 3:15). Nothing inside quotation marks is altered. The framing line "To the serpent God says," is narrator voice, so it doesn't count against that check.

**Findings**

1. **The cross is missing from the gospel claim.** The quote includes "thou shalt bruise his heel", but the landing says only that Jesus "crushed the serpent's head." The heel bruise is the cross, and it is the cost of the rescue. Without it the landing is a victory with no price. For a Christ-through-the-canon piece that is a real gap. The promise is also left half-explained.
2. **"Call it a curse formula — the order still stands" is confusing and should go.**
   - "Curse formula" is jargon a newcomer won't parse.
   - It is a defensive aside that sounds like an argument with an unseen critic.
   - It muddies the thread, because God never curses Adam and Eve directly. He curses the serpent and the ground.
   - It breaks the flow from the proof straight into the conviction.
3. **Unexplained load-bearing words, which breaks the global-audience rule.** "Enmity" and "seed" carry the whole logic. A zero-knowledge or ESL listener will not know that "seed" means offspring or descendant. The line "Jesus, the Seed" lands on a word never made plain.
4. **Embellished details not in the text.** "Dust on their skin" and "tied with shaking hands" are invented. Gen 3 gives fig leaves and hiding among the trees, but no dust on their skin and no shaking hands. "Waiting to hear what God will do to them" is plausible, but the order of events needs care. God questions Adam, then Eve, then the serpent, and the sentences come in the order serpent, Eve, Adam. The script's "sentence" order is correct. The "Dust on their skin" open is atmospheric filler, though, and it pulls attention from the real hook, which is that God turns to the snake first.
5. **"The Voice that spoke first" is ambiguous.**
   - It blurs God the Father and Jesus.
   - It also invites the "Jesus is the Word spoken in Eden" reading, which is defensible but not made in the text.
   - A newcomer will not know who "the Voice" is.
   - "Come meet" is a close cousin of stock closers ("Come to Jesus"), so it could end another piece.
6. **"Your sentence" assumes the listener is a condemned party.** That is true theologically, but it is never established. It lands in the same line as the CTA, so the grace is asserted rather than earned.
7. **Grace framing is fine.** There is no fear or gain-loss, and "before you'd done one thing to earn it, or even asked" is good, orthodox grace. The thread (order of speech → promise → Jesus) is one spine and does not swap.

**Scores**
- **Hook: 6/10.** It is a sensory scene-setter. The real hook, "God turns to the snake first," is buried in sentence two.
- **Landing: 6/10.** "Grace was spoken before your sentence" is a good line, but it lacks the cross, "Voice" is vague, and "come meet" is close to stock. Both scores are under 8, so the verdict is REVISE.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Add the cross to the promise and the landing. Say plainly that the Seed's heel was bruised (the nails) while He crushed the serpent. Gloss "seed" once as "a child, born of her line." Gloss "enmity" or replace it with "war".
2. Delete the "Call it a curse formula" line. Move the strong hook up front, for example "Before God says a word of punishment to Adam and Eve, He turns to the snake." Drop the invented "dust" and "shaking hands" details.
3. Replace "the Voice that spoke first" and "come meet" with a closer built on this piece's own image (promise spoken first, before the sentence). Use plain words and name Jesus directly. It should be a line that could not end another piece.
