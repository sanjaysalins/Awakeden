# Independent review — gemini (OK, 72s)

Here is the independent, adversarial review of the narration artifact:

**Doctrinal soundness**: PASS. The theology of the Protoevangelium (Genesis 3:15) is orthodox, and the sequence of God's addresses (Serpent, then Eve, then Adam) is textually accurate to Genesis 3. No invented doctrine.
**KJV verbatim**: PASS. Genesis 3:15 is quoted exactly.
**Clarity on first hearing**: ISSUE. "Call it a curse formula" sounds like a seminary lecture. It breaks the immersive, narrative tone established in the hook and introduces unnecessary academic jargon for a zero-knowledge listener. Additionally, the KJV quote explicitly says "bruise thy head," but the script immediately follows up with "crushed the serpent's head." While "crushed" is common evangelical shorthand, swapping the verb immediately after quoting the KJV creates cognitive dissonance for a first-time listener.
**Grace-anchored conviction**: PASS. "before you'd done one thing to earn it, or even asked" is a beautiful, pure-grace framing with zero works/fear manipulation.
**Freshness**: PASS. Highlighting the *chronological order* of the judgments (that God spoke the promise of rescue to the serpent before handing down the sentence to the humans) is a fantastic, text-driven insight.
**One thread spine**: PASS. The motif of "sentence," "spoken," and "first" runs cleanly from the hook to the landing.
**SCORE THE HOOK**: 9. "Dust on their skin, fig leaves tied with shaking hands..." is a vivid, sensory, in-scene opening that immediately grabs attention without relying on a template.
**SCORE THE LANDING**: 7. "Grace was spoken before your sentence — come meet the Voice that spoke first." The first half is brilliant and perfectly tied to the piece's thread. However, "meet the Voice" is a clunky mixed metaphor (you *hear* a voice, you *meet* a person/Speaker). Because the final imperative stumbles on its own imagery, it drops below an 8.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Fix the mixed metaphor in the CTA: change "meet the Voice" to something rhetorically coherent like "listen to the Voice," "hear the Voice," or "meet the God who spoke first."
2. Remove the academic phrase "Call it a curse formula" to maintain the immersive, narrative tone.
3. Align the exposition with the KJV quote: change "crushed the serpent's head" to "bruised the serpent's head" so the listener isn't confused by a suddenly swapped verb.
