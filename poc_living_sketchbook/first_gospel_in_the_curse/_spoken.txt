Dust on their skin, fig leaves tied with shaking hands — Adam and Eve stand in the trees, waiting to hear what God will do to them. But before God ever tells them their sentence, He turns to the snake first and buries a promise for them inside that curse. To the serpent God says, "And I will put enmity between thee and the woman, and between thy seed and her seed; it shall bruise thy head, and thou shalt bruise his heel." Only then does He turn to Eve, then Adam, to speak their own sentence. Call it a curse formula — the order still stands: rescue spoken before ruin. It's easy to admire a God who promises rescue. It's harder to believe He meant it for you — before you'd done one thing to earn it, or even asked. That promise has a name now: Jesus, the Seed who crushed the serpent's head. Grace was spoken before your sentence — come meet the Voice that spoke first.
