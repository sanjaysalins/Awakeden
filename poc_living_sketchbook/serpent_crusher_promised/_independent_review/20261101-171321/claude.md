# Independent review — claude (OK, 54s)

**Hook: 4/10.** "You've heard this before - a curse in a garden, a serpent's head crushed under a heel." This is a soft template open that assumes prior knowledge, and a newcomer hasn't heard it before. It has no stake, no question and no tension in the first 5 seconds. The strong question ("If Christ won at the cross, why still future?") comes too late.

**Landing: 6/10.** "Stand where His grace has set your feet." It is built from the piece's own "feet" image, which is good. It is still mostly a posture line, though. It never invites anyone to Jesus, and it doesn't name what grace has done. "Set your feet" is also not in the verse. Paul's "grace be with you" is only a closing benediction, and the script turns it into a doctrinal claim.

**Findings:**
- **KJV:** The quote of Romans 16:20 is verbatim, including "shortly" and "Amen". No issue.
- **Logic and clarity:**
  - "If Christ won at the cross, why still future? Because the blow already fell - the fight isn't still in doubt." This does not answer the question it asks. Why is the crushing still "shortly" if the blow already fell? A listener gets a slogan, not an explanation. The missing idea is "won, but not yet fully revealed or completed."
  - "That head was never yours to crush" sits uneasily with the quoted words "under your feet". The verse says the believers' feet are the place. The script never reconciles that, so a newcomer hears a contradiction.
- **Invented contrast:** "the God of peace, not force". The verse doesn't set peace against force. This is a contrast the script adds, and the phrase "God of peace" in Paul often goes with God's active victory. It is mildly misleading.
- **Presumed state:** "You want to be the one who crushes what's crushing you - your willpower, your vigilance." This assumes what the listener wants and then reads that into the verse. It is not exactly gain/loss framing, and the grace point is sound. But "what's crushing you" is vague, and it makes the verse about self-help rather than Satan.
- **Global audience:**
  - "bruise" and "shortly" are archaic. Here "shortly" means "soon", and a non-native speaker may read it as "briefly".
  - Neither word is glossed, and "shortly" is the hinge of the question.
  - "Eden" and "heel" are assumed knowledge. The Genesis 3:15 echo is never named, so "echoing Eden's promise" is asserted but not shown.
- **Thread spine:** The "crush / feet" thread is consistent from hook to landing. There is no thread-swapping. The thread does drift, though, from Satan being crushed to the listener's own struggle.
- **Doctrine:** The claims are orthodox: Christ's victory is decisive, and the final defeat is God's work. There is no invented doctrine. The weakness is the "already / not yet" tension, which is stated but not explained.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Replace the "You've heard this before" hook with a concrete, curious opening that doesn't need prior knowledge. For example: "Paul tells ordinary people God will crush Satan under THEIR feet. So why can't you win?" Name Genesis 3:15 explicitly.
2. Fix the "why still future?" logic. Explain "already won, not yet finished" in plain words, and gloss "shortly" as "soon" and "bruise" as "crush". Reconcile "under your feet" with "never yours to crush": God does the crushing, and we stand where He does it.
3. Rebuild the landing from the piece's own verb or image so that it points to Jesus's finished work and not only to a posture. For example, "He crushes it. You just stand on His side." Drop the invented "not force" contrast.
