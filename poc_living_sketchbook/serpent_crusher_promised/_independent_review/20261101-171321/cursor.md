## Independent adversarial review

### KJV verbatim
The only quoted block matches project KJV for Romans 16:20 (including the benediction and “Amen.”). **No altered words inside the quotation marks.**

Outside quotes, the hook uses **“crushed”** where Genesis 3:15 KJV has **“bruise”** (“it shall bruise thy head”). That is not a quote violation, but it is louder/more final than the text’s verb and can skew the listener before Romans’s **“bruise Satan.”**

### Doctrinal soundness
The peace-title vs crushing-verb angle is orthodox and interesting. Problems:

1. **Paul’s “your feet” vs “never yours to crush”** — The script has the listener hear KJV **“bruise Satan under your feet”**, then **“But that head was never yours to crush.”** A careful listener (or a critic) hears the narrator undo the pronoun Paul just used. Reformed/evangelical readings often keep **your feet** as **God’s work in which you stand**, not self-powered conquest — but that distinction is never stated in plain speech, so the piece reads like it **corrects Scripture** instead of **explaining** it.

2. **“Scripture calls Him the God of peace, not force”** — **“God of peace”** is in the verse; **“not force”** is editorial. Worse, the same verse assigns **bruising/crushing** — so **“not force”** fights the passage’s own imagery unless you name the paradox (peace-title, crushing-act) instead of denying “force.”

3. **“That crushing was never handed to a warrior”** — Defensible as anti-self-help, but it can sound like it erases **how** Genesis 3:15 is usually read (the seed’s strike) and **who** does the bruising in Romans (the **God of peace**, acting for/on believers). “Warrior” is imported; it is not from either cited text.

4. **“echoing Eden's promise”** — Typological, common in-house, but **Romans 16:20 does not quote Genesis 3:15**. For a zero-Bible listener who never hears Gen 3:15 in KJV, **“Eden's promise”** is asserted, not shown. That is not heresy, but it is **authority by narration** rather than by the text in their ear.

5. **Jesus on the last beat** — The spine wants **Christ, finishing what He won**, but the **final sentence** is **“Stand where His grace has set your feet.”** “His” likely means Christ, yet the **closing audible landing is grace + posture**, not the gospel pivot name/image. For a “whole Bible through Jesus” short, that is a soft landing, not a wrong one.

### Clarity on first hearing
- **“It isn't finished yet”** then **“Because the blow already fell”** — Valid already/not-yet, but with no label a stranger hears **contradiction**, not tension.
- **“If Christ won at the cross, why still future?”** — Strong question; the answer (**fight isn't still in doubt**) does not fully answer **why “shortly” is still ahead** in Paul’s future tense.
- **“Scripture calls Him”** — After garden/cross language, **“Him”** may snap to **Christ** before the quote shows the subject is **“the God of peace.”** Minor pronoun slip risk.
- The Romans quote includes **“The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with you. Amen.”** — KJV-accurate but **~half the quoted material is farewell**, so the ear works through benediction before the script returns to **bruise / feet / shortly**.

### Grace-anchored conviction
**Strong.** **“your willpower, your vigilance”** and **“You want to be the one who crushes”** diagnose self-reliance without threats or heaven/hell leverage. The Spirit-convicts / script-invites balance mostly holds.

### Freshness vs faithful depth
**“God of peace” + crushing** is a real textual surprise. Weakened by familiar Eden-in-garden setup and a closer that could sit on many **grace + stand firm** shorts without the serpent/bruise thread.

### One thread (hook → middle → CTA)
Mostly coherent: **unfinished Eden head/heel → peace not self-warrior → Romans → cross already won → reject DIY crushing → Christ finishing → feet/grace.**

Thread wobble: the piece **centers Paul’s “your feet”** in audio, then **denies “yours to crush,”** then **reuses “your feet”** only via **“His grace has set your feet.”** That is one thread only if the listener already knows the sermon trick.

### Hook score: **6 / 10**
**“You've heard this before”** is a soft, familiarity frame (not a capped “Did you know,” but not a cold-open punch). **“It isn't finished yet”** helps. For a **stranger** with no Bible background, **“curse in a garden”** is evocative but not uniquely stopping; for Bible-literate scrollers it risks **“yes, Genesis 3, skip.”**

### Landing score: **6 / 10**
**“Stand where His grace has set your feet”** picks up **feet** from Romans and avoids naked **“come to Jesus.”** It could still close **many** grace/identity/Romans pieces; it does not **require** serpent, bruise, peace-title, or Eden. The piece-specific verb (**bruise/crush**) drops out of the **last** sentence; **Christ** is not in that final line.
