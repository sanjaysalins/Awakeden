# Independent review — codex (OK, 97s)

The central problem is the invented opposition between God’s peace and His power to defeat evil. Several later lines then blur who acts, what remains future, and what the listener should do.

- **Doctrinal distortion:** “That crushing was never handed to a warrior” and “the God of peace, not force.” Romans names God as the one who crushes Satan; His peace does not exclude His victorious judgment. Scripture explicitly depicts God as a warrior ([Exodus 15:3](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Exodus%2015%3A3&version=KJV)). The supposed surprise depends on a false contrast, so it fails faithful freshness.

- **An apparent contradiction left unexplained:** “under your feet” followed by “that head was never yours to crush.” These can be reconciled: God acts, and His people share His victory. The narration never explains that distinction. Instead, its application appears to retract the image it just quoted. ([Romans 16:20](https://biblehub.com/kjv/romans/16-20.htm))

- **The future-tense question receives an incomplete answer:** “If Christ won at the cross, why still future? Because the blow already fell.” That explains why victory is certain, but not what remains future. “It isn’t finished yet” and “Christ, finishing what He won” need to distinguish Christ’s accomplished saving work from the final defeat of evil. Otherwise, a novice can hear that the cross left His saving work unfinished.

- **Grace becomes vulnerable to a passivity reading:** “your willpower, your vigilance.” The intended criticism seems to be self-reliance, but the wording also discredits vigilance itself. Scripture commands vigilance and resistance in faith; Romans’ immediate context also calls for discernment and obedience. Dependence on grace does not cancel those responses. ([1 Peter 5:8–9](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Peter%205%3A8-9&version=KJV), [Romans 16:17–20](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Romans%2016%3A17-20&version=KJV))

- **The thread shifts into personal relief:** “crushes what’s crushing you” moves from Satan’s defeat to unspecified personal burdens. It risks making relief from the listener’s troubles the promise. “You want to be the one” also assigns a motive without establishing it. There is no explicit fear or works bargain, but this pivot weakens the grace-centered invitation.

- **Hook: 4/10.** “You’ve heard this before” presumes familiarity that the stipulated newcomer lacks. The garden, curse, serpent, and heel arrive without explanation; the curiosity gap comes after that barrier.

- **Landing: 5/10.** “Stand where His grace has set your feet” recalls the feet image, but never explains what standing means. The exact sentence could close another piece about grace, assurance, or spiritual warfare, so the stock-closer cap applies.

- **KJV verbatim:** The complete quoted Romans 16:20 matches the KJV. No altered words found. ([KJV text](https://biblehub.com/kjv/romans/16-20.htm))

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Remove the false peace-versus-warrior contrast; distinguish God’s action from believers sharing His victory.
2. Explain accomplished victory and future fulfillment without implying unfinished saving work or dismissing vigilance.
3. Make the opening accessible to newcomers and the closing invitation specific to this passage’s promise.
