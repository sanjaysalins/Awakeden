## Style 12 — TEMPEST INK ("the ink obeys")

- **The gap it fills:** Storm's own thesis, made of medium. In Style 1 the storm is a wash ON a
  drawing; here the storm IS the ink and the calm IS the paper — one colossal wet black brush-arc
  rearing over the boat, and where His arm points, nothing but untouched cream and two thin
  horizon strokes. The fear-to-calm arc becomes a material fact of the page: the violence is all
  ink, the calm is all paper. This is the still-layer sibling of the wash-creep device (the
  enhancement brief's "the sea obeys because the paint obeys"), and its natural partner.
- **Why it's different from 1-11:** Style 1 is a finished illustration with washes; Style 2 is
  charcoal figure-gesture on gray; Style 9 is a quiet few-stroke travel sketch. Tempest Ink is
  the ELEMENT register — maximum wet-ink energy for the element, minimum marks for everything
  else. No existing style makes the medium carry the miracle.
- **Prompt approach:** bold wet black calligraphic ink + charcoal-gray wash on warm cream; huge
  confident strokes, dry-brush spray, flung droplets arrested mid-air, feathered blooming edges;
  large untouched paper regions carry the stillness; single muted ink-blue undertone. No lettering.
- **Series fit beyond Storm:** walking on the water (Matt 14 — the sea as ink, His path as
  paper); Jonah's tempest; the Red Sea walls (two ink masses, a dry paper path between them);
  the great catch (Luke 5 — the net straining against an ink swirl); any "creation convulses /
  Christ is still" beat.
- **Proof still:** `style12_tempestink_rebuke.png` (v2). v1 rendered Jesus's tunic knee-length
  with bare feet — the exact §8a.2 canon drift, fail-closed on a Jesus frame; re-rolled with
  ONE-continuous-garment-to-the-deck + sandals-visible language, now clean (kept for the record
  as `style12_tempestink_rebuke.v1_costume_defect.png`).
- **Honest flags:** the tunic drapes over his forward raised knee, showing the lower leg + sandal
  — a natural drape, not trousers, but production wording should plant both feet flat if you want
  the hem dead-ankle. Face is canon-loose at this scale (right for the register, but any close-up
  in this style needs the anchor chained).
- **Animation-ready?** Made for the $0 paper devices — wash-creep IS this style's motion language
  (the arc advancing 6px, then withdrawing on the rebuke), plus damp-cockle/line-boil. Generative
  motion: risky (a wet-ink wave invites the model to break it); untested this round by design.
- **Cost:** 2cr/still. **Confidence: HIGH as the storm-episode hero register.**

## Style 13 — MARINER'S CHART ("the sea, charted")

- **The gap it fills:** Style 3's sea-specific sibling. Scholar's Margin analyses TWO things side
  by side; the Mariner's Chart analyses ONE journey from above — the crossing as a pilot's own
  working document: wind-rose, rhumb lines, soundings, the squall drawn as a whorl of hand-inked
  wind-lines mid-lake, and at the exact heart of the chaos the chart's single gold element: a
  pressed-gold flame-point touching the boat — the One asleep aboard her. The theology is the
  composition: the storm is charted AROUND a fixed point of glory.
- **Why it's different from 1-11 (and from `/map`):** no existing style is cartographic. The
  `/map` engine is a $0 deterministic route-ANIMATION tool (caravan marker, place labels); this
  is a rendered STILL register for the page itself — they're natural partners, not duplicates
  (the /map camera could tour this chart).
- **Prompt approach:** iron-gall sepia on sea-stained parchment; hatched coastlines, wind-rose of
  plain points, ruled rhumb lines, plain-dot soundings, dotted route with open-chevron arrows,
  the squall as dense hatched wind-whorl; exactly ONE gold-leaf element; baked text limited to
  two rubric-red labels ("GALILEE", "MATTHEW 8" — Style 3's short-label doctrine).
- **Series fit beyond Storm:** Jonah (Joppa→Tarshish, the storm mid-route — the chart of a man
  running FROM the gold point); Paul's shipwreck (Acts 27, the drift of Adria); the Red Sea
  crossing; the Exodus route; "he must needs go through Samaria" (John 4). Every journey episode
  gains an establishing or recap spread.
- **Proof still:** `style13_chart_crossing.png` — first-pass PASS. Both labels letter-perfect,
  wind-rose stayed letterless, squall-whorl bends around the gold flame exactly as designed, no
  stray pseudo-text, a real fold-crease aging the sheet.
- **Honest flags:** the lake reads as a generic pear-shaped inland sea, not the Kinneret's true
  harp outline — fine for a hand-chart idiom, but name the real shape in production if the
  episode's geography is load-bearing. Route runs NW→SE (Capernaum→Gadara, correct) but neither
  town is drawn; adding their two labels is a second lettering risk to test before relying on it.
- **Animation-ready?** By design NO generative motion (baked lettering — never-animate-writing);
  its motion language is the $0 device family: the controlled reading-order pan proven on Style 3
  (`style3_controlled_pan_test.mp4`), voyage-camera, measuring-reed/ARC arrows, raking-light.
- **Cost:** 2cr/still. **Confidence: HIGHEST of the round — it fills a real structural gap
  (journey/geography episodes) and its lettering discipline is now twice-proven.**

## Style 14 — GRAVER'S TEMPEST ("the line follows the wind")

- **The gap it fills:** awe-scale weather rendered as pure line-FORCE. An intaglio engraving
  where every stroke in the sky is a wind-line and every stroke in the sea is a current-line —
  one continuous field of force. The register's promise for the series: when He speaks, the
  line-field combs straight — the same lines, obedient. It gives nature-power episodes a
  documentary-print gravitas neither the watercolor spine nor the woodcut's blocky drama has.
- **Why it's different from 1-11:** Style 7 (woodcut) is bold chunky relief — few thick marks,
  stark black/white + ochre. This is the opposite pole of printmaking: thousands of fine swelling
  engraved lines, silvery tonal grays, atmosphere. Style 2 is loose; this is the most controlled
  style in the toolkit. Nothing else in 1-11 is tonal-line-system work.
- **Prompt approach:** intaglio line-engraving, warm black on ivory laid paper; dense swelling/
  tapering line systems, cross-hatched shadow, burin flicks for spray, rain as fine diagonals;
  deep blacks, silvery middles, sparing paper lights. No lettering.
- **Series fit beyond Storm:** the wind-field idea generalises to every "creation obeys" episode
  — fire from heaven (1 Kings 18), the withered fig tree, the earthquake at the tomb, Sinai in
  smoke, the plagues' hail; also Revelation's formal terrors, where engraving's severity fits.
- **Proof still:** `style14_engraving_tempest.png` (v2). v1 dressed the crew in brimmed European
  fisherman hats — the exact period-drift the lock exists for; re-rolled with positive-only
  wording (bare-headed, ancient Judean hair/beards, knee-length girded tunics), now clean (v1
  kept as `style14_engraving_tempest.v1_hats_defect.png`).
- **Honest flags:** prompt asked for three crew, the render seats FOUR (all bare-headed, all
  period) — four smallish engraved faces brushes the ≤3-distinct-faces guideline; acceptable at
  this scale but re-state the count in production. The two bailing buckets are hooped wooden
  pails — period-plausible but slightly European-cooper in feel; a plain clay/leather bailer
  would be safer. The sky's spiral vortex is spectacular but strong enough that using this
  register more than ~once per episode would exhaust it.
- **Animation-ready?** Line-boil was invented for exactly this surface, plus raking-light and a
  slow controlled pan; generative motion would shred the line systems — never send it there.
- **Cost:** 2cr/still. **Confidence: HIGH as the awe/severity register; use sparingly.**

## Style 15 — DEEP VIGIL ("the enormity of the deep") — a named variation of Style 5

- **The gap it fills:** Storm's fear beats at COSMIC scale. Style 5 (Passion Vigil) is intimate
  grave chiaroscuro — one figure, one light. Deep Vigil turns the same restraint outward: the sea
  and the night sky one continuous near-black, a tiny lantern-lit boat almost nothing between
  mountainous swells, one cold break of moonlight far above. It is Psalm 107:23-27 as a page —
  "they that go down to the sea in ships... they reel to and fro... and are at their wit's end"
  — the verse family the concordance already tied to this exact episode.
- **Why a variation, not a new style:** deliberately pitched as Style 5's storm-mood (the brief
  invites named variations); same paper, same palette discipline (slate/ash/bone), same
  one-pale-light theology — the ONLY changes are subject scale and a second permitted light (the
  tiny warm lantern, which is the human smallness the cold light answers). Adopting it costs
  nothing new: it inherits Style 5's ADOPT verdict and animation profile.
- **Prompt approach:** grave chiaroscuro ink nocturne on ash-gray; near-black masses hold almost
  the whole page; exactly TWO light sources (cold moonbreak above, warm lantern point below);
  nearly monochrome. No lettering.
- **Series fit beyond Storm:** Jonah thrown to the deep ("out of the belly of hell" — the same
  page with the lantern gone); the disciples rowing against the wind before the fourth watch
  (Mark 6:48); Paul's fourteenth night on Adria (Acts 27:27); "the waters saw thee; they were
  afraid" (Ps 77:16).
- **Proof still:** `style15_deepvigil_perish.png` — first-pass PASS. The scale statement lands:
  boat almost nothing against the deep, the deep almost nothing against the light.
- **Honest flags:** the lantern halo reads a touch too perfectly circular (slightly composited);
  the moonbreak is generous — for the very darkest beat ("carest thou not that we perish?") a
  meaner sliver would be crueler and better. Both are one-line prompt tweaks, not style failures.
- **Animation-ready?** Same profile as Style 5 (its Seedance moonbeam test passed in round 1):
  calm generative motion plausible; candle-only/raking-light/held-breath are the natural $0
  devices — the lantern is literally a candle-only anchor waiting for the skill.
- **Cost:** 2cr/still. **Confidence: HIGH — inherits an adopted register's proven physics.**

## The two COMBINATION proofs (the direct answer to "used in combination with it")

### Combo A — Style 3 USED WITH STORM: the Jonah 1 / Psalm 107:29 echo sheet

`combo_style3_jonah_echo.png` — first-pass PASS, and the round's most important lettering
result: **all three rubric-red labels rendered letter-perfect** — "JONAH 1", "MATTHEW 8",
"PSALM 107:29" (Style 3's native-lettering ceiling just moved from 2 labels to 3).

- **What it proves:** the shipped Storm episode's own review confirmed it shipped with ZERO OT
  echo, while concordance-loom had already surfaced Jonah 1:4 (verbatim storm-at-sea match) and
  Psalm 107:23-30 ("he maketh the storm a calm"). This sheet is that missing beat, in the
  already-adopted working-diagram register: LEFT, the ship of Joppa in tempest, one man cast
  over her side (Jonah 1); RIGHT, the Galilean boat on dead-flat water, Christ standing in her
  stern, HIS figure the sheet's only gold (Matthew 8); a sepia chevron arrow carrying the
  typology left-to-right; below, a small study of one storm wave settling into a flat calm line
  (Psalm 107:29). The arrow is doctrinally His own claim — "a greater than Jonas is here"
  (Matt 12:41): Jonah's storm was stilled by a man thrown OUT of the ship; this storm by the Man
  standing IN it. The Psalm names the LORD as the storm-calmer; Matthew shows Jesus doing it —
  the deity claim is the diagram.
- **How it sits WITH Style 1:** a 3-4s insert spread between the calm (s10/s11) and the landing
  (s13) — the "this has happened before, and it was the LORD" beat — treated with the controlled
  reading-order pan already proven on Style 3, never generative motion.
- **Honest flags:** (1) renders as a photographed bound book on a neutral ground, not a
  full-bleed spread — beautiful as an artifact, but integration into Storm needs a $0 crop or a
  re-prompt with the collage framing; (2) the gold landed on Christ's GARMENT (plus halo rays)
  rather than the light around him — within the "gold = the Christ element" discipline and it
  reads as glory-not-costume, but if the show prefers canon-undyed cloth with gold radiance,
  say so in production wording; (3) right-panel scale reads figure-study rather than naturalistic
  (his boat is dinghy-small against him — diagram idiom absorbs it, §8a.3 would not forgive it
  in a narrative spread); (4) the Jonah panel seats ~5 small crew faces — study-sheet scale
  absorbs it, but production wording should shadow the extras.

### Combo B — Style 6 USED WITH STORM: the "what manner of man" gold-ground plate

`combo_style6_manner_of_man.png` — first-pass PASS.

- **What it proves:** Storm's climax question — "what manner of man is this, that even the winds
  and the sea obey him!" (Matt 8:27) — is a deity-claim beat, and Style 6 exists for exactly
  those. The plate answers the question the disciples couldn't: the entire sky from horizon to
  page-top is burnished gold leaf (visible seams, burnish marks — the heavens holding His glory,
  the storm simply GONE), the sea lies as flat lapis glass carrying one narrow gold reflection
  column straight down from Him (the calm water giving His glory back — the still-water-mirror
  idea in pigment), and three disciples shrink back in lapis shadow, faces lifted. Christ's
  raiment stays canon-undyed homespun — the glory is the GROUND he stands against, not a costume.
- **How it sits WITH Style 1:** the episode's one gold-ground plate, on the climax question or as
  the landing's alternate — 1-2 per episode ever, per the register's own scarcity theology. Gold
  sheen breathing (the round-1 Kling lesson says there's nothing generative to animate in an icon
  — use raking-light's gold flare instead, one flare per episode on this exact spread).
- **Honest flags:** (1) same photographed-codex framing as Combo A (crop or re-prompt for
  integration); (2) the mainsail is half-furled on the yard — after "there was a great calm" a
  fully furled sail would be tidier history (they were rowing; the storm had owned the sail);
  minor, one prompt line; (3) disciple at center-right grips the gunwale with a slightly rubbery
  wrist at extreme zoom — passes §8a.1 (five fingers, one hand) but noted for honesty.
