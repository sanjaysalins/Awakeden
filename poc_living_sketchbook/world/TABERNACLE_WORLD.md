# TABERNACLE WORLD -- object + setting canon (Day of Atonement, repo-level)

Extends `cast/` (recurring PEOPLE) to recurring OBJECTS and SETTINGS, per the
repeated-element census standing rule added 2026-08-03
(`.claude/skills/living-sketchbook/SKILL.md` sec.2). Every entry below is
grounded in an explicit KJV citation -- nothing invented that Scripture
states, nothing claimed that Scripture is silent on (same discipline as
[[feedback-verify-character-age-scale-before-render]], extended from
character ages to object/room scale). Anchor PNGs live in this same
`world/` folder; a regenerated anchor is a DIFFERENT object, never lose it.

## Correction, same session: the "cross-time NT-quote device" is NOT a new object

An earlier pass of this census (2 turns ago) listed "the signposted
cross-time NT-quote device (EW-INV-11)" as something needing a locked
visual treatment, on the assumption it was a visual technique. Checked
against the actual repo usage: **EW-INV-11 is a NARRATION-VOICE invariant**
(it permits an eyewitness to speak with knowledge from "the far side of his
life" -- found in independent-review notes for EW02_Abraham, e.g.
`longform/EW02_Abraham/v1/_independent_review/20260703-083505/grok.md`),
not a visual device at all. Beat 6's four NT/OT quotes (Hebrews x3, Isaiah,
Matthew) need no new anchor -- they render through the EXISTING lettering
grammar already locked in SKILL.md sec.5 (Scribed Ink for verse reveals,
Illuminated Rubric for formal beats, the Thread Device for an OT-to-NT
pairing). The only real note for Beat 6 is pacing: four quote-reveals back
to back in one beat is a lot, worth a deliberate rhythm check at the scene-
plan stage, not a new build item here.

## 1. The Tabernacle (exterior) -- SETTING -- anchor: `world/tabernacle_ref.png`

Exodus 26:1-30 (structure: acacia-wood boards overlaid with gold, silver
sockets, joined by bars) + Exodus 26:14 (coverings: goats'-hair curtains,
then rams' skins dyed red, then badgers'/sea cows' skins on top -- the
outward-facing layer is a plain, humble hide roof, NOT gold or ornament) +
Exodus 27:9-19 (a surrounding courtyard, linen hangings on bronze pillars).

> A low, rectangular tent shrine standing inside a wide courtyard fenced by
> plain white linen hangings on bronze pillars. The tent's outward roof is
> plain, weathered reddish-brown and grey animal hide -- humble and
> unornamented from the outside, nothing gold visible from without. Gold
> only shows at the structure's base, in a glimpse of gold-overlaid wooden
> wall-boards beneath the hide covering. Desert wilderness setting, low
> raking light, long shadows across sand.

## 2. The door-curtain (tabernacle entrance) -- OBJECT -- folded into #1's render

Exodus 26:36-37: blue, purple, scarlet, and fine twined linen, wrought with
NEEDLEWORK (a deliberate contrast -- no cherubim), hung on 5 pillars of
acacia overlaid with gold, standing in sockets of BRASS (bronze), not
silver. This is the curtain Aaron stands at in Beat 2 ("the door of the
tabernacle") while forbidden to mourn -- a DIFFERENT curtain from the veil
below, and must read as visually simpler (no cherubim, bronze not silver)
so the two are never confused. Shown as part of the tabernacle exterior
shot (#1), not a separate render.

## 3. The Holy Place (inner chamber, outside the veil) -- SETTING -- text-locked only

Exodus 25:23-30 (table of shewbread, north side, twelve loaves) + Exodus
25:31-40 (the golden lampstand, south side) + Exodus 30:1-10 (the golden
altar of incense, standing before the veil). Walls are the same gold-
overlaid acacia boards as the exterior structure, seen from within. Low
drift-risk as a passage-through space Aaron crosses to reach the veil -- no
dedicated render; use this text if a spread ever needs to show it directly.

## 4. The veil (inner curtain) -- OBJECT -- anchor: `world/veil_ref.png`

Exodus 26:31-33: blue, purple, scarlet, and fine twined linen, **with
cherubim of cunning work woven into it** (the one detail that must set it
apart from the plainer door-curtain above), hung on four pillars of acacia
overlaid with gold, gold hooks, upon four sockets of SILVER.

> A heavy hanging curtain of deep blue, purple, and scarlet wool woven with
> fine linen, with ancient winged sacred beings woven directly into the
> fabric in gold thread -- the SAME golden winged form as the cherubim on
> the ark's mercy seat: solemn, otherworldly, composite winged shapes,
> faces obscured or turned away, wings dominant. NOT cute Western
> cherub-babies, no halos, no rounded infant faces, nothing Renaissance or
> Baroque -- these are ancient Near Eastern sacred emblems, not Christian
> religious art. Hung from gold hooks on four gold-clad pillars. Richer
> and more ornamented than a plain doorway hanging -- this is the curtain
> that divides the holy place from the most holy place.

**Real defect caught + fixed, first render (2026-08-03):** the first roll
rendered the cherubim as Western cherub-babies with halos -- a post-biblical
Christian iconographic convention, and inconsistent with the Ark's own
cherubim in the same batch (rendered as abstract golden wings, no faces).
Fixed by naming the failure mode explicitly in the prompt and pointing at
the Ark's cherubim as the shared reference; the corrected render uses
composite ancient Near-Eastern winged forms (echoing Ezekiel 1/10's
lion/man/eagle-faced cherubim), no halos, no infant faces.

**Continuity across the story's whole timespan:** Beats 1 and 3 show this
curtain whole; Beats 6 and 7 show it torn "from the top to the bottom"
(Matthew 27:51). Scripturally the torn veil is a later, physical curtain in
Herod's Temple, not this wilderness-tabernacle one -- but the narration
treats it as one continuous image ("the veil I trembled before... was
torn"). Keep the SAME design across both beats; do not redesign a
"temple version." The torn state chains this anchor as a reference and
adds a vertical rent from top to bottom, gold light spilling through from
behind -- this is also the project's own standard torn-page/torn-veil
landing device (SKILL.md sec.1), a natural fit already in the toolkit.

## 5. The Holy of Holies + the Ark of the Covenant + Mercy Seat -- SETTING+OBJECT combined -- anchor: `world/holyofholies_ref.png`

Exodus 25:10-22: the ark is acacia wood overlaid with gold within and
without, "two cubits and a half... a cubit and a half... a cubit and a
half" (roughly the size of a large chest, waist-to-chest high), a gold
crown/molding round the top, four gold rings with acacia-and-gold staves
for carrying (never set down bare). The mercy seat is pure gold, the same
footprint as the ark's top, with two gold cherubim of hammered work at
each end, wings stretched upward, covering the mercy seat, faces turned
toward each other and down toward the seat.

> A small, perfectly square, windowless chamber, utterly dark except for a
> low golden cloud-glow filling the space -- and otherwise EMPTY. In the
> center: a gold-overlaid wooden chest about waist-high, its lid two facing
> golden cherubim with wings stretched up and inward, their faces bowed
> toward the space between them. Nothing else stands in this room -- no
> other furniture, no chair, no seat for a man.

The room's emptiness is not a style choice -- it is the narration's own
point (Beat 6: "I always stood at my work; there was no chair behind that
veil"). Any spread depicting this space must keep it visually bare besides
the ark itself, or the line's payoff is undercut.

## 6. The altar (bronze, courtyard) -- OBJECT -- anchor: `world/altar_ref.png`

Exodus 27:1-8: acacia wood overlaid with bronze (not gold), "five cubits
long, and five cubits broad... foursquare... three cubits" high (a
substantial altar, roughly shoulder-high and several strides across, not a
small tabletop object), a horn at each of the four corners, a bronze
grating/network.

> A large, waist-to-shoulder-high foursquare altar of dark weathered
> bronze, a raised horn projecting from each of its four corners, its
> surface scorched and stained dark from repeated burning -- rough,
> heavy, functional metal, deliberately plain next to the gold furnishings
> kept inside the tent. Standing in the open courtyard under open sky.

## 7. The two goats -- OBJECT -- anchor: `world/goat_ref.png` -- ONE design for BOTH animals

Leviticus 16:5-10 gives no physical distinction between the two goats --
the LOT (cast by Aaron) decides which is slain and which is sent away, not
anything visible about the animal itself. **Deliberately do not invent a
color, marking, or size difference between them** -- that would misstate
the actual point of the rite (the outcome is not read off the creature, it
falls by lot). One locked goat design, reused for both:

> An ordinary young wilderness goat, short coarse brown-and-cream coat,
> plain and unremarkable -- a common sin-offering animal, nothing
> distinguished about its appearance. The coat is clean, dry fur only --
> no red or blue ink, no wet marks, no drips or streaks of any color
> anywhere on the animal's body or legs.

**Real defect caught + fixed, first render (2026-08-03):** the first roll
bled the page style's own muted ink-red/ink-blue accent washes onto the
goat's legs and neck as vertical streaks -- readable as blood or dye on a
sacrificial animal, a real problem for this scene. Fixed by explicitly
confining those accent colors to the paper border in the prompt; the
corrected render has a clean, unmarked coat.

Distinguish "the goat for the LORD" (slain) from "the scapegoat" (sent
away) by STAGING only, never by appearance: the first appears at the
altar/blood context, the second appears led away and receding into the
wilderness distance.

## 8. The blood basin -- OBJECT -- text-locked only, no render

Scripture does not specify the basin's material here (Exodus 24:6 uses the
plural "basons" for a comparable rite, likewise without describing them).

> A plain, unadorned shallow bowl of hammered bronze or fired clay -- humble
> and functional, no ornamentation.

Low drift-risk as a small generic prop -- not worth a dedicated reference
render; use this text description wherever it appears.

## 9. The city gate (1st-century Jerusalem) -- SETTING -- anchor: `world/citygate_ref.png` -- DIFFERENT ERA from items 1-8

Hebrews 13:12 "Jesus... suffered without the gate" places this at
1st-century Jerusalem (Second Temple period), NOT the wilderness camp of
items 1-8 above -- spread 57 pairs them as a typological MV (Christ led
out of the city / the goat's body carried outside the camp), two
different times shown side by side, never merged into one setting. Added
2026-08-04, mid-episode, when the plan reached spread 57/58 and no anchor
existed yet for it.

> A massive ancient city gate built of huge dressed limestone blocks with
> drafted margins (Herodian-era ashlar masonry), set into a thick
> sun-baked stone city wall, a tall arched gateway opening onto a rough
> dirt road leading out into hilly Judean wilderness beyond -- weathered,
> sun-bleached stone, harsh midday light, no ornament, no modern
> hardware, no medieval towers or crenellations -- plain functional
> 1st-century Near Eastern construction.

## Usage

Chain the relevant anchor via `--image` + append "the SAME [object/room] as
the reference image -- identical materials, colors, and proportions" for
every prompt showing that element, the same discipline as `cast/`'s
character anchors (SKILL.md sec.2).
