- Global audience: simple words, short sentences, easy to follow by ear (it will be read aloud by a voice actor). No archaic word carrying the main point.

## Originals and what the independent panel (5 reviewers) found wrong

### jericho (Joshua 2, 6; Hebrews 11:31; Matthew 1:5). Original (153 words):
Jericho had the strongest walls in the world. And God told His army to march around them... in silence. Six days they circled. Thirteen laps in all. Not one arrow. Just feet on sand... and one scarlet cord, hanging from one window. Inside those walls lived Rahab. A woman with a past... who hid God's spies, and believed. Their answer: "Bind this line of scarlet thread in the window." On the seventh day the trumpets tore the sky. "The people shouted with a great shout... the wall fell down flat." Every stone came down. One window stood. Scripture says it plainly: "By faith the harlot Rahab perished not." Not spared for being good. Spared for believing. Now read Jesus' family tree: "Salmon begat Booz of Rachab." The woman behind the scarlet cord became His family. There is a scarlet line running from her window to a cross. His blood. Bind everything to it.
Problems: invented "strongest walls in the world"; "Not one arrow" and "one window stood" are not in the text (Joshua 6:20 says the wall fell down flat; Rahab's household was saved, 6:25); quotes were not exact KJV (missing words, ellipses); "Their answer:" appears with no setup (the spies' promise, Joshua 2); "Bind everything to it" is vague and can sound like works; the genealogy line sounds like a reading instruction; the blood link must be framed as a picture, not a verse-stated fact; Rahab = Rachab confuses listeners.

### two_goats (Leviticus 16; Isaiah 53:6; Hebrews 9-10; Matthew 27:51). Original (189 words, first-person high priest):
Once a year I walked behind a curtain to meet God and I was never sure I would walk back out. The law gave me two goats for one sin. I killed the first and carried its blood inside the veil. The second I did not kill I laid my hands on its head, confessed every sin of the people over it, and drove it into the desert to be lost. One to pay. One to carry it away. For years I asked why it took two. I know now. No single creature could hold both halves. Long after me a Man named Jesus did what my two goats only pictured He was the price paid, and the One the guilt was laid on: and the LORD hath laid on him the iniquity of us all. He carried it away Himself once and sat down. Finished. The curtain I trembled behind tore in two the hour He died. I was only the sign. He is the substance. Don't come to me, or a goat, or an altar. Come to Jesus the way in is torn wide open. Walk in.
Problems: "two goats for one sin" misstates Leviticus 16 (two goats, ONE sin offering for the people's sins, once a year); "never sure I would walk back out" and "years I asked why" are invented testimony; "no single creature could hold both halves" is invented explanation; "drove it into the desert to be lost" misdescribes the scapegoat (it bears the iniquities into a land not inhabited, v.22); the priest narrator is never identified (name him: Aaron, the first high priest); the Isaiah 53:6 quote lands mid-sentence with no lead-in (must be its own clean sentence, attributed); missing sentence stops make it hard to hear; "Come to Jesus" is a stock cap; "Don't come to me, or a goat, or an altar" is confusing. Keep "tore in two the hour He died" ONLY as Matthew 27:51 allows (the veil of the temple was rent in twain from the top to the bottom).

### bronze_serpent (Numbers 21:7-9; John 3:14-15; Galatians 3:13). Original (203 words, first-person Moses):
I am Moses. My people were dying of snakebite - and God told me to forge a snake of bronze and lift it on a pole. The serpents were no accident - we had spoken against God, and the venom was the judgment our sin had earned. I begged Him to take the snakes away. He would not. Instead He told me to forge the image of the very thing killing us, and lift it high. The bitten had only to look - and live. I speak now from the far side of my life, by the light that came after - a night I never saw, when one they called Teacher answered a seeker: And as Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, even so must the Son of man be lifted up: My bronze was only a shadow. They lifted Jesus on a Roman pole, made a curse for us, bearing our judgment in our place. So hear me, you who are bitten - that is every one of us. The cure was never in you; it hangs in plain sight, and costs you nothing but a look. Lift your eyes to Jesus, lifted up for you. Look, and live.
Problems: "I begged Him to take the snakes away. He would not." is invented (Numbers 21:7: the people confessed and asked Moses to pray; the LORD answered with the serpent remedy); the John 3:14 quote is not marked as Jesus' words and cuts off mid-sentence; "a night I never saw ... a Teacher answered a seeker" confuses newcomers (say plainly: Jesus told Nicodemus); "look" must be tied to believing (John 3:15, "whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have eternal life"); "costs you nothing but a look" and "Roman pole" are weak/unsupported; "made a curse for us" is a half-quote of Galatians 3:13 (quote it right or say it plainly); "forge" is not in the text (it says Moses made a serpent of brass); "the very thing killing us" overstates (a brass serpent LIKE the ones that were biting).
