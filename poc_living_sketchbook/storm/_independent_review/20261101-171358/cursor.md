## Independent adversarial review (narration)

### Doctrinal soundness
- **Invented motive for the nap:** *“He is not asleep because He doesn't care. He is asleep because He has nothing to fear from the very thing that is killing them.”* The pericope states that **he was asleep** (Matt 8:24) and that the disciples feared perishing (8:25); it does **not** explain his inner calm as “nothing to fear.” That is pastoral psychologizing, not exposition from the text. Orthodox, but easy to over-read as “Jesus felt no human pressure in the boat,” which can brush against the humanity side of the incarnation if a listener pushes on it.
- **Faith growth claim:** *“He doesn't despise little faith. He grows it by showing you Himself.”* “O ye of little faith” supports the first sentence; **“grows it by showing you Himself”** is a general soteriological/pastoral gloss, not something this scene states. Acceptable evangelical habit, but it is **added doctrine**, not drawn from the storm narrative itself.
- **“You'll say — easy for Him, He's God. Exactly.”** Doctrinally fine for evangelical Christology and matches the implied deity of the rebuke. No contrarian reading detected.

### KJV verbatim
- Presented as scripture (after *“He opens His eyes:”*): *“Why are ye fearful, O ye of little faith? Then he arose, and rebuked the winds and the sea; and there was a great calm.”* Matches **Matthew 8:26** KJV for that substring (verified against project `kjv_cache.json`).
- **No quotation marks** anywhere; your gate is “inside quotation marks,” so formally nothing is flagged as mis-quoted—but listeners may still hear the middle block as **Scripture voice**. Worth noting for production: attribution is thin (no *“Scripture says”* / book reference).
- Closing: *“why are you afraid?”* is **modern paraphrase** of *“Why are ye fearful?”* (not in quotes). Fine for accessibility; it **diverges** from the KJV anchor word *fearful* used eight seconds earlier in the piece—mild ear inconsistency for KJV-purists.

### Clarity on first hearing (zero Bible knowledge)
- **Unidentified “He”:** *“And in the stern — He is asleep.”* A stranger has not been told this is Jesus, the Lord, or the Christ until *“You'll say — easy for Him, He's God”* and later *“the Christ in the boat.”* Five seconds in, “He” is ambiguous (captain? passenger?).
- **Assumed church memory:** *“You have admired this scene your whole life.”* Excludes listeners who have **never** heard the story; the hook’s “you in the boat” works, this line does not.
- **“Men screaming”** vs the Gospel’s **disciples** addressing *“Lord, save us: we perish”* (Matt 8:25): cinematic, but it **blurs who is in the boat** and weakens the later mirror (*you* = disciple) for someone tracking facts for the first time.

### Grace-anchored conviction
- **Accusatory middle:** *“when the water reaches your knees, you don't trust the Christ in the boat. You wake Him, furious, certain He doesn't care.”* Strong mirror of the disciples; grace softening arrives only in the next breath (*“He doesn't despise little faith”*). On one pass it can land as **“you fail faith”** before invitation—borderline gain/loss of moral status (“you don't trust”) rather than pure invitation.
- No explicit works-salvation or damnation scare; storm stays metaphorical. Acceptable but **tight** on tone.

### Freshness = faithful depth
- **Strong:** sleep in the stern while the boat dies; tying **sleep → authority to calm** (*“The One who could sleep through it is the One who silenced it with a word”*) is a legitimate textual irony from Matthew’s ordering (asleep → rebuke → calm).
- **Weaker / generic:** *“He grows it by showing you Himself”* could sit on almost any miracle story.
