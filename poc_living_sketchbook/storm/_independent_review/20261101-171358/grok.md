# Independent review — grok (OK, 164s)

Hook **8**. Landing **5**. The last sentence could close any fear reel, so the landing stays at the stock-closer cap.

The KJV run is exact Matthew 8:26 from “Why are ye fearful, O ye of little faith?” through “there was a great calm.” No altered word. The scene is orthodox: He is God, He is in the boat, little faith is met by showing Himself. It is not heresy. It does not hold together on a first hear.

**The care line is never paid.** “He is not asleep because He doesn't care” is the promise. The proof that follows is “He has nothing to fear” and “The One who could sleep through it is the One who silenced it with a word.” Fearlessness and power do not answer care. A listener can grant that He is unafraid and still ask whether He cares. The text’s own answer is the part this cut skips: He gets up, the sea obeys, and they say what manner of man this is. You denied the accusation and then proved a different claim.

**The colon makes Matthew’s narrator sound like Jesus.** “He opens His eyes: Why are ye fearful, O ye of little faith? Then he arose, and rebuked the winds and the sea…” “Then he arose…” is not His sentence. On a first hear it is one speech, and He seems to narrate Himself in the third person.

**“Exactly” is a trick, not a turn.** “You'll say — easy for Him, He's God. Exactly.” A new listener hears you agree that the scene does not apply to them. The next line tries to flip it. The flip is never said in plain words: you are not asked to sleep like God; you are asked to trust the One who slept and who stilled the sea.

**Zero Bible knowledge gets lost, then accused.** “You have admired this scene your whole life” assumes a church memory the stranger does not have. “Water in the boat past your knees” puts me in the boat; “the very thing that is killing them” and “They shake Him” jump to other men; “when the water reaches your knees, you don't trust” jumps back and prosecutes. “You wake Him, furious, certain He doesn't care” is the narrator assigning a motive. The Spirit can convict. This script indicts. “He grows it by showing you Himself” is the one grace line, and it is crowded out by that charge.

**The storm’s tone is heightened past the verse.** Matthew has “Lord, save us: we perish.” Mark has “carest thou not that we perish?” Neither says they are “raging,” and the listener is not shown to be “furious.” “Past your knees” and “He opens His eyes” are also extra. “He is asleep because He has nothing to fear” states a motive the text does not state. Mark also has Him asleep on a pillow. Worn-out and Lord of the storm can both be true. This script keeps only the second.

**Spine frays at the close.** Hook image: asleep in the stern while the boat fills. Middle: deity, then a trust failure, then little faith. Close: “Come to the Christ who slept unafraid” still belongs to this piece. “He is asking you — why are you afraid?” does not. That question could end a different short. It also leaves the piece on the rebuke and drops the passage’s own landing, who He is.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Pay the care claim with the text’s own ending — He rises, the sea obeys, “what manner of man” — and make the final sentence carry this boat and this sleep so it cannot close another piece.
2. Cut “admired this scene your whole life” and “you don't trust… furious, certain He doesn't care”; let His question and the calm invite, and keep one “you” in the boat from the first line to the last.
3. Split “Then he arose…” off His words, drop the “Exactly” fake-agree, and stop narrating “raging,” “furious,” and an unstated reason for the sleep.
