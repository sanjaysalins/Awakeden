The one idea: the sketchbook has a person. Someone is keeping this book — studying the story, reacting in real time, thinking on paper. Every mark below is that one person's habit: their quick hand (never the formal Word register), their doodled studies, their crossed-out word search. The rule that keeps it from being random: if a real journal-keeper wouldn't make that mark, it doesn't ship. The Keeper's words here are placeholder voice — panel-reviewed like narration before anything real ships.

A · The panic entry — handwriting as acting

On the first wave spread: the Keeper hunts for the word — storm struck through, wind struck through, "fear." left standing — then "water at our knees" written jagged and rushed, baseline heaving, letters leaning… and the last stroke SKIDS off the page as something hits the boat.

The mechanics: one energy number (0–1) drives jitter, heave, lean, pressure, and burst-timing of the reveal. It's the same engine as clip B — only the fear is different. The narration's own energy curve (held-breath) would drive it in a real episode.
The Word never shakes: this hand is Ink Free (a working pencil hand), never Kunstler — the KJV's formal register stays untouched and central. Human words search and shake; the Word stands.
Fable's eye-check, honest: works — the heave and the skid read. One placement miss: "storm" (the first struck word) lands half on the dark wave art and loses legibility. Real placement planning fixes this; noted so you judge the DEVICE, not the placement.

B · The calm line — the rebuke, readable in the handwriting

On the glass-sea spread: one line, dead steady, evenly paced — "not a breath of wind. not one."

Play A then B back to back. Same hand, same ink, same engine — the storm and the calm told entirely by HOW a person writes. This is the pair that makes the device: not decoration, but a performance of the story's turn.

C · The lamp studies — doodles with a reason

On the "asleep" spread: two quick pencil studies of the LAMP appear in the bottom margin — the same subject tried twice, the way an artist draws what they can't stop looking at — then a wobbly leader line up to the real lamp, and a tiny note: "still burning."

Why the lamp: the one warm thing on the page while the storm rages — the Keeper noticed. The studies are derived from the spread's own art ($0, pencil-conversion) so they can never contradict the drawing.
Fable's eye-check, honest: the idea reads but the studies render too FAINT at video scale — they need roughly double the graphite contrast to feel deliberate rather than ghostly. One-parameter fix if the device survives your taste gate.

D · The field header — every episode is an entry

The episode opens the way a journal entry opens: "Galilee. evening. crossing over." — steady hand, small, top margin. ("When the even was come" — Mark 4:35. No invented dates, ever.)

The quiet win: the series stops being clips and becomes ONE BOOK with many entries. Episode 2 is the next entry. That's seriality the audience can feel without being told.
