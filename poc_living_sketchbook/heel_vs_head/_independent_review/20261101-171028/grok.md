# Independent review — grok (OK, 169s)

Hook: 4. Landing: 6. Both sit under 8, so this does not pass.

**The script fights the verse it quotes.** It promises "something sharper": "one of them gets a bruise, and the other gets a crushed skull." It then says God tells the serpent "his head will be crushed," offers "a crushed skull on one side and a survivable bruise on the other," and closes on "a crushed head that stays crushed forever." The KJV line it reads uses one verb for both wounds: "it shall bruise thy head, and thou shalt bruise his heel." "Crushed," "skull," and "survivable" are not in that sentence. The sharpness that is actually in the KJV is the place of the wound, head against heel. A first-time listener hears the narrator say "crushed," then hears Scripture say "bruise."

**"Survivable bruise" cuts the cost of the cross.** The same piece later says the heel "carried a cross." Those claims collide. In the Christian reading the heel wound is real death. "Survivable" makes the cross sound like a scrape he walked off.

**The scene is misstated.** "God tells the serpent his head will be crushed, and tells the woman's seed only His heel will be bruised." Genesis 3 speaks to the serpent. The seed is spoken about. "Only" is added. On audio, capital "His" is silent, so the Christ cue never arrives.

**KJV quote.** The words inside the quotes match except the opening word. KJV Genesis 3:15 begins "And I will put enmity..." The script starts the quotation at "I will put enmity..."

**Time is muddy.** "The outcome was fixed before the fight began" sits next to "Christ already delivered the blow." A new listener cannot tell whether the cross was the fight or a result already settled before anyone was hurt.

**A stranger cannot follow the open.** "You've heard Genesis 3:15" assumes the scene. "One of them" never says who gets the skull, so for a moment Jesus may be the one with the crushed skull. "Seed" and "enmity" stay unpacked. "Seed" will be heard as a plant. "For a promise this old" never explains why matching wounds would be expected. "A bruised heel that carried a cross" is a riddle. A heel does not carry a cross.

**Grace is scolded, not invited.** "You keep trying to land your own blow against what haunts you, hoping for a tie you can live with" puts the listener in the dock and aims at what they can live with. That is works and self-interest. "Christ already delivered the blow that ends it" is the grace note, and it never says the blow was for the hearer. No one is invited.

**The middle swaps the enemy.** "Not a tie" does run from the open to the last line. The foe in the text is the serpent. "What haunts you" can mean fear, grief, or a bad week. Genesis 3:15 does not say Christ crushed whatever haunts the listener.

**Hook: 4.** The first five seconds are "You've heard Genesis 3:15 means Jesus beats the devil. It says something..." "You've heard" is a corrective template, and the citation gives a stranger nothing. "Crushed skull" is about 26 words in, past the window, and still unnamed as to who receives it.

**Landing: 6.** "Not a tie - a bruised heel that carried a cross, and a crushed head that stays crushed forever" is built from this text's own images, so it is not a stock "come to Jesus" closer. It still misses the bones. It repeats the thesis, the heel-carrying-a-cross image snags, "forever" is not in the verse, and the listener is not addressed.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Drop "crushed skull" and "survivable bruise." Let the sharpness be head versus heel under the KJV verb "bruise," and let the cross be real death.
2. Quote "And I will put enmity..." in full, and fix the scene: God speaks to the serpent about the seed. He does not address the seed, and "only" is not in the verse.
3. Put who is wounded, and where, in the first sentence for a stranger. Cut "what haunts you" and "a heel that carried a cross." Close by inviting the hearer with the heel and the head.
