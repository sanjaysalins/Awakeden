# Independent review — gemini (OK, 32s)

Here is the independent review of the narration artifact:

**FINDINGS:**

*   **Clarity on first hearing / Self-contradiction (CRITICAL):** The entire script is built on a verbal contrast between a "bruise" and a "crushed skull" (e.g., *"instead you get a crushed skull on one side and a survivable bruise on the other"*). However, the script then quotes the KJV verbatim: *"it shall **bruise** thy head, and thou shalt **bruise** his heel."* The KJV uses the exact same word ("bruise") for both actions. A listener will immediately hear that the Bible verse just quoted does *not* say "crush," which makes the narrator sound like they are misquoting or exaggerating the text. You cannot build a script's central conceit around the word "crush" when the mandatory KJV text explicitly uses "bruise" for both.
*   **Doctrinal soundness / KJV verbatim:** The KJV quote itself is accurate, but the script's exposition forces a modern translation's vocabulary (crush) onto the KJV text. The contrast in Genesis 3:15 isn't in the *verb* (the Hebrew word *shuph* is used for both, hence the KJV's matching "bruise"), but in the *target* of the blow (a heel wound is painful but survivable; a head wound from a stomping foot is fatal).
*   **Grace-anchored conviction:** The conviction is excellent. *"You keep trying to land your own blow against what haunts you, hoping for a tie you can live with"* is a profound, grace-anchored pivot that avoids works-based framing.
*   **One thread spine:** The "not a tie" thread is strong and carries all the way through, but the execution is broken by the vocabulary mismatch.
*   **HOOK SCORE: 6/10.** The opening is punchy (*"one of them gets a bruise, and the other gets a crushed skull"*), but it sets up a direct contradiction with the KJV verse that follows 10 seconds later. 
*   **LANDING SCORE: 8/10.** *"A bruised heel that carried a cross, and a crushed head that stays crushed forever"* is a fantastic, piece-specific landing that avoids stock closers, but it currently relies on the flawed "crush" vocabulary.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Rebuild the central contrast around the *location* of the wound (the fatal head vs. the survivable heel) rather than the *verb* (crush vs. bruise), since the KJV uses "bruise" for both.
2. Remove all references to "crushed skull" or "crushed head" so the script doesn't contradict its own KJV quotation. 
3. Maintain the excellent "not a tie" thread and the grace-anchored CTA, but ground the victory in the anatomy of the strike (the Seed stomping the head) rather than a word the KJV doesn't use.
