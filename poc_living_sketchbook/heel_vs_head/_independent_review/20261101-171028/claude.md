# Independent review — claude (OK, 28s)

**Hook: 5/10. Landing: 4/10.** Both are under 8, so the verdict is REVISE.

**Findings**

1. **The hook contradicts the quoted KJV.**
   - The hook says "one of them gets a bruise, and the other gets a crushed skull."
   - The KJV it then quotes says "it shall bruise thy head, and thou shalt bruise his heel." Both wounds are "bruise," so the KJV has no bruise-versus-crush contrast.
   - The "crushed" contrast comes from other translations. The narration builds its whole thesis ("This isn't a tie," "crushed skull on one side") on a word the quoted text doesn't have.
   - A listener hears "bruise… bruise" in the quote and the hook falls apart. The piece rests on a translation claim, not on what the text says, and it reads as a freshness stretch.
   - Fix this first.

2. **Wrong speaker or audience.** "God… tells the woman's seed only His heel will be bruised." God speaks to the serpent in Genesis 3. The seed isn't addressed, and the phrase "only His heel" isn't in the verse.

3. **Imprecise doctrine on the cross.**
   - "A bruised heel that carried a cross" mixes up two things. The heel bruise is the suffering of the crucifixion, not carrying wood.
   - "Survivable bruise" is odd for a Christ who died.
   - "A crushed head that stays crushed forever" is unclear while the enemy still acts. It needs a plain "defeated at the cross" framing.
   - "Christ already delivered the blow" is fine, but the piece never names Jesus or the cross before the last line.

4. **Muddled logic in the middle.** "You might expect matching wounds for a promise this old" is a non-sequitur: age has nothing to do with matching wounds. It also restates the hook, so the same point appears three times.

5. **Unearned pivot to "you."**
   - "You keep trying to land your own blow against what haunts you, hoping for a tie" has no basis in the text. It invents a psychology.
   - It leans toward works and self-effort. The point of the pivot is that you can't win, but the line never says that.
   - It is vague ("what haunts you") and could mean anything.
   - It is not an invitation. Nothing in the piece asks the listener to do anything.

6. **Global audience.** A zero-knowledge listener isn't told who the serpent, the woman or "seed" are. "Tie" and "seed" are idioms and jargon. "Jesus beats the devil" is assumed and never set up.

7. **The landing could end other pieces.** "Not a tie - a bruised heel… crushed head" is only a recap of the hook. It is not a CTA, and it has no verb for the listener. It does reuse the piece's own image, which saves it from the lowest score.

8. **Quote check.** The quoted words match the KJV (the leading "And" is dropped, which is acceptable). The quote is not the problem. The surrounding paraphrase is.

**Doctrine:** The Christ-lens reading of Genesis 3:15 is orthodox. The problems are the translation-based asymmetry claim and the loose cross language, not the reading itself.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Rebuild the hook on what the KJV actually says. Use "bruise" for both, and take the asymmetry from the real contrast: the head is the fatal place and the heel is not. Drop "crushed skull" unless it is framed as another translation.
2. Replace the invented "you keep trying to land your own blow… hoping for a tie" with a text-grounded, grace-anchored invitation. Name Jesus and the cross plainly, say the heel-bruise is his suffering and death, and end on a specific verb drawn from the piece, not a recap.
3. Fix the accuracy and clarity problems. God speaks to the serpent only. Remove "carried a cross" and "survivable." Explain serpent, seed and "tie" in plain words, and cut the repeated restatements and the "promise this old" non-sequitur.
