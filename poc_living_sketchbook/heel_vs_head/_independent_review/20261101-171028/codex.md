# Independent review — codex (OK, 50s)

1. **Major doctrinal problem: “a survivable bruise.”** Paired with “only His heel,” this implies Jesus survived the decisive wound. He actually died and rose again. The heel imagery cannot establish that His suffering was nonfatal; Hebrews explicitly locates His victory **through death**. Omitting the resurrection makes this distortion harder for a newcomer to detect. [Hebrews 2:14–15, KJV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Hebrews+2%3A14-15&version=KJV)

2. **The novelty claim overstates the text: “It says something sharper.”** The KJV uses *bruise* for both wounds. Their locations support the asymmetry; contrasting a minor bruise with a crushed skull adds an interpretation and then presents it as the verse’s explicit wording. “You might expect matching wounds” manufactures an expectation the passage never creates. The orthodox claim of Christ’s victory does not need this contrivance. [Genesis 3:15, KJV](https://search.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Gen+3%3A15&version=KJV)

3. **KJV quotation: missing opening word.** The quotation begins “I will put”; the verse begins **“And I will put.”** Every remaining quoted word matches. This is an omitted opening word, rather than an altered word within the retained excerpt, but restore it for the requested strict verbatim standard. [Genesis 3:15, KJV](https://search.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Gen+3%3A15&version=KJV)

4. **First-hearing confusion: “one of them” and “tells the woman’s seed.”** The opening has not clearly identified both combatants, and *seed* remains unexplained. God addresses the serpent throughout the quoted verse; He does not switch to addressing the woman’s offspring. “A bruised heel that carried a cross” further makes the body part perform the person’s action.

5. **The application swaps the thread: “what haunts you.”** This replaces the specific conflict with an undefined personal struggle—potentially grief, trauma, anxiety, or temptation. “Christ already delivered the blow that ends it” can consequently sound like a promise that this struggle is already over. Christ’s decisive victory does not mean the devil has ceased acting. [1 Peter 5:8, KJV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1+Peter+5%3A8&version=KJV)

6. **Grace and invitation: “You keep trying … hoping for a tie you can live with.”** This invents the listener’s motives and accuses them of bargaining for stalemate. It is unsupported psychological diagnosis. The script names Christ’s accomplishment but never develops a gracious invitation from it.

**HOOK: 5/10.** “You’ve heard Genesis 3:15 means…” assumes Bible familiarity and spends the opening seconds referencing an interpretation. The arresting wound contrast arrives afterward and depends on the overstatement above.

**LANDING: 6/10.** “Not a tie—a bruised heel…” belongs to this piece’s imagery, so it avoids the stock-closer cap. But it repeats the thesis rather than offering a CTA, and its emotional force depends on the misleading survivable-wound contrast.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Remove “survivable bruise”; explicitly ground Christ’s victory in His actual death and resurrection.
2. Restore the opening “And,” distinguish interpretation from wording, and explain the offspring imagery.
3. Replace the assumed personal struggle with a gracious invitation that follows the passage’s specific conflict and victory.
