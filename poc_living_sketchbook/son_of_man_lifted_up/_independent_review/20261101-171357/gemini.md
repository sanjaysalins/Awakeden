- **Doctrinal soundness**: The theology linking the bronze serpent to Christ's crucifixion and exaltation is orthodox and biblically faithful (Johannine theology of *hupsoo* / lifting up).
- **KJV verbatim**: The quote from John 3:14-15 is exact KJV. No issues here.

  1. *"this defeat is what John calls His glory"* — The word "defeat" has zero antecedent. The listener has no idea what defeat is being discussed because the cross hasn't been mentioned yet. 
  2. *"A skeptic hears cruelty. John uses that word again at the cross..."* — Grammatically, a listener will assume "that word" refers to the immediately preceding noun, "cruelty." You meant "lifted up" or "glory", but the audio flow makes it sound like John is using the word "cruelty" at the cross.
- **Grace-anchored conviction**: The script doesn't use fear or works, but the conviction feels like an academic theological argument (*"That word will not let you split them"*) rather than a direct, grace-anchored invitation.

- **SCORE THE LANDING**: 7/10. *"Believe on Christ, lifted up for you, and live."* It earns points for reusing the core verb ("lifted up") and the serpent motif ("and live"), but it still reads very close to a stock "Believe on Him and be saved" closer.
