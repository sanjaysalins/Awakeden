## Independent adversarial review (narration)

### KJV verbatim
The quoted block matches cached KJV **John 3:14–15** exactly (including *Son of man*, colon before *That whosoever*, *believeth*, *perish*, *eternal life*). **No altered words inside the quotation marks.**

### Doctrinal soundness
- **Core typology is orthodox:** bronze serpent lifted → Son of Man lifted; belief → life (John 3:14–15). That is standard evangelical Johannine reading, not a contrarian take.
- **Overclaim / fuzzy attribution:** *“John uses that word again at the cross: shame and glory, one lifting.”* In John, **“glorified / glory”** clusters around the **hour of lifting** (e.g. John 12:23–33; 13:31–32), not as a clear, repeated “glory” refrain **in the crucifixion narrative** (John 19). **“Shame”** is not John’s paired vocabulary at Golgotha the way this line implies; cross-shame is clearer elsewhere (e.g. Heb. 12:2). The piece **reads Johannine theology into a specific “at the cross, that word again” claim** the text does not cleanly support—hostile listener hears **proof-texting by association**.
- **“He was shamed to become your glory.”** The landing idea (cross → benefit for the believer) is fine, but **“your glory”** is **not** the verb/image of the embedded KJV (*eternal life* / *not perish*). It shifts from **life** to **glory-as-possession**, which can sound like **benefit framing** (“you get glory”) rather than **believe and live**. Not heresy; **imprecise and slightly self-oriented** next to the quoted verse.

### Clarity on first hearing
- **Hook antecedent failure:** *“this defeat is what John calls His glory”* — **“this defeat”** has **no referent yet**. Crucifixion/lifting hasn’t been named; Nicodemus’s night conversation isn’t “defeat” in plain language. A zero-Bible listener hears **a paradox with a missing subject**.
- **Pronoun / thread break:** *“A skeptic hears cruelty. John uses **that word** again at the cross…”* The nearest salient noun is **“cruelty,”** not *glory*, *shame*, or *lifting*. On one pass, **“that word”** is **ambiguous or wrong**—classic **logic trick** feel even if a Bible-literate editor can re-parse it.
- **“Jesus means it literally”** — literally **what**? Defeat = glory? Serpent = Son? **Two beats compete** before the KJV quote grounds the listener.
- **“One lifting”** is elegant for insiders; outsiders may not know **lifted up** = crucifixion exaltation until the quote lands—and the hook already spent the paradox budget.

### Grace-anchored conviction
- **Mostly invitation-shaped** at the end (*“Believe on Christ… and live”*), aligned with the quoted *believeth*.
- **“You cannot want Christ’s glory without His cross”** is **cost-of-discipleship truth**, not works-salvation—but it **leans confrontational** (what you *cannot* want) vs. the **look-and-live** grace image in the serpent line. Tone is **challenge-first**, not fear/gain in the crude sense, but **less “Spirit invites”** than the Numbers 21 “look and live” moment allows.

### Freshness = faithful depth
- **Strong fresh entry:** night, Pharisee, serpent healed **with a look**—surprising detail **from the text**, not novelty doctrine.
- **Weaker middle:** shame/glory pairing is **real Johannine depth** but **muddied** by the **“at the cross / that word”** compression and the **unmotivated “skeptic / cruelty”** beat (feels like a **freshness insert** rather than Nicodemus’s actual stumbling block).

### One thread spine (hook → middle → CTA)
- **Spine intended:** lifted up → shame/glory inseparable → believe → live. **Good.**
- **Frays:** (1) **defeat** in the hook vs. **serpent/healing** vs. **cruelty/skeptic** vs. **shame/glory**—four labels for one pole before the listener has one picture. (2) CTA returns to **lifted up / believe / live** (good recovery), but **“your glory”** is a **late swap** from **eternal life** in the only verbatim Scripture.

### Hook score: **6/10**
Not a capped template (*Did you know* etc.), and **night + Pharisee + paradox** has scroll-stop potential. **Capped by** *“this defeat”* with no anchor and **abstract glory** before scene or verse—many strangers bounce confused rather than hooked.

### Landing score: **6/10**
*“Believe on Christ, lifted up for you, and live”* **does** reuse **lifted up** and **believe** from this piece—better than a naked stock closer. Still **portable** to **any** crucifixion-exaltation short; **“and live”** is generic evangelism language. **“Your glory”** in the prior sentence is **not** the KJV landing verb (*eternal life*), so the close **doesn’t fully land in the bones of the quoted promise**.
