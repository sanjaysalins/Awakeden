# Independent review — grok (OK, 154s)

Hook: 5. Landing: 6. Both are under 8, so this is a revise.

The KJV quote is exact. John 3:14–15 matches the text word for word: "And as Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, even so must the Son of man be lifted up: That whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have eternal life."

The problems are the frame around that quote.

**Doctrine.** "This defeat is what John calls His glory" puts a word in John that John does not use. In John, the lifting is the hour He is glorified. He lays His life down. He is not defeated. "Jesus means it literally: what shames Him is what glorifies Him" is not what He says on that night. The quote is about the Son of man lifted up so that whoever believes should not perish but have eternal life. Shame is not in that sentence. John does not say "shame" at the cross. "John uses that word again at the cross: shame and glory, one lifting" glues Hebrews' shame ("despising the shame") onto John's "lifted up," then calls the mix John's word. "A skeptic hears cruelty" adds a charge the passage never makes, and the script never answers it. The real surprise in the text is already enough: one lifting, the serpent and the Son.

**First hearing.** A stranger never learns what "this defeat" is. The cross is not named until "You cannot want Christ's glory without His cross." "Jesus means it literally" has no "it" yet. Jesus has not spoken. "That word" and "them" in "That word will not let you split them" have no clear noun. The word you mean is "lifted up." You never say so. "A skeptic hears cruelty" arrives right after a promise of life, with no reason. Night, the Pharisee, and Nicodemus are not enough for someone who does not know the story. "Centuries earlier" also undersells the gap. It is well over a thousand years.

**Grace.** "You cannot want Christ's glory without His cross" makes the cross the price of a glory the hearer wants. "He was shamed to become your glory" moves the payoff onto the listener. The passage's invitation is different. He is lifted up, and the one who believes lives. That is gift. It is not a trade for "your glory."

**Spine.** The hook is defeat = His glory. The middle swaps in shame, then cruelty, then a nameless word. The close swaps again, from His glory to "your glory," then to "and live." "By night" never returns. The serpent and "lifted up" could have held one line the whole way. They get crowded out.

**Hook: 5.** "By night, Jesus tells a Pharisee something strange: this defeat..." is a scene plus a paradox, so it is not the canned "Did you know" open. A stranger still has no picture of the defeat. "Something strange" tells us to be impressed. The paradox cannot land until the cross exists in the ear. It does not, not in the first five seconds.

**Landing: 6.** "Believe on Christ, lifted up for you, and live" does use this piece's verbs, lifted up and live. It is not the stock "Come to Jesus." It still does not carry this film's claim. The bones were meant to be one lifting you cannot split. That claim dies in the line before, on "your glory." "Believe on Christ... and live" could close another John 3 short with almost no change. It invites. It does not land.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Drop "defeat," "cruelty," and John-saying-"shame." Say only what John says: the Son of man must be lifted up, and that lifting is His glory.
2. Name the cross and the word "lifted up" before any paradox, so "this defeat," "that word," and "them" are gone.
3. End on His lifting, so the dying live by believing. Cut "become your glory" and the want-glory trade. Make that last sentence impossible to reuse on another film.
