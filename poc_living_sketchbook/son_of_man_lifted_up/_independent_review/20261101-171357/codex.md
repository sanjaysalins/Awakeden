# Independent review — codex (OK, 50s)

**HOOK: 4/10.** “By night, Jesus tells a Pharisee something strange” spends the opening seconds on scene-setting and an unexplained religious title. “This defeat” then names an event the listener has not heard about.

**LANDING: 7/10.** “Believe on Christ, lifted up for you, and live” uses this piece’s lifting-and-life imagery, so it avoids the stock-closer cap. But the middle develops shame and glory rather than looking and living; the ending returns to a thread the argument has partly abandoned.

1. **The opening misattributes the argument.** “Jesus tells a Pharisee … this defeat is what John calls His glory” makes the narrator’s synthesis sound like Jesus’ actual statement to Nicodemus. John 3:14–15 supplies the serpent comparison and promise of life; the explicit connection with glorification comes later. The connection is defensible, but this presentation blurs separate passages. [John 3:14–15](https://biblia.com/bible/kjv/john/3/14-15), [John 12:23–33](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John%2012%3A23-33&version=KJV)

2. **The wordplay carries more weight than the explanation.** “John uses that word again at the cross” never identifies *lifted up* clearly. If it means John 12:32–33, that passage anticipates the crucifixion; it is not spoken at the cross. “That word will not let you split them” then treats a verbal connection as a complete theological argument. Explain what Christ accomplishes through His death instead of making the word itself prove the claim. [John 12:32–33](https://biblia.com/bible/kjv/john/12/32-33)

3. **“What shames Him is what glorifies Him” needs a crucial distinction.** Human humiliation and divine glory meet in the cross, but shame itself is not what makes Jesus glorious. Without explaining His saving love and obedience, the sentence sounds like a clever reversal rather than faithful depth. “This defeat” likewise needs qualification as an *apparent* defeat.

4. **“A bronze serpent healed dying Israel with a look” obscures God’s agency.** A newcomer could hear a magical object healing a nation. God provided the remedy; bitten individuals looked and lived. “Bronze” is outside quotation marks, so it is **not** a KJV-verbatim violation. [Numbers 21:8–9](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Numbers%2021%3A8-9&version=KJV)

5. **The conviction becomes accusatory before grace is explained.** “You cannot want Christ’s glory without His cross” assumes the listener wants glory while rejecting the cross. It is not explicitly works-based, but it introduces a rebuke unsupported by the listener’s situation. “He was shamed to become your glory” then leaves *your glory* undefined and risks making personal exaltation the attraction.

6. **“A skeptic hears cruelty” opens an objection and abandons it.** Cruelty in the snake episode, crucifixion, or God’s saving purpose? The script neither specifies nor answers it. This interrupts the central thread and introduces suspicion without providing understanding.

**KJV check:** The entire quotation matches John 3:14–15 word for word. No altered words. [KJV text](https://biblia.com/bible/kjv/john/3/14-15)

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Distinguish Jesus’ words to Nicodemus from the later glorification passage; identify the repeated phrase accurately.
2. Explain God’s saving action so shame-and-glory wordplay does not substitute for the gospel.
3. Build one continuous thread into the lifting-and-life invitation; remove the unanswered skeptic objection and assumed accusation.
