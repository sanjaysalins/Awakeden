# Independent review — gemini (OK, 67s)

**Doctrinal Soundness & Grace-Anchored Conviction**
The doctrine is sound and deeply evangelical. "Not spared for being good. Spared for believing" is an excellent, succinct distillation of justification by faith. It completely avoids works-based or fear-based framing. The typological connection of Rahab’s scarlet cord to the blood of Christ is orthodox, historically grounded, and faithful.

**KJV Verbatim**
All quotes check out perfectly against the KJV:
- “Bind this line of scarlet thread in the window” (Joshua 2:18)
- “The people shouted with a great shout... the wall fell down flat” (Joshua 6:20)
- “By faith the harlot Rahab perished not” (Hebrews 11:31)
- “Salmon begat Booz of Rachab” (Matthew 1:5)

**Clarity & One Thread Spine**
The spine is brilliantly executed. The "scarlet cord" is introduced in the first movement, anchored by the KJV quote in the middle, and carries the entire metaphorical weight of the landing ("scarlet line running from her window to a cross"). It never swaps threads. 

**SCORE THE HOOK: 6/10**
"Jericho had the strongest walls in the world. And God told His army to march around them... in silence." 
This is a slow, history-channel documentary open. While it avoids the banned template phrasing, it lacks a visceral, scroll-stopping punch. A scroller might swipe past the generic "strongest walls" history lesson before reaching the actual tension of the silent march. 

**SCORE THE LANDING: 10/10**
"There is a scarlet line running from her window to a cross. His blood. Bind everything to it." 
Flawless. It repurposes the exact KJV verb ("Bind") and the specific visual of the piece ("scarlet line") into a powerful, grace-anchored CTA. This is the exact opposite of a stock closer; it could not be pasted onto any other script.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Rewrite the hook to be immediate and arresting. Drop the generic "Jericho had the strongest walls..." history lesson and start directly with the bizarre tension of the siege (e.g., "To break the strongest fortress in the world, God's army didn't fire a single arrow. They just walked in silence.").
2. Smooth the transition into the genealogy. "Now read Jesus' family tree:" feels a bit instructional and breaks the narrative immersion. Try something more seamless like, "Centuries later, look at the lineage of Jesus:" or "Open the New Testament:".
3. Maintain the exact KJV quotes while implementing the above fixes, ensuring the "scarlet thread" spine remains untouched.
