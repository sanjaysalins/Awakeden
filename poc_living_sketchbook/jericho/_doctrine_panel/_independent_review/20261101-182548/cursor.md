## Independent adversarial review — Jericho / Rahab narration

### Doctrinal soundness
- **Mostly orthodox:** faith-not-merit (“Not spared for being good. Spared for believing.”), Rahab in the lineage (Matthew 1), scarlet→blood typology is standard evangelical echo, not a contrarian reading.
- **“Jericho had the strongest walls in the world.”** — Not Scripture; superlative history claim. A hostile listener can call it pulpit hype and it weakens “faithful depth” (surprise should come from the text, not invented scale).
- **“One window stood.”** — Joshua 6 spares **Rahab’s house** and those inside (Joshua 2:18–19; 6:22–25), not “one window” as the physical survivor. Poetic, but it **distorts the event** for anyone who later reads Joshua and invites “so the rest of the wall vanished but one window remained?” confusion.
- **Hebrews quote trimmed:** “By faith the harlot Rahab perished not.” drops **“with them that believed not”** — the verse’s point is contrast with unbelieving Jericho. Truncation isn’t heresy, but it **softens the text’s own edge** (faith vs the city that didn’t believe).

### KJV verbatim (quotation marks)
Flag every deviation from contiguous KJV wording:

| Artifact quote | Issue |
|----------------|--------|
| **“Bind this line of scarlet thread in the window.”** | KJV (Joshua 2:18): *“thou shalt bind this line of scarlet thread in the window which thou didst let us down by”* — drops **“thou shalt”**, drops the trailing clause; leading **“Bind”** is not a KJV substring as given (capitalization aside, the spoken words aren’t verbatim). |
| **“The people shouted with a great shout... the wall fell down flat.”** | KJV (Joshua 6:20): *“and the people shouted with a great shout, that the wall fell down flat”* — opening **“The people”** vs **“and the people”** is an altered word at the quote boundary. **“the wall fell down flat”** is exact. |
| **“By faith the harlot Rahab perished not.”** | The substring matches KJV (Hebrews 11:31) through *“perished not”*; remainder of verse omitted (allowed only if your gate treats elision as still “exact” for the words you retain — the retained words are exact). |
| **“Salmon begat Booz of Rachab.”** | KJV (Matthew 1:5): *“And Salmon begat Booz of Rachab”* — missing leading **“And”**. |

For a **KJV-verbatim** narration gate, the Joshua 2, Joshua 6, and Matthew quotes are **FAIL-level** as written.

### Clarity on first hearing
- **Strong:** march, silence, trumpets, shout, walls fall, Rahab/spies, scarlet cord — followable without prior Bible literacy.
- **Weak:** **“Now read Jesus' family tree”** — no listener is “reading”; it’s audio. Sounds like stage direction, not spoken word.
- **Weak:** **“Salmon begat Booz of Rachab.”** — **Rachab** with no bridge to **Rahab**; zero-Bible listeners may think two women or a typo.
- **Compressed jump:** walls fall → Hebrews → genealogy → cross in a few breaths; **“The woman behind the scarlet cord became His family”** saves it, but the genealogy line still lands as jargon unless Rachab=Rahab is spelled out once in plain speech.

### Grace-anchored conviction
- **Good:** “Not spared for being good. Spared for believing.” — invitation by example, not merit ledger or terror framing.
- **Watch:** **“Bind everything to it.”** — echoes the spies’ **“Bind this line”** (good thread echo), but **“everything”** is vague (life? sin? works?). Borderline imperative; still closer to **piece-native** than “repent or perish.” Not a major gain/loss violation.

### Freshness = faithful depth
- **Fresh enough:** thirteen laps (6+7), silence, scarlet cord → lineage → cross is a **text-anchored** scarlet thread, not a gimmick hook.
- **Less fresh:** “scarlet line … cross … His blood” is **well-worn** sermon glue; not wrong, but not deeply surprising to anyone who’s heard one Rahab message.

### One thread spine (hook → middle → CTA)
- **Single spine:** scarlet cord in Jericho → Rahab’s faith → same scarlet in the line to Christ → **“Bind everything to it.”** No major thread swap.
- Minor wobble: **“Every stone came down. One window stood.”** momentarily shifts from **faith/sparing** to **visual stunt**, then back to Hebrews.

### Hook score: **7 / 10**
- Opens with concrete scene (**walls**, **march**, **silence**) — not a “Did you know / Imagine” template cap.
- Not a sharp scroll-stop: no paradox or punch in the first ~5 seconds beyond “strongest walls … silence,” which is solid but familiar Jericho territory.

### Landing score: **8 / 10**
- **“There is a scarlet line running from her window to a cross. His blood. Bind everything to it.”** — CTA grows from **this** piece’s cord/blood image; not a generic “come to Jesus” cap-5 closer.
- Slightly short of 9–10: **“Bind everything to it”** could fit other blood/covenant pieces with a word swap; **“His blood”** as a two-word fragment is thin on the ear.
