# Independent review — codex (OK, 68s)

**Hook: 6/10. Landing: 4/10.** The narration builds its strongest images from unsupported details, then makes an interpretive scarlet-cord connection carry the gospel invitation.

1. **“Jericho had the strongest walls in the world.”** Unsupported superlative. Joshua establishes a fortified, closed city; it never ranks its walls worldwide. The hook gets its scale from an invented claim. The marching command creates curiosity, but the opening spends its first seconds on exaggerated exposition. [Joshua 6](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Joshua+6&version=KJV)

2. **“In silence” / “Just feet on sand” / “On the seventh day the trumpets tore the sky.”** This sequence implies six quiet days followed by trumpets on day seven. The priests blew trumpets during the earlier circuits too; the restriction concerned the people’s voices. “Just feet” actively misrepresents the soundscape. Also, placing **“Thirteen laps in all”** immediately after **“Six days they circled”** momentarily assigns thirteen laps to those six days. Thirteen is correct across all seven days. [Joshua 6:3–15](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Joshua+6&version=KJV)

3. **“Every stone came down. One window stood.”** Neither detail is stated. Scripture records the wall falling and Rahab’s household being brought out; it does not describe one surviving window. “Every stone” also clashes with the next sentence’s surviving structure. This is the narration’s central visual revelation, so the invention materially affects its claimed depth. [Joshua 6:20–25](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Joshua+6&version=KJV)

4. **The quotation audit finds two problems:**
   - **“Bind this line of scarlet thread in the window.”** KJV has **“thou shalt bind this line…”** The narration removes the subject and auxiliary and presents the remainder as a standalone imperative. Under your strict verbatim requirement, restore the wording or clearly mark the excerpt. [Joshua 2:18](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Joshua+2%3A18&version=KJV)
   - **“The people shouted with a great shout... the wall fell down flat.”** The ellipsis drops **“that”** between these clauses. The remaining words match, but this is an edited quotation, not an uninterrupted KJV sentence. **“By faith the harlot Rahab perished not”** and **“Salmon begat Booz of Rachab”** are word-exact excerpts. [Joshua 6:20](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Joshua+6&version=KJV), [Hebrews 11:31](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Hebrews+11%3A31&version=KJV), [Matthew 1:5](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Matthew+1%3A5&version=KJV)

5. **“There is a scarlet line running from her window to a cross. His blood.”** Christ’s saving blood is orthodox; identifying this cord as its divinely intended symbol is an interpretive step that these passages do not establish. The narration announces the connection as fact. Its freshness depends on a familiar devotional association presented with more certainty than the text supplies.

6. **“A woman with a past” / “Spared for believing” / “became His family.”** A newcomer must decode a euphemism, supply whom she believed, and understand that she became Jesus’ ancestor. “Rachab” is never identified as the same Rahab. The faith statement is defensible, but its object needs clarity; otherwise believing sounds like a saving quality in itself.

7. **“Bind everything to it.”** This recalls the cord’s verb, so it avoids a completely stock closer. But what does the listener actually do—bind possessions, problems, conduct, or trust to blood? The imperative demands interpretation instead of delivering an intelligible invitation. There is no explicit fear bargain or works doctrine, but grace remains underexplained while the listener receives a sweeping task. The thread also shifts from military impossibility to rescue, ancestry, and blood symbolism without adequately connecting those movements.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Remove the invented wall ranking and surviving-window claim; correct the misleading silence.
2. Restore strict KJV quotation wording and distinguish textual claims from scarlet-cord interpretation.
3. Clarify whom Rahab trusted and make the closing invitation understandable without decoding the metaphor.
