# Independent review — grok (OK, 311s)

I'll check the quoted lines against the KJV text before judging the narration.**Hook: 6. Landing: 5.** Both are under 8, so this is a revise.

The first five seconds are “Jericho had the strongest walls in the world. And God told His army to march…” The silence twist arrives after the pause, and the opening claim is not in the text. The last sentence, “Bind everything to it,” drops the window, the cord, and Rahab. That same sentence could end any other piece about the blood.

**KJV.** The words inside the quotes are real KJV words, in order. Joshua 2:18 has “bind this line of scarlet thread in the window.” Hebrews 11:31 has “By faith the harlot Rahab perished not.” Matthew 1:5 has “Salmon begat Booz of Rachab.” No word is swapped. The shout quote is edited: Joshua 6:20 reads “the people shouted with a great shout, that the wall fell down flat.” The ellipsis deletes “that.”

**The window is an invented miracle.** “Every stone came down. One window stood. Scripture says it plainly” puts a picture the Bible never draws under a claim of plain Scripture. The verse that follows is Hebrews 11:31, which says she perished not. It says nothing about a window. Joshua 6:20 says the wall fell down flat. Joshua 2:15 says her house was upon the town wall. “Every stone came down” and “One window stood” contradict each other in the same breath.

**The cord’s job is skipped, then replaced.** A first-time listener never hears why the thread is in the window. Joshua 2:18 makes it the sign by which her household is spared. “Their answer” has no question in the script. “A woman with a past” never says what the past is, or what she believed. The piece then states as fact, “There is a scarlet line running from her window to a cross. His blood.” Hebrews, James, and Matthew never connect that thread to the cross. That is a sermon picture stated as doctrine.

**The faith line overreaches the trimmed verse.** “Not spared for being good. Spared for believing” is the evangelical point, and it is not a fear pitch. The quote is cut before “with them that believed not, when she had received the spies with peace.” Joshua 6:17 gives the stated reason: she hid the messengers. “Spared for believing” makes faith sound like the coin she paid. “Scripture says it plainly” does not cover that gloss.

**Names break on first hearing.** The story says Rahab. The quote says Rachab and Booz. Nothing says these are the same woman. “Begat” will not land for a new listener. “The woman behind the scarlet cord became His family” is the clear line, and it arrives one sentence too late.

**The spine swaps.** The hook is walls, silence, “Six days they circled. Thirteen laps in all,” “Not one arrow.” The thirteen laps match Joshua 6:3–4 (six days once, the seventh day seven times). That count is fine. The close is “Bind everything to it.” The laps, the arrows, and “feet on sand” never pay off. “On the seventh day the trumpets tore the sky” also mis-times the text: Joshua 6:8–13 has the priests blowing on the earlier days. The long blast is the seventh day. Silence is Joshua’s order to the people in Joshua 6:10, while God speaks the plan to Joshua in Joshua 6:2–5.

**Grace.** The listener is not threatened or offered a prize. The close still puts the work on them: “Bind everything to it.” It never says what to bind, or that the blood is already given.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Cut “Every stone came down. One window stood.” The wall fell flat, her house was on that wall, and Hebrews 11:31 does not say a window survived. Move “Scripture says it plainly” so it cannot vouch for that picture.
2. Say what the scarlet thread is for (the sign that her house is spared) before any cross line. Do not state “a scarlet line running from her window to a cross” as a Bible fact. In the same breath, say Rachab is Rahab, and let “by faith… perished not” stand without “Spared for believing.”
3. Keep one spine from the first line to the last. Open on the cord and the woman who should have died with the city, or end on the wall falling at God’s word. Replace “Bind everything to it” with a last line that still has this window, this cord, and this family in it.
4. Say what she believed, and whose answer the spies give. “A woman with a past” and “Their answer” do not carry a first-time listener.
