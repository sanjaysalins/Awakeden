# Independent review — grok (OK, 167s)

Hook: 8. Landing: 5.

The opening can stop a thumb. "Once a year I walked behind a curtain to meet God and I was never sure I would walk back out." is concrete and has a stake. It is not a "Did you know / Imagine / What if" template. The close cannot stand. "Come to Jesus the way in is torn wide open. Walk in." uses the stock invite the rubric names, then ends on two generic words. "Walk in" could close any other piece. Landing stays at 5, so the piece is a revise.

**The middle answer contradicts itself.** "For years I asked why it took two. I know now. No single creature could hold both halves." Then: "a Man named Jesus did what my two goats only pictured." A man is one creature, and he is said to hold both halves. Leviticus never gives that reason. The two goats are one sin offering (Leviticus 16:5) with two sides: blood brought in, and iniquity borne away (Leviticus 16:15, 21-22). The line invents a rule the next sentence breaks. "I know now" lands on the invented rule, and it is spoken before "Long after me," so the priest claims the answer before the event that would teach it.

**"Never sure I would walk back out" overstates the rite.** Leviticus 16:2 and 16:13 warn that a wrong entry means death. They do not make a faithful priest unsure he will survive the year. That yearly coin-flip is the later rope legend, which is not in the text. The fear is also left hanging. The hook asks whether he walks out. The piece never answers it. The questions swap: his survival, then "why two," then the listener's "Walk in."

**A first-time hearer loses the thread in the wording.**
- Nobody names the speaker. "I" is never "high priest" or Aaron. With no Bible background, the "I" could be anyone.
- "The law gave me two goats for one sin" fights "confessed every sin of the people." The rite is for all their iniquities (Leviticus 16:21), and the bull for the priest's own sin goes inside the veil first (Leviticus 16:11-14). "I killed the first and carried its blood inside" skips that blood.
- "The second I did not kill I laid my hands" has no pause. On one hearing it clumps into nonsense.
- "drove it into the desert to be lost" misses the text. A fit man leads the goat (Leviticus 16:21). It is let go to bear iniquity into a land not inhabited (Leviticus 16:22). "Lost" sounds like a stray animal, which is a different idea.
- "did what my two goats only pictured He was the price paid" runs two sentences together.
- "He carried it away Himself once and sat down. Finished." stacks three pictures with no map. "It is finished" is said at death (John 19:30). He sits down after, at the right hand of God (Hebrews 10:12). "Finished" also shortens that KJV line. A newcomer can hear "sat down in the desert, done."
- "I was only the sign. He is the substance." "Substance" is insider vocabulary. Heard once, it does not mean "the real thing the picture pointed to."
- "Come to Jesus the way in is torn wide open." On one pass it sounds like "Come to Jesus the way," then "in is torn."

**KJV check.** No words sit inside quotation marks. The clause "the LORD hath laid on him the iniquity of us all" matches Isaiah 53:6 and should stay word for word. It is not marked as a quote, so it sounds like the priest still talking. "Finished" is the only shortened near-quote.

**Grace.** The veil torn and the way open is a real invite, and that part is sound. The piece still opens on fear ("never sure," later "The curtain I trembled behind") and closes by scolding: "Don't come to me, or a goat, or an altar." A hearer today was not on their way to a goat. The negative list arrives before the welcome and softens the landing.

The Christ claim itself is the ordinary one: the slain goat and the goat that bears sin away meet in Jesus, and his death opens the veil. That claim can stay. The reason given for two goats, the unnamed speaker, the smashed sentences, and the stock last line cannot.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Replace "No single creature could hold both halves" with the textual claim: one sin offering, two sides (blood in, guilt borne away), both met in Jesus — so the "why two" answer stops contradicting the one Man.
2. Name the high priest in the first line, drop the "never sure I would walk back out" coin-flip, and fix the hear-once snarls ("one sin" / "every sin," "to be lost," the missing pause after "did not kill," "sat down. Finished." in cross-then-session order).
3. Rebuild the last sentence from this piece's own picture (hands on the goat, blood inside, curtain torn) so it does not end on "Come to Jesus" plus a generic "Walk in."
