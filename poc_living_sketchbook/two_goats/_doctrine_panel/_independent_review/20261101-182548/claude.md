# Independent review — claude (OK, 56s)

**Review of "two_goats" (narration)**

**KJV verbatim.** The only quoted text is "the LORD hath laid on him the iniquity of us all" (Isaiah 53:6). It is exact. The artifact has no quotation marks around it, though, and it is joined awkwardly to the sentence before it. "…the One the guilt was laid on: and the LORD hath laid…" repeats the idea in two voices, which makes the line stumble. Fix the framing and the punctuation.

**Doctrine.** The core typology is orthodox:
- Two goats in Leviticus 16.
- A sin offering and a scapegoat that carries the sin away.
- Isaiah 53, Hebrews 9–10, and the veil torn in Matthew 27:51.

I found three things to tighten:
- **"No single creature could hold both halves."** This is stated as fact, and the very next line has Jesus doing exactly that. It works as a twist, but it is an unsupported claim. It also reads as a contradiction on first hearing, because Jesus is then described as a "Man" and a "creature."
- **"a Man named Jesus."** This undersells who He is. It never says He is God, and the doctrine panel may flag that. One clause about God Himself coming would fix it.
- **"I know now."** A dead priest speaks across centuries. This is a device, and it is acceptable. But the speaker is never identified, so a newcomer has no anchor for "the law," "the veil," or "the people."

**Clarity on first hearing.**
- "Curtain," "veil," and "curtain" again switch terms. A newcomer may think these are two different things.
- Missing punctuation makes the audio hard to parse:
  - "I did not kill I laid"
  - "only pictured He was the price paid"
  - "Come to Jesus the way in is torn wide open"
- "I was only the sign. He is the substance." is abstract. It is also close to the vocabulary-over-structure trap in the global-audience rule.
- "pictured" and "iniquity" are the hard words. The Isaiah line must stay, so paraphrase around it.

**Grace-anchored conviction.** There is no fear or gain-loss framing. "Don't come to me, or a goat, or an altar" is a bit commanding, and it points at what people might do rather than what is already done. "Walk in" is an invitation, so this is acceptable.

**One thread.** The spine is the curtain and the two goats, tied together at "torn wide open." That is solid. "He carried it away Himself once and sat down. Finished." adds the Hebrews 10:12 and John 19:30 echoes. They are unmarked, and they crowd the line.

**HOOK: 7/10.** "Once a year I walked behind a curtain to meet God and I was never sure I would walk back out" is a fresh first-person stake and not a template open. It is slightly slow, and "two goats" does not appear until the second sentence.

**LANDING: 7/10.** "Walk in" and "torn wide open" are built from this piece's own curtain image, which is good. The sentence "Come to Jesus" is the stock closer that could end any piece. It carries the CTA, and "Don't come to me, or a goat, or an altar" is a weak setup for it. The landing is capped below 8.

Both scores are under 8, so the verdict is REVISE.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Rebuild the landing around the piece's own image and drop the stock "Come to Jesus." For example, have the curtain-keeper step aside: "The curtain I trembled behind is torn. Nobody has to wait outside. Walk in." Cut or replace "Don't come to me, or a goat, or an altar."
2. Fix the punctuation and framing so the audio parses. Add stops after "kill," "pictured," and "Jesus." Put the Isaiah 53:6 quote cleanly in its own sentence and drop the duplicated "the One the guilt was laid on:" lead-in. Use "veil" or "curtain" consistently, and say once who the speaker is (a high priest).
3. Resolve the "no single creature" contradiction and the "a Man named Jesus" wording. For example: "No goat could do both. God's own Son did." Also replace "pictured" and "substance" with plain words such as "a shadow of" and "the real thing."
