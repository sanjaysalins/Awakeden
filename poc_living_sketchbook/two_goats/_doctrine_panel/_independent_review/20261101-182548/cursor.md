## Independent adversarial review — `two_goats` narration

### Doctrinal soundness
- **Mostly sound typology** (dual goats → sacrifice + removal; Christ as fulfillment; veil torn at death; sign vs substance). That matches mainstream evangelical reading of Leviticus 16 + Isaiah 53 + Hebrews.
- **Slip: “two goats for one sin.”** Leviticus 16 is **annual, corporate** atonement for **the people’s sins**, not “one sin.” A careful listener (or any Jew/Christian who knows Yom Kippur) will hear that as **oversimplified or wrong**, not poetic compression.

  > *“The law gave me two goats for one sin.”*

- **Persona risk (minor):** First-person high priest says *“Come to Jesus”* as if he knows the name and the cross historically. It works as **typological drama**, but a literal-minded hearer may feel a **time-warp** (Aaron/Leviticus voice giving a modern evangelistic line). Not heresy—**clarity/framing** issue unless the audio/visual frame makes “sign speaking from hindsight” obvious.

### KJV verbatim
- The only clear KJV clause is embedded without quote marks:

  > *“and the LORD hath laid on him the iniquity of us all.”*

  That matches **Isaiah 53:6** (KJV) for that substring. **No altered words spotted** in that phrase.
- **No other material is marked as KJV in quotes**; the Leviticus material is **paraphrase**, not presented as quoted Scripture—fine, but see clarity below.

### Clarity on first hearing
- **Strong:** *“One to pay. One to carry it away.”* and *“No single creature could hold both halves.”* — clear logic for a stranger.
- **Weak:** *“for one sin”* (see above).
- **Run-on / TTS hazard:** missing pause before the pivot to Christ reads like a grammar glitch:

  > *“…what my two goats only pictured He was the price paid…”*

  Should be heard as *“…only pictured. He was…”* — as written, it can sound like **one broken sentence**.
- **Isaiah line without a attribution frame:** a zero-Bible listener may not know that line is **Scripture** vs the priest’s voice—doctrine is still intelligible, but the **authority beat** is muddier.

### Grace-anchored conviction
- **No works/gain-loss gospel.** Conviction comes from the **weight of the old rite** (*“never sure I would walk back out”*) and from **what Christ finished**, not “do better or else.”
- Opening dread is **descriptive of the old covenant**, not threatening the listener with damnation—acceptable, but keep it from sounding like the **CTA is escape-from-fear** rather than **invitation through an opened way** (the torn-curtain image helps).

### Freshness = faithful depth
- **Fresh entry:** annual veil, two goats, “why two,” “both halves”—**text-grounded**, not clickbait.
- **Less fresh:** curtain torn + “Finished” + priest as shadow—** familiar sermon moves** (still orthodox).

### One thread (hook → middle → CTA)
- **Single spine holds:** terror/veil → two goats → “both halves” → Jesus → torn curtain → reject goat/altar → enter.
- **No thread swap.**

### Additional adversarial nits
- *“drove it into the desert to be lost”* — “lost” is interpretive (wilderness/Azazel tradition); not wildly off, but **“sent away”** is closer to the text’s neutral tone.
- *“He carried it away Himself once and sat down. Finished.”* — packs **Hebrews “sat down”** + **“It is finished”** without quoting; orthodox, but **dense** for one breath—easy to blur on first listen.
