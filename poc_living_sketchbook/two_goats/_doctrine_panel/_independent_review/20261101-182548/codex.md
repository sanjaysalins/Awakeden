# Independent review — codex (OK, 68s)

**Hook: 7/10.** “Once a year I walked behind a curtain to meet God” creates curiosity, but the danger arrives late in a 24-word sentence. “I was never sure I would walk back out” supplies suspense through an invented inner experience. The narrator’s identity is also withheld from listeners who need it.

**Landing: 5/10.** “Walk in” echoes the opening movement behind the curtain, but that exact final sentence could close numerous invitation pieces. Your stock-closer cap applies. The preceding “Come to Jesus” is also generic, and neither phrase resolves the distinctive two-goat image.

1. **The central explanation asserts a reason Scripture does not give.**  
   “For years I asked why it took two. I know now. No single creature could hold both halves.” Leviticus prescribes two goats; it does not explain their number through an animal’s inability to represent both aspects. The script turns a typological observation into a confident divine rationale. This is precisely where freshness becomes invented doctrine. Presenting Christ as fulfilling the sacrificial and sin-bearing imagery does not require this claim. See [Leviticus 16:5–10](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Leviticus+16&version=KJV).

2. **“Two goats for one sin” misstates what the rite addresses.**  
   One sin **offering** is not one individual sin. The ceremony concerns the people’s many sins, which the narration itself subsequently acknowledges in “every sin of the people.” A novice hears a contradiction rather than a meaningful distinction. [Leviticus 16:5, 21–22](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Leviticus+16&version=KJV).

3. **The first-person conceit manufactures testimony and blurs historical identity.**  
   “I was never sure,” “For years I asked,” and “I know now” assign unrecorded emotions and theological discoveries to an unidentified priest. Leviticus gives genuine warnings about death and prescribed safeguards; it does not report this recurring uncertainty. “Long after me” then suggests an ancient individual, while “The curtain I trembled behind tore in two” implies his own curtain survived until Jesus’ death. If Aaron is intended, his sanctuary was the tabernacle; Matthew describes the temple veil. The composite speaker needs clarification. [Leviticus 16:2, 13](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Leviticus+16&version=KJV); [Matthew 27:51](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Matthew+27%3A51&version=KJV).

4. **The scapegoat action is inaccurately personalized.**  
   “I … drove it into the desert to be lost” assigns the escort’s action to the priest. The prescribed procedure sends it through an appointed man and releases it in uninhabited land. “To be lost” adds a purpose the text does not state. [Leviticus 16:21–22](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Leviticus+16&version=KJV).

5. **The spine changes emphasis before its central image pays off.**  
   “One to pay. One to carry it away” establishes sacrifice and removal. “The curtain … tore in two” switches the controlling image to access. Those themes belong together biblically, but the narration leaves their connection implicit. “I was only the sign. He is the substance” adds abstract vocabulary without explaining it to an unfamiliar listener.

6. **The invitation needs a clearer object and response.**  
   “The way in is torn wide open. Walk in.” Into what—God’s presence, a building, salvation? The completed-work emphasis avoids works-based earning, but the opening still uses imagined mortal fear as its attention mechanism. The ending should make approaching God through faith intelligible; Hebrews explicitly connects access with Jesus’ blood and drawing near in faith. [Hebrews 10:19–22](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Hebrews+10%3A1-22&version=KJV).

**KJV check:** There are no quotation marks in the supplied narration. The apparent Scripture quotation, “and the LORD hath laid on him the iniquity of us all,” is word-for-word KJV; no altered word found. “Finished” is unmarked paraphrase, not the full biblical utterance. [Isaiah 53:6](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Isaiah+53%3A6&version=KJV).

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Remove the invented explanation “No single creature could hold both halves” and correct “one sin.”
2. Clarify the priestly narrator and correct the invented testimony, curtain continuity, and scapegoat procedure.
3. Carry the two-goat image through to a specific, understandable invitation grounded in Christ’s completed work.
