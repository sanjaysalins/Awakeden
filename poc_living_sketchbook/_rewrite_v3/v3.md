## jericho
One red cord kept a woman alive when a city fell. Her name was Rahab, and people knew her for her sin. But she hid two of God's spies, because she believed in Him. They promised her: "thou shalt bind this line of scarlet thread in the window". That cord was the sign of the promise. Then God's army marched around her city, and on the seventh day "the people shouted with a great shout, that the wall fell down flat". The city fell. But Rahab and her family were saved, because she trusted what they said. Hebrews gives the reason: "By faith the harlot Rahab perished not with them that believed not". It was her faith, not her past. Matthew even names her in the family line of Jesus: "And Salmon begat Booz of Rachab". Christians see a picture in that cord: the blood of Jesus, which covers all who trust Him. Rahab could not hold up a wall. She only hung a cord in her window, and trusted the promise. Hang your hope on Jesus the same way.

## two_goats
One goat died. One goat was sent away alive. Why two? I am Aaron, the first high priest, and once a year I handled both. The law gave me two goats, and together they were one sin offering for the people. The first I killed, and I carried its blood inside the veil, the curtain that shut people out of God's presence. The second I did not kill. I laid both hands on its head, and confessed over it all the sins of the people. Then it was led into the wilderness, to a land where no one lived. Two goats pictured one rescue. One showed sin paid for by death. One showed sin carried far away. Long after me, Jesus did what my goats only pictured. Isaiah wrote of Him: "the LORD hath laid on him the iniquity of us all". He died for our sins, and He bore them away. When He died, "the veil of the temple was rent in twain from the top to the bottom". The curtain is open. For all who trust Him, sin has gone where that goat went, to a land where no one lives. Walk in.

## bronze_serpent
God answered snakebite with a snake on a pole. I am Moses, and I made it. My people were dying of snakebites, because they had spoken against God. They came to me: "We have sinned, for we have spoken against the LORD, and against thee". I prayed. Instead of taking the snakes away, God told me to make a serpent of brass, and set it on a pole. Whoever was bitten looked at it, and lived. Long after me, Jesus told a man named Nicodemus: "And as Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, even so must the Son of man be lifted up: That whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have eternal life." My brass serpent was only a picture. On the cross, Jesus took on Himself the sin that bites us all, and Paul says He was "made a curse for us". Lifted up where all can see, He is the cure. Lift your eyes to Him, and live.
