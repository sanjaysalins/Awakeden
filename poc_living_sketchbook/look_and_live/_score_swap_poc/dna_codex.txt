1. **TEMPO** — Approximately **137.0-137.1 BPM**, 4/4. Some analyses round it to 136 or 138 BPM, but the famous Dream/radio version sits at about **137 BPM**. ([xavierblogsblog.wordpress.com](https://xavierblogsblog.wordpress.com/2016/08/22/case-study-robert-miles-children/)) ([hooktheory.com](https://www.hooktheory.com/theorytab/advanced-search?artist=&beatCountSelect=any&chordString=i+VI&genreSelect=any&ignoreModifiers=&keySelect=any&page=3&scaleSelect=any&song=&startOfSection=&utm_source=openai))

2. **KEY / MODE** — **F minor**, effectively **F Aeolian/natural minor**. No modulation occurs in the opening build; the harmony loops in the same tonal field. Automated DJ-key sites sometimes misread it as C/C major, but the musical intro is F minor. ([hooktheory.com](https://www.hooktheory.com/theorytab/view/robert-miles/children?utm_source=openai)) ([fabermusic.com](https://www.fabermusic.com/shop/children-d32228?utm_source=openai))

3. **CHORD PROGRESSION** — The intro loop is essentially **D♭ → B♭m/B♭sus → Fm**, functionally **VI → iv → i** in F minor. In bar-feel terms: **D♭sus2/add9 | B♭m7sus/B♭sus | Fm(add9)/Fm7 | Fm**, then repeat. The Fm tonic usually occupies the longer landing space. ([hooktheory.com](https://www.hooktheory.com/theorytab/advanced-search?artist=&beatCountSelect=any&chordString=i+VI&genreSelect=any&ignoreModifiers=&keySelect=any&page=3&scaleSelect=any&song=&startOfSection=)) ([guitarx.com](https://guitarx.com/chord/children/))

4. **ARPEGGIO PATTERN** — It is not a continuous EDM 16th-note arpeggiator. It is a **composed, high-register broken-chord piano ostinato** on a 16th-note grid, mostly heard as repeated quarter/eighth-note attacks with short 16th pickups. The note vocabulary emphasizes **add9/sus color tones**: D♭ chord uses D♭/E♭/F/A♭ colors; B♭ chord uses B♭/C/F/A♭ or suspended/minor colors; Fm uses F/G/A♭/C/E♭ colors. The first 16 bars repeat the same recognisable piano cell; the final 8 bars use a quieter alternate piano figure and then a small rising pickup into the drop.

5. **LAYERING ORDER** — Opening build is about **24 bars**, roughly **42 seconds** at 137 BPM. Bars 1-8: piano riff plus low bass hum and percussive organ-type stab, not truly dry solo piano. Bars 9-16: a brief acoustic/clean guitar sample layer enters. Bars 17-24: texture drops quieter with a different piano figure, then crescendos; organ drops out; snare appears in the last 4 bars. After bar 24, the beat/drop brings kick, bass, hats, strings, and fuller arrangement. ([xavierblogsblog.wordpress.com](https://xavierblogsblog.wordpress.com/2016/08/22/case-study-robert-miles-children/))

6. **INSTRUMENTATION** — Main piano is a bright 1990s sampled acoustic/rompler piano, commonly identified with **Kurzweil K2000/K2000 V3** rather than Rhodes/electric piano; hard transient, glassy upper mids, heavily reverbed/delayed, high-passed. Supporting sounds: low detuned synth hum/drone, short percussive house-organ stab in the M1/01W family, brief clean acoustic-guitar sample, ambient thunder/rain/noise wash, then snare before the drop. ([decadancebook.wordpress.com](https://decadancebook.wordpress.com/2019/05/09/robert-miles-children-dbx-records/comment-page-1/?utm_source=openai)) ([bonedo.de](https://www.bonedo.de/artikel/kurzweil-v-a-s-t-die-unterschaetzte-synthese-engine/?utm_source=openai)) ([synthmania.com](https://www.synthmania.com/k2000_v3.htm?utm_source=openai))

7. **DYNAMICS ARC** — The build grows by **arrangement density and space**, not by harmonic change. Start: exposed, bright piano with large wet ambience and restrained low end. Middle: guitar and ambient layers widen the image. Final 8 bars: deliberate pullback in level/density, then crescendo via piano intensity and snare tension. Stereo width comes mostly from reverb/delay returns, not from stacked synths.

8. **OTHER STRUCTURAL DETAIL** — No kick-driven sidechain pumping before the drop. Low end is present as a sustained hum, but there is no full bassline groove until the beat enters. Use long hall/plate reverb plus tempo-synced delay on piano; keep the dry piano attack clear. Avoid a modern riser-heavy trance build: the feel comes from **minor-mode suspended/add9 harmony, repeated piano ostinato, restrained low drone, spacious ambience, and a 24-bar delayed gratification before the drums arrive**.
