# Independent review — grok (OK, 132s)

The hook scores **8**. The landing scores **6**. The landing is under 8, so this is a revise.

The spine holds. A snakebite, a serpent on a pole, a look, and life. That is Numbers 21, and the jump to Christ is John 3:14, which is orthodox. The grace line is an invitation. "God hung the remedy in plain sight and asked only that we look" does not threaten, bargain, or make the listener earn it.

What fails is the last step, the quote join, and one unguarded claim.

**The landing is a stock closer.** "Look at what was lifted up for you, and live." "Look" and "live" come from this text, but the sentence never uses the pole, the brass, or the bite. The same line could end any cross talk. "What was lifted up" is unnamed. A stranger who does not know the Bible has not been told it was a cross, or why a man matches a snake.

**"Shaped like the curse" is the sharp line, and it is also the risk.** "The cure was always shaped like the curse, lifted up - and so was Christ." The serpent was the shape of the judgment. Jesus himself makes the "lifted up" parallel. The script then says Christ was shaped like the curse, and it never says he was without sin, bearing our curse for us. A first-time listener can hear "Jesus was the snake" or "Jesus was evil." "Always" makes one story sound like a rule for every cure.

**The quote words are exact KJV. The join is not.** "Make thee a fiery serpent, and set it upon a pole: and it shall come to pass, that every one that is bitten, when he looketh upon it, shall live" is Numbers 21:8. "if a serpent had bitten any man, when he beheld the serpent of brass, he lived" is Numbers 21:9. No word inside the quotes is altered. The "..." welds God's command to the narrator's result, so "he lived" sounds like part of the same speech. It is not. God told Moses to make it. Moses "put it upon a pole." The line "God put a bronze one on a pole" misses Moses, and "bronze" fights the KJV word in the quote, "serpent of brass."

**Smaller drifts.** "We keep reaching for our own cure - our own effort" is the narrator's moral. Numbers 21 is grumbling, then a look. "Call it strange if you like" tells the listener it is strange instead of letting the text do that. "Just a look at the thing that was killing them" is the right picture. They looked at the likeness, not at the snakes that bit them. "The exact shape of their death" already says that better.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Land on this pole: name the cross in the last sentence, and say Christ was lifted up bearing our curse while he himself was without sin — do not leave "shaped like the curse" bare.
2. Split the quote: God's words (Numbers 21:8), then what happened (21:9). Say "brass," and say Moses set it on the pole.
3. Replace "Look at what was lifted up for you, and live" with a closer that still has the serpent, the pole, and the look, so it cannot end a different piece.
