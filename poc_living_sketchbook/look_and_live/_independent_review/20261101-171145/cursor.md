## Independent adversarial review (narration)

### Doctrinal soundness
- **Mostly orthodox** (Numbers 21 typology → Christ lifted up; faith-as-looking, not self-cure). Standard evangelical reading; no contrarian twist.
- **Soft imprecision:** *“The cure was always shaped like the curse, lifted up”* — Numbers 21 does not call the serpents or the pole “the curse”; that language pulls from **Galatians 3:13** (“made a curse for us”) without grounding. A careful listener may hear you **equating the bronze serpent with “the curse”** rather than with **the image of what was killing them**. Not heresy, but **blurred exegesis** for a “faithful depth” piece.
- **Christ jump is asserted, not anchored:** *“and so was Christ”* / *“what was lifted up for you”* assumes crucifixion literacy. The project’s own bronze-serpent pieces often tie **John 3:14–15** explicitly; this script never quotes or paraphrases that anchor, so the NT link is **sermon-truthy** rather than **text-shown**.

### KJV verbatim
Checked against KJV Numbers 21:8–9 (project cache):
- First quoted stretch: *“Make thee a fiery serpent, and set it upon a pole: and it shall come to pass, that every one that is bitten, when he looketh upon it, shall live”* — **exact** (lawful elision of *“And the LORD said unto Moses,”* if treated as a partial quote).
- Second stretch: *“if a serpent had bitten any man, when he beheld the serpent of brass, he lived.”* — **exact** as a substring of 21:9 (elides *“And it came to pass, that”*).

**Process flag (not a word change):** Both fragments sit in one **continuous quoted block** separated by ellipsis, with **no narrator attribution** between divine command (21:8) and narrative result (21:9). Per your engine norms, **attribution frames stay in narrator voice** — here the listener may think one continuous speech is Scripture when it’s **spliced verses**.

**Prose vs KJV:** Opening uses *“bronze”* while the quote correctly has *“serpent of brass”* — fine (not inside quotes).

### Clarity on first hearing (zero Bible knowledge)
- Hook and wilderness episode are **clear**: bite → look at snake on pole → live.
- **Drop-off at the pivot:** *“and so was Christ”* — who Christ is, what “lifted up” means (cross? exaltation?), and why that connects to snakebite is **not spelled out**. A stranger can follow Moses; they **cannot follow the landing** without prior church vocabulary.
- *“the exact shape of their death”* is vivid but slightly **over-metaphorical** (it’s a **representation** of a serpent, not literally the fangs that bit them).

### Grace-anchored conviction
- *“We keep reaching for our own cure - our own effort”* vs *“asked only that we look”* — **works contrast**, but here it functions as **faith vs self-reliance**, not “do more to be saved.” No fear/gain-loss pitch.
- **Watch the edge:** “our own effort” can sound like a **moral scold** on a cold open; it’s brief and not the landing, so **acceptable**, not FAIL-level.

### Freshness = faithful depth
- Paradox (*cure = the thing that kills*) is **faithful** and **text-rooted**, but for this corpus it’s **well-mined** (bronze serpent cluster / longform already use look-and-live). Surprise is **moderate**, not novel entry — not clickbait, just **familiar**.

### One thread (hook → middle → CTA)
- **Single spine:** wrong-kind-of-remedy → God’s look-at-the-lifted-image → our self-cure habit → Christ lifted up → look and live. **No thread swap.**
