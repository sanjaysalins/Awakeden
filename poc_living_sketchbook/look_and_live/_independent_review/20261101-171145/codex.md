# Independent review — codex (OK, 39s)

**Hook: 8/10.** “You'd think the cure for a snakebite would be anything but a snake” creates immediate, understandable tension. It earns attention without a template opening.

**Landing: 5/10.** “Look at what was lifted up for you, and live” reuses the piece’s verbs, but could close another crucifixion narration unchanged. “What” also leaves the listener looking toward an unidentified object when the invitation should clearly concern Christ.

The substantive problems:

1. **The central claim exceeds the stated biblical connection.** “The cure was always shaped like the curse” turns one event into a sweeping rule. “The exact shape of their death” adds rhetorical precision the passage does not establish. Jesus explicitly connects the serpent’s lifting with His own lifting and connects eternal life with believing in Him. That supports the typology; it does not establish a universal principle about cures resembling curses. Christ bearing the curse is biblical, but needs explanation rather than a visual slogan. ([John 3:14–16](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John+3%3A14-16&version=KJV), [Galatians 3:13](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Galatians+3%3A13&version=KJV))

2. **The gospel bridge assumes the knowledge your target listener lacks.** “And so was Christ” never explains who Christ is, that He was crucified, or what His death addresses. “Asked only that we look” transfers a physical action into Christian application without explaining faith. A newcomer can follow the snakebite story and still miss the salvation claim. John supplies the missing bridge: believing in the Son whom God gave in love. ([John 3:14–16](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John+3%3A14-16&version=KJV))

3. **The quotation merges two speakers without an audible boundary.** No quoted word is altered: both excerpts match the KJV, with an indicated omission. However, after “God … said,” the quotation moves from God’s instruction into the narrator’s account of what happened. An ellipsis on the page does not tell a listener that the speaker has changed. This is an attribution problem, not a verbatim failure. ([Numbers 21:8–9](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Numbers+21%3A8-9&version=KJV))

4. **The grace application is asserted before it is explained.** “We keep reaching for our own cure—our own effort” introduces a generic accusation without identifying the problem our effort cannot resolve. There is no explicit fear manipulation or works requirement, but the invitation rests mainly on obtaining life; God’s love and Christ’s saving work remain unstated.

5. **The spine holds, but repetition crowds out depth.** “Not medicine. Not an antidote,” “Just a look,” and “asked only that we look” repeat the same contrast. Meanwhile, “Call it strange if you like” spends time anticipating resistance. The missing depth is Jesus’ own explanation of this event, not another formulation of its strangeness.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Replace the sweeping cure-and-curse claim with the specific connection Jesus makes in John 3.
2. Explain Christ’s crucifixion and believing in Him, grounding the invitation in God’s love.
3. Separate the quotation’s speakers and make the closing invitation specific enough to escape the stock-closer cap.
