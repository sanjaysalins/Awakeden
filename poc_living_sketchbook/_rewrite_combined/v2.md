## jericho
One red cord kept a woman alive when a city fell. Her name was Rahab. She had a bad name, but she hid two of God's spies, and she believed in Him. They promised her: "thou shalt bind this line of scarlet thread in the window". Then God's army marched around her city, and on the seventh day "the people shouted with a great shout, that the wall fell down flat". The city fell. Rahab and her family were saved. Hebrews says why: "By faith the harlot Rahab perished not with them that believed not". Not for being good. For trusting God's promise. Matthew puts her in the family line of Jesus: "And Salmon begat Booz of Rachab". Rachab is Rahab. Christians see a picture in her window: a scarlet cord, and the blood of Jesus. Rahab could not hold up a wall. She could only hang a cord, and trust the promise. Hang your hope on Jesus the same way.

## two_goats
One goat died. One goat was sent away alive. Why two? I am Aaron, the first high priest, and once a year I handled both. The law gave me two goats, and together they were one sin offering. The first I killed, and I carried its blood inside the veil. The second I did not kill. I laid both hands on its head, and confessed over it all the sins of the people. Then it was led into the wilderness, to a land where no one lived, carrying those sins away. One goat paid with its blood. One goat carried the sin away. Long after me, Jesus did what my two goats only pictured. Isaiah wrote of Him: "the LORD hath laid on him the iniquity of us all". He paid, and He carried it away. The sins laid on that goat were laid on Him. And when He died, "the veil of the temple was rent in twain from the top to the bottom". The way to God is open. Come, and your sin is carried away. Walk in.

## bronze_serpent
I am Moses. My people were dying of snakebite, and God did not take the snakes away. The people came to me: "We have sinned, for we have spoken against the LORD, and against thee". I prayed. And God answered with something strange. He told me to make a serpent and put it on a pole. So I made a serpent of brass, like the ones that bit them, and set it on a pole. Whoever was bitten looked at it, and lived. Long after me, Jesus told a man named Nicodemus: "And as Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, even so must the Son of man be lifted up: That whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have eternal life." My brass serpent was only a shadow. Jesus was lifted up on a cross, and Paul the apostle says He was "made a curse for us". Sin bites us all, and the cure is not in you. It was lifted up where you can see it. To look at Jesus is to believe in Him. Look, and live.
