# Independent review — codex (OK, 65s)

**Hook: 8/10.** “One goat died. One goat was sent away alive. Why two?” creates an immediate, concrete question without a template opening. The following introduction delays the answer, but the opening earns attention.

**Landing: 5/10.** “Walk in.” could end numerous pieces about doors, the temple, or access to God. It resolves the late veil image rather than the opening question about two goats. Under your stock-closer rule, it caps at 5.

1. **“One goat paid with its blood” overstates what the animal accomplished.** “Paid” sounds like an effective settlement of sin, while Hebrews 10:4 explicitly denies that animal blood can take sins away. “Jesus did what my two goats only pictured” subsequently qualifies this, but the listener must reconcile the two claims. Distinguish the symbolic sacrifice from Christ’s effective accomplishment when introducing payment. [Hebrews 10:1–14, KJV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Hebrews+10&version=KJV)

2. **“Come, and your sin is carried away” leaves the response undefined.** Come to whom, and how? For a listener without Bible knowledge, “Walk in” supplies another spatial command rather than clarifying trust in Christ. This is an ambiguity, not an explicit works gospel, but the invitation needs to locate assurance in Christ’s finished sacrifice and identify faith as the response. Hebrews connects drawing near with Christ’s blood and assurance of faith. [Hebrews 10:19–22, KJV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Hebrews+10&version=KJV)

3. **“Inside the veil” assumes the knowledge the ending requires.** Nothing explains that this is a sanctuary curtain restricting access to God’s presence. Later, “the veil of the temple was rent in twain” adds archaic language without explaining it. A novice may hear that fabric tore without understanding why this means “The way to God is open.”

4. **The final movement changes the controlling image.** “He paid, and He carried it away” answers “Why two?” The narration then moves into a different question: how can we enter God’s presence? These themes are biblically connected, but the script has not made that connection sufficiently clear. “Come … carried away. Walk in” consequently asks the listener to process sin going outward and the listener going inward in rapid succession.

5. **“The sins laid on that goat were laid on Him” blurs picture and fulfillment.** In context, this can express orthodox substitution. However, “that goat” can make it sound like the ritual’s particular load of sins was subsequently transferred from the animal to Jesus. The distinction between a ritual representation and Christ actually bearing sin needs greater precision.

**KJV check:** Both biblical quotations are exact continuous KJV excerpts; no altered words found. Omitting surrounding clauses does not corrupt either excerpt. [Isaiah 53:6](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Isaiah+53%3A6&version=KJV), [Matthew 27:51](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Matthew+27%3A51&version=KJV)

**Other lens checks:** “Together they were one sin offering” has textual support in Leviticus 16:5; the hands, confession, and uninhabited wilderness follow verses 21–22. The two-goat connection supplies faithful textual depth without requiring a contrarian reading. There is no fear, prosperity, or earned-acceptance appeal here; the problem is precision and coherence, not manipulative conviction. [Leviticus 16, KJV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Leviticus+16&version=KJV)

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Distinguish the goats’ symbolic role from Christ’s actual accomplishment; correct “One goat paid.”
2. Build the CTA from the two-goat thread and make trust in Christ explicit.
3. Explain the veil’s significance before relying on it, or remove that competing closing image.
