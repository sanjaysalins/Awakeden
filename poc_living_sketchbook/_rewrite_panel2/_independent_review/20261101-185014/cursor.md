## Independent adversarial review — Jericho / Rahab (v2)

### Doctrinal soundness
- **Mostly orthodox:** faith-not-merit (“Not for being good. For trusting God's promise.”), Rahab in Christ’s lineage (Matthew 1), scarlet→blood as **“Christians see a picture”** (typology framed as Christian reading, not “Joshua says blood”) — acceptable evangelical echo, not a contrarian hook.
- **Faith vs. her act:** You say she **“hid two of God's spies, and she believed in Him”** then attribute rescue to faith, not goodness. That’s defensible, but on first hearing the **spy-hiding** can sound like the reason she lived until the Hebrews beat lands. The Hebrews line you quote **cuts** “when she had received the spies with peace,” which is exactly where the canon links **faith and her welcome of the spies** — you lose nuance and invite “faith vs. works” misread for a beat.
- **Typology:** **“a scarlet cord, and the blood of Jesus”** has no Joshua proof-text; you hedge with **“Christians see a picture.”** Sound for a doctrine panel, but a hostile listener can still hear **invented allegory** unless **trust in the spies’ promise** is nailed before the blood line.

### KJV verbatim
Against project KJV cache: the **word strings inside quotes are exact** (partial excerpts allowed).
- **Joshua 2:18** fragment: **“thou shalt bind this line of scarlet thread in the window”** — verbatim substring.
- **Joshua 6:20**: **“the people shouted with a great shout, that the wall fell down flat”** — verbatim (includes **“that”**).
- **Hebrews 11:31**: **“By faith the harlot Rahab perished not with them that believed not”** — verbatim; trailing clause omitted.
- **Matthew 1:5**: **“And Salmon begat Booz of Rachab”** — verbatim.

**No swapped words** inside quotation marks.

### Clarity on first hearing
- **Big logic gap:** **“the wall fell down flat”** + **“The city fell. Rahab and her family were saved”** with **no** explanation that the **scarlet mark in the window** was the **sign of the spies’ promise** to spare that house (Joshua 2:18–21; 6:22–25). A zero-Bible listener gets **miracle rescue with no causal thread** — cord mentioned early, then forgotten until the typology and **“hang a cord.”**
- **Hebrews on audio:** **“perished not with them that believed not”** is **KJV-faithful but listener-hostile** — double negative + **“them that believed not”** does not parse as “everyone else in the city died” without a plain gloss. **“Hebrews says why:”** promises explanation; the quote **does not** explain *why the cord worked*, only *faith*.
- **Names:** **“Rachab is Rahab”** helps; **“Booz”** and **“begat”** still land weak for global/non-church ears unless you say **ancestor of Jesus** in the same breath (you do say **“family line of Jesus”** before the quote — OK, but **Booz/Rachab** remain opaque sounds).
- **“She had a bad name”** — cute parallel to **“Her name was Rahab”**; the next KJV **“harlot”** clarifies for attentive listeners; others may still miss that **“bad name”** means **moral reputation**, not spelling.

### Grace-anchored conviction
- **Strong:** **“Not for being good. For trusting God's promise.”** — invitation by trust, no fear/gain-loss, no “be good like Rahab.”
- **Minor:** **“Hang your hope on Jesus the same way”** is imperative but **trust-shaped**, not merit-shaped — acceptable.

### Freshness = faithful depth
- **Fresh entry:** **“One red cord kept a woman alive when a city fell”** — concrete, text-rooted.
- **Weaker depth:** Skipping **what the cord was for** (sign of sparing) makes the middle feel like **story summary + standard scarlet/blood sermon** rather than **surprising text detail** (promise, mark, household).

### One thread (hook → middle → CTA)
- Single spine: **cord → faith → lineage → scarlet picture → passive trust vs. falling wall → hang hope.** No major thread swap.
- **Structural wobble:** Early promise quote **“bind this line… in the window”** is not **paid off** in prose before the closing **“hang a cord”** — listener must remember the opening quote unaided.
