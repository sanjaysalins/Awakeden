# Independent review — claude (OK, 53s)

**Hook: 7/10.** "I am Moses. My people were dying of snakebite, and God did not take the snakes away" has a real stake and a real puzzle.
- "I am Moses" is a flat, slow first beat.
- It is not a template open, but a stranger has no reason to care yet. The line that makes someone stop is "God answered with something strange", and that comes later.

**Landing: 7/10.** "Look, and live" is built from this piece's own verb. It echoes Numbers 21:8, "looketh upon it, shall live", so it is earned.
- It is also very close to the stock "look to Jesus" family. Cut off from the rest, it could end other pieces on Isaiah 45:22 or on the cross.
- The line before it, "To look at Jesus is to believe in Him", is a flat explanatory gloss that deflates the close.
- The ending is two imperatives, with no image or detail from the pole scene (the pole, the bite, the wound).

**KJV verbatim: clean.**
- Numbers 21:7 is truncated, but every quoted word is exact.
- John 3:14-15 is exact.
- "made a curse for us" is exact (Galatians 3:13). The script names Paul but never gives the reference, which is fine for audio.

**Clarity problems:**
1. **The central logic is never stated.** Why did God use a serpent, the thing that bit them? Why does Paul's "curse" line fit? The piece never says that the serpent on the pole stands for sin and the curse falling on the one lifted up. A zero-knowledge listener hears "brass snake = Jesus" and can't tell why a snake stands for Christ. "My brass serpent was only a shadow" is the only bridge, and it is vague.
2. **The cause is missing.** The people say "We have sinned" with no reason given. The listener never learns that the snakes were judgment for complaining (Numbers 21:5-6). So "Sin bites us all" is asserted rather than shown.
3. **The hook's puzzle is not answered.** "God did not take the snakes away" is left hanging. The payoff, that God gave a way through the bite instead of removing the danger, is never said.
4. **"made a curse for us" is archaic, and "curse" is not explained.** Under your global-audience rule, a non-native listener won't understand it.
5. **"the cure is not in you" is awkward.** "It was lifted up where you can see it" is also vague about what "it" is.
6. **The speaker's position is odd.** Moses narrates Nicodemus, Paul and the cross ("Long after me"), and then speaks directly to the viewer. The frame is a little muddled, but not fatal.

**Doctrine:**
- It is sound and orthodox. The Numbers 21 / John 3 / Galatians 3 typology is standard.
- Grace framing is good: "the cure is not in you".
- There is no fear framing or gain/loss pressure.
- "To look at Jesus is to believe in Him" is a defensible gloss, though it is the writer's equation and not a quote.
- "Look" is imperative, but within the grace frame it reads as an invitation, not a work.

**Thread spine:** consistent, looking to be healed, from hook to CTA. There is no thread-swapping.

Both scores are under 8, so this needs a revise.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Add the missing bridge in plain words, in one or two sentences. The snake on the pole is the thing that bit them, lifted up and made harmless. Jesus took the curse (the sin-bite) on Himself on the cross, so looking at Him heals. Also say why they were bitten (they spoke against God) so "Sin bites us all" is earned. This pays off both the "strange answer" gap and the "did not take the snakes away" hook.
2. Rebuild the landing from a concrete image in the piece (the pole, the bitten person lifting their eyes). Cut or fold the flat gloss "To look at Jesus is to believe in Him". Avoid ending on a bare "Look, and live" that could close any cross piece.
3. Sharpen the hook by opening on the strange answer or the paradox ("God answered snakebite with a snake on a pole"). Drop "I am Moses" as the first beat, and paraphrase or unpack "made a curse for us" for global listeners.
