## jericho
Jericho was a walled city, and God told His army to march around it, in silence. Six days, once around. On the seventh day, seven times. And from one window in the wall hung a scarlet cord. That window was Rahab's, a woman with a bad name. She hid God's two spies, and she believed in Him. They told her: "thou shalt bind this line of scarlet thread in the window". On the seventh day "the people shouted with a great shout, that the wall fell down flat". But Rahab and her family were saved. Scripture says why: "By faith the harlot Rahab perished not with them that believed not". Not saved for being good. Saved for trusting God's promise. Matthew lists her in Jesus' own family line, spelled Rachab: "Salmon begat Booz of Rachab". Many see a picture here: a scarlet cord at her window, and the blood of Jesus on the cross. She tied her hope to a promise. Tie yours to Him.

## two_goats
I am Aaron, the first high priest. Once a year, on the Day of Atonement, I went behind the curtain to stand before God for the people. The law gave me two goats for one offering. I killed the first, and carried its blood inside the veil. The second goat I did not kill. I laid both my hands on its head, and confessed the sins of the people over it. Then it was sent away into the wilderness, carrying their sins with it. One goat paid. One goat carried the sin away. Christians see in these two goats a picture of Jesus. Isaiah wrote of Him: "the LORD hath laid on him the iniquity of us all". He gave His own life, and the guilt was carried away. Hebrews says He did it once, and sat down. When He died, the curtain of the temple was torn in two, from the top to the bottom. I was only the sign. He is the real thing. The way in is open for you. Walk in.

## bronze_serpent
I am Moses. My people were dying of snakebite, one after another. They came to me and said, "We have sinned, for we have spoken against the LORD, and against thee". I prayed for them. And the LORD told me to make a serpent of brass, and put it on a pole. Anyone who was bitten only had to look at it, and they lived. Nothing else was asked of them. That brass serpent was only a shadow. Many years later, Jesus told a man named Nicodemus: "And as Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, even so must the Son of man be lifted up: That whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have eternal life." Jesus was lifted up on a cross. Galatians says "Christ hath redeemed us from the curse of the law, being made a curse for us". So hear me, you who are bitten. That is every one of us. The cure is not in you. It hangs in plain sight. Look to Jesus, lifted up for you. Believe in Him, and you will live.
