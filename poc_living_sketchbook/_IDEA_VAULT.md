## Lane 1 — How the Word arrives (the deepest ones)

1. **The Word Arrives Whole.** Human words write letter by letter — strokes take
   time. When JESUS speaks, the words don't write: they are simply THERE, complete,
   between one frame and the next. His word doesn't take time; ours does. The
   entire theology of authority in a timing decision. Costs nothing. Might be the
   best idea in this file.
2. ~~**Negative-Space Light.**~~ **REJECTED by user 2026-07-30** after POC
   (`storm/_vault_poc/vault_2_negative_space.mp4`). The user's reason, verbatim in
   spirit: it did not fit the overall look and feel — it sat outside the world.
   **The law it teaches (apply to every future device): a device must be something
   that could HAPPEN IN THE BOOK** — a mark the Keeper makes, a thing paper does,
   light falling on a desk. A full-page symbolic composition is POSTER grammar,
   not journal grammar — designed-image language breaks the world even when the
   theology is right. (The caption-box lesson, one level deeper: you can't
   texture your way out of a borrowed visual grammar.) Do not re-pitch; cousins
   like gold-through-creases must pass the "could this happen at the desk?" test
   before any POC.
3. **The Permanence Split.** Across one spread, human ink visibly ages — browning,
   fading in fast-time — while the gold stays exactly as bright as the frame it
   arrived. "Heaven and earth shall pass away: but my words shall not pass away."
   Decay as proof, permanence as doctrine.

## Lane 2 — The Keeper's body (intimacy)

4. **The Inkwell Runs Dry.** Mid-line the ink starves to a dry scratch, the line
   stops; a pause; a small re-dip blot; the line resumes darker. For exhaustion
   beats — "at their wit's end." Human frailty inside the act of writing.
5. **The Dog-Ear.** At THE verse — the one the episode turns on — the Keeper folds
   the page corner down, on camera. Not the ribbon (that's the episode's end); the
   dog-ear marks the WORD mid-episode: "this one I keep."
6. **The Page Breathes.** held-breath already knows the narration's silences: let
   the page rise/fall almost imperceptibly, like a chest. At a death beat it STOPS.
   At the resurrection it resumes. The book as a body.
7. **Entries Across Days.** The Keeper writes "the end." after the crucifixion.
   Later — same episode, different ink, steadier hand — "the end." is struck
   through and beneath it: "the third day." Journaling across days inside one
   episode; the strike-through as gospel.

## Lane 3 — The desk and the world (atmosphere)

8. **Rain-Shadow.** During storm episodes, the soft moving shadow of rain on an
   unseen window runs down the page — the world outside the book touching the
   light that falls on it. Never droplets ON the page (that's the Bleed's job) —
   only the shadow of weather.
9. **Candle-Only Spread.** One spread lit solely by the drawn flame inside it:
   beyond its radius, near-black paper. The radius grows or shrinks with the story.
   For the wise virgins, for Peter's fireside denial — the light budget IS the drama.
10. **The Wax Seal.** At covenant moments, sealing wax drips, a signet presses, the
    seal stays forever — rubric red. Noah's bow, Abraham's stars. His promises
    arrive sealed. (Signet design = user decision, series-constant, like the
    watermark symbol.)

## Lane 4 — The book across episodes (the long game)

11. **The Blind Emboss.** Each episode opens carrying the faint colorless
    impression — pressure, no ink — of the PREVIOUS episode's final line, pressed
    through the page above. Seriality you can touch. (set-off is ink within an
    episode; this is pressure across episodes.)
12. **The Flyleaf Census.** The inside cover fills, one line per finished episode,
    in the Keeper's hand: "the storm — stilled." "the leper — cleansed." The
    series' index as living content; a finale where the camera just reads the list.
13. **The Pricked Margin.** Medieval scribes pricked ruling holes down the page
    edge. Thirteen tiny pricks, one per spread; the current one catches the light.
    A diegetic progress bar no interface designer could ever ship.
14. **The Second Hand.** Years later, another hand annotates the same book —
    different ink, different age: the Keeper's older self, or a child inheriting
    it ("this was my father's book"). For a special episode or the series' end.
    The book outlives its keeper; the Word outlives the book.

## Lane 5 — Density (professional simultaneity)

15. **Two Hands at Once.** The margin note WRITES WHILE the drawing moves — panic
    journaling live against the storm clip, both in motion. Right now everything
    is sequential; simultaneity is what dense, professional sequences feel like.
