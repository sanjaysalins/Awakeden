# Independent review — codex (OK, 38s)

**Hook: 4/10. Landing: 5/10.**

1. **“One goat paid.” — the most serious doctrinal problem.** This states the symbol as an accomplished payment. A newcomer can reasonably hear that an animal actually settled human guilt. Hebrews 10:4 explicitly denies that animal blood can take away sins. “Christians see … a picture of Jesus” arrives afterward and does not adequately qualify the earlier assertion. Distinguish the ritual’s portrayal from Christ’s effective sacrifice. ([Hebrews 10:1–14](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Hebrews+10%3A1-14&version=KJV))

2. **“I am Aaron” → “Christians see” → “Hebrews says” — unstable narrator.** Historical dramatization becomes retrospective Christian exposition without a transition. For someone with no Bible knowledge, Aaron now appears to know and narrate events and writings from centuries after his lifetime. This is a clarity problem, not necessarily invented doctrine.

3. **“Hebrews says He did it once, and sat down.” — compression removes the meaning.** “Did it” leaves the action vague; “sat down” sounds like resting after exertion. The passage specifies one sacrifice for sins and sitting at God’s right hand. Without that explanation, a potentially weighty detail becomes an unexplained gesture. ([Hebrews 10:11–12](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Hebrews+10%3A11-12&version=KJV))

4. **The spine shifts from sins carried away to access through a curtain.** “He gave His own life, and the guilt was carried away” completes the two-goat argument. The script then introduces sitting down, the torn curtain, and Aaron as “the sign.” These are related biblical themes, but their relationship is compressed beyond first-hearing clarity. The opening curtain gives the ending some preparation; the central goat image nevertheless disappears from the CTA.

5. **“The way in is open for you. Walk in.” — insufficiently defined invitation.** In to what, and how? Hebrews connects access to Jesus’ blood and drawing near in faith. The narration leaves those connections implicit. This is not overt works teaching or fear manipulation, but its final imperative needs a clearer dependence on Christ’s finished work. ([Hebrews 10:19–22](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Hebrews+10%3A19-22&version=KJV))

6. **Hook: “I am Aaron, the first high priest.”** This supplies identity before giving a stranger a reason to care. “High priest” itself needs context. The distinctive tension—two goats with different roles—arrives too late to carry the first five seconds.

7. **Landing: “Walk in.”** It uses the curtain/access image, but this exact sentence could close many pieces about doors, temples, welcome, or belonging. Under your portability test, it caps at **5/10**. It also abandons the piece’s most specific verb: carrying sins away.

8. **KJV check: no altered words in the sole quotation.** “the LORD hath laid on him the iniquity of us all” is an exact excerpt from Isaiah 53:6. The unquoted temple-curtain sentence is a faithful paraphrase, not a quotation violation. ([Isaiah 53:6](https://www.bible.com/bible/1/ISA.53.6.KJV), [Matthew 27:51](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Matthew+27%3A51&version=KJV))

The two-goat connection offers faithful textual depth; it does not require a contrarian reading. The weaknesses are the literal-sounding payment claim, unstable viewpoint, and an ending that loses the central image.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Qualify “One goat paid” so the ritual points to the payment Christ alone accomplishes.
2. Establish a coherent narrator and explain what Christ’s sitting down signifies.
3. Strengthen the opening and build a faith-explicit invitation from the two-goat image.
