- **KJV Check:** The quote `"the LORD hath laid on him the iniquity of us all"` correctly matches Isaiah 53:6.

**CLARITY & POV SHIFT**
- The script suffers a jarring, clinical break in voice. It starts as a first-person immersive narrative ("I am Aaron...", "The law gave me..."), but then awkwardly breaks the fourth wall with: *"Christians see in these two goats a picture of Jesus."* This sounds like a textbook commentary, not a cohesive script. It destroys the immersion.

**ONE THREAD SPINE**
- **Thread-swapping:** The script violates the single-spine rule. The hook and middle are entirely about the **two goats** (one paying, one carrying sin away). However, the climax and landing completely drop the goats and pivot to the **temple curtain** ("the curtain of the temple was torn in two... The way in is open"). You swapped the "bearing our sin" thread for an "access to God's presence" thread. 

**HOOK SCORE: 5/10**
- *"I am Aaron, the first high priest. Once a year..."* 
- It avoids the cheapest templates, but it reads like a slow Sunday School play introduction. There is zero immediate tension, mystery, or hooky visual to stop a stranger from scrolling in the first 3 seconds. 

**LANDING SCORE: 4/10**
- *"I was only the sign. He is the real thing. The way in is open for you. Walk in."*
- This fails the prompt's specific CTA test. Could this end a different piece? Yes. It could end literally any piece about the Tabernacle, the Holy of Holies, the torn veil, or the Temple. It is completely disconnected from the piece's own title and core imagery (the two goats). It also feels slightly works-adjacent/instructional ("Walk in") rather than a grace-anchored rest in what was already carried away. 

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Fix the thread-swap and landing: Make the CTA resolve the *two goats* imagery (sin paid for and carried far away) rather than abandoning it for the torn curtain. 
2. Delete the textbook transition ("Christians see in these two goats a picture of Jesus") and transition naturally from the shadow (the goats) to the substance (Christ).
3. Punch up the hook to introduce immediate intrigue or tension about the goats themselves, rather than a slow biographical intro from Aaron.
