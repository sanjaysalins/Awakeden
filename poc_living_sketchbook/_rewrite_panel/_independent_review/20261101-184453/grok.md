# Independent review — grok (OK, 245s)

Hook **5**. Landing **5**. Both are under 8, so this is a revise.

**Hook (5).** A stranger’s first seconds are “I am Aaron, the first high priest. Once a year, on the Day of Atonement…” That is a name and a feast title. No stake. The line that could stop a scroll — “The second goat I did not kill.” — comes later. This is not a “Did you know / Imagine / What if” open. The score is low because the opening itself is an introduction.

**Landing (5).** The close is “The way in is open for you. Walk in.” The torn curtain is in the piece, so the image is related. The exact last sentence still fails the portability test. “Walk in.” could end a door piece, an ark piece, or any veil short. This script’s own verbs are kill, carry, lay, confess, send, laid, sat. “Walk” shows up only at the end. Stock closer. Cap at 5.

**KJV.** The only words in quotation marks are “the LORD hath laid on him the iniquity of us all”. That is Isaiah 53:6, word for word. Keep it. “Torn in two, from the top to the bottom” is outside quotes. It retells Matthew 27:51, where the KJV says the veil was “rent in twain”. Do not put “curtain” or “torn” inside quotation marks later.

**Doctrine.** The big claim is sound: two goats, one offering, one Jesus, sins borne away, once for all, access opened. Azazel is left out. That is the right restraint. These lines still miss.

- “One goat paid.” Leviticus 16:5 is two goats for one sin offering. The live goat is also “to make an atonement with him” (Lev 16:10). “Paid” turns one offering into a payer and a carrier. “He gave His own life, and the guilt was carried away” tries to join them after the split is already taught.
- “I killed the first.” The lots decide which goat dies and which leaves (Lev 16:8–10). “The first” adds an order the text does not have.
- “I went behind the curtain to stand before God for the people”, then only the goat’s blood goes in. Aaron first brought a bull’s blood for his own sin (Lev 16:11–14; Heb 7:27). That omission flattens the contrast this script’s Hebrews line depends on.
- “I am Aaron, the first high priest.” The same script cites Hebrews. Hebrews 7 says Melchizedek was priest before Aaron. “First” is a soft overclaim beside that book.
- “When He died, the curtain of the temple was torn…” Aaron’s veil was the tabernacle. In his voice (“I went behind the curtain” … “the curtain … was torn”) it sounds like one room in his lifetime.
- “I was only the sign. He is the real thing.” “Christians see in these two goats a picture of Jesus” already marks the typology honestly. “The real thing” adds nothing.

**Clarity.** A listener with no Bible has to carry “high priest,” “Day of Atonement,” “the law,” “veil,” “Isaiah,” “Hebrews,” and “the temple” with no gloss.

- Same object, three names: “behind the curtain”, then “inside the veil”, then “the curtain of the temple.”
- “Hebrews says He did it once, and sat down.” “Hebrews” is an unexplained name. “Did it” has no clear target. “Once a year” was the setup for a finished work. “Sat down” hides that. A newcomer hears a man taking a seat.
- “from the top to the bottom” never gets a meaning.
- “I am Aaron” is present tense, then Isaiah, Christians, Hebrews, and the torn temple arrive with no time jump. He sounds like he watched the cross.
- “The second goat I did not kill” and then “He gave His own life” can sound like the picture broke. It needs one plain line: both goats are one picture of one Jesus, who died and bore the sin away.

**Grace.** No threat and no loss-frame. “The way in is open” rests on His death. “Walk in” does not say into God’s presence, and it does not say the step is trust. It stays an invitation. It is vague, not a works pitch.

**Freshness.** The real surprise is already in the text: one offering, the goat he did not kill, sins confessed on its head, “laid on him,” once and sat down. “Paid” and “the real thing” are less exact than that text.

**Spine.** Two threads. Curtain → torn curtain → “Walk in.” And two goats → sin carried away → iniquity laid on Him. The middle is the goats. The last sentence does not need either goat. The goat thread dies before the CTA.

Leave these: “both my hands,” “confessed … over it,” “wilderness,” the Isaiah clause, “once” against “once a year.”

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Replace "One goat paid" and "I killed the first": both goats are one sin offering, the lots choose, and Aaron's own bull goes in first, so Jesus is the one offering those rites only pictured.
2. End on this piece's goat — sins laid on Him and carried away — so the last sentence cannot close a different "come in" short.
3. Open on "The second goat I did not kill," use one word for the veil, say "sat down" means the work is finished, and mark that Aaron is speaking past his own lifetime.
