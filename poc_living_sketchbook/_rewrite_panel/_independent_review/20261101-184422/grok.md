# Independent review — grok (OK, 197s)

The scarlet cord is doing too much work, and the spoken order contradicts itself. Hook **5**. Landing **4**. Both sit under 8.

**KJV.** The four quoted strings match the KJV wording. No altered word inside the quotes: Joshua 2:18 (“thou shalt bind this line of scarlet thread in the window”), Joshua 6:20 (“the people shouted with a great shout, that the wall fell down flat”), Hebrews 11:31 (“By faith the harlot Rahab perished not with them that believed not”), Matthew 1:5 (“Salmon begat Booz of Rachab”).

**Doctrine.** “Not saved for being good. Saved for trusting God’s promise.” is the sound line, and it matches the Hebrews quote you just read. Then the piece leaves that claim. “Many see a picture here: a scarlet cord at her window, and the blood of Jesus on the cross.” Scripture never says the cord is Jesus’ blood. Hebrews 11:31 (the verse you quote) gives faith, and the words you cut off are “when she had received the spies with peace.” “Many see” is a crowd, not a text. That picture is an old illustration set in the seat of the point. “Trusting God’s promise” also tightens the story: the pledge in Joshua 2 is the spies’ oath, and her confession is that the LORD is God in heaven and earth. “In silence” is the other factual slip. Joshua 6:10 forbids a word from their mouths. The priests still blew trumpets every circuit (Joshua 6:4, 8–9, 13). A hearer is told the march was soundless.

**First hearing.** The timeline runs backward. “From one window in the wall hung a scarlet cord” comes first. Then “They told her: ‘thou shalt bind this line of scarlet thread in the window’.” The cord is already up, and then she is ordered to hang it. Bible order is the reverse: she hides the spies, they give the sign, she binds the line (Joshua 2:21), the army marches later. “A woman with a bad name” does not tell a newcomer what she was. The quote then says “harlot,” which many listeners will not know. Spoken “spelled Rachab” cannot be heard. Rahab and Rachab sound like the same name, and “Booz” lands like “booze.” “Matthew lists her” assumes a Gospel the listener has not been given. “Perished not with them that believed not” is a double negative. The next two sentences rescue the faith point and still leave “perished not” muddy. Prose says “cord,” the quote says “thread.” Those can sound like two objects.

**Grace.** The fear-or-merit close is avoided. “Not saved for being good” holds the gospel. The last verb pulls the other way. She is not saved for a deed, then the hearer is told “Tie yours to Him,” as if the saving move is something they perform. “Yours” has no noun in that sentence.

**Spine.** The open is walls, march, silence. The middle adds a cord, a faith gloss, a genealogy spelling note, then a blood picture. The close is a tie-to-Him slogan. Silence never pays off. The genealogy never reaches the last line. Hook image and final verb are different threads.

**Freshness.** The surprising clause is already in the quote: by faith the harlot did not perish with them that believed not. The same city had the news (Joshua 2:9–11). That contrast is dropped for the most familiar Rahab allegory.

**Hook: 5.** First words are “Jericho was a walled city,” a definition. “In silence” is the only odd fact, and it sits at the end of a lecture sentence. No template phrase, and still no reason a stranger stops in five seconds.

**Landing: 4.** “She tied her hope to a promise” borrows this story’s verb. The actual last sentence, “Tie yours to Him,” drops cord, window, wall, and faith. That sentence could end a different piece. Stock closer.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Keep one spine — by faith she was spared when the wall fell — and end on this piece’s own image (the line in her window), not on “Tie yours to Him.” Move “Many see… the blood of Jesus” out of the landing so an illustration is not the doctrine.
2. Tell the events in Bible order: hide the spies, believe, bind the line, wordless march with trumpets, shout, wall falls, she and her house are spared. Cut spoken “bad name,” “spelled Rachab,” and the bare name “Matthew.”
3. Open on the strange stake (one window marked, or the wall fell and that house stood), not “Jericho was a walled city.”
