# Independent review — claude (OK, 31s)

**Quote check (KJV verbatim).** All four quotes match the KJV exactly:
- Joshua 2:18, "thou shalt bind this line of scarlet thread in the window"
- Joshua 6:20, "the people shouted with a great shout, that the wall fell down flat"
- Hebrews 11:31, "By faith the harlot Rahab perished not with them that believed not"
- Matthew 1:5, "Salmon begat Booz of Rachab"

**Findings**

1. **"march around it, in silence" is imprecise.** Joshua 6:4 has priests blowing seven trumpets of rams' horns, and the people were silent only until the shout (6:10). A Bible reader will catch this. "In silence" also sets up a trumpet-free picture that the text doesn't support.

2. **The scarlet cord comes in out of order.** The opening says a scarlet cord already hangs from the window, then later the spies tell her to bind it. The thread appears before the promise that explains it. A first-time listener may wonder why a cord is there at all.

3. **The cord's job is never stated.** The script never says the cord marked her house to be spared (Josh 2:18–19). The line "But Rahab and her family were saved" jumps straight from the wall falling, and the link between cord and rescue is left implicit. The whole typology depends on this link.

4. **The Christ link is thin and asserted.** "Many see a picture here: a scarlet cord at her window, and the blood of Jesus on the cross" is honest hedging and doctrinally safe. But it gives no reason a newcomer should care, and it doesn't use the Passover blood-on-the-doorpost parallel that would make the picture land. The Christ moment is one short sentence, so the spine feels bolted on at the end.

5. **"Saved for trusting God's promise" slips toward merit.** "Not saved for being good. Saved for trusting" can sound like faith is the price paid. "Saved by trusting" or "saved through" is closer to Hebrews 11:31 and the grace framing. There is also an unaddressed tension with James 2:25. It isn't a doctrinal error, but one phrase fixes it.

6. **Clutter and ESL friction.** "spelled Rachab" is a spelling aside that a newcomer doesn't need. "Salmon begat Booz" is archaic, and the listener has no idea why they should care. Lead with the point instead: she ended up in Jesus' own family line.

7. **Mixed metaphor in the CTA.** In the story, the cord is tied in the window, and her hope is tied to a promise. "Tie yours to Him" turns the cord into hope and then into a person. It is cohesive enough to hear, but it doesn't say what promise Jesus makes or what tying means. It is the piece's own image and verb, which is a strength.

8. **Conviction and framing.** There is no fear or gain/loss framing, and grace is respected. The trust-not-goodness line is the right instinct.

**Scores**
- **Hook: 5/10.** "Jericho was a walled city" is a flat, textbook open, and there is no curiosity gap. A scarlet cord in a window is a strong image, but it is buried. An opening on the cord and the woman with a bad name would be stronger.
- **Landing: 7/10.** "She tied her hope to a promise. Tie yours to Him." comes from this piece's own cord image and verb. It is not a pure stock closer. But "Tie yours to Him" is still soft and ambiguous about what the promise is. A line that names Jesus' promise or blood would land harder.

Both scores are under 8, so the verdict is REVISE.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Rebuild the hook around the scarlet cord and the woman with a bad name, and drop the "Jericho was a walled city" textbook open. Also fix "in silence" (priests blew trumpets; the people were silent until the shout) and the cord's order. Let the spies give the cord command before it is shown.
2. State plainly that the cord marked her house to be spared, then connect it to the Passover blood or Jesus' blood with one clear line, so the Christ link has a reason and isn't just asserted.
3. Sharpen the landing so "Tie yours to Him" names what He promised, and change "saved for trusting" to "saved by trusting" so the grace framing stays intact. Cut the "spelled Rachab" aside.
