# Independent review — codex (OK, 53s)

**Hook: 4/10.** “Jericho was a walled city, and God told His army to march around it” opens with background information. The distinctive image—the scarlet cord in a threatened wall—arrives after the marching schedule. The first five seconds provide little reason for a stranger to stop.

**Landing: 7/10.** “She tied her hope to a promise. Tie yours to Him.” The tying verb belongs to this piece, so this avoids the stock-closer cap. But the metaphor carries more weight than the explanation: a newcomer has not been told what Jesus promises or how His death grounds that hope. It sounds finished before the gospel connection is established.

1. **The central Christ connection rests on an unestablished interpretation.** “Many see a picture here: a scarlet cord at her window, and the blood of Jesus on the cross.” Neither the cited Joshua account nor the cited New Testament passages identifies the cord as a symbol of Christ’s blood. “Many see” appropriately signals interpretation, but the narration still makes that association its climactic discovery. Under your requirement against contested readings used for freshness, this is the principal doctrinal weakness. The explicit connections—Rahab’s faith and her place in Jesus’ genealogy—provide firmer ground. [Cited passages, KJV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Joshua+2%3B+6%3A16-25%3B+Matthew+1%3A5%3B+James+2%3A25%3B+Hebrews+11%3A31&version=KJV)

2. **“Saved for trusting God’s promise” compresses two different matters.** The immediate rescue is from Jericho’s destruction; the ending invites trust in Christ. The script needs to explain that movement. Also, the protection arrangement is an oath made by the spies, grounded in their allegiance to the Lord; the narration calls it “God’s promise” without establishing that distinction. Hebrews explicitly attributes Rahab’s deliverance to faith, but does not make her rescue a general promise of physical safety to believers. [Joshua 2; Hebrews 11:31, KJV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Joshua+2%3BHebrews+11%3A31&version=KJV)

3. **Several phrases obstruct first-hearing clarity.** “His army” and “God’s two spies” never identify Israel or explain why Jericho is being approached. “A woman with a bad name” suggests reputation or gossip, then the unexplained “harlot” introduces archaic vocabulary. “Spelled Rachab” addresses a visual spelling issue in spoken narration, while “Salmon begat Booz of Rachab” adds three unfamiliar names and archaic syntax without explaining the relationship.

4. **The thread takes a detour at its strongest opportunity.** “Matthew lists her in Jesus’ own family line” could deepen the movement from outsider to gracious inclusion. Instead, the script immediately leaves that development for the scarlet/blood association. The result is two competing routes to Jesus: genealogy and colour symbolism.

5. **“In silence” overstates the soundscape.** The people were commanded to withhold their voices, but the priests blew trumpets during the march. For an unfamiliar listener, “in silence” naturally implies a silent procession. Specify whose silence is meant. [Joshua 6:8–10, KJV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Joshua+6%3A8-10&version=KJV)

**KJV verbatim:** All four quoted excerpts contain exact, consecutive KJV wording. No altered words found; excerpting verses is not itself a quotation error. [Joshua 2:18; Matthew 1:5; Hebrews 11:31](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Joshua+2%3A18%3BMatthew+1%3A5%3BHebrews+11%3A31&version=KJV), [Joshua 6:20](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Joshua+6%3A20&version=KJV).

**Grace framing:** No explicit threat, bargain, or works requirement appears. “Not saved for being good” rejects merit. The weakness is that Christ’s gracious action remains underexplained while the listener’s act of trusting receives the final emphasis.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Ground the Christ connection in explicit biblical teaching rather than making scarlet-cord symbolism carry the climax.
2. Clarify the protection oath, physical rescue, and transition to Christ’s saving work.
3. Bring the distinctive image into the hook and give the tying-based invitation a clearly explained gospel foundation.
