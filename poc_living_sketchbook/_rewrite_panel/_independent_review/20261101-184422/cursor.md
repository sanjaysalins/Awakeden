## Independent adversarial review — Jericho candidate B (Sonnet)

### Doctrinal soundness
- **Mostly orthodox:** faith-not-merit (“Not saved for being good. Saved for trusting God's promise.”), Rahab in Christ’s lineage, and typology hedged with **“Many see a picture here”** — not stated as explicit Scripture.
- **Works-adjacent sequencing:** **“She hid God's two spies, and she believed in Him”** sits immediately before the rescue and the Hebrews line. A listener with no Bible background can easily read the story as *saved because she helped the spies*, then hear the narrator deny that only one beat later. The theology is right; the **story order fights it**.
- **“Saved for trusting God's promise”** — Joshua 2 is a **sign + stay-in-house** arrangement as much as a bare “promise.” Not heresy, but slightly **smooths the text** toward a generic faith slogan.
- **Scarlet cord → blood of Jesus:** With **“Many see a picture here”** you avoid inventing doctrine, but it is still **standard sermon typology** — low risk, also low “faithful depth” surprise.

### Clarity on first hearing
- **“a woman with a bad name”** then **“the harlot Rahab”** — same woman, two registers; some listeners will not connect **“bad name”** to **harlot** before the quote lands.
- **“and she believed in Him”** — on audio, **Him** is weak after **“God's two spies”**; many will not catch *faith in the God of Israel* without one plain phrase (e.g. that she believed **what God was about to do**).
- **“perished not with them that believed not”** — KJV-faithful but **hard to parse in one pass** (who “believed not,” and why that matters). This is a clarity hit, not a doctrine hit.
- **“Salmon begat Booz of Rachab”** — **“spelled Rachab”** helps; **Booz / begat** still lands as jargon for a zero-Bible audience unless you add one plain clause (*she became an ancestor of Jesus*).

### Grace-anchored conviction
- **“Not saved for being good. Saved for trusting God's promise.”** — invitation-shaped, not fear or merit ledger. Good.
- **“Tie yours to Him.”** — imperative, but tied to **“She tied her hope to a promise”**; not **“come to Jesus”** stock. Slight risk of sounding like **do something to secure hope** unless **Him** is clearly the promiser, not the cord.

### Freshness = faithful depth
- B drops the earlier draft’s invented scale (**“strongest walls,” “one window stood”**) — better grounding.
- What’s left that’s “fresh” is mostly **compressed factual Joshua** plus **familiar Rahab–scarlet–blood** — faithful, not very surprising for anyone who’s heard one Jericho/Rahab message.

### One thread spine
- **Scarlet cord → Rahab’s faith → walls fall / she’s spared → Hebrews → Matthew → typology → tie hope to Him.** No major thread swap; typology is the same thread, not a new topic.

### Hook score: **5/10**
**“Jericho was a walled city, and God told His army to march around it, in silence.”** — Not a template **“Did you know”** opener, but it reads like a **slow Bible-summary cold open**. The strange detail (silent march) is not in the first clause; a stranger may not feel tension in the first ~5 seconds. Familiar Jericho territory caps impact.

### Landing score: **7/10**
**“She tied her hope to a promise. Tie yours to Him.”** — Built from this piece’s **tie / hope / promise** language, not a naked **“turn to Jesus.”** Still short and portable: the same line could close other **faith-object** shorts without the scarlet window. Not capped at 5, but **not ≥8**.
