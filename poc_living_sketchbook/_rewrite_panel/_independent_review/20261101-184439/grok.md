# Independent review — grok (OK, 166s)

The two-goat picture is orthodox. The veil line is not, and two scores sit under 8.

**Hook: 6/10.** The first words are “I am Aaron, the first high priest of Israel.” That is a name and a job title. “Alone behind the veil” adds a picture, and “carrying blood for the sins of the people” arrives after the five-second window. A stranger has little reason to stop.

**Landing: 6/10.** “The veil is torn, and Jesus is the way in. Walk in.” uses this piece’s veil, so it is not the stock “Come to Jesus.” It still drops the piece’s own verbs, “paid” and “carried.” “Walk in” could close any access short, and it never says into what.

**KJV quotes that are exact.** “the LORD hath laid on him the iniquity of us all.” “by his own blood.” “sat down.” “was rent in twain from the top to the bottom.” No word inside those marks is altered.

**Doctrinal / historical.** “The veil I stood behind ‘was rent in twain from the top to the bottom’ when He died” makes Aaron’s tabernacle curtain the cloth that tore at the cross. Aaron’s veil and the temple veil are not the same object. The quote is exact. The mouth that speaks it invents the history. A first-time hearer will believe his curtain ripped.

“Hebrews says He went in ‘by his own blood,’ once for ever, and ‘sat down.’” “Once for ever” is not in quotes, and it is not a KJV phrase. Hebrews says he entered “once” (Hebrews 9:12) and that he offered one sacrifice “for ever” and then “sat down” (Hebrews 10:12). “Once for all” (Hebrews 10:10) is a different wording. The sentence glues three moments into one and sounds like Scripture.

“Sat down” is verbatim and still empty on a first hear. The text’s point is that other priests stand daily because the work never ends, and he sits because it is done. None of that is said. A newcomer hears that Jesus took a seat.

“I was the sign. He is the real thing.” The picture just built is the two goats. This line moves the sign onto Aaron. Hebrews does treat the high priest as a shadow, but the sentence swaps the thread at the worst moment.

Smaller thinning: “carrying blood for the sins of the people” skips the bull for Aaron’s own sins (Leviticus 16:6, 11; Hebrews 9:7). That gap is how the text shows he is not the final priest. Lots for the two goats are also skipped. Neither claim is false. Both are thin.

**Clarity.** “Veil,” “sin offering,” “iniquity,” and “rent in twain” are never unpacked in the moment. “The veil is torn” arrives only at the end, so “rent” can pass as noise. “Why two? Together they made one picture” is clear, and “One goat paid with its blood. One goat carried the sin away” is the line a newcomer can repeat. “He paid, and He carried” is faithful to Isaiah 53:6 and to the live goat that bears sin into “a land not inhabited.”

**Grace.** No hell-threat, no loss frame, no works ladder. “Walk in” invites. It does not say the way in is his blood, so it is slightly bare, not a works gospel.

**Spine.** Hook is the veiled room. Middle is two goats, pay and carry. Close is the torn veil. Those belong to one Day of Atonement day, then the last beat leaves “paid” and “carried” behind. “Walk in” is a second ending.

**Freshness.** “Why two?” is a real surprise in the text, and the landing on Christ who both pays and bears is orthodox. The fresh part is not what the last sentence spends.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Stop saying the veil Aaron stood behind “was rent” at the cross. Keep the KJV line on the temple veil, and say his curtain was the earlier picture.
2. Cut or unpack “once for ever” and “sat down.” Quote a full KJV clause, and say in plain words that the work is finished so he sits.
3. Land on this piece’s own verbs. The last line should carry “paid” and “carried” through the torn veil, so it cannot end a different short.
