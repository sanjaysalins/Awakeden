# Independent review — codex (OK, 52s)

**Hook: 4/10.** “I am Aaron, the first high priest of Israel” opens with identification and religious office. A stranger has no reason yet to care. The actual tension—one goat dies, another carries sins away—arrives after the opening five seconds.

**Landing: 5/10.** “Walk in” recalls the veil, but abandons the distinctive two-goat image. That exact sentence could close numerous doorway or temple pieces, so the stock-closer cap applies. A newcomer also cannot tell what action it requests: trust Christ, pray, enter a church, or something else.

Concrete findings:

1. **Historical conflation:** “The veil I stood behind … was rent in twain” identifies Aaron’s tabernacle curtain with the temple veil torn at Jesus’ death. These were different curtains in different structures, centuries apart. The quotation is accurate; its attachment to Aaron’s particular veil is inaccurate. See [Leviticus 16:15–16](https://www.bible.com/bible/1/LEV.16.KJV) and [Matthew 27:51](https://biblia.com/bible/kjv1900/matthew/27/51).

2. **The strongest doctrinal risk is “One goat paid with its blood.”** This assigns accomplished payment to the animal before explaining the symbolism. “My two goats only pictured” subsequently qualifies it, but listeners must revise what they just heard. Hebrews explicitly denies that animal blood can take away sins. Distinguish the appointed sacrificial sign from Christ’s effective accomplishment at the first assertion. [Hebrews 10:4, 11–14](https://www.bible.com/bible/1/HEB.10.KJV)

3. **The spine changes during the final third.** “He paid, and He carried” completes the two-goat argument. Then “sat down,” “I was the sign,” and “the way in” introduce completed priestly work, Aaron’s typology, and access through the veil. These are related doctrines, but the narration does not establish their connections clearly enough for one hearing. The closing answers *how we enter*, after the middle has emphasized *how sin is borne away*.

4. **The novice listener must decode too much.** “High priest,” “veil,” “sin offering,” and “iniquity” accumulate without sufficient explanation. “Sat down” is particularly opaque: without its context, sitting communicates posture rather than a completed sacrifice. [Hebrews 10:11–12](https://www.bible.com/bible/1/HEB.10.KJV)

5. **Grace framing needs a clearer response, not stronger pressure.** There is no explicit fear, reward bargain, or demand to earn acceptance. However, “Walk in” leaves faith unstated and the destination undefined. Hebrews grounds drawing near to God in Jesus’ blood and explicitly names faith; the CTA needs that intelligibility. [Hebrews 10:19–22](https://www.bible.com/bible/1/HEB.10.KJV)

**KJV check:** No altered words in the four quoted excerpts: [Isaiah 53:6](https://www.bible.com/bible/1/ISA.53.6.KJV), [Hebrews 9:12](https://biblia.com/bible/kjv/hebrews/9/12), Hebrews 10:12, and Matthew 27:51. “Once for ever” is outside quotation marks; it is not a verbatim-quotation violation.

**Freshness:** “Together they were one sin offering” has textual grounding in Leviticus 16:5. The problem is imprecise exposition and a divided landing, rather than a need for a more novel doctrine.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Correct the Aaron/temple-veil conflation and distinguish animal symbolism from Christ’s actual payment.
2. Carry the two-goat thread through a concrete, faith-explicit closing invitation.
3. Put the textual tension in the opening five seconds and explain essential religious terms.
