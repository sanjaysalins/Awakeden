# Independent review — claude (OK, 42s)

**Review of two_goats candidate A_fable**

**Hook: 5/10.** "I am Aaron, the first high priest of Israel" is a name-and-title introduction.
- A stranger gets a résumé, not a stake.
- "Why two?" is the real hook, and it arrives about 80 words (~30s) in.
- Nothing before it creates tension or curiosity.

**Landing: 6/10.** "The veil is torn, and Jesus is the way in. Walk in." does use this piece's image, so it isn't a stock closer.
- It drops the goat spine. The whole piece builds on "paid" and "carried," but the landing switches to the veil.
- "Walk in" has no object. Into what? God's presence is never named.
- It never says where the listener's own sin goes.

**KJV verbatim.** I found no altered words.
- Isaiah 53:6, "the LORD hath laid on him the iniquity of us all," is exact.
- Matthew 27:51, "was rent in twain from the top to the bottom," is exact.
- Hebrews 9:12, "by his own blood," and Hebrews 10:12, "sat down," are exact.
- "once for ever" is outside the quote marks. It is a blend: 9:12 has "once," 10:12 has "for ever," and 10:10 has "once for all." It reads as quotation when spoken, so either cite it or paraphrase it.
- "Hebrews says He went in 'by his own blood'… and 'sat down'" stitches 9:12 and 10:12 together as if they were one sentence.

**Doctrine.** It is mostly sound and gospel-centered. Specific concerns:
- **Voice logic:** Aaron cites Isaiah and Hebrews ("Long after me… Hebrews says"). He lived long before either was written. A newcomer will find this odd, and a Bible reader will notice the anachronism.
- **Veil mismatch:** "The veil I stood behind 'was rent'" blends Aaron's tabernacle veil with the Matthew temple veil. It is a defensible type link, but it is stated as if it were the same cloth.
- **Missing human need:** "One goat paid with its blood" leaves out Hebrews 9:7 and 10:4. Aaron offered for himself first, and goat blood could never actually take sin away. "Only pictured" partly covers this, but "paid" overstates it.
- **Contested reading:** the second goat (Azazel) as a type of Christ carrying sin is mainstream. The text names the second lot "for Azazel," and the script asserts a tidy one-picture answer with no hedge.
- **Missed link:** the laying on of hands (Lev 16:21) is the best visual match for Isaiah's "laid on him." The script never connects them.

**Clarity on first hearing.**
- "Veil," "sin offering," "iniquity," and "sat down" are unexplained. A newcomer never learns that the veil walls God off from people.
- "Why two? Together they made one picture" gives the answer before the picture has been shown.
- "Hebrews says" assumes the listener knows Hebrews is a book of the Bible.

**Grace-anchored.** There is no fear, gain/loss, or works framing. The bare imperative "Walk in." is slightly abrupt, but it reads as an invitation.

**Freshness.** The two-goats-as-one-picture idea is fresh and faithful. The payoff is weakened because the listener is never named. "Us all" is the only second-person hook, and the listener's own sin is never mentioned.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Rebuild the hook so the opening carries the stake, such as one goat killed and one sent away alive. Put "Why two?" in the first ~8 seconds. Drop "I am Aaron, the first high priest" as the opener.
2. Make the landing keep the goat spine. Tie Isaiah's "laid on him" to Aaron's hands laid on the goat, and say the listener's sin was carried away. Give "walk in" an object, such as into God's presence. Define "veil" in plain words when it first appears.
3. Fix the voice and quote logic. Explain or drop Aaron's anachronistic knowledge of Hebrews and Isaiah. Cite "once for ever" properly or paraphrase it. Add a plain gloss for "sat down" (finished). Add a line that goat blood only pictured payment.
