# Independent review — claude (OK, 49s)

**Review of candidate A_fable (bronze serpent narration)**

**KJV check.** All four quotes match the KJV text.
- Num 21:7 is "We have sinned, for we have spoken against the LORD". It is clipped mid-verse, which is acceptable.
- Num 21:8 is "every one that is bitten, when he looketh upon it, shall live".
- John 3:14–15 is quoted exactly.
- Gal 3:13 is "made a curse for us". The full phrase is "being made a curse for us", so the clip is fine.

No altered words.

**Doctrine.** It is sound and has no contested readings.
- Numbers 21 and John 3 are linked directly, as Jesus himself linked them.
- "Sin bites us all, and the cure is not in you" is grace-anchored.
- "To look at Jesus is to believe in Him" restates John 3 and does not turn looking into a work.
- There is no fear or gain/loss framing.

Two small points:
- "like the ones that bit them" is a gloss. The text says the serpent was made "of brass" and nothing more.
- "My serpent of brass was only a shadow" has Moses use a Hebrews/Colossians category he would not have used. It is orthodox, but the "only" slightly belittles a God-commanded sign.

**Clarity on first hearing.**
- *Moses narrates from outside time.* "Long after me, Jesus said…" and "Paul the apostle wrote…" make Moses a time-traveling narrator. A newcomer has to absorb the shift while four names pile up (Moses, Nicodemus, Jesus, Paul).
- *"Paul the apostle" appears from nowhere.* The listener has no idea who he is, so "made a curse for us" lands cold.
- *"made a curse for us" is not unpacked.* The thing that makes this piece fresh is never cashed in: the cure *looks like the curse* (a serpent on a pole, and Christ made a curse). The line "like the ones that bit them" is planted, but the piece never connects it to "made a curse." That is the missed payoff.
- *Ambiguous "it."* "It was lifted up where you can see it" could mean the cure or the serpent. The pivot from "Sin bites us all" to "the cure is not in you" is also abrupt.

**Thread.** It is one spine (look → live) from start to finish, with no thread-swapping. This is good.

**HOOK: 5/10.**
- "I am Moses. In the wilderness, serpents bit my people, and they were dying." is a flat identity-plus-setting open.
- It states the situation but gives no surprise or open question.
- The real hook is that God refused to remove the serpents and instead commanded a snake on a pole. That arrives about 25 words in and is not used as the opener.
- A stranger has no reason to stay past 5 seconds.

**LANDING: 7/10.**
- "Look, and live." is built from the piece's own image and verb (Num 21:8 "looketh… shall live").
- It is short and memorable.
- It is not a stock closer, but "look to Jesus" is a close cousin of the generic family.
- It is not tied to the paradox of the cure looking like the curse, so it could nearly end any cross-looking piece.
- The line before it, "To look at Jesus is to believe in Him," is explanatory and flattens the build.

Both scores are under 8.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Rebuild the hook around the strange fact (God refuses to take the serpents away and commands a snake on a pole), not "I am Moses… serpents bit my people." Open on the paradox, within the first 5 seconds.
2. Cash in the paradox the piece plants. Link "a serpent of brass, like the ones that bit them" to "made a curse for us" in one plain line: the cure looked like the curse. Introduce Paul in a few words ("a later follower, Paul") or drop his name and quote Gal 3:13 attributed more simply. Remove the time-traveling Moses voice, or limit it to one clean handoff.
3. Make the landing earned and the pivot clean. Replace "It was lifted up where you can see it" with an unambiguous referent. Cut or fold the explanatory "To look at Jesus is to believe in Him." Let "Look, and live" land after a concrete image from the piece, so it cannot end a different episode.
