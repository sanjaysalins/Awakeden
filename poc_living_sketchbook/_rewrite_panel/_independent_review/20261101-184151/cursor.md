## Independent adversarial review — `jericho candidate A_fable`

### Doctrinal soundness
- **Mostly orthodox:** faith-not-merit (**“Not for being good. For believing.”**), Rahab spared by faith (Hebrews 11), genealogy to Christ (Matthew 1), scarlet→blood framed as reader typology (**“Christians read that cord as a picture of His blood”**) rather than as KJV fact — appropriate hedge.
- **No invented spectacle** (unlike older Jericho drafts): no “strongest walls in the world,” no “one window stood.” That helps fidelity.
- **Mild softening:** **“A prostitute, who hid God's spies, and believed.”** hides the spies and faith in one breath; Joshua’s point is faith *shown* in receiving the spies (Hebrews 11:31 continues **“when she had received the spies with peace”**). Not heresy, but the **object** of faith (God’s word through the spies) is thinner than the text.
- **Typology is standard, not fresh:** scarlet cord → blood is sermon-catalog material; **“Christians read…”** keeps it honest but doesn’t deepen the *text* much beyond what many listeners already assume.

### Clarity on first hearing
- **Strong:** walled city, silent march, trumpets, shout, walls fall, Rahab spared — followable with no Bible background.
- **Weak spots:**
  - **“and all inside live”** — telegraphic, not spoken English; a new listener may not connect this to the spies’ house marker (Joshua 2:18–19).
  - **“Salmon begat Booz of Rachab.”** — **“Booz”** and **“begat”** are opaque on audio even after **“Rachab is Rahab.”** (that line helps; the genealogy quote still lands as jargon).
  - **“Rahab lived there. A prostitute, who hid…”** — staccato fragment; sounds like a caption, not a sentence.
- **Omission (not false, but thin):** seventh day in Joshua is **seven** circuits, not “one lap” like the prior six days. You don’t claim “one lap” on day seven, but you also never signal the intensification; the fall can feel abrupt after a very compressed siege.

### Grace-anchored conviction
- **Sound:** **“Not for being good. For believing.”** avoids merit and fear.
- **Watch:** **“She could only hang a cord, and trust the promise.”** is narratively right (sign + trust), but paired with **“Trust Jesus like that”** the *physical* cord act can blur into “do something like Rahab did” unless trust stays clearly **in His word**, not in the symbol. One clarifying beat (*trust the promise the cord pointed to*) would tighten grace.

### Freshness = faithful depth
- Best original move: **“Rahab could not hold up a wall. She could only hang a cord, and trust the promise.”** — surprising in *role* (powerless woman vs army), orthodox in claim.
- Weaker: blood typology is expected; the piece doesn’t mine a sharper textual detail (e.g., Gentile in Messiah’s line, faith vs doomed city language from Hebrews’ full verse).

### One thread spine
- **Single spine holds:** cord in window → faith/sparing → Rachab in Jesus’ line → scarlet as blood (typology) → trust/promise. No major thread swap.
