# Independent review — codex (OK, 65s)

**Hook: 4/10.** “Jericho was a walled city, shut against God's people” opens as background information. It supplies neither a compelling question nor the piece’s distinctive tension. The red cord arrives after several setup sentences; a stranger must wait for the interesting image.

**Landing: 4/10.** “Trust Jesus like that. His promise holds” could close numerous Bible narrations. “Holds” faintly recalls the wall, but the final sentence neither completes the cord image nor identifies what Jesus promises.

The substantive problems:

1. **The cord-to-blood connection carries more weight than the text establishes.** “Christians read that cord as a picture of His blood” identifies an interpretation, but then makes it the bridge to the invitation. Neither Joshua’s account nor the cited New Testament passages identifies the cord with Christ’s blood. Under your strict standard against contested readings carrying the message, this is an insecure foundation. Distinguish a devotional analogy from the passage’s explicit teaching. [Joshua 2; Hebrews 11:31; Matthew 1:5](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Joshua+2%3BHebrews+11%3A31%3BMatthew+1%3A5&version=KJV)

2. **“She could only hang a cord, and trust the promise” overstates her passivity.** She also sheltered the spies, helped them escape, and gathered her household. Hebrews connects her faith with receiving the spies; James explicitly discusses her actions. “Not for being good. For believing” can communicate salvation without merit, but “could only” risks making obedient action irrelevant. The distinction needed is between earning rescue and acting from faith. [Joshua 2; Hebrews 11:31; James 2:25](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Joshua+2%3BHebrews+11%3A31%3BJames+2%3A25&version=KJV)

3. **The invitation leaves its essential content unspecified.** “Trust Jesus like that” transfers a promise of physical preservation during conquest into Christian faith without explaining the connection. “His promise holds” leaves a Bible newcomer asking: which promise? Forgiveness, acceptance, physical protection? Mentioning “His blood” supplies Christian vocabulary without explaining Christ’s saving work. This is the largest first-hearing clarity problem.

4. **The middle branches instead of deepening one thread.** “The woman with the red cord became family to Jesus” introduces belonging and ancestry as a possible destination. The narration immediately leaves that destination for blood symbolism, then returns to trusting a rescue promise. These connections are related, but none receives enough development to make the landing feel inevitable.

5. **The wall creates an unresolved visual problem.** “From one window in that wall” followed by “the wall fell down flat” naturally raises the question of how that household survived. “Rahab could not hold up a wall” brings that physical puzzle back into focus without resolving it.

**KJV check:** All four quotations contain exact KJV words in their original sequence. No altered word found; they are excerpts rather than complete verses. [KJV passages](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Joshua+2%3A18%3BJoshua+6%3A20%3BHebrews+11%3A31%3BMatthew+1%3A5&version=KJV)

**Grace framing:** There is no explicit threat or bargain directed at the listener. The weakness is an underexplained gospel invitation, rather than overt fear or works pressure.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Ground the invitation in explicit biblical teaching and identify what Jesus promises.
2. Remove the absolute “could only”; distinguish faith’s obedience from earning salvation.
3. Build the hook and closing from one sustained image, eliminating the genealogy-to-blood-to-promise detour.
