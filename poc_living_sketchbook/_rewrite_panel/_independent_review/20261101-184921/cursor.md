## Independent adversarial review — `bronze_serpent` candidate B_sonnet

### Doctrinal soundness
- **Core typology is standard and orthodox:** wilderness serpent → look → live; Jesus’s **“lifted up”** (John 3:14–15); substitution language via Galatians 3:13. No contrarian readings, no works-salvation pitch.
- **Galatians is compressed in a way that can mis-teach by omission:** You quote **“Christ hath redeemed us from the curse of the law, being made a curse for us”** but drop the immediate KJV glue **“for it is written, Cursed is every one that hangeth on a tree.”** Without that line (or plain paraphrase), a listener with no Bible background may not know *why* Galatians appears after **“Jesus was lifted up on a cross”** — the **curse ↔ cross** link is assumed, not shown. Not heresy, but **doctrine-by-skip** for the stated audience.
- **Moses-as-omniscient-narrator:** **“Many years later, Jesus told a man named Nicodemus”** and **“Galatians says”** are true, but **not** something wilderness Moses would say in voice. That is a **dramatic conceit**, not false doctrine — yet it blurs *who is speaking* at the exact moment you apply the story (**“So hear me, you who are bitten”**).

### KJV verbatim (quoted spans only)
- **Numbers 21:7 (partial):** **“We have sinned, for we have spoken against the LORD, and against thee”** — word match to KJV for this excerpt (KJV continues with **“; pray unto the LORD…”**; truncation is fine if intentional).
- **John 3:14–15:** **“And as Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, even so must the Son of man be lifted up: That whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have eternal life.”** — matches project KJV (including **“Son of man”** and lowercase **“him”**).
- **Galatians 3:13 (partial):** **“Christ hath redeemed us from the curse of the law, being made a curse for us”** — verbatim for the span quoted.
- **No altered KJV words inside quotation marks.**  
- **Header says “KJV quotes”** but most of the Numbers pole account is **unquoted paraphrase** (**“make a serpent of brass, and put it on a pole”** vs KJV **“Make thee a fiery serpent, and set it upon a pole”**). Acceptable in narrator prose; flag only because delivery may sound like Scripture when it is not.

### Clarity on first hearing
- **Wilderness arc is clear:** dying → pole → look → live → **“Nothing else was asked of them.”**
- **Christ link is clearer than many Moses POV drafts** (Nicodemus is named; John 3:15 is included — fixes the “look without believe” risk).
- **Weak spots for zero Bible knowledge:**
  - **“That brass serpent was only a shadow”** — *shadow of what?* No plain noun (Christ, the cross, the Son) in that sentence; the next sentence jumps to Jesus, but **“shadow”** is insider vocabulary.
  - **“So hear me, you who are bitten. That is every one of us.”** The metaphor works if bite = sin is already felt; you never say **sin** or **death** explicitly, only **bitten** and **curse of the law** (which is unexplained).
  - **“Believe in Him, and you will live”** echoes John’s **eternal life** in paraphrase; **“you will live”** can sound like **the snakebite cure** (physical stay-alive) rather than **eternal life**, because the closing line does not repeat **“eternal life”** from the quote you just read.

### Grace-anchored conviction
- **Strong:** **“Nothing else was asked of them,”** **“The cure is not in you,”** **“It hangs in plain sight”** — invitation and external remedy, not self-cure or works.
- **No obvious fear/gain-loss CTA** (“or else,” “before it’s too late”).
- **Mild judgment weight:** **“curse of the law”** without unpacking can sound like **legal condemnation** landing on the listener before the believe line; not works-framing, but emotionally heavier than the serpent story alone.

### Freshness = faithful depth
- **Faithful, not gimmicky:** the strange cure (**look at the image of the thing killing you**) is the text’s own offense; you do not invent a novel doctrine.
- **Freshness is moderate:** bronze → lifted Son → believe is **the** standard John 3 sermon path; naming Nicodemus helps, but you do not mine an overlooked detail (e.g. fiery serpent, pole vs cross, Nehushtan later destroyed) — acceptable for a short panel, not a hooky contrarian take.

### One thread spine (hook → middle → CTA)
- **Spine holds:** bite → brass on pole → look/live → Jesus lifted → believe/live.
- **Galatians is a short parallel lane** (curse/redemption) that **supports** the cross beat rather than swapping threads; still a **second Pauline register** after John, which costs a beat of simplicity.

### Hook score: **7/10**
- **Not a template open** (no “Did you know / Imagine…” cap).
- **“I am Moses. My people were dying of snakebite, one after another.”** is concrete and urgent in the first ~5 seconds.
- **Not a 9–10:** **“I am Moses”** asks immediate buy-in to a persona; the **irony of the brass serpent** arrives a sentence later, not in the opening breath.

### Landing score: **5/10** (stock-closer cap applies)
- **“Look to Jesus, lifted up for you”** is the project’s own **stock-shaped look-CTA** pattern (`look to Jesus`); your quoted KJV blocks use **“looketh upon it”** (Numbers, unquoted paraphrase) and **“believeth”** (John), not **“look to Jesus.”** The pole/brass image is gone from the last two sentences — only **lifted up** remains.
- **“Believe in Him, and you will live.”** — **Could this exact final sentence end a different piece?** **Yes.** Any faith-invite short could close on **believe … live**; it is not locked to bronze, pole, or bitten camp.
- Rubric: **under 8 on landing → REVISE** (and **≤5** if treated as stock **“look to Jesus”** family).
