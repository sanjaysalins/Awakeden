**FINDINGS & LENS ANALYSIS**

- **Doctrinal Soundness & Clarity on first hearing**: You have a fatal perspective break. You open with "I am Moses," establishing a first-person historical conceit. But then Moses says, "Long after me, Jesus said..." and "Paul the apostle wrote..." Moses cannot quote the New Testament in the past tense. This shatters the framing device, breaks immersion, and sounds absurd. You must either drop the "I am Moses" gimmick entirely or transition to an omniscient narrator.

- **One thread spine**: PASS. The theme of being bitten, the cure being lifted up, and the act of "looking" runs from start to finish.

It is a slow, dry historical recounting that lacks any viral tension, irony, or mystery to stop a scroller. You completely missed the actual hook of this passage: that God's cure looked exactly like the monster that was killing them.
- **SCORE THE LANDING: 8/10**. "Sin bites us all... Look, and live." This is strong. It perfectly repurposes the historical mechanism (look at the serpent) into the spiritual CTA (look at Christ) without relying on stock phrases. 

Because the hook is under 8, the KJV is misquoted, and the narrative perspective collapses halfway through, this must be revised.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Fix the broken narrative perspective: either drop the "I am Moses" gimmick and use a standard narrator, or don't have Moses quoting Paul and Jesus in the past tense. 
2. Rewrite the hook to grab attention immediately using the irony of the passage (e.g., the cure looked like the killer), rather than a slow, dry character introduction.
3. Correct the KJV truncation in Numbers 21:7 to include "and against thee" or remove the quotation marks to paraphrase.
