# Independent review — codex (OK, 56s)

**Hook: 5/10.** “I am Moses. In the wilderness, serpents bit my people” starts with an identity a Bible newcomer may not recognize. The danger supplies interest, but the opening does not yet expose the story’s arresting question: why would looking at a serpent bring life?

**Landing: 5/10.** “Look, and live” follows this piece’s image, but it could also close another narration about faith or healing. Under your portability rule, it earns the stock-closer cap. “To look at Jesus is to believe in Him” explains the metaphor only by substituting another term a newcomer may not understand.

Specific findings:

1. **“Whoever was bitten looked at it, and lived” overstates the account.** This says every bitten person looked and survived. Scripture makes survival conditional on looking; it does not say everyone bitten did so. This is a substantive narrative accuracy problem. [Numbers 21:9, KJV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Numbers+21%3A5-9&version=KJV)

2. **“God answered, but not by taking the serpents away” presents an inference as an explicit fact.** The passage records God providing the serpent on the pole; it does not explicitly state that he never removed the serpents. The contrast risks making the piece’s freshest observation depend on more certainty than the text supplies. [Numbers 21:7–9, KJV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Numbers+21%3A5-9&version=KJV)

3. **“I am Moses” becomes an unexplained retrospective conceit.** “Long after me, Jesus said” and “Paul the apostle wrote” require Moses to narrate events after his earthly lifetime. That is a dramatic device, but an uninformed listener receives no signal distinguishing imagined narration from biblical testimony.

4. **“It was lifted up where you can see it” blurs the object and the invitation.** Grammatically, “it” refers to “the cure,” making Jesus sound like an object. “Where you can see it” also suggests present physical visibility. The next sentence partially clarifies this, but the listener must repair the meaning while listening.

5. **The grace anchor is present but underexplained.** “He took our judgment, in our place” supplies substitution; “the cure is not in you” rejects self-salvation. Nevertheless, the final movement remains organized around personal danger and obtaining life. Under your strict brief, God’s gracious initiative needs more explanatory weight. There is no explicit works demand or coercive threat; the problem is emphasis and clarity.

6. **The spine weakens around “made a curse for us.”** The quotation introduces law-and-curse language without explaining its relationship to the serpent image. “He took our judgment” helps, but a newcomer still has to connect bite, brass serpent, curse, cross, and faith in rapid succession.

**KJV audit:** No altered words inside the quotation marks. The confession ends before “and against thee”; that is a shortened excerpt, not a substituted wording. The Numbers 21:8 excerpt, John 3:14–15 quotation, and “made a curse for us” are word-exact. [Numbers](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Numbers+21%3A5-9&version=KJV), [John](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John+3%3A14-16&version=KJV), [Galatians](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Galatians+3%3A13&version=KJV)

The serpent-to-Christ connection itself is explicitly grounded in Jesus’ words; it is not an invented or contrarian interpretation. The principal doctrinal problems are narrative overstatement and insufficient explanation.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Correct the universal claim about bitten people and qualify the unsupported claim about the serpents remaining.
2. Resolve the retrospective Moses conceit and clarify how looking means trusting Christ’s gracious provision.
3. Strengthen the opening and make the closing invitation inseparable from this particular serpent-and-cross account.
