# Independent review — grok (OK, 205s)

The KJV lines inside the quotes match the text. The spine is one thread: bite, pole, look, live, Jesus lifted up, look and believe. The grace line invites. Hook and landing both sit under 8, so this is a revise.

**Hook: 7.** The first words are "I am Moses." A stranger does not know that name, so the first second is spent on an insider. "In the wilderness, serpents bit my people, and they were dying" is a real picture, and it is already over. The strange thing in this text, a serpent set up to cure serpent bites, is not in the first five seconds.

**Landing: 5.** The last sentence is "Look, and live." Those two verbs come from this story. That same sentence can still end a different film, any "look to Jesus" short or an Isaiah 45 short. The serpent, the pole, and the bite are gone from the final line. That is a stock closer, so the cap is 5.

**The condition is flattened.** "Whoever was bitten looked at it, and lived." Numbers 21:9 is a when: if a serpent had bitten any man, when he beheld the serpent of brass, he lived. The script turns that when into "everyone who was bitten did look." The later call, "Look, and live," needs the look to stay a real response.

**First-hear snags.**
- "They knew why, and came to me" uses "why" before the sin is said.
- "My serpent of brass was only a shadow." One line earlier, looking really healed them. "Shadow" is insider speech for a newcomer.
- "a man named Nicodemus" adds a name with no job in the story.
- "made a curse for us" is the hard phrase. "He took our judgment, in our place" is the plain gloss, and it is the right one.
- "To look at Jesus is to believe in Him" can sound like staring at a picture. The look in the story is the picture of believing.

**Doctrine.** John 3:14-15 as Jesus' words, the brass serpent as the picture, Christ lifted up, judgment borne in our place, look as believe: that follows the text. "God answered, but not by taking the serpents away" states an inference as a fact. The chapter records the pole as the answer. It never says the snakes stayed.

**Grace.** "The cure is not in you" points outward. "Look, and live" invites. The conviction sits on the bitten people and on "sin bites us all," then the script opens the look.

**Freshness.** The real surprise is already in the text: the cure was "a serpent of brass, like the ones that bit them," and Jesus was "made a curse for us." That likeness gets one clause, then the landing drops it.

VERDICT: REVISE
TOP FIXES:
1. Rebuild the last sentence on this piece's own image (serpent, pole, bite) so "Look, and live" is no longer a closer any other film could use.
2. Put the strange cure, a serpent raised against the serpents, inside the first five seconds, and let "I am Moses" wait.
3. Change "Whoever was bitten looked at it, and lived" back to the condition: when the bitten person looked, he lived.
