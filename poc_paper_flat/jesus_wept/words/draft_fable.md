# Jesus Wept — draft (angle A: He already knew)

## (a) Script

[narrator] Jesus cried for a dead friend He was about to bring back. Why?
[narrator] The friend was Lazarus. Before He even arrived, Jesus said:
[jesus] "I go, that I may awake him."
[narrator] He arrived. Lazarus's sister Mary fell at His feet, crying. Friends cried too.
[narrator] Then, two words:
[narrator] "Jesus wept."
[crowd] "Behold how he loved him!"
[narrator] Then He called:
[jesus] "Lazarus, come forth."
[narrator] And the dead man walked out.
[narrator] So why cry, if you know the ending?
[narrator] John says: He saw them weeping.
[pause ~0.8 s]
[narrator] He knew the ending. And He still cried with the people He loved.

## (d) The 4 questions

- **Who is this for:** anyone who knows "Jesus wept" only as the shortest verse, and anyone who is grieving.
- **Main take-away:** Jesus knew He would raise Lazarus, and He still cried with the people He loved.
- **The curiosity gap:** why cry if you already know the happy ending? Opened in line 1, asked again near the end, answered from John 11:33 in the last two lines.
- **Why a global newcomer gets it:** short, common words; Lazarus is named as His friend who died; the one older word ("awake") is explained by line 1 ("bring back") and by the dead man walking out.

## Notes for the judge

- The reason for the tears stays inside the text: He saw them weeping (11:33) and the mourners' own words (11:36). No invented feelings.
- The ending makes no promise to the viewer. It only says what Jesus did that day.
- "The Jews" in John 11 are called "friends" here. No group is blamed.
