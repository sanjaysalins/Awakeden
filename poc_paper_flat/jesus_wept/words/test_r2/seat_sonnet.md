## P
1. Newcomer: mostly yes. Who = Jesus, Lazarus, two sisters. What = Jesus cried, then raised a dead man. Why = "He saw them weeping" and loved them. Unclear lines: "I am the resurrection, and the life" (the word "resurrection" is never explained; the later picture of Lazarus walking out only half decodes it). "Thy brother shall rise again" is fine. "Then two words:" is a bit odd for ESL.
2. Bible reader: 6. The "knew the ending, still cried" idea is known but well framed. Putting the Martha lines first and the tears second is a fresh order.
3. Curiosity gap: 8. "Why?" opens in 3 seconds. The payoff is explicit ("He saw them weeping... He knew the ending, still cried"). It pays off, but a bit flat.
4. Accuracy: quotes match KJV (11:23, 25, 35, 36, 43). "Dead four days" is OK (v17, v39). "Thy brother" is said to Martha, who is unnamed and then "the other sister" is Mary - fine. "What moved Him? He saw them weeping" simplifies v33 (he groaned in the spirit and was troubled); mild. "Crowd" for the Jews is neutral, nobody is blamed (the doubters of v37 are left out). The resurrection line is attached to Martha's scene, so it is not a blanket promise. Chronology: "I am the resurrection" comes before Mary, which is right.
5. Landing: 6. It lands on Jesus' heart, not on who He is. There is no stock closer. Earned, but soft.
6. Fix: say what "resurrection" means in plain words, e.g. "He is the one who gives life back", or cut the line.
Overall: 6.5/10.

## Q
1. Newcomer: mostly yes. Same who/what. Why is a bit tighter: "He saw them weeping, and He wept with them." The closer, "I am the resurrection, and the life," is again not decoded, but it comes straight after the picture of Lazarus coming out, so it reads as Jesus naming what we just saw. Good.
2. Bible reader: 7. Moving the "I am" line to the end, after the miracle, is a new, strong frame.
3. Curiosity gap: 8. Same opening; the payoff is faster and sharper.
4. Accuracy: the quotes are the same and correct. Watch-out: the final line was spoken to Martha BEFORE the weeping, so ending on it reorders time. It is spoken in Jesus' voice with no frame, so a listener may think He said it at the tomb after the raising. Mild, but a hostile Bible reader may flag it. "Wept with them": the KJV says He wept, and was moved by their weeping, so "with" is a fair inference. The final line alone could sound like a promise to everyone; the context of Lazarus keeps it anchored. No group blamed.
5. Landing: 8. It ends on Jesus' own words, earned by the story, and there is no stock closer.
6. Fix: add a short frame to the last line: "Days before, He had told Martha:" or keep a one-word "Earlier He said," so the order is honest.
Overall: 7.5/10.
