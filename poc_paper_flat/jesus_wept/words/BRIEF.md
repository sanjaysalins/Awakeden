## The fresh angle (binding: build the whole short around ONE fresh, true detail)
Pick ONE of these as the hook and spine. Do not use both as equal ideas.
- A. He already knew. Before He came, Jesus said "Our friend Lazarus sleepeth; but I go, that I may awake him" (John 11:11), and "This sickness is not unto death" (11:4). He knew He would raise Lazarus in minutes. And still: "Jesus wept" (11:35). Why cry if you know the happy ending?
- B. Two different words. In John 11:33 Mary and the mourners are "weeping" (Greek klaio: loud crying, wailing). In 11:35 "Jesus wept" uses a different word (dakryo: tears falling, quiet tears). Careful: say it plainly and modestly ("John uses a different word"), no lecture, no overclaim.
Most people only know "the shortest verse in the Bible". The short must give them something they did not know.

## Rules (owner house rules)
1. Hook in the first 2.5 s: a newcomer must learn who / what / why fast. Open a curiosity gap that pays off at the end.
2. Global audience: plain, common English. A newcomer with English as a second language, with no Bible knowledge, must follow it. Say who Lazarus is (Jesus's friend) and that he had died. Never lean on an archaic KJV word for the meaning.
3. Land on Jesus, grace-anchored: NO fear, NO gain/loss pressure, NO stock closer ("come to Jesus", "just ask Him", "will you..."). Earn the ending from the story itself.
4. Doctrine sound and Bible-grounded. Do not invent feelings or reasons the text does not give. If you say why He wept, keep it to what the text shows (He saw them weeping, 11:33; "Behold how he loved him", 11:36). You may use Hebrews 4:15 or Isaiah 53:3 only if it truly helps; not required.
5. The last line must NOT read as a blanket promise to everyone ("He will raise your loved one", "everyone will..."). Keep promises tied to what the text says (11:25-26 is "he that believeth in me").
6. Do NOT blame "the Jews" or any people group. In John 11 "the Jews" here are mourners and friends; call them "the mourners" or "friends".
7. No AI/method wording. No hashtags. Short sentences. Leave a pause before the landing line.
