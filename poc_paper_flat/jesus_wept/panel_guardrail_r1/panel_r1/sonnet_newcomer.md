## 1. WHO IS THIS FOR
A named viewer: Amara, 26, a nurse in Lagos, on her phone, lost someone and felt God was absent. Line 1 hooks her ("You said it to God, alone"). She stops at line 1 and finishes if the grief theme holds. She shares if line 12 or 18 lands. Not served: someone with no grief, or someone who wants history or teaching. The film assumes she accepts that Jesus is a real, alive person.

## 2. MAIN TAKEAWAY
"Jesus cried with grieving people, brought a dead man back to life, and died and rose himself, so He is alive." Lines 12, 14, 18 carry it. Mostly clear, but the "why does that matter to me" step is thin. Line 16 ("He went into one, for us") is the big claim and I do not understand it: who is "us", and what did his death do?

## 3. GLOBAL AUDIENCE
Who: Jesus, a man, with two sisters and a dead brother Lazarus (lines 2-3). What: the sisters blamed Him, He wept, then made the brother alive (12, 14). Why: leaders then wanted to kill Him (15), and He died and "rose" (17).
Lost or misled:
- Line 1: "you" is a woman alone, then line 2 says two sisters. I did not connect the opening woman to Mary until much later. I guessed she was Mary.
- Line 2: "that same sentence". I have to look back at line 1 to see it.
- Line 5: "Her sister Mary". I do not know which sister is Mary until line 7.
- Line 7: "the one who stayed behind". Fine, but "He was asking" needs line 6.
- Line 14: "called Lazarus out of the grave". Fine.
- Line 15: "the leaders". Which leaders? Of what? Why would they want to kill Him for giving life? Not explained.
- Line 16: "He went into one" (a grave). Confusing grammar for a second-language listener.
- Line 17: "rose" is idiom. Rose from where?
- Line 18: "The Jesus who wept is alive." I do not know who Jesus is as a person: a teacher, a king, God? The film never says.
Words that fail: "Master" (6), "Lord" (4, 8; is that a title?), "Behold" (13), "thou hadst" (4, 8), "wept" (12), "grave", "linen".

## 4. BIBLICALLY ACCURATE
I cannot judge accuracy. What I was TOLD that I could not follow: "Lord" and "Master" as titles for Jesus (4, 6); why leaders wanted to kill Him (15); how his death on a cross is "for us" (16-17); what "rose" means (17); that Jesus is more than a man (18). I was also not told why He wept, since He then undoes the death anyway.

## 5. BEST VERSION POSSIBLE
The best explainer would say once, early, who Jesus is in plain words ("Jesus, a teacher many believed was God's Son") and make the two sisters visually distinct from the opening woman (blue dress throughout, named by line 2). The ONE highest-value change: line 16-17 needs one plain sentence on what His death was for. At the moment the ending asks me to accept a huge claim with no bridge. Score: 6/10. Emotionally strong, clear first 40 seconds, but the ending leaves a newcomer with a question and no answer.

## CURIOSITY GAP
Opens at line 1 ("they would not have died", who said this, to whom?). Pays off partly at line 12 ("Jesus wept") and line 14 (he lives again). The second gap, why would He die, opens at line 15-16 and is not paid off. Score 7/10. Worth the wait for the first, not the second.

## THREE SMALLEST FIXES
1. Line 2: "Two sisters, Martha and Mary, said that same sentence to Jesus."
2. Line 15: "He gave a dead man life, so the rulers who feared Him planned to kill Him."
3. Line 16: "He did not only cry at a grave. He went into one, to take our place." (and line 17: "He died on a cross, and three days later, He rose alive.")
