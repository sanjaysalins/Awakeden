## 1. WHO IS THIS FOR
This is for Ana, 30, in Manila or São Paulo. English is her second language. Her mother died last year and she still asks God "where were You?" The hook at line 1 stops her. The two sisters repeating the same sentence (lines 4 and 8) keeps her watching. She would share it with a grieving friend.

It does not serve a viewer who wants the "why". The film never says Jesus waited on purpose (John 11:6, 15), so "why didn't He come?" stays open. It also does not serve a viewer who has never prayed, because "You said it to God" assumes they have.

## 2. MAIN TAKEAWAY
"Jesus cried with people in their grief, and He is alive." Lines 12 and 18 carry it. That half is clear. The other half is not: the film never brings the "you" from line 1 back at the end. Line 18 ends on Jesus, not on the viewer's own sentence.

## 3. GLOBAL AUDIENCE
In my own words: a friend of Jesus died. His two sisters each told Jesus "if You were here, he would not have died." Jesus cried, then raised the man. That miracle led the leaders to plan His death. He died, rose, and is alive. The arc is clear. Grief and "if only you had come" are understood in every culture.

Words that fail:
- **Line 1, "You said it to God, alone":** "alone" floats. It could mean "only to God" or "when you were by yourself". The woman on screen is later Mary (line 5), so "You" seems to point at her, not the viewer.
- **Lines 4 and 8, "had not died":** a learner reads this as a past fact ("he had not died", so he is alive?). Line 1 already says "would not have died", which helps. Keep that plain line.
- **Line 6, "is come… calleth… thee"; line 10, "ye laid"; line 13, "Behold":** these are archaic but short and tagged, so they are fine to quote. None of them carries the meaning.
- **Line 12, "wept":** many learners don't know this word. Line 16 then says "cry", so the link between the two breaks.
- **Line 15, "planned to take His":** the missing word ("life") is hard for learners and for machine translation. Translators often render "take" as "grab" or "arrest".
- **Lines 16-17:** "for us" is split from its sentence across two captions.

Cultures where men don't cry in public: line 13, "how he loved him", turns the tears into love, not weakness. Good.

## 4. BIBLICALLY ACCURATE
- **Line 2, "same sentence":** RIGHT. John 11:21 and 11:32 are word-for-word the same in the KJV.
- **Line 3, "friend… four days":** RIGHT (11:11, 11:17, 11:39).
- **Line 5, "whispered":** RIGHT. It is a fair reading of "secretly" in 11:28.
- **Line 7, "asking for the one who stayed behind":** RIGHT (11:20, 11:28).
- **Line 7 screen, Jesus kneeling with His hand on her head:** UNSUPPORTED. The text only says Mary fell at His feet (11:32). Fine as art, but it is invented.
- **Line 13 screen, mourners "outside a house":** slightly off. In the text this is on the way to the tomb.
- **Line 14:** RIGHT (11:43-44).
- **Line 15, "so":** RIGHT. John 11:45-53 links the miracle to the plot.
- **Lines 16-18:** RIGHT, and sound doctrine.

## 5. BEST VERSION POSSIBLE
The best explainer would say the fact that makes the tears land: Jesus knew He was about to raise Lazarus, and He still cried. Without that, a newcomer asks "why cry if He will fix it?"

The ONE change: after line 12, add "He knew He would raise him. He still cried." It costs about 2 seconds, and it explains "wept" to learners for free.

Score: **7/10**.

## CURIOSITY GAP
It opens at line 1 ("If You had been here…"). It partly pays off at line 12 and finishes at line 18. Score **6/10**. It's worth the wait emotionally, but the "where were You?" question gets tears and life back, not an answer, and the viewer's own sentence is never closed.

## THREE SMALLEST FIXES
1. **Line 15:** "He gave a dead man life, so the leaders planned to **kill Him**."
2. **Lines 16-17 captions:** put "He did not only cry at a grave. He went into one, for us." on one caption. Start the next with "He died on a cross, and He rose."
3. **Line 1:** "**Maybe you have said this to God:** 'If You had been here, they would not have died.'" That drops the floating "alone". Also, the woman in line 1 should not be the same woman in blue who is later Mary.
