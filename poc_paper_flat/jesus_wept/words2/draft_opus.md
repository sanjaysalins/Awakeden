# Jesus wept — blind draft (opus)

## (a) Script

[narrator] Maybe you said it to God, alone: "If You had been here, they would not have died."
[narrator] Two sisters said that sentence to Jesus.
[narrator] Their brother Lazarus, Jesus' friend, died. He was four days buried when Jesus came.
[narrator] Martha met Him.
[martha] Lord, if thou hadst been here, my brother had not died.
[narrator] Mary "sat still in the house." Her sister whispered, "The Master is come, and calleth for thee." He asked for the one who could not get up.
[narrator] Mary hurried to Him and said the same sentence.
[mary] Lord, if thou hadst been here, my brother had not died.
[narrator] He did not argue. He asked,
[jesus] Where have ye laid him?
[mourners] Lord, come and see.
[narrator] (pause) Jesus wept.
[narrator] He called Lazarus out of the grave. From that day, the leaders planned His death.
[narrator] He did not just cry at a grave. He went into one, for us. And He rose.

## (d) The 4 questions

- Who it is for: anyone alone after a loss who has told God "You came too late."
- Main take-away: Jesus does not argue with your grief. He weeps with it, then goes into the grave for us, and rises.
- Curiosity gap: two sisters say the same hard sentence to Jesus. What will He say back?
- Why a global newcomer gets it: plain words, three named people, one repeated sentence everyone has thought, and a clear end: He cried, He died, He rose.
