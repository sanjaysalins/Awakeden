# Seat: Sonnet (blind) - K vs L

## 1. NEWCOMER
K: Mostly yes. Who: Jesus, two sisters, dead brother. What: they grieve, Jesus weeps, raises him. Why: He enters death for us. Unclear lines: "Mary stayed in the house, until her sister whispered" (who is "her sister"? Martha is not named as the whisperer clearly); "He asked for the one who stayed behind" (who is "He"?); "the leaders planned to kill Jesus" (jump, why?); "He went into one, for us" (abstract). Martha/Mary lines are repeated verbatim, which helps ESL.
L: Mostly yes. Clearer opening ("You prayed. Help came too late"). Martha and Mary are named. Unclear: "I am the resurrection, and the life" is heavy for a newcomer and arrives before the grave, so it deflates the weeping. "Behold how he loved him" is archaic. "the leaders planned to kill Him" is unexplained. "The Jesus who wept is alive" is clear.

## 2. FEEL SEEN (0-10)
K: 7. The opening is a lonely private prayer, and the ending is gentle.
L: 8. "You prayed. Help came too late" names the exact hurt in 3 seconds. Slightly more preachy via the resurrection line.

## 3. BIBLE READER (0-10)
K: 8. Mary's repeat of Martha's sentence, "He asked for the one who stayed behind," and "He did not argue" are fresh.
L: 7. Same insight, but adds the well-known "I am the resurrection" and "Behold how he loved him!" Less new, but richer.

## 4. CURIOSITY GAP (0-10)
K: 7. The gap is "what did Jesus say to the same sentence?" Paid off: He asks, then weeps.
L: 7. The gap is "where were You?" Paid off by the resurrection line, but that comes too early, which weakens the tension before the weeping.

## 5. ACCURACY
K: "they would not have died" is a paraphrase of the first line, and the quotes are marked as KJV only for the sisters. The "two sisters said that same sentence" claim is true (11:21, 11:32). "Mary stayed in the house" is true (11:20). Martha called her "secretly," which is fine. "He asked for the one who stayed behind" is a mild inference. "Lord, come and see" is said by "they," not clearly just mourners; acceptable. Order is right. "From that day, the leaders planned to kill Jesus" is correct (11:53), though John also says some went to the Pharisees; the group is "the leaders," not "the Jews," so no blame problem. Ending: cross and rose are clear and sound. "He went into one [a grave], for us" is a theological compression, but sound. Also, "Jesus's friend... in the grave four days" is right.
L: The "I am the resurrection" quote is real and goes to Martha, but it is placed before Mary is called. In KJV Martha said her line, then Jesus said it, then she fetched Mary. Order is correct. "Behold how he loved him!" is said by "the Jews" in KJV, labelled "mourners": fine. "Mary stayed in the house... whispered" is correct. The ending drops "died on a cross," so the gospel is thinner: "went into one, for us. And He rose." That is a gap. No blanket promise, but "The Jesus who wept is alive" is sound.

## 6. LANDING (0-10)
K: 8. Earned by the story (wept, went into the grave, died, rose). The cross is named. No stock closer.
L: 7. "The Jesus who wept is alive" is a lovely line, but the cross is missing, and "for us" is unsupported.

## 7. SHARE
K: Yes. Gentle, honest, and does not rush comfort.
L: Yes. It speaks to the pain faster.

## 8. One fix each
K: Name Martha as the whisperer; define "He" and tie the leaders' plot to a reason (they feared his power).
L: Move "I am the resurrection" after the weeping or cut it, and add "died on a cross."
