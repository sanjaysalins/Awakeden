# Seat: opus (blind)

## K
1. NEWCOMER: yes. Who (Jesus, two sisters, dead brother), what (He cries, then raises him), why (grief, then love) all clear. The plain first line explains the KJV "thou hadst been here" before it comes. Hard lines: "The Master is come, and calleth for thee" ("Master" and "calleth"), "Where have ye laid him?" ("laid" = buried?). "Come and see" saves it. The WHY of the tears is felt but never said.
2. FEEL SEEN: 8. "Maybe you said it to God, alone" names the private prayer at once. "He asked for the one who stayed behind" is the most tender line in either script.
3. BIBLE READER: 7. "Asked for the one who stayed behind", the same sentence twice, and the raising leading to His own death are all fresh angles.
4. CURIOSITY GAP: 7. The question (what does Jesus say to that sentence?) opens in 2 s. "He did not argue" plus "Jesus wept" pays it off.
5. ACCURACY: clean. Quotes match KJV. Order is right. "From that day" matches 11:53. "Leaders", not "the Jews", so no group is blamed. "Asked for" rests on Martha's report, which is fine. "Whispered" for "secretly" is fair. No blanket promise. Gospel is explicit: "died on a cross, and He rose."
6. LANDING: 8. Grave to grave, earned, no stock closer. A little abrupt.
7. SHARE: yes. It does not tell the friend to stop grieving. Jesus cries first.
8. FIX: add "[mourners] Behold how he loved him!" after "Jesus wept" so the why is spoken.

## L
1. NEWCOMER: mostly yes. Hard lines: "I am the resurrection, and the life" (resurrection is a hard word for a second-language listener, and nothing explains it), "calleth for thee", "laid". "Behold how he loved him" answers the why.
2. FEEL SEEN: 8. "Help came too late. Where were You?" hits hard in 3 s. It can sound like it agrees God was late, but the story corrects that.
3. BIBLE READER: 7. "The same words" and the cost of the raising. No new angle beyond K's.
4. CURIOSITY GAP: 8. "Where were You?" is a sharper question. "He loved him" plus "alive" answers it.
5. ACCURACY: quotes and order are correct. Leaders, not a group, are blamed. No blanket promise. GAP: the cross is never named. "Went into one, for us" implies death, but a newcomer will not know how He died. The gospel ending is incomplete.
6. LANDING: 8. "The Jesus who wept is alive" is warm, present tense and earned. It is the best last line of the two.
7. SHARE: yes, but a friend could ask "died how?"
8. FIX: "He went into one, for us. He died on a cross. And He rose. The Jesus who wept is alive."

## Rank
1. K: overall 7.5 (sound, complete gospel, most tender middle)
2. L: overall 7.0 (better hook and last line, but no cross)
Best build: K's body plus L's "Behold how he loved him!" and "The Jesus who wept is alive."
