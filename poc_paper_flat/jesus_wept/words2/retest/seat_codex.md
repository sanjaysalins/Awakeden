**F — Overall: 7/10**

1. **NEWCOMER: No, not fully clear after one listen.** WHO and WHAT are understandable; WHY Jesus raises Lazarus or dies “for us” remains unexplained. Difficult lines: “if thou hadst been here,” “my brother had not died,” “The Master is come, and calleth for thee,” “Where have ye laid him?” and “Behold how he loved him!” “He was asking for the one who stayed behind” also requires inference.

2. **FEEL SEEN: 7/10.** The opening names a painful thought immediately, but “You said it” assumes both belief and a particular grief experience. Jesus’s tears offer comfort without a lecture. Lazarus’s restoration could sharpen the listener’s unanswered question: why wasn’t my person restored?

3. **BIBLE READER: 7/10.** The attention to Mary staying behind offers a fresh emphasis. Connecting Lazarus’s restored life with the council’s death plot adds weight.

4. **CURIOSITY GAP: 6/10.** The opening raises “Where was God?” The story demonstrates Jesus’s love, but does not answer his absence. The ending partly pays off a different question: whether Jesus understands grief.

5. **ACCURACY:** All direct biblical quotations match the supplied text. The opening is a modern paraphrase, so “that same sentence” overstates exact sameness. “Whispered” invents a delivery detail; KJV says “secretly.” Jesus calling Mary is reported by Martha; his actual request is not narrated. “He did not argue” fits the immediate exchange with Mary, but should not imply he offered no teaching elsewhere. Resurrection followed by the council’s plot is correctly ordered. “The leaders” is vague: specify chief priests and Pharisees; no entire people is blamed. No explicit blanket promise appears. Cross, burial and resurrection are clearly stated and broadly Christian, but **the supplied excerpts do not verify those events**; “for us” is consistent with the sacrificial direction of John 11:50–52.

6. **LANDING: 8/10.** “The Jesus who wept is alive” connects compassion and resurrection memorably. The gospel transition is compressed.

7. **SHARE: Yes**, for a grieving Christian friend; less confidently for someone unfamiliar with Christianity because of the archaic speech and assumed prayer experience.

8. **One fix:** Replace the opening with: “Have you ever wondered why God wasn’t there when someone you loved died?”
