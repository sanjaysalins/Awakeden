**(a) The Script**

[narrator] When you lose someone, one sentence plays on repeat. Jesus's friend Lazarus died. His sisters were Martha and Mary. Martha met Him with that sentence.
[martha] "Lord, if thou hadst been here, my brother had not died."
[narrator] Jesus told her,
[jesus] "I am the resurrection, and the life."
[narrator] But Mary sat still in the house. Her sister whispered, "The Master is come, and calleth for thee." He asked for the one who could not get up. Mary fell at His feet with the same words.
[mary] "Lord, if thou hadst been here, my brother had not died."
[narrator] He did not argue. He asked,
[jesus] "Where have ye laid him?"
[narrator] The mourners answered,
[mourners] "Lord, come and see."
[narrator] "Jesus wept." (pause) He called Lazarus out. This cost Him. From that day forth, the leaders planned to put Him to death. He did not just cry at a grave. He went into one for us, and rose.

**(d) The 4 Questions**
- **Who it is for:** For anyone sitting alone in the aftermath of loss, feeling like God arrived too late to help.
- **Main take-away:** Jesus doesn't argue with our grief; He enters into it, and He conquered the grave for us.
- **The curiosity gap:** Opening with "one sentence plays on repeat" makes the viewer wait to hear what that universal sentence is.
- **Why a global newcomer gets it:** The raw emotions of grief, a delayed friend, and shared tears are universally understood without needing prior biblical context.
