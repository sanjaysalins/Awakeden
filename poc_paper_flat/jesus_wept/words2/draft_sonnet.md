# Draft (Sonnet) - "Jesus wept"

## (a) Script

[narrator] You prayed. Help came too late. And you wondered: where were You?

[narrator] Two sisters, Martha and Mary, wondered it too. Their brother Lazarus, a friend of Jesus, had been dead four days.

[martha] "Lord, if thou hadst been here, my brother had not died."

[narrator] Mary sat still in the house. Her sister whispered, "The Master is come, and calleth for thee." She got up and went to Him.

[mary] "Lord, if thou hadst been here, my brother had not died."

[narrator] The very same words. He did not argue. He did not explain. He asked one thing.

[jesus] "Where have ye laid him?"

[mourners] "Lord, come and see."

(pause)

[narrator] "Jesus wept."

[narrator] Then He called Lazarus out of that grave, alive. And from that day, the chief priests and Pharisees planned to kill Him.

[narrator] The tears cost Him. He did not only cry at a grave. He went into one, for us. And He rose.

- Mary "sat still in the house" - John 11:20
- "Where have ye laid him?" - John 11:34 (Jesus)
- "Lord, come and see." - John 11:34 (mourners)
- "Jesus wept." - John 11:35
- Four days in the grave - John 11:17; "Lazarus, come forth" (called out) - John 11:43
- "from that day forth they took counsel together for to put him to death" - John 11:53; chief priests and Pharisees - John 11:47

## (d) The 4 questions

1. Who it is for: anyone grieving, lonely, or feeling God came too late.
2. Main take-away: Jesus does not argue with your hardest sentence; He weeps, goes into the grave for us, and rose.
3. Curiosity gap: "Where were You?" - what does Jesus say back? He says nothing; He asks where they laid him, and weeps.
4. Why a global newcomer gets it: short plain words, a simple story of a dead friend and two sisters, and the same sentence said twice.
