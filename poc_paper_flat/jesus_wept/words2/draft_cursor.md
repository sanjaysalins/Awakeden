(a) Script

[narrator] You have said it alone. If only He had been here, this would not have happened.

(pause)

[narrator] Jesus loved Martha, Mary, and Lazarus. Lazarus died. Jesus waited two more days, then walked to Bethany — four days in the grave.

[martha] Lord, if thou hadst been here, my brother had not died.

[narrator] Martha said it to His face. Mary stayed in the house, still, until her sister whispered, "The Master is come, and calleth for thee." Jesus called for her.

[mary] Lord, if thou hadst been here, my brother had not died.

[narrator] The same sentence. He did not argue with either sister. He had already told Martha, "I am the resurrection, and the life." Now He asked,

[jesus] Where have ye laid him?

[mourners] Lord, come and see.

[narrator] Jesus wept. Friends cried, "Behold how he loved him!" He called Lazarus out of the tomb. From that day forth, the leaders plotted His death. He did not only weep at a grave — He went into one for us, and rose. The Jesus who wept is alive.

(d) Four questions (one line each)
- Who it is for: Anyone grieving who has whispered “too late” to God and feels unseen in the silence after loss.
- Main take-away: Jesus meets that exact sentence without a fight, weeps at the grave, and the same Lord who cried there died and rose for us.
- The curiosity gap: Both sisters say the same reproach — so why does He weep instead of answering, and what does He do next?
- Why a global newcomer gets it: Plain names, one repeated human line everyone has thought, tears at a tomb, then a clear fact — He died and rose — with no church jargon or pressure.
