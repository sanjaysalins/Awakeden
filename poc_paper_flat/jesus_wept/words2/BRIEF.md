# Write ONE narration from scratch — "Jesus wept" (John 11). You are ONE blind writer; you have seen no other draft.

## Who it is for
People who feel lost, lonely or grieving. Someone who prayed and felt God came too late. Someone sitting alone in a room after a loss. Worldwide; many speak English as a second language; many know nothing about the Bible. It must be something a person sends to a grieving friend.

## The chosen idea (owner-approved; binding)
SPINE: "The sentence you said too." Both sisters say the exact same words to Jesus: "Lord, if thou hadst been here, my brother had not died" (11:21, 11:32). It is the sentence every grieving person has thought. He does not argue with either of them. He goes to the grave. "Jesus wept."
INSIDE IT: Mary "sat still in the house" (11:20) until her sister whispered, "The Master is come, and calleth for thee" (11:28). He asked for the one who could not get up.
ENDING: The tears cost Him. He called Lazarus out (11:43), and "from that day forth" the leaders planned to put Him to death (11:53). He did not only cry at a grave; He went into one, for us — and (state plainly, as gospel fact, no KJV quote needed) He rose. The last line must land on Jesus and on the cross/resurrection, earned by the story, grace-anchored.
Facts you may use: Jesus loved the family (11:5) and stayed two more days (11:6); four days in the grave (11:17); "Where have ye laid him?" / "Lord, come and see" (11:34); "Behold how he loved him!" (11:36); "I am the resurrection, and the life" (11:25, said to Martha BEFORE the grave — if used, keep it in order or frame it "He had told her"). Other KJV in the file: Isaiah 53:3, Psalm 34:18, Hebrews 4:15, John 14:18.

## Hard rules
1. First 3 seconds: the viewer feels SEEN (their own grief), and a curiosity gap opens. Plain words.
2. Name who's who simply: Jesus, His friend Lazarus who died, Lazarus's sisters Martha and Mary.
3. KJV quotes verbatim, short. Never let an old word carry the meaning alone; plain English around it.
4. Doctrine sound. Do not invent feelings or reasons (no "He knew they needed..."). Do not explain why He waited beyond the text. "Calleth for thee" is Martha's report — say "her sister whispered".
5. No blanket promise ("your loved one will come back", "He will fix it now"). The good news is who Jesus is and what He did.
6. No stock closer ("come to Jesus", "just ask Him", "He's waiting for you", "will you…?"). No fear, no pressure.
7. Never blame "the Jews" or any people group: mourners/friends; the plotters are "the leaders" / "the chief priests and Pharisees" (11:47).
8. 125-150 spoken words (about 52-60 s). One line per voice: [narrator], [jesus], [martha], [mary], [mourners]. Mark one (pause).
9. No wording about AI or how the film is made. No hashtags.

## Output (only this)
(a) the script; (b) word count; (c) KJV fragments with verse refs; (d) the 4 questions, one line each: who it is for, main take-away, the curiosity gap, why a global newcomer gets it.
