# Ideas round — "Jesus wept" (John 11). You are ONE blind voice; you have seen no other voice's work.

## Who we are making this for
People who feel LOST, LONELY or GRIEVING. Someone who prayed and felt God came too late. Someone sitting alone in a room after a loss. Someone who thinks nobody sees them cry. Worldwide audience; many speak English as a second language; many know nothing about the Bible.
The owner's words: "we have a very, very rich context here to help so many people who feel lost and lonely, and we are not giving our best." A first attempt was judged WEAK: it was built on a clever fact ("Jesus knew He would raise Lazarus, so why cry?") with a polite ending. Do NOT make trivia the core. Do not make "the shortest verse in the Bible" the core.

## What we want
A 45-60 second vertical Short (YouTube Shorts / TikTok), narrated, with KJV quotes in character voices, over illustrated pictures.
It must make a lonely viewer feel SEEN in the first 3 seconds, take them through the story so they feel it, and land on Jesus in a way that is earned by the story, true to the text, and actually helps them. It should be something a person would send to a friend who is grieving.

## The material (all in KJV.txt — read it; quote only from it, verbatim)
Some true details people often miss (use any, or find better ones in the text):
- Jesus loved this family (11:5) and still "abode two days still in the same place" (11:6). He came "late".
- Both sisters said the same sentence to Him: "Lord, if thou hadst been here, my brother had not died" (11:21, 11:32).
- Martha went out to meet Him; "Mary sat still in the house" (11:20) — until Martha whispered, "The Master is come, and calleth for thee" (11:28).
- He "groaned in the spirit, and was troubled" (11:33), asked "Where have ye laid him?" and they said "Lord, come and see" (11:34) — the same words Jesus Himself once used to invite His first followers (John 1:39 "Come and see").
- "Jesus wept" (11:35). "Behold how he loved him!" (11:36). Some doubted (11:37).
- "Take ye away the stone" (11:39). "Lazarus, come forth" (11:43). "Loose him, and let him go" (11:44) — others had to unwrap him.
- From that day His enemies planned His death (11:53); they even planned to kill Lazarus (12:10). Giving Lazarus life helped set Jesus's own death in motion.
- "I am the resurrection, and the life" (11:25), said to a grieving woman BEFORE the miracle, followed by "Believest thou this?" (11:26).
Other KJV in the file you may use: John 14:18, Isaiah 53:3, Hebrews 4:15, Psalm 34:18, Revelation 21:4.

## Hard rules
1. Doctrine sound and grounded in the text. Do not invent feelings, reasons or events the text does not give. Keep any "why" to what the text shows.
2. KJV quotes verbatim (short phrases). Never let an old KJV word carry the meaning alone; plain modern English around it.
3. Grace-anchored landing on Jesus. NO fear, NO pressure, NO stock closers ("come to Jesus", "just ask Him", "He's waiting for you", "will you...?").
4. No blanket promise the text does not make (e.g. "He will bring your loved one back"). Promises stay tied to the text (11:25-26 is "he that believeth in me").
5. Never blame "the Jews" or any group. In John 11 "the Jews" are mostly mourners and friends — call them mourners/friends; the plotters are "the chief priests and Pharisees" / "the leaders" (11:47).
6. No wording about AI or how the film is made.

## Output (plain text, under 900 words)
Part 1 — THREE ideas, each truly different (different setup, different turn, different ending):
For each: TITLE (max 6 words) / FIRST 3 SECONDS (the exact opening line) / SETUP / THE TURN (the true detail that changes everything, with verse) / LAST LINE (exact) / WHY A LONELY PERSON WOULD SHARE IT (one line) / RISK (one line: what could go wrong doctrinally or for a newcomer).
Part 2 — Your BEST idea as a full draft script, 110-140 words, one line per voice: [narrator], [jesus], [martha], [mary], [mourners]. Mark one (pause).
Part 3 — The 4 questions in one line each: who is it for, main take-away, the curiosity gap, why a global newcomer gets it.
