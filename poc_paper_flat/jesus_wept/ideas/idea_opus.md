PART 1 — THREE IDEAS

IDEA A — "The Sentence You Said Too"
FIRST 3 SECONDS: "If you ever told God, 'You were too late' — two sisters said it first."
SETUP: Jesus loved Lazarus and his sisters (11:5). He heard Lazarus was sick and stayed two more days (11:6). When He came, Lazarus had been in the grave four days (11:17). Two grieving sisters meet Him, one by one.
THE TURN: Both sisters say the SAME sentence, word for word: "Lord, if thou hadst been here, my brother had not died" (11:21, 11:32). He does not argue with either one. To Martha, who comes with questions, He gives words: "I am the resurrection, and the life" (11:25). To Mary, who can only fall at His feet crying, He gives no speech. He sees her weeping, is deeply troubled (11:33), and "Jesus wept" (11:35). Same sentence. Two different answers. Each one fits the person.
LAST LINE: "You can say that sentence to Him. He has heard it before. He did not walk away. He came closer."
WHY A LONELY PERSON WOULD SHARE IT: It gives them permission to say their hardest, angriest prayer — and shows it is not the end of the conversation.
RISK: "He came closer" must stay tied to the story (He went to the grave, 11:38); do not turn it into a promise of a miracle for the viewer.

IDEA B — "For the One Who Stayed Inside"
FIRST 3 SECONDS: "This is for the person who can't leave the room today."
SETUP: A house full of mourners. Martha runs out to meet Jesus. "But Mary sat still in the house" (11:20). Grief has her. She does not go.
THE TURN: Jesus does not walk into the crowded house. He stops outside the town (11:30) and sends for her by name. Martha whispers: "The Master is come, and calleth for thee" (11:28). Mary gets up quickly. She reaches Him, falls at His feet, cries — and He cries with her (11:33-35). The friends watching say: "Behold how he loved him!" (11:36).
LAST LINE: "Mary didn't have to find the strength to go to Him. He asked for her first."
WHY A LONELY PERSON WOULD SHARE IT: It speaks to the one who has no energy left to pray or reach out — and says they were not forgotten.
RISK: Do not tell the viewer "He is calling you right now" as a direct promise; keep the call inside the story, and let Psalm 34:18 ("nigh unto them that are of a broken heart") carry the wider truth.

IDEA C — "Come and See Where It Hurts"
FIRST 3 SECONDS: "Jesus once said 'Come and see.' One day, grieving people said it back to Him."
SETUP: At the start of John, Jesus invites His first friends: "Come and see" (1:39) — come see where I live. Years later, at a grave, He asks the mourners, "Where have ye laid him?" (11:34).
THE TURN: They answer with His own words: "Lord, come and see" (11:34). This time, "come and see" means: come see where we are broken. And He comes. He weeps (11:35). He raises Lazarus (11:43). And from that day the leaders plan to kill Him (11:53). Going to their grave set Him on the road to His own.
LAST LINE: "He came to see where it hurts. And it cost Him His life to stay."
WHY A LONELY PERSON WOULD SHARE IT: It shows a God who does not watch grief from far away — He walks into it, and pays for it.
RISK: The 1:39 / 11:34 link is the same words, not a stated plan by John — say "the same words", never "on purpose"; and "cost Him His life" needs a quick, clear bridge to the cross for newcomers.

MY BEST: IDEA A — "The Sentence You Said Too."


PART 2 — FULL DRAFT (Idea A, ~135 words)

[narrator] If you ever told God, "You were too late" — two sisters said it first.
[narrator] Jesus loved them. Their brother Lazarus was dying. Jesus came… four days after the funeral.
[martha] Lord, if thou hadst been here, my brother had not died.
[narrator] Then her sister Mary came, and said the very same words.
[mary] Lord, if thou hadst been here, my brother had not died.
[narrator] He didn't argue. To Martha, He gave a promise.
[jesus] I am the resurrection, and the life.
[narrator] But Mary was on the ground, crying. So He gave her no speech.
[jesus] Where have ye laid him?
[mourners] Lord, come and see.
[narrator] (pause) Jesus wept.
[mourners] Behold how he loved him!
[narrator] Then He went to the grave — and called Lazarus out alive.
[narrator] You can say that sentence to Him. He has heard it before. He did not walk away. He came closer.

(Note: "four days after the funeral" = the text's "lain in the grave four days already" (11:17), said in plain words.)


PART 3 — THE 4 QUESTIONS

WHO IS IT FOR: The person who prayed, lost someone anyway, and quietly thinks God came too late.
MAIN TAKE-AWAY: Your hardest sentence to God is not the end — Jesus heard it from two grieving sisters, did not argue, and came closer and wept with them.
THE CURIOSITY GAP: Two sisters say the exact same words to Jesus — why does He answer each one so differently, and what does He do next?
WHY A GLOBAL NEWCOMER GETS IT: No Bible knowledge needed — a family, a death, a friend who came late, a sentence everyone who has grieved has thought, and tears anyone can understand.
