Part 1 — THREE ideas

---

**TITLE:** Still in the House  
**FIRST 3 SECONDS:** You don’t have to get up. You don’t have to perform grief for anyone.  
**SETUP:** Lazarus is dead four days. Mourners fill the house. Martha runs out when she hears Jesus is near. Mary stays inside—still, while friends try to comfort her.  
**THE TURN:** Martha doesn’t shout. She slips close and whispers: “The Master is come, and calleth for thee” (11:28). Mary rises—not because shame pushed her, but because she was called by name through someone who knew where she was. At His feet she says what Martha already said: “Lord, if thou hadst been here, my brother had not died” (11:32). He sees her weeping, groans in the spirit, is troubled—and “Jesus wept” (11:35). The mourners see love, not a lecture.  
**LAST LINE (exact):** When you cannot move, someone can still tell you: He has come—and He is not afraid of your tears.  
**WHY A LONELY PERSON WOULD SHARE IT:** It honors staying frozen instead of shaming it.  
**RISK:** Could sound like you must wait for a friend to fetch you—text shows Mary chose to rise when called.

---

**TITLE:** If Thou Hadst Been Here  
**FIRST 3 SECONDS:** If you’ve ever prayed—and felt Heaven waited too long—this line is for you.  
**SETUP:** Jesus loved Martha, Mary, and Lazarus (11:5). Word came: “he whom thou lovest is sick.” He stayed two more days where He was (11:6). By the time He arrived, Lazarus had been in the grave four days (11:17).  
**THE TURN:** Martha meets Him first: “Lord, if thou hadst been here, my brother had not died” (11:21). Mary falls at His feet and says the same words (11:32)—same ache, two voices. He doesn’t correct their timing; He groans, is troubled, asks where they laid him. They answer with words He once used to invite disciples: “Lord, come and see” (11:34; cf. 1:39). Then “Jesus wept” (11:35). “Behold how he loved him!” (11:36).  
**LAST LINE (exact):** Late by our clocks is not loveless—and the God who raised Lazarus is “a man of sorrows, and acquainted with grief” (Isa 53:3).  
**WHY A LONELY PERSON WOULD SHARE IT:** It names “too late” without blaming the griever.  
**RISK:** “Two days” could feel like explaining away pain unless we stay on sisters’ words and His tears, not timing trivia.

---

**TITLE:** Loose Him, Let Him Go  
**FIRST 3 SECONDS:** Even after the impossible happens, you still need people beside you.  
**SETUP:** At the tomb, stone rolled back, Jesus cries, “Lazarus, come forth” (11:43). The dead man walks out bound in graveclothes, face covered.  
**THE TURN:** Jesus does not unbind him alone. He tells the community: “Loose him, and let him go” (11:44). Life is His voice—but hands nearby finish the work. Earlier, before the miracle, He told grieving Martha: “I am the resurrection, and the life” (11:25), and asked, “Believest thou this?” (11:26)—hope anchored in Him, not in a formula.  
**LAST LINE (exact):** “The LORD is nigh unto them that are of a broken heart” (Ps 34:18)—and sometimes “nigh” looks like someone helping you breathe again.  
**WHY A LONELY PERSON WOULD SHARE IT:** It says needing others after crisis is normal, not failure.  
**RISK:** Could over-promise community always shows up; text shows He commands it, not that every viewer has it now.

Part 3 — Four questions (one line each)

**Who is it for?** Anyone grieving alone who wonders if God cares that help felt late.  
**Main take-away?** The same “too late” prayer Martha and Mary spoke was met with groaning, tears, and resurrection—not dismissal.  
**The curiosity gap?** He knew He would raise Lazarus—so why groan, weep, and ask “Where have ye laid him?” before He calls Lazarus out.  
**Why a global newcomer gets it?** Two sisters say one plain sentence everyone understands; you see love in two words—“Jesus wept”—without needing church background.
