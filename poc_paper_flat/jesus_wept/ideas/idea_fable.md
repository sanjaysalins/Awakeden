# Ideas — "Jesus wept" (John 11) — seat: fable

## Part 1 — Three ideas

### Idea 1 — SHE STAYED IN THE HOUSE
- FIRST 3 SECONDS: "Still sitting in that room, since the day you lost them?"
- SETUP: Lazarus is four days dead. Jesus, the family's friend, did not come in time. Martha goes out to meet Him. Mary "sat still in the house" (11:20). We stay with the one who could not get up.
- THE TURN: Jesus does not wait for her to be strong. Martha whispers, "The Master is come, and calleth for thee" (11:28). Mary runs, falls at His feet, and says the hard sentence (11:32). He does not argue. He sees her weeping, asks for the grave, and "Jesus wept" (11:35) — and then He calls the dead man out (11:43).
- LAST LINE: "He is 'nigh unto them that are of a broken heart' — near. Even in that room."
- WHY A LONELY PERSON WOULD SHARE IT: It says "you were asked for by name while you could not move" — a thing you can send without preaching at a friend.
- RISK: "He does not argue" is read from silence; and Psalm 34:18 must not sound like "He will raise your loved one now."

### Idea 2 — THE SAME SENTENCE, TWICE
- FIRST 3 SECONDS: "Have you ever said to God: where were You?"
- SETUP: Two sisters, one grave. Each says the exact same words to Jesus: "Lord, if thou hadst been here, my brother had not died" (11:21, 11:32). The film shows the line twice, in two voices.
- THE TURN: He answers the same sentence two ways. To Martha, standing, He gives a promise: "I am the resurrection, and the life" (11:25). To Mary, on the ground crying, He gives no speech. He "groaned in the spirit, and was troubled" (11:33) and wept (11:35). Both answers are His.
- LAST LINE: "Bring Him the sentence. He has heard it before — and He stayed."
- WHY A LONELY PERSON WOULD SHARE IT: It gives permission to say the angry prayer out loud, and shows it was not punished.
- RISK: Could read as "Martha = head, Mary = heart" pop psychology the text never states; keep to what He did, not why.

### Idea 3 — THE TEARS THAT COST HIM
- FIRST 3 SECONDS: "When did someone last cry for you?"
- SETUP: A crowd of mourners watches a man cry at a friend's grave and says, "Behold how he loved him!" (11:36). His friends had warned Him that going back there was dangerous (11:8). He went.
- THE TURN: He gives Lazarus his life back — and "from that day forth they took counsel together for to put him to death" (11:53). The leaders even planned to kill Lazarus (12:10). The grave He emptied put Him on the road to His own.
- LAST LINE: "'A man of sorrows, and acquainted with grief.' He did not only cry at a grave. He went into one — for you."
- WHY A LONELY PERSON WOULD SHARE IT: It answers "does anyone care this much?" with a price paid, not a nice feeling.
- RISK: Blame language — must say "the chief priests and Pharisees," never a people; and "for you" needs the cross made clear for a newcomer in very few words.

## Part 3 — The 4 questions

- WHO IT IS FOR: The person who cannot get up — grieving, alone, sure God came too late.
- MAIN TAKE-AWAY: Jesus asks for the one who stayed behind, weeps with her before He fixes anything, and is stronger than the death that hurt her.
- THE CURIOSITY GAP: She would not go out to Him — so what does He do about the one who stays in the house? (Paid off at "calleth for thee," then "Jesus wept," then the grave.)
- WHY A GLOBAL NEWCOMER GETS IT: One room, one sister, one sentence, one tear, one grave — short plain words, every old KJV phrase explained by the line next to it, and no Bible knowledge needed.
