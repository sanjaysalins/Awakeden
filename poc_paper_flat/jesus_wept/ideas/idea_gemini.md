Part 1
TITLE: When Heaven Arrives Too Late
FIRST 3 SECONDS: Have you ever felt like God missed the deadline?
SETUP: Two sisters watch their brother die. When Jesus finally arrives days later, they both hit Him with the exact same heartbroken accusation.
THE TURN: Jesus doesn't defend His timing or offer polite theology. Instead, He asks to see the grave, and the mourners invite Him with the exact words He once used to call His disciples: "Lord, come and see."
LAST LINE: He is near to the brokenhearted, and He will step into your pain before He takes it away.
WHY A LONELY PERSON WOULD SHARE IT: It validates the anger of feeling abandoned while proving God still intimately cares.
RISK: It might sound like God makes mistakes if the delay isn't clearly framed as having a purpose.

TITLE: For the One Sitting Frozen
FIRST 3 SECONDS: When your world shatters, do you just sit in the dark?
SETUP: Martha runs out to meet Jesus, but Mary is paralyzed by grief. She "sat still in the house," thinking she is entirely alone.
THE TURN: Jesus doesn't leave her inside. He pauses the whole crowd to send a secret, personal invitation to the one who couldn't get out of bed: "The Master is come, and calleth for thee."
LAST LINE: He sees you in the quiet room, and He will not leave you comfortless.
WHY A LONELY PERSON WOULD SHARE IT: It perfectly captures the isolating paralysis of deep depression and grief.
RISK: It could unintentionally minimize Martha's active faith by focusing too heavily on Mary's isolation.

TITLE: The Miracle That Cost Everything
FIRST 3 SECONDS: What if helping your friend meant signing your own death warrant?
SETUP: Jesus stands at a tomb, surrounded by weeping friends. He knows He can raise Lazarus, but He also knows the terrible price of doing it.
THE TURN: The moment He says "Lazarus, come forth," He seals His own fate. From that exact day, the leaders "took counsel together for to put him to death."
LAST LINE: He stepped into our grave, so we could walk out of it.
WHY A LONELY PERSON WOULD SHARE IT: It shows that God's love isn't cheap or distant; He traded His life for theirs.
RISK: The focus on the leaders plotting might distract from the immediate emotional comfort of the grieving viewer.

Part 3
Who is it for: The person paralyzed by grief, sitting alone, feeling unseen and left behind by God.
Main take-away: God doesn't demand you be strong; He calls for you in the dark and weeps with you in the pain.
The curiosity gap: The contrast between the sister who runs out to fight and the one who sits frozen—and who Jesus asks for.
Why a global newcomer gets it: Everyone understands the isolating paralysis of losing someone you love, and the shock of a powerful figure breaking down in tears.
