Part 1 — THREE IDEAS

1. TITLE: You Prayed. They Still Died.
FIRST 3 SECONDS: “You prayed. And someone you loved still died.”
SETUP: Two sisters sent for Jesus because their brother was sick. Jesus loved them. He stayed where He was for two more days.
THE TURN: Both sisters tell Him, “Lord, if thou hadst been here, my brother had not died.” Their painful words become part of their meeting with Jesus. He sees Mary crying, is troubled, and weeps (11:21, 32–35).
LAST LINE: “Your grief does not mean Jesus has stopped loving you.”
WHY A LONELY PERSON WOULD SHARE IT: It gives their hardest prayer words they can send to someone else.
RISK: Raising Lazarus must not become a promise that every bereavement will be reversed now.

2. TITLE: Still in the House
FIRST 3 SECONDS: “Still sitting in the room where you cried?”
SETUP: Martha goes to meet Jesus. Mary stays sitting in the house while friends comfort her.
THE TURN: Martha comes back with a quiet message: “The Master is come, and calleth for thee.” Explain plainly: Jesus is here and is calling for Mary. She comes with tears, falls at His feet, and He weeps too (11:20, 28–35).
LAST LINE: “Mary came to Jesus crying. Their meeting held His tears too.”
WHY A LONELY PERSON WOULD SHARE IT: It makes room for someone who has no strength to sound hopeful.
RISK: Do not invent why Mary stayed seated or imply every grieving viewer will receive a personal summons like hers.

3. TITLE: What Giving Life Cost
FIRST 3 SECONDS: “After the funeral, the empty chair stays.”
SETUP: Lazarus’s place is empty. At his tomb, Jesus cries, calls him out, and tells the people to unwrap him.
THE TURN: From that day, the leaders plan Jesus’s death. Later, Lazarus is sitting at a meal with Him (11:43–44, 53; 12:1–2). The restored place at the table stands beside a gathering threat to Jesus’s life.
LAST LINE: “The Jesus who wept at the grave gave life—and faced death Himself.”
WHY A LONELY PERSON WOULD SHARE IT: It shows that Jesus’s compassion has weight and cost.
RISK: Do not imply the leaders’ plot was the whole meaning of Jesus’s death, or turn their actions into blame against a people.

Part 3 — FOUR QUESTIONS

Who is it for? Someone whose unanswered prayer now feels like evidence that God never loved them.
Main take-away? Jesus’s love, His tears, and His promise of life can meet us inside grief.
The curiosity gap? What happens when two grieving sisters tell Jesus He could have prevented their loss?
Why a global newcomer gets it? A sick brother, a delayed arrival, two sisters’ tears, and a grave make the story understandable before any theological explanation.
