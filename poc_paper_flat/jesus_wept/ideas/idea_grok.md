Part 1 — Three ideas

1. TITLE: The One Still Inside
FIRST 3 SECONDS: You are still in the room. You did not go out.
SETUP: Friends have come to comfort the house. Martha hears that Jesus is near and goes out to Him. Mary stays where she is. The film stays with the one who does not move. Same loss, two ways of bearing it.
THE TURN: John 11:20 says Mary sat still in the house. John 11:28: Martha calls her secretly and says, "The Master is come, and calleth for thee." Mary gets up, falls at His feet, and says the late sentence. He sees the weeping, groans, and weeps (11:32–35).
LAST LINE: Mary sat still. Then she heard, "The Master is come, and calleth for thee."
WHY A LONELY PERSON WOULD SHARE IT: It is for the friend who cannot make themselves leave the room.
RISK: "Calleth for thee" is Martha's report. The text never quotes Jesus sending for Mary, so a viewer must not hear a private promise that He has summoned them by name.

2. TITLE: If Only You Had Come
FIRST 3 SECONDS: You said it again. If only You had been here.
SETUP: The prayer feels too late. Lazarus has already been in the grave four days. Martha says the hard sentence out loud. Jesus does not shame her. While her brother is still dead, He tells her who He is, and He ties it to believing in Him.
THE TURN: Mary says the same words at His feet (11:32). He sees her weeping, and the friends weeping, groans in the spirit, and is troubled (11:33). They say, "Lord, come and see" (11:34). Then "Jesus wept" (11:35), and the friends say, "Behold how he loved him!" (11:36). He had already said, "I am the resurrection, and the life" (11:25). The tears come after that name, not instead of it.
LAST LINE: He is the life. He stood in that place and cried.
WHY A LONELY PERSON WOULD SHARE IT: A friend who is angry that God came late can hear their own sentence without being shamed, and without being promised their person will walk back in.
RISK: "Though he were dead, yet shall he live" can be heard as "your loved one returns now." The words belong to "he that believeth in me," and this film must not show a second miracle as the viewer's ending.

3. TITLE: Loose Him, Let Him Go
FIRST 3 SECONDS: The worst day has passed. You are still wrapped up.
SETUP: At the cave Martha says he has been dead four days and already smells. Jesus says, "Take ye away the stone" (11:39). He cries with a loud voice, "Lazarus, come forth" (11:43). The dead man comes out, still bound hand and foot, face still covered.
THE TURN: John 11:44. Jesus says to the people around him, "Loose him, and let him go." Life is His gift. The grave clothes come off by other hands. From that day the chief priests and Pharisees take counsel to put Jesus to death (11:53), and the leaders plan to kill Lazarus too (12:10).
LAST LINE: He cried, "Lazarus, come forth." Then He said, "Loose him, and let him go."
WHY A LONELY PERSON WOULD SHARE IT: It gives a grieving person permission to still be bound, and it tells their friends they may help with the unwrapping.
RISK: A person in fresh grief can wait for their own dead to come out. The film has to say, in plain words, that this was one man in Bethany, and that the leaders' plot is history, not a threat held over the viewer.

Part 3

Who it is for: someone who prayed, felt God came too late, and cries where they think no one sees.
Main take-away: Jesus received that sentence, cried beside them, and He is the resurrection and the life for the one who believes.
The curiosity gap: what does He do when you say, "If only You had been here"?
Why a global newcomer gets it: a brother has died, two sisters say the same sad line, Jesus cries, and He says He is the life.
